## Judicial Review vs Judicial Activism

### Q6 · 30 marks · ~250 words
Is it possible to distinguish between judicial review and judicial activism in India? Does the recent behaviour of the Indian judiciary partake more of judicial activism? Argue with suitable examples.

`Judicial review` `Judicial activism`

> This theme recurs strongly: **2008** (Q5a), **2014** (GS-II Q1, basic structure and judicial activism), **2020** (GS-II Q12, judicial legislation) and **2024** (GS-II Q14, PIL).

## Panchayati Raj & Secularism

### Q7 · 30 marks · ~250 words · *answer any one*
**(a)** Would you say that the implementation of the Panchayati System in the last ten years has led to a real restructuring of the Indian polity?

**(b)** Give your views on the right to freedom of religion as enshrined in the Indian Constitution. Do they make India a secular State?

`73rd Amendment` `Panchayati Raj` `Articles 25-28` `Secularism`

> Q7(a) is timed to the tenth anniversary of the 73rd/74th Amendments. Secularism returns in **2019** (GS-II Q5, France) and **2024** (GS-II Q15, USA).

## Rights, Environment & the Election Commission

### Q8 · 2 × 15 = 30 marks · ~150 words each · *answer any two*
**(a)** What are the constitutional limitations on the free movements of Indians throughout the country?

**(b)** How has the Indian State tackled the trade-off between environment and development?

**(c)** What are the steps that the Election Commission may take if a recalcitrant State Government wants to put off Assembly Elections?

`Article 19(1)(d)` `Article 21` `Article 324` `ECI powers`

> Q8(a) is asked again in **2022** (GS-II Q2) on rights of movement and residence not being absolute.
