## 🔀 The two eras

The exam changed shape completely in 2013. Read older papers with that in mind.

| | **2000–2012** | **2013–present** |
|---|---|---|
| Where polity sits | A section **inside GS Paper I** (usually Q6–Q9) | **GS Paper II**, its own 250-mark paper |
| Paper title | General Studies Paper I | *Governance, Constitution, Polity, Social Justice and International Relations* |
| Choice | Internal choice throughout ("answer any one / any two") | **None** — every question compulsory |
| Question style | Factual and institutional — *"How is the Election Commission constituted?"* | Analytical and statement-based — *"Comment", "Critically examine", "Elucidate"* |
| Answer lengths | 250 / 150 / 50 / 20 words | 250 / 150 words only |
| Polity marks | ~100/300 (2000–2008), falling to ~37/300 by 2012 | ~110–175 of 250 |

**The 2009–2012 slide is real and worth noting.** The tidy Q6–Q9 polity block dissolves after 2008; by 2012 polity survives only as 50-word and 20-word items. The 2013 restructuring was a correction to exactly that.

## ⚠️ A note on year labels

**Popular topic-wise PYQ compilations misdate questions frequently.** While building this set I cross-checked the widely circulated compilations against the actual year-wise question papers and found errors in both directions. Confirmed examples:

| Question | Commonly listed as | **Actually** |
|---|---|---|
| "Resorting to ordinances has always raised concern…" | 2016 | **2015** Q5 |
| "Starting from inventing the 'basic structure' doctrine…" | 2015 | **2014** Q1 |
| "Discuss the role of the Vice-President of India…" | 2024 | **2022** Q4 |
| "The growth of cabinet system has practically resulted…" | 2022 | **2024** Q3 |
| "Right of movement and residence throughout the territory…" | 2023 | **2022** Q2 |
| "The most significant achievement of modern law in India…" | 2023 | **2022** Q1 |
| "Do you think that constitution of India does not accept…strict separation of powers" | 2020 | **2019** Q1 |
| "Which steps are required for constitutionalization of a Commission?" | 2021 | **2020** Q15 |
| "Though the Human Rights Commissions have contributed…" | 2022 | **2021** Q12 |
| "The Central Administrative Tribunal…" | 2020 | **2019** Q2 |
| "How far do you think cooperation, competition and confrontation…" | 2018 / 2021 | **2020** Q3 |

Everything in these files is keyed to the **year-wise question papers**, not to topic-wise compilations. If you see a mismatch against a coaching handout, this set is the one to trust — but the year-wise paper itself is the final authority.

## 📖 How to use this set

1. **Working through a Laxmikanth chapter?** Open the theme index above, pull every question on that theme, and answer them before moving on. That is the fastest way to convert reading into Mains-ready material.
2. **Studying a specific year?** The year files show the *whole* GS-II paper, with non-polity questions listed at the bottom — so you can see how much of the paper polity actually carried.
3. **Watch the repeat chains.** They are the single best predictor of what gets asked next.
4. **Read the pre-2013 files for content, not format.** The 2000–2012 questions are factual and institutional; they map beautifully onto Laxmikanth chapters and are excellent for testing whether you actually know the Constitution. The 2013+ questions test whether you can argue.

## 📌 Sourcing & fidelity

- Compiled from **year-wise question papers**, cross-checked against two independent topic-wise compilations.
- Question text is reproduced as printed. Where an archived transcription contained obvious OCR errors (e.g. the 2014 paper's *"Staring"*, *"hat speech"*, *"NIIRC"*, *"Rail Tarif"*, and the 2015 paper's *"Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1966"*), the standard published wording is used and the correction is noted in that year's file.
- Three places carry a flagged caveat rather than certainty, noted at the foot of the relevant file: **2000** Q10's sub-clauses, **2005** Q6's missing alternative, and **2012**'s word-limit for the Presidential-election-vote item.
