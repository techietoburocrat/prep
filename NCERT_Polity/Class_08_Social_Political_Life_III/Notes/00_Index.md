## How the chapters connect (the story of the whole book)
The book is organised into **5 Units**, each building on the last:
1. **Unit 1 – The Constitution (Ch 1–2):** the foundational document and its commitment to secularism.
2. **Unit 2 – Parliament (Ch 3):** how laws are actually made, and how citizens shape that process.
3. **Unit 3 – Judiciary (Ch 4):** how laws are enforced and interpreted, and how ordinary people get access to justice.
4. **Unit 4 – Social Justice and the Marginalised (Ch 5–6):** how inequality plays out for Adivasis, Muslims, and Dalits, and how they confront it using the Constitution.
5. **Unit 5 – Economic Presence of the Government (Ch 7–8):** why the government must actively provide public facilities and regulate markets to protect the vulnerable.

Chapter 8 closes the book with **"The Constitution as a Living Ideal"** — showing how the Right to Life (Article 21) has been continuously expanded by ordinary citizens fighting in court, tying every chapter's case studies (health, livelihood, environment, education, shelter) back to Chapter 1's opening idea: a Constitution lays out the ideals a country wants to live by, and each generation must keep working to make those ideals real.
