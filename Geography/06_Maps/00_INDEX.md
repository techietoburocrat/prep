## 📌 Why this folder exists

**Your [START_HERE §2](../00_START_HERE.md) says the atlas is a habit, not a book — 15 minutes every day. That advice was right but incomplete: a habit needs a QUEUE. Without one, "do 15 minutes of maps" becomes "open the atlas, look at India, close the atlas."**

**This folder is the queue.** **Every map pin emitted by every note in the Geography folder — 23 notes, ~110 pins — consolidated, deduplicated, organised by MAP rather than by chapter, and sequenced into a 40-week rotation.**

> ⭐ **And the [Prelims PYQ data](../03_PYQ_Prelims/00_INDEX.md) justifies the whole enterprise: "World Political Geography & Borders" alone is 44 of 503 Prelims geography questions.** **That is a topic no amount of reading touches. Add rivers (46), Indian physical (42) and world physical (32) and you are looking at well over a third of Prelims geography sitting on map knowledge.**

## THE THREE RULES

### 1. Fifteen minutes, every day, no exceptions — and no more than fifteen

**You cannot cram maps.** **Nobody has ever sat down in April for a six-hour map session and retained it.** **But 15 minutes a day for 40 weeks is 70 hours of spaced repetition on exactly the content UPSC likes asking about — and it costs you nothing, because it fits in the gaps.**

⚠️ **The "no more than fifteen" half matters too.** *A 90-minute map session on a Sunday feels productive and is worth less than six ordinary days. Consistency is the mechanism; intensity isn't.*

### 2. TRACE, don't look

**Reading a place name off a page does nothing. Finding it, tracing it with a finger, and then drawing it blank is what builds the mental map.**

**The daily loop:**
```
   1. Read today's item from the rotation      (30 sec)
   2. Find it in the atlas                     (2 min)
   3. Trace it — river source to mouth, range
        end to end, border country by country     (5 min)
   4. Close the atlas. Draw it blank.          (5 min)
   5. Reopen. Check what you got wrong.        (2 min)
```
**Step 4 is the whole exercise. Steps 1–3 are preparation for it.**

### 3. ⭐ Bolt it onto the current-affairs slot

**You already write [Current Affairs](../../Current%20Affairs/00_START_HERE.md) daily.** **Add the map lookup to that same session and Stage 7 costs you no new time at all.**

> **And do this specifically: whatever place was in this week's news — a strait, a conflict zone, a summit venue, a newly-declared Ramsar site, a cyclone landfall — find it on the map that day, IN ADDITION to the rotation item.**
>
> **This is the trick that welds the two subjects together. A place you looked up because it was in the news sticks in a way a place from a list does not — and UPSC's map questions are drawn disproportionately from places that have been in the news.**

## 🛒 The atlas

**[Oxford Student Atlas for India](https://www.flipkart.com/oxford-student-atlas-india-for-competitive-exams-6th/p/itm275e6a52f36ce)** — **~₹252, physical copy, ⚠️ check the title says "for India".** *Full buying guide and the three listing traps are in [START_HERE](../00_START_HERE.md).*

⚠️ **Buy it physical, not as a PDF.** **The only atlas that works is one you can flip open in fifteen seconds and trace with a finger. A PDF adds friction to a habit whose entire value is that it has none.**

**Also useful and free:** **blank outline maps of India and the world — print twenty of each and use one a day for step 4.**

## ✅ PROGRESS TRACKING

**Keep it trivial or you won't do it.** **One line per day appended to a file:**

```
2026-08-12  W1D1  Indian rivers — Ganga system        ✅ drew blank, missed Gomti + Ghaghara order
2026-08-13  W1D2  Indian rivers — Peninsular east     ✅ clean
2026-08-14  W1D3  Indian rivers — Peninsular west     ⚠️ confused Periyar/Bharathapuzha — repeat
2026-08-15  CA    Strait of Hormuz (in the news)      ✅
```

**The ⚠️ lines are the useful ones. Anything marked ⚠️ goes back into the queue a week later** — *that is your spaced-repetition system, and it costs one line of typing.*

## ⚠️ What this folder is NOT

**It is not a substitute for the notes.** **Knowing WHERE the Western Ghats are is Prelims; knowing WHY they make Pune dry is Mains.** **The map tells you where; [Leong Climate 2](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md) tells you why. You need both, and this folder only does the first.**
