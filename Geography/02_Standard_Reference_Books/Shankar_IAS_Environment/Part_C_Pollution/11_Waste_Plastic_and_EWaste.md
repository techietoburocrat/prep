# ♻️ C11 — Solid Waste, Plastic, E-Waste, Noise & Radioactive

> **The one-line point:** ⭐⭐ **India's waste rules have converged on one idea — EXTENDED PRODUCER RESPONSIBILITY. Whoever puts a product on the market owns it at the end of its life. Learn that principle and half of Part C's law becomes derivable.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ THE WASTE HIERARCHY — the organising principle

```
   ⭐⭐ MOST PREFERRED
        ↑
   ⭐ 1. PREVENT / REFUSE ...... don't generate it
   ⭐ 2. REDUCE ................ use less
   ⭐ 3. REUSE ................. use again as-is
   ⭐ 4. RECYCLE ............... reprocess into new material
   ⭐ 5. RECOVER .............. energy from waste, composting
   ⭐ 6. DISPOSE ............... landfill, incineration
        ↓
   ⭐⭐ LEAST PREFERRED
```

> ⭐⭐ **The point most answers miss: ⭐ RECYCLING SITS FOURTH, not first.** ⭐ **Public discourse treats recycling as the solution; the hierarchy treats it as a mid-tier fallback, because recycling still consumes energy, degrades material quality each cycle, and legitimises continued consumption.** 🎯 *Saying this correctly signals you understand the framework rather than the slogan.*

⭐ **The ⭐ CIRCULAR ECONOMY generalises the hierarchy: ⭐ design out waste, keep materials in use, and regenerate natural systems — as opposed to the LINEAR "take–make–dispose" model.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ MUNICIPAL SOLID WASTE

⭐ **Composition in India: ⭐ a HIGH ORGANIC/BIODEGRADABLE fraction (typically ~50%+), which matters because it makes ⭐ COMPOSTING and BIOMETHANATION the natural primary treatment — and makes incineration inefficient, since wet waste has low calorific value.** 🎯 *This is why imported waste-to-energy models often underperform in India.*

⭐ **⭐ SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT RULES, 2016 — key features:**
- ⭐⭐ **Mandatory ⭐ SEGREGATION AT SOURCE into three streams: ⭐ WET (biodegradable) · DRY (recyclable) · DOMESTIC HAZARDOUS**
- ⭐ **Applies beyond municipal areas — to census towns, notified industrial townships, SEZs, railway and defence areas, places of pilgrimage**
- ⭐ **User fees; spot fines for littering**
- ⭐⭐ **Formal ⭐ RECOGNITION AND INTEGRATION OF WASTE PICKERS / the informal sector** — ⭐ *a genuinely important provision, since India's recycling rates are achieved largely by informal workers with no protection*
- ⭐ **Bio-degradable waste to be processed on-site by bulk generators; landfill only for residual, non-usable waste**

⭐ **Problems:** ⭐ **legacy dumpsites and their fires (Ghazipur, Deonar, Bhalswa) · leachate contaminating groundwater · methane emissions · segregation compliance remaining weak · and the fact that most "processing" capacity exists on paper.** ⭐ **⭐ Bioremediation/biomining of legacy dumps is the current policy push under Swachh Bharat 2.0.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ PLASTIC WASTE

| ⭐ Aspect | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The problem** | ⭐ **Non-biodegradable; persists for centuries; ⭐ fragments into MICROPLASTICS (<5 mm) and nanoplastics that enter food chains and drinking water; clogs drains (⭐ a direct cause of urban flooding); killed livestock and marine animals through ingestion** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ocean accumulation** | ⭐ **Currents concentrate floating debris in the gyres — ⭐ the Great Pacific Garbage Patch** *(see [Leong A11](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/11_Oceans_Currents_Tides_Salinity.md))* |
| ⭐⭐ **Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 (amended since)** | ⭐⭐ **⭐ EPR mandatory for producers, importers and brand-owners · ⭐ minimum thickness rules for carry bags (raised over successive amendments) · ⭐ a ban on identified ⭐ SINGLE-USE PLASTIC items from ⭐ 1 July 2022 · plastic-waste processing targets · ⭐ EPR certificates and a centralised portal** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *current thickness thresholds and banned-item list — both have been revised* |
| ⭐ **Alternatives & uses** | ⭐ **Compostable/biodegradable plastics** ⚠️ *(which require industrial composting to actually degrade — a common misconception)* · ⭐ **plastic in road construction — India has used waste plastic in bitumen roads at scale** · co-processing in cement kilns |
| ⭐ **International** | ⭐ **A ⭐ GLOBAL PLASTICS TREATY has been under negotiation through the UNEA process** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *its status — this is live and highly examinable* · ⭐ **the ⭐ BASEL CONVENTION's Plastic Waste Amendments (2019) brought plastic-waste trade under control** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **EXTENDED PRODUCER RESPONSIBILITY — understand the logic, not just the acronym.**
>
> ⭐ **Ordinarily, a producer's cost ends at the point of sale and the disposal cost falls on the municipality and the environment — ⭐ a classic EXTERNALITY.**
>
> ⭐⭐ **EPR internalises it: ⭐ by making the producer legally responsible for collecting and processing an equivalent quantity of what it sold, the disposal cost enters the producer's balance sheet — and therefore its DESIGN decisions.** ⭐ **The intended effect is upstream: less packaging, more recyclable materials.**
>
> ⭐ **India applies EPR to ⭐ plastics, ⭐ e-waste, ⭐ batteries, ⭐ tyres and ⭐ used oil.** 🎯 **Naming the economic logic — internalising an externality — is what turns this from a list into an argument.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ E-WASTE

| ⭐ Aspect | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Scale** | ⭐ **India is among the world's largest e-waste generators** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *ranking and tonnage* |
| ⭐⭐ **What's in it** | ⭐⭐ **Hazardous: ⭐ LEAD, MERCURY, CADMIUM, chromium, brominated flame retardants.** ⭐⭐ **Valuable: ⭐ GOLD, silver, copper, palladium and ⭐ RARE EARTH ELEMENTS** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **URBAN MINING** | ⭐⭐ **The concept worth carrying: ⭐ the concentration of gold in a tonne of discarded circuit boards can EXCEED that in a tonne of ore from a gold mine.** ⭐⭐ **So e-waste is simultaneously a hazard and an ore body** — ⭐ *and for a country importing critical minerals, recovering them domestically is a strategic as well as environmental objective* 🎯 **A strong, distinctive Mains point** |
| ⭐⭐ **The informal-sector problem** | ⭐⭐ **The overwhelming majority of India's e-waste is handled informally — ⭐ open burning of cables to recover copper, ⭐ acid baths to leach gold — exposing workers, often including children, to heavy metals and dioxins, and destroying most of the recoverable value.** ⭐ **Seelampur (Delhi) and Moradabad are the known clusters** |
| ⭐ **E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022** | ⭐ **EPR with tradable ⭐ EPR CERTIFICATES; expanded coverage of electronic items; restriction of hazardous substances (RoHS); producer targets** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` |
| ⭐ **International** | ⭐⭐ **The ⭐ BASEL CONVENTION governs transboundary movement of hazardous waste, ⭐ specifically to stop rich countries dumping it on poor ones** — *see [E17](../Part_E_Governance/17_International_Conventions.md)* |

## 5️⃣ ⭐ OTHER WASTE STREAMS

| ⭐ Stream | ⭐ Rules & key points |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **BIOMEDICAL WASTE** | ⭐ **Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016.** ⭐⭐ **⭐ COLOUR-CODED segregation: ⭐ YELLOW (incineration — anatomical, soiled, chemical) · ⭐ RED (autoclave/shred — contaminated recyclable plastics, tubing, syringes without needles) · ⭐ WHITE/translucent (sharps — needles, blades) · ⭐ BLUE (glassware, metallic implants)** 🎯 *The colour codes are asked* |
| ⭐ **HAZARDOUS WASTE** | ⭐ **Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016.** Common treatment and disposal facilities; manifest system |
| ⭐ **CONSTRUCTION & DEMOLITION** | ⭐ **C&D Waste Rules 2016** — a huge and under-managed stream in Indian cities; a major PM₁₀ source |
| ⭐ **BATTERY WASTE** | ⭐ **Battery Waste Management Rules 2022 — EPR; critical as EV adoption grows** |
| ⭐ **RADIOACTIVE WASTE** | ⭐ **Classified low / intermediate / high level. ⭐ Regulated by the ⭐ AERB (Atomic Energy Regulatory Board).** ⭐ **High-level waste requires isolation for millennia — ⭐ deep geological repositories are the accepted solution and almost none exist worldwide.** ⭐ *Half-life is the governing variable* |

## 6️⃣ ⭐ NOISE POLLUTION

| ⭐ Aspect | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Measurement** | ⭐ **Decibels (dB), a ⭐ LOGARITHMIC scale — ⭐ a 10 dB rise is a tenfold increase in intensity** |
| ⭐⭐ **Standards** | ⭐ **Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000.** ⭐⭐ **Four zones with day/night limits (dB(A) Leq): ⭐ INDUSTRIAL 75/70 · COMMERCIAL 65/55 · RESIDENTIAL 55/45 · ⭐ SILENCE ZONE 50/40** 🎯 *These numbers are asked* |
| ⭐ **Silence zones** | ⭐ **Areas within 100 m of hospitals, educational institutions and courts** |
| ⭐ **Effects** | Hearing loss, hypertension, sleep disturbance, stress, cognitive impairment in children; ⭐ **and significant wildlife effects — ⭐ ocean noise disrupts whale and dolphin communication** |
| ⭐ **Sources** | Traffic, construction, industry, loudspeakers, firecrackers, aircraft |

## 📝 `[P]` / `[M]` / 🎯

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- ⭐⭐ **Waste hierarchy: prevent → reduce → reuse → RECYCLE (4th) → recover → dispose**
- ⭐⭐ **SWM Rules 2016: ⭐ 3-way source segregation (wet/dry/domestic hazardous); waste-picker integration**
- ⭐⭐ **India's MSW has a HIGH organic fraction → composting/biomethanation suit it; incineration doesn't**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **EPR applies to plastics, e-waste, batteries, tyres, used oil**
- ⭐⭐ **Single-use plastic ban from ⭐ 1 July 2022; PWM Rules 2016**
- ⭐⭐ **Microplastics = <5 mm**
- ⭐⭐ **E-waste: hazardous = lead, mercury, cadmium, BFRs. Valuable = gold, silver, copper, palladium, REEs. ⭐ "URBAN MINING"**
- ⭐⭐ **⭐ BIOMEDICAL colour codes: YELLOW (incinerate) · RED (autoclave) · WHITE (sharps) · BLUE (glass)**
- ⭐⭐ **⭐ NOISE limits day/night: Industrial 75/70 · Commercial 65/55 · Residential 55/45 · Silence 50/40. ⭐ Silence zone = within 100 m of hospitals/schools/courts**
- ⭐ **dB is LOGARITHMIC — +10 dB = 10× intensity**
- ⭐ **BASEL Convention = transboundary hazardous waste; its 2019 amendments cover plastics**
- ⭐ **AERB regulates radioactive waste**

### `[M]` MAINS
