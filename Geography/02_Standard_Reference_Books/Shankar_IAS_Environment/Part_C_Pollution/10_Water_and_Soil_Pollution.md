# 💧 C10 — Water & Soil Pollution

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ MEASURING WATER QUALITY

| ⭐ Parameter | ⭐ Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **BOD — Biochemical Oxygen Demand** | ⭐⭐ **The oxygen needed by MICROBES to decompose the ORGANIC matter in a water sample (measured over 5 days at 20 °C).** ⭐ **HIGH BOD = heavy organic pollution = sewage** |
| ⭐⭐ **COD — Chemical Oxygen Demand** | ⭐ **Oxygen needed to oxidise ALL oxidisable matter, organic and inorganic, chemically.** ⭐⭐ **COD is ALWAYS ≥ BOD**, because it captures material microbes can't digest |
| ⭐⭐ **DO — Dissolved Oxygen** | ⭐ **The oxygen actually present. ⭐ HIGH DO = healthy. ⭐ Fish need roughly 4–5 mg/L; below that, kills begin** |
| ⭐ **TDS, turbidity, pH, coliform count** | ⭐ **Faecal coliform is the standard indicator of SEWAGE contamination** |

> ⭐⭐ **The relationship to state in one line: ⭐ high BOD causes low DO — the microbes decomposing the organic load consume the oxygen the fish need.** ⭐ **It is the same mechanism as [eutrophication](../Part_A_Ecology/04_Aquatic_and_Wetland_Ecosystems.md), arriving by a different route.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ SOURCES OF WATER POLLUTION

| | ⭐⭐ **POINT source** | ⭐⭐ **NON-POINT (diffuse) source** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Definition** | ⭐ **A single identifiable outlet** | ⭐ **Spread over an area, no single discharge point** |
| ⭐ **Examples** | Industrial effluent pipe, sewage outfall, power-plant discharge | ⭐ **Agricultural runoff, urban stormwater, atmospheric deposition** |
| ⭐⭐ **Regulability** | ⭐ **EASY — you can find it, meter it and prosecute it** | ⭐⭐ **HARD — this is why fertiliser runoff and eutrophication remain unsolved almost everywhere** 🎯 *A good governance point* |

⭐ **India's dominant load: ⭐ UNTREATED OR PARTIALLY TREATED SEWAGE, not industry.** ⭐ **A large share of India's urban sewage is generated without matching treatment capacity** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *current CPCB figures.* ⭐ *This matters strategically: the intuitive villain is the factory, but the arithmetic points at municipal sewerage.*

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ GROUNDWATER CONTAMINATION — India's most serious water problem

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The framing that makes this topic land: ⭐ arsenic and fluoride are NATURALLY present in the rock. ⭐ They became a public-health catastrophe because of a DEVELOPMENT SUCCESS — the mass shift from contaminated surface water to "safe" tubewells from the 1970s onward.**
>
> ⭐⭐ **We solved a bacteriological problem and created a geochemical one.** 🎯 *One of the best "unintended consequences" case studies available.*

| ⭐ Contaminant | ⭐ Where | ⭐ Effects |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **ARSENIC** | ⭐⭐ **The Ganga–Brahmaputra–Meghna alluvial plains — ⭐ West Bengal, Bihar, UP, Jharkhand, Assam; and Bangladesh** | ⭐⭐ **ARSENICOSIS — skin lesions (melanosis, keratosis), ⭐ "blackfoot disease", cancers of skin, bladder and lung; neurological and developmental effects** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **FLUORIDE** | ⭐⭐ **Rajasthan, Gujarat, AP, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, MP — ⭐ hard-rock and arid terrain.** ⭐ *The most widespread geogenic contaminant in India by area* | ⭐⭐ **⭐ DENTAL fluorosis (mottled, stained teeth) and ⭐ SKELETAL fluorosis (bone deformity, crippling, joint pain)** ⭐ *Irreversible* |
| ⭐⭐ **NITRATE** | ⭐ **Agricultural areas — fertiliser leaching; also sewage** | ⭐⭐ **BLUE BABY SYNDROME (methaemoglobinaemia) in infants** |
| ⭐ **Iron, salinity** | Widespread / coastal | Taste, staining; ⭐ **coastal salinity ingress from over-pumping** |
| ⭐ **Uranium, heavy metals** | Localised — parts of Punjab, industrial belts | |

⭐ **Why arsenic mobilises:** ⭐ **it is bound to iron oxides in the sediment; ⭐ under REDUCING (oxygen-poor) conditions in the aquifer, those oxides dissolve and release the arsenic into the water.** ⭐ *Heavy pumping and organic-matter infiltration promote exactly those conditions.*

⭐ **Responses:** ⭐ **Jal Jeevan Mission (piped water to households)** · ⭐ **switching to surface water or deeper/shallower safe aquifers** · ⭐ **community treatment plants, activated alumina, reverse osmosis** · ⭐ **the National Water Quality Sub-Mission** · rainwater harvesting.

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ RIVER POLLUTION IN INDIA

| ⭐ Programme | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **NAMAMI GANGE** | ⭐ **Launched 2014–15; implemented by the ⭐ National Mission for Clean Ganga (NMCG) under the ⭐ National Ganga Council.** ⭐ **Pillars: sewerage infrastructure, river-front development, river-surface cleaning, afforestation, biodiversity, public participation, industrial effluent monitoring** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ganga's legal status** | ⭐⭐ **The ⭐ Ganga and Yamuna were declared "legal persons" by the Uttarakhand High Court (2017)** ⚠️ *— a landmark, though the order was subsequently stayed by the Supreme Court. ⭐ Part of a global "rights of nature" trend (New Zealand's Whanganui, Ecuador's constitution)* 🎯 *A distinctive point for a GS-II/GS-III answer* |
| ⭐ **Earlier programmes** | ⭐ **Ganga Action Plan (1985), Yamuna Action Plan, National River Conservation Plan** ⚠️ *— widely judged to have under-delivered, largely because sewage-treatment capacity was built but not operated* |
| ⭐⭐ **The core diagnosis** | ⭐⭐ **The problem is not primarily industrial. ⭐ It is (a) untreated municipal sewage and (b) INSUFFICIENT FLOW — abstraction for irrigation leaves too little water to dilute what is discharged.** ⭐ **Hence ⭐ ENVIRONMENTAL FLOWS ("e-flows") — a mandated minimum river flow — is as important as any treatment plant** 🎯 *This is the sophisticated version of the answer* |
| ⭐ **Yamuna** | ⭐ **The Delhi stretch is among India's most polluted; ⭐ the froth is largely from phosphate-rich detergent effluent and low flow** |

## 5️⃣ ⭐ MARINE & THERMAL POLLUTION

| ⭐ Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Marine pollution** | ⭐ **~80% of marine pollution originates on LAND** — rivers, runoff, sewage, atmospheric deposition. ⭐ **Plus oil spills, shipping discharge, ballast water (⭐ a major vector for invasive species), and plastics** |
| ⭐⭐ **OIL SPILLS** | ⭐ **Form a surface film that blocks gas exchange and light; coats birds' feathers destroying insulation.** ⭐ **Response: booms, skimmers, dispersants, ⭐ bioremediation using oil-degrading bacteria — ⭐ *Oilzapper*, developed by TERI, is the Indian example** |
| ⭐⭐ **THERMAL pollution** | ⭐⭐ **Heated water discharged from power plants and industry.** ⭐⭐ **Key mechanism: ⭐ WARM WATER HOLDS LESS DISSOLVED OXYGEN — so the discharge simultaneously RAISES metabolic oxygen demand and LOWERS oxygen availability.** ⭐ Also causes thermal shock and species shifts |
| ⭐ **Dead zones** | ⭐ **Gulf of Mexico; ⭐ the Arabian Sea's expanding oxygen minimum zone** |

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐ SOIL & LAND POLLUTION

| ⭐ Problem | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Agrochemicals** | ⭐ **Pesticide residues; ⭐ persistent organochlorines biomagnify** *(see [A2](../Part_A_Ecology/02_Ecosystem_Energy_Flow_and_Cycles.md))*. ⭐ **Excess urea → soil acidification and NPK imbalance** — ⭐ *India's fertiliser subsidy structure, which favours urea, has skewed the N:P:K ratio away from the agronomic optimum. **A subsidy design producing a soil-chemistry problem*** |
| ⭐⭐ **SALINISATION & waterlogging** | ⭐ **Over-irrigation without drainage in arid zones** — *the [Indira Gandhi Canal](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/07_Arid_and_Desert_Landforms.md) case* |
| ⭐⭐ **Heavy metals** | ⭐ **Industrial effluent, mine tailings, e-waste, sewage sludge — ⭐ lead, cadmium, chromium, mercury.** ⭐ *Enter the food chain via crops* |
| ⭐ **Fly ash** | ⭐ **Thermal power plants; ⭐ mandated utilisation in cement, bricks and road embankments** |
| ⭐ **Mining & overburden** | Acid mine drainage, subsidence, tailings dams |
| ⭐⭐ **Land degradation** | ⭐ **~30% of India's land area is degraded** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *— erosion, salinity, waterlogging, acidification, desertification. ⭐ India's UNCCD pledge is to restore 26 Mha by 2030* |

⭐ **REMEDIATION techniques worth naming:** ⭐ **BIOREMEDIATION** (microbes degrade contaminants) · ⭐ **PHYTOREMEDIATION** (plants extract, stabilise or degrade — ⭐ *sunflower and mustard for heavy metals; water hyacinth, ironically, for some effluents*) · ⭐ **mycoremediation** (fungi) · capping and containment · ⭐ **constructed wetlands** for effluent polishing.

## 7️⃣ ⭐ THE LAW & INSTITUTIONS

⭐ **⭐ Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 — ⭐ India's FIRST major environmental statute, and the one that created the ⭐ CPCB and the SPCBs.** ⭐ **Water Cess Act 1977.** ⭐ **Environment (Protection) Act 1986 — the umbrella.** ⭐ **NGT for adjudication.** ⭐ **CGWA / the Central Ground Water Board for groundwater regulation.** *Full treatment in [E15](../Part_E_Governance/15_Indian_Environmental_Acts.md) and [E18](../Part_E_Governance/18_Institutions_National_and_International.md).*

⚠️ ⭐⭐ **A recurring structural point: ⭐ WATER IS A STATE SUBJECT in the Constitution (Entry 17, State List), while inter-state rivers are Union (Entry 56).** ⭐ **So river-basin management crosses a constitutional seam — which is a large part of why basin-wide action is so hard and why inter-state water disputes recur.** 🎯 *Links directly to your [Polity notes](../../../../Polity/00_START_HERE.md).*
