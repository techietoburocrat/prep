# 🐅 B8 — Species Conservation Projects

> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **India runs species-specific conservation programmes because flagship species pull funding and public attention that ecosystems alone never would — and where they have been given legal teeth, they have worked.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ PROJECT TIGER (1973) — the flagship

| Aspect | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Launched** | ⭐ **1973**, with 9 reserves |
| ⭐⭐ **Legal status** | ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Began as a centrally sponsored SCHEME. ⭐ Given STATUTORY backing only by the WPA amendment of 2006**, which created the ⭐ **NTCA** and the concept of an inviolate ⭐ **CRITICAL TIGER HABITAT** |
| ⭐⭐ **Trigger for 2006** | ⭐⭐ **The ⭐ SARISKA SHOCK (2004–05) — Sariska was found to have lost ALL its tigers to poaching, and the official monitoring had not noticed.** ⭐ **The Tiger Task Force that followed produced the NTCA** 🎯 *A failure that produced institutional reform — a good governance example* |
| ⭐ **Monitoring** | ⭐ **All-India Tiger Estimation, every 4 years, using ⭐ camera traps + the double-sampling "Phase IV" method.** ⭐ *A shift from the discredited pugmark-census method* |
| ⭐ **Also** | ⭐ **M-STrIPES** (patrol monitoring app) · **Management Effectiveness Evaluation** of reserves |
| ⭐ **International** | ⭐ **Global Tiger Forum (HQ New Delhi)** · the St Petersburg TX2 goal of doubling wild tigers |
| ⚠️ **Numbers** | ⚠️`[VERIFY]` **India holds roughly 70%+ of the world's wild tigers; the population has risen substantially since the 2006 low** |

> ⭐⭐ **Why it worked, in one line: ⭐ a charismatic ⭐ [umbrella species](06_India_Biogeographic_Zones_and_Species.md) + statutory inviolate cores + independent periodic monitoring + a dedicated authority.** ⭐ **Remove any one of those four and it would not have.** 🎯 *Compare with Project Elephant, which has only the first.*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ THE OTHER MAJOR PROJECTS

| ⭐ Project | ⭐ Year | ⭐ Key points |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **PROJECT ELEPHANT** | ⭐ **1992** | ⚠️ ⭐ **NOT statutory.** ⭐ **Elephant is India's ⭐ NATIONAL HERITAGE ANIMAL. ⭐ Focus on habitat, CORRIDORS and human–elephant conflict. ⭐ MIKE programme (Monitoring the Illegal Killing of Elephants) under CITES.** ⭐ *Corridor protection is the central unmet need* |
| ⭐⭐ **PROJECT SNOW LEOPARD** | ⭐ **2009** | ⭐ **Trans-Himalayan states. ⭐ SECURE Himalaya project; ⭐ the Global Snow Leopard & Ecosystem Protection (GSLEP) Programme; ⭐ the first national snow leopard population assessment (SPAI)** |
| ⭐⭐ **PROJECT DOLPHIN** | ⭐ **2020** | ⭐ **Gangetic dolphin = ⭐ India's NATIONAL AQUATIC ANIMAL (2009). ⭐ Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary, Bihar. Blind; navigates by echolocation. Threats: dams and barrages fragmenting the river, bycatch, pollution** |
| ⭐⭐ **PROJECT CHEETAH** | ⭐ **2022** | ⭐⭐ **Reintroduction of the ⭐ AFRICAN cheetah to ⭐ KUNO NP, Madhya Pradesh.** ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Note carefully: the ⭐ ASIATIC cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952 and survives only in Iran. ⭐ So this is an INTRODUCTION of a different subspecies, not a restoration of the original one** — ⭐ *scientifically contested on exactly that ground, plus habitat suitability and prey density* 🎯 **The nuance is what the question will test** |
| ⭐⭐ **CROCODILE CONSERVATION PROJECT** | ⭐ **1975** | ⭐ **Covers all three Indian species: ⭐ GHARIAL (critically endangered, Chambal), MUGGER (marsh crocodile), and SALTWATER crocodile (Bhitarkanika). ⭐ A genuine early success — gharials were brought back from near-extinction by captive rearing and release** |
| ⭐ **PROJECT HANGUL** | — | ⭐ **Kashmir stag; Dachigam NP; critically small population** |
| ⭐ **PROJECT RED PANDA / others** | — | Sikkim, NE |
| ⭐⭐ **INDIAN RHINO VISION 2020** | ⭐ **2005** | ⭐ **Assam — translocating rhinos to spread the population across several parks (Manas, Pobitora, Orang) rather than concentrating it in Kaziranga.** ⭐ *The "don't keep all your eggs in one basket" strategy — ⭐ the same argument used for the Asiatic lion's second home* |
| ⭐ **SEA TURTLE PROJECT** | 1999 | ⭐ **Olive ridley; ⭐ Gahirmatha & Rushikulya, Odisha; ⭐ TED (Turtle Excluder Devices) mandatory on trawl nets** |
| ⭐ **PROJECT GREAT INDIAN BUSTARD** | — | ⭐ **Rajasthan; conservation breeding at Sam/Sudasari; ⭐ the power-line mortality litigation** |

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ THE VULTURE CRISIS — the best case study in the folder

> ⭐⭐⭐ **India's vulture populations collapsed by ⭐ over 97–99% during the 1990s. ⭐ It is one of the fastest declines of any bird species ever recorded, anywhere.**

```
   ⭐⭐ THE CAUSAL CHAIN

   ⭐ DICLOFENAC — a cheap veterinary anti-inflammatory —
     given to cattle
              ↓
   ⭐ Cattle dies; carcass left in the open, as is customary
              ↓
   ⭐⭐ VULTURES feed on the carcass and ingest the residue
              ↓
   ⭐⭐ Diclofenac causes ⭐ RENAL FAILURE and visceral gout
      in vultures — fatal within days
              ↓
   ⭐⭐ Because ⭐ MANY vultures feed on ONE carcass, a single
      treated animal can kill dozens of birds
              ↓
   ⭐⭐⭐ POPULATION COLLAPSE
```

⭐ **The response:** ⭐ **veterinary diclofenac was BANNED in India in 2006** · ⭐ **MELOXICAM promoted as the safe substitute** · ⭐ **Vulture Conservation Breeding Centres (Pinjore, Rajabhatkhawa, Rani)** · ⭐ **"Vulture Safe Zones"** · ⭐ **the Action Plan for Vulture Conservation.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *current recovery status — it is slow, since vultures are extreme [K-strategists](../Part_A_Ecology/01_Ecology_Fundamentals.md) breeding roughly one chick a year.*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Why this case is worth more than the facts — the CONSEQUENCES of losing a scavenger:**
>
> ⭐ **Carcasses that vultures used to strip in hours now rot for days.** ⭐ **Feral dog populations exploded on the surplus, and with them ⭐ dog bites and RABIES — a documented human public-health cost estimated in the billions of dollars.** ⭐ **Rats increased. Anthrax risk rose. ⭐ And the Parsi community's Towers of Silence, which depend on vultures, were left without their means of disposal.**
>
> ⭐⭐ **This is the single clearest demonstration available that ⭐ ECOSYSTEM SERVICES ARE REAL AND EXPENSIVE TO REPLACE** — a "useless" scavenger turned out to be performing free public sanitation at national scale. 🎯 **Deploy it in any biodiversity, ecosystem-services or One Health answer. It beats an abstract argument every time.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐ WILDLIFE TRADE & POACHING

| ⭐ Aspect | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Enforcement bodies** | ⭐ **WCCB (Wildlife Crime Control Bureau)** · ⭐ **TRAFFIC** (the wildlife-trade monitoring network, a WWF–IUCN joint programme) · State forest departments · ⭐ **CITES** implementation |
| ⭐ **Major targets** | ⭐ **Tiger (bones, skin) · elephant (ivory) · rhino (horn) · pangolin (⭐ the world's most trafficked mammal) · red sanders · star tortoise · mongoose hair · sea cucumber · shahtoosh (chiru wool)** |
| ⭐⭐ **SHAHTOOSH** | ⭐⭐ **Wool from the ⭐ Tibetan antelope (CHIRU), which must be KILLED to obtain it — unlike pashmina, which is combed from a living goat.** ⭐ **Banned; a standard Prelims contrast** |
| ⭐ **Drivers** | Traditional medicine demand (largely East Asian), luxury goods, pets, local subsistence |
| ⭐ **India's stance** | ⭐ **India has consistently opposed reopening international ivory trade at CITES CoPs** |

## 5️⃣ 🌱 THEMES THAT CUT ACROSS ALL PROJECTS

| ⭐ Theme | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Single-population risk** | ⭐ **Asiatic lion in Gir; rhino concentrated in Kaziranga; GIB in Rajasthan.** ⭐ **One epidemic, fire or flood could take the lot** — ⭐ *hence Indian Rhino Vision 2020 and the long-running Supreme Court-backed proposal for a second lion home at Kuno* ⚠️ *(which instead received cheetahs — a genuine and citable policy tension)* |
| ⭐⭐ **Corridors matter more than reserves** | ⭐ **Isolated populations lose genetic diversity and cannot recolonise.** ⭐ **Linear infrastructure — highways, rail, canals — is the main severing force** |
| ⭐⭐ **Species-first vs habitat-first** | ⭐⭐ **The critique of flagship-led conservation: money follows charisma, and ⭐ [keystone but unglamorous species](06_India_Biogeographic_Zones_and_Species.md) — vultures, bats, pollinators, dung beetles — get nothing until they collapse.** ⭐ *The vulture case is exactly what that neglect looks like* |
| ⭐⭐ **Reintroduction is hard** | ⭐ **Requires suitable habitat, adequate prey, low conflict, genetic suitability and long-term funding.** ⭐ **Successes: gharial, rhino translocation, Panna and Sariska tiger repopulation. ⭐ Contested: Project Cheetah** |
| ⭐ **One Health** | ⭐ **The vulture→feral dog→rabies chain, and zoonotic spillover generally, show wildlife, livestock and human health are one system** |
