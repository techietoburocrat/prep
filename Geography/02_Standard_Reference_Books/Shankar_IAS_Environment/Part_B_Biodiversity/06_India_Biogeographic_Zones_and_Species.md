# 🇮🇳 B6 — India's Biogeographic Zones & Key Species

> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **India occupies 2.4% of the world's land and holds about 8% of its recorded species — because it is the only country that contains Himalayan alpine, tropical rainforest, hot desert, cold desert, mangrove and coral within one border.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ THE TEN BIOGEOGRAPHIC ZONES — a standing Prelims item

⭐ **Classification by ⭐ RODGERS & PANWAR (1988), for the Wildlife Institute of India.** ⭐ **The framework used for planning India's protected-area network.**

| # | ⭐ Zone | 🗺️ Where | ⭐ Character & signature species |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **1** | ⭐⭐ **TRANS-HIMALAYA** | ⭐ **Ladakh, Lahaul-Spiti, north Sikkim** | ⭐⭐ **COLD DESERT, beyond the Great Himalaya. ⭐ Snow leopard, Tibetan wild ass (kiang), blue sheep, Tibetan antelope (chiru), black-necked crane** |
| ⭐ **2** | ⭐⭐ **HIMALAYA** | ⭐ **The Himalayan arc proper** | ⭐ **Steep altitudinal zonation — subtropical to alpine. ⭐ Red panda, musk deer, Himalayan tahr, monal, brown & Himalayan black bear** |
| ⭐ **3** | ⭐⭐ **DESERT** | ⭐ **Thar (Rajasthan, Gujarat), Rann of Kachchh** | ⭐ **Hot arid. ⭐ Great Indian Bustard, chinkara, blackbuck, desert fox, ⭐ Indian wild ass** |
| ⭐ **4** | ⭐⭐ **SEMI-ARID** | ⭐ **Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat, Rajasthan's east, MP's west** | ⭐ Transition between desert and the Gangetic plain. ⭐ **Asiatic lion (Gir), caracal, wolf, blackbuck** |
| ⭐ **5** | ⭐⭐ **WESTERN GHATS** | ⭐ **Gujarat → Kanyakumari** | ⭐⭐ **A biodiversity hotspot. ⭐ Lion-tailed macaque, Nilgiri tahr, Malabar giant squirrel, Nilgiri langur; huge amphibian endemism** |
| ⭐ **6** | ⭐⭐ **DECCAN PENINSULA** | ⭐ **The largest zone by area** — central & southern plateau | ⭐ **Dry deciduous forest & scrub. ⭐ Tiger, chital, sambar, sloth bear, gaur, wild dog (dhole)** |
| ⭐ **7** | ⭐⭐ **GANGETIC PLAIN** | ⭐ **From the Yamuna to the Bengal delta** | ⭐ **Alluvial, densely settled, little natural habitat left. ⭐ Gangetic dolphin, gharial, swamp deer, hog deer** |
| ⭐ **8** | ⭐⭐ **COASTS** | ⭐ **Both coasts, ~7,500 km, plus Lakshadweep** | ⭐ **Mangroves, lagoons, beaches, coral. ⭐ Dugong, olive ridley, marine turtles** |
| ⭐ **9** | ⭐⭐ **NORTH-EAST** | ⭐ **NE states (excluding the Himalayan parts)** | ⭐⭐ **Part of the Indo-Burma hotspot. ⭐ Hoolock gibbon (India's only ape), clouded leopard, one-horned rhino, golden langur, pygmy hog** |
| ⭐ **10** | ⭐⭐ **ISLANDS** | ⭐ **Andaman & Nicobar; Lakshadweep** | ⭐ **Very high ENDEMISM. ⭐ Narcondam hornbill, Nicobar megapode, Andaman wild pig, dugong, saltwater crocodile** |

> ⭐⭐ **Two things this table is testing beyond the list itself:**
> ⭐ **(a) ⭐ TRANS-HIMALAYA is a SEPARATE zone from HIMALAYA** — the cold desert beyond the main range is ecologically nothing like the forested southern slopes.
> ⭐ **(b) ⭐ The DECCAN PENINSULA is the LARGEST zone by area**, and the ⭐ **GANGETIC PLAIN retains the least natural habitat** — because it is the most fertile and hence the most cultivated.

⚠️ ⭐ **Do not confuse this 10-zone scheme with: ⭐ Champion & Seth's 16 FOREST types ([A3](../Part_A_Ecology/03_Succession_and_Terrestrial_Ecosystems.md)) · ⭐ the 4 biodiversity HOTSPOTS ([B5](05_Biodiversity_and_Hotspots.md)) · or the physiographic divisions of [Cl.11 *India* Ch.2](../../../01_NCERT/Class_11_India_Physical_Environment/Notes/02_Structure_and_Physiography.md).** ⭐ **Four different classifications of the same country, for four different purposes.** 🎯 *Prelims exploits exactly this confusion.*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ SPECIES CATEGORIES — the conservation vocabulary

| ⭐ Category | ⭐ Definition | ⭐ Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **KEYSTONE species** | ⭐⭐ **A species whose effect on the ecosystem is DISPROPORTIONATELY LARGE relative to its abundance. ⭐ Remove it and the ecosystem restructures** | ⭐ **The FIG tree (fruits when little else does, feeding many species through the lean season) · elephants (open forest, disperse seeds) · sea otters · tigers as apex predators** |
| ⭐⭐ **FLAGSHIP species** | ⭐⭐ **A charismatic species used to RAISE PUBLIC SUPPORT and funding for conservation.** ⭐ *Chosen for appeal, not ecological role* | ⭐ **Tiger · elephant · rhino · giant panda · snow leopard** |
| ⭐⭐ **UMBRELLA species** | ⭐⭐ **A species with LARGE habitat requirements — protecting it automatically protects many others sharing its range** | ⭐ **Tiger · elephant · snow leopard** |
| ⭐⭐ **INDICATOR species** | ⭐⭐ **A species whose condition signals the health of the ecosystem** | ⭐ **Lichens (air quality) · amphibians (water quality & UV) · ⭐ mayfly larvae (river health) · polar bear (Arctic ice)** |
| ⭐ **Endemic species** | Found nowhere else | ⭐ **Nilgiri tahr, lion-tailed macaque, Nicobar megapode, sangai deer** |
| ⭐ **Foundation species** | Creates or defines the habitat itself | ⭐ **Corals · mangroves · kelp** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The distinction that gets asked: ⭐ KEYSTONE is an ECOLOGICAL fact; FLAGSHIP is a MARKETING choice.**
>
> ⭐ **A species can be both — the tiger is keystone, flagship AND umbrella, which is exactly why Project Tiger has been such an effective conservation vehicle.**
>
> ⭐⭐ **But they can also come apart, and that is the interesting case: ⭐ many keystone species are unglamorous — figs, bats, dung beetles, pollinating insects — and get no funding at all, while charismatic non-keystone species attract millions.** 🎯 ⭐ **"Conservation funding follows charisma, not ecological importance" is a sharp, defensible line for a Mains answer.**

⭐ **Also worth knowing: ⭐ CRYPTIC species** (genetically distinct but look identical) · ⭐ **relict species** (survivors of a formerly wider distribution) · ⭐ **LAZARUS species** (rediscovered after being presumed extinct — ⭐ *the Jerdon's courser in Andhra Pradesh, rediscovered in 1986*).

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ INDIA'S ENDEMISM & NOTABLE SPECIES

⭐ **India holds roughly 8% of the world's recorded species on 2.4% of its land area.** ⭐ **Endemism is concentrated in the ⭐ Western Ghats, the ⭐ Northeast and the ⭐ Andaman & Nicobar Islands** — ⭐ *islands and isolated mountain ranges, exactly as biogeography predicts.*

### ⭐ Some India-specific species worth having ready

| ⭐ Species | ⭐ Note |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Asiatic lion** | ⭐ **Gir, Gujarat — the ONLY wild population in the world.** ⭐ *A single-site population, hence the long-running debate on a second home (Kuno)* |
| ⭐⭐ **One-horned rhinoceros** | ⭐ **Kaziranga, Manas, Pobitora, Orang, Dudhwa, Jaldapara** |
| ⭐⭐ **Snow leopard** | ⭐ **Trans-Himalaya; ⭐ Project Snow Leopard; ⭐ Hemis NP** |
| ⭐⭐ **Great Indian Bustard (GIB)** | ⭐⭐ **Critically endangered; ⭐ Desert National Park; ⭐ killed by power lines from solar/wind farms — the renewables-vs-biodiversity conflict** |
| ⭐⭐ **Gangetic dolphin** | ⭐⭐ **India's NATIONAL AQUATIC ANIMAL; blind, echolocating; ⭐ Vikramshila Dolphin Sanctuary (Bihar); ⭐ Project Dolphin** |
| ⭐⭐ **Sangai (brow-antlered deer)** | ⭐ **Endemic to ⭐ Keibul Lamjao, Loktak — the world's only floating national park** |
| ⭐⭐ **Hoolock gibbon** | ⭐ **India's only ape; NE India** |
| ⭐ **Lion-tailed macaque, Nilgiri tahr** | Western Ghats endemics |
| ⭐ **Dugong** | ⭐ **"Sea cow"; Gulf of Mannar, Palk Bay, Andamans; India's first ⭐ Dugong Conservation Reserve (Tamil Nadu)** |
| ⭐⭐ **Gharial** | ⭐ **Critically endangered; ⭐ Chambal (National Chambal Sanctuary), Girwa, Son** |
| ⭐ **Olive ridley turtle** | ⭐ **Gahirmatha & Rushikulya, Odisha — ⭐ ARRIBADA mass nesting** |
| ⭐⭐ **Vultures** | ⭐⭐ **Catastrophic ~99% decline caused by ⭐ DICLOFENAC in cattle carcasses — see [B8](08_Species_Conservation_Projects.md)** |
| ⭐ **Pygmy hog, golden langur, red panda, clouded leopard** | NE endemics/near-endemics |
| ⭐ **Nicobar megapode, Narcondam hornbill** | Island endemics — ⭐ *the Narcondam hornbill exists on ONE small island* |

⭐ **National symbols:** ⭐ **National animal — TIGER · National bird — INDIAN PEACOCK · National aquatic animal — GANGETIC DOLPHIN · National heritage animal — ELEPHANT · National tree — BANYAN · National flower — LOTUS · National fruit — MANGO · National river — GANGA.** 🎯 *Cheap, and occasionally asked.*

## 4️⃣ 🌱 ⭐⭐ HUMAN–WILDLIFE CONFLICT — an under-prepared Mains topic

| ⭐ Aspect | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Scale** | ⭐ **Elephants and humans both die in large numbers annually in India** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *current figures.* ⭐ **Elephant–train collisions, crop raiding, leopards in urban fringes, tiger straying, monkey conflict** |
| ⭐⭐ **Causes** | ⭐⭐ **Habitat fragmentation and blocked CORRIDORS · land-use change on migration routes · linear infrastructure (roads, rail, canals, fences) · declining wild prey · rising populations of some species inside shrinking ranges** |
| ⭐⭐ **The uncomfortable truth worth stating** | ⭐⭐ **Conservation successes CREATE conflict.** ⭐ *More tigers and more elephants in the same or smaller habitat means more encounters. ⭐ Conflict is partly a symptom of success, and pretending otherwise makes the policy discussion dishonest* |
| ⭐ **Responses** | ⭐ **Corridor protection & eco-sensitive zones · underpasses and overpasses on highways/rail · early-warning systems and SMS alerts · solar fencing & bio-fencing (chilli, bee) · rapid compensation for crop and life loss · insurance schemes · Primary Response Teams** |
| ⭐⭐ **The equity dimension** | ⭐⭐ **The costs of conservation fall on the poorest people living beside protected areas, while the benefits are national and global.** ⭐ **Unless compensation is fast and adequate, local tolerance collapses — and local tolerance is what conservation actually runs on** 🎯 *This framing is what lifts the answer* |

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The 10 biogeographic zones (Rodgers & Panwar, WII): Trans-Himalaya · Himalaya · Desert · Semi-arid · Western Ghats · Deccan Peninsula · Gangetic Plain · Coasts · North-East · Islands**
- ⭐⭐ **TRANS-HIMALAYA ≠ Himalaya. ⭐ Deccan Peninsula = largest zone**
- ⚠️ ⭐ **Don't confuse: 10 biogeographic zones ≠ 16 forest types ≠ 4 hotspots**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **KEYSTONE (disproportionate ecological role — fig, elephant) vs FLAGSHIP (charisma — tiger, panda) vs UMBRELLA (large range) vs INDICATOR (lichen, amphibian, mayfly)**

- ⭐ **India: 2.4% of land, ~8% of species; one of 17 megadiverse countries**
