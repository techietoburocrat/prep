## 1️⃣ THE VOCABULARY OF AQUATIC ECOSYSTEMS

**Two great divisions: FRESHWATER** (lentic = still — lakes, ponds; lotic = flowing — rivers, streams) and **MARINE** (oceans, seas), with **BRACKISH** systems (estuaries, lagoons, mangroves) between them.

### Zonation — by light and by depth

| Zone | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PHOTIC / euphotic** | **The sunlit upper layer where photosynthesis exceeds respiration — roughly the top 200 m in clear ocean.** **All primary production happens here** |
| **APHOTIC** | **Below it — no light, no photosynthesis. Depends entirely on organic matter sinking from above ("marine snow")** |
| **Littoral** | Shallow, near-shore, rooted plants can grow |
| **Limnetic** | Open water of a lake, within the photic zone |
| **Profundal** | Deep water of a lake, below light penetration |
| **Benthic / benthos** | **The bottom, and the organisms living on or in it** |
| **Pelagic** | The open water column |
| **Neritic** | Over the continental shelf |
| **Abyssal / hadal** | Deep ocean / trenches |

**Organism groups:** **PLANKTON** (drifting — *phytoplankton = the ocean's producers; zooplankton = primary consumers*) · **NEKTON** (actively swimming — fish, whales, squid) · **BENTHOS** (bottom-dwelling) · **NEUSTON** (at the surface film) · **PERIPHYTON** (attached to surfaces).

**THERMOCLINE** = the layer of rapid temperature change with depth; **HALOCLINE** = of salinity; **PYCNOCLINE** = of density. *These layers act as barriers to vertical mixing — which is exactly why the [open ocean is nutrient-poor](02_Ecosystem_Energy_Flow_and_Cycles.md) and why upwelling matters so much.*

## 2️⃣ ESTUARIES — where a river meets the sea

**An ESTUARY is a partly enclosed coastal body of water where freshwater from a river mixes with saltwater from the sea.** **It is the classic [ECOTONE](01_Ecology_Fundamentals.md).**

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Among the most PRODUCTIVE ecosystems on Earth** | **Nutrients delivered by the river + shallow sunlit water + tidal mixing that keeps nutrients suspended** |
| **NURSERY GROUNDS** | **A very large share of commercial marine fish species spend their juvenile stage in estuaries.** *Destroy the estuary and the offshore fishery collapses years later — the damage is displaced in both space and time, which is why it is politically easy to ignore* |
| **Harsh for its inhabitants** | **Salinity swings twice daily with the tide — so estuarine species must be EURYHALINE.** *Species diversity is often LOW even though productivity is very high* |
| **Sediment trap** | Fine sediment settles where flow slows — building deltas and mudflats |

**India's major estuaries:** **the Hooghly, Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Narmada, Tapi and Mandovi–Zuari.** *Note the [Narmada and Tapi are estuarine rather than deltaic because they are rift valleys](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/04_River_Landforms.md).*

## 3️⃣ ⭐ MANGROVES — the highest-value topic in this file

**Salt-tolerant forests of the intertidal zone in tropical and subtropical sheltered coasts.**

### The adaptations — each solves a specific problem

| Adaptation | Problem it solves |
|---|---|
| **PNEUMATOPHORES — breathing roots** | **Waterlogged mud is ANOXIC. These roots grow vertically UPWARD out of the mud and take in air through pores (lenticels)** |
| **STILT / PROP ROOTS** | **Stability in soft mud against tide and storm** |
| **VIVIPARY** | **The seed GERMINATES WHILE STILL ATTACHED to the parent tree, dropping as a ready seedling that can root immediately.** *A normal seed would simply be washed away by the tide before germinating — vivipary is the answer to a moving substrate* |
| **Salt exclusion at the roots** | Ultrafiltration keeps most salt out |
| **Salt-excreting glands on leaves** | Expels the salt that gets through — visible as crystals |
| **Thick waxy succulent leaves** | Water conservation — *saline water is physiologically "dry"* |

### Why mangroves matter — the four services

1. ⭐ **CYCLONE AND TSUNAMI BUFFER** — **they dissipate wave energy and storm surge.** *Post-2004-tsunami studies in Tamil Nadu and Odisha found villages behind intact mangroves suffered markedly less damage.* **Green infrastructure that costs nothing to maintain.**
2. **NURSERY for fish and prawns** — supporting coastal fisheries and livelihoods.
3. **BLUE CARBON** — **mangroves, seagrass and salt marsh sequester carbon per hectare at rates several times a tropical forest's, and store most of it in the SOIL where it can persist for millennia.** *Which also means draining them releases centuries of stored carbon at once.*
4. **Coastal stabilisation & sediment trapping** — they build land rather than losing it.

### India's mangroves ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *for all area figures — use the latest ISFR*

| 🗺️ Site | Note |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **SUNDARBANS** (West Bengal) | **The world's largest mangrove forest and the largest in India.** **UNESCO World Heritage Site · Biosphere Reserve · Ramsar · Tiger Reserve.** **The only mangrove forest with a tiger population** |
| **BHITARKANIKA** (Odisha) | **India's second-largest; Ramsar; saltwater crocodile stronghold; Gahirmatha beach nearby — the world's largest olive ridley nesting site** |
| **Godavari–Krishna mangroves** (Andhra Pradesh) | **Coringa Wildlife Sanctuary** |
| **Pichavaram & Muthupet** (Tamil Nadu) | Pichavaram is among the healthiest |
| **Gulf of Kachchh** (Gujarat) | **Gujarat has among the largest mangrove areas of any Indian state** |
| **Andaman & Nicobar** | Extensive and relatively undisturbed |
| **Baratang, Mahanadi delta, Ratnagiri, Goa** | Smaller patches |

**Key species:** ***Heritiera fomes* — "SUNDARI," the tree the Sundarbans is named after** · ***Rhizophora*** (stilt roots) · ***Avicennia*** (pneumatophores) · ***Sonneratia*** · ***Bruguiera*** · ***Ceriops*** · ***Nypa*** palm.

🌱 **Threats:** **aquaculture/shrimp farm conversion (the biggest single driver globally) · reduced freshwater and sediment supply because of upstream dams · salinity rise · sea-level rise · industrial and port development · firewood cutting.**
**Protection:** **CRZ-I classification (the most protected category) · Ramsar listing · the Mangrove Initiative for Shoreline Habitats & Tangible Incomes (MISHTI) · and Mangrove Alliance for Climate.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *current status of both.*

## 4️⃣ WETLANDS

> **The RAMSAR definition — quote it, it is broad and that breadth is the point:**
>
> ***"Areas of marsh, fen, peatland or water, whether natural or artificial, permanent or temporary, with water that is static or flowing, fresh, brackish or salt, including areas of marine water the depth of which at low tide does not exceed six metres."***
>
> **Note what that includes: artificial wetlands (reservoirs, tanks, paddy fields), temporary ones, and shallow marine areas.** 🎯 **The "6 metres at low tide" figure is a classic Prelims detail.**

**Types:** **marsh · swamp (woody) · bog & fen (peat) · lagoon · estuary · mangrove · floodplain · oxbow · high-altitude lake · man-made tank & reservoir.**

### Ecosystem services — the reason wetlands are called "the kidneys of the landscape"

| Service | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Flood control** | **They absorb and slowly release floodwater.** *Urban wetland loss is a principal cause of urban flooding — Chennai's Pallikaranai marsh, Bengaluru's tank system, Mumbai's Mithi floodplain* |
| **Water purification** | **Sediment settles; plants and microbes absorb nutrients and break down pollutants** — hence "kidneys" |
| **Groundwater recharge** | Slow percolation |
| **Biodiversity & migratory birds** | **Critical stopovers on the CENTRAL ASIAN FLYWAY, for which India is a key country** |
| **Carbon storage** | **Peatlands hold enormous soil carbon; draining them releases it** |
| **Livelihoods** | Fisheries, fodder, reeds, tourism |

**Law & policy in India:** **Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017** — *prohibits conversion, encroachment, solid-waste dumping; requires State Wetland Authorities and a brief document for each wetland.* ⚠️ *Criticised for dropping the earlier Central Regulatory Authority and for leaving "wise use" to states.* **Also: the National Plan for Conservation of Aquatic Ecosystems (NPCA) and the Amrit Dharohar initiative.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]`

**Ramsar in India:** **India acceded in 1982; Chilika and Keoladeo were the first two sites (1981 designation).** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` **the current site count — India has been adding sites rapidly and now leads Asia.** ** MONTREUX RECORD = the list of Ramsar sites whose ecological character is threatened; Keoladeo and Loktak have featured on it.** *Full convention machinery in [E17](../Part_E_Governance/17_International_Conventions.md); the lakes themselves in [Leong A9](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/09_Lakes.md).*

## 5️⃣ ⭐ EUTROPHICATION — get the mechanism exactly right

```
   NUTRIENT ENRICHMENT — nitrates & PHOSPHATES from
     sewage, fertiliser runoff, detergents
              ↓
   ALGAL BLOOM — explosive growth of algae and
     cyanobacteria; a green surface mat
              ↓
   The mat BLOCKS LIGHT → submerged plants die
              ↓
   THE BLOOM ITSELF DIES (algae are short-lived)
              ↓
   AEROBIC BACTERIA DECOMPOSE the dead mass —
      and CONSUME DISSOLVED OXYGEN doing it
              ↓
   HYPOXIA / ANOXIA — oxygen crashes
              ↓
   FISH KILL. Anaerobic bacteria take over,
      producing H₂S and methane — the smell
              ↓
   A "DEAD ZONE"
```

> ⭐ **The step candidates get wrong: it is not the algal growth that kills the lake — it is the DECOMPOSITION of that growth, which strips the oxygen out of the water.**
>
> **Say "decomposition of the bloom depletes dissolved oxygen" and you have demonstrated you understand it. Say "algae cover the water and fish die" and you haven't.**

**Related terms:** **OLIGOTROPHIC** (nutrient-poor, clear, oxygen-rich — a healthy deep lake) → **MESOTROPHIC** → **EUTROPHIC** (nutrient-rich) → **HYPEREUTROPHIC.** **CULTURAL eutrophication = the human-accelerated version** *(natural eutrophication takes millennia).*
**ALGAL BLOOMS:** **HARMFUL ALGAL BLOOMS (HABs) · "red tide" · cyanobacterial toxins (microcystin) that poison fish, livestock and people.**
**BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand)** = oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter — **high BOD means heavy organic pollution.** *Detail in [C10](../Part_C_Pollution/10_Water_and_Soil_Pollution.md).*

**Indian examples:** **Bengaluru's Bellandur and Varthur lakes — foam and fire · Hussain Sagar · Dal Lake · Chilika's weed problem · Kolleru.**
**Global:** **the Gulf of Mexico dead zone (Mississippi-basin fertiliser)** · the Baltic · **the Arabian Sea's expanding oxygen minimum zone.**

- **Photic (top ~200 m, photosynthesis) vs aphotic · benthic · pelagic · neritic**
- **Plankton · nekton · benthos · neuston. Thermocline/halocline/pycnocline block mixing**
