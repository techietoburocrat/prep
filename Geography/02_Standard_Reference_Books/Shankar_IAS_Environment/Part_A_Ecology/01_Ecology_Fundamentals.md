## 1️⃣ THE LEVELS OF ORGANISATION

```
   THE ECOLOGICAL HIERARCHY — each level is the unit of study
      for a different kind of question

   INDIVIDUAL / ORGANISM  ── how does one organism survive here?
              ↓                  (adaptation, tolerance)
   POPULATION             ── all individuals of ONE SPECIES in an area
              ↓                  (growth, density, natality, mortality)
   COMMUNITY (biotic)     ── all populations of ALL species together
              ↓                  (interactions, succession)
   ECOSYSTEM             ── COMMUNITY + its PHYSICAL ENVIRONMENT
              ↓                  (energy flow, nutrient cycling)
   LANDSCAPE              ── a mosaic of ecosystems
              ↓
   BIOME                 ── a major regional ecosystem type,
              ↓                  defined by CLIMATE + vegetation
   BIOSPHERE              ── the global sum of all ecosystems;
                                the zone of life on Earth
```

> **The definition to be exact about: an ECOSYSTEM is a BIOTIC COMMUNITY plus its ABIOTIC ENVIRONMENT, interacting as a functional unit.** **The phrase "functional unit" is doing real work — what makes it an ecosystem is that energy flows through it and nutrients cycle within it.**

**The BIOSPHERE is thin.** **Life is confined to roughly 10 km up and a few km down — a film on the surface of a planet 12,742 km across.** *Proportionally thinner than the skin on an apple. Worth stating in an essay.*

**Ecosystem components:**
- **ABIOTIC** — sunlight, temperature, water, soil, air, minerals, pH.
- **BIOTIC** — **PRODUCERS (autotrophs) · CONSUMERS (heterotrophs) · DECOMPOSERS (saprotrophs)**.

**By origin:** **NATURAL** (forest, grassland, desert, pond, ocean) vs **ARTIFICIAL / man-made** (cropland, aquarium, reservoir, city). *Artificial ecosystems are typically simple, unstable and dependent on continuous human energy subsidy — which is exactly why monoculture farming needs fertiliser and pesticide.*

## 2️⃣ HABITAT vs NICHE — a favourite Prelims distinction

| | **HABITAT** | **NICHE** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | **The physical PLACE where an organism lives** | **The organism's functional ROLE — what it eats, when it is active, what eats it, how it reproduces, its tolerance ranges** |
| **Analogy** | **Its "address"** | **Its "profession"** |
| **Sharing** | **Many species can share one habitat** | **NO TWO SPECIES CAN OCCUPY THE SAME NICHE INDEFINITELY** |

> ⭐ **GAUSE'S COMPETITIVE EXCLUSION PRINCIPLE: two species competing for exactly the same limiting resource cannot coexist indefinitely — the more efficient competitor eliminates the other.**
>
> **And the important corollary, which is what makes it interesting rather than merely bleak: species avoid this by NICHE DIFFERENTIATION / RESOURCE PARTITIONING — feeding at different heights, at different times of day, on different prey sizes.**
>
> **This is why a forest can hold dozens of bird species that all appear to "eat insects": they are actually eating different insects, in different parts of the tree, at different times.** 🎯 *A clean explanation of how biodiversity is possible at all.*

**FUNDAMENTAL niche** = the full range an organism *could* occupy. **REALISED niche** = the narrower range it *actually* occupies, once competitors are present.

## 3️⃣ ECOTONE & EDGE EFFECT

> **An ECOTONE is the TRANSITION ZONE between two adjacent ecosystems** — where a forest meets grassland, where a river meets the sea (**an estuary**), where land meets water (**a wetland**), where a mangrove meets the shore.

| Property of an ecotone | Detail |
|---|---|
| **It has species from BOTH communities** | Plus some of its own |
| **EDGE EFFECT** | **Species DENSITY and DIVERSITY are often HIGHER in the ecotone than in either community on its own** |
| **EDGE SPECIES** | **Species that occur most abundantly in the ecotone** |
| **It is a zone of tension** | Conditions change steeply across it |

> **Why the edge effect happens: an ecotone offers resources from both sides plus its own conditions, so it supports organisms from both plus specialists.** **Estuaries, mangroves and wetlands are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth for exactly this reason.**
>
> ⚠️ **But note the double edge — and this is the sophisticated version:** **habitat FRAGMENTATION creates enormous amounts of new edge.** **A road through a forest increases edge and can raise local species counts — while destroying the deep-forest interior conditions that specialist species need.** **More edge is not more conservation.** 🎯 *This distinction — between edge-tolerant generalists and interior specialists — is what separates a real answer on fragmentation from a superficial one.*

## 4️⃣ ADAPTATION & TOLERANCE

**An ADAPTATION is any attribute — morphological, physiological or behavioural — that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its habitat.**

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Morphological** | Cactus spines · kangaroo rat's water economy · blubber in seals · the conical shape of [taiga conifers](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/09_Siberian_Type_Taiga.md) |
| **Physiological** | **Camel tolerating 25% dehydration · desert kangaroo rat producing water metabolically and never drinking · high-altitude adaptation (more RBCs)** |
| **Behavioural** | **Aestivation** (summer dormancy — snails, fish) · **hibernation** (winter dormancy — bears, frogs) · **diapause** (suspended development in zooplankton) · migration · basking |

**Responses to stress:** **REGULATE** (maintain constant internal conditions — homeostasis; birds, mammals) · **CONFORM** (internal state changes with the environment; most animals and plants) · **MIGRATE** (leave temporarily) · **SUSPEND** (spores, seeds, dormancy).

> **SHELFORD'S LAW OF TOLERANCE: each species has a range of tolerance for every environmental factor — with an optimum in the middle and zones of stress at either end, beyond which it cannot survive.**
> **LIEBIG'S LAW OF THE MINIMUM: growth is limited by whichever essential resource is SCARCEST, not by the total supply of all resources.**
> 🎯 **Together these explain distribution: a species lives where EVERY factor is within tolerance and NO essential factor is limiting. One factor out of range is enough to exclude it.**

**STENO- vs EURY-:** **stenothermal/stenohaline = narrow tolerance** (temperature/salinity); **eurythermal/euryhaline = wide tolerance.** *Estuarine species are typically euryhaline — they must be, since salinity there changes twice a day.*

## 5️⃣ ⭐ POPULATION INTERACTIONS — a certain Prelims item

**Denote the effect on each of the two species as + (benefit), − (harm), or 0 (no effect).**

| Interaction | Sp. A | Sp. B | Description & example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MUTUALISM** | **+** | **+** | **Both benefit.** **Lichen (fungus + alga) · mycorrhiza (fungus + plant roots) · CORAL + ZOOXANTHELLAE · rhizobium + legume root nodules · fig & fig wasp · pollinators & flowers** |
| **COMMENSALISM** | **+** | **0** | **One benefits, the other is unaffected.** **Orchids/epiphytes on a tree · barnacles on a whale · cattle egret following grazing cattle · remora on a shark** |
| **PARASITISM** | **+** | **−** | **One benefits at the other's expense, usually WITHOUT killing it quickly.** **Ticks, lice, tapeworm, Cuscuta (dodder) on plants · brood parasitism — the cuckoo laying eggs in a crow's nest** |
| **PREDATION** | **+** | **−** | **One kills and eats the other.** *Also includes herbivory. Predators keep prey populations in check and are essential to ecosystem stability* |
| **COMPETITION** | **−** | **−** | **Both harmed, competing for a limiting resource.** **INTERspecific (between species) and INTRAspecific (within a species — often the fiercer)** |
| **AMENSALISM** | **−** | **0** | **One is harmed, the other unaffected.** **ALLELOPATHY — a plant releasing chemicals that suppress its neighbours (walnut, eucalyptus, Parthenium)** |
| **Protocooperation** | **+** | **+** | Both benefit but the association is **not obligatory** *(contrast mutualism)* |
| **Neutralism** | **0** | **0** | No effect either way |

> **The two easiest to confuse — get this straight now:**
> **MUTUALISM = + / + · COMMENSALISM = + / 0 · AMENSALISM = − / 0 · PARASITISM = + / −**
>
> **And the memory hook: "amensalism" contains "a-mens" — the harmed one gets nothing back and the harmer gains nothing either. It is pure collateral damage.**

**Defence adaptations worth naming:** **MIMICRY** (Batesian — a harmless species imitating a harmful one; Müllerian — several harmful species converging on one warning pattern) · **CAMOUFLAGE / crypsis** · **APOSEMATISM** (bright warning colouration) · **chemical defence** (toxins, thorns, tannins).

## 6️⃣ POPULATION ATTRIBUTES & GROWTH — *(NCERT Cl.12 Bio Ch.13 content)*

**Attributes a POPULATION has but an INDIVIDUAL cannot:** **birth rate (natality) · death rate (mortality) · sex ratio · age distribution / age pyramid · population density.**

### The two growth models

```
   EXPONENTIAL (J-shaped)              LOGISTIC (S-shaped / sigmoid)
      unlimited resources                    limited resources

   N │        ╱                          N │        ╭──────── K
     │       ╱                             │      ╱   (CARRYING CAPACITY)
     │     ╱                               │    ╱
     │   ╱                                 │  ╱
     │╱                                    │╱
     └──────────── time                    └──────────── time

   dN/dt = rN                          dN/dt = rN (K−N)/K
   Cannot continue — no environment    THE REALISTIC MODEL —
     supports unlimited growth              growth slows as N approaches K
```

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **r — intrinsic rate of natural increase** | The population's inherent growth capacity |
| **K — CARRYING CAPACITY** | **The maximum population the environment can sustain indefinitely** |
| **Environmental resistance** | The sum of factors (food, space, disease, predation) that hold a population below its exponential potential |

> ⭐ **CARRYING CAPACITY is the concept to carry out of this file, because it is the hinge of the whole book.**
>
> **Overgrazing in the [Sahel](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/03_Savanna_Sudan_Type.md), overfishing at the [Grand Banks](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/10_Laurentian_Type_Cool_Temperate_Eastern_Margin.md), groundwater depletion in Punjab, the [Aral Sea](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/09_Lakes.md) — every one is the same event: a system driven past its carrying capacity, followed by a crash rather than a plateau.**
>
> **And note WHY it crashes rather than levelling off: exceeding K doesn't just stop growth, it DEGRADES K itself.** **Overgrazed land supports fewer animals afterwards than before. The ceiling comes down.** 🎯 **That sentence is worth deploying in any sustainability answer.**

**r-selected vs K-selected species** — **r-strategists: many small offspring, little parental care, short life, boom-and-bust (insects, weeds, rodents). K-strategists: few large offspring, heavy parental care, long life, stable near K (elephant, tiger, whale, humans).**
⚠️ **Conservation implication: K-strategists recover far more slowly from population crashes — which is precisely why large mammals dominate the endangered lists and why "it will bounce back" is wrong for a tiger and roughly right for a locust.**

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- **Ecosystem = biotic community + abiotic environment, as a FUNCTIONAL unit**
- **HABITAT = address · NICHE = profession**
- **Gause's COMPETITIVE EXCLUSION principle; resource partitioning**
- **ECOTONE = transition zone · EDGE EFFECT = higher diversity there · edge species**

- **Batesian vs Müllerian mimicry; aposematism**

- **Shelford's law of TOLERANCE · Liebig's law of the MINIMUM**
- **Steno- = narrow tolerance · Eury- = wide (euryhaline estuarine species)**
- **Exponential = J-curve · Logistic = S-curve · K = carrying capacity**
- **r-selected (many offspring, boom-bust) vs K-selected (few, slow recovery)**

### `[M]` MAINS
- ⭐ **Carrying capacity — and the point that exceeding it DEGRADES it.** The unifying frame for overgrazing, overfishing, groundwater and the Aral Sea
- **The edge-effect paradox** — fragmentation increases edge and can raise species counts while destroying interior habitat
- **Niche differentiation** as the explanation for how high biodiversity is possible
- **K-strategists and slow recovery** — why megafauna conservation is inherently long-horizon
- **Mutualism as a point of fragility** — coral bleaching as a broken symbiosis
