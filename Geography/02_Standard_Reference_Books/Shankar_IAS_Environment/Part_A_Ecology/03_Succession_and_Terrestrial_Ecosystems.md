## 1️⃣ ECOLOGICAL SUCCESSION

**The orderly, predictable, progressive replacement of one community by another at a site, until a stable community is reached.**

```
   THE SEQUENCE

   NUDATION ......... a bare site is created
        ↓               (volcano, landslide, glacier retreat, fire, abandonment)
   INVASION ......... PIONEER SPECIES arrive — migration,
        ↓                 establishment, aggregation
   COMPETITION ...... species compete; the site's own conditions change
        ↓
   REACTION ........ THE KEY STAGE — the organisms MODIFY the
        ↓                 habitat, making it LESS suitable for
        ↓                 themselves and MORE suitable for the next lot
   STABILISATION .... CLIMAX COMMUNITY — stable, self-perpetuating,
                          in equilibrium with the climate
```

> ⭐ **The genuinely elegant idea, and the one worth understanding rather than memorising: each community DESTROYS ITS OWN HABITAT.**
>
> **Lichens break rock into soil — and lichens don't grow well on soil. Grasses build up more soil and shade — and shade suppresses grasses. Shrubs create conditions for tree seedlings — and trees then overtop the shrubs.**
>
> **Succession is not a race between species. It is a sequence in which each occupant makes way for the next by improving the site.** 🎯 *That sentence is worth more in an answer than the whole stage list.*

### Primary vs secondary succession

| | **PRIMARY** | **SECONDARY** |
|---|---|---|
| **Starting point** | **A site with NO SOIL and no prior life** — bare rock, a new volcanic island, land freshly exposed by a retreating glacier, a new sand dune | **A site where the community was destroyed but THE SOIL REMAINS** — after fire, flood, felling, or abandoned farmland |
| **Pioneers** | **LICHENS and mosses** — *they can colonise bare rock and begin making soil* | **Grasses and weeds** — seeds and roots often already present in the soil |
| **Speed** | **VERY SLOW — centuries to millennia** (soil formation is the bottleneck) | **MUCH FASTER — decades** |

> **The reason for the speed difference is the whole distinction: soil takes centuries to form and only moments to keep.** **Secondary succession starts with the hard part already done.**
>
> 🎯 **Restoration implication worth stating: this is why preventing soil loss matters so much more than replanting.** **A cleared forest with its soil intact can recover in decades; a cleared forest whose soil has eroded away is starting from primary succession.** *Connect to [equatorial laterite](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/01_Equatorial_Hot_Wet.md) and the [Dust Bowl](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/06_Steppe_Temperate_Grassland.md).*

### Types by moisture, and the terminology

| Type | Starting condition | Pioneer |
|---|---|---|
| **XERARCH / xerosere** | **Dry — bare rock, sand, desert** | **Lichens** |
| **HYDRARCH / hydrosere** | **Wet — pond, lake** | **Phytoplankton** |
| **Mesarch** | Adequate moisture | Herbs |

> **Both xerarch and hydrarch converge on the SAME MESIC (moderate-moisture) climax.** **A pond fills in and dries toward mesic; a bare rock accumulates soil and moisture toward mesic.** *They approach the same destination from opposite directions — a nice detail.*

**SERE** = the whole sequence. **SERAL stages** = the intermediate communities. **CLIMAX** = the stable end-state.

### Climax theories & the sub-climax point

| Theory | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Monoclimax** | One climate → **one** climax community for the whole region |
| **Polyclimax** | Several stable climaxes are possible, set by soil, drainage, fire etc. |
| **Climax pattern** | A continuum of climaxes, varying with local conditions |

> **DISCLIMAX / PLAGIOCLIMAX — a community held short of its true climax by a persistent disturbance.**
>
> **This is where succession meets your Leong notes: the [SAVANNA](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/03_Savanna_Sudan_Type.md) and the [TEMPERATE GRASSLANDS](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/06_Steppe_Temperate_Grassland.md) are argued to be FIRE-and-GRAZING climaxes — kept treeless by burning and grazing rather than purely by climate.**
>
> **India's own version: much of the Shola–grassland mosaic and many Himalayan pastures are maintained by grazing.** ⚠️ **And this creates a real conservation dilemma: if a grassland is a grazing climax, then REMOVING grazing to "protect" it converts it to scrub and destroys the very grassland species you meant to save.** 🎯 *Very few candidates can make this argument. It is worth having.*

## 2️⃣ INDIA'S FOREST TYPES — Champion & Seth

**The standard classification (1968) recognises 16 major forest type groups under 5 climatic categories.** **Know the main ones, their rainfall and their species.**

| Forest type | Rainfall | Where | Species |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tropical WET EVERGREEN** | **> 250 cm** | **Western Ghats, NE India, Andamans** | Rosewood, mahogany, ebony, dipterocarps. *Multi-layered, no leafless period* |
| **Tropical SEMI-EVERGREEN** | 200–250 cm | Same regions, drier margins | Laurel, rosewood, mesua |
| **Tropical MOIST DECIDUOUS** | **100–200 cm** | **The MOST WIDESPREAD Indian forest** — the eastern slopes of the Ghats, MP, Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh | **TEAK, SAL, sandalwood, shisham, bamboo** |
| **Tropical DRY DECIDUOUS** | **70–100 cm** | Central India, the Deccan | Teak, sal, axlewood, tendu |
| **Tropical THORN** | **< 70 cm** | **Rajasthan, Gujarat, parts of the Deccan** | **Acacia/babul, khejri, kikar, euphorbia, cacti** |
| **Tropical DRY EVERGREEN** | — | **The Tamil Nadu coast** | Hardy evergreen, small-leaved |
| **MONTANE SUBTROPICAL & TEMPERATE** | — | **Himalaya, 1,000–3,500 m** | **Chir pine → oak, deodar, blue pine, spruce, fir, rhododendron** |
| **SUB-ALPINE & ALPINE** | — | **Above ~3,500 m** | **Stunted birch, juniper, rhododendron scrub → alpine meadows (*bugyals*)** |
| **LITTORAL & SWAMP (MANGROVE)** | — | **Deltas and tidal coasts** | **Sundari, Avicennia, Rhizophora** *(see [A4](04_Aquatic_and_Wetland_Ecosystems.md))* |

> **The organising rule to remember instead of the table: Indian forest type is set primarily by RAINFALL in the plains and by ALTITUDE in the mountains.** **Evergreen → moist deciduous → dry deciduous → thorn is simply a rainfall gradient from >250 cm down to <70 cm.** *Cross-link: [Leong Climate 2](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md).*

⚠️ **FOREST COVER FIGURES** ⚠️`[VERIFY]`
**The INDIA STATE OF FOREST REPORT (ISFR), published biennially by the FOREST SURVEY OF INDIA (FSI), Dehradun**, is the authoritative source. **Broad orientation: forest cover is around a fifth of India's geographical area and forest-plus-tree cover around a quarter; the National Forest Policy target is 33%.** ⚠️ **Do NOT quote a precise percentage from memory — refresh it from the latest ISFR.**

⚠️ **And a definitional trap that Prelims genuinely exploits: FSI defines "forest cover" as ALL land over 1 hectare with >10% tree canopy, REGARDLESS of ownership, legal status or species.** **So a commercial rubber, coffee or eucalyptus plantation counts as "forest cover" while a degraded natural forest may not.** 🎯 **Which is why forest cover can rise while natural forest quality falls — a first-rate Mains point about what an indicator actually measures.**

**FSI density classes:** **Very Dense Forest (canopy >70%) · Moderately Dense (40–70%) · Open Forest (10–40%) · Scrub (<10%).**

## 3️⃣ INDIA'S GRASSLANDS — and why they're administratively invisible

| Grassland type | Where |
|---|---|
| **Semi-arid / Sehima–Dichanthium** | Peninsular India, Gujarat, Rajasthan |
| **Dry subhumid / Dichanthium–Cenchrus–Lasiurus** | The northern plains |
| **TERAI–DUAR savanna & grassland** | **The Himalayan foothill belt — "elephant grass"; Kaziranga, Dudhwa, Manas** |
| **Shola grasslands** | **The Western Ghats' high montane mosaic of grassland with stunted evergreen "shola" forest in the folds** |
| **BANNI grassland** | **Kachchh, Gujarat — one of Asia's largest tropical grasslands** |
| **Alpine meadows (*bugyals*)** | Above the Himalayan treeline |

> ⚠️ ⭐ **THE POINT WORTH MAKING, and it is unusually sharp:**
>
> **Indian revenue records still classify most grasslands as "WASTELAND."** *A colonial-era category, based on the fact that they yield no timber and no revenue.*
>
> **The consequence is not semantic. "Wasteland" is administratively easy to divert — to plantations, solar parks, industry and compensatory afforestation. So India's grasslands are being converted precisely because they were misnamed.**
>
> **And they are not empty: the GREAT INDIAN BUSTARD, lesser florican, blackbuck, Indian wolf, caracal and Indian fox are grassland species, and several are critically endangered.** ⚠️ **Note the irony that "afforesting" a grassland DESTROYS it — planting trees on a grassland is habitat conversion, not restoration.**
>
> 🎯 **Temperate and tropical grasslands are globally the most-converted and least-protected biome.** *Cross-link to [Leong Climate 6](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/06_Steppe_Temperate_Grassland.md).*

## 4️⃣ INDIA'S DESERT & ARID ECOSYSTEMS

| | **THAR — hot desert** | **LADAKH / Spiti — cold desert** |
|---|---|---|
| **Where** | Rajasthan, Gujarat | Ladakh, Lahaul-Spiti, parts of Kinnaur |
| **Cause** | Subtropical high + the [Aravalli-parallel effect](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md) | **Rain shadow of the Greater Himalaya** |
| **Flora** | **Khejri (*Prosopis cineraria*, Rajasthan's state tree), acacia, cacti, ephemerals** | Sparse alpine scrub, seabuckthorn |
| **Fauna** | **Great Indian Bustard, chinkara, blackbuck, desert fox, Indian wild ass (Little Rann)** | **Snow leopard, Tibetan wild ass (kiang), blue sheep, Changthangi pashmina goat, black-necked crane** |
| **Protected areas** | **Desert National Park (Jaisalmer), Wild Ass Sanctuary (Little Rann)** | **Hemis NP, Changthang WLS, Pin Valley NP** |
| 🌱 **Pressures** | Desertification, solar-park expansion vs GIB, canal-induced waterlogging | Glacier retreat, tourism, grazing pressure |

## 5️⃣ 🌱 ENVIRONMENT LINKS

| 🌱 Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Afforestation vs restoration** | **Planting trees is not always restoration.** **Monoculture plantations on grassland or on natural forest destroy habitat while improving the "forest cover" statistic.** **Ecological restoration means restoring the ORIGINAL ecosystem type, which for a grassland means grass** |
| **CAMPA** | **Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning Authority — funds collected when forest land is diverted.** ⚠️ *Criticised for planting on common lands and grasslands, and for treating a mature forest as replaceable by saplings elsewhere* |
| **Bonn Challenge & land restoration** | **India pledged to restore 26 million hectares of degraded land by 2030** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` |
| **Forest fires** | Increasingly frequent — Uttarakhand's chir pine forests (resinous, fire-prone), the Western Ghats, Similipal. FSI fire alert system |
| **Invasive species in grasslands** | ***Prosopis juliflora* (vilayati kikar), *Lantana camara*, *Parthenium*** — *all three are transforming Indian grasslands and dry forests* |

## 📝 `[P]` / `[M]` / 🎯

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- **Succession stages: nudation → invasion → competition → REACTION → stabilisation (CLIMAX)**
- **PRIMARY = no soil, pioneers are LICHENS, very slow. SECONDARY = soil remains, faster**
- **XERARCH (dry, lichen pioneer) vs HYDRARCH (wet, phytoplankton pioneer); both converge on a MESIC climax**
- **Sere · seral stages · climax · DISCLIMAX/plagioclimax (fire- or grazing-maintained)**
- **Champion & Seth: 16 forest type groups. Moist deciduous (teak, sal) is the most widespread; evergreen >250 cm; thorn <70 cm**
- **FSI density classes: Very Dense >70% · Moderately Dense 40–70% · Open 10–40% · Scrub <10%**
- ⚠️ **"Forest cover" = >1 ha with >10% canopy, ANY ownership or species — so plantations count**
- **ISFR by FSI Dehradun, biennial; National Forest Policy target 33%**
- **Grasslands: Terai–Duar · Shola · Banni (Kachchh) · bugyals**
- **Thar fauna: GIB, chinkara, Indian wild ass. Cold desert: snow leopard, kiang, black-necked crane**
- **Invasives: *Prosopis juliflora*, *Lantana camara*, *Parthenium***

### `[M]` MAINS
- ⭐ **Grasslands classified as "wasteland"** — a colonial category driving present-day habitat loss; the GIB case
- ⭐ **"Forest cover" as a misleading indicator** — plantations counted, natural forest quality invisible
- **Afforestation ≠ restoration** — and why planting trees on grassland is destruction
- **Each successional community destroys its own habitat** — the mechanism, not the list
- **Soil loss converts secondary succession into primary** — why erosion control outranks replanting
- **The grazing-climax dilemma** — protecting a grassland by removing grazing can destroy it
