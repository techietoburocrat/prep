# 📋 E16 — Environmental Impact Assessment

> **The one-line point:** ⭐⭐ **EIA is the one point in the development process where a project must publicly justify its environmental cost before it is built. Everything contentious about it comes down to whether that moment is real or procedural.**

⭐ **Explicitly named in the ⭐ GS-III syllabus: *"Conservation, environmental pollution and degradation, environmental impact assessment."***

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ WHAT IT IS

⭐ **⭐ EIA is a process for predicting the environmental consequences of a proposed project BEFORE a decision is taken, and identifying measures to avoid, minimise or compensate for them.**

| ⭐ Item | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Legal basis in India** | ⭐⭐ **The ⭐ EIA NOTIFICATION, 2006, issued under the ⭐ ENVIRONMENT (PROTECTION) ACT, 1986** ⭐ *(the first EIA notification was 1994)* |
| ⭐ **Also covers** | ⭐ **Environmental Management Plan (EMP), Social Impact Assessment, risk assessment, cumulative impact** |
| ⭐ **Who decides** | ⭐⭐ **Category A → the ⭐ CENTRE (MoEFCC), advised by an ⭐ EAC (Expert Appraisal Committee). ⭐ Category B → the ⭐ STATE (SEIAA), advised by an ⭐ SEAC** |

### ⭐⭐ Categorisation

| | ⭐⭐ **CATEGORY A** | ⭐⭐ **CATEGORY B** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Cleared by** | ⭐ **Central government (MoEFCC)** | ⭐ **State (SEIAA)** |
| ⭐ **EIA required?** | ⭐⭐ **ALWAYS — mandatory** | ⭐ **Depends on SCREENING** |
| ⭐ **Sub-types** | — | ⭐⭐ **B1 — EIA required · ⭐ B2 — EIA NOT required** |
| ⭐ **Basis** | Project type, size and location | Same, with thresholds |

⚠️ ⭐ **Location matters independently of size: ⭐ a project near a protected area, an eco-sensitive zone, an inter-state or international boundary can be escalated to Category A regardless of its scale** *(the "General Condition").*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ THE FOUR STAGES — learn the order

```
   ⭐⭐ THE EIA PROCESS

   ⭐ 1. SCREENING
        → Does this project need an EIA at all?
        → (Category B only; Category A always does)
              ↓
   ⭐⭐ 2. SCOPING
        → What impacts must be studied?
        → ⭐ The EAC/SEAC issues the ⭐ TERMS OF REFERENCE (ToR)
        → ⭐ This stage decides what the study will and won't look at
              ↓
   ⭐⭐⭐ 3. PUBLIC CONSULTATION
        → ⭐ A PUBLIC HEARING at the site + ⭐ written responses
        → ⭐ Conducted by the State Pollution Control Board
        → ⭐⭐ THE ONLY STAGE WHERE AFFECTED PEOPLE HAVE A VOICE
              ↓
   ⭐⭐ 4. APPRAISAL
        → ⭐ EAC/SEAC scrutinises the final EIA + EMP + hearing record
        → ⭐ Recommends grant or rejection, with conditions
              ↓
   ⭐ DECISION → ⭐ ENVIRONMENTAL CLEARANCE (EC), with conditions
              ↓
   ⭐ POST-CLEARANCE MONITORING
        → ⭐ Half-yearly compliance reports
        ⚠️ ⭐⭐ THE WEAKEST LINK IN THE ENTIRE SYSTEM
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Two things about this process that most answers miss:**
>
> ⭐ **1. ⭐ SCOPING is where the outcome is often quietly decided.** ⭐ **If the Terms of Reference don't require cumulative impact assessment, or exclude a downstream district, the EIA will be silent on it — and silence reads as "no impact."** ⭐ *What a study is told to examine determines what it can find.*
>
> ⭐ **2. ⭐⭐ POST-CLEARANCE MONITORING is where the system actually fails.** ⭐ **Clearance conditions are extensive; verification is minimal. ⭐ An EIA regime with rigorous appraisal and no monitoring is a permitting system wearing an assessment costume.** 🎯 **This is the sharpest single criticism you can make, and it is well-founded.**

⭐ **Certain categories are ⭐ EXEMPT or on a shorter route:** ⭐ **some B2 projects, and various small-scale activities.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *the current exemption list — it has been amended repeatedly.*

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ THE 2020 DRAFT EIA NOTIFICATION — the controversy

⚠️`[VERIFY]` *its final status — the draft attracted an extraordinary volume of public objection and litigation.*

| ⭐ Proposed change | ⭐ Why it was contested |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **POST-FACTO CLEARANCE** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Allowing projects that had ALREADY BEGUN WITHOUT clearance to regularise afterwards.** ⭐⭐ **This is the core objection: ⭐ EIA's entire logic is that assessment precedes decision. ⭐ A clearance granted after construction cannot influence anything — it can only forgive.** ⚠️ *The Supreme Court has held post-facto clearance to be contrary to environmental jurisprudence* 🎯 **The strongest argument available on this topic** |
| ⭐⭐ **Reduced public-consultation notice period** | ⭐ **Cut from 30 days to 20 for responses — ⭐ a real burden where documents are technical, in English, and affected communities are rural and often not literate in it** |
| ⭐⭐ **Expanded exemption list** | More project categories excluded from prior clearance entirely |
| ⭐⭐ **Reduced compliance-report frequency** | ⭐ **From half-yearly to annual — ⭐ weakening the stage that was already the weakest** |
| ⭐ **Restricting who may report violations** | Complaints channelled through the government rather than allowing public reporting |

> ⭐⭐ **How to write about this in Mains: ⭐ do NOT simply denounce it.** ⭐ **State the genuine problem it was responding to — India's clearance process is slow, opaque and a real constraint on infrastructure — and then explain why these particular remedies attacked the safeguards rather than the delays.** ⭐⭐ **The defensible position: ⭐ streamline the PROCEDURE (digital tracking, time-bound appraisal, better technical capacity in SEIAAs), don't dilute the STANDARD.** 🎯 *A balanced answer scores far higher than an indignant one.*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ CRITICISMS OF INDIA'S EIA REGIME — the honest list

| ⭐ Criticism | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The consultant conflict of interest** | ⭐⭐ **The EIA report is commissioned and PAID FOR by the PROJECT PROPONENT. ⭐ The consultant's future work depends on producing clearable reports.** ⭐⭐ **This is a structural conflict, not a matter of individual honesty** — ⭐ *documented cases of cut-and-paste reports, fabricated baseline data, and EIAs describing the wrong ecosystem* 🎯 **The single best criticism to lead with** |
| ⭐⭐ **Weak public consultation** | Technical documents, short notice, English-language, hearings held far from affected villages, and no obligation to accept objections |
| ⭐⭐ **No cumulative impact assessment** | ⭐⭐ **Each dam on a river is assessed alone. ⭐ Twenty dams assessed individually as "minor impact" produce a destroyed river.** ⭐ *This is a real and specific defect — the Ganga basin hydropower studies are the standard illustration* |
| ⭐⭐ **Project splitting** | Dividing a large project into sub-threshold pieces to avoid Category A |
| ⭐⭐ **Post-clearance monitoring** | As above — the enforcement gap |
| ⭐ **Capacity** | SEIAAs and SEACs are under-resourced relative to caseload |
| ⭐ **Delay** | ⭐ *And this is the genuine counter-argument: clearance timelines are a real cost to infrastructure and industry, and pretending otherwise is not credible* |

⭐ **Reform directions worth proposing:** ⭐ **an independent, publicly funded pool of accredited consultants paid from a central fund rather than by the proponent (breaking the conflict) · ⭐ mandatory CUMULATIVE and STRATEGIC (basin/regional) impact assessment · ⭐ genuine post-clearance audit with penalties · ⭐ vernacular, simplified disclosure and longer notice · ⭐ time-bound but standard-preserving digital appraisal · ⭐ strengthening NGT's enforcement reach.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐ RELATED CLEARANCE REGIMES

| ⭐ Clearance | ⭐ Basis |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **FOREST clearance** | ⭐ **Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 — ⭐ a SEPARATE approval from environmental clearance.** ⭐ *A project may need both* |
| ⭐⭐ **WILDLIFE clearance** | ⭐ **From the ⭐ National Board for Wildlife (NBWL) / Standing Committee, for projects in or near protected areas and ESZs** |
| ⭐⭐ **CRZ clearance** | ⭐ **Coastal Regulation Zone Notification (1991, 2011, 2019) under the EPA.** ⭐ **CRZ-I (most sensitive — mangroves, turtle nesting, intertidal) to CRZ-IV (water area)** |
| ⭐ **Consent to Establish / Operate** | From the SPCB, under the Water Act 1974 and Air Act 1981 |
| ⭐ **FRA compliance** | ⭐ **Gram Sabha consent and settlement of forest rights, per FRA 2006** |
| ⭐ **PARIVESH** | ⭐ **The single-window online portal for environment, forest, wildlife and CRZ clearances** |

## 📝 `[P]` / `[M]` / 🎯

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- ⭐⭐ **EIA Notification ⭐ 2006 (first in 1994), issued under the ⭐ EPA 1986**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Four stages: ⭐ SCREENING → SCOPING (ToR) → ⭐ PUBLIC CONSULTATION → APPRAISAL**
- ⭐⭐ **Category A → Centre (MoEFCC + EAC), EIA always required. ⭐ Category B → State (SEIAA + SEAC); ⭐ B1 needs EIA, B2 doesn't**
- ⭐ **Public hearing conducted by the ⭐ State Pollution Control Board**
- ⭐⭐ **⭐ POST-FACTO clearance was the core objection to the 2020 draft**
- ⭐⭐ **Separate clearances: environmental (EPA) · forest (FCA 1980) · wildlife (NBWL) · CRZ · SPCB consents**
- ⭐ **CRZ-I is the most protected; ⭐ PARIVESH is the single-window portal**
- ⭐ **The EIA report is prepared by a consultant paid by the PROPONENT**

### `[M]` MAINS
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The consultant conflict of interest** — structural, not individual; and the funding-pool remedy
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Post-facto clearance inverts EIA's logic** — assessment must precede decision or it forgives rather than informs
- ⭐⭐⭐ **No cumulative impact assessment** — twenty individually minor dams destroy a river
- ⭐⭐ **Scoping determines findings** — what the ToR omits, the EIA cannot find
- ⭐⭐ **Post-clearance monitoring is the enforcement gap** — appraisal without audit is permitting
- ⭐⭐ **The balanced reform position** — streamline procedure, don't dilute standard
