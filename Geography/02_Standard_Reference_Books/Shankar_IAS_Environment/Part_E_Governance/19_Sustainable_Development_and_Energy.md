## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT

⭐ **Defined in the ⭐ BRUNDTLAND REPORT, *Our Common Future*, ⭐ 1987** *(World Commission on Environment and Development, chaired by Gro Harlem Brundtland of Norway).*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The definition, verbatim — worth quoting exactly:**
>
> ⭐ ***"Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."***

⭐ **⭐ THE THREE PILLARS: ⭐ ECONOMIC · ⭐ SOCIAL · ⭐ ENVIRONMENTAL** *(sometimes "profit, people, planet").*

> ⭐⭐ **Two competing models worth knowing, because the distinction is genuinely important:**
>
> | ⭐ **WEAK sustainability** | ⭐ **STRONG sustainability** |
> |---|---|
> | ⭐ **Natural capital and man-made capital are SUBSTITUTABLE** — a lost forest is acceptable if the wealth created exceeds its value | ⭐ **Some natural capital is ⭐ CRITICAL and CANNOT be substituted** — a climate system, an aquifer, an extinct species |
> | The basis of standard cost-benefit analysis | The basis of the ⭐ precautionary principle |
>
> ⭐ **The strong-sustainability position rests on ⭐ IRREVERSIBILITY: ⭐ you can rebuild a factory; you cannot rebuild a species or refill a fossil aquifer.** 🎯 **Introducing this distinction lifts a sustainability answer above the standard three-pillars recitation.**

⭐ **Related concepts:** ⭐ **ECOLOGICAL FOOTPRINT** (the land and sea area needed to support consumption) · ⭐ **BIOCAPACITY** and ⭐ **EARTH OVERSHOOT DAY** (the date each year when humanity's demand exceeds what Earth can regenerate) · ⭐ **CARRYING CAPACITY** *(see [A1](../Part_A_Ecology/01_Ecology_Fundamentals.md))* · ⭐ **PLANETARY BOUNDARIES** (the nine Earth-system limits framework — climate change, biodiversity loss, nitrogen/phosphorus flows, land-system change, freshwater, ocean acidification, ozone, aerosols, novel entities) · ⭐ **DOUGHNUT ECONOMICS** (a social floor inside an ecological ceiling).

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ THE SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT GOALS

⭐ **⭐ 17 GOALS and ⭐ 169 TARGETS, adopted ⭐ 2015, running to ⭐ 2030 — the ⭐ 2030 AGENDA.** ⭐ **Successors to the ⭐ MDGs (2000–2015, 8 goals).**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No Poverty | | ⭐ **10** | Reduced Inequalities |
| 2 | Zero Hunger | | ⭐ **11** | ⭐ **Sustainable Cities and Communities** |
| 3 | Good Health and Well-being | | ⭐ **12** | ⭐ **Responsible Consumption and Production** |
| 4 | Quality Education | | ⭐⭐ **13** | ⭐⭐ **CLIMATE ACTION** |

> ⭐⭐ **The four explicitly environmental goals: ⭐ 6 (water) · ⭐ 7 (energy) · ⭐ 13 (climate) · ⭐ 14 (oceans) · ⭐ 15 (land).** 🎯 *Knowing that 13/14/15 are climate/water/land in that order is a reliable Prelims mark.*

⭐ **How the MDGs and SDGs differ:** ⭐ **MDGs applied to DEVELOPING countries; ⭐ SDGs are UNIVERSAL. ⭐ MDGs were drafted by experts; ⭐ SDGs came from an intergovernmental negotiation. ⭐ SDGs integrate the environmental dimension throughout rather than isolating it in one goal.**

⭐ **India:** ⭐ **NITI Aayog is the nodal body; ⭐ it publishes the ⭐ SDG India Index, ranking states and UTs.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *India's current index score and rankings.*

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ THE CIRCULAR ECONOMY

| | ⭐ **LINEAR** | ⭐⭐ **CIRCULAR** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Model** | ⭐ **Take → Make → Use → DISPOSE** | ⭐ **Design out waste · keep materials in use · regenerate natural systems** |
| ⭐ **Waste** | An externality, someone else's problem | ⭐ **An input to another process** |
| ⭐ **Value** | Extracted once | ⭐ **Retained through multiple cycles** |

⭐ **Instruments: ⭐ EPR** *(see [C11](../Part_C_Pollution/11_Waste_Plastic_and_EWaste.md))* · ⭐ **product design for repair and disassembly · the ⭐ right to repair · industrial symbiosis (one plant's waste is another's feedstock) · ⭐ urban mining · remanufacturing · product-as-a-service.**

⭐ **India:** ⭐ **the various EPR-based Rules; ⭐ Mission LiFE; ⭐ the Green Credit Programme; ⭐ Vehicle Scrappage Policy; ⭐ GOBARdhan (biogas from cattle waste).** ⚠️`[VERIFY]`

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ ENERGY & THE TRANSITION

### ⭐ Renewables

| ⭐ Source | ⭐ Points |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **SOLAR** | ⭐ **India's largest renewable resource. ⭐ Bhadla (Rajasthan), Pavagada (Karnataka), Kurnool (AP) among the largest parks. ⭐ PM-KUSUM for farmers; PM Surya Ghar for rooftop** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` ⚠️ *Land use, and the [GIB conflict](../Part_B_Biodiversity/06_India_Biogeographic_Zones_and_Species.md)* |
| ⭐⭐ **WIND** | ⭐ **Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra; ⭐ Muppandal (TN) is a major cluster. ⭐ Offshore wind policy exists but deployment is nascent** |
| ⭐⭐ **HYDRO** | ⭐ **Large hydro is renewable but not uncontroversial — ⭐ displacement, submergence, sedimentation, methane from reservoirs, and [GLOF exposure](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/06_Glacial_Landforms.md). ⭐ Small hydro is separately categorised** |
| ⭐ **BIOMASS & biogas** | ⭐ **Bagasse cogeneration; ⭐ GOBARdhan; ⭐ addresses stubble burning if the supply chain works** |
| ⭐⭐ **NUCLEAR** | ⭐ **Low-carbon but contested — waste, safety, cost, siting. ⭐ India's three-stage programme rests on its ⭐ THORIUM reserves (Kerala/Odisha monazite sands) — among the world's largest** |
| ⭐ **GEOTHERMAL & tidal** | ⭐ **Puga (Ladakh), Tattapani (Chhattisgarh); ⭐ Gulf of Kachchh & Khambhat for tidal — both long-identified, barely developed** |
| ⭐⭐ **GREEN HYDROGEN** | ⭐⭐ **Made by electrolysis using renewable electricity. ⭐ The ⭐ NATIONAL GREEN HYDROGEN MISSION (2023). ⭐ Its real value is in ⭐ HARD-TO-ABATE sectors — steel, fertiliser, refining, shipping, long-haul transport — where direct electrification does not work** 🎯 *Saying* why *green hydrogen matters (hard-to-abate, not everything) is the mark* |

⭐ **Colour codes:** ⭐ **GREEN hydrogen = renewable electrolysis · ⭐ GREY = from natural gas, CO₂ vented · ⭐ BLUE = from natural gas with CCS · ⭐ BROWN/BLACK = from coal.**

### ⭐⭐ Biofuels

⭐ **⭐ FIRST generation — from food crops (sugarcane ethanol, corn).** ⚠️ ⭐ **Raises the ⭐ FOOD-vs-FUEL problem and can drive land-use change.**
⭐ **⭐ SECOND generation — from ⭐ non-food LIGNOCELLULOSIC biomass: ⭐ crop residues, bagasse, wood waste.** ⭐ *Better on food security; harder technically.*
⭐ **⭐ THIRD generation — from ⭐ ALGAE.** ⭐ *High yield per area, no farmland needed; still costly.*
⭐ **⭐ FOURTH generation — engineered organisms / carbon-negative routes.**

⭐ **India: ⭐ the National Policy on Biofuels (2018, amended 2022); ⭐ the ETHANOL BLENDING programme (E20 target); ⭐ the ⭐ GLOBAL BIOFUELS ALLIANCE launched at India's ⭐ G20 presidency in 2023.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *blending achievement and target dates.*

### ⭐ The transition's real difficulties

| ⭐ Issue | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **INTERMITTENCY** | ⭐ **Solar and wind are variable; ⭐ storage, grid flexibility and demand management are the binding constraint, not generation cost** |
| ⭐⭐ **CRITICAL MINERALS** | ⭐⭐ **Lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earths — ⭐ geographically concentrated, with real mining and geopolitical costs.** ⭐ *A clean-energy system is a mineral-intensive system* |
| ⭐⭐ **JUST TRANSITION** | ⭐⭐ **Coal supports large workforces and entire district economies — ⭐ Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and state revenues from coal royalties.** ⭐ **A transition without a plan for those workers and districts will be resisted, and rightly** 🎯 *Naming "just transition" is the mark* |
| ⭐ **Land** | Solar and wind are land-hungry; agrivoltaics and rooftop reduce the conflict |
| ⭐ **Grid & finance** | Transmission build-out; DISCOM financial health; cost of capital |

## 5️⃣ ⭐ ENVIRONMENTAL ECONOMICS — the concepts worth naming

| ⭐ Concept | ⭐ Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **EXTERNALITY** | ⭐ **A cost (or benefit) borne by someone outside the transaction.** ⭐ **Pollution is the standard negative externality** |
| ⭐⭐ **Internalising the externality** | ⭐ **Making the polluter bear the cost — via tax, cap-and-trade, EPR, or liability** |
| ⭐⭐ **POLLUTER PAYS principle** | ⭐ **Part of Indian law via Supreme Court rulings** |
| ⭐⭐ **PRECAUTIONARY principle** | ⭐ **Absence of full scientific certainty is not a reason to postpone action against serious or irreversible harm** — ⭐ *Rio Declaration Principle 15* |
| ⭐⭐ **TRAGEDY OF THE COMMONS** | ⭐ **Hardin, 1968 — individually rational use of a shared resource produces collective ruin.** ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The important corrective: ⭐ ELINOR OSTROM showed empirically that communities DO successfully self-govern commons under identifiable conditions (clear boundaries, local rule-making, monitoring, graduated sanctions).** ⭐ **She won the Nobel for it.** 🎯 ⭐ **Citing Ostrom against Hardin is one of the strongest moves available in a commons answer — and it directly supports community forest rights and JFM** |
| ⭐ **Natural capital accounting / Green GDP** | Valuing environmental stocks and flows in national accounts |
| ⭐ **PES** | Payment for Ecosystem Services — ⭐ *India's Finance Commission forest-cover devolution is a large real-world example* |

- ⭐⭐⭐ **Weak vs strong sustainability** — irreversibility as the dividing line; the intellectual basis of the precautionary principle
