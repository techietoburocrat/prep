# ⚖️ E15 — India's Environmental Laws

> **The one-line point:** **Almost every Indian environmental statute traces to one of two moments — the 1972 Stockholm Conference, or the 1984 Bhopal disaster. Knowing which explains what each Act was designed to do.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐ THE CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION

| Provision | Text / effect |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Article 48A** *(DPSP)* | **"The State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wild life of the country."** *Added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976* |
| ⭐ **Article 51A(g)** *(Fundamental Duty)* | **"...to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures."** *Also 42nd Amendment, 1976* |
| ⭐ **Article 21** | ⭐ **The Supreme Court has read the RIGHT TO A CLEAN AND HEALTHY ENVIRONMENT into the RIGHT TO LIFE.** *This judicial expansion is what gives environmental claims constitutional force, since 48A and 51A(g) are not directly enforceable* 🎯 **The single most important constitutional point here** |
| **Article 253** | Parliament may legislate to implement international treaties — *the basis for the Air Act 1981 and the EPA 1986, both enacted to give effect to Stockholm* |
| **7th Schedule** | **Forests and "protection of wild animals and birds" were moved from the State List to the CONCURRENT LIST by the 42nd Amendment.** ⚠️ **But WATER remains a STATE subject (Entry 17), with inter-state rivers in the Union List (Entry 56)** |
| **73rd & 74th Amendments** | Devolve sanitation, water and environmental functions to local bodies |

> **The 42nd Amendment (1976) is the constitutional hinge — it added both environmental provisions AND moved forests to the Concurrent List, in the immediate aftermath of Stockholm 1972.** 🎯 *Links directly to your [Polity notes](../../../../Polity/00_START_HERE.md).*

**Judicially developed principles:** **the PRECAUTIONARY principle · the POLLUTER PAYS principle · the PUBLIC TRUST doctrine · SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT as part of Indian law · ABSOLUTE LIABILITY** (*from* **M.C. Mehta v. Union of India** *(the Oleum gas leak case) — a stricter standard than the English* Rylands v. Fletcher *rule, with no exceptions*).

## 2️⃣ ⭐ THE MAJOR ACTS — in chronological order

| Year | Act | Key provisions |
|---|---|---|
| **1927** | **INDIAN FOREST ACT** | **Colonial revenue statute. Creates RESERVED, PROTECTED and VILLAGE forests.** ⚠️ **RESERVED is STRICTER than PROTECTED** *(everything prohibited unless permitted, vs everything permitted unless prohibited)* |
| ⭐ **1972** | ⭐ **WILDLIFE (PROTECTION) ACT** | **Creates National Parks and Sanctuaries; SCHEDULES of protected species; bans hunting; regulates trade in wildlife articles.** **Key amendments: 2002 (added Conservation & Community Reserves) · 2006 (created the NTCA and statutory tiger reserves) · 2022 (restructured the schedules and aligned the Act with CITES)** |
| ⭐ **1974** | ⭐ **WATER (PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF POLLUTION) ACT** | ** INDIA'S FIRST MAJOR ENVIRONMENTAL STATUTE. Created the CPCB and the SPCBs.** *Consent-to-establish and consent-to-operate regime* |
| **1977** | **WATER CESS ACT** | Levy on water consumed by industry (*since subsumed by GST-era changes*) |
| ⭐ **1980** | ⭐ **FOREST (CONSERVATION) ACT** | ⭐ **The single most consequential forest law: NO forest land may be diverted to NON-FOREST purposes WITHOUT PRIOR CENTRAL GOVERNMENT APPROVAL.** *It took the decision away from the states, and it is the reason India's deforestation rate fell sharply after 1980* ⚠️ **Amended in 2023 as the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, which narrowed the Act's applicability and has been challenged** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *litigation status* |
| **1981** | **AIR (PREVENTION AND CONTROL OF POLLUTION) ACT** | **Extends the CPCB/SPCB machinery to air; declares air-pollution-control areas.** *Enacted under Article 253 to implement Stockholm* |
| ⭐ **1986** | ⭐ **ENVIRONMENT (PROTECTION) ACT — "the EPA"** | ⭐ **THE UMBRELLA LEGISLATION. Enacted in direct response to the BHOPAL GAS TRAGEDY (Dec 1984).** **Gives the Centre sweeping powers to set standards, restrict industries in any area, and issue directions.** ** The EIA Notification, CRZ Notification, Eco-Sensitive Zones and most waste-management Rules are all issued UNDER this Act** — *which is why it is called umbrella legislation* |
| **1991** | **PUBLIC LIABILITY INSURANCE ACT** | **Mandatory insurance for handlers of hazardous substances; NO-FAULT liability for immediate relief.** *Also a Bhopal consequence* |
| **2002** | **BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY ACT** | **Implements the CBD. Three-tier structure: NBA (national) → SBBs (state) → BMCs (Biodiversity Management Committees at local level, which maintain People's Biodiversity Registers).** **Regulates ACCESS AND BENEFIT SHARING and prevents BIOPIRACY** |
| ⭐ **2006** | ⭐ **FOREST RIGHTS ACT** *(Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act)* | **Recognises INDIVIDUAL forest rights and COMMUNITY FOREST RESOURCE rights; empowers the GRAM SABHA to protect and manage forest resources; requires free informed consent before relocation from critical wildlife habitat.** **Framed as correcting a "historical injustice" of colonial forest law** |
| **2010** | **NATIONAL GREEN TRIBUNAL ACT** | **Creates the NGT** — *see [E18](18_Institutions_National_and_International.md)* |
| **2016** | **CAMPA ACT** | **Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act — governs the money collected when forest land is diverted, including NPV (net present value) of the lost forest** |

## 3️⃣ ⭐ THE WILDLIFE PROTECTION ACT SCHEDULES

> ⚠️⭐ **CRITICAL: the 2022 AMENDMENT RESTRUCTURED the schedules.** **The old six-schedule structure (with Schedules I–IV for animals by protection level, V for vermin, VI for plants) was consolidated.**
>
> **The post-2022 structure: Schedule I — species with the HIGHEST protection · Schedule II — lesser protection · Schedule III — plants · Schedule IV — specimens listed under CITES appendices.**
>
> ⚠️ **Many coaching notes and older books still describe the pre-2022 six-schedule system.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` **the current structure and any species' schedule from the bare Act before relying on it.** 🎯 *This is exactly the sort of recent change UPSC likes, and exactly where stale notes fail.*

**What survives unchanged and is safe to state:** **Schedule I offences carry the highest penalties · hunting is prohibited except in narrow circumstances (self-defence, disease, a declared dangerous animal) · the Chief Wildlife Warden is the key statutory officer · vermin declaration remains legally and politically contentious** (*states have declared nilgai, wild boar and monkeys as vermin in crop-conflict situations — a genuine tension between the Act and farmer livelihoods*).

## 4️⃣ THE BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY ACT & BIOPIRACY

**The Act exists to implement the CBD's third objective: FAIR AND EQUITABLE SHARING of benefits arising from genetic resources.**

| Tier | Body | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **National** | **NBA — National Biodiversity Authority, Chennai** | **Approves access by FOREIGN entities and IPR applications based on Indian biological resources** |
| **State** | **SBB — State Biodiversity Board** | Regulates access by Indian entities |
| **Local** | **BMC — Biodiversity Management Committee** | **At panchayat/municipal level; maintains PEOPLE'S BIODIVERSITY REGISTERS documenting local knowledge and resources** |

> ⭐ **THE BIOPIRACY CASES — these are asked and they make excellent examples:**
>
> | Case | What happened |
> |---|---|
> | **TURMERIC** | **A US patent on turmeric for wound healing was REVOKED (1997) after CSIR produced ancient Sanskrit texts and a 1953 journal paper as prior art** |
> | **NEEM** | **A European Patent Office patent on a neem-based fungicide was REVOKED (2000/2005) on prior-art grounds** |
> | **BASMATI** | **RiceTec's US patent claims were substantially narrowed after India challenged them** |
>
> **India's institutional answer: the TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE DIGITAL LIBRARY (TKDL) — a searchable database of Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha and Yoga knowledge in multiple languages, shared with patent offices worldwide so examiners can find prior art.** **It converts an oral and Sanskrit tradition into a form patent examiners can actually search — a genuinely clever solution to a structural problem.** 🎯 *An outstanding example of India shaping international IP practice.*

⚠️ **The Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act 2023 eased compliance for AYUSH practitioners and cultivated medicinal plants** — ⚠️ *criticised as diluting benefit-sharing.* ⚠️`[VERIFY]`

## 5️⃣ FOREST RIGHTS ACT — the tensions worth knowing

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What it recognises** | **Individual rights (cultivation, habitation) · COMMUNITY rights (grazing, minor forest produce, fishing) · COMMUNITY FOREST RESOURCE rights (to protect and manage) · development rights** |
| **The Gram Sabha's power** | **The Gram Sabha initiates the claims process AND its consent is required for diversion of forest land in many circumstances** — *the NIYAMGIRI case (2013), where Gram Sabhas rejected bauxite mining on Dongria Kondh sacred hills, is the landmark* 🎯 **A superb example of environmental democracy in practice** |
| **The MFP provision** | **Ownership of MINOR FOREST PRODUCE vests in forest dwellers — tendu leaf, mahua, bamboo** *(bamboo was reclassified as a grass outside forest areas in 2017, easing its trade)* |
| ⚠️ **The conservation tension** | **Conservationists argue FRA weakens protected-area integrity; forest-rights groups argue exclusion was itself unjust and ineffective.** **Both cite the same Act.** *The honest position: [see B7](../Part_B_Biodiversity/07_Protected_Area_Network.md) — conservation without local consent does not endure* |
| ⚠️ **Implementation** | Uneven across states; community forest rights recognised far less than individual rights |

## 📝 `[P]` / `[M]` / 🎯

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- ⭐ **Art 48A (DPSP) & 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty) — both added by the 42nd Amendment 1976. Art 21 read to include a clean environment**
- **42nd Amendment moved FORESTS to the CONCURRENT list. WATER stays State (Entry 17); inter-state rivers Union (Entry 56)**
- ⭐ ** WATER ACT 1974 = India's FIRST major environmental Act; created CPCB/SPCB**
- ⭐ ** FOREST (CONSERVATION) ACT 1980 = no diversion of forest land without PRIOR CENTRAL approval**
- ⭐ ** EPA 1986 = UMBRELLA Act, a response to BHOPAL 1984; EIA, CRZ, ESZ and waste Rules are all issued under it**
- **Air Act 1981 & EPA 1986 enacted under Article 253 (treaty implementation)**
- **WPA 1972 amendments: 2002 (Conservation & Community Reserves) · 2006 (NTCA) · 2022 (schedules restructured, CITES alignment)**
- ⚠️ **WPA schedules were RESTRUCTURED in 2022 — verify before quoting old six-schedule descriptions**
- **Biological Diversity Act 2002: NBA (Chennai) → SBB → BMC with People's Biodiversity Registers**
- **Biopiracy: turmeric, neem, basmati patents challenged; TKDL is India's answer**
- **FRA 2006: individual, community, community forest resource rights; Gram Sabha consent; NIYAMGIRI 2013**
- **Absolute liability from M.C. Mehta (Oleum gas leak) — stricter than *Rylands v. Fletcher***
- **Reserved Forest is stricter than Protected Forest (IFA 1927)**

### `[M]` MAINS
- ⭐ **Article 21 as the real engine of Indian environmental law** — because 48A and 51A(g) are unenforceable
- ⭐ **The Forest Conservation Act 1980 centralised the diversion decision and that is why it worked** — and why the 2023 amendment is contested
- ⭐ **Biopiracy and the TKDL** — India converting a knowledge asymmetry into an institutional solution
- **FRA: environmental democracy vs protected-area integrity** — Niyamgiri as the positive case
- **EPA as umbrella legislation** — flexible rule-making without fresh statutes, and the accountability cost of that flexibility
- **Bhopal's legislative legacy** — EPA 1986 and the Public Liability Insurance Act 1991
