## 1️⃣ ⭐ OZONE DEPLETION

### The good ozone

**The OZONE LAYER sits in the STRATOSPHERE, roughly 15–35 km up.** **It absorbs harmful UV-B and UV-C radiation.** **Measured in DOBSON UNITS (DU); a "hole" is conventionally defined as below 220 DU.**

⚠️ **Don't confuse with [ground-level ozone](../Part_C_Pollution/09_Air_Pollution.md), which is a pollutant. "Good up high, bad nearby."**

### The mechanism

```
   CFCs released at the surface (refrigerants, aerosols, foams)
              ↓  They are INERT — so they survive the troposphere
                   and drift up over years
   In the STRATOSPHERE, intense UV breaks them apart
              ↓
   Free CHLORINE atoms released
              ↓
   Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂
   ClO + O → Cl + O₂          THE CHLORINE IS REGENERATED
              ↓
   A SINGLE chlorine atom can destroy
      TENS OF THOUSANDS of ozone molecules —
      it is a CATALYST, not a reactant
```

> ⭐ **WHY THE HOLE IS OVER ANTARCTICA — this is the question that gets asked, and it has a precise answer:**
>
> **1. The POLAR VORTEX in the Antarctic winter isolates the air mass over the continent — nothing mixes in from outside.**
> **2. Extreme cold (below about −78 °C) forms POLAR STRATOSPHERIC CLOUDS (PSCs).**
> **3. Chemical reactions ON THE SURFACE of those ice crystals convert inactive chlorine reservoirs into reactive forms — the clouds act as a catalyst bed.**
> **4. When the sun returns in SPRING (September–October), sunlight triggers the reactions and ozone is destroyed rapidly.**
>
> **So the hole appears over ANTARCTICA in SEPTEMBER–OCTOBER — polar spring, not winter.** **The Arctic gets a weaker, less consistent version because its vortex is less stable and less cold.** 🎯 **Getting "spring, not winter" right is the mark.**

**Effects of depletion:** **skin cancer, cataracts, immune suppression; damage to phytoplankton (the base of the marine food chain) and to crops.**

### ⭐ THE MONTREAL PROTOCOL — the success story

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Framework** | **Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer, 1985** (framework only, no targets) |
| ⭐ **The protocol** | **MONTREAL PROTOCOL, 1987** — phase-out schedules for **CFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride, methyl chloroform, HCFCs, methyl bromide** |
| **Why it worked** | ** UNIVERSAL RATIFICATION — the only UN treaty ratified by every country · a SMALL number of producers and substances · VIABLE SUBSTITUTES existed · CBDR built in, with a later phase-out schedule and a MULTILATERAL FUND for developing countries · regular scientific review** |
| ⭐ **KIGALI AMENDMENT, 2016** | **Phases down HFCs. ⚠️ CRUCIAL POINT: HFCs do NOT deplete ozone — they were the SUBSTITUTE for CFCs. But they are extremely potent GREENHOUSE GASES.** **So an OZONE treaty was used to deliver a CLIMATE outcome** 🎯 *An elegant, highly examinable fact* |
| **India** | **Ratified Kigali in 2021; India Cooling Action Plan (2019)** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` |
| **Recovery** | **The ozone layer is on a recovery track; full Antarctic recovery is projected around the mid-21st century** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *the current projection* |

> ⭐ **THE COMPARISON THAT WINS MARKS — why Montreal succeeded where climate treaties struggle:**
>
> | | **OZONE (Montreal)** | **CLIMATE (UNFCCC/Paris)** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Number of substances** | **A handful** | **Effectively all economic activity** |
> | **Number of producers** | **A few dozen firms** | **Every firm and household on Earth** |
> | **Substitutes** | **Available and affordable** | **Partial; transforms entire energy systems** |
> | **Cost of action** | **Modest** | **Enormous, and unevenly distributed** |
> | **Public perception of risk** | **Immediate and personal — skin cancer** | **Diffuse, delayed, statistical** |
> | **Outcome** | **SOLVED** | **Unresolved** |
>
> **The lesson to state: Montreal is not proof that treaties work in general — it is proof that treaties work when the problem is CONCENTRATED, SUBSTITUTABLE and CHEAP TO FIX.** **Climate change is none of those things.** 🎯 **This is a genuinely strong argument and very few candidates make it.**

## 2️⃣ ACID RAIN

** SO₂ and NOₓ, mainly from coal combustion and vehicles, react with water vapour to form SULPHURIC and NITRIC acids, which fall as WET deposition (rain, snow, fog) or DRY deposition (particles, gases).**

**Normal rain is already mildly acidic (pH ~5.6, from dissolved CO₂). "Acid rain" means below that.**

| Effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Aquatic** | **Lake acidification; fish eggs fail; acid mobilises ALUMINIUM from soils into water, which is toxic to fish gills** — *the aluminium, not the acid itself, often does the killing* |
| **Forests** | **Leaches base cations (Ca, Mg) from soil; damages leaf cuticles; weakens trees against frost and pests** — *Germany's* Waldsterben *(forest death) in the 1980s* |
| **Buildings & monuments** | **Dissolves limestone and marble — "STONE LEPROSY". THE TAJ MAHAL is the standing Indian example (with the Taj Trapezium Zone created to control emissions around it)** |
| **Transboundary** | **Emissions in one country fall in another** — the defining political feature |

**The governance success: the 1979 GENEVA CONVENTION ON LONG-RANGE TRANSBOUNDARY AIR POLLUTION (CLRTAP) and its protocols (notably the Helsinki and Oslo sulphur protocols) substantially solved European acid rain; the US Clean Air Act Amendments of 1990 introduced the SO₂ CAP-AND-TRADE programme, which cut emissions faster and cheaper than predicted.**

> **That US SO₂ programme is worth remembering for a separate reason: it is the most-cited proof that MARKET-BASED environmental instruments can work — and it is the intellectual ancestor of carbon markets.** 🎯 *Deploy it in any carbon-pricing answer.*

## 3️⃣ ⭐ OCEAN ACIDIFICATION — "the other CO₂ problem"

```
   The ocean absorbs ~25–30% of human CO₂ emissions
              ↓
   CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid)
              ↓
   H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
              ↓
   More H⁺ → pH FALLS (the ocean becomes less alkaline)
              ↓
   Those extra H⁺ ions react with CARBONATE ions (CO₃²⁻),
      converting them to bicarbonate
              ↓
   CARBONATE — the building block of shells and coral
      skeletons — BECOMES SCARCER
              ↓
   Corals, molluscs, pteropods and some plankton
      struggle to CALCIFY; existing shells can dissolve
```

> ⭐ **The three things to say that most answers don't:**
> 1. **Acidification is a SEPARATE problem from warming.** **Same cause, different mechanism.** **It would happen even if CO₂ trapped no heat at all.**
> 2. **It cannot be geoengineered away.** **Solar radiation management could in principle cool the planet — but it would do NOTHING for ocean chemistry, because the CO₂ would still be dissolving.** 🎯 *This is the strongest single argument against treating geoengineering as a substitute for emission cuts*
> 3. **The ocean has become measurably more acidic since pre-industrial times, and pH is logarithmic — a small pH change is a large change in hydrogen-ion concentration.**

**Consequences:** **coral reefs (compounding [bleaching](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/10_Islands_and_Coral_Reefs.md)) · shellfish and aquaculture · pteropods, a key link in polar food webs · fisheries.**

## 4️⃣ DESERTIFICATION & LAND DEGRADATION

📎 **The mechanism — the Sahel, overgrazing, the albedo feedback, the borehole paradox — is in [Leong Climate 3](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/03_Savanna_Sudan_Type.md). Here: the definitions and the governance.**

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **DESERTIFICATION** | **Land degradation in ARID, SEMI-ARID and DRY SUB-HUMID areas, resulting from climatic variations and human activities.** ⚠️ **Note: it is NOT "the desert advancing," and it does NOT occur in hyper-arid true desert. You cannot desertify a desert** |
| **Land degradation** | The broader term, applicable in any climatic zone |
| **LDN — Land Degradation Neutrality** | **SDG target 15.3 — that the amount of healthy, productive land remains STABLE or INCREASES: any new degradation must be offset by restoration** |

**Governance:** **UNCCD — the UN Convention to Combat Desertification, 1994**, the third Rio convention. ** India hosted UNCCD COP-14 in 2019 at New Delhi, producing the NEW DELHI DECLARATION.** **India's pledge: restore 26 million hectares of degraded land by 2030** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` · **the BONN CHALLENGE** · **Africa's GREAT GREEN WALL** · **India's Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas (ISRO/SAC)** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *the current degraded-area figure, around 30% of India's land.*
