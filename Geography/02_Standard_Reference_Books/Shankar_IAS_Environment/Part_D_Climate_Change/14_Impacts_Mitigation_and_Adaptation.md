# 🔥 D14 — Climate Impacts, Mitigation & Adaptation

> **The one-line point:** **India is among the countries most exposed to climate change and among the least responsible for causing it. Every position India takes in negotiations follows from that single asymmetry.**

## 1️⃣ IMPACTS ON INDIA — the sector-by-sector map

| Sector | Impact |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **MONSOON** | **The signal is NOT "less rain." It is FEWER RAINY DAYS delivering the same or more total — i.e. MORE INTENSE events separated by LONGER DRY SPELLS.** **More flood AND more drought from the same annual figure** *(see [Leong Climate 2](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md))* |
| **AGRICULTURE** | **Heat stress at grain-filling reduces wheat yields; rice sterility above threshold temperatures; shifting pest and disease ranges; ground-level ozone yield losses.** *India's agriculture is disproportionately exposed because it is largely rain-fed and smallholder* |
| ⭐ **WATER** | **Himalayan glacier retreat affects DRY-SEASON flow (the Indus far more than the Ganga, which is monsoon-fed); groundwater stress; competing demands** *(see [Leong A6](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/06_Glacial_Landforms.md))* |
| **SEA LEVEL & COASTS** | **~7,500 km of coastline; Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Kochi, Visakhapatnam exposed; the Sundarbans has already lost inhabited islands; saline ingress into coastal aquifers and farmland** |
| **EXTREME EVENTS** | **Heat waves (the Loo, and rising WET-BULB temperatures); intensifying cyclones — notably in the ARABIAN SEA, historically the quieter basin; urban flooding; GLOFs** |
| **HEALTH** | **Heat mortality; expanding vector ranges (malaria, dengue moving to higher altitudes and latitudes); water-borne disease after floods; malnutrition via crop failure** |
| **BIODIVERSITY** | **Species shifting poleward and upslope; mountain-top species have nowhere to go — the "escalator to extinction"; phenological mismatch (flowering and pollinator timing drifting apart); coral bleaching** |
| **ECONOMY & EQUITY** | **Outdoor and informal workers lose labour hours to heat; smallholder farmers absorb the yield shocks; the poorest have the least adaptive capacity.** *Climate impact is regressive* |

> ⭐ ** WET-BULB TEMPERATURE is the concept to carry: it combines heat and humidity, and it is the measure of whether a human body can cool itself by sweating at all.** **Above roughly 35 °C wet-bulb, a healthy person in the shade with unlimited water cannot survive extended exposure.** **Parts of the Indo-Gangetic plain and coastal India are projected to approach these thresholds.** 🎯 **Naming wet-bulb rather than just "heat" signals real understanding.**

## 2️⃣ MITIGATION vs ADAPTATION — the distinction, and India's position

| | **MITIGATION** | **ADAPTATION** |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | **Reducing emissions or enhancing sinks — attacking the CAUSE** | **Adjusting to actual or expected effects — managing the CONSEQUENCE** |
| **Examples** | Renewables, efficiency, afforestation, EVs, CCS | **Drought-resistant crops, flood defences, early warning, heat action plans, crop insurance, mangrove restoration** |
| **Benefit accrues** | **GLOBALLY** — one country's cut helps everyone | **LOCALLY** — one country's defences help only itself |
| **Incentive problem** | **A CLASSIC FREE-RIDER problem — you can benefit from others' mitigation without doing any.** *Which is precisely why it needs a treaty* | **No free-rider problem — so it happens more readily, but requires FINANCE that poor countries lack** |

> ⭐ **This asymmetry explains the whole structure of climate politics: mitigation is a global public good that markets and nations under-supply; adaptation is a local private good that the poor cannot afford.** **Hence developing countries push for ADAPTATION FINANCE and LOSS AND DAMAGE, while developed countries have historically emphasised mitigation targets.** 🎯 **This paragraph reframes a list into an argument — use it.**

** LOSS AND DAMAGE** = **compensation for climate harm that is beyond adaptation** (a submerged island, a destroyed harvest). **A Loss and Damage Fund was agreed at COP-27 (2022) and operationalised at COP-28 (2023).** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *its capitalisation and status — this is live.*

## 3️⃣ ⭐ INDIA'S CLIMATE POLICY

### NAPCC — National Action Plan on Climate Change (2008) and its EIGHT MISSIONS

| # | Mission | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **National SOLAR Mission** (JNNSM) | Solar capacity |
| **2** | **National Mission for ENHANCED ENERGY EFFICIENCY** | **PAT scheme (Perform, Achieve, Trade) — tradable energy-saving certificates for industry** |
| **3** | **National Mission on SUSTAINABLE HABITAT** | Urban planning, buildings, transport, waste |
| **4** | **National WATER Mission** | Water-use efficiency |
| **5** | **National Mission for Sustaining the HIMALAYAN ECOSYSTEM** | Glaciers, biodiversity |
| **6** | **National Mission for a GREEN INDIA** | Forest and tree cover |
| **7** | **National Mission for SUSTAINABLE AGRICULTURE** | Climate-resilient farming |
| **8** | **National Mission on STRATEGIC KNOWLEDGE for Climate Change** | Research |

 *Mnemonic for the eight: **S**olar · **E**nergy efficiency · **H**abitat · **W**ater · **H**imalaya · **G**reen India · **A**griculture · **K**nowledge.* **States also have SAPCCs (State Action Plans on Climate Change).**

### India's NDCs and net zero ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *— targets have been revised and will be again*

**India's updated NDC commitments centre on: reducing the EMISSIONS INTENSITY of GDP · achieving a large share of installed electric power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030 · creating an additional carbon sink through forest and tree cover · and a net-zero target year of 2070 announced at COP-26 (Glasgow, 2021) as part of the "PANCHAMRIT" five-point commitment.**

> ⚠️ **Verify the exact percentages, capacity figures and dates from the UNFCCC NDC registry or MoEFCC before quoting.** **The one stable, quotable fact is the structure: India commits to INTENSITY reduction, not absolute emission cuts — because as a developing economy its absolute emissions must still rise.** 🎯 **Understanding *why* it is an intensity target is worth more than the number.**

### Other Indian instruments

** LiFE — Lifestyle for Environment**, India's behavioural-change initiative internationalised at COP-26 · **the INTERNATIONAL SOLAR ALLIANCE (ISA), co-founded by India and France in 2015, headquartered at Gurugram** · **the CDRI (Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure), launched by India in 2019** · **Green Credit Programme · National Green Hydrogen Mission · PM-KUSUM (solar for farmers) · FAME/EV policy · the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *status of the domestic carbon market.*

## 4️⃣ CARBON PRICING & MARKETS

| Instrument | How it works |
|---|---|
| **CARBON TAX** | **Set the PRICE, let the quantity adjust.** Simple, predictable revenue |
| **CAP-AND-TRADE (ETS)** | **Set the QUANTITY (the cap), let the market find the price.** *The EU ETS is the largest; the US SO₂ programme was the proof of concept* |
| **Offsets / credits** | **Pay for emission reductions elsewhere.** ⚠️ *Persistent problems with ADDITIONALITY (would it have happened anyway?), PERMANENCE (a forest can burn) and LEAKAGE (the activity relocates)* |
| **CBAM** | **The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism — a levy on the embedded carbon of imports.** **India has objected strongly, arguing it is protectionism dressed as climate policy and violates CBDR.** 🎯 *A live trade-and-climate flashpoint, excellent for GS-II and GS-III* |
| **India's CCTS** | A domestic compliance carbon market, evolving from the PAT scheme ⚠️`[VERIFY]` |

## 5️⃣ GEOENGINEERING — and why to be sceptical

| Approach | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SRM — Solar Radiation Management** | **Stratospheric aerosol injection (mimicking Mt Pinatubo 1991, which cooled the planet ~0.5 °C for two years), marine cloud brightening, space mirrors** |
| **CDR — Carbon Dioxide Removal** | **Afforestation, BECCS, DAC, ocean fertilisation, enhanced weathering** *(see [D12](12_Greenhouse_Effect_and_Global_Warming.md))* |

> ⭐ **The four objections to SRM, and they are strong:**
> 1. **It treats the symptom.** **It does NOTHING for OCEAN ACIDIFICATION** — *see [D13](13_Ozone_Acid_Rain_and_Global_Issues.md).*
> 2. ** TERMINATION SHOCK — stop abruptly and the suppressed warming arrives all at once, far faster than ecosystems can track.**
> 3. **Regional effects are unequal and unpredictable — modelling suggests it could disrupt the MONSOON, which is an existential concern for India specifically.**
> 4. ** Governance: who decides the global thermostat setting? It is cheap enough that a single country could do it unilaterally.**
>
> **Plus the MORAL HAZARD: the mere prospect reduces pressure to cut emissions.**
