## 1️⃣ THE CASE STUDIES — six that cover almost everything

**Learn these six properly and you can illustrate any DM question.** Each is chosen because it demonstrates a *mechanism*, not just an event.

### ODISHA CYCLONES — the success

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | 1999 super-cyclone ~10,000 deaths → Phailin 2013 and later storms, deaths in the tens |
| **Why it changed** | Purpose-built cyclone shelters · a state disaster response force (OSDMA) · last-mile warning to village level · pre-emptive evacuation of ~1 million · rehearsed protocols |
| **Use it for** | **Preparedness works · early warning · the reactive-to-proactive shift · that DM success is possible in a poorer state** |

### BHUJ EARTHQUAKE, 2001 — the institutional trigger

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | ~20,000 deaths; collapse of unreinforced masonry and poorly built multi-storey buildings |
| **Consequence** | Gujarat State Disaster Management Authority — India's first — and momentum toward the **DM Act 2005** |
| **Use it for** | **Building codes vs enforcement · "buildings kill, not earthquakes" · disaster as a driver of institutional reform** |

### THE 2004 TSUNAMI — the legal turning point

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | ~230,000 deaths across the Indian Ocean; India's coasts and the Andamans hit with no warning |
| **Consequence** | **The DM Act 2005 · NDMA · INCOIS tsunami warning centre (2007) · the Hyogo Framework internationally** |
| **Use it for** | **The absence of early warning · regional cooperation · how one event rewrote a country's disaster law** |

### KEDARNATH, 2013 — development in a fragile zone

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | Extreme rainfall plus the breach of Chorabari lake; thousands dead; pilgrimage infrastructure destroyed |
| **Causes beyond the rain** | Unregulated construction in the floodplain and on old river channels · hydropower and road cutting · no carrying-capacity limits on pilgrim numbers |
| **Use it for** | **Carrying capacity · Himalayan fragility · why a natural trigger became a disaster through land-use decisions** |

### CHENNAI FLOODS, 2015 and 2023 — the urban case

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | Record rainfall on a city whose drainage had been built over |
| **Causes** | Pallikaranai marsh and the Adyar/Cooum floodplains encroached · lakes and tanks filled for construction · drains blocked with solid waste · reservoir release timing |
| **Use it for** | **Urban flooding · wetlands AS infrastructure · why CRZ and wetland rules matter in practice** |

### SIKKIM GLOF, 2023 — the emerging hazard

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | South Lhonak glacial lake burst; the Teesta-III dam at Chungthang destroyed; the Teesta valley devastated |
| **Use it for** | **Climate change producing NEW hazards · hydropower siting in glaciated valleys · the limits of designing infrastructure for historical rather than projected risk** |

> **Pair them deliberately.** *Odisha against Katrina 2005 makes the point that institutions matter more than wealth. Bhuj against Japan makes the point about enforcement. Kedarnath and Chennai together make the point that land-use decisions convert hazards into disasters.*

## 2️⃣ THE ANSWER FRAMES

**Four structures that fit almost every DM question. Fill with the case studies above.**

### Frame A — "Discuss hazard X in India and suggest measures"

```
1. DEFINE and locate what it is, where in India, scale of exposure
2. WHY INDIA the physical mechanism [link to the Leong note]
3. WHY IT BECOMES A vulnerability + exposure, not just the hazard
   DISASTER — use the risk equation
4. EXISTING RESPONSE agency, guidelines, plan, warning system
5. GAPS be specific: enforcement, last mile, funding,
                          short lead time, coordination
6. WAY FORWARD structural + non-structural, split explicitly
7. CLOSE Sendai / Build Back Better / a named success
```

### Frame B — "Compare or evaluate a framework"

```
1. CONTEXT what problem it was created to solve
2. STRUCTURE priorities, targets, period, scope
3. WHAT CHANGED against its predecessor — the Sendai/Hyogo table
4. INDIA'S ALIGNMENT NDMP 2016, the Ten Point Agenda
5. ASSESSMENT what it achieved, what it did not
```

### Frame C — "Define a concept" *(resilience, vulnerability, carrying capacity)*

```
1. DEFINITION precise, in the standard wording
2. COMPONENTS the types or elements
3. HOW MEASURED indicators — this is what most answers omit
4. ILLUSTRATION one named Indian example
5. POLICY RELEVANCE why it changes what you would do
```

### Frame D — "Why did this disaster happen?"

```
1. THE TRIGGER the natural event
2. THE MULTIPLIERS land use, construction, degraded ecosystems,
                          warning failure, evacuation failure
3. THE STRUCTURAL CAUSE poverty, informality, weak enforcement
4. COUNTERFACTUAL what would have reduced the toll
5. LESSON transferable, not event-specific
```

> ⭐ **The single most reusable move across all four: separate the TRIGGER from the MULTIPLIERS.** *Most answers describe the event. The good answer explains why the same event produced a catastrophe here and an inconvenience elsewhere.*

## 3️⃣ THE LINES WORTH HAVING READY

Six sentences that carry a paragraph each:

1. **"A hazard becomes a disaster only when it meets vulnerability — so disaster management is the management of vulnerability, not of nature."**
2. **"Earthquakes don't kill people; buildings do. India's problem is not its building codes but their enforcement."**
3. **"Embankments reduce flood frequency and increase flood severity — structural defences transfer risk rather than removing it."**
4. **"Urban flooding is not riverine flooding in a city. The wetlands that were filled in WERE the drainage."**
5. **"Hyogo told countries to manage disasters; Sendai tells them to manage risk — and gave them numbers to be measured against."**
6. **"Odisha shows that disaster outcomes track institutions more closely than income."**

## 6️⃣ 📝 THE TEN-QUESTION SELF-TEST

*Cover the right column.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| The risk equation | **Hazard × Vulnerability × Exposure ÷ Capacity** |
| Sendai period, priorities, targets | **2015–2030 · 4 priorities · 7 targets** |

| The key conceptual shift Sendai made | **Disaster management → disaster RISK management; preventing NEW risk** |
