# 🌪️ F21 — India's Hazards: Management, Hazard by Hazard

> **The one-line point:** **India is exposed to almost every hazard type there is — and the notes already explain why each one happens. This file covers what is done about it.**

**Roughly 58% of India's landmass is earthquake-prone, 12% flood-prone, 5,700 km of coastline cyclone-prone, and 68% of cropped area drought-prone.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *these headline shares from NDMA before quoting.*

## 1️⃣ EARTHQUAKES

📎 **Mechanism:** [Leong A2 §5](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/02_Earth_Movements_and_Vulcanicity.md) — plate boundaries, focus vs epicentre, the Alpine–Himalayan belt.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Zones** | **Seismic Zones II to V** *(there is no Zone I — it was merged)*. **Zone V is the highest: the whole North-East, Kashmir, western Himachal, Uttarakhand, the Rann of Kachchh and the Andamans** |
| **Why India** | The Indian plate is converging with the Eurasian plate at ~5 cm/year; the Himalaya is seismically active along its entire length |
| **The gap** | **Seismologists identify "seismic gaps" in the central Himalaya where strain has accumulated without release — the basis for expecting a major Himalayan event** |
| **Mitigation** | **Building codes (IS 1893, IS 13920), retrofitting, base isolation.** ⚠️ **The real problem is not the codes but their ENFORCEMENT — India has good codes and poor compliance, especially in the unregulated self-built housing that makes up most of the vulnerable stock** |
| **Response** | NDRF, NDMA earthquake guidelines, structural safety audits |

> **The answer that scores: "earthquakes do not kill people, buildings do."** *Japan and Chile experience larger quakes with far lower mortality. The difference is construction quality and enforcement, both of which are policy variables — which brings you straight back to the [risk equation](20_Concepts_and_Frameworks.md).*

## 2️⃣ CYCLONES

📎 **Mechanism:** [Leong Climate 2](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md) — formation conditions, the 26.5 °C threshold, why the Bay of Bengal is deadlier.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Asymmetry** | **The Bay of Bengal is far deadlier than the Arabian Sea** — shallower, warmer, a funnel-shaped coast that amplifies storm surge, and a dense low-lying delta population |
| **Emerging change** | **The Arabian Sea is warming and generating more and stronger cyclones than it historically did** — a genuine shift worth citing |
| **Killer** | **Storm SURGE, not wind, causes the large majority of cyclone deaths** |
| **Warning** | **IMD colour codes: GREEN (no action) · YELLOW (be aware) · ORANGE (be prepared) · RED (take action)** *— asked in 2022* |
| **Mitigation** | **Cyclone shelters, embankments, coastal MANGROVE belts as natural buffers, evacuation routes, the NCRMP (National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project)** |

> ⭐ **THE ODISHA MODEL — India's best-documented disaster-management success, and the case to lead with.**
>
> **1999 Odisha super-cyclone: roughly 10,000 deaths. Cyclone Phailin, 2013, of comparable intensity: deaths in the tens.**
>
> **What changed:** **a network of purpose-built cyclone shelters · a professional state disaster response force (OSDMA) · last-mile warning dissemination reaching village level · pre-emptive evacuation of nearly a million people · and clear evacuation protocols rehearsed in advance.**
>
> 👉 **Use it deliberately as a POSITIVE example. Indian DM answers are usually a list of failures, and an examiner reading the fortieth such answer notices the one that can name a success and explain the mechanism.**

## 3️⃣ FLOODS

📎 **Mechanism:** [Leong Climate 2](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md) — monsoon variability; [Leong A4](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/04_River_Landforms.md) — river regimes.

**Types worth distinguishing:** **riverine · flash · coastal (surge) · urban · glacial (GLOF) · dam-burst.**

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Most affected** | **Bihar (the Kosi — "the Sorrow of Bihar"), Assam (Brahmaputra), UP, West Bengal, Odisha** |
| **Why the Kosi** | **It carries an enormous silt load from young Himalayan rock, raises its own bed, and shifts course dramatically — it has migrated ~120 km west over two centuries** |
| **Agency** | **Central Water Commission** for forecasting |
| **Structural measures** | Embankments, dams, channel improvement, detention basins |
| **Non-structural** | **FLOOD-PLAIN ZONING, flood forecasting, insurance, wetland restoration** |

> ⚠️ **The embankment paradox is the sophisticated point here.** **Embankments reduce flood frequency but INCREASE its severity when they fail: they confine silt to the channel, so the riverbed rises, so the water is held ever higher above the surrounding plain. Bihar has more embankment length than any other state and more flood-affected area than before they were built.**
>
> **The lesson generalises: structural defences transfer risk rather than removing it, and can create false confidence that encourages settlement in the protected zone.** *Compare with [coastal groynes](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/08_Coastal_Landforms.md), which relocate erosion downdrift.*

### Urban flooding — treat it separately

**Asked in 2016, 2020 and 2024.** It is not riverine flooding in a city; it is a different phenomenon.

**Causes:** **encroached and filled wetlands and lakes that once WERE the drainage · concretised catchments raising run-off coefficients · colonial-era drains designed for lower intensities · solid waste choking what drainage remains · unregulated construction on natural channels · and more intense rainfall events under climate change.**

**Examples:** **Mumbai 2005 · Chennai 2015 and 2023 · Hyderabad 2020 · Bengaluru 2022.**

**Remedies:** restore urban water bodies, permeable surfaces and rain gardens, stormwater master plans, **enforce floodplain and wetland regulation**, real-time urban flood warning. *See [Shankar A4 §4](../Part_A_Ecology/04_Aquatic_and_Wetland_Ecosystems.md).*

## 4️⃣ LANDSLIDES

📎 **Mechanism and the Himalaya-vs-Ghats contrast:** [Leong A3 §2](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/03_Weathering_and_Mass_Movement.md).

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Extent** | **~12–13% of India's land area is landslide-prone** |
| **Nodal agency** | **Geological Survey of India** |
| **Strategy** | **National Landslide Risk Management Strategy, 2019** *(asked in 2021)* — its components: **hazard zonation mapping · early warning · capacity building · regulation and policy · stabilisation and mitigation · awareness** |
| **Human triggers** | **Road and highway cutting, hydropower tunnelling and muck disposal, deforestation, unregulated hill construction, quarrying** |
| **Recent** | Kerala 2018–19 and Wayanad 2024, Chamoli 2021, Joshimath subsidence 2023, Sikkim 2023 |

⭐ **The practical insight: water is the usual trigger, so DRAINAGE is the usual fix** — slope drains, retaining walls, rock bolting, bio-engineering with deep-rooted vegetation.

## 5️⃣ DROUGHT

📎 **Mechanism:** [Leong Climate 3](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/03_Savanna_Sudan_Type.md) — rainfall variability; [Climate 2](../../GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md) — the monsoon.

⭐ **The four types are a standard Prelims and Mains item:**

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Meteorological** | Rainfall deficiency against the long-period average |
| **Hydrological** | Depletion of surface and groundwater storage |
| **Agricultural** | Soil moisture inadequate for crops — *the one that matters to farmers* |
| **Socio-economic** | Supply of food and income falls below demand; distress, migration |

> **The sequence matters: a meteorological drought does not automatically become an agricultural one if irrigation and soil moisture buffer it, and an agricultural drought does not become socio-economic if relief and employment schemes work. Each arrow is a policy opportunity.**

**Management:** **the Drought Management Manual, watershed development, MGNREGA as a distress-employment buffer, crop insurance (PMFBY), drought-resistant varieties, contingency crop plans, and the Drought Prone Areas Programme.** ⚠️ **Drought declaration is a STATE responsibility, which creates a chronic incentive problem — declaring drought unlocks central assistance but signals administrative failure.**

## 6️⃣ OTHER HAZARDS — briefly

| Hazard | Key points |
|---|---|
| **TSUNAMI** | 📎 [Leong A2](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/02_Earth_Movements_and_Vulcanicity.md). **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami → INCOIS tsunami warning centre, Hyderabad, operational 2007.** Coastal bio-shields, vertical evacuation structures |
| **GLOF** | 📎 [Leong A6 §4](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/06_Glacial_Landforms.md). **South Lhonak, Sikkim 2023 destroyed the Teesta-III dam; Chamoli 2021.** Lake inventorying, controlled siphoning, early warning, **hydropower siting policy in GLOF-prone valleys** |
| **CLOUDBURSTS** | 📎 [Leong A12](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/12_Weather_Elements_and_Local_Winds.md). **>100 mm in an hour over a small area; orographic lift in the Himalaya. Very short lead time — the hardest hazard to warn for.** Asked 2016, 2022, 2024 |
| **HEAT WAVES** | 📎 [Leong A12](../../GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/12_Weather_Elements_and_Local_Winds.md). **NDMA Heat Action Plans; Ahmedabad's (2013) was South Asia's first and is the template.** **Wet-bulb temperature is the survivability metric.** Cool roofs, revised work hours, hydration points |
| **FOREST FIRES** | FSI fire alert system; chir pine forests are resinous and fire-prone; Uttarakhand, Similipal, the Western Ghats |
| **CHEMICAL / INDUSTRIAL** | **Bhopal 1984 — the origin of the EPA 1986 and the Public Liability Insurance Act 1991.** Off-site and on-site emergency plans, MAH (Major Accident Hazard) units |
| **BIOLOGICAL** | Pandemics; **explicitly within Sendai's broadened scope, and the DM Act 2005 was the legal basis for India's COVID-19 response** — a notable use of disaster law for a biological hazard |
| **AVALANCHE** | DGRE, Chandigarh; Himalayan passes and military positions |

## 7️⃣ 📝 `[P]` / `[M]` / 🎯

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- **Seismic Zones II–V; no Zone I; Zone V = NE, Kashmir, W Himachal, Uttarakhand, Kachchh, Andamans**
- **IMD cyclone colour codes: green, yellow, orange, red**
- **Storm surge, not wind, causes most cyclone deaths**
- **Odisha: 1999 super-cyclone ~10,000 deaths → Phailin 2013, deaths in the tens**
- **Drought types: meteorological · hydrological · agricultural · socio-economic**
- **Drought declaration is a STATE responsibility**
- **GSI = landslides nodal agency; National Landslide Risk Management Strategy 2019**
- **CWC = floods · INCOIS = tsunami (2007) · IMD = cyclones and heat · DGRE = avalanches**
- **Cloudburst = >100 mm in an hour**
- **Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan 2013 = South Asia's first**
- **DM Act 2005 was the legal basis for the COVID-19 response**

### `[M]` MAINS
- **"Earthquakes don't kill people, buildings do"** — codes exist, enforcement doesn't
- **The Odisha model** — what specifically changed, deployed as a positive case
- **The embankment paradox** — structural defences transfer risk and breed false confidence
- **Urban flooding is not riverine flooding** — filled wetlands were the drainage
- **The drought cascade** — meteorological → agricultural → socio-economic, with a policy lever at each arrow
- **Bay of Bengal vs Arabian Sea asymmetry, and the Arabian Sea's emerging change**
- **India's early-warning asymmetry** — strong on long-lead hazards, weak on cloudbursts and urban floods
