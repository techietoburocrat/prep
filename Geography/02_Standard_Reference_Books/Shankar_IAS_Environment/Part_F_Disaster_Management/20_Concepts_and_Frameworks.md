# 🚨 F20 — Disaster Management: Concepts, Law & Frameworks

> **The one-line point:** **A hazard is a natural event; a disaster is what happens when that event meets a vulnerable population. Nothing in this subject makes sense until you separate the two — because you cannot stop an earthquake, but you can stop it killing people.**

🔴 **This is the single biggest gap the notes had.** Disaster management is **~29% of GS-III's environment questions** — the largest cluster — and it is an explicit syllabus line: *"Disaster and disaster management."*

## 1️⃣ THE VOCABULARY — get these exactly right

Examiners test the definitions directly, and a loose answer here reads as untrained.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **HAZARD** | A potentially damaging **event or phenomenon**. An earthquake in an empty desert is a hazard that never becomes a disaster |
| **EXPOSURE** | The people, property and systems **located in** hazard-prone areas |
| **VULNERABILITY** | The **conditions that make exposed people susceptible** to harm — poor housing, poverty, age, disability, weak institutions |
| **CAPACITY** | The **resources and abilities** available to cope and recover |
| **DISASTER** | A serious **disruption** exceeding the community's ability to cope using its own resources |
| **RISK** | The **probable loss** — a function of the three above |
| **RESILIENCE** | The ability to **resist, absorb, accommodate, adapt to and recover** from a hazard promptly and efficiently, **including preserving essential structures and functions** |

⭐ **THE RISK EQUATION — the single most quotable line in this Part:**

```
                HAZARD  ×  VULNERABILITY  ×  EXPOSURE
     RISK  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────
                             CAPACITY
```

> **What it tells you is the whole policy argument: you cannot change the numerator's first term.** No government prevents an earthquake or a cyclone. **But vulnerability, exposure and capacity are all human variables — and all three are things policy can move.**
>
> 👉 **So disaster management is not about controlling nature. It is about reducing the human multipliers.** *State this and the rest of any DM answer writes itself.*

### The types of vulnerability

Naming the *type* is what turns a generic answer into a specific one.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | Unreinforced masonry, buildings on floodplains, kutcha housing |
| **Social** | Age, gender, disability, caste, isolation, literacy |
| **Economic** | Poverty, single-crop dependence, no insurance or savings |
| **Environmental** | Degraded mangroves, deforested slopes, filled wetlands |
| **Attitudinal** | Fatalism, distrust of warnings, refusal to evacuate |

> **The equity point worth making: disasters are not natural levellers.** The same cyclone kills the fisherman in a thatched hut and inconveniences the official in a concrete building. **Vulnerability is socially produced, so disaster impact tracks poverty, gender and caste.** *This framing lifts a GS-III answer into GS-I society territory and examiners reward it.*

## 2️⃣ THE DISASTER MANAGEMENT CYCLE

```
   ┌──────────────────── PRE-DISASTER ────────────────────┐
   │                                                      │
   │   PREVENTION        MITIGATION       PREPAREDNESS    │
   │   eliminate the     reduce the       be ready to     │
   │   risk entirely     impact           respond         │
   │                                                      │
   │   no building in    retrofitting,    early warning,  │
   │   the floodplain    mangrove belts   drills, stocks  │
   └───────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                           ↓
                     ⚡  DISASTER
                           ↓
   ┌───────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
   │                                                      │
   │   RESPONSE          RECOVERY         RECONSTRUCTION  │
   │   rescue, relief,   restore          rebuild — and   │
   │   immediate needs   services and     this is where   │
   │                     livelihoods      BUILD BACK      │
   │                                      BETTER applies  │
   └──────────────────── POST-DISASTER ───────────────────┘
                           ↓
                 feeds back into PREVENTION
```

> **The shift that defines modern DM policy, in India and globally: from a REACTIVE, relief-centric model to a PROACTIVE, prevention-and-preparedness model.**
>
> **The old approach treated disasters as unpredictable acts of god requiring generous relief afterwards. The new one treats them as predictable consequences of known risk, requiring investment beforehand.** *India's turning point was the Disaster Management Act of 2005; globally it was Hyogo the same year.*
>
> **The economics back it: every rupee spent on mitigation saves several rupees in response.** That ratio is the standard justification for pre-disaster spending.

## 3️⃣ THE DISASTER MANAGEMENT ACT, 2005 — India's architecture

**Enacted in the aftermath of the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami.**

| Level | Body | Chaired by |
|---|---|---|
| **National** | **NDMA** — National Disaster Management Authority | **The PRIME MINISTER** |
| National (executive) | **NEC** — National Executive Committee | **Union Home Secretary** |
| **State** | **SDMA** | **The CHIEF MINISTER** |
| **District** | **DDMA** | **The District Collector / Magistrate** *(co-chaired by the elected head of the district council)* |

**Supporting institutions:**

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **NDRF** — National Disaster Response **Force** | A dedicated, specialised response force. ⚠️ **Do not confuse with the identically-abbreviated fund below** |
| **NDRF** — National Disaster Response **Fund** | The financing pool at the Centre |
| **SDRF** | State Disaster Response Fund — *and State Disaster Response Force in some states, another acronym clash* |
| **NDMF / SDMF** | **National and State Disaster MITIGATION Funds** — created following the 15th Finance Commission, and significant because **they finance pre-disaster work rather than only post-disaster relief** |
| **NIDM** | National Institute of Disaster Management — training, research, capacity building |

> ⚠️ **The acronym trap worth memorising: NDRF is BOTH the Force and the Fund.** Prelims has exploited exactly this kind of collision before.

**Policy instruments:**
- **National Policy on Disaster Management, 2009**
- **National Disaster Management Plan, 2016** — **India's first, and explicitly aligned to the Sendai Framework**; revised 2019
- NDMA guidelines, hazard by hazard *(earthquakes, cyclones, floods, landslides, drought, heat waves, chemical disasters)*

## 4️⃣ ⭐ SENDAI vs HYOGO — the comparison UPSC asks directly

⭐ **This has been asked outright** *(2018: "how is Sendai different from Hyogo?")* **and it is the highest-value single item in this Part.**

| | **HYOGO Framework for Action** | **SENDAI Framework for DRR** |
|---|---|---|
| **Period** | **2005–2015** | **2015–2030** |
| **Adopted at** | Kobe, Hyogo, Japan | **Sendai, Japan** |
| **Trigger** | The 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami | Review of Hyogo's shortfalls |
| **Priorities** | **5** | **4** |
| **Global targets** | ⚠️ **None — this was its central weakness** | **7, and measurable** |
| **Focus** | Disaster **MANAGEMENT** — managing the event | **Disaster RISK management — managing the risk** |
| **New risk** | Largely silent | **Explicitly aims to PREVENT NEW risk, not only reduce existing risk** |
| **Scope** | Chiefly natural hazards | **Natural AND man-made, plus biological, technological and environmental hazards** |
| **Responsibility** | State-centric | **"All-of-society, all-of-State"** — the primary duty stays with the State but engages all stakeholders |
| **Recovery** | Little emphasis | **"BUILD BACK BETTER"** made an explicit priority |
| **Status** | Voluntary, non-binding | Voluntary, non-binding |

### Sendai's four priorities for action

1. **Understanding disaster risk**
2. **Strengthening disaster risk governance**
3. **Investing in disaster risk reduction for resilience**
4. **Enhancing disaster preparedness for effective response, and to "Build Back Better" in recovery, rehabilitation and reconstruction**

### Sendai's seven global targets

Conventionally lettered **(a)–(g)**. Four are reductions, three are increases.

| | Target |
|---|---|
| **(a)** | Substantially **reduce global disaster MORTALITY** |
| **(b)** | Substantially **reduce the number of people AFFECTED** |
| **(c)** | Reduce direct **economic loss** relative to GDP |
| **(d)** | Substantially reduce damage to **critical infrastructure** and disruption of **basic services**, health and education facilities among them |
| **(e)** | Substantially **increase the number of countries with national and local DRR strategies** |
| **(f)** | Enhance **international cooperation** with developing countries |
| **(g)** | **Substantially increase availability of and access to MULTI-HAZARD EARLY WARNING SYSTEMS** and disaster risk information |

> **Mnemonic for the direction of travel: the first four are what we want LESS of (deaths, affected people, economic loss, infrastructure damage); the last three are what we want MORE of (national strategies, international cooperation, early warning).**

> **The one-line verdict to carry: Hyogo told countries to manage disasters; Sendai tells them to manage RISK — and, crucially, gave them numbers to be measured against.** *A framework without targets is an aspiration, which is precisely why Hyogo underdelivered.*

### 🇮🇳 India's contribution — the Ten Point Agenda

⭐ **Announced by the Prime Minister at the Asian Ministerial Conference on DRR, New Delhi, 2016.** Worth naming because it is India-specific and rarely quoted.

Its most-cited points: **all development sectors must imbibe DRR principles · risk coverage must extend from poor households to nations · greater involvement and leadership of women · invest in risk mapping globally · leverage technology · develop a network of universities working on disaster issues · utilise social media and mobile technologies · build on local capacity and initiative · ensure opportunity to learn from every disaster · bring greater cohesion in international response.**

## 5️⃣ EARLY WARNING — who warns about what

⭐ **Sendai target (g) is about early warning, and India's institutional map is a clean Prelims item.**

| Hazard | Agency |
|---|---|
| Cyclones, heat waves, heavy rainfall, thunderstorms | **IMD** — India Meteorological Department |
| **Tsunami** | **INCOIS, Hyderabad** — the Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre, operational **2007** after the 2004 event |
| Floods | **CWC** — Central Water Commission |
| Landslides | **GSI** — Geological Survey of India *(nodal agency)* |
| Earthquakes | **National Center for Seismology** |
| Avalanches | **DGRE** (formerly SASE), Chandigarh |
| Forest fires | **FSI** — fire alert system |
| Drought & crop | **Ministry of Agriculture / MNCFC** |
| Satellite & mapping support | **NRSC / ISRO** |

⭐ **The multi-hazard picture: India's warning capability is strong for cyclones and tsunami, moderate for floods, and weakest for landslides, cloudbursts and urban flooding — which are precisely the hazards with the shortest lead times.** *That asymmetry is a good, defensible criticism to offer.*

## 6️⃣ 📝 `[P]` / `[M]` / 🎯

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- **Hazard ≠ disaster; vulnerability, exposure, capacity, resilience — the definitions**
- **Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability × Exposure ÷ Capacity**
- **DM cycle: prevention · mitigation · preparedness → response → recovery · reconstruction**
- **DM Act 2005; NDMA chaired by the PM, SDMA by the CM, DDMA by the District Collector; NEC under the Home Secretary**
- ⚠️ **NDRF = both the response FORCE and the response FUND. NDMF/SDMF are the MITIGATION funds**
- **NIDM = training and research; National DM Plan 2016 was India's first, aligned to Sendai**
- **Sendai 2015–2030: 4 priorities, 7 targets. Hyogo 2005–2015: 5 priorities, NO targets**
- **Sendai adds: prevention of NEW risk · man-made and biological hazards · Build Back Better · all-of-society**
- **Sendai target (g) = multi-hazard early warning**
- **PM's Ten Point Agenda, New Delhi, 2016**
- **INCOIS = tsunami (2007) · CWC = floods · GSI = landslides · IMD = cyclones and heat**

### `[M]` MAINS
- **The risk equation as the organising argument** — you cannot change the hazard, only vulnerability, exposure and capacity
- **Sendai vs Hyogo, fully compared** — and why measurable targets were the decisive addition
- **The reactive-to-proactive shift**, and the mitigation-spending economics that justify it
- **Vulnerability is socially produced** — disasters track poverty, gender and caste rather than falling evenly
- **India's early-warning asymmetry** — strong on cyclones, weak on short-lead hazards
- **Build Back Better** — recovery as an opportunity to reduce future risk rather than restore prior vulnerability
