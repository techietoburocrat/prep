# 🐟 Climate 10 — Laurentian Type / Cool Temperate Eastern Margin `Dfb` / `Dwb`

> **The one-line point:** **This is the FISHING climate. Where a cold current meets a warm one over a shallow shelf, you get fog, and you get the richest fishing grounds on Earth — the Grand Banks and the seas off Japan. Everything else here is secondary.**

🟡 **MEDIUM-HIGH — ~35 min.** **The least distinctive climate in Part B *as a climate*, but it owns two of the best case studies in the syllabus: why fishing grounds form, and the Grand Banks cod collapse.**

## 1️⃣ 🗺️ WHERE — two regions only, and again NH-only

**40°–60° N, on the EASTERN margins of continents.**

| Sub-type | 🗺️ Region |
|---|---|
| **LAURENTIAN type** | **Northeastern North America** — the Canadian Maritimes (Newfoundland, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick), Quebec, and New England |
| **MANCHURIAN type** | **Northeastern Asia** — Manchuria, northern China, North Korea, **Hokkaido (northern Japan)**, Sakhalin, and the Russian Pacific coast |

> **Northern Hemisphere only — for the same reason as the [taiga](09_Siberian_Type_Taiga.md).** **There is no land on an eastern continental margin at 40°–60°S. The only candidate is Patagonia, which is a [rain-shadow desert](04_Hot_and_Mid_Latitude_Deserts.md) instead.**

---

## 2️⃣ WHY IT'S THERE — an intermediate climate

> **Position this climate between two you already know, and it becomes easy:**
>
> ```
> [BRITISH TYPE] LAURENTIAN [SIBERIAN TYPE]
> western margin ←── eastern margin ──→ interior
> fully maritime INTERMEDIATE fully continental
> mild winter cold winter severe winter
> small range large range greatest range
> rain all year rain all year, low precip,
> summer max summer max
> ```
>
> **It has a continental winter because the prevailing westerlies arrive OFF the land, having crossed the whole continent.** **But it has maritime moisture because it sits on a coast and receives cyclonic and onshore inputs.** **Cold like the interior, wet like a coast.**

**Two other controls:**
- **Cold currents flow equatorward along these coasts** — **the Labrador Current** (NE North America) and **the Oyashio/Kurile Current** (NE Asia) — **chilling the coast and keeping summers cool.**
- **The Manchurian version has a genuinely DRY winter** (`Dwb`), because **the Siberian High pushes cold dry offshore air over it** — the winter monsoon. **The Laurentian version keeps winter precipitation (as snow) from Atlantic depressions, so it is `Dfb`.**

## 3️⃣ 🌡️ TEMPERATURE

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Summer mean | **18–21 °C — warm** |
| Winter mean | **−7 to −12 °C — well below freezing** |
| Annual range | **~25–30 °C — large, but less than Siberia's** |
| Snow | **Heavy and reliable** — Quebec, New England and Hokkaido are major snow regions |
| **Fog** | **Very frequent in spring and summer — the region's signature** |

## 4️⃣ 🌧️ PRECIPITATION

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Annual total | **750–1,500 mm** |
| **Regime** | **Rain all year with a SUMMER MAXIMUM** *(Manchurian type: drier winter)* |
| Winter form | **Snow — and lots of it** |
| Source | Summer: convectional + onshore. Winter: cyclonic (Laurentian) / dry offshore (Manchurian) |

### ⭐ FOG — where cold water meets warm

> **This is the mechanism to learn, because it explains both the fog AND the fisheries:**
>
> ```
> WARM current flowing poleward
> (Gulf Stream / Kuroshio)
> ╲
> ╲ THEY MEET
> ╱
> COLD current flowing equatorward
> (Labrador / Oyashio)
> ↓
> Warm MOIST air over the warm current
> drifts across the COLD water
> ↓
> It is chilled BELOW ITS DEW POINT
> ↓
> ADVECTION FOG — dense, persistent, and
> exactly why these coasts are the foggiest
> in the world
> ```
>
> **The Grand Banks off Newfoundland are among the foggiest places on Earth for precisely this reason.** **So is the sea off Hokkaido.**

| Meeting point | Currents | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **The Grand Banks, Newfoundland** | **Labrador (cold) × Gulf Stream (warm)** | **Fog + the world's most famous fishery** |
| **Off Hokkaido & the Kuriles** | **Oyashio (cold) × Kuroshio (warm)** | **Fog + the NW Pacific fishery** |

## 5️⃣ 🌳 VEGETATION — mixed forest

**A transition between the [taiga](09_Siberian_Type_Taiga.md) to the north and the deciduous forest to the south — so it contains both.**

| Zone | Species |
|---|---|
| **Northern part** | Conifers — spruce, fir, pine, hemlock |
| **Southern part** | **Deciduous hardwoods — MAPLE, oak, birch, beech, hickory** |
| **Mixed belt** | Both together — **the classic "mixed forest"** |

**The MAPLE is the signature tree** — **Canada's national emblem, the source of MAPLE SYRUP (tapped from the spring sap rise), and responsible for the New England "fall colours" that draw millions of tourists.** *A vegetation type that is itself a tourist industry.*

---

## 6️⃣ 🪨 SOIL

**Podzolic and thin, glacially scoured across much of the Canadian Shield — bare rock, boulder clay, and countless lakes rather than good farmland.** **Better brown-earth soils in the southern valleys — the St Lawrence lowlands and the Manchurian plain, which IS genuinely fertile.**

## 7️⃣ 👥 HUMAN ACTIVITY

### ⭐ FISHING — the defining activity

> **WHY THE WORLD'S GREAT FISHERIES ARE HERE — five reasons. This is a stock question and the full list is the answer:**
>
> | # | Reason |
> |---|---|
> | 1 | **BROAD, SHALLOW CONTINENTAL SHELF** — the Grand Banks are submerged plateaus. **Sunlight reaches the seabed, so plankton grows abundantly** |
> | 2 | **MIXING OF WARM AND COLD CURRENTS** — **stirs nutrients up from below into the sunlit layer.** Plankton needs light *and* nutrients, and mixing is what delivers both to the same place |
> | 3 | **COOL WATER HOLDS MORE DISSOLVED OXYGEN** than warm water |
> | 4 | **INDENTED, ISLAND-STREWN COASTS** — sheltered natural harbours everywhere |
> | 5 | **POOR FARMLAND ONSHORE** — *the glaciated Shield gave coastal populations no alternative but the sea. A push factor, not just a pull* |
>
> **Plus, historically: cold winters allowed natural refrigeration and preservation before mechanical refrigeration existed.**

**Species:** **cod (the historic king of the Grand Banks), herring, mackerel, haddock, halibut, salmon, lobster; and in the NW Pacific — salmon, tuna, pollock, crab.** **Japan is among the world's greatest fishing nations, and this climate's seas are why.**

### Lumbering & pulp

**Softwood + hardwood; Canada is a world leader in newsprint and pulp.** Same logic as the [taiga](09_Siberian_Type_Taiga.md).

### Farming — limited

**Short growing season + poor soils = hay, oats, potatoes, dairying, and fodder.** **The St Lawrence lowland and the Manchurian plain are the exceptions** — Manchuria is a major producer of **soybeans (its historic speciality), maize, spring wheat, kaoliang (sorghum) and sugar beet.**
**Maple syrup** (Quebec produces the large majority of the world's supply). **Fruit** — apples in Nova Scotia and New England.

### Industry & mining

**The northeastern US and southeastern Canadian manufacturing belt** — one of the world's historic industrial cores (coal, iron, the Great Lakes, the St Lawrence Seaway). **Mining:** nickel (Sudbury), iron (Labrador), asbestos, gold. **Hydroelectricity** — Quebec is a major exporter.

## 8️⃣ 🌱 ENVIRONMENT OVERLAY

### ⭐ THE GRAND BANKS COD COLLAPSE — the fisheries case study

> **For five centuries, the Grand Banks were the most productive fishing ground on Earth.** **In 1992 Canada declared a total moratorium on northern cod. Roughly 35,000 people lost their livelihoods overnight in a single province.**

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Post-1950s industrial trawling** — factory ships, sonar, and bottom trawls replaced hook-and-line |
| 2 | **Catches soared, appearing to prove the stock was fine** ⚠️ *— technology masked depletion: fishermen caught more per hour even as fish grew scarcer* |
| 3 | **Scientific advice was overridden for political and employment reasons; quotas were set above sustainable levels year after year** |
| 4 | **Bottom trawling destroyed the seafloor habitat itself**, not just the fish |
| 5 | **Stock collapsed to ~1% of its historic biomass** |
| 6 | **Thirty years on it has still not properly recovered** — *the ecosystem appears to have shifted to a new state dominated by crab and shrimp, in which cod cannot easily re-establish* |

> ⭐ **The four lessons, and they generalise to every renewable-resource question you will ever be asked:**
> 1. **Catch-per-effort is a misleading indicator when technology improves** — the data looked healthy while the stock died.
> 2. **Short-term employment pressure systematically defeats long-term science in resource governance.**
> 3. **Ecosystems can cross thresholds and NOT return** — recovery is not symmetric with collapse.
> 4. **A resource can be renewable and still be destroyed permanently.**
>
> 🎯 **Bring it home: India's marine fisheries face the same pattern** — **declining catch per unit effort, juvenile fishing, bottom trawling conflicts in Tamil Nadu and Kerala, the Palk Bay dispute with Sri Lanka, and monsoon fishing bans as the main regulatory instrument.** **Also the Blue Revolution and PM Matsya Sampada Yojana.** *The Grand Banks is the warning; India is the live case.*

### Other

| 🌱 Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Acid rain** | **The classic North American case** — Midwest US emissions falling on Ontario, Quebec and New England lakes and maple forests. **Addressed by the US Clean Air Act amendments (1990) and the US–Canada Air Quality Agreement.** *Another transboundary success story, alongside Europe's* |
| **Ocean warming** | **The Gulf of Maine is warming faster than ~99% of the world ocean** — lobster and cod ranges shifting poleward, and fisheries management struggling to follow |
| **Icebergs** | **The Labrador Current carries Greenland icebergs south across the shipping lanes** — *this is what sank the Titanic, and why the International Ice Patrol exists* |
| **Bottom trawling** | Habitat destruction; now a major issue in global high-seas governance and the **BBNJ / High Seas Treaty** |
