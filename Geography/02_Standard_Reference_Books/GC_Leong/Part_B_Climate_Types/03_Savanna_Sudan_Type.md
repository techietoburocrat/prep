## 1️⃣ 🗺️ WHERE

⭐ **Between the equatorial forest and the hot desert — roughly 5°–20° N and S.** ⭐ **A transitional belt on both sides of the equatorial zone.**

| Region | 🗺️ Local name |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The Sudan belt**, Africa | ⭐ **The type region** — a continuous band from Senegal east to Ethiopia, south of the Sahara |
| ⭐ **East Africa** | Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda — the great game parks |
| ⭐ **Southern Africa** | Zambia, Zimbabwe, Angola, Botswana's north |
| ⭐ **Orinoco basin**, Venezuela/Colombia | ⭐ **LLANOS** |
| ⭐ **Brazilian Highlands** | ⭐ **CAMPOS** *(also 'cerrado')* |
| ⭐ **Northern Australia** | Inland of the monsoon coast |
| ⭐ **Parts of peninsular India** | Interior Deccan, and the drier Gangetic tracts |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐ **Prelims loves the grassland-name matching. Lock these four apart now:**
>
> | Name | Type | Where |
> |---|---|---|
> | ⭐ **Selvas** | Equatorial **forest** | Amazon |
> | ⭐ **Llanos** | **Tropical** grassland | Orinoco, Venezuela |
> | ⭐ **Campos** | **Tropical** grassland | Brazilian Highlands |
> | ⭐ **Pampas** | ⭐ **TEMPERATE** grassland | Argentina — *[a different climate entirely](06_Steppe_Temperate_Grassland.md)* |
>
> ⚠️ **Pampas is the trap.** Llanos and Campos are savanna; **Pampas is temperate steppe.** All three are in South America and all three sound alike.

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ WHY IT'S THERE — a climate on a swing

```
   ⭐ THE ITCZ MIGRATES WITH THE OVERHEAD SUN

   HIGH-SUN SEASON (local summer)      LOW-SUN SEASON (local winter)
   ─────────────────────────────      ────────────────────────────
   ITCZ swings INTO the belt           ITCZ retreats to the other side
        ↓                                   ↓
   Equatorial low conditions           ⭐ SUBTROPICAL HIGH moves in
        ↓                                   ↓
   Convergence, rising air             Descending, warming, drying air
        ↓                                   ↓
   ⭐ RAINY SEASON                      ⭐ Off-shore TRADE WINDS — dry
                                            ↓
                                       ⭐ PROLONGED DROUGHT
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The savanna is not a climate in its own right so much as a place that experiences TWO climates alternately** — equatorial for part of the year, desert for the rest.
>
> ⭐ **And the consequence follows immediately: the closer you are to the equator, the longer the wet season; the closer to the desert, the longer the drought.** ⭐ **So the savanna is a GRADIENT, not a band** — tall grass and scattered woodland on the equatorial side, thorn scrub grading into desert on the poleward side.
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐ **That gradient is exactly why desertification happens here and nowhere else.** The boundary is climatic, gradual, and mobile — so a run of dry years, or heavy grazing, moves it. **There is no line on the ground for it to defend.**

## 3️⃣ 🌡️ TEMPERATURE

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean annual | ⭐ **~24–27 °C** — hot all year |
| ⭐ **Annual range** | ⭐ **~8–11 °C — larger than equatorial**, because the dry season has clear skies |
| ⭐ **Hottest month** | ⭐ **Just BEFORE the rains break** — clear skies + high sun + no evaporative cooling |
| Coolest month | The middle of the dry season, when nights are clear and radiate heat |
| Diurnal range | ⭐ **Large in the dry season** (clear skies), small in the wet |

> ⭐ **Same principle as the [monsoon](02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md): the pre-rain heat peak.** Once cloud arrives, insolation drops and evaporation absorbs heat. ⭐ **You have now seen this twice — it is a general tropical rule, not an Indian quirk.**

## 4️⃣ 🌧️ RAINFALL

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Annual total | ⭐ **~750–1,500 mm**, declining sharply toward the desert margin |
| ⭐⭐ **Regime** | ⭐ **STRONG SUMMER MAXIMUM · long, absolute dry season (4–8 months)** |
| Type | Convectional, often violent |
| ⭐⭐ **Variability** | ⭐ **HIGH and rising toward the desert edge — the defining hazard** |

⭐ **Rain arrives in heavy convectional downpours** — much of it runs off rather than soaking in, so the *effective* moisture is less than the total suggests.

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The savanna's problem is not the amount of rain; it is the RELIABILITY.**
>
> ⭐ **A place with 600 mm every year can support settled farming. A place averaging 600 mm that delivers 900 mm one year and 250 mm the next cannot.** ⭐ **The savanna margin is the second kind.**
>
> 👉 ⭐ **This is why the traditional adaptation is NOMADIC HERDING rather than cultivation. Animals can be walked to where the rain fell; a field cannot.** *Mobility is a rational response to variance.* **Say this in any Sahel or pastoralism answer.**

## 5️⃣ 🌳 VEGETATION — tall grass with scattered trees

⭐ **"Parkland" or "grassland with scattered trees" — the classic savanna landscape.**

### ⭐ The grasses

⭐ **Tall, coarse, growing in tussocks — up to 3–4 m in the wetter parts.** ⭐ **ELEPHANT GRASS is the type species.** ⭐ **Green and lush in the rains; yellow, dry and inflammable in the drought.** ⭐ **Grasses survive the dry season by dying back above ground and surviving in the roots** — the same strategy as a deciduous tree, applied to a grass.

### ⭐ The trees — drought-resistant, deciduous, thorny

| ⭐ Species | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Acacia** | ⭐ **Umbrella/flat-topped crown**, thorns, small leaves, deep roots. **The signature savanna silhouette** |
| ⭐⭐ **Baobab** | ⭐ **Enormously swollen trunk that stores water**; leafless for much of the year. *"The upside-down tree"* |
| **Eucalyptus** | Australian savanna |
| **Doum palm, shea, locust bean** | Sudan belt |

⭐ **General xerophytic adaptations:** ⭐ **long tap roots to reach deep water · thick, fire-resistant bark · small, waxy or thorn-like leaves to cut transpiration · deciduous habit in the dry season.**

### ⭐⭐ Fire — the savanna's third climate factor

⭐ **Dry-season bush fires, natural and deliberate, sweep the savanna every year.** ⭐ **Fire kills tree seedlings but not established grasses (whose growing points are at or below ground level).**

> ⭐⭐ **So fire actively MAINTAINS the grassland.** Suppress it and woodland encroaches. ⭐ **Many ecologists argue much of the savanna is a FIRE-CLIMAX community, not a purely climatic one — i.e. it is partly man-made, sustained by millennia of deliberate burning.** 🎯 *A genuinely sophisticated point for a vegetation answer.*

### ⭐ The gradient, stated as vegetation

```
   EQUATOR SIDE                              DESERT SIDE
   ────────────                              ───────────
   Tall grass + more trees  →  shorter grass, thornier trees
                            →  thorn scrub
                            →  ⭐ SEMI-DESERT  →  desert
```

## 6️⃣ 🪨 SOIL

⭐ **Reddish, lateritic in the wetter parts; thin and easily eroded once the grass cover is lost.** ⭐ **Low humus, because the dry season and the fires destroy litter before it can rot in.** ⭐ **Highly vulnerable to sheet and gully erosion in the violent first rains after a bare dry season.**

## 7️⃣ 👥 HUMAN ACTIVITY

### ⭐⭐ Wildlife — the greatest concentration of large grazing animals on Earth

⭐ **Grazers:** zebra, giraffe, wildebeest, antelope, gazelle, buffalo, elephant, rhino.
⭐ **Carnivores:** lion, leopard, cheetah, hyena, jackal.
⭐ **Scavengers:** vulture.
⭐ **The abundant grass supports huge herbivore herds, which support the world's densest large-predator populations.** ⭐ **Hence "big game country"** — Serengeti, Masai Mara, Kruger.

### ⭐⭐ Two ways to live here — the classic Leong contrast

| | ⭐ **THE MASAI** | ⭐ **THE HAUSA** |
|---|---|---|
| Where | ⭐ Kenya & Tanzania (East Africa) | ⭐ Northern Nigeria (West Africa) |
| Way of life | ⭐⭐ **Pastoral nomads** | ⭐⭐ **Settled cultivators** |
| Wealth measured in | ⭐ **Cattle — as prestige, not as meat** | Land and crops |
| Diet | Milk, blood, occasional meat | Millet, sorghum, vegetables |
| Movement | Seasonal, following pasture and water | Sedentary villages |
| Adaptation logic | ⭐ **Mobility beats rainfall variance** | ⭐ **Where rain is more reliable, settle** |

> ⭐ **Also:** the ⭐ **Fulani** (West African cattle herders, often in symbiosis with Hausa farmers — manure for stubble), the ⭐ **Bushmen/San** on the Kalahari margin (hunter-gatherers), and shifting cultivators on the wetter side.
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The Fulani–Hausa relationship is worth knowing because its BREAKDOWN is current affairs** — herder-farmer conflict across the Sahel, intensified by desertification pushing herders south into farmland. **A textbook example of climate stress becoming security conflict. Usable in GS-I, GS-II and GS-III.**

### ⭐ Crops

⭐ **Millet · sorghum (guinea corn) · groundnut · cotton · maize · sisal (Tanzania) · cassava.** ⭐ **Drought-resistant cereals dominate — millets are the savanna's rice.**

### ⚠️ Problems of development

⭐ **Unreliable rainfall · poor, easily eroded soil · bush fires · locust swarms · and above all the ⭐⭐ TSETSE FLY.**

> ⭐⭐ **The tsetse fly deserves its own line, because it is a genuine climatic-geographical constraint on an entire continent.**
>
> ⭐ **The tsetse transmits *nagana* to cattle and *sleeping sickness* (trypanosomiasis) to humans.** ⭐ **It infests some 10 million km² of Africa's wet savanna and woodland.**
>
> 👉 ⭐ **Where the tsetse lives, cattle cannot — so mixed farming (animals + crops + manure + draught power) was impossible over huge areas of Africa.** ⭐ **This is one of the most-cited physical explanations for the historical divergence of African and Eurasian agriculture.** *A first-rate example of physical geography determining economic geography.*

## 8️⃣ 🌱 ENVIRONMENT OVERLAY — ⭐⭐ the most important in Part B

### ⭐⭐⭐ DESERTIFICATION and the SAHEL

⭐ **The Sahel is the semi-arid savanna belt immediately south of the Sahara** — Senegal, Mauritania, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Chad, Sudan.

| Driver | ⭐ Mechanism |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Rainfall decline & variability** | Prolonged droughts, notably **1968–73 and the 1980s**, killing hundreds of thousands |
| ⭐⭐ **Overgrazing** | Herd sizes grow in good years; the drought then arrives with too many animals on the land |
| ⭐ **Overcultivation** | Fallow periods shortened under population pressure; soil never recovers |
| ⭐ **Deforestation for fuelwood** | The primary energy source; removes the last soil-binding cover |
| ⭐⭐ **The albedo feedback** | ⭐ **Bare ground reflects more radiation than vegetation → less surface heating → less convection → LESS RAIN.** ⭐ **Degradation reinforces the drought that caused it.** *This is the mechanism worth naming — most answers stop at "overgrazing"* |
| ⭐ **Borehole effect** | ⭐ Wells drilled to help herders concentrate cattle around them, stripping the land bare in rings. ⭐ *Well-intentioned intervention, perverse outcome — an excellent governance point* |

### ⭐ Responses

⭐ **UNCCD** (UN Convention to Combat Desertification — ⭐ **India hosted COP-14 in 2019, New Delhi Declaration**) · ⭐⭐ **The Great Green Wall** (an 8,000 km African belt of restoration from Senegal to Djibouti) · agroforestry & ⭐ **farmer-managed natural regeneration** (the low-cost method that has actually worked in Niger) · rotational grazing · fuel-efficient stoves.

> ⭐⭐ **India's own savanna-margin story:** ⭐ **~30% of India's land is degraded**; the ⭐ **Aravallis** function as the barrier holding the Thar back from the Gangetic plain, and their illegal quarrying is a live issue. ⭐ **India has pledged to restore 26 million hectares by 2030.** *Always bring a global topic home — it is what separates a 7/10 answer from a 5/10.*

### 🌱 Other

⭐ **Wildlife conservation & poaching** (ivory, rhino horn, CITES) · **human–wildlife conflict** · **protected-area management vs pastoralist eviction** (the Masai and the Serengeti — a real justice tension worth acknowledging) · **bush fires and carbon** · **locust outbreaks** (2019–20 East Africa & into Rajasthan/Gujarat).
