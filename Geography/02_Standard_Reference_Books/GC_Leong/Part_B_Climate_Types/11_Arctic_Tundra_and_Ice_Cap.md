# ❄️ Climate 11 — The Arctic / Polar: Tundra & Ice Cap `ET` / `EF`

> **The one-line point:** ⭐⭐ **A desert that looks like a swamp. It receives almost no precipitation, but nothing evaporates and nothing drains through the frozen ground — so the driest climate on Earth after the hot deserts spends its summer as a bog.**

## 1️⃣ 🗺️ WHERE

⭐ **Poleward of roughly 66.5°N (the Arctic Circle), though the boundary is really the TREELINE, not a parallel.**

| Region | 🗺️ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Arctic Canada & Alaska's north slope** | The largest tundra expanse |
| ⭐ **Greenland's coastal fringe** | ⚠️ *the interior is ice cap, `EF`* |
| ⭐ **Iceland, Svalbard** | |
| ⭐ **Northern Scandinavia — LAPLAND** | Norway, Sweden, Finland |
| ⭐ **The Siberian Arctic coast** | Russia's northern rim |
| ⭐ **ICE CAP `EF`** | ⭐ **Antarctica and interior Greenland — permanent ice, no vegetation at all** |

> ⭐⭐ **Almost absent from the Southern Hemisphere**, and for the familiar reason: ⭐ **there is no land at 60°–70°S except a few islands.** ⭐ **The Southern Hemisphere skips straight from ocean to the Antarctic ice cap — it has `EF` in abundance and almost no `ET`.**

> ⭐ **The proper boundary is the TREELINE.** ⭐ **South of it, trees; north of it, none. And the treeline corresponds closely to the ⭐ 10 °C isotherm of the warmest month** — ⭐ **below that, trees cannot complete a growth cycle.** 🎯 *That correspondence between a temperature threshold and a visible line on the ground is Köppen's whole method in miniature — worth saying.*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ WHY IT'S THERE

```
   ⭐ VERY HIGH LATITUDE
        ↓
   ⭐ Sun's rays strike at an extremely oblique angle
     → energy spread over a large area
     → and passes through more atmosphere
        ↓
   ⭐⭐ POLAR NIGHT in winter — the sun does not rise
      for weeks or months
        ↓
   ⭐⭐ HIGH ALBEDO — snow and ice reflect 80–90% of
      what little insolation does arrive
        ↓
   ⭐ Cold, dense, DESCENDING air → ⭐ POLAR HIGH PRESSURE
        ↓
   ⭐ Descending air cannot rain
   ⭐ Cold air holds almost no moisture anyway
        ↓
   ⭐⭐ VERY LOW PRECIPITATION — a POLAR DESERT
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE ALBEDO TRAP — and this is the mechanism that matters most:**
>
> ⭐ **Snow reflects ~80–90% of incoming sunlight. Open ocean reflects ~6%.**
>
> ⭐ **So a snow-covered surface stays cold because it is white, and stays white because it is cold.** ⭐⭐ **A self-sustaining loop.**
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐ **Break it and it runs the other way with equal force:** ⭐ **melt the ice → expose dark ocean → absorb far more heat → warm faster → melt more ice.** ⭐⭐ **This is ICE–ALBEDO FEEDBACK, and it is the primary reason the Arctic is warming three to four times faster than the global average.** 🎯 **Learn this properly — it is the highest-value climate mechanism in Part B alongside [permafrost thaw](09_Siberian_Type_Taiga.md).**

## 3️⃣ 🌡️ TEMPERATURE

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Warmest month** | ⭐⭐ **BELOW 10 °C — this IS the definition of tundra (`ET`)** |
| ⭐ **Ice cap `EF`** | ⭐ **Warmest month below 0 °C — never thaws** |
| Winter mean | ⭐ **−20 to −40 °C** |
| Summer mean | ⭐ **~5–10 °C, and only for 2–4 months** |
| ⭐ **Growing season** | ⭐⭐ **50–80 days** |
| ⭐ Day length | ⭐⭐ **The MIDNIGHT SUN in summer; the POLAR NIGHT in winter** |
| Wind | ⭐ **Severe — ⭐ BLIZZARDS; nothing stands in the way** |

> ⭐ **The very long summer DAY partly compensates for the very short summer SEASON** — 24 hours of daylight means plants photosynthesise around the clock. ⭐ **This is why the tundra can produce a spectacular flowering in a few weeks: it packs a season's growth into a fortnight of continuous light.**

## 4️⃣ 🌧️ PRECIPITATION — ⭐⭐ the paradox

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Annual total | ⭐⭐ **Under 250 mm — as dry as a desert** |
| Form | ⭐ **Mostly snow** |
| Regime | Slight summer maximum |
| ⭐⭐ **Appearance** | ⭐⭐ **WATERLOGGED, boggy and marshy all summer** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE POLAR DESERT PARADOX — a genuinely good question, and it gets asked:**
>
> **"How can the tundra receive desert-level precipitation and still be a swamp?"**
>
> ⭐ **Three reasons, all necessary:**
> 1. ⭐⭐ **EVAPORATION IS NEGLIGIBLE.** It is far too cold. Water that arrives, stays.
> 2. ⭐⭐ **PERMAFROST BLOCKS DRAINAGE.** ⭐ **The frozen subsoil is impermeable — meltwater cannot sink away.**
> 3. ⭐ **The land is flat**, so there is little run-off either.
>
> 👉 ⭐⭐ **So a tiny water input, unable to evaporate, sink or drain, simply accumulates at the surface.** ⭐ **A desert's precipitation ponded on a frozen floor.**
>
> ⭐ **And this is why the Arctic summer is famous for MOSQUITOES in swarms** — endless shallow standing water is perfect breeding habitat.

### ⭐⭐ PERMAFROST and the ACTIVE LAYER

⭐ **PERMAFROST = ground frozen for two or more consecutive years.** ⭐ **It can be hundreds of metres deep.**

⭐ **THE ACTIVE LAYER is the shallow surface zone (a few centimetres to a couple of metres) that thaws each summer and refreezes each winter.** ⭐⭐ **All tundra life — every root, every burrow, every foundation — exists within the active layer.**

⭐ **Types:** ⭐ **continuous** (far north, unbroken) → ⭐ **discontinuous** → ⭐ **sporadic** (patchy, at the southern margin).
⭐ **Associated features:** ⭐ **patterned ground / stone polygons, pingos (ice-cored mounds), solifluction lobes, thermokarst** (the hummocky, pitted terrain left where ice-rich permafrost thaws and the ground collapses).

## 5️⃣ 🌿 VEGETATION — ⭐ no trees, and everything is low

⭐⭐ **The absence of trees is the tundra's defining biological fact.** ⭐ *"Tundra" comes from a Sámi/Russian word meaning "treeless plain."*

| ⭐ Plant type | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Mosses & LICHENS** | ⭐ **REINDEER MOSS is actually a LICHEN, not a moss** — ⭐ *and it is the keystone winter food of caribou and reindeer* |
| ⭐ **Sedges, cotton grass, rushes** | The bulk of the summer ground cover |
| ⭐ **Dwarf shrubs** | ⭐ **Dwarf willow and dwarf birch — the same genera as forest trees, but PROSTRATE, growing horizontally along the ground** |
| ⭐ **Berry shrubs** | Crowberry, bilberry, cloudberry |
| ⭐ **Cushion plants & brief flowering herbs** | Saxifrage, Arctic poppy |

### ⭐ Adaptations

⭐ **Low, ground-hugging growth** — ⭐ *escapes the wind and exploits the slightly warmer boundary layer near the ground.*
⭐ **Shallow, spreading roots** — ⭐ *permafrost allows nothing deeper.*
⭐ **Perennial habit; rapid flowering and seed-set** — ⭐ *no time for an annual life cycle in 60 days.*
⭐ **Dark pigmentation and hairy stems** — absorb and retain heat.
⭐ **Some plants track the sun** (Arctic poppy), focusing warmth on the flower.

> ⭐⭐ **Short, simple food chains — and this is the ecological point that matters:**
>
> ⭐ **Lichen → caribou → wolf.** That is nearly the whole system.
>
> ⭐ **Few species, few links, no redundancy.** ⭐⭐ **Remove one link and the chain has no alternative route — which is exactly why tundra ecosystems are so FRAGILE and recover so slowly.** ⭐ **A tyre track across tundra can remain visible for decades.** 🎯 *Ecological fragility here is a function of simplicity, not of harshness — a distinction worth making.*

---

## 6️⃣ 🪨 SOIL

⭐ **Thin, immature, acidic, waterlogged, and low in nutrients.** ⭐ **Decomposition is extremely slow in the cold, so ⭐ organic matter ACCUMULATES undecayed as peat** — ⭐ *and that accumulated, frozen organic carbon is precisely the store that [permafrost thaw](09_Siberian_Type_Taiga.md) threatens to release.* ⭐ **Gleysols and cryosols; patterned ground from repeated freeze–thaw.**

---

## 7️⃣ 👥 HUMAN ACTIVITY

⭐ **The most sparsely populated inhabited climate on Earth. Traditionally: hunting, fishing and herding — no agriculture whatsoever.**

### ⭐⭐ The peoples

| ⭐ People | Where | ⭐ Way of life |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **INUIT / Eskimo** | ⭐ **Arctic Canada, Greenland, Alaska** | ⭐ **Hunting and fishing — seal, walrus, caribou, whale, fish.** ⭐ **IGLOO (winter snow-house), skin tent (summer), KAYAK (one-person) and UMIAK (larger, open), harpoon, dog sled, sealskin clothing** *(⭐ "Inuit" is the preferred term; "Eskimo" is Leong's older usage)* |
| ⭐⭐ **LAPPS / SÁMI** | ⭐ **Northern Scandinavia — Lapland** | ⭐⭐ **REINDEER HERDING — semi-nomadic, following the herds between winter forest and summer tundra** |
| ⭐ **Samoyeds, Yakuts, Chukchi, Nenets, Evenki** | ⭐ **Siberian Arctic** | ⭐ **Reindeer herding and hunting** |
| ⭐ **Aleuts** | Aleutian Islands | Marine hunting and fishing |

> ⭐⭐ **The great adaptive divide worth noticing: ⭐ the Inuit HUNT, the Eurasian Arctic peoples HERD.** ⭐ **Reindeer were domesticated in Eurasia and not in North America — so the same climate produced two quite different economies on either side of the Arctic Ocean.** ⭐ *Culture is not simply dictated by environment; environment sets constraints and history chooses within them. **This is the correct, non-deterministic way to phrase any climate-and-culture point.***

### ⭐ The modern Arctic economy

⭐ **OIL & GAS** — ⭐ **Alaska's North Slope/Prudhoe Bay and the Trans-Alaska Pipeline; the West Siberian and Yamal fields; Norwegian and Russian offshore.** ⭐ *The Trans-Alaska pipeline is raised on refrigerated piles so its own warmth won't melt the permafrost and sink it — ⭐ an engineering detail that makes the permafrost problem vivid.*
⭐ **MINING** — nickel, iron, gold, diamonds, rare earths.
⭐ **Fishing** — the Barents and Bering Seas.
⭐ **Strategic & scientific** — military bases, research stations, ⭐ **India's HIMADRI station at Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard (established 2008)**.
⭐ **Tourism** — cruise, aurora, wildlife; growing fast and itself a pressure.

## 8️⃣ 🌱 ENVIRONMENT OVERLAY — ⭐⭐⭐ the densest in Part B

| 🌱 Issue | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **ARCTIC AMPLIFICATION** | ⭐⭐ **The Arctic is warming ~3–4× faster than the global average.** ⭐ **Mechanism: ice–albedo feedback (above), plus a thinner atmospheric layer to warm, plus reduced heat loss as sea ice thins.** ⭐ **THE headline Arctic fact** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Sea-ice loss** | ⭐ **September (summer minimum) extent has declined dramatically since satellite records began in 1979; the old multi-year ice is largely gone.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Note the precision point: melting SEA ice does NOT raise sea level (it is already floating and displacing its own weight). ⭐ Melting LAND ice — Greenland, Antarctica, glaciers — DOES.** 🎯 *Getting this distinction right is a reliable mark and a common error* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Permafrost thaw** | ⭐ **The methane/CO₂ feedback — see [Climate 9](09_Siberian_Type_Taiga.md) for the full chain.** Plus thermokarst, infrastructure collapse, and ⭐ *the re-emergence of long-frozen pathogens* |
| ⭐⭐ **Greenland ice sheet** | ⭐ **Contains enough water for ~7 m of global sea-level rise.** Currently the single largest cryospheric contributor to sea level. ⭐ **Its meltwater is also what threatens the [AMOC](08_British_Type_Cool_Temperate_Western_Margin.md)** |
| ⭐⭐ **POPs & biomagnification** | ⭐⭐ **Persistent Organic Pollutants — PCBs, DDT — released in industrial mid-latitudes, travel poleward on air and ocean currents, and CONCENTRATE up the Arctic's short fat-rich food chains.** ⭐ **Inuit mothers' milk carries some of the highest POP burdens measured anywhere on Earth, despite the Arctic having no industry.** ⭐ **The ⭐ STOCKHOLM CONVENTION (2001) exists largely because of this finding.** 🎯 ⭐⭐ *A superb environmental-justice example: the people who emitted nothing bear the highest exposure. Use it whenever a question touches equity, transboundary pollution, or the polluter-pays principle* |
| ⭐⭐ **Polar bear** | ⭐ **The indicator species — dependent on sea ice as a hunting platform for seals; as the ice season shortens, the hunting season shortens** |
| ⭐⭐ **Geopolitics** | ⭐ **The ARCTIC COUNCIL (8 member states, plus observers). ⭐ INDIA IS AN OBSERVER (since 2013) and released its ⭐ ARCTIC POLICY in 2022.** ⭐ **India's stakes: the Himadri research station, monsoon–Arctic teleconnections, sea-level rise on a 7,500 km coastline, and shipping-route economics** |
| ⭐⭐ **Northern Sea Route** | ⭐ **Retreating ice is opening an Arctic passage that cuts the Europe–Asia voyage by ~40% versus Suez.** ⭐ **Economic opportunity, ecological risk (spills in an ecosystem with no clean-up capacity), and a Russia–China–NATO strategic contest** |
| ⭐ **Resource scramble** | ⭐ **An estimated ~13% of undiscovered oil and ~30% of undiscovered gas.** ⭐ **Overlapping continental-shelf claims under UNCLOS — Russia, Canada, Denmark and the Lomonosov Ridge** |
| ⭐ **Indigenous rights** | Displacement, herding disrupted by thaw and industry, food security, ⭐ *and Arctic peoples' formal seat at the Arctic Council as Permanent Participants* |

## 9️⃣ 📝 `[P]` / `[M]` / 🎯

### `[P]` PRELIMS

- ⭐⭐ **`ET` = warmest month BELOW 10 °C · `EF` = warmest month below 0 °C (ice cap)**
- ⭐⭐ **The TREELINE ≈ the 10 °C isotherm of the warmest month** — the real boundary
- ⭐⭐ **A POLAR DESERT — under 250 mm — yet waterlogged.** Three reasons: no evaporation, permafrost blocks drainage, flat land
- ⭐⭐ **Permafrost · ACTIVE LAYER · continuous/discontinuous/sporadic · pingo · thermokarst · patterned ground**
- ⭐⭐ **REINDEER MOSS IS A LICHEN**
- ⭐ **Dwarf willow, dwarf birch, cotton grass, saxifrage**
- ⭐ **Inuit = hunters (igloo, kayak, umiak, harpoon) · Sámi/Lapps = reindeer herders · Chukchi, Nenets, Yakuts, Samoyeds, Aleuts**
- ⭐⭐ **Melting SEA ice does not raise sea level; melting LAND ice does**
- ⭐⭐ **India: Arctic Council OBSERVER since 2013 · HIMADRI station, Svalbard, 2008 · Arctic Policy 2022**
