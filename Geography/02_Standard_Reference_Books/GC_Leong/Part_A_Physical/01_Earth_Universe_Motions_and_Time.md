## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ LONGITUDE AND TIME — the arithmetic

⭐ **The Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours.**

```
   ⭐ 360° ÷ 24 hrs = 15° PER HOUR
   ⭐ 15° ÷ 60 min  = 1° PER 4 MINUTES
```

| Rule | ⭐ Statement |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Direction** | ⭐ **Places EAST are AHEAD in time; places WEST are BEHIND** *(the sun rises in the east)* |
| ⭐ **Per degree** | ⭐ **4 minutes** |
| ⭐ **Per 15°** | ⭐ **1 hour** |

### ⭐ Standard Time and India

⭐ **A country uses a STANDARD MERIDIAN so the whole territory keeps one clock.** ⭐ **India's is 82°30′E, passing through Mirzapur (UP).** ⭐ **IST = GMT + 5:30.**

> ⭐⭐ **Why India's offset is a half-hour, which people find odd:** ⭐ **82°30′ × 4 minutes per degree = 330 minutes = 5 hours 30 minutes.** ⭐ **The half-hour isn't arbitrary — it's what 82°30′ arithmetically produces.**

⚠️ ⭐ **India spans ~68°7′E to 97°25′E — about 29° of longitude, i.e. nearly 2 hours of real solar time.** ⭐ **So sunrise in Arunachal is roughly two hours before Gujarat, while both keep the same clock.** 🎯 ⭐ **This is the basis of the recurring "should India have two time zones?" debate — the argument being that the Northeast wastes daylight and burns evening electricity. A live policy question with a purely geographical cause.**

### ⭐ Countries with multiple time zones

⭐ **Russia (11) · USA · Canada · Australia · Brazil.** ⚠️ ⭐ **CHINA spans ~60° of longitude — five zones' worth — but uses a SINGLE time zone (Beijing Time).** ⭐ *A deliberate political choice, and the reason the sun can rise at 10 am in Xinjiang.*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ THE INTERNATIONAL DATE LINE

⭐ **Roughly the 180° meridian, in the Pacific.** ⭐ **Crossing it changes the DATE.**

| Direction | ⭐ Effect |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Crossing WESTWARD** (Asia-bound) | ⭐ **GAIN a day** — advance the date |
| ⭐ **Crossing EASTWARD** (America-bound) | ⭐ **LOSE a day** — go back a date |

> ⭐⭐ **Why it must exist at all:** ⭐ **going east, you add an hour every 15°. Go right round and you've added 24 hours — a whole day — and returned to where you started.** ⭐ **The date line is the seam where that accumulated day is cancelled.** ⭐ *Magellan's surviving crew discovered this in 1522 by returning home a day out and being unable to explain it.*

### ⚠️⭐⭐ Why the line is not straight — a favourite Prelims point

⭐ **The IDL zigzags deliberately, to avoid splitting a country or island group across two dates:**

| Deviation | ⭐ Reason |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Bends EAST around Siberia's Chukchi Peninsula** | Keeps all of Russia on one date |
| ⭐ **Bends WEST around the Aleutian Islands** | Keeps all of Alaska/USA on one date |
| ⭐ **Bends EAST around Kiribati** | ⭐ **Kiribati moved the line in 1995 so its whole territory shared a date** — creating the largest eastward bulge, and making it the first country to see each new day |
| ⭐ **Bends around Fiji, Tonga, Samoa, NZ's Chathams** | Same logic |

## 3️⃣ ⭐ ECLIPSES — and why they don't happen monthly

| | ⭐ **SOLAR eclipse** | ⭐ **LUNAR eclipse** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Order** | ⭐ **Sun — MOON — Earth** | ⭐ **Sun — EARTH — Moon** |
| ⭐ **Moon phase** | ⭐ **NEW moon** | ⭐ **FULL moon** |
| ⭐ **What's shadowed** | The Earth | The Moon |
| Duration | ⭐ **Minutes** | ⭐ **Hours** |
| Visible from | ⭐ **A narrow track only** | ⭐ **The whole night side of Earth** |
| Types | ⭐ **Total · Partial · ANNULAR** (moon too far to cover the sun fully, leaving a "ring of fire") | Total · Partial · Penumbral |

> ⭐⭐ **THE QUESTION THAT GETS ASKED: "Why isn't there an eclipse every month?"**
>
> ⭐ **Because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to the plane of the Earth's orbit (the ecliptic).**
>
> ⭐ **So at most new moons the Moon's shadow passes above or below the Earth, and at most full moons the Moon passes above or below the Earth's shadow.** ⭐⭐ **Eclipses only occur when a new or full moon happens near a NODE — one of the two points where the two orbital planes intersect.**
>
> ⭐ *Without that 5° tilt there would be an eclipse of each kind every single month.* 🎯 *A clean "explain the anomaly" answer.*

⭐ **Also worth knowing: a total solar eclipse is possible only because of a coincidence — ⭐ the Sun is about 400× wider than the Moon and also about 400× further away, so the two appear almost exactly the same size from Earth.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐ GREAT CIRCLES — and why flight paths look wrong

⭐ **A GREAT CIRCLE is any circle on the globe whose plane passes through the Earth's CENTRE, dividing it into two equal halves.**

| ⭐ Is it a great circle? | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The Equator** | ✅ **Yes — the only parallel that is** |
| ⭐ **Every meridian (with its opposite)** | ✅ **Yes** |
| ⭐ **Tropic of Cancer, Arctic Circle, any other parallel** | ❌ **No — these are "small circles"** |

> ⭐⭐ **Why it matters practically: ⭐ the shortest route between any two points on a sphere is the arc of the GREAT CIRCLE joining them.**
>
> ⭐ **This is why a flight from Delhi to New York goes far north over Central Asia and the Arctic rather than in the straight line you'd draw on a wall map.** ⭐ **The wall map is a flat projection; it distorts. The great-circle route only looks like a detour on paper.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **Great-circle logic also explains the strategic value of polar air routes and the shortening of Arctic shipping — link it to the [Northern Sea Route](../Part_B_Climate_Types/11_Arctic_Tundra_and_Ice_Cap.md).**

## 5️⃣ ⭐ Quick reference — the important parallels & their significance

| Parallel | ⭐ Why it matters |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Equator, 0°** | Only parallel that is a great circle; equal day & night all year |
| ⭐ **Tropic of Cancer, 23½°N** | ⭐ **Northernmost overhead sun (21 June).** ⭐ **Passes through 8 Indian states** — *Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram* |
| ⭐ **Tropic of Capricorn, 23½°S** | Southernmost overhead sun (22 Dec) |
| ⭐ **Arctic Circle, 66½°N** | ⭐ **Poleward limit of the midnight sun / polar night** |
| ⭐ **Antarctic Circle, 66½°S** | Same, southern |

⭐ **Tropic of Cancer states, mnemonic:** ⭐ **"Gujarat Rajasthan Madhya Chhattisgarh Jharkhand West Tripura Mizoram"** — 🎯 *asked in Prelims more than once.*
