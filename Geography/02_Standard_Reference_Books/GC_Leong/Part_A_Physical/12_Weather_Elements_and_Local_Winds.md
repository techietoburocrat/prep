## 1️⃣ ⭐ NAMED LOCAL WINDS — the reference list

> **Don't memorise this as a list — learn the three rules, then the list becomes derivable:**
>
> | Rule | Result |
> |---|---|
> | **A wind blowing FROM a desert or hot interior** | 🔥 **HOT and DRY** |
> | **A wind blowing FROM a cold plateau, snowfield or high latitude** | ❄️ **COLD** |
> | **A wind DESCENDING the LEE side of a mountain** | 🔥 **WARM and DRY — the FOEHN EFFECT** *(it lost its moisture rising on the windward side, then warms by compression coming down)* |

### 🔥 HOT / WARM WINDS

| Wind | Region | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **SIROCCO** | Sahara → Mediterranean (Italy, Malta, Sicily) | **Hot, dry, dusty; picks up moisture over the sea; its red dust gives "BLOOD RAIN"** |
| **KHAMSIN** | Egypt | Hot, sand-laden ("fifty," for its ~50-day season) |
| **HARMATTAN** | Sahara → West Africa | **Hot and very dry — but called "THE DOCTOR" in Guinea, because its dryness is a RELIEF from the oppressive coastal humidity.** 🎯 *A lovely counter-intuitive detail and a favourite Prelims item* |
| **SIMOOM** | Arabia, Sahara | Hot, suffocating, sand-laden |
| **LOO** | **North India, Pakistan** | **Hot dry summer afternoon wind; causes heatstroke deaths — the reason NDMA Heat Action Plans exist** |
| **CHINOOK** | **Eastern Rockies (USA/Canada)** | **A foehn wind. Called the "SNOW-EATER" — it can raise temperatures by 20 °C in hours and strip the snow off the Prairies, exposing pasture for livestock.** *A wind with an economic function* |
| **FOEHN** | **The Alps (leeward, Swiss/Austrian valleys)** | **The type example of the effect; ripens grapes and triggers avalanches** |
| **ZONDA** | Argentina (leeward Andes) | Foehn |
| **SANTA ANA** | **Southern California** | **Hot, extremely dry, offshore — the wind that drives California's worst WILDFIRES** |
| **BERG** | South Africa | Hot, dry, offshore |
| **BRICKFIELDER** | Southern Australia | Hot, dry, dusty |
| **Nor'wester** | New Zealand (leeward Southern Alps) | Foehn over the Canterbury Plains |

### ❄️ COLD WINDS

| Wind | Region | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **MISTRAL** | **The Rhône valley, southern France** | **Cold, dry, violent — funnelled between the Alps and the Massif Central.** *Damages crops; Provençal farmhouses are built with their backs to it* |
| **BORA** | Adriatic coast (Croatia, NE Italy) | Cold, squally, off the Balkan plateau |
| **TRAMONTANA** | Spain, Italy | Cold northerly |
| **BISE** | Switzerland, France | Cold, dry northeasterly |
| **BURAN / PURGA** | **Russia & Central Asia / Siberia** | **Cold blizzard wind of the [steppe](../Part_B_Climate_Types/06_Steppe_Temperate_Grassland.md) and [taiga](../Part_B_Climate_Types/09_Siberian_Type_Taiga.md)** |
| **PAMPERO** | Argentina (the Pampas) | Cold squall from the south/southwest |
| **NORTHER / NORTE** | Texas, the Gulf, Mexico | Cold outbreak from the north |
| **LEVANTER** | Strait of Gibraltar | Easterly, humid, brings cloud |
| **Blizzard** | High latitudes generally | Cold + snow + high wind |

### INDIA'S LOCAL WINDS & STORMS — know these cold

| Name | Where | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **LOO** | N India, Apr–Jun | Hot, dry, deadly |
| **KALBAISAKHI / NOR'WESTERS** | **West Bengal, Assam, Bihar, Odisha; Apr–May** | **Violent evening thunderstorms. "Kalbaisakhi" = "the calamity of Baisakh."** **Beneficial to jute, rice and tea despite the damage they do** |
| **MANGO SHOWERS** | **Kerala, coastal Karnataka; Apr–May** | **Pre-monsoon showers that help ripen mangoes** |
| **BLOSSOM / CHERRY BLOSSOM SHOWERS** | **Karnataka (Kodagu)** | **Help the COFFEE blossom set** |
| **BARDOLI CHHEERHA** | Assam | Local name for the Nor'westers |
| **Norwesters of Assam** | Assam | Tea-beneficial |

> 🎯 **The Indian four — Loo, Kalbaisakhi, Mango showers, Blossom showers — appear in Prelims regularly and are trivially learnable. Note that three of the four are BENEFICIAL, which is the angle a question may test.**

### Diurnal local circulations

**LAND & SEA BREEZE** — **sea breeze by DAY** (land heats faster → low pressure over land → air flows in from the sea); **land breeze by NIGHT** (reversed). *The reason coastal afternoons are cooler than they should be.*
**MOUNTAIN & VALLEY BREEZE** — **ANABATIC (up-valley) by day** as slopes heat; **KATABATIC (down-slope) by night** as dense cold air drains downhill. **Katabatic drainage pools cold air in valley floors — causing valley frost and morning fog, and the reason orchards are planted on slopes rather than valley bottoms.**

## 2️⃣ TYPES OF RAINFALL — with named examples

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **CONVECTIONAL** | **Surface heating → air rises → cools → condenses.** Afternoon peak, short, violent, thundery | **The [equatorial](../Part_B_Climate_Types/01_Equatorial_Hot_Wet.md) daily storm; summer afternoon storms in the [steppe](../Part_B_Climate_Types/06_Steppe_Temperate_Grassland.md)** |
| **OROGRAPHIC / RELIEF** | **Air forced up over a barrier → cools → rains on the WINDWARD side.** **Descending on the leeward side it warms and dries → RAIN SHADOW** | **Western Ghats (Mahabaleshwar wet, Pune dry) · Meghalaya (Mawsynram) · the Himalaya** |
| **CYCLONIC / FRONTAL** | **Warm air forced to rise over cold air at a FRONT.** Steady, prolonged, over a wide area | **[NW Europe](../Part_B_Climate_Types/08_British_Type_Cool_Temperate_Western_Margin.md) · the [Mediterranean](../Part_B_Climate_Types/05_Mediterranean.md) in winter · India's western disturbances** |

> **The RAIN SHADOW is the highest-value item here because it explains so much of India.** **Pune sits ~100 km east of Mahabaleshwar and gets roughly a quarter of its rain. The whole Deccan interior's semi-aridity is a rain-shadow effect of the Western Ghats.** 🎯 *Combine with the [Aravalli-parallel point](../Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md) and you can explain the entire rainfall map of peninsular and northwestern India from two principles.*

## 3️⃣ FOG TYPES — a small, cheap Prelims topic

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **RADIATION fog** | **Ground cools by radiation on a clear, calm night → chills the air above it below its dew point** | **North India's winter fog — the Indo-Gangetic plain's December–January blanket** |
| **ADVECTION fog** | **WARM MOIST AIR moves horizontally over a COLD surface** | **The [Grand Banks](../Part_B_Climate_Types/10_Laurentian_Type_Cool_Temperate_Eastern_Margin.md) · off Hokkaido · San Francisco · the Namib and Atacama coasts** |
| **Frontal fog** | At the boundary of warm and cold air masses | Temperate cyclones |
| **Upslope fog** | Air cooled as it is forced up a slope | Hill stations |
| **Steam fog / Arctic sea smoke** | Cold air over much warmer water | High latitudes |

**Condensation forms generally:** **dew · frost · fog · mist · haze · cloud.** **Dew forms when the dew point is ABOVE freezing; frost when it is BELOW.**

## 4️⃣ TEMPERATURE INVERSION — and why Delhi's air is worse in winter

**Normally temperature FALLS with height. An INVERSION is when a layer of warmer air sits ON TOP of colder air, reversing that.**

```
   Clear, calm, long WINTER NIGHT
              ↓
   The ground radiates heat away rapidly
              ↓
   The air TOUCHING the ground becomes colder than
     the air above it
              ↓
   INVERSION — cold dense air trapped BELOW warm air
              ↓
   Cold air cannot rise through warm air.
      VERTICAL MIXING STOPS.
              ↓
   Everything emitted at ground level — vehicle exhaust,
      industrial emissions, stubble-burning smoke, dust —
      is TRAPPED in a shallow layer and ACCUMULATES
              ↓
   SMOG
```

> ⭐ **This is why Delhi's air quality collapses in winter and not in summer, and it is a genuinely important thing to be able to say.**
>
> **The EMISSIONS are not dramatically higher in December than in July.** **What changes is the ATMOSPHERE'S CAPACITY TO DISPERSE THEM.** **In summer, strong convection and higher winds mix pollutants through a deep boundary layer. In winter, inversion collapses that layer to a few hundred metres and the wind drops.** **The same emissions in a tenth of the volume produce ten times the concentration.**
>
> **Add the geography: Delhi sits in a plain hemmed by the Himalaya to the north, so pollutants cannot ventilate out — and stubble burning in Punjab and Haryana coincides exactly with the onset of inversion season.**
>
> 🎯 **Meteorology, agriculture and urban policy meeting in one paragraph. Deploy this whenever air quality comes up in GS-I or GS-III — most answers list sources and never mention why the season matters.**

**Other inversion types:** **valley/katabatic inversion** (cold air draining into a hollow — *the reason hill-station valley floors are colder than the slopes above, and why orchards are planted on slopes*) · **subsidence inversion** (descending air in a high-pressure system — *the mechanism that keeps [cold-current desert coasts](../Part_B_Climate_Types/04_Hot_and_Mid_Latitude_Deserts.md) foggy but rainless*).

## 5️⃣ INSTRUMENTS — a quick Prelims sweep

| Instrument | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Barometer** (mercury / aneroid) | Atmospheric pressure |
| **Thermometer**; max–min thermometer | Temperature |
| **Hygrometer / psychrometer** | Humidity |
| **Anemometer** | Wind speed |
| **Wind vane** | Wind direction |
| **Rain gauge** | Precipitation |
| **STEVENSON SCREEN** | **The louvred white box that houses thermometers — shields them from direct sun and rain while allowing free airflow, so readings are comparable everywhere** |
| **Beaufort Scale** | **Wind force, 0 (calm) to 12 (hurricane)** — *by observed effect, not instrument* |

## 6️⃣ 🌱 ENVIRONMENT OVERLAY

| 🌱 Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Winter smog & inversion** | **Delhi-NCR; stubble burning; the Commission for Air Quality Management; GRAP (the Graded Response Action Plan); the National Clean Air Programme.** *And the meteorological point above — it is what most answers miss* |
| **URBAN HEAT ISLAND** | **Cities are several degrees warmer than their surroundings: concrete and asphalt absorb and store heat, there is little vegetation or evaporative cooling, buildings trap radiation in "urban canyons," and vehicles and air-conditioners add waste heat.** **Remedies: urban greening, water bodies, cool/reflective roofs, ventilation corridors** |
| **HEAT WAVES** | **The Loo and pre-monsoon mortality; NDMA Heat Action Plans — Ahmedabad's (2013) was South Asia's first and is the model.** **Wet-bulb temperature is the metric that actually matters for human survivability** |
| **Fog & transport** | Winter fog disrupts aviation and rail across north India; a real economic cost |
| **Aerosols & the monsoon** | **Atmospheric brown clouds may be weakening monsoon circulation by dimming the surface and reducing the land–sea thermal contrast** — *an air-pollution problem that becomes a water-availability problem* |
| **Black carbon** | **Soot on Himalayan snow lowers albedo and accelerates melt.** *See [A6](06_Glacial_Landforms.md)* |
