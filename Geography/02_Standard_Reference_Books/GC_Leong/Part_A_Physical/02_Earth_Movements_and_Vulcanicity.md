## 1️⃣ ⭐ INTRUSIVE IGNEOUS LANDFORMS — the biggest gap NCERT leaves

**When magma cools BELOW the surface it forms intrusive (plutonic) bodies. Later erosion exposes them.** **Leong classifies them by shape and by their relationship to the surrounding rock beds — and this classification is a repeat Prelims topic.**

```
        INTRUSIVE IGNEOUS FORMS

   ~~~~~~~~~ surface ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
     ═════╤═════════════╤══════════════  bedding planes
          │ DYKE      │
     ═════│═════   SILL ══════════════
          │      ▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄
     ═════│═══ ▟ LACCOLITH ▙ ═════════   (dome-shaped, arches
          │    ▀▀▀▀▀▀▀▀▀▀▀▀                  the beds UPWARD)
     ═════│═════  ╲▁▁▁▁▁▁╱ ═══════════
          │        LOPOLITH             (saucer/basin-shaped,
          │                                  sags DOWNWARD)
        ▟▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▙
       ▟▓▓▓ BATHOLITH ▓▓▓▙            (enormous, deep-seated,
      ▟▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▙              no visible floor)
```

| Form | Shape & orientation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **BATHOLITH** | **Enormous dome-shaped mass, deep-seated, no known base.** The solidified root of a magma chamber | **The core of many fold mountains — the Sierra Nevada, and much of the Chota Nagpur plateau's granite** |
| **LACCOLITH** | **DOME-shaped, flat-floored, arches the overlying beds UPWARD.** Fed from below by a pipe | **Karnataka's granite domes; Henry Mountains, Utah** |
| **LOPOLITH** | **SAUCER / basin-shaped — sags DOWNWARD** | **The Bushveld Complex, South Africa** |
| **PHACOLITH** | **Lens-shaped, occupying the CREST of an anticline or the TROUGH of a syncline** | Corndon Hill, UK |
| **SILL** | **Sheet-like, injected PARALLEL to (concordant with) the bedding planes — HORIZONTAL** | **Common in the Deccan Traps** |
| **DYKE** | **Sheet-like, CUTTING ACROSS (discordant to) the bedding — VERTICAL or steeply inclined** | **Very common in the Deccan Traps and around the Narmada** |

> ⭐ **The two you must never confuse — and the one that is actually asked:**
>
> **SILL = along the bedding = horizontal sheet.** **DYKE = across the bedding = vertical sheet.**
>
> **Mnemonic: a dyke stands up like a wall (and the word means a wall); a sill lies flat like a window sill.**
>
> **Geomorphological consequence: a dyke of hard rock cutting through soft rock erodes more slowly and is left standing as a WALL-like ridge across the landscape.** **A sill of hard rock produces a flat-topped bench or a WATERFALL where a river crosses it.** *(Both features appear in [A4 River Landforms](04_River_Landforms.md).)*

## 2️⃣ EXTRUSIVE VULCANICITY — volcano types by lava chemistry

> **The organising principle: the SHAPE of a volcano is determined by the VISCOSITY of its lava, and viscosity is determined by SILICA content.** **Learn the principle and the four types follow.**

| Lava type | Silica | Viscosity | Flows | Volcano shape | Eruption |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BASIC (basaltic)** | Low | **Low — runny** | **Far and thin** | **SHIELD volcano — broad, gently sloping** | **Quiet, effusive** |
| **ACID (rhyolitic)** | High | **High — sticky** | **Short, piles up** | **Steep dome / cone; may plug the vent** | **EXPLOSIVE** |

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Shield volcano** | Broad, gentle, basic lava | **Mauna Loa & Kilauea, Hawaii** |
| **Composite / strato-volcano** | **Alternating layers of lava AND ash** — steep, classic cone | **Fujiyama, Vesuvius, Mayon, Etna, Stromboli** |
| **Cinder / ash cone** | Loose pyroclastic material only | Paricutín, Mexico |
| **CALDERA** | **A vast crater formed when the summit COLLAPSES into the emptied magma chamber** — not simply blown off | **Krakatoa (1883), Toba (Sumatra), Crater Lake (Oregon), Lonar** *(⚠️ Lonar is actually a **meteorite** crater — see [A9](09_Lakes.md))* |
| **Volcanic plug / neck** | Solidified lava in the vent, left standing after the cone erodes | Devil's Tower, Wyoming |
| **Lava plateau** | **Fissure eruptions of very fluid basic lava flooding huge areas** | **The DECCAN TRAPS (India) — one of the largest on Earth; Columbia Plateau, USA** |

**Volcanic materials:** lava · **pyroclasts** (bombs, lapilli, ash, **pumice** — so light it floats) · gases (**water vapour dominates**, plus CO₂, SO₂).
**Classification by activity:** **active · dormant · extinct.**

### Minor / geothermal forms

**HOT SPRINGS** · **GEYSERS** (*intermittent jets — **Old Faithful, Yellowstone**; **Iceland — the word "geyser" is Icelandic**; **Rotorua, New Zealand***) · **FUMAROLES** (steam/gas vents) · **SOLFATARA** (sulphurous vents) · **MUD VOLCANOES** (**Baratang, Andaman Islands — India's only ones**).

**India's geothermal springs:** **Manikaran (HP) · Puga & Chumathang (Ladakh) · Tattapani (Chhattisgarh) · Bakreshwar (WB) · Tapoban (Uttarakhand).** 🌱 *Puga and Tattapani are India's leading geothermal-energy prospects.*

### India's volcanoes — a reliable Prelims item

| Volcano | Status |
|---|---|
| **Barren Island**, Andaman | **INDIA'S ONLY ACTIVE VOLCANO** — and the only active one in South Asia |
| **Narcondam Island**, Andaman | **Dormant** |
| **Baratang**, Andaman | **Mud volcano** |
| **Dhinodhar & Dhosi Hills** | Extinct |

## 3️⃣ FOLDING — the classification

**Compressional forces buckle rock strata into folds.**

```
      ANTICLINE = up-fold (arch)      SYNCLINE = down-fold (trough)

         ╱▔▔╲      ╱▔▔╲
        ╱    ╲    ╱    ╲              LIMB = the side of a fold
       ╱      ╲__╱      ╲             AXIS = the line of maximum bending
                ▲
             syncline
```

| Fold type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Symmetrical** | **Both limbs dip equally** — gentle compression |
| **Asymmetrical** | **One limb steeper than the other** |
| **Monocline** | A single one-sided flexure in otherwise horizontal beds |
| **Isoclinal** | Both limbs parallel, dipping the same way |
| **Overfold** | **One limb pushed OVER the other** |
| **Recumbent** | **Folded so far it lies almost HORIZONTAL** |
| **NAPPE / overthrust** | **The fold FRACTURES and a whole sheet of rock is driven forward over the rocks in front of it — sometimes for tens of kilometres** |

> **Why nappes matter to you specifically: the Himalaya are built of enormous nappe structures.** **The Main Central Thrust and the Main Boundary Thrust are exactly this — vast sheets of older rock pushed northward-to-southward over younger rock.** **This is also why Himalayan stratigraphy is "upside down" in places, with older rocks sitting on younger ones.** 🎯 *A precise, uncommon detail for any Himalayan-structure answer.*

**Mountain types by origin:** **FOLD** (Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies — young; Aravalli, Appalachians — old) · **BLOCK** (Vosges, Black Forest, Sierra Nevada, **Satpura**) · **VOLCANIC** (Fuji, Kilimanjaro, Mt Kenya) · **RESIDUAL/relict** (Nilgiris, Parasnath, Rajmahal — what erosion left behind).

## 4️⃣ FAULTING — and the two landforms it makes

**A FAULT is a fracture along which the rocks on either side have MOVED.**

| Fault type | Movement | Force |
|---|---|---|
| **NORMAL** | **One block slides DOWN relative to the other** | **TENSION — pulling apart** |
| **REVERSE / thrust** | **One block pushed UP over the other** | **COMPRESSION — pushing together** |
| **TEAR / STRIKE-SLIP / transform** | **Horizontal sliding past, no vertical movement** | **SHEAR** — **the San Andreas Fault** |

### ⭐ The two great fault landforms

```
   RIFT VALLEY (GRABEN)          BLOCK MOUNTAIN (HORST)

   ▓▓▓│              │▓▓▓             │▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│
   ▓▓▓│   SUBSIDES   │▓▓▓          ───│  UPLIFTED  │───
   ▓▓▓│      ▼       │▓▓▓          ▼  │▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│  ▼
   ▓▓▓│▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁│▓▓▓             │▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│
      ↑ two roughly parallel            ↑ central block stands high
        faults; the CENTRAL               because the flanks dropped,
        block drops                       or it was pushed up
```

| Landform | Examples |
|---|---|
| **RIFT VALLEY / GRABEN** | **The East African Rift Valley — the world's greatest, running ~6,000 km from the Jordan valley through the Red Sea to Mozambique.** **The Rhine rift (between the Vosges and the Black Forest).** **INDIA: the NARMADA and TAPI valleys are rift valleys** — *which is precisely why they flow WEST while nearly every other peninsular river flows east, and why they have no deltas but estuaries instead* |
| **BLOCK MOUNTAIN / HORST** | **The Vosges and the Black Forest** (the flanks of the Rhine graben) · **Sierra Nevada, USA** · **INDIA: the Satpura range** |

> ⭐ **The Narmada–Tapi point is one of the highest-value facts in Indian physiography, because it explains four things at once:**
> **(a) why they flow west** — they follow a pre-existing fault trough; **(b) why they have estuaries not deltas** — a rift valley is a steep, fast trough that carries sediment out to sea rather than depositing it; **(c) why the Satpura lies between them** — it's the horst between two grabens; **(d) why they're seismically notable** — active faults. 🎯 **Deploy this whenever peninsular drainage comes up.** *(See [Cl.11 India Ch.3](../../../01_NCERT/Class_11_India_Physical_Environment/Notes/03_Drainage_System.md).)*

## 5️⃣ EARTHQUAKES — the delta only

**Seismic waves, shadow zones, magnitude and Indian seismic zones are all in [Cl.11 Ch.3](../../../01_NCERT/Class_11_Fundamentals_of_Physical_Geography/Notes/03_Interior_of_the_Earth.md). Only these additions:**

**Focus/hypocentre = where it starts, underground. Epicentre = the point on the surface directly above.**
**Classification by depth: shallow (<70 km) · intermediate (70–300 km) · deep (300–700 km).** **Deep-focus quakes occur only along subduction zones** — *because that is the only place where cold, brittle rock exists at that depth.*
**Secondary effects:** **TSUNAMI · liquefaction · landslides · fire · seiche.**
**The PACIFIC RING OF FIRE** — **~75% of the world's active volcanoes and ~90% of its earthquakes**, following the subduction zones round the Pacific.
**Other belts:** **the Mid-Continental / Alpine–Himalayan belt** (*India's own — Himalaya, Iran, Turkey, Mediterranean*) and **the mid-Atlantic ridge belt.**

| 🌱 Issue | Detail |

| **Earthquake disaster management** | **Explicit GS-III item.** **India: Bhuj 2001, Latur 1993, Uttarkashi, Sikkim 2011.** **Seismic zones II–V; Zone V covers the entire Northeast, Kashmir, western Himachal, Uttarakhand, the Rann of Kachchh and the Andamans.** **NDMA guidelines, retrofitting, building codes and — the real problem — code ENFORCEMENT** |
| **Tsunami** | **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami** → **the Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (INCOIS, Hyderabad), operational 2007** |
| **Volcanic hazard & benefit** | **Ash clouds and aviation; but also volcanic soils are exceptionally fertile — the Deccan black soil and Java's population density are both volcanic gifts.** *Hazard and resource from the same process* |
| **Geothermal energy** | **Iceland (~all its heating), the Philippines, Kenya's Rift Valley.** **India: Puga (Ladakh) and Tattapani (Chhattisgarh) — long-discussed, still barely developed** |
| **Volcanoes & climate** | **Pinatubo 1991 cooled the global average by ~0.5 °C for about two years** via stratospheric sulphate aerosols. *The natural experiment that underpins solar-geoengineering proposals — and the reason to be cautious about them* |
| **Reservoir-induced seismicity** | **Koyna, Maharashtra (1967)** — the standard Indian example, relevant to any large-dam debate |
