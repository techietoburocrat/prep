## 1️⃣ ⭐ TYPES OF GLACIER

| ⭐ Type | ⭐ Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Continental ice sheet** | ⭐ **Vast, covering entire landmasses, burying the relief** | ⭐ **Antarctica, Greenland** |
| ⭐ **Ice cap** | Smaller dome, over a plateau or upland | Iceland's Vatnajökull |
| ⭐⭐ **Valley / Alpine glacier** | ⭐ **Confined to a valley, flowing downhill like a river of ice** | ⭐ **Himalayan and Alpine glaciers** |
| ⭐ **Piedmont glacier** | ⭐ **A valley glacier that spreads out on reaching a plain** | ⭐ **Malaspina, Alaska** |
| ⭐ **Cirque / corrie glacier** | Small, in a mountainside hollow | Widespread |

⭐ **How glaciers erode:** ⭐ **PLUCKING** (meltwater freezes into joints, then the moving ice rips blocks out) and ⭐ **ABRASION** (embedded rock fragments scour the bed — producing ⭐ **striations** and ⭐ **glacial polish**).

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ EROSIONAL LANDFORMS

```
        ⭐ THE HIGH MOUNTAIN GLACIAL ASSEMBLAGE

              ⭐ PYRAMIDAL PEAK / HORN
                      ╱▲╲
           ⭐ ARÊTE ──╱  ╲── ⭐ ARÊTE
                    ╱    ╲
          ⭐ CIRQUE ◡      ◡ ⭐ CIRQUE
           (+ ⭐ TARN lake)
                    ╲    ╱
                     ╲  ╱
              ⭐ U-SHAPED VALLEY (glacial trough)
                 with ⭐ TRUNCATED SPURS
                 and ⭐ HANGING VALLEYS entering high above
```

| ⭐ Landform | ⭐ Description |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **CIRQUE / CORRIE / CWM** | ⭐ **Armchair-shaped hollow at the glacier's head**, with a steep back wall. ⭐ **Often holds a small lake — a TARN — after the ice goes** |
| ⭐⭐ **ARÊTE** | ⭐ **A knife-edged ridge between two adjacent cirques** |
| ⭐⭐ **PYRAMIDAL PEAK / HORN** | ⭐ **Where three or more cirques cut back into the same summit** — ⭐ **the MATTERHORN is the type example** |
| ⭐⭐ **U-SHAPED VALLEY / glacial trough** | ⭐ **The signature glacial landform. Steep sides, flat floor.** ⭐ *Contrast the river's V-shaped valley — ⭐ ice occupies the whole valley width and erodes the sides as well as the bed* |
| ⭐⭐ **HANGING VALLEY** | ⭐ **A tributary valley whose floor is left high above the main valley**, because the smaller tributary glacier eroded less deeply. ⭐ **Waterfalls plunge from them** |
| ⭐ **TRUNCATED SPUR** | ⭐ **Interlocking spurs sliced off square by the rigid glacier** — *ice cannot wind around them the way a river does* |
| ⭐ **ROCHE MOUTONNÉE** | ⭐ **A rock knoll, SMOOTHED and gently sloping on the up-glacier side (abrasion) and STEEP and jagged on the down-glacier side (plucking).** ⭐ *Diagnostic of ice-flow direction* |
| ⭐ **CRAG AND TAIL** | ⭐ **A resistant crag with a tapering ridge of softer material protected in its lee** — ⭐ **Edinburgh Castle rock and the Royal Mile** |
| ⭐⭐ **FJORD** | ⭐⭐ **A glacial trough drowned by the sea — deep, steep-sided, long.** ⭐ **NORWAY (Sognefjord — the longest and deepest), Chile, New Zealand (Milford Sound), Greenland, British Columbia** |
| ⭐ **Rock basin / ribbon lake / finger lake** | Overdeepened stretches of the trough floor, now lakes — ⭐ *the English Lake District, the Finger Lakes (New York)* |

> ⭐⭐ **Fjords are worth a moment because they explain a settlement pattern:** ⭐ **they give Norway a coastline of enormous length with countless deep sheltered harbours, but almost no flat land.** ⭐ **Hence: a maritime, fishing and shipping nation with towns strung along the water and nothing inland.** *Landform → economy, cleanly.*

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ DEPOSITIONAL LANDFORMS — the part NCERT skips

⭐ **All glacial deposit is collectively called DRIFT.** ⭐ **TILL / boulder clay = material dropped directly by the ice — UNSORTED and ANGULAR.** ⭐ **Fluvio-glacial = material carried by meltwater — SORTED and ROUNDED.**

> ⭐⭐ **That sorted/unsorted distinction is the key to the whole table: ⭐ ice dumps everything together indiscriminately; water sorts by size and rounds by attrition.** ⭐ **So if a deposit is sorted, meltwater made it.**

| ⭐ Landform | ⭐ Description |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **MORAINE** | ⭐ **Ridges of till.** ⭐ **LATERAL** (along the glacier's sides) · ⭐ **MEDIAL** (where two glaciers merge, their inner laterals combine) · ⭐ **TERMINAL/end** (at the furthest advance) · ⭐ **RECESSIONAL** (marking pauses during retreat) · ⭐ **GROUND** (spread beneath) |
| ⭐⭐ **DRUMLIN** | ⭐⭐ **A smooth, elongated, egg-shaped hill of till, aligned with ice flow — ⭐ STEEP end facing up-glacier, tapering tail down-glacier** *(the opposite asymmetry to a roche moutonnée)*. ⭐ **Occur in swarms — "BASKET OF EGGS" topography** |
| ⭐⭐ **ESKER** | ⭐⭐ **A long, winding ridge of SORTED sand and gravel — the bed of a river that flowed in a tunnel WITHIN or UNDER the ice, left standing when the ice melted away.** ⭐ *A river deposit shaped like a ridge — which is why it looks so strange* |
| ⭐ **KAME** | ⭐ **An irregular mound of sorted sand and gravel**, deposited against or on top of the ice |
| ⭐ **KETTLE HOLE** | ⭐⭐ **A hollow left where a detached block of ice was buried in drift, then melted, and the ground above it collapsed.** ⭐ **Often water-filled → KETTLE LAKES** |
| ⭐ **OUTWASH PLAIN (sandur)** | ⭐ **A broad sorted apron of sand and gravel spread by meltwater beyond the terminal moraine** |
| ⭐ **ERRATIC** | ⭐ **A boulder of foreign rock carried far from its source and dumped.** ⭐ *Erratics are how geologists first proved the ice ages happened — a granite boulder sitting on limestone had to have been carried there* |
| ⭐ **VARVE** | ⭐ **Annual paired layers (coarse summer / fine winter) in a glacial lake bed** — ⭐ *countable like tree rings, so they date the deposit* |
| ⭐ **LOESS** | ⭐ **Wind-blown glacial rock flour deposited far beyond the ice** — *see [A7](07_Arid_and_Desert_Landforms.md)* |

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ THE HIMALAYAN CRYOSPHERE — India-specific, and heavily examined

| ⭐ Glacier | ⭐ Where | ⭐ Note |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **SIACHEN** | Karakoram, Ladakh | ⭐⭐ **The largest glacier in the Karakoram and the longest in the Indian Himalaya (~76 km).** ⭐ *Also the world's highest battlefield — a glacier with a geopolitics* |
| ⭐⭐ **GANGOTRI** | Uttarakhand | ⭐⭐ **Its snout, GAUMUKH ("cow's mouth"), is the source of the BHAGIRATHI — the headstream of the Ganga.** ⭐ *Retreating measurably* |
| ⭐ **Zemu** | Sikkim | ⭐ **The largest glacier in the Eastern Himalaya** |
| ⭐ **Bara Shigri** | Himachal (Lahaul) | ⭐ **The largest in Himachal Pradesh** |
| ⭐ **Pindari, Milam, Kafni** | Uttarakhand (Kumaon) | Trekking-accessible; well-monitored retreat |
| ⭐ **Baltoro, Biafo, Hispar** | Karakoram | Among the world's longest outside the poles |

> ⭐⭐ **"The THIRD POLE"** — ⭐ **the Hindu Kush–Himalaya holds the largest body of ice outside the polar regions**, and ⭐ **feeds ten major river systems — the Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra, Irrawaddy, Salween, Mekong, Yangtze, Yellow, Amu Darya and Tarim — on which roughly ⭐ 1.9 billion people depend.** 🎯 *That figure is the reason Himalayan glaciers are a water-security question and not merely a scenic one.*

### ⭐⭐⭐ GLOF — Glacial Lake Outburst Flood

> ⭐ **The mechanism, stated properly:**
>
> ```
>    ⭐ Glacier retreats
>          ↓
>    ⭐ Meltwater ponds behind the loose TERMINAL MORAINE
>      the glacier left at its old maximum
>          ↓
>    ⭐⭐ That moraine is an UNCONSOLIDATED dam of
>      ice-cored rubble — it was never engineered
>      and it is not stable
>          ↓
>    ⭐ A trigger: an avalanche or landslide into the lake,
>      an earthquake, the ice core melting, or simple overtopping
>          ↓
>    ⭐⭐ The dam FAILS CATASTROPHICALLY
>          ↓
>    ⭐⭐ A wall of water, rock and debris sweeps down
>      the valley — hitting settlements, roads and
>      hydropower projects far downstream
> ```

| ⭐ Event | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **South Lhonak Lake, Sikkim — October 2023** | ⭐ **India's most severe GLOF.** Destroyed the ⭐ **Teesta-III (Chungthang) hydropower dam**, killed dozens, devastated the Teesta valley |
| ⭐⭐ **Chamoli, Uttarakhand — February 2021** | ⭐ **A rock-and-ice avalanche** (not strictly a GLOF) destroyed the Rishiganga and Tapovan projects; ~200 dead |
| ⭐ **Kedarnath, 2013** | Chorabari lake breach plus extreme rainfall |

⭐ **Mitigation:** ⭐ **inventorying and monitoring high-risk lakes (NDMA has identified several hundred), controlled siphoning/lowering of lake levels, early-warning systems, and — most contentiously — ⭐ hydropower siting policy in GLOF-prone valleys.**

## 5️⃣ 🌱 ENVIRONMENT OVERLAY

| 🌱 Issue | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Glacier retreat** | ⭐ **Most Himalayan glaciers are losing mass; the rate has accelerated since ~2000.** ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Be precise: the notorious IPCC AR4 error claiming Himalayan glaciers would vanish by 2035 was WRONG and was withdrawn. ⭐ Don't repeat exaggerated timelines — the real finding (sustained accelerating mass loss, highly variable between basins) is serious enough and stating it accurately is more impressive than alarmism** |
| ⭐⭐ **The Karakoram Anomaly** | ⭐⭐ **Karakoram glaciers have been stable or even slightly ADVANCING while the rest of the Himalaya retreats.** ⭐ *Probably due to winter westerly precipitation and heavy debris cover.* 🎯 ⭐ **Knowing this exception is a strong signal that you've read actual science rather than a coaching summary** |
| ⭐⭐ **Black carbon** | ⭐⭐ **Soot from cooking fires, diesel and crop burning settles on snow, DARKENS it, LOWERS its albedo and accelerates melting.** ⭐ *An air-pollution problem that becomes a water-security problem — and one India can act on unilaterally, unlike CO₂* |
| ⭐⭐ **Water security** | ⭐ **Glacier melt matters most in the DRY season and in the arid upper basins** — ⭐ *the Indus is far more glacier-dependent than the Ganga, which is monsoon-fed.* ⭐ **So Pakistan and Ladakh are far more exposed than Bengal** |
| ⭐ **Hydropower in glaciated valleys** | The Chamoli and Sikkim disasters both destroyed hydro projects — ⭐ **a live debate about siting large infrastructure in an actively destabilising cryosphere** |
| ⭐ **Sea level** | ⭐ **Mountain glaciers + Greenland + Antarctica are the land-ice contributors.** ⭐ *Recall from [Climate 11](../Part_B_Climate_Types/11_Arctic_Tundra_and_Ice_Cap.md): sea ice melting does NOT raise sea level; this does* |
| ⭐ **Ice stupas** | ⭐ **Ladakh's artificial glaciers (Sonam Wangchuk)** — freeze winter water into cones that melt slowly in spring. ⭐ *A genuinely elegant low-tech adaptation, and a good positive example* |
