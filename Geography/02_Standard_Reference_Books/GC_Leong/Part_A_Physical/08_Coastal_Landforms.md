## 1️⃣ ⭐ THE PROCESSES

⭐ **Waves do the work.** ⭐ **SWASH** = water rushing up the beach; ⭐ **BACKWASH** = water draining back.
⭐ **Constructive waves** (low, long) build beaches — strong swash, weak backwash. ⭐ **Destructive waves** (high, steep, frequent) erode them — weak swash, strong backwash.
⭐ **FETCH** = the distance of open water a wave has crossed. ⭐ **Longer fetch → bigger waves.**

### ⭐⭐ LONGSHORE DRIFT — the process that explains half of coastal geography

```
   ⭐ Waves approach the beach OBLIQUELY (at an angle to the shore)
              ↓
   ⭐ SWASH carries material UP the beach at that angle
              ↓
   ⭐ BACKWASH drags it STRAIGHT DOWN the slope (gravity)
              ↓
   ⭐⭐ Net effect: material ZIGZAGS ALONG the coast
              ↓
   ⭐ This is what builds SPITS, BARS and TOMBOLOS —
     and what strips one beach to feed another
```

> ⭐⭐ **Longshore drift is why coastal engineering so often backfires.** ⭐ **Build a groyne or a harbour breakwater and it traps drifting sediment on the up-drift side — starving the beach DOWN-drift, which then erodes.** ⭐ **You have not stopped the erosion; you have relocated it onto your neighbour.** 🎯 *An excellent point for any coastal-management answer, and directly relevant to India's port-driven coastal erosion.*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ EROSIONAL LANDFORMS

```
   ⭐⭐ THE HEADLAND EROSION SEQUENCE — know this in order

   ⭐ CRACK → ⭐ CAVE → ⭐ ARCH → ⭐ STACK → ⭐ STUMP

   Waves exploit    Cave enlarges,   Roof of arch    Stack is
   a joint in       cuts through     collapses       worn down
   the headland     the headland                     to a stub
```

| ⭐ Landform | ⭐ Description |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **CLIFF** | Steep rock face; retreats as waves undercut it |
| ⭐⭐ **WAVE-CUT NOTCH** | ⭐ **The undercut groove at the cliff base**, at high-tide level — the cliff eventually collapses into it |
| ⭐⭐ **WAVE-CUT / SHORE PLATFORM** | ⭐ **The gently sloping rock bench left as the cliff retreats**, exposed at low tide |
| ⭐⭐ **CAVE → ARCH → STACK → STUMP** | The headland sequence above |
| ⭐ **BLOWHOLE / gloup** | ⭐ **A vertical shaft from a cave roof to the surface** — water and air spout through it |
| ⭐ **GEO** | A narrow steep-sided inlet where a cave has collapsed along its length |
| ⭐⭐ **HEADLAND & BAY** | ⭐ **Where hard and soft rock alternate along a coast, the soft rock erodes into bays and the hard rock stands out as headlands.** ⭐ *Then wave refraction concentrates energy ON the headlands and disperses it IN the bays — so the coast tends to straighten over time* |

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ DEPOSITIONAL LANDFORMS

| ⭐ Landform | ⭐ Description | ⭐ Example |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **BEACH** | Accumulation of sand/shingle between tide levels | Everywhere |
| ⭐⭐ **SPIT** | ⭐ **A ridge of sand built by longshore drift, ATTACHED to the land at one end and projecting into open water at the other.** ⭐ **Often has a curved/hooked ("recurved") end where waves wrap around it** | Spurn Head (UK) |
| ⭐⭐ **BAR** | ⭐ **A ridge built right ACROSS a bay or inlet, sealing it off** | |
| ⭐⭐ **LAGOON** | ⭐⭐ **The body of water cut off behind a bar or spit** — ⭐ **brackish, shallow** | ⭐⭐ **CHILIKA (Odisha) · PULICAT (AP–TN) · VEMBANAD (Kerala)** |
| ⭐⭐ **TOMBOLO** | ⭐⭐ **A bar or spit that JOINS AN ISLAND TO THE MAINLAND** | ⭐⭐ **Sriharikota is linked in this way; ⭐ Rameswaram/Pamban and the Dhanushkodi spit; Chesil Beach (UK)** |
| ⭐ **BARRIER BEACH / offshore bar** | A ridge lying offshore, parallel to the coast | The US Atlantic barrier islands |
| ⭐ **CUSPATE FORELAND** | Triangular projection where two spits meet | Dungeness (UK) |
| ⭐ **Coastal / mud flats, salt marsh** | Fine sediment in sheltered water | Gulf of Kachchh |

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ SUBMERGENT vs EMERGENT COASTS — the classification that explains India

> ⭐⭐ **A coast records whether the LAND went down (or the sea came up) — SUBMERGENCE — or the LAND went up (or the sea fell) — EMERGENCE.** ⭐ **The two produce completely different coastlines.**

| | ⭐⭐ **SUBMERGENT (coast of submergence)** | ⭐⭐ **EMERGENT (coast of emergence)** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Cause** | ⭐ **Land subsides / sea level rises** | ⭐ **Land uplifts / sea level falls** |
| ⭐ **Character** | ⭐⭐ **Highly INDENTED, irregular, drowned valleys** | ⭐⭐ **STRAIGHT, smooth, gently sloping** |
| ⭐ **Water depth** | ⭐ **Deep close inshore** | ⭐ **Shallow, wide shelf** |
| ⭐ **Landforms** | ⭐⭐ **RIA** (drowned river valley) · ⭐ **FJORD** (drowned glacial trough) · ⭐ **Dalmatian** (drowned ridges parallel to the coast) · drowned estuaries | ⭐ **Raised beach · relict cliff · coastal plain · offshore bars & lagoons** |
| ⭐ **Harbours** | ⭐⭐ **EXCELLENT — deep, sheltered natural harbours** | ⭐⭐ **POOR — shallow, silting; ports need dredging** |
| ⭐ **Examples** | ⭐ **SW England & Brittany (rias) · Norway & Chile (fjords) · Croatia (Dalmatian)** | ⭐ **The US Gulf coast; India's east coast** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ India's two coasts — the comparison that gets asked

| | ⭐ **WEST COAST (Konkan–Malabar)** | ⭐ **EAST COAST (Coromandel–Northern Circars)** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Type** | ⭐⭐ **Largely SUBMERGENT** | ⭐⭐ **Largely EMERGENT** |
| ⭐ **Shape** | ⭐ **Narrow, indented, steep — the Ghats come close to the sea** | ⭐ **Broad, straight, gently sloping** |
| ⭐ **Rivers** | ⭐ **Short, swift; ⭐ ESTUARIES (Narmada, Tapi)** | ⭐ **Long, gentle; ⭐ large DELTAS (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery)** |
| ⭐ **Shelf** | ⭐ **Narrow, deeper inshore** | ⭐ **Broad, shallow** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ports** | ⭐⭐ **Natural deep harbours — Mumbai, Marmagao, Kochi, Mangaluru** | ⭐⭐ **Mostly ARTIFICIAL, requiring dredging — Chennai, Paradip, Kandla-type works.** ⚠️ ⭐ **VISAKHAPATNAM is the notable natural exception on the east coast** |
| ⭐ **Features** | Lagoons/backwaters in Kerala (⭐ **Vembanad**), cliffs, coves | ⭐ **Lagoons (Chilika, Pulicat), sand bars, wide beaches (⭐ Marina, Chennai)** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐ **This table answers a whole family of questions at once — why India's best natural ports are on the west, why the deltas are on the east, why cyclone storm surges are worse on the east (shallow shelf amplifies them), and why the two coasts need different management.** ⭐ **Combine it with the [delta/estuary reasoning in A4](04_River_Landforms.md) and you have a complete Indian coastal answer.**

## 6️⃣ 🌱 ENVIRONMENT OVERLAY

| 🌱 Issue | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Coastal erosion** | ⭐ **~one-third of India's ~7,500 km coastline shows erosion.** ⭐ **Drivers: ports and breakwaters interrupting longshore drift · dams starving the coast of river sediment · sand mining · mangrove loss · sea-level rise.** ⭐⭐ *Note how many of these are UPSTREAM causes — a dam in Maharashtra can erode a beach in Kerala* |
| ⭐⭐ **CRZ — Coastal Regulation Zone** | ⭐ **CRZ Notification (1991, revised 2011 and 2019) divides the coast into CRZ-I to IV and restricts activity.** ⭐ **The 2019 revision relaxed norms for tourism and construction — ⭐ contested as pro-development and anti-fisher.** ⭐ **Coastal Zone Management Plans; the National Centre for Sustainable Coastal Management** |
| ⭐⭐ **Sea-level rise** | ⭐ **Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Kochi and Visakhapatnam are all exposed.** ⭐ **The Sundarbans has already lost inhabited islands (Lohachara, Ghoramara shrinking)** |
| ⭐⭐ **Mangroves** | ⭐⭐ **Storm-surge buffers, nurseries for fish, carbon-rich "blue carbon" stores.** ⭐ **Sundarbans, Bhitarkanika, Pichavaram, Godavari–Krishna, Gulf of Kachchh.** ⭐ *Their loss to aquaculture and construction is what turned some coastal cyclones into disasters* |
| ⭐ **Storm surge** | ⭐ **Worse on the east coast — a shallow shelf and a funnel-shaped bay pile the water higher.** *Connect to [Climate 2](../Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md)* |
| ⭐ **Sand mining** | Beach and dune sand for construction; a large illegal economy |
| ⭐ **Pollution & plastics** | Sewage, industrial effluent, marine plastic; ⭐ *and see the ocean gyres in [A11](11_Oceans_Currents_Tides_Salinity.md)* |
| ⭐ **Blue Economy** | ⭐ **India's Deep Ocean Mission; sustainable fisheries; coastal shipping (Sagarmala)** |
