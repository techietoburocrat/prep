## 1️⃣ THE ONE CHEMICAL REACTION THAT MAKES ALL OF IT

**Karst is unusual among landform systems: it is made by SOLUTION, not by mechanical erosion.**

```
   THE CARBONATION REACTION

   CO₂  +  H₂O   →   H₂CO₃              (rainwater absorbs
   carbon   water     carbonic acid          atmospheric &
   dioxide             — a WEAK acid          soil CO₂)

   CaCO₃      +  H₂CO₃   →   Ca(HCO₃)₂
   calcium         carbonic       calcium bicarbonate
   carbonate       acid           — SOLUBLE, and carried away
   (LIMESTONE)                        in solution
```

> ⭐ **The whole of karst follows from one property: limestone is insoluble in pure water but SOLUBLE in weakly acidic water — and all rainwater is weakly acidic because it dissolves CO₂ on the way down.**
>
> **And the reaction is REVERSIBLE.** **Where the water loses CO₂ (by evaporating, or by dripping into open air in a cave), the calcium carbonate comes back OUT of solution and is redeposited.** **That is exactly how stalactites and stalagmites form. The same equation, run backwards.**
>
> 🎯 **Say this and you have explained both the erosional and the depositional half of karst in one sentence.**

### The five conditions karst needs

| # | Condition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Thick, massive, PURE limestone** | Impurities leave an insoluble residue that clogs the system |
| 2 | **Well-JOINTED and bedded rock** | **Joints and bedding planes are the routes water travels. No joints, no karst** |
| 3 | **The limestone must lie ABOVE the water table** | So water can percolate DOWN through it |
| 4 | **Adequate rainfall** | The solvent |
| 5 | **A warm/humid climate helps** | Speeds chemical reaction, and vegetation raises soil CO₂ |

⚠️ *Karst therefore develops best in warm humid limestone uplands — which is why the type areas are Mediterranean and monsoonal rather than polar.*

## 2️⃣ SURFACE LANDFORMS

```
   SURFACE KARST — from smallest to largest

   LAPIES/KARREN ─→ SWALLOW HOLE ─→ DOLINE ─→ UVALA ─→ POLJE
   (grooved rock)      (water sinks)     (funnel)   (merged)   (huge flat
                                                                 basin)
```

| Landform | Description |
|---|---|
| **LAPIES / KARREN** | **Solution grooves and ridges on bare limestone**, following the joints. **Where blocks are separated: the flat blocks are CLINTS and the widened joints between them are GRIKES — together, a "limestone pavement"** |
| **SWALLOW HOLE / SINKHOLE / SINK** | **The opening where a surface stream disappears underground** |
| **DOLINE** | **A funnel- or saucer-shaped closed depression**, from solution or from roof collapse. The basic karst hollow |
| **UVALA** | **Several dolines merged into one larger, irregular depression** |
| **POLJE** | **A very large, flat-floored, steep-sided elongated basin — often kilometres across, sometimes farmed or seasonally flooded.** *The Slovenian/Croatian karst's most striking feature* |
| **DRY VALLEY** | **A valley with no river in it — the drainage has gone underground.** *Diagnostic of karst* |
| **KARST WINDOW** | A collapsed section revealing the underground river below |
| **NATURAL BRIDGE / ARCH** | **A remnant of a cave roof left spanning the gap after the rest collapsed** |
| **HUM / MOGOTE — TOWER KARST** | **Isolated steep residual limestone towers left standing when everything around them dissolved away.** **The spectacular landscape of GUILIN and Yangshuo (China), Ha Long Bay (Vietnam), and Phang Nga Bay (Thailand)** |

> **The single most diagnostic feature of a karst region is the ABSENCE of surface drainage.** **A limestone upland in a wet climate with no rivers on it is karst — the water is all underground.** *That absence is why karst areas are often dry, infertile and thinly populated despite plentiful rainfall — a nice environment-and-livelihood link.*

## 3️⃣ UNDERGROUND LANDFORMS

| Landform | Description |
|---|---|
| **CAVERN / CAVE** | **Solution chambers along joints and bedding planes; can form huge galleries** |
| **STALACTITE** | **Hangs DOWN from the ceiling.** Grows as CaCO₃ is deposited at the drip point |
| **STALAGMITE** | **Grows UP from the floor**, where the drip lands. **Thicker and blunter than a stalactite** |
| **PILLAR / COLUMN** | **A stalactite and stalagmite that have met and joined** |
| **Helictite** | Twisted, gravity-defying deposits |
| **Flowstone / curtain / drapery** | Sheet deposits on cave walls |
| **Underground river** | The swallowed surface stream, flowing through the cave system |
| **RESURGENCE / spring** | **Where the underground river re-emerges at the surface**, usually at the base of the limestone where an impermeable layer stops it |

> **The classic memory aid, and it works:** **stalac**T**ite has a T for "**T**op" · stalag**M**ite has an M for "**M**ound/ground." *Or: tights come down, mites go up.*

**Speleothem** = the general term for any cave deposit. **Speleology** = the study/exploration of caves. 🌱 *Speleothems are also valuable palaeoclimate archives — their growth layers record past rainfall, like tree rings.*

| **BORRA CAVES** | **Andhra Pradesh** (Araku valley, Ananthagiri hills) | **Among India's largest; on the Gosthani river** |

| **Meghalaya caves** — **Krem Liat Prah**, Mawsmai, Krem Puri | **Meghalaya** (Jaintia & Khasi Hills) | **India's longest cave system is Krem Liat Prah. Meghalaya holds the great majority of India's known caves — high rainfall on thick limestone** |

**India's limestone belts:** the Vindhyan and Cuddapah basins, the Meghalaya plateau, parts of the Himalaya, and the Bastar region.

## 5️⃣ 🌱 ENVIRONMENT OVERLAY — karst has real, distinctive environmental problems

| 🌱 Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Aquifer vulnerability** | **Karst aquifers are the most easily contaminated groundwater on Earth.** **Why: water enters through open swallow holes and travels fast through open conduits, so it gets NO natural filtration — unlike a sand or gravel aquifer where slow percolation cleans it.** **A pollutant dumped in a sinkhole can appear at a spring kilometres away within hours.** 🎯 *A precise, uncommon point for any groundwater-contamination question* |
| **Sinkhole collapse** | **Ground subsidence swallowing roads and buildings** — *often triggered by groundwater over-pumping, which removes the water that was buoying up the cavity roof* |
| **Limestone quarrying** | **Limestone is the raw material of CEMENT, and India is the world's second-largest cement producer.** **So karst regions face intense quarrying pressure — Meghalaya's caves, the Aravallis, and the Himalayan limestone belts.** *A direct conflict between an industrial input and an irreplaceable landform* |
| **Cave ecosystems** | **Troglobites — cave-adapted species that are blind, unpigmented and often endemic to a single cave.** **Kotumsar's blind fish.** *Extremely vulnerable: an endemic confined to one cave can be extinguished by one quarry* |
| **Bats** | **Caves are critical bat roosts** — pollinators, insect controllers, and guano producers. Disturbance and white-nose-type threats |
| **Tourism pressure** | Lighting alters cave ecology and promotes algae; breath and body heat change humidity; stalactite breakage |
| **Acid rain** | *Accelerates limestone solution — visible on limestone and marble monuments; a link to the Taj Mahal's marble degradation* |
| **Palaeoclimate value** | **Speleothem layers archive past monsoon variability** — *Indian cave records have been used to reconstruct monsoon strength over millennia* |
