## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ MASS MOVEMENT — the taxonomy, by speed

⭐ **MASS MOVEMENT (or mass wasting) is the downslope movement of rock and soil UNDER GRAVITY ALONE — without a transporting agent like a river, glacier or wind.** ⭐ *That last clause is the definition's whole point: gravity does the work directly.*

| ⭐ Type | Speed | ⭐ Description |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **SOIL CREEP** | ⭐ **Imperceptibly slow — mm/year** | ⭐ **The slowest and the most widespread.** ⭐ **Evidence: tilted fence posts and telegraph poles, bent tree trunks curving upslope at the base, terracettes ("sheep tracks") on hillsides** |
| ⭐⭐ **SOLIFLUCTION** | Slow | ⭐⭐ **Saturated soil flowing over FROZEN subsoil.** ⭐ **Characteristic of PERIGLACIAL and [tundra](../Part_B_Climate_Types/11_Arctic_Tundra_and_Ice_Cap.md) regions** — *the active layer thaws, can't drain through the permafrost, and slides* |
| ⭐ **EARTHFLOW / MUDFLOW** | ⭐ **Rapid** | ⭐ **Water-saturated debris flowing.** ⭐ **Lahar = a volcanic mudflow** — *extremely destructive; Armero, Colombia 1985* |
| ⭐⭐ **SLUMPING (rotational slip)** | Rapid | ⭐ **A block rotates backwards along a CURVED (concave) slip plane** — *common in coastal cliffs and river banks; leaves a crescent-shaped scar* |
| ⭐⭐ **LANDSLIDE / debris slide** | ⭐ **Very rapid** | ⭐ **Movement along a broadly PLANAR surface** |
| ⭐ **ROCKFALL** | ⭐ **Instantaneous** | Free fall from a cliff → builds ⭐ **SCREE / TALUS** at the base |
| ⭐ **AVALANCHE** | Very rapid | ⭐ **Snow, or snow + debris** |

> ⭐⭐ **The organising principle worth stating in an answer: mass movement occurs when the DOWNSLOPE FORCE exceeds the RESISTING FORCE.**
>
> ⭐ **Downslope force increases with: steeper slope · added weight (buildings, waterlogging, spoil dumping) · earthquake shaking.**
> ⭐ **Resistance decreases with: water lubricating the slip plane · loss of vegetation and its binding roots · weathering weakening the rock · undercutting at the slope foot (by a road cut or a river).**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **Almost every human-triggered landslide comes down to one of: cutting the toe of a slope, loading the top of it, or removing the vegetation from it.** *Three causes, and they cover the whole of Himalayan landslide policy.*

## 2️⃣ 🌱 ⭐⭐ LANDSLIDES IN INDIA — GS-III disaster management

> ⭐ **This is the reason the file exists. Landslides are an explicit disaster-management topic and India is among the most landslide-prone countries on Earth.**

| Aspect | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Where** | ⭐⭐ **The Himalaya (Uttarakhand, Himachal, Sikkim, the Northeast — especially Arunachal and Mizoram) and the Western Ghats (Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra).** ⭐ **~12–13% of India's land area is landslide-prone** |
| ⭐⭐ **Why the Himalaya** | ⭐ **Young, tectonically active, still rising · steep slopes · highly fractured rock from [thrusting and nappes](02_Earth_Movements_and_Vulcanicity.md) · intense monsoon rainfall · high seismicity** — ⭐ *every single risk factor present simultaneously* |
| ⭐⭐ **Human triggers** | ⭐ **Road and highway cutting (the Char Dham project debate) · hydropower tunnelling and muck disposal · deforestation · unregulated hill-town construction · quarrying · terracing failure** |
| ⭐ **Recent events** | ⭐ **Kerala 2018 & 2019 (Western Ghats, post-Gadgil-report debate) · Wayanad 2024 · Chamoli 2021 (rock-ice avalanche) · Joshimath subsidence 2023 · Sikkim 2023 (GLOF-triggered)** |
| ⭐ **Mitigation** | ⭐ **Hazard zonation mapping (GSI) · slope stabilisation, retaining walls, rock bolting · proper drainage (⭐ *water is the usual trigger, so drainage is the usual fix*) · afforestation and bio-engineering · construction regulation and its enforcement · early warning** |
| ⭐ **Institutions** | ⭐ **GSI is the nodal agency for landslides; NDMA guidelines; the National Landslide Risk Management Strategy (2019)** |

> ⭐⭐ **The Gadgil vs Kasturirangan debate on the Western Ghats is the sharpest policy example available here** — ⭐ **the Gadgil Committee (2011) recommended treating ~64% of the Ghats as ecologically sensitive; the Kasturirangan Committee (2013) cut that to ~37%; neither has been fully implemented; and the landslides have continued.** 🎯 *A rare case where you can name a scientific recommendation, a political dilution, and an observed consequence in one sentence.*

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Two small additions on weathering itself

⭐ **Everything else is in [Cl.11 Ch.6](../../../01_NCERT/Class_11_Fundamentals_of_Physical_Geography/Notes/06_Geomorphic_Processes.md). Only these two are worth adding:**

**⭐ The climate control on weathering type — state it as a rule:**
- ⭐ **Hot + wet → CHEMICAL weathering dominates** *(reactions need water and speed up with heat)* → ⭐ **deep weathering profiles, laterite, [karst](05_Karst_and_Limestone_Landforms.md)**
- ⭐ **Cold or dry → PHYSICAL weathering dominates** *(frost shattering in cold, insolation/salt weathering in deserts)* → ⭐ **angular debris, scree, [desert landforms](07_Arid_and_Desert_Landforms.md)**

**⭐ EXFOLIATION / onion-skin weathering** deserves its own line because Leong illustrates it and it appears in Prelims: ⭐ **the outer shell of a rock heats and expands by day and cools and contracts by night faster than its interior, so successive curved shells peel away.** ⭐ **Produces rounded "exfoliation domes" — ⭐ India's granite inselbergs in Karnataka and Andhra are classic examples.**

### `[P]` PRELIMS
- ⭐ **Mass movement = downslope motion under GRAVITY alone, no transporting agent**
- ⭐⭐ **Soil creep = slowest, most widespread; evidence = tilted poles, bent tree bases**
- ⭐⭐ **Solifluction = saturated soil over FROZEN subsoil → periglacial/tundra**
- ⭐ **Slumping = curved slip plane; landslide = planar; scree/talus from rockfall**
- ⭐ **Lahar = volcanic mudflow**
- ⭐ **GSI = nodal agency for landslides**
- ⭐ **~12–13% of India is landslide-prone**
- ⭐ **Hot+wet → chemical weathering; cold/dry → physical**

### `[M]` MAINS
- ⭐⭐ **Himalayan landslides** — why every risk factor coincides, the human triggers, and the mitigation set
- ⭐⭐ **Gadgil vs Kasturirangan** — science recommended, politics diluted, consequences followed
- ⭐ **Downslope force vs resisting force** — the framework that makes a landslide answer analytical rather than a list
