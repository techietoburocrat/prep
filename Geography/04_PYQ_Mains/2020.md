### Q · 10m · 150w 🌱
The process of desertification does not have climatic boundaries. Justify with examples.
`Desertification` `UNCCD` `Land degradation`
🔗 [Leong Climate 3 §8](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/03_Savanna_Sudan_Type.md) · [Shankar D13 §4](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Shankar_IAS_Environment/Part_D_Climate_Change/13_Ozone_Acid_Rain_and_Global_Issues.md)
> **Careful — the statement is only partly right and a good answer says so.** **Desertification is formally DEFINED as degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid zones — so it does have a definitional boundary. But the DRIVERS (overgrazing, deforestation, salinisation, over-irrigation, mismanagement) operate in any climate, and degradation of that kind occurs everywhere.** *Engage with the premise rather than just illustrating it.*

### Q · 10m · 150w 🌱 🔁
How will the melting of Himalayan glaciers have a far-reaching impact on the water resources of India?
`Glacier retreat` `Third Pole` `Water security`
🔗 [Leong A6 §4](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/06_Glacial_Landforms.md)
> **The precision that scores: glacier melt matters most for DRY-SEASON flow, and the INDUS is far more glacier-dependent than the monsoon-fed Ganga.** *Also mention the Karakoram Anomaly and avoid the withdrawn "2035" claim.*

### Q · 15m · 250w 🌱
The interlinking of rivers can provide viable solutions to the multi-dimensional inter-related problems of droughts, floods and interrupted navigation. Critically examine.
`River interlinking` `Ken-Betwa`
🔗 [Leong A4 §8](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/04_River_Landforms.md) · [Cl.12 *India* Ch.6](../01_NCERT/Class_12_India_People_and_Economy/Notes/06_Water_Resources.md)
> **"Critically examine" = both sides.** **Against: ecological disruption, displacement, cost, seismicity, and the question of whether "surplus" basins really are surplus in a changing monsoon.**

### Q · 15m · 250w 🔁 🌱
India has immense potential of solar energy though there are regional variations in its development. Elaborate.
`Solar` `Renewables` `Regional variation`

### Q · 15m · 250w 🌱
Examine the status of forest resources of India and its resultant impact on climate change.
`ISFR` `Carbon sink` `Forest cover`
🔗 [Shankar A3 §2](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Shankar_IAS_Environment/Part_A_Ecology/03_Succession_and_Terrestrial_Ecosystems.md)
> **The high-value move: interrogate the "forest cover" statistic.** **FSI counts anything >1 ha with >10% canopy regardless of species — so plantations count and cover can rise while natural forest quality falls.** ⚠️`[VERIFY]` *the current ISFR figures.*

### Q · 10m · 150w
Account for the present location of iron and steel industries away from the source of raw material, by giving examples.
`Industrial location` `Weber` `Footloose industry`
🔗 [Cl.12 *Fundamentals* Ch.6 — Secondary Activities](../01_NCERT/Class_12_Fundamentals_of_Human_Geography/Notes/06_Secondary_Activities.md)
> **The classic answer: improved transport, use of scrap and imported ore, market orientation, port locations (Japan imports everything), and technological reduction in raw-material weight loss.**

### Q · 15m · 250w 🔁 🌱
Account for the huge flooding of million cities in India including the smart ones like Hyderabad and Pune. Suggest lasting remedial measures.
`Urban flooding` `Wetland loss` `Drainage`
🔗 [Shankar A4 §4](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Shankar_IAS_Environment/Part_A_Ecology/04_Aquatic_and_Wetland_Ecosystems.md)
> 🔁 *Urban flooding also asked in 2016.* **Causes: encroached wetlands and lake beds, concretised catchments, colonial-era drainage designed for lower intensity, solid waste choking drains, and more intense rainfall events under climate change.**
