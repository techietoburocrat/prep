### Q · 10m · 150w
Describe the characteristics and types of primary rocks.

`Igneous rocks` `Rock cycle`

> ⭐ **"Primary rocks" = IGNEOUS** — the first to form, from cooling magma. ⭐ **Intrusive/plutonic vs extrusive/volcanic; acidic vs basic.** ⭐ *Leong's [intrusive landform taxonomy](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/02_Earth_Movements_and_Vulcanicity.md) — batholith, laccolith, sill, dyke — is exactly the value-add here.*

### Q · 15m · 250w ⭐
Troposphere is a very significant atmospheric layer that determines weather processes. How?

`Troposphere` `Lapse rate` `Weather`
🔗 [Cl.11 Ch.8 — Composition and Structure of Atmosphere](../01_NCERT/Class_11_Fundamentals_of_Physical_Geography/Notes/08_Composition_and_Structure_of_Atmosphere.md)

> ⭐ **Because it holds ~99% of atmospheric water vapour and ~80% of the mass, has a steep lapse rate permitting convection, and is where clouds, precipitation and all weather systems occur.** ⭐ *The stratosphere above is stable and inverted — which is why it has no weather.*

### Q · 10m · 150w ⭐ 🌱
Examine the potential of wind energy in India and explain the reasons for their limited spatial spread.

`Wind energy` `Renewables`
🔗 [Shankar E19 §4](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Shankar_IAS_Environment/Part_E_Governance/19_Sustainable_Development_and_Energy.md)

> ⭐ **The "limited spatial spread" half is the real question: wind needs sustained high wind speeds, which in India means the western/southern peninsular corridors — Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Rajasthan — shaped by the monsoon and the terrain funnels.**

### Q · 15m · 250w 🌱
Describing the distribution of rubber-producing countries, indicate the major environmental issues faced by them.

`Rubber` `Equatorial plantations` `Deforestation`
🔗 [Leong Climate 1 §7](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/01_Equatorial_Hot_Wet.md)

> ⭐ **The Leong fact that answers it: rubber (*Hevea brasiliensis*) is AMAZON-NATIVE but ~90% grown in SOUTHEAST ASIA.** ⭐ **The environmental issue is monoculture plantation replacing rainforest — the same driver as oil palm.**

### Q · 15m · 250w ⭐⭐ 🔁
What are the forces that influence ocean currents? Describe their role in the fishing industry of the world.

`Ocean currents` `Upwelling` `Fisheries`
🔗 [Leong A11 §2–3](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/11_Oceans_Currents_Tides_Salinity.md) · [Cl.11 Ch.14](../01_NCERT/Class_11_Fundamentals_of_Physical_Geography/Notes/14_Movements_of_Ocean_Water.md)

> ⭐⭐ 🔁 **Ocean currents have now been asked in 2015, 2019 and 2022 — the single most repeated Mains geography theme.** ⭐ **The fisheries half wants: shallow shelf + warm-cold mixing + upwelling → plankton. Grand Banks, Hokkaido, Peru, Benguela, North Sea.**

### Q · 15m · 250w ⭐
Mention the significance of straits and isthmus in international trade.

`Chokepoints` `Straits` `Isthmus`
🔗 [Cl.12 *Fundamentals* Ch.9 — International Trade](../01_NCERT/Class_12_Fundamentals_of_Human_Geography/Notes/09_International_Trade.md) · 🗺️ [06_Maps](../06_Maps/)

> ⭐⭐ **A pure MAP question in disguise.** ⭐ **Hormuz, Malacca, Bab-el-Mandeb, Gibraltar, Bosphorus, Dover, Sunda, Palk. ⭐ Isthmus: Panama, Suez (Suez is technically an isthmus cut by a canal), Kra.** ⭐ *If you can't draw them, you can't answer this.*

## 📊 What 2022 tells you

⭐ **A notably resource-and-trade-heavy year** — rubber, wind, straits, fisheries. ⭐⭐ **Only 2 of 7 are "pure" physical geography.** ⭐ **The lesson: economic geography and resource distribution are consistently examined and consistently under-prepared, because candidates over-invest in landforms.**
