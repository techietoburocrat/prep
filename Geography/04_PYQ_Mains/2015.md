### Q · 15m · 250w
Why did the industrial revolution first occur in England? Discuss the quality of life of the people there during the industrialization. How does it compare with that in India at present?
`Industrial Revolution` `Urbanisation`
🔗 [Leong Climate 8 §7](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/08_British_Type_Cool_Temperate_Western_Margin.md) · [Cl.12 *Fundamentals* Ch.6](../01_NCERT/Class_12_Fundamentals_of_Human_Geography/Notes/06_Secondary_Activities.md)
> ⚠️ **Handle the geography carefully. Coal + iron + navigable rivers + ports + a mild year-round working climate were ENABLING conditions — but the causation runs through capital, institutions, enclosure, colonial markets and technology.** **Do NOT write climate determinism.** *[Leong Climate 8](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/08_British_Type_Cool_Temperate_Western_Margin.md) §7 flags this trap explicitly.*

### Q · 15m · 250w 🔁
India is well endowed with freshwater resources. Critically examine why it still suffers from water scarcity.
`Water scarcity` `Availability vs access`
🔗 [Cl.12 *India* Ch.6](../01_NCERT/Class_12_India_People_and_Economy/Notes/06_Water_Resources.md)
> 🔁 *Reappears in 2023 in world form.* **The answer is the availability/access split: spatial mismatch (Brahmaputra basin has water, the Deccan doesn't) · temporal mismatch (75–80% of rain in four months) · cropping choices · pollution making water unusable · groundwater over-extraction · leakage and pricing.**

### Q · 15m · 250w 🌱 🔁
The states of Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand are reaching the limits of their ecological carrying capacity due to tourism. Critically evaluate.
`Carrying capacity` `Himalayan ecology` `Tourism`
🔗 [Shankar A1 §6](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Shankar_IAS_Environment/Part_A_Ecology/01_Ecology_Fundamentals.md) · [Leong A6 §5](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/06_Glacial_Landforms.md)
> **Use the ecological concept properly: carrying capacity is the maximum load a system can sustain INDEFINITELY, and exceeding it DEGRADES the capacity itself.** *Joshimath, Char Dham, waste and water stress in hill towns, GLOF-exposed infrastructure.* 🔁 *Related to 2019's mountain-ecosystem question.*

### Q · 15m · 250w
How far do you agree that the behaviour of the Indian monsoon has been changing due to humanizing landscapes? Discuss.
`Monsoon` `Land-use change` `Aerosols`
🔗 [Cl.11 *India* Ch.4](../01_NCERT/Class_11_India_Physical_Environment/Notes/04_Climate.md) · [Leong Climate 2 §8](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/02_Tropical_Monsoon_and_Marine.md)
> **"How far do you agree" invites qualification.** **Yes: land-use change, urban heat islands, aerosols/brown clouds dimming the surface and reducing land–sea contrast, irrigation altering local humidity. But: the monsoon is fundamentally driven by planetary-scale forcing — differential heating, ITCZ, jet streams, ENSO/IOD — which humans have not replaced.** *Attribution is partial, and saying so is the mark.*

### Q · 10m · 150w
Mumbai, Delhi, and Kolkata are the three megacities of the country but air pollution is a much more serious problem in Delhi as compared to the other two. Why is this so?
`Air pollution` `Inversion` `Landlocked`
🔗 [Leong A12 §4](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/12_Weather_Elements_and_Local_Winds.md) · [Shankar C9](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Shankar_IAS_Environment/Part_C_Pollution/09_Air_Pollution.md)
> ⭐ **THE answer is meteorological and geographical, not just about emissions: Delhi is LANDLOCKED with no coastal breeze to ventilate it · hemmed by the Himalaya to the north so pollutants cannot escape · winter TEMPERATURE INVERSION collapses the mixing layer · and crop-residue burning in Punjab/Haryana coincides exactly with that season.** **Mumbai and Kolkata are coastal and ventilate.** *Very few candidates give the inversion mechanism.*

### Q · 15m · 250w 🔁
Explain the factors responsible for the origin of ocean currents? How do they influence regional climates, fishing, and navigation?
`Ocean currents` `Fisheries` `Climate`
🔗 [Leong A11 §2–3](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/11_Oceans_Currents_Tides_Salinity.md) · [Cl.11 Ch.14](../01_NCERT/Class_11_Fundamentals_of_Physical_Geography/Notes/14_Movements_of_Ocean_Water.md)
> 🔁 **Ocean currents, first of three appearances (2015, 2019, 2022).** **Origin: prevailing winds, Coriolis, temperature/salinity density differences, landmass configuration. Effects: Bergen vs Labrador for climate; Grand Banks and Peru for fishing; historic sailing routes for navigation.**

### Q · 10m · 150w 🌱
What are the economic significances of the discovery of oil in the Arctic Sea and its possible environmental consequences?
`Arctic` `Hydrocarbons` `Fragile ecosystem`
🔗 [Leong Climate 11 §8](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/11_Arctic_Tundra_and_Ice_Cap.md)
> 🔁 *Related to 2018's "why is India interested in the Arctic".* **~13% of undiscovered oil, ~30% of undiscovered gas; UNCLOS shelf claims; and an ecosystem with essentially no oil-spill clean-up capacity in ice.**

## 📊 What 2015 tells you

**5 of 7 are 15-markers, and several open with a proposition to evaluate** ("how far do you agree", "critically examine", "critically evaluate"). **These reward QUALIFICATION, not agreement. The examiner is testing whether you can hold a position while acknowledging its limits — which is the core Mains skill.**
