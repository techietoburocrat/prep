### ⚠️⚠️ Why 2000–2012 is not simply "more of the same"

**Before 2013, Mains was a completely different exam:** **Essay + TWO General Studies papers + TWO papers of ONE optional subject.** **"GS Paper I — Indian Heritage and Culture, History and Geography of the World and Society" did not exist.** **Geography appeared inside a differently-shaped GS paper, mixed with statistics, science and current events, at a different depth and in a different question format.**

## HOW TO USE THIS FOLDER

1. **Read a whole YEAR before you read a topic.** **A year file shows you what UPSC actually does in one sitting — the mix of 10s and 15s, the balance of physical/human/Indian/world, and the fact that roughly a third of "geography" questions are really environment or economy questions wearing a geography costume.**
2. **Then work through the [Topic Analysis](90_Topic_Analysis.md).** **That is where the pattern lives — and the pattern is what tells you where your remaining hours should go.**
3. **Every question is tagged with the note that answers it.** **If you can't answer a question, the tag tells you which file to reread. That closes the loop between the notes and the paper.**
4. **Write answers, don't just read questions.** **10 marks = 150 words, 15 marks = 250 words, and the word limit is the real constraint. A brilliant 400-word answer to a 150-word question scores worse than a competent 150-word one.**

### The paper's shape

| | |
|---|---|
| **Paper** | **GS Paper I** — Indian Heritage & Culture, History & Geography of the World & Society |
| **Total** | **250 marks · 20 questions · 3 hours · all compulsory** |
| **Structure** | **Q1–Q10 = 10 marks / 150 words · Q11–Q20 = 15 marks / 250 words** |
| **Geography's share** | **~6.8 questions/year average → roughly 85–100 marks of 250** |
| **Also** | **GS-III carries disaster management, agriculture, resources and environment separately** — *see [90_Topic_Analysis.md](90_Topic_Analysis.md) §5* |
