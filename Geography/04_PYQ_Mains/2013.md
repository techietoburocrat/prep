### Q · 15m · 250w ⭐⭐ 🔁
What do you understand by the theory of 'continental drift'? Discuss the prominent evidences in its support.
`Continental drift` `Wegener` `Pangaea`
🔗 [Cl.11 Ch.4 — Distribution of Oceans and Continents](../01_NCERT/Class_11_Fundamentals_of_Physical_Geography/Notes/04_Distribution_of_Oceans_and_Continents.md)
> 🔁 ⭐ **Re-asked in 2025 as tectonic change in continents and ocean basins.** ⭐ **The evidence set: ⭐ jig-saw fit · ⭐ matching rock ages and mountain chains across the Atlantic · ⭐ tillite deposits · ⭐ placer deposits · ⭐ fossil distribution (*Mesosaurus*, *Glossopteris*) · ⭐ palaeomagnetism.** ⭐ *And be ready to say why Wegener was rejected initially — he had no MECHANISM. Sea-floor spreading supplied it.*

### Q · 15m · 250w
With the growing scarcity of fossil fuels, atomic energy is gaining more and more significance in India. Discuss the availability of raw materials required for the generation of atomic energy in India and in the world.
`Nuclear` `Thorium` `Monazite`
🔗 [Cl.12 *India* Ch.7 — Mineral and Energy Resources](../01_NCERT/Class_12_India_People_and_Economy/Notes/07_Mineral_and_Energy_Resources.md) · [Shankar E19 §4](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Shankar_IAS_Environment/Part_E_Governance/19_Sustainable_Development_and_Energy.md)
> ⭐⭐ **The India-specific fact: ⭐ India is uranium-POOR but ⭐ THORIUM-RICH — monazite sands of Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Odisha and Andhra, among the world's largest reserves.** ⭐ **Hence the ⭐ three-stage nuclear programme, designed specifically to reach a thorium fuel cycle.**

### Q · 15m · 250w
It is said that India has substantial reserves of shale oil and gas, which can feed the needs of the country for a quarter-century. However, tapping of the resource does not appear to be high on the agenda. Discuss critically the availability and issues involved.
`Shale gas` `Fracking` `Water use`
🔗 [Cl.12 *India* Ch.7](../01_NCERT/Class_12_India_People_and_Economy/Notes/07_Mineral_and_Energy_Resources.md)
> ⭐ **The "issues" half: ⭐ hydraulic fracturing is enormously WATER-INTENSIVE in a water-stressed country · ⭐ groundwater contamination risk · ⭐ induced seismicity · ⭐ land acquisition in densely populated basins (Cambay, Krishna-Godavari, Cauvery, Damodar) · ⭐ and methane leakage undermining the climate case.**

### Q · 10m · 150w ⭐⭐
**(a)** Bring out the causes for the formation of heat islands in the urban habitat of the world. **(b)** What do you understand by the phenomenon of 'temperature inversion' in meteorology? How does it affect the weather and the inhabitants of the place?
`Urban heat island` `Temperature inversion` `Smog`
🔗 [Leong A12 §4 & §6](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/12_Weather_Elements_and_Local_Winds.md) · [Shankar C9](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Shankar_IAS_Environment/Part_C_Pollution/09_Air_Pollution.md)
> ⭐⭐ **Both halves are in [Leong A12](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_A_Physical/12_Weather_Elements_and_Local_Winds.md).** ⭐ **UHI: concrete and asphalt store heat, no vegetation or evaporative cooling, urban canyons trap radiation, waste heat from vehicles and AC.** ⭐ **Inversion: warm air over cold air stops vertical mixing → pollutants accumulate → ⭐ smog.** ⭐ *This pairing is exactly what 2015's Delhi air-pollution question needed.*

### Q · 10m · 150w ⭐⭐
Major hot deserts in the northern hemisphere are located between 20–30° N latitudes and on the western side of the continents. Why?
`Hot deserts` `Subtropical high` `Cold currents`
🔗 [Leong Climate 4 §2](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/04_Hot_and_Mid_Latitude_Deserts.md)
> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE flagship derivation question, and [Leong Climate 4](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/GC_Leong/Part_B_Climate_Types/04_Hot_and_Mid_Latitude_Deserts.md) is built around answering it.** ⭐ **Five causes: ⭐ subtropical high pressure (descending, warming air) · ⭐ offshore trade winds · ⭐ COLD OCEAN CURRENTS stabilising the air (Canary, Benguela, Peru, California, West Australian) · ⭐ rain shadow · ⭐ continentality.** ⭐ **Name the currents — that is what separates a 6/10 from a 9/10.**
