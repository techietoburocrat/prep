# Chapter 1 — India: Size and Location

> **The one-line point:** India's position is not just a set of coordinates — it is a **strategic asset**. It sits at the head of the Indian Ocean, astride the sea routes between Europe and East Asia, walled by the Himalayas, and split neatly in half by the Tropic of Cancer. **Every one of those facts has consequences.**

## 1️⃣ Size and Extent

| | Value |
|---|---|
| **Latitude** | **8°4′N to 37°6′N** |
| **Longitude** | **68°7′E to 97°25′E** |
| **Area** | **3.28 million sq km** = **2.4% of world land area** |
| **Rank by area** | **7th largest** country |
| **North–south extent** | ~**3,214 km** |
| **East–west extent** | ~**2,933 km** |
| **Land boundary** | ~**15,200 km** |
| **Coastline** | **7,516.6 km** (mainland + Andaman & Nicobar + Lakshadweep) |
| **Mainland coastline** | ~6,100 km |

> ⚠️ **Two numbers that are easy to confuse:** the **total coastline is 7,516.6 km** (including islands); the **mainland coastline is ~6,100 km**. Prelims has asked for both.

> **The southernmost point question — worth getting right.**
> - **Southernmost point of the Indian mainland:** **Kanniyakumari** (~8°4′N)
> - **Southernmost point of the Republic of India:** **Indira Point**, Great Nicobar (~6°45′N)
>
> **Indira Point was submerged during the 2004 tsunami.** The distinction between "mainland" and "Republic" is exactly what a question tests.

### The extent paradox

India spans ~30° of latitude and ~29° of longitude — nearly the same figure. **But the consequences are completely different:**

| | Latitude (~30°) | Longitude (~29°) |
|---|---|---|
| **Effect** | **Enormous climatic and biological variety** — tropical south, temperate north | Only a **~2-hour** difference in local time |
| **India's response** | Embraces it | **Suppresses it** — one time zone |

> 🧠 **Why?** Latitude changes the **angle of incoming sunlight** (Cl.6 Ch.2), which changes temperature, which changes everything living. Longitude only changes **what time it is.** **Same number of degrees, entirely different consequence.**

**And a further asymmetry:** because meridians converge toward the poles (Cl.6 Ch.2), those ~29° of longitude cover **fewer actual kilometres in northern India than in the south.** So the east–west *distance* (2,933 km) is less than the north–south distance (3,214 km) despite similar degree spans.

## 2️⃣ The Tropic of Cancer and the Standard Meridian

**The Tropic of Cancer (23°30′N) passes through 8 states**, west → east:

```
   Gujarat → Rajasthan → Madhya Pradesh → Chhattisgarh →
   Jharkhand → West Bengal → Tripura → Mizoram
```

⚠️ **Not Odisha, not Bihar, not Maharashtra, not Assam.** Those four are the standard distractors.

**Consequence:** the Tropic roughly **halves India** — the south lies in the tropics (Torrid Zone), the north in the subtropics (North Temperate Zone). **This is a major reason for India's climatic range.**

**Standard Meridian: 82°30′E**, passing through **Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh.** IST = **GMT + 5:30**.

**Why 82°30′E?** It runs near the **centre** of the country, so the error is shared roughly equally between east and west. The half-hour offset exists because 82.5 ÷ 15 = 5.5 — the meridian isn't a multiple of 15°.

## 3️⃣ India and the World — the Strategic Argument

### Why India's position matters

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Head of the Indian Ocean** | **The only country with an ocean named after it.** India's peninsula juts ~1,600 km into it, giving command of the sea lanes |
| **On the Europe–East Asia route** | The shortest sea route between Europe and East Asia passes through the Indian Ocean |
| **Long coastline + islands** | 7,516.6 km; a large **Exclusive Economic Zone** |
| **Land routes north-west** | The historic passes — Khyber, Bolan — through which trade and invasion both came |
| **Central to trade** | Historically traded with Rome, Arabia, SE Asia, China |

> **The Suez Canal (opened 1869) is a fact worth knowing with its consequence.** It cut the sea distance from India to Europe by roughly **7,000 km**, because ships no longer had to round the Cape of Good Hope. **This transformed India's trade position and tightened Britain's imperial grip on it** — a rare fact that works in Geography, Modern History and International Relations alike.

> 🚢 **The modern relevance is direct.** Roughly **80% of global seaborne oil trade** passes through Indian Ocean chokepoints — the **Strait of Hormuz**, the **Strait of Malacca**, **Bab-el-Mandeb**. India's location adjacent to these routes is the geographic basis for its maritime strategy, the "**SAGAR**" doctrine (Security and Growth for All in the Region), and its role in the Quad. **Tag for GS-II and [Current Affairs](../../../../Current%20Affairs/00_START_HERE.md).**

### Neighbours

| | Countries |
|---|---|
| **Land (7)** | **Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar** |
| **Sea (2)** | **Sri Lanka** (across the **Palk Strait** and **Gulf of Mannar**), **Maldives** |

**Longest land border: Bangladesh** (~4,096 km). **Shortest: Afghanistan** (~106 km, via PoK).

**India + 6 others form the subcontinent:** India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Maldives — the **SAARC** grouping (plus Afghanistan since 2007).

**Administrative units:** **28 States and 8 Union Territories.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **8°4′N–37°6′N, 68°7′E–97°25′E. 3.28 mn sq km = 2.4% of world land. 7th largest by area, 1st by population.**
2. **Coastline 7,516.6 km total; ~6,100 km mainland.**
3. **~30° of latitude gives climatic variety; ~29° of longitude gives only 2 hours** — because latitude changes sun angle and longitude only changes time.
4. **Tropic of Cancer through 8 states:** Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, WB, Tripura, Mizoram.
5. **Standard Meridian 82°30′E through Mirzapur.** Chosen because it's central; the half-hour offset follows from 82.5 ÷ 15.
6. **Kanniyakumari = mainland's southernmost. Indira Point = Republic's** (submerged in the 2004 tsunami).
7. **7 land neighbours + 2 sea neighbours.** Longest border **Bangladesh**; shortest **Afghanistan**.
8. **The Indian Ocean is the only ocean named after a country**, and India's peninsula commands it.
9. **Suez Canal (1869) cut the India–Europe sea route by ~7,000 km** — reshaping trade and tightening imperial control.
10. **~80% of global seaborne oil crosses Indian Ocean chokepoints** — Hormuz, Malacca, Bab-el-Mandeb.
