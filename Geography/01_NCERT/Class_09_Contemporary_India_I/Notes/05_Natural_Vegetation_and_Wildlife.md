## 1️⃣ Why India Is So Biodiverse

**India has ~2.4% of world land area but ~8% of recorded species** — roughly 47,000 plant species and 90,000 animal species.

| Physical variety | Biological consequence |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **~30° of latitude** — tropical to temperate | Multiple climatic zones in one country |
| ⭐ **Sea level to 8,586 m** | Every altitudinal zone from mangrove to alpine to permanent snow |
| ⭐ **Rainfall from ~10 cm (Ladakh) to ~1,100 cm (Mawsynram)** | Desert, scrub, deciduous, rainforest — all present |
| **7,516 km of coastline + islands** | Mangroves, coral reefs, lagoons, estuaries |
| **Ancient plateau + young mountains** | Very old lineages alongside recently evolved species |

⭐ **India is one of the world's ~17 "megadiverse" countries**, and contains **4 of the ~36 global biodiversity hotspots:**

1. ⭐ **The Himalaya**
2. ⭐ **The Western Ghats & Sri Lanka**
3. ⭐ **Indo-Burma** (includes NE India)
4. ⭐ **Sundaland** (includes the Nicobar Islands)

> ⭐ **A biodiversity hotspot requires TWO conditions** — this precision is often tested: it must have **at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species**, *and* it must have **lost at least 70% of its original habitat.** **A hotspot is defined by being both exceptionally rich and exceptionally threatened.** Richness alone doesn't qualify.

## 2️⃣ ⭐ The Five Vegetation Types

| Type | Rainfall | Where | ⭐ Trees | Wildlife |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🌴 **TROPICAL EVERGREEN (Rainforest)** | ⭐ **>200 cm** | Western Ghats windward, Andaman & Nicobar, upper Assam, Tamil Nadu coast | ⭐ **Ebony, mahogany, rosewood, rubber, cinchona** | Elephant, monkey, lemur, deer, one-horned rhino |
| 🌳 **TROPICAL DECIDUOUS (Monsoon)** | ⭐ **70–200 cm** — ⭐ **India's most widespread** | Most of India | ⭐ **Moist deciduous (100–200 cm): teak, bamboo, sal, shisham, sandalwood, khair, kusum** · ⭐ **Dry deciduous (70–100 cm): teak, sal, peepal, neem** | ⭐ **Tiger, lion, elephant, pig, deer** |
| 🌵 **THORN FORESTS & SCRUB** | ⭐ **<70 cm** | Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, Chhattisgarh, UP, Haryana | ⭐ **Acacia, palms, euphorbias, cacti, kikar, babool, khair** | ⭐ **Camels, rats, mice, rabbits, fox, wolf, wild ass, horses** |
| 🌲 **MONTANE (Mountain)** | Varies with altitude | Himalayas | ⭐ Wet temperate (1,000–2,000 m): **evergreen oak, chestnut** · Temperate (1,500–3,000 m): **pine, deodar, silver fir, spruce, cedar** · Above: **alpine — silver fir, juniper, birch, rhododendron**, then grassland | ⭐ **Kashmir stag, spotted deer, wild sheep, jack rabbit, yak, snow leopard, squirrels, Tibetan antelope** |
| 🌊 **MANGROVE (Tidal)** | Coastal deltas | ⭐ **Sundarbans (Ganga-Brahmaputra), Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri deltas** | ⭐ **Sundari tree**, palm, coconut, keora, agar | ⭐ **Royal Bengal Tiger, turtles, crocodiles, gharials, snakes** |

> ⭐ **The altitudinal sequence in the Himalayas is the vertical version of the latitudinal sequence** (Cl.7 Ch.6): tropical → wet temperate (oak, chestnut) → temperate coniferous (pine, deodar, fir) → alpine (birch, juniper, rhododendron) → alpine grassland → permanent snow. **Climb 3,000 m and you have travelled from Kerala to Siberia.**

> 🌱 **The Sundarbans, once more, from every angle** — because it is the best single example of why these subjects are merged:
> **World's largest delta** (Geography) · **world's largest mangrove forest**, named for the **Sundari** tree (Ecology) · **Royal Bengal Tiger** habitat (Wildlife) · **National Park + Biosphere Reserve + Ramsar site + UNESCO World Heritage Site** (Conservation) · **a natural storm barrier** protecting Kolkata and coastal Bengal (Disaster management).

## 3️⃣ 🌱 Wildlife and Conservation

### Threats

**Habitat loss** (the largest cause) · **fragmentation** · poaching · pollution · forest fires · invasive species · human–wildlife conflict · climate change.

### ⭐ The conservation framework

| Instrument | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Wildlife Protection Act, 1972** | The core statute; created protected areas and schedules of protected species |
| ⭐ **Forest Conservation Act, 1980** | Restricts diversion of forest land for non-forest use |
| **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** | Umbrella environmental legislation (enacted after Bhopal) |
| **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** | Implements the CBD in India |
| ⭐ **Project Tiger (1973)** · **Project Elephant (1992)** | Flagship species programmes |
| **National Parks / Sanctuaries / Biosphere Reserves** | See the comparison in [Cl.6 Ch.8](../../Class_06_The_Earth_Our_Habitat/Notes/08_India_Climate_Vegetation_Wildlife.md) |
| ⭐ **18 Biosphere Reserves** | India has 18; several are on UNESCO's world network |

⭐ **Key biosphere reserves to know:** **Nilgiri** (India's first, 1986), **Sundarbans**, **Nanda Devi**, **Gulf of Mannar**, **Nokrek**, **Pachmarhi**, **Simlipal**, **Great Nicobar**, **Agasthyamalai**, **Achanakmar-Amarkantak**, **Khangchendzonga**, **Panna**.

⭐ **Bird sanctuaries:** **Keoladeo Ghana (Bharatpur)** — migratory birds; **Ranganathittu**; **Chilika**.

### 🌱 Why conservation matters — arguments in several registers

| Register | Argument |
|---|---|
| **Ecological** | Food webs interlock; removing predators collapses the system (Cl.6 Ch.5) |
| **Economic** | Forests supply timber, medicine, fuel and fodder; pollinators underwrite agriculture |
| **Scientific** | Undiscovered species hold undiscovered medicines — cinchona (quinine) is an Indian forest example |
| **Cultural** | ⭐ **Sacred groves** — patches of forest protected by religious custom for centuries |
| **Constitutional** | ⭐ **Article 48A** (State's duty) and **Article 51A(g)** (citizen's Fundamental Duty) |

> ⭐ **Sacred groves are worth knowing by name and region** — they demonstrate that community conservation predates state conservation in India by centuries: **Kavu** (Kerala), **Devrai/Devrahati** (Maharashtra), **Sarna** (Jharkhand/Chhattisgarh), **Law Kyntang** (Meghalaya), **Orans** (Rajasthan). **Some hold species found nowhere else in the region because they were never cleared.**
>
> 🎯 **The argument this supports:** community-based conservation with genuine local ownership has a strong Indian precedent and often outperforms purely state-enforced exclusion. **This connects to the Forest Rights Act 2006, PESA, and the biosphere reserve model** — and it's a substantive point rather than a sentimental one.

> 🌱 **The forest-cover caveat, repeated because it matters:** the **India State of Forest Report** distinguishes **forest cover** from **tree cover**, and plantations count towards the former. **A eucalyptus monoculture is not ecologically equivalent to natural forest**, even though both register in the statistics. Knowing this distinction is a genuine differentiator.

**Trap 1:** *"Tropical evergreen is India's most widespread forest."* ⭐ **Tropical deciduous** is.
**Trap 2:** *"A biodiversity hotspot is simply a species-rich area."* ⭐ **It must also have lost ≥70% of its habitat.**
**Trap 3:** *"Sundarbans is named after the Sundarban tribe."* Named after the **Sundari tree**.
**Trap 4:** *"Evergreen forests never shed leaves."* They shed **continuously, not all at once**.
**Trap 5:** *"India has 4 megadiverse regions."* It has **4 hotspots** and **is one of ~17 megadiverse countries.** Different concepts.

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Flora / fauna** | Plant life / animal life of a region |
| **Natural vegetation** | Plant life growing without human help |
| **Virgin vegetation** | Purely natural, undisturbed vegetation |
| **Endemic species** | Native to and found only in a specific region |
| **Exotic species** | Introduced from outside |
| ⭐ **Biodiversity hotspot** | ≥1,500 endemic plants **and** ≥70% habitat loss |
| **Megadiverse country** | One of ~17 countries holding most of Earth's species |
| **Biome** | A large ecological community defined by climate and vegetation |
| **Mangrove** | Salt-tolerant tidal forest |
| **Sundari** | The tree the Sundarbans is named for |
| ⭐ **Sacred grove** | Forest patch protected by religious custom |
| **Biosphere reserve** | Large protected area zoned core/buffer/transition |
| **WPA 1972 / FCA 1980 / EPA 1986 / BDA 2002** | India's core environmental statutes |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **India: 2.4% of world land, ~8% of recorded species** — because of the physical variety in Ch.2–4.
2. **One of ~17 megadiverse countries; contains 4 of ~36 hotspots:** Himalaya, Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Sundaland.
3. ⭐ **A hotspot needs BOTH ≥1,500 endemic plants AND ≥70% habitat loss** — rich *and* threatened.
4. ⭐ **Five vegetation types by rainfall: evergreen (>200 cm) · deciduous (70–200 cm, most widespread) · thorn (<70 cm) · montane · mangrove.**
5. **Moist deciduous: teak, sal, bamboo, shisham. Dry deciduous: teak, sal, peepal, neem.**
6. **Himalayan altitudinal sequence = the latitudinal sequence turned vertical.**
7. ⭐ **Sundarbans: largest delta + largest mangrove + Sundari tree + Royal Bengal Tiger + NP/BR/Ramsar/UNESCO + natural storm barrier.**
8. 🌱 **Habitat loss is the biggest threat**, compounded by fragmentation.
9. ⭐ **WPA 1972, FCA 1980, EPA 1986, BDA 2002.** **Project Tiger 1973, Project Elephant 1992.**
10. **Nilgiri was India's first biosphere reserve (1986); India has 18.**
11. ⭐ **Sacred groves (Kavu, Devrai, Sarna, Law Kyntang, Orans)** show community conservation predates state conservation by centuries.
12. 🌱 **Plantations count as "forest cover" but are not natural forest.**
