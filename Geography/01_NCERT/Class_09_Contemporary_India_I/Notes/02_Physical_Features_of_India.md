# Chapter 2 — Physical Features of India

> **The one-line point:** India contains **the world's youngest mountains and one of its oldest plateaus, side by side** — because they were formed hundreds of millions of years apart by completely different processes. The six physical divisions are the result, and together they give India almost every landform on Earth.

## 1️⃣ How India Was Formed

```
   ~200 million years ago          Then                    Now
   ────────────────────           ────                    ───

   GONDWANALAND                   Indian plate drifts     Collision continues;
   (the southern super-           NORTH across the        Himalayas still rising
    continent: India,             TETHYS SEA               ~5 mm/year
    Africa, S.America,                 ↓
    Australia, Antarctica)        Collides with the
        │                         EURASIAN PLATE
        │ breaks up                    ↓
        ▼                         Sediments of the Tethys
   Indian plate separates         floor CRUMPLE UPWARD
                                       ↓
                                  ⭐ THE HIMALAYAS
```

> ⭐ **This explains the chapter's central oddity — why India has the world's youngest mountains and one of its oldest plateaus.**
>
> - **The Peninsular Plateau is a fragment of Gondwanaland** — ancient, rigid, stable, made of hard igneous and metamorphic rock. It has been above water for hundreds of millions of years, and has simply been eroding ever since.
> - **The Himalayas are the crumpled floor of a vanished sea**, thrown up by a collision that is still happening.
>
> **Two completely different origins, hundreds of millions of years apart, now adjacent.** And the Northern Plains between them are just the trough that filled with sediment washed off the new mountains. **One story explains all three divisions.**

🐚 **A striking piece of evidence:** **marine fossils are found high in the Himalayas.** They are the remains of creatures from the Tethys Sea floor — now sitting several kilometres above sea level. **The mountains are made of an old seabed.**

## 3️⃣ ⛰️ The Himalayan Mountains

Young, **fold** mountains; the world's highest range; ~2,400 km long; width 400 km (Kashmir) to 150 km (Arunachal).

### ⭐ The three parallel ranges (north → south)

| Range | Also called | Character |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **HIMADRI** | **Great / Inner Himalaya** | **Most continuous, highest** — average 6,000 m. **Perennially snowbound**; source of the great glaciers. All the loftiest peaks |
| ⭐ **HIMACHAL** | **Lesser Himalaya** | Altitude 3,700–4,500 m. **Highly compressed and folded.** Ranges: **Pir Panjal** (longest), **Dhauladhar**, **Mahabharat**. ⭐ Famous **valleys: Kashmir, Kangra, Kullu**. Hill stations |
| ⭐ **SHIWALIK** | **Outer Himalaya** | **Lowest and youngest** — 900–1,100 m. Made of **unconsolidated sediments** washed down from above. ⭐ **DUNS** lie between Shiwalik and Himachal — Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun |

> 🧠 **"Hi-Hi-Shi," north to south.** Height decreases southward, and so does age — the Shiwaliks are the youngest, built from the debris of the ranges above them.

> ⚠️ **The Shiwaliks' composition matters.** Being made of loose, unconsolidated sediment, they are **highly prone to landslides and erosion** — which is why deforestation and road-building in the Shiwalik belt is so damaging. **A GS-III disaster-management point.**

### ⭐ The regional (west → east) divisions

Cut the Himalayas the other way and you get four sections, divided by rivers:

| Section | Between | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Punjab Himalaya** | Indus ↔ Satluj | Also called **Kashmir** and **Himachal Himalaya** |
| ⭐ **Kumaon Himalaya** | Satluj ↔ Kali | |
| ⭐ **Nepal Himalaya** | Kali ↔ Teesta | Contains Everest |
| ⭐ **Assam Himalaya** | Teesta ↔ Dihang | |

⭐ **Beyond the Dihang gorge**, the Himalayas bend sharply south — the **PURVANCHAL** (Eastern Hills): **Patkai Bum, Naga Hills, Manipur Hills, Mizo (Lushai) Hills**. These are **low hills** made largely of **strong sandstone**, covered in dense forest.

> 🧠 **Two different ways to slice the same mountains — and both get examined.** *Longitudinally* (parallel ranges: Himadri/Himachal/Shiwalik) and *regionally* (west-to-east sections divided by rivers). Don't confuse them.

**Key peaks:** ⭐ **Kanchenjunga (8,586 m)** — highest peak wholly in India · **Nanda Devi**, **Kamet**, **Namcha Barwa**, **Nanga Parbat**.

## 4️⃣ 🌾 The Northern Plains

Formed by the alluvial deposits of ⭐ **three river systems: the Indus, the Ganga and the Brahmaputra.** ~2,400 km long, 240–320 km broad. **Among the most densely populated regions on Earth.**

### ⭐⭐ The four relief belts (north → south) — high-yield content

```
   HIMALAYAS
   ═══════════════════════════════════════════
   1. BHABAR    Narrow (8–16 km) belt of PEBBLES at the foothills.
                ⭐ STREAMS DISAPPEAR underground here — the porous
                   pebble bed swallows them.
   ═══════════════════════════════════════════
   2. TERAI     South of Bhabar. ⭐ Streams RE-EMERGE, creating a
                WET, SWAMPY, MARSHY belt. Dense forest, rich wildlife.
                (Much of it has been cleared for farming.)
   ═══════════════════════════════════════════
   3. BHANGAR   ⭐ The OLDER alluvium. Lies ABOVE the floodplain,
                forms a terrace. Contains ⭐ KANKAR — calcareous
                nodules. Less fertile.
   ═══════════════════════════════════════════
   4. KHADAR    ⭐ The NEWER alluvium of the floodplains.
                RENEWED ALMOST EVERY YEAR by floods.
                ⭐ THE MOST FERTILE LAND IN INDIA.
   ═══════════════════════════════════════════
```

> ⭐ **Bhabar and Terai are cause and effect, and that's how to remember them.** Rivers leaving the mountains hit a belt of coarse **pebbles** — so porous that the water **sinks out of sight (Bhabar)**. A little further south, where the sediment becomes finer and less permeable, that water **comes back up (Terai)** — creating swamp. **One belt swallows the rivers, the next spits them out.**

> ⭐ **Bhangar vs Khadar is the pairing most often asked.** **Khadar = newer, lower, flooded annually, most fertile. Bhangar = older, higher, above the flood level, contains kankar nodules, less fertile.**
> **Memory hook:** **Kh**adar — **Kh**ush (fresh/happy, renewed each year). **Bhangar** — sounds like "banger", old and worn.

### The three sections

| Section | River system | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Punjab Plains** | Indus and its tributaries | Largely in Pakistan. **Doabs** — land between two rivers |
| **Ganga Plains** | Ganga | From Haryana to WB — the largest section |
| **Brahmaputra Plains** | Brahmaputra | Mainly Assam |

⭐ **Majuli**, in the Brahmaputra, is **the world's largest river island.**

## 5️⃣ 🏜️ The Peninsular Plateau

A **tableland of old crystalline igneous and metamorphic rock**, formed from the break-up of Gondwanaland. **Broad shallow valleys, rounded hills.** Two divisions:

| | ⭐ **CENTRAL HIGHLANDS** | ⭐ **DECCAN PLATEAU** |
|---|---|---|
| **Where** | North of the Narmada, covering most of the Malwa plateau | South of the Narmada |
| **Bounded by** | Vindhyas (south), Aravalli (NW) | Satpura (N), Western Ghats (W), Eastern Ghats (E) |
| **Slopes** | East and north-east | Gently east (which is why the big rivers flow east) |
| **Includes** | Malwa, Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, ⭐ **Chhotanagpur Plateau** (mineral-rich, drained by the Damodar) | Deccan Trap (basalt), ⭐ **Meghalaya/Karbi-Anglong Plateau** — a north-east extension separated by a fault |

### ⭐ Western vs Eastern Ghats — a favourite comparison

| | **WESTERN GHATS (Sahyadri)** | **EASTERN GHATS** |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuity** | ⭐ **Continuous** — crossed only through **passes** | ⭐ **Discontinuous, irregular** — cut by rivers |
| **Height** | ⭐ **Higher** — 900–1,600 m | Lower — ~600 m |
| **Rainfall** | ⭐ **Very high** (windward, orographic) | Lower |
| **Highest peaks** | **Anai Mudi (2,695 m)** — highest in South India; Doda Betta | **Mahendragiri** |
| **Passes** | ⭐ **Palghat, Thal Ghat, Bhor Ghat** | — |
| **Status** | ⭐ **Biodiversity hotspot**, UNESCO site | — |

> ⭐ **Why the difference?** The peninsula **tilts eastward**. So the major rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) flow **east**, cutting straight through the Eastern Ghats and fragmenting them into detached hill groups. **The Western Ghats, having no large rivers crossing them, stayed continuous.** Drainage explains the relief.

**Also note:** the **Deccan Trap** is black **basalt** of volcanic origin (Cl.7 Ch.2) — weathering into **black soil**, ideal for cotton. And the **Aravalli Hills** are among the world's oldest ranges, now worn to low stubs.

## 6️⃣ 🏜️ The Indian Desert

- **West of the Aravalli Hills** — the **Thar**.
- Undulating sandy plain covered with **sand dunes** — ⭐ **barchans** (crescent-shaped) and longitudinal dunes.
- Rainfall **below 150 mm/year**; arid, low vegetation.
- ⭐ **The Luni is the only large river**, and it is seasonal.

| | ⭐ **WESTERN COASTAL PLAIN** | ⭐ **EASTERN COASTAL PLAIN** |
|---|---|---|
| **Between** | Western Ghats ↔ Arabian Sea | Eastern Ghats ↔ Bay of Bengal |
| **Width** | ⭐ **Narrow** | ⭐ **Broad** |
| **Deltas?** | ❌ No large deltas | ⭐ **Large deltas** — Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri |
| **Divisions** | **Konkan** (north) · **Kannad** (middle) · **Malabar** (south) | **Northern Circars** (north) · **Coromandel** (south) |
| **Features** | **Backwaters** (Kerala) | ⭐ **Lagoons — Chilika** (Odisha, India's largest), **Pulicat** |

## 8️⃣ 🏝️ The Islands

| | ⭐ **LAKSHADWEEP** | ⭐ **ANDAMAN & NICOBAR** |
|---|---|---|
| **Sea** | **Arabian Sea** | **Bay of Bengal** |
| **Origin** | ⭐ **CORAL** — built by coral polyps | ⭐ **Volcanic / tectonic** — tops of a submerged range |
| **Size** | Small — 32 sq km total | Much larger; ~572 islands |
| **Divided** | — | Andaman (north) and Nicobar (south), by the **Ten Degree Channel** |
| **Capital** | **Kavaratti** | **Port Blair** |
| **Notable** | **Pitti** bird sanctuary | ⭐ **Barren Island** — India's **only active volcano**; **Indira Point** |

⚠️ **Do not swap these.** **Lakshadweep = Arabian Sea + coral. Andaman & Nicobar = Bay of Bengal + volcanic.**

> 🌱 **Both groups are ecologically sensitive.** Lakshadweep's coral is vulnerable to bleaching from ocean warming; the Nicobars are part of the **Sundaland biodiversity hotspot**. The **Great Nicobar development project** is a live conservation controversy — **tag for GS-III and current affairs.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **India has the world's youngest mountains and one of its oldest plateaus** — different origins, hundreds of millions of years apart.
2. **Peninsular Plateau = a fragment of Gondwanaland. Himalayas = the crumpled floor of the Tethys Sea.** Marine fossils high in the Himalayas prove it.
3. **Six divisions:** Himalayas · Northern Plains · Peninsular Plateau · Indian Desert · Coastal Plains · Islands.
4. ⭐ **Himadri (highest) > Himachal (Pir Panjal, Kashmir/Kangra/Kullu valleys) > Shiwalik (loose sediment, landslide-prone).** **Duns** between the last two.
5. ⭐ **West–east: Punjab, Kumaon, Nepal, Assam Himalaya**, divided by rivers. Beyond the Dihang gorge: **Purvanchal**.
6. ⭐ **Bhabar swallows the streams; Terai spits them back out.**
7. ⭐ **Khadar = newer, annually flooded, most fertile. Bhangar = older, higher, kankar.**
8. **Majuli = world's largest river island.**
9. **Peninsular Plateau: Central Highlands (north of Narmada) + Deccan Plateau (south).** Chhotanagpur is the mineral belt.
10. ⭐ **Western Ghats continuous and higher; Eastern Ghats discontinuous — because the peninsula tilts east and rivers cut through them.**
11. **Anai Mudi = highest in South India. Kanchenjunga = highest wholly in India.**
12. ⭐ **Lakshadweep = Arabian Sea, coral. Andaman & Nicobar = Bay of Bengal, volcanic.** Separated by the **Ten Degree Channel**.
