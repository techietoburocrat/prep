## 1️⃣ Why People Are the Ultimate Resource

> **Human resources** = people — their **number, education, skills, health and attitude** — which make them able to create and use other resources.

Nature's bounty is only potential until people can use it. **Japan has almost no natural resources and is a leading economy; several resource-rich countries are poor.** The difference is human capital and institutions.

> 🔑 **This closes the argument the book opened with in Ch.1.** "Resources are not, they become" — and **what makes them become is people.** Investment in health and education is therefore not welfare spending competing with development; **it is the highest-return development spending there is.** That framing works in an essay and in GS-II.

## 2️⃣ Distribution and Density

> **Population distribution** = how people are spread across an area.
> **Population density** = the **number of people per square kilometre**.

⚠️ **The two are different questions.** Distribution is about *pattern*; density is about *concentration*. A country can have low average density and extremely uneven distribution — India's Thar vs the Gangetic plain.

### ⭐ Factors affecting distribution

| **GEOGRAPHICAL** | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Topography** | ⭐ **Plains are densely populated**; mountains sparsely — flat land is easy to farm and build on |
| **Climate** | Moderate climates attract; extreme cold/heat repel — Sahara, Antarctica nearly empty |
| **Soil** | Fertile alluvial soils support dense populations — the Ganga plain |
| **Water** | ⭐ **The first requirement.** River valleys are the most densely settled regions on Earth |
| **Minerals** | Attract settlement — mining towns |

| **HUMAN** | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Social** | Better housing, education, health facilities attract people |
| **Cultural** | Religious or historical significance — Varanasi, Jerusalem, Mecca |
| **Economic** | ⭐ **Industrial and job opportunities** — Mumbai, Osaka, Delhi |
| **Political** | Government policy, stability, incentives |

> 🌏 **~90% of the world's people live on about 30% of the land area.** The great concentrations are **South Asia, East Asia, Europe and north-eastern North America** — all of which are river plains or coastal lowlands with moderate climates. **This is Class 6 Ch.6's causal chain (flat land → fertile soil → surplus → dense settlement) shown at world scale.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Population Change

> **Population change** = the change in the number of people over a specific period.

```
   THREE DRIVERS

   ⬆️  BIRTHS      measured as BIRTH RATE
                   = live births per 1,000 people per year

   ⬇️  DEATHS      measured as DEATH RATE
                   = deaths per 1,000 people per year

   ↔️  MIGRATION   movement of people IN or OUT
                   ├── IMMIGRATION  = people coming IN
                   └── EMIGRATION   = people going OUT
```

> ⭐ **NATURAL GROWTH RATE = BIRTH RATE − DEATH RATE.**
> ⚠️ **Note it excludes migration** — that's why it's called *natural*. Total population change includes migration as well.

### Why world population grew so fast

For most of history, growth was very slow: **high birth rates were matched by high death rates.** The explosion since ~1800, and especially since 1950, came almost entirely from **death rates falling** while birth rates stayed high.

**What made death rates fall:** better food supply, clean water and sanitation, vaccines and antibiotics, and public health measures — not any rise in births.

> 🧠 **The insight worth carrying: the population explosion was caused by people ceasing to die, not by people having more children.** That reframing is more accurate and much more useful in an answer than "people had too many children."

### Push and pull factors in migration

| **PUSH** (drive people out) | **PULL** (draw people in) |
|---|---|
| Unemployment, poverty | Jobs, higher wages |
| Drought, floods, disaster | Better living conditions |
| War, conflict, persecution | Security and stability |
| Poor services | Education, healthcare |

## 4️⃣ Population Composition

> **Population composition** = the structure of a population — age, sex, literacy, occupation, income, health.

### ⭐ The age-sex pyramid

A graph showing the population in each age group, males on one side, females on the other.

```
   ⭐ THE THREE SHAPES — AND WHAT EACH MEANS

   1. WIDE BASE (expanding)     2. BARREL (stable)      3. NARROW BASE (contracting)
      ▁▁▁                          ▁▁▁▁▁                     ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁
     ▁▁▁▁▁                        ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                   ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁
    ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                      ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                  ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁
   ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                     ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                   ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁
  ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                    ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                    ▁▁▁▁▁

   High birth rate,              Birth ≈ death rate,      Low birth rate,
   many young people             population steady        ageing population
   e.g. Nigeria, Kenya           e.g. USA, Australia      e.g. JAPAN, Germany
```

> ⭐ **How to read one in three seconds:** look at the **base**. **Wide base = lots of children = growing population.** **Narrow base = few children = shrinking, ageing population.** The **top** tells you life expectancy — a broad top means many elderly.

### Key measures

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Sex ratio** | Number of females per 1,000 males *(India's definition — note some countries invert it)* |
| **Literacy rate** | % of people aged 7+ who can read and write with understanding |
| **Life expectancy** | Average number of years a person is expected to live |
| ⭐ **Dependency ratio** | Ratio of dependants (0–14 and 65+) to the working-age population (15–64) |

## 5️⃣ ⭐ India's Demographic Dividend

India's population pyramid currently has a **large working-age bulge** — a very high share of people aged 15–64 relative to dependants.

> ⭐ **Demographic dividend** = the economic growth potential that arises when the working-age share of the population is large relative to the dependent share.

**Why it helps:** more earners per dependant → higher savings, higher output, higher tax base, more investment.

| ✅ The opportunity | ⚠️ The conditions |
|---|---|
| One of the world's youngest large workforces | ⭐ **It is a window, not a guarantee.** The bulge ages |
| Potential for sustained high growth | Requires **jobs** — a young population without employment is a liability, not an asset |
| Large domestic market | Requires **education and skills** — hence Skill India, NEP 2020 |
| | Requires **health and nutrition** — stunting and anaemia reduce lifetime productivity |
| | ⭐ Requires **female labour force participation**, which in India is low and has been falling |

> 🎯 **The nuance that separates a good answer from an average one:** the demographic dividend is **conditional and time-bound.** East Asia converted its dividend into growth because it created manufacturing jobs and invested heavily in schooling. **A large young population with poor education and no jobs produces unemployment and unrest, not growth.** India's window is estimated to run to roughly the 2040s, after which the population begins ageing.
>
> **And note the internal variation:** southern states (Kerala, Tamil Nadu) are already ageing, while Bihar and UP remain very young. **India will have a demographic dividend and a demographic burden simultaneously, in different states.** That's a sharp, specific point — and it links to delimitation, fiscal devolution and internal migration debates in GS-II.

| Topic | The specific thing |
|---|---|
| **Distribution factors** ⭐⭐⭐ | Geographical (topography, climate, soil, **water**, minerals) + human (social, cultural, economic, political) |
| **Natural growth rate** ⭐⭐⭐ | **Birth rate − death rate** (excludes migration) |
| **Age-sex pyramid shapes** ⭐⭐⭐ | Wide base = growing · barrel = stable · narrow base = ageing |
| **Population density** ⭐⭐ | People per sq km |
| **Immigration vs emigration** ⭐⭐ | In vs out |
| **Sex ratio (India)** ⭐⭐ | Females per 1,000 males |
| **Dependency ratio** ⭐⭐ | Dependants (0–14, 65+) to working age (15–64) |
| ⭐ **Demographic dividend** ⭐⭐⭐ | Conditional and time-bound; needs jobs, skills, health, female participation |
| **~90% of people on ~30% of land** ⭐ | Concentrated in river plains and coastal lowlands |

**Trap 1:** *"Natural growth rate includes migration."* **It does not** — that's why it's "natural."
**Trap 2:** *"A wide-based pyramid means an ageing population."* **The opposite** — it means many children and a growing population.
**Trap 3:** *"Population distribution and density mean the same thing."* Distribution = pattern; density = people per sq km.
**Trap 4:** *"The demographic dividend is automatic."* ⭐ **It requires jobs, education and health.** Otherwise it becomes a liability.
**Trap 5:** *"Sex ratio is males per 1,000 females."* In India it is **females per 1,000 males.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **People are the ultimate resource** — nothing becomes a resource until someone knows how to use it. This closes Ch.1's argument.
2. **Distribution = pattern. Density = people per sq km.** Different questions.
3. ⭐ **Geographical factors: topography, climate, soil, water, minerals. Human factors: social, cultural, economic, political.**
4. **~90% of the world's people live on ~30% of the land** — river plains and coastal lowlands.
5. ⭐ **Natural growth rate = birth rate − death rate.** Migration is excluded.
6. ⭐ **The population explosion happened because death rates fell**, not because birth rates rose.
7. **Migration is driven by push (unemployment, disaster, conflict) and pull (jobs, services, security) factors.**
8. ⭐ **Pyramid shapes: wide base = growing (Nigeria) · barrel = stable (USA) · narrow base = ageing (Japan).** Read the base first.
9. **Sex ratio in India = females per 1,000 males.** Dependency ratio = dependants to working age.
10. ⭐ **India's demographic dividend is real but conditional and time-bound** — it needs jobs, education, health and female participation, and the window runs to roughly the 2040s.
11. **India will have a dividend and a burden at once** — young northern states, ageing southern ones.
