# Chapter 3 — Mineral and Power Resources 🌱

> **The one-line point:** Minerals are unevenly scattered, finite, and take millions of years to form — which makes **where they are** a permanent fact of world politics. And the energy built on them is now splitting into two camps: **conventional** (fossil, finite, polluting) and **non-conventional** (renewable, clean, and rapidly getting cheaper).

## 1️⃣ Minerals

> **Mineral** = a naturally occurring substance with a definite chemical composition.
> **Ore** = a rock from which a mineral can be **profitably** extracted.

⚠️ **Not every mineral deposit is an ore.** If extraction costs more than the mineral is worth, it stays rock. **"Ore" is an economic word, not a geological one** — the same point as Ch.1's "resources become."

### ⭐ Classification

```
   MINERALS
      │
      ├── METALLIC ──┬── FERROUS (contain IRON)
      │              │     Iron ore, manganese, nickel, cobalt, chromite
      │              │
      │              └── NON-FERROUS (no iron)
      │                    Copper, bauxite (aluminium), lead, zinc, gold, silver, tin
      │
      ├── NON-METALLIC
      │      Limestone, mica, gypsum, dolomite, salt, potash, DIAMOND
      │
      └── MINERAL FUELS (energy minerals)
             Coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium
```

> 🧠 **Ferrous = ferrum = iron.** If the name reminds you of iron, it's ferrous. Manganese, nickel, cobalt and chromite are the ones people forget belong here — they're alloyed **with** iron to make steel.

### Where minerals are found

| Occurrence | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Igneous & metamorphic rock** | In **cracks, crevices, faults and joints** — smaller deposits called **veins**, larger ones **lodes** |
| **Sedimentary rock** | In **horizontal beds/layers** — coal, limestone, potash, gypsum |
| **Decomposition of surface rock** | Leaves behind ores — **bauxite** forms this way |
| **Alluvial / placer deposits** | Found in **valley floors and sands** — **gold, silver, tin, platinum**, because they resist chemical weathering |
| **Ocean water** | Contains dissolved minerals — **common salt, magnesium, bromine** |

> 💰 **Why placer deposits contain gold specifically:** gold, platinum and tin do **not dissolve or corrode**. When gold-bearing rock weathers, most minerals break down chemically, but the gold survives as particles, is washed downstream, and settles in riverbeds because it is dense. **That is why panning for gold in rivers works** — the river has already done the sorting.

### 🌍 Global distribution — the big producers

| Mineral | Major producers |
|---|---|
| **Iron ore** | ⭐ Australia, Brazil, China, India, Russia |
| **Coal** | ⭐ China, India, USA, Australia, Indonesia |
| **Petroleum** | ⭐ **West Asia (Gulf)**, USA, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Venezuela |
| **Bauxite** | Australia, Guinea, China, Brazil |
| **Copper** | ⭐ **Chile**, Peru, USA |
| **Gold** | China, Australia, Russia, South Africa |
| **Manganese** | South Africa, Australia, **India** |
| **Tin** | China, Indonesia, Myanmar |

### 🇮🇳 Indian distribution — the essentials

| Mineral | Where |
|---|---|
| **Iron ore** | ⭐ **Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka** — the Chhotanagpur belt |
| **Coal** | ⭐ **Jharkhand (Jharia, Bokaro), Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal (Raniganj), MP** |
| **Bauxite** | Odisha, Jharkhand, Gujarat, Maharashtra |
| **Manganese** | Odisha, Karnataka, MP, Maharashtra |
| **Mica** | ⭐ **Jharkhand, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan** — India is a leading producer |
| **Copper** | Rajasthan (Khetri), Jharkhand, MP (Malanjkhand) |
| **Petroleum** | ⭐ **Mumbai High (offshore), Gujarat (Ankleshwar), Assam (Digboi)** |
| **Limestone** | MP, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat |
| **Gold** | Kolar (Karnataka — now largely closed), Hutti |

> ⛏️ **Notice the pattern:** India's mineral wealth is overwhelmingly concentrated in the **Chhotanagpur Plateau** — Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal. **This follows directly from Cl.6 Ch.6:** old, stable, deeply eroded plateau rock exposes ore. It's why India's steel plants (Jamshedpur, Rourkela, Bhilai, Durgapur, Bokaro) all cluster there. **Geology → minerals → industry → regional economy.**
>
> 🇮🇳 **Digboi (Assam) was the site of Asia's first oil well (1889)** — a favourite one-liner.

### Mining methods

| Method | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Open-cast / surface mining** | Minerals **near the surface** are removed by stripping off the overburden. Cheaper, safer, but hugely destructive to the landscape |
| ⭐ **Shaft mining** | Deep vertical shafts sunk to reach minerals **far below the surface**. Expensive, dangerous |
| **Quarrying** | Minerals near the surface simply **dug out** — stone, marble |
| **Drilling** | Deep wells bored to bring up **liquids and gases** — petroleum, natural gas |

> 🌱 **The environmental cost of mining** — worth naming specifically for GS-III: **land degradation**, deforestation, **acid mine drainage** contaminating water, dust and air pollution, subsidence, and displacement of local (often tribal) populations. **India's mineral belt overlaps almost exactly with its tribal belt and its densest forests** — which is why mining conflicts in Jharkhand, Odisha and Chhattisgarh are simultaneously environmental, constitutional (Fifth Schedule, PESA, Forest Rights Act) and law-and-order issues. **This overlap is a strong GS-II/GS-III point.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Power Resources

### The two families

| | ⛽ **CONVENTIONAL** | ☀️ **NON-CONVENTIONAL** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | Mostly **non-renewable**; long-established | **Renewable**; newer at scale |
| **Includes** | **Firewood**, **fossil fuels** (coal, petroleum, natural gas), **hydel**, **nuclear** | **Solar, wind, tidal, geothermal, biogas** |
| **Pollution** | High (except hydel/nuclear at point of use) | Low |
| **Availability** | Finite; unevenly distributed | Effectively unlimited; but site-dependent |

⚠️ **Note the two that trip people up: hydel and nuclear are classed as CONVENTIONAL.** Hydel is renewable but conventional; nuclear is non-renewable and conventional. **Conventional/non-conventional is about how long-established the source is, not about whether it renews.**

### Conventional sources

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Firewood** | Still a major domestic fuel in rural India |
| ⭐ **Coal** | The most abundant fossil fuel. Called **"Black Gold"**. Types by carbon content: **peat < lignite < bituminous < anthracite** (anthracite is the highest quality) |
| ⭐ **Petroleum** | **"Liquid Gold."** Found between sedimentary rock layers. Refined into petrol, diesel, kerosene, plastics |
| **Natural gas** | Cleanest fossil fuel. Found with petroleum. **CNG/PNG** |
| ⭐ **Hydel power** | From falling water. **~1/4 of world electricity.** India: Bhakra Nangal, Damodar Valley, Nagarjuna Sagar |
| ⭐ **Nuclear** | From **uranium and thorium**. India's uranium: Jharkhand, Rajasthan (Aravalli). ⭐ **Thorium: Kerala's monazite sands** — India has among the world's largest thorium reserves |

> 🇮🇳 **The thorium point is genuinely important for India** and shows up in S&T and energy questions. India has limited uranium but **vast thorium reserves in the monazite sands of Kerala and Odisha's beaches.** This is why India's nuclear programme is built as a **three-stage plan** designed to eventually run on thorium. **Cross-tag to Science & Technology.**

### 🌱 Non-conventional sources

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| ☀️ **Solar** | Photovoltaic cells. ⭐ India's potential is huge — **Rajasthan, Gujarat**. **Bhadla Solar Park (Rajasthan)** is among the world's largest |
| 💨 **Wind** | ⭐ India is among the world's largest wind-power producers. **Tamil Nadu (Muppandal), Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka** |
| 🌊 **Tidal** | From tidal movement. Potential in the **Gulf of Khambhat and Gulf of Kachchh** (Cl.7 Ch.5) |
| 🌋 **Geothermal** | Heat from the Earth's interior. **Manikaran (HP), Puga Valley (Ladakh)**. Global leaders: **Iceland, USA, New Zealand** |
| 🔥 **Biogas** | From organic waste and dung. Rural India — **gobar gas plants** |

> 🌱 **Why the transition is happening — three reasons, and it's worth being able to give all three:**
> 1. **Fossil fuels are finite** and increasingly costly to extract.
> 2. **They drive climate change** — the enhanced greenhouse effect (Cl.6 Ch.1).
> 3. ⭐ **Renewables have become cheaper.** Solar costs have fallen dramatically; in India solar power is now often cheaper per unit than new coal. **This is the decisive change — the transition is now economic, not only environmental.** Answers that mention only the environmental argument miss the strongest one.

> 🇮🇳 **India's commitments — high-value for [Current Affairs](../../../../Current%20Affairs/00_START_HERE.md) and GS-III:**
> - **500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030**
> - **Net zero by 2070**
> - **International Solar Alliance (ISA)** — co-founded and headquartered in India (Gurugram)
> - **National Solar Mission**, PM-KUSUM, PM Surya Ghar
>
> ⚠️ **The honest tension worth acknowledging in an answer:** India still generates the majority of its electricity from **coal**, has large coal reserves, and coal employs a great many people. **The transition is a genuine trilemma — energy security, affordability, and sustainability — not a simple choice.** Saying so is more credible than pretending it's easy.

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mineral** | Naturally occurring substance of definite chemical composition |
| **Ore** | Rock from which a mineral can be **profitably** extracted |
| **Ferrous** | Metallic minerals containing **iron** |
| **Non-ferrous** | Metallic minerals without iron — copper, bauxite, gold |
| **Vein / lode** | Smaller / larger mineral deposits in rock cracks |
| **Placer deposit** | Minerals concentrated in valley floors and sands — gold, tin |
| **Open-cast mining** | Surface mining by stripping overburden |
| **Shaft mining** | Deep vertical shafts to reach buried minerals |
| **Quarrying / drilling** | Digging surface stone / boring wells for liquids and gases |
| **Conventional energy** | Long-established — firewood, fossil fuels, **hydel, nuclear** |
| **Non-conventional energy** | Solar, wind, tidal, geothermal, biogas |
| **Coal grades** | Peat → lignite → bituminous → **anthracite** |
| **Monazite sands** | Kerala beach sands rich in **thorium** |
| 🌱 **ISA** | International Solar Alliance, headquartered in India |
| 🌱 **Net zero** | Balancing emissions produced with emissions removed |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Mineral = definite chemical composition. Ore = profitably extractable.** "Ore" is an economic word.
2. ⭐ **Metallic (ferrous / non-ferrous) · non-metallic · mineral fuels.** Manganese, nickel, cobalt, chromite are **ferrous**.
3. **Placer deposits hold gold, silver, tin, platinum** — because they resist weathering and the river sorts them by density.
4. ⭐ **India's mineral belt = Chhotanagpur Plateau** (Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, WB) — which is why the steel plants are there.
5. **Petroleum: Mumbai High, Gujarat, Assam. Digboi = Asia's first oil well (1889).**
6. **Mining: open-cast (surface), shaft (deep), quarrying (stone), drilling (liquids/gases).**
7. 🌱 **India's mineral belt overlaps its tribal belt and its forests** — hence mining conflicts are simultaneously environmental, constitutional and law-and-order issues.
8. ⚠️ ⭐ **Conventional = firewood, fossil fuels, HYDEL and NUCLEAR.** Non-conventional = solar, wind, tidal, geothermal, biogas.
9. **Coal grades: peat < lignite < bituminous < anthracite.**
10. ⭐ **Thorium in Kerala's monazite sands** underpins India's three-stage nuclear programme.
11. 🌱 **The energy transition is driven by cost as much as by climate** — solar is now often cheaper than new coal.
12. 🇮🇳 **500 GW non-fossil by 2030; net zero by 2070; ISA headquartered in Gurugram.**
