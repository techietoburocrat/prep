# Chapter 1 — Resources 🌱

> **The one-line point:** Nothing is a resource by nature. **A substance becomes a resource only when people have a use for it, the technology to extract it, and a reason to want it.** Coal was worthless black rock for thousands of years. Uranium was a curiosity until 1945. **Resources are created by human need and knowledge, not found.**

## 1️⃣ What Makes Something a Resource

> **Resource** = anything that has **utility** and **value** — that can be used to satisfy a human need.

**Three conditions must all be met:**

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  1. UTILITY          — it must be USEFUL         │
   │  2. TECHNOLOGY       — we must be able to        │
   │                        ACCESS and use it         │
   │  3. ACCESSIBILITY /  — it must be economically   │
   │     ECONOMIC VIABILITY  worth extracting         │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> ⭐ **This is why "resources are not, they become."**
>
> | Substance | Before | After |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Coal** | Useless black rock | Fuel of the Industrial Revolution — once the steam engine existed |
> | **Petroleum** | A nuisance seeping into fields | The most strategically important commodity on Earth |
> | **Uranium** | A laboratory oddity | Nuclear fuel — after 1945 |
> | **Silicon (sand)** | Sand | The basis of the entire computing industry |
> | **Lithium** | Obscure metal | Critical mineral, because of batteries |
>
> **In every case the substance didn't change. Human knowledge did.** That's a strong, compact idea for an essay opening.

> 🌱 **And the reverse also happens** — resources can *stop* being resources. Whale oil lit the world until kerosene replaced it. Coal is being deliberately abandoned despite abundant reserves. **Technology and values can both destroy a resource's value.** This is exactly what "stranded assets" means in climate economics.

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Classification of Resources

### By origin

| | **NATURAL** | **HUMAN-MADE** | **HUMAN** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What** | Drawn from nature, used with little modification | Things people build using natural resources | ⭐ **People themselves** — their number, skill, knowledge, health |
| **Examples** | Air, water, soil, minerals, forests | Buildings, roads, machinery, bridges, technology | Doctors, engineers, teachers, farmers |

### Natural resources, sub-classified

```
   NATURAL RESOURCES
        │
        ├── BY ORIGIN
        │     ├── BIOTIC      — from living things (plants, animals, fossil fuels)
        │     └── ABIOTIC     — from non-living things (rocks, metals, air, water)
        │
        ├── BY EXHAUSTIBILITY
        │     ├── RENEWABLE      — replenish naturally (solar, wind, water, forest)
        │     └── NON-RENEWABLE  — limited stock, take millions of years to form
        │                          (coal, petroleum, minerals)
        │
        ├── BY DISTRIBUTION
        │     ├── UBIQUITOUS  — found everywhere (air)
        │     └── LOCALISED   — found only in some places (copper, iron ore)
        │
        └── BY DEVELOPMENT
              ├── ACTUAL     — quantity is KNOWN and currently BEING USED
              │                 (e.g. petroleum in West Asia)
              └── POTENTIAL  — exists but is NOT yet used
                                (e.g. solar potential in Rajasthan, uranium in Ladakh)
```

> ⚠️ **Actual vs potential is a favourite Prelims distinction.** **Actual** = surveyed, quantified, being used now. **Potential** = known to exist but not yet exploited, whether for technological, economic or political reasons. Rajasthan's solar potential is enormous and only partly actualised — it's the standard Indian example.

> 🌱 **The complication in "renewable" that's worth knowing.** Renewable does not mean inexhaustible. **A forest renews itself only if cut slower than it regrows.** Groundwater recharges only if pumped slower than it refills. **Renewability is a rate, not a property.**
>
> This is exactly India's groundwater crisis: water is renewable, and India is still depleting it, because extraction outpaces recharge. **Saying "renewable resources can also be exhausted if used faster than they regenerate" in a GS-III answer shows real understanding.**

## 3️⃣ Conservation

> **Conservation** = using resources **carefully and giving them time to renew.**

Not the same as non-use. It means matching consumption to regeneration, avoiding waste, and substituting scarce resources with abundant ones.

---

## 4️⃣ ⭐🌱 Sustainable Development

> **Sustainable development** = development that meets the needs of the **present** without compromising the ability of **future generations** to meet their own needs.

*(The Brundtland Commission definition, 1987 — worth being able to state verbatim.)*

### The principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Respect and care for all forms of life** | Not only human interests count |
| **Improve quality of human life** | Development is still the goal — this is not anti-development |
| **Conserve the Earth's vitality and diversity** | Protect ecosystems and species |
| **Minimise depletion of natural resources** | Use less, use efficiently |
| **Change personal attitudes and practices** | Individual behaviour matters |
| **Enable communities to care for their own environments** | Local control, not only central regulation |

> ⭐ **The core of the idea is intergenerational equity** — the notion that people not yet born have a claim on resources we are currently spending. That is an **ethical** proposition as much as an economic one, which is why it connects directly to your [Ethics notes](../../../../Ethics/00_START_HERE.md).
>
> 🎯 **The nuance that scores in Mains:** sustainable development is not "stop developing." It explicitly commits to **improving quality of life**. The tension is between *how fast* and *at what cost*, not between development and conservation as such. Answers that frame it as an either/or lose marks; answers that frame it as a **rate and distribution problem** do well.

> 🇮🇳 **Where to attach this:** the **UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** — 17 goals adopted in 2015, target year 2030. India reports progress via **NITI Aayog's SDG India Index**. **Tag for GS-II and GS-III**, and for [Current Affairs](../../../../Current%20Affairs/00_START_HERE.md).

| **Biotic vs abiotic** ⭐⭐ | From living vs non-living sources |
| **Three conditions for a resource** ⭐⭐ | Utility + technology + economic viability |

| **SDGs** ⭐⭐ | 17 goals, adopted 2015, target 2030 |

**Trap 1:** *"Renewable resources cannot be exhausted."* **They can** — if used faster than they regenerate. Groundwater and forests are the standard examples.
**Trap 2:** *"Fossil fuels are abiotic."* ⚠️ **Biotic** — they formed from ancient living matter, despite being found in rock.
**Trap 3:** *"Sustainable development means limiting development."* **No** — it explicitly includes improving quality of life.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **"Resources are not, they become."** Something is a resource only when utility, technology and economic viability all exist.
2. **Coal, petroleum, uranium, silicon, lithium** — the substances never changed; human knowledge did.
3. **Three origins: natural, human-made, human.**
4. **Biotic (from living, including fossil fuels) vs abiotic (from non-living).**
5. **Renewable vs non-renewable** — and ⭐ **renewability is a rate**, so forests and groundwater *can* be exhausted.
6. ⭐ **Actual = known and in use. Potential = exists but unexploited** (Rajasthan's solar).
7. **Ubiquitous vs localised** — air vs iron ore.
8. **Conservation = using at a rate that permits renewal**, not non-use.
9. ⭐ 🌱 **Sustainable development (Brundtland 1987): meeting present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs.**
10. **It is not anti-development** — it explicitly commits to improving quality of life. The question is rate and distribution.
11. **People are the ultimate resource** — nothing is a resource until someone knows how to use it.
