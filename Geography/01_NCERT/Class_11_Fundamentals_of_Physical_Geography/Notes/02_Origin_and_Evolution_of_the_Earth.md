# Chapter 2 — The Origin and Evolution of the Earth

> **The one-line point:** The Earth began as a cloud of gas and dust, heated, **sorted itself by density into layers**, and then slowly outgassed the atmosphere and oceans from its own interior. **That density sorting is why the core is iron and the crust is silica — and it is the single fact this chapter exists to establish.**

## 1️⃣ Early Theories

| Theory | Proposed by | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Nebular Hypothesis** | **Immanuel Kant (1755)**, revised by **Laplace (1796)** | Planets formed from a **cloud of material associated with a youthful sun**, which was slowly rotating |
| **Planetesimal Hypothesis** | **Chamberlin and Moulton (1900)** | **A wandering star approached the sun**, pulling out cigar-shaped material which condensed into **planetesimals** that later formed planets |
| **Binary Theories** | **Sir James Jeans** and later **Sir Harold Jeffreys** | Also invoked a **companion star** to the sun |
| **Revised nebular hypothesis** | **Otto Schmidt (Russia)** and **Carl Weizsäcker (Germany)** | **The sun was surrounded by a solar nebula containing mostly hydrogen and helium along with dust.** Friction and collision led to the formation of a **disc-shaped cloud**, and planets formed through **accretion** |

> **The pattern worth noticing:** early theories all needed a **second body** — a passing star — to pull material out of the sun. Modern theories don't. **The material was already there, in the nebula that formed the sun itself.** Planets are the **leftovers of star formation**, not debris torn from a star.

## 2️⃣ Modern Theory — The Big Bang

**The most popular argument regarding the origin of the universe is the BIG BANG THEORY.** **It is also called the EXPANDING UNIVERSE HYPOTHESIS.** **Edwin Hubble, in 1920, provided evidence that the universe is expanding.**

### The three stages

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| **(i)** | **In the beginning, all matter forming the universe existed in one place in the form of a "tiny ball" (singular atom) with an unimaginably small volume, infinite temperature and infinite density** |
| **(ii)** | **At the Big Bang, the "tiny ball" exploded violently. This led to a huge expansion.** **It is now generally accepted that the event of the Big Bang took place 13.7 billion years before the present.** **The expansion continues even to the present day.** As it grew, some energy was converted into matter. **There was particularly rapid expansion within fractions of a second after the bang — thereafter, expansion slowed down.** **Within the first three minutes from the Big Bang event, the first atom began to form** |
| **(iii)** | **Within 300,000 years from the Big Bang, temperature dropped to 4,500 K (Kelvin) and gave rise to atomic matter. The universe became transparent** |

⚠️ **The expansion of the universe means increase in space between the galaxies.** **An alternative to this was Hoyle's concept of a STEADY STATE — it considered the universe to be roughly the same at any point of time. However, with greater evidence becoming available about the expanding universe, scientific community at present favours the argument of expanding universe.**

> **The critical nuance about what "expanding" means** — a genuine conceptual trap: the Big Bang was **not an explosion *into* pre-existing space.** **It was an expansion *of* space itself.** Galaxies are not flying outward through space like shrapnel; **the space between them is stretching.** There is no centre and no edge to point to.
>
> 🍞 **The comparison that fits: raisins in rising bread dough.** As the dough expands, every raisin moves away from every other raisin — and from the point of view of any one raisin, all the others are receding. **No raisin is at the centre; the dough itself is growing.** That is exactly what Hubble observed: every distant galaxy receding from us, and the more distant ones receding faster.

### The formation of stars and planets

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nebula** | **The distribution of matter and energy was not even in the early universe.** These differences caused **differences in gravitational forces**, and matter got drawn together, forming **galaxies** |
| **Galaxy formation** | **A galaxy starts to form by accumulation of hydrogen gas in the form of a very large cloud called a NEBULA.** **Eventually, growing nebula develops localised clumps of gas. These clumps continue to grow into even denser gaseous bodies, giving rise to formation of stars.** **The formation of stars is believed to have taken place some 5–6 billion years ago** |
| **Light year** | **Distances in space are measured in light years.** **One light year = 9.461 × 10¹² km.** **The mean distance between the sun and the earth is 149,598,000 km — in terms of light years, it is 8.311 minutes** |

## 3️⃣ The Origin of the Earth — Modern View

**The universe is made up of about 2,000 billion galaxies. Our galaxy is the MILKY WAY.** **Its shape is like a saucer with a convex centre. It contains about 2,000 crore stars.**

**In the solar system, the sun is at the centre. Around it revolve eight planets.** **Our solar system consists of the sun (the star), 8 planets, 63 moons, millions of smaller bodies like asteroids and comets and huge quantity of dust-grains and gases.**

### How the planets formed — the accepted three-stage sequence

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **(i)** | **The stars are localised lumps of gas within a nebula. The gravitational force within the lumps leads to the formation of a core to the gas cloud and a huge rotating disc of gas and dust develops around the gas core** |
| **(ii)** | **In the next stage, the gas cloud starts getting condensed and the matter around the core develops into small-rounded objects. These small-rounded objects by the process of COHESION develop into what is called PLANETESIMALS.** **Larger bodies start forming by collision, and gravitational attraction causes the material to stick together** |
| **(iii)** | **In the final stage, these large number of small planetesimals accrete to form a fewer large bodies in the form of planets** |

### Why the inner and outer planets differ

**The planets were formed within the same nebular cloud, so they share a common origin — yet they differ sharply.** The reason:

| | Inner (terrestrial) | Outer (Jovian) |
|---|---|---|
| **Formed** | **Close to the parent star**, where it was **too warm for gases to condense to solid particles** | **Far away**, where it was **cold enough for gases to condense** |
| **Solar wind** | **Solar wind was most intense nearer the sun, so it stripped off much of the gas and dust from the terrestrial planets** | **Solar wind was weaker at greater distances, so gases were retained** |
| **Size** | **Smaller — so their lower gravity could not hold escaping gases** | **Much larger — higher gravity retained gases** |

> **Three reinforcing reasons, not one.** Temperature, solar wind and gravity all pushed in the same direction. **This is a much better answer than "the inner planets are rocky because they're close to the sun."**

## 4️⃣ The Moon, and How the Earth Became Layered

### Origin of the Moon

**In 1838, Sir George Darwin suggested that the Earth and Moon were one, and the Moon separated from what we have at present the depression occupied by the Pacific Ocean.**

**The currently accepted argument is the GIANT IMPACT HYPOTHESIS ("the big splat"):** **A body of the size of one to three times that of Mars collided into the Earth sometime shortly after the Earth was formed.** **It blasted a large part of the Earth into space. This portion of blasted material then continued to orbit the Earth and eventually formed into the present Moon about 4.44 billion years ago.**

### Evolution of the Earth — DIFFERENTIATION

**This is the most important section of the chapter.**

**The Earth was mostly in a volatile state during its primordial stage. Due to gradual increase in density the temperature inside has increased.** **As a result the material inside started getting separated depending on their densities.**

```
   DENSITY SORTING — WHY THE EARTH IS LAYERED

   A hot, molten, homogeneous ball
              ↓
   HEAVIER materials (IRON, NICKEL) SINK toward the centre
   LIGHTER materials (SILICA) RISE toward the surface
              ↓
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  CRUST     — lightest (silica, alumina)  │
   │  MANTLE    — intermediate                │
   │  CORE      — heaviest (iron, nickel)     │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────┘

   This process is called DIFFERENTIATION.
```

**This allowed one of the heaviest elements, iron, to sink towards the centre of the Earth, and the lighter ones to move towards the surface.** **With passage of time it cooled further and solidified and condensed into a smaller size. This later led to the development of the outer surface in the form of a crust.**

**During the formation of the moon, due to the giant impact, the earth was further heated up.** **It is through the process of DIFFERENTIATION that the earth forming material got separated into different layers.** **Starting from the surface to the central parts, we have layers like the crust, mantle, outer core and inner core.**

> **Why this section is load-bearing.** It explains, in one mechanism, several facts you will need repeatedly:
> - **Why the core is iron and nickel** and the crust is silica-rich (Ch.3)
> - **Why the Earth has a magnetic field** — a liquid iron outer core in motion (Ch.3), which gives us the compass and shields us from solar radiation
> - **Why continental crust (SIAL, lighter) "floats" higher than oceanic crust (SIMA, denser)** — the basis of isostasy and of why continents stand above sea level at all (Ch.4)
>
> **One process — density sorting — explains the entire vertical structure of the planet.** Learn it as a mechanism and Chapter 3 becomes almost free.

## 5️⃣ Origin of the Atmosphere and the Oceans

**The present composition of Earth's atmosphere is chiefly contributed by nitrogen and oxygen. There are three stages in the evolution of the present atmosphere.**

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| **(i)** | **The primordial atmosphere — loss of the primordial atmosphere.** The early atmosphere, with hydrogen and helium, is supposed to have been **stripped off as a result of the SOLAR WINDS** |
| **(ii)** | **DEGASSING — the evolution of the present atmosphere.** **The hot interior of the earth contributed to the evolution of the atmosphere.** **During the cooling of the earth, gases and water vapour were released from the interior solid earth. This started the evolution of the present atmosphere.** **The early atmosphere largely contained water vapour, nitrogen, carbon dioxide, methane, ammonia and very little of free oxygen.** **The process through which the gases were outpoured from the interior is called DEGASSING** |
| **(iii)** | **Modification of the atmosphere by the living world (PHOTOSYNTHESIS).** **Continuous volcanic eruptions contributed water vapour and gases to the atmosphere. As the earth cooled, the water vapour released started getting condensed. The carbon dioxide in the atmosphere got dissolved in rainwater and the temperature further decreased causing more condensation of water vapour** |

### Origin of life

**The rainwater falling onto the surface got collected in the depressions to give rise to oceans.** **The earth's oceans were formed within 500 million years from the formation of the earth. This tells us that the oceans are as old as 4,000 million years.**

**Sometime around 3,800 million years ago, life began to evolve.** **However, around 2,500–3,000 million years before the present, the process of PHOTOSYNTHESIS got evolved.** **Life was confined to the oceans for a long time. Oceans began to have the contribution of oxygen through the process of photosynthesis. Eventually, oceans were saturated with oxygen, and 2,000 million years ago, oxygen began to flood the atmosphere.**

> 🌱 **This is one of the most important ideas in environmental science, and it is easy to miss.**
>
> **The Earth's oxygen-rich atmosphere was CREATED BY LIFE.** It is not an original feature of the planet. Early Earth's atmosphere had **very little free oxygen** — it was water vapour, nitrogen, CO₂, methane and ammonia. Photosynthesising organisms produced oxygen for hundreds of millions of years, first saturating the oceans, then flooding the atmosphere (the "Great Oxygenation Event").
>
> **Two consequences worth carrying:**
> 1. **Life and the atmosphere are mutually constituted.** Life did not merely adapt to the planet — **it remade it.** The very oxygen you are breathing is a biological product.
> 2. 🌱 **It also means the atmosphere is not a fixed backdrop.** Its composition has changed radically before, driven by living things. **That is precisely why anthropogenic changes to atmospheric composition are credible — there is precedent, on a planetary scale.**
>
> 🎯 **In a GS-III climate answer, "the atmosphere's composition has been biologically altered before, and is being altered again — this time by one species in two centuries rather than by all life over two billion years" is a compact and striking opening.**

## 6️⃣ The Geological Time Scale

Broad divisions, from oldest to youngest:

| Era | Period | Approx. years before present | Life |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PRE-CAMBRIAN** | — | 4,600–570 million | Origin of life; earliest bacteria and algae |
| **PALAEOZOIC** | Cambrian, Ordovician, Silurian, Devonian, Carboniferous, Permian | 570–245 million | Marine invertebrates, fish, amphibians, reptiles; **coal-forming forests (Carboniferous)** |
| **MESOZOIC** | Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous | 245–65 million | **Age of dinosaurs**; first birds and mammals. **Ends with the mass extinction ~65 million years ago** |
| **CENOZOIC** | Tertiary (Palaeocene → Pliocene), Quaternary (Pleistocene, Holocene) | 65 million – present | **Age of mammals.** **Homo sapiens appears in the Pleistocene; Holocene is the present epoch (~10,000 yrs)** |

**Note the origins that matter for geography:** **Gondwana coal is over 200 million years old (Carboniferous/Permian, Palaeozoic); tertiary coal is ~55 million years old (Cenozoic)** — the pairing from Cl.10 Ch.5. **Petroleum is found in tertiary rock formations.** **The Himalayas began forming in the Tertiary.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Early theories (Kant 1755, Laplace 1796, Chamberlin & Moulton 1900, Jeans, Jeffreys) all needed a second star.** Modern ones don't — planets are leftovers of star formation.
2. **Schmidt and Weizsäcker's revised nebular hypothesis** — solar nebula of hydrogen, helium and dust; disc-shaped cloud; accretion.
3. **Big Bang = 13.7 billion years ago. Hubble (1920) showed the universe is expanding.** Three stages: tiny ball → explosion (first atom within 3 minutes) → 300,000 yrs, 4,500 K, universe transparent.
4. **"Expanding" means space itself is stretching** — like raisins in rising dough. No centre, no edge. **Hoyle's steady state is the rejected alternative.**
5. **Planets formed in three stages: gas core + rotating disc → planetesimals by cohesion → accretion into planets.**
6. **Inner/outer difference has three reinforcing causes:** too warm to condense gases, intense solar wind stripping them, and lower gravity failing to hold them.
7. **Moon: giant impact ("big splat") by a Mars-sized body; formed ~4.44 billion years ago.** Sir George Darwin's 1838 Pacific-depression idea is the historical alternative.
8. **DIFFERENTIATION — density sorting — gave the Earth its layers.** Iron sank to the core; silica rose to the crust. **One mechanism explains the whole vertical structure, the magnetic field, and why continents float higher than ocean floors.**
9. **DEGASSING built the atmosphere** from the Earth's own interior — water vapour, nitrogen, CO₂, methane, ammonia, **very little free oxygen.**
10. **Oceans formed within 500 million years — they are ~4,000 million years old.** Life began ~3,800 mya; photosynthesis evolved 2,500–3,000 mya.
11. 🌱 **Oxygen flooded the atmosphere ~2,000 million years ago — and it was CREATED BY LIFE.** The atmosphere is a biological product, not a fixed backdrop. **That precedent is what makes anthropogenic atmospheric change credible.**
