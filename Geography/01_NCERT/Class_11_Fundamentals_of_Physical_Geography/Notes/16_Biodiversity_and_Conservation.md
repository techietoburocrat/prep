## 1️⃣ What Biodiversity Is

> **BIODIVERSITY as we perceive today, is a result of some 2.5–3.5 billion years of evolution.**

**Biodiversity is the sum total of all the varieties of species of plants, animals and micro-organisms living on the earth.** **It also includes the habitats in which they live.**

**The term 'biodiversity' is a shortened form of two words 'biological' and 'diversity'.** **It refers to all the variety of life that can be found on Earth (plants, animals, fungi and micro-organisms) as well as to the communities that they form and the habitats in which they live.**

### The three levels — a very frequently asked classification

| Level | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1. GENETIC DIVERSITY** | **Genes are the basic building blocks of various forms of life.** **Genetic biodiversity refers to the variation of genes within species.** **Groups of individual organisms having certain similarities in their physical characteristics are called SPECIES.** **Human beings genetically belong to the homo sapiens group and also differ in their characteristics such as height, colour, physical appearance etc., considerably.** **This is due to genetic diversity.** **This genetic diversity is essential for a healthy breeding of population of species** |
| **2. SPECIES DIVERSITY** | **This refers to the variety of species.** **It relates to the number of species in a defined area.** **The diversity of species can be measured through its richness, abundance and types.** **Some areas are more rich in species than others.** **Areas rich in species diversity are called HOTSPOTS of diversity** |
| **3. ECOSYSTEM DIVERSITY** | **You have read about the ecosystem in the previous chapter.** **The broad differences between ecosystem types and the diversity of habitats and ecological processes occurring within each ecosystem type constitute the ecosystem diversity.** **The boundaries of communities (associations of species) and ecosystems are not very rigidly defined.** **Thus, the demarcation of ecosystem boundaries is difficult and complex** |

> **Why genetic diversity matters more than most candidates realise — and this makes a strong Mains point.**
>
> **A species with low genetic diversity is a species with no options.** If every individual is nearly identical, then **one disease, one pest or one climatic shift can eliminate all of them at once** — none has the variant gene that would have conferred resistance.
>
> **The historical case: the Irish potato famine (1845).** Ireland grew a **genetically near-uniform potato crop**, and a single blight destroyed it entirely. **A genetically diverse crop would have lost part of the harvest, not all of it.**
>
> 🌱 **This is exactly why the loss of traditional crop varieties matters** — India once grew tens of thousands of rice varieties and now depends heavily on a handful of high-yielding ones. **The Green Revolution raised yields and narrowed the gene pool at the same time.** **It is also why seed banks, and movements like Navdanya and Beej Bachao Andolan (Cl.10 Ch.2), are a food-security measure rather than a sentimental one.**

## 2️⃣ Importance of Biodiversity

**Biodiversity has contributed in many ways to the development of human culture and, in turn, human communities have played a major role in shaping the diversity of nature at the genetic, species and ecological levels.**

**Biodiversity plays the following roles: ECOLOGICAL, ECONOMIC and SCIENTIFIC.**

| Role | Detail |
|---|---|
| **ECOLOGICAL ROLE** | **Species of many kinds perform some function or the other in an ecosystem.** **Every organism, besides extracting its needs, also contributes something of use to other organisms.** **Species capture and store energy, produce and decompose organic materials, help to cycle water and nutrients throughout the ecosystem, fix atmospheric gases and help regulate the climate.** **These functions are important for ecosystem function and human survival.** ⚠️ **The more diverse an ecosystem, better are the chances for the species to survive through adversities and attacks, and consequently is more productive.** **Hence, the loss of species would decrease the ability of the system to maintain itself or to recover from damage or disturbance** |
| **ECONOMIC ROLE** | **For all humans, biodiversity is an important resource in their day-to-day life.** **One important part of biodiversity is 'crop diversity', which is also called AGRO-BIODIVERSITY.** **Biodiversity is seen as a reservoir of resources to be drawn upon for the manufacture of food, pharmaceutical, and cosmetic products.** **This concept of biological resources is responsible for the deterioration of biodiversity.** **Some of the important economic commodities that biodiversity supplies to humankind are: FOOD (crops, livestock, forestry, fish); MEDICINE (wild plant species have been used for medicinal purposes since before the beginning of recorded history — for example quinine comes from the cinchona tree, digitalis from the foxglove plant and morphine from the poppy plant); INDUSTRY (fibres for clothing, wood for shelter and warmth)** |
| **SCIENTIFIC ROLE** | **Biodiversity is important because each species can give us some indication as to how life evolved and will continue to evolve on Earth.** **In addition, biodiversity helps us understand how life functions and the role of each species in sustaining ecosystems of which we are also a species** |

> **The medicine examples are precise enough to be Prelims answers, and they make the "unknown value" argument concrete:**
> **QUININE ← cinchona tree** (anti-malarial) · **DIGITALIS ← foxglove** (heart medicine) · **MORPHINE ← poppy** (analgesic).
>
> **The argument that follows: we have chemically screened only a small fraction of the world's plant species.** **Every extinction is an unrepeatable experiment destroyed before it was read.** **You cannot know the value of what you have not yet examined — which is precisely why the precautionary principle applies.**

> **And note NCERT's sharp, almost self-critical line:** *"This concept of biological resources is responsible for the deterioration of biodiversity."* **Treating living systems purely as a "reservoir of resources to be drawn upon" is itself part of the problem.** **That is an unusually pointed observation for a textbook, and it works well in an Ethics or Essay context.**

## 3️⃣ Loss of Biodiversity

**Biodiversity is undergoing rapid depletion.** **The current rate of extinction is much higher than the natural rate.**

**The main causes of the loss of biodiversity are:**

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Natural causes** | **Narrow geographical area, low population, low breeding rate, natural calamities such as floods, earthquakes and volcanic eruptions** |
| **HABITAT DESTRUCTION** | **The most important cause.** **Clearing of forests for agriculture, settlement, mining, dams and industry** |
| **Hunting and poaching** | **For commercial purposes — ivory, horn, skin, fur** |
| **Over-exploitation** | **Of resources beyond regeneration capacity** |
| **Pollution** | **Air, water, soil and chemical contamination** |
| **Introduction of EXOTIC species** | **Non-native species outcompete natives** |
| **Habitat fragmentation** | **Breaking habitat into isolated patches** |
| **Climate change** | **Shifts habitats faster than species can migrate** |

**The rate of extinction has increased due to human activities.** **The introduction of exotic species and the destruction of natural habitats have been the major causes.**

> 🌱 **Habitat loss is by far the largest driver** — and **fragmentation compounds it in a way worth stating explicitly.**
>
> **Ten scattered 100-hectare forest patches are NOT ecologically equivalent to one 1,000-hectare forest.** **Large predators need continuous territory; small isolated populations inbreed and lose genetic diversity; animals crossing between patches are killed on roads.**
>
> **This is why WILDLIFE CORRIDORS matter as much as protected areas themselves**, and why linear infrastructure (roads, railways, canals) through forests is so contested. **It is also a genuinely precise argument that most candidates do not make.**

> ⚠️ **Note the subtle point about EXOTIC species: they are not harmful because they are foreign, but because they arrive WITHOUT their natural predators and competitors.** **Freed from the checks that limited them at home, they can expand without restraint.** **Indian examples: *Lantana camara*, water hyacinth ("Bengal terror"), *Prosopis juliflora*, African catfish.**

## 4️⃣ Biodiversity Hotspots

**Some areas of the world are richer in species than others.** **Areas rich in species diversity are called HOTSPOTS of diversity.**

**A hotspot is an area which faces serious threat from human activities and which is rich in biodiversity.**

### The two criteria — both must be met

**To qualify as a biodiversity hotspot, a region must meet TWO strict criteria:**
1. **It must contain at least 1,500 species of vascular plants as ENDEMICS**
2. **It must have lost at least 70% of its ORIGINAL HABITAT**

> **This double criterion is examined and routinely got wrong.** **Species richness ALONE does not make a hotspot.** **A hotspot is defined by being both exceptionally rich AND exceptionally THREATENED.**
>
> **The Amazon, for instance, is enormously biodiverse but is not classed as a hotspot in this scheme**, because it has not lost 70% of its original habitat. **The concept exists to prioritise where conservation money will save the most, fastest — it is a triage tool, not a ranking of natural wealth.**

### India's four hotspots

| Hotspot | Covers |
|---|---|
| **1. THE HIMALAYA** | The Eastern Himalaya and adjoining ranges |
| **2. THE WESTERN GHATS & SRI LANKA** | The Sahyadri range and Sri Lanka |
| **3. INDO-BURMA** | Includes North-East India |
| **4. SUNDALAND** | Includes the **Nicobar Islands** |

**India is also one of the world's ~17 MEGADIVERSE countries** — holding about **8% of the world's recorded species on 2.4% of its land area.**

⚠️ **Do not confuse the two concepts: India HAS 4 hotspots and IS one of ~17 megadiverse countries.** **Different classifications entirely.**

## 5️⃣ Conservation of Biodiversity

**The conservation of biodiversity has many tangible economic and aesthetic benefits.** **It is essential to preserve the biodiversity for the following reasons.**

**Conservation should take into consideration: (i) the maintenance of essential ecological processes and life supporting systems; (ii) the preservation of genetic diversity; (iii) the sustainable utilisation of species and ecosystems.**

### In-situ vs Ex-situ conservation

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **IN-SITU (on-site)** | **Conservation of species IN THEIR NATURAL HABITAT** | **National parks · wildlife sanctuaries · biosphere reserves · sacred groves · reserved forests** |
| **EX-SITU (off-site)** | **Conservation of species OUTSIDE their natural habitat** | **Zoos · botanical gardens · seed banks · gene banks · cryopreservation · aquaria** |

> **The distinction matters analytically, not just as a definition.**
>
> **IN-SITU is far superior** because it conserves not just the species but **the ecosystem, the interactions, and the ongoing evolutionary process.** A tiger in a forest is still subject to natural selection; a tiger in a zoo is not.
>
> **EX-SITU is a backup, not a substitute** — used when a population is too small to survive in the wild, or to preserve genetic material. **Seed banks (like the Svalbard Global Seed Vault) protect crop genetic diversity that no longer exists in fields.**
>
> 🎯 **In a Mains answer: "ex-situ conservation preserves the species; in-situ conservation preserves the species AND the system that produced it."** That is the sentence that shows you understand the difference.

### The protected-area system

*(Detail in [Cl.6 Ch.8](../../Class_06_The_Earth_Our_Habitat/Notes/08_India_Climate_Vegetation_Wildlife.md) and [Cl.10 Ch.2](../../Class_10_Contemporary_India_II/Notes/02_Forest_and_Wildlife_Resources.md).)*

| | **NATIONAL PARK** | **WILDLIFE SANCTUARY** | **BIOSPHERE RESERVE** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Protects** | Entire ecosystem | Mainly specific species | **Ecosystem + PEOPLE** |
| **Human activity** | **Strictly prohibited** | Some permitted | Permitted in **buffer and transition** zones |
| **Zoning** | — | — | **Core / buffer / transition** |

> **The BIOSPHERE RESERVE concept is the one worth understanding properly**, because it embodies the chapter's central argument: **conservation that excludes local communities usually fails.** **So the reserve is zoned — a strictly protected CORE, a BUFFER for research and limited use, and a TRANSITION zone with settlements and sustainable activity.** **Conservation *with* people, not against them.**

### International efforts

| Instrument | Detail |
|---|---|
| **IUCN** | **International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources.** **Maintains the RED LIST / Red Data Book of threatened species** |
| **IUCN Red List categories** | **Extinct → Extinct in the Wild → CRITICALLY ENDANGERED → ENDANGERED → VULNERABLE → Near Threatened → Least Concern** |
| **CITES** | **Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora.** **Regulates CROSS-BORDER TRADE in listed species** |
| **CBD** | **Convention on Biological Diversity — adopted at the RIO EARTH SUMMIT, 1992.** **Three objectives: conservation of biodiversity · sustainable use of its components · fair and equitable sharing of benefits from genetic resources** |
| **RAMSAR Convention** | **On wetlands of international importance, 1971** |
| **UNESCO — Man and Biosphere (MAB)** | **The programme under which biosphere reserves are designated** |
| **UNESCO World Heritage** | **Natural sites of outstanding universal value** |

**India's own legislation:** **Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 · Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 · Environment (Protection) Act 1986 · Biological Diversity Act 2002 (which implements the CBD in India, through the National Biodiversity Authority).**

> **The CBD's THIRD objective is the one candidates forget and the one India cares about most.**
> **"Fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising out of the utilisation of genetic resources"** — this is the ACCESS AND BENEFIT SHARING (ABS) principle, given effect by the **NAGOYA PROTOCOL (2010)**.
>
> **Why it matters to India:** genetic resources and traditional knowledge are concentrated in biodiversity-rich developing countries, while the capacity to commercialise them sits elsewhere. **The neem and turmeric patent cases** — where patents were granted abroad on uses long known in India, and later revoked — are the standard examples of **BIOPIRACY**. **India's response includes the Biological Diversity Act 2002 and the Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL).**
>
> 🎯 **This turns a conservation question into an equity question**, and connects GS-III to GS-II. **A strong, specific line for either.**

**Trap 1:** *"A biodiversity hotspot is simply a species-rich area."* **It must ALSO have lost ≥70% of its original habitat.** Both criteria.
**Trap 2:** *"India has 4 megadiverse regions."* **India HAS 4 hotspots and IS one of ~17 megadiverse countries.**
**Trap 3:** *"Zoos are in-situ conservation."* **EX-SITU.** In-situ means the natural habitat.
**Trap 4:** *"CITES protects habitats."* **It regulates international TRADE.** Habitat protection is domestic law.
**Trap 5:** *"The CBD has two objectives."* **THREE** — the third, benefit-sharing, is the one that matters most to India.
**Trap 6:** *"Exotic species are harmful because they are foreign."* **Because they arrive without their natural predators and competitors.**
**Trap 7:** *"Genetic diversity matters less than species diversity."* **Low genetic diversity means no resistance options** — the Irish potato famine is the case.

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Biodiversity** | Total variety of species, genes and ecosystems, with their habitats |
| **Genetic diversity** | Variation of genes within a species |
| **Species diversity** | Variety and number of species in an area |
| **Ecosystem diversity** | Variety of ecosystem types and habitats |
| **Agro-biodiversity** | Crop diversity |
| **Endemic species** | Found only in a specific area |
| **Exotic / alien species** | Non-native species introduced to an area |
| **Biodiversity hotspot** | ≥1,500 endemic vascular plants **and** ≥70% habitat lost |
| **Megadiverse country** | One of ~17 countries holding most of Earth's species |
| **In-situ conservation** | Conservation in the natural habitat |
| **Ex-situ conservation** | Conservation outside the natural habitat |
| **IUCN Red List** | Global classification of species by extinction risk |
| **CITES** | Treaty regulating international trade in endangered species |
| **CBD** | Convention on Biological Diversity, Rio 1992 |
| **Nagoya Protocol** | 2010 protocol on Access and Benefit Sharing |
| **Biopiracy** | Commercial use of genetic resources/traditional knowledge without consent or benefit sharing |
| **TKDL** | Traditional Knowledge Digital Library |
| **Wildlife corridor** | Connecting strip allowing movement between habitats |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Biodiversity is the result of 2.5–3.5 billion years of evolution.**
2. **Three levels: GENETIC (within species) · SPECIES (number and variety) · ECOSYSTEM (types of habitat and process).**
3. **Low genetic diversity = no resistance options.** The Irish potato famine is the case; the narrowing of Indian crop varieties is the current risk.
4. **Three roles: ECOLOGICAL (energy, nutrient cycling, climate regulation) · ECONOMIC (food, medicine, industry) · SCIENTIFIC (understanding evolution).**
5. **Medicines: quinine ← cinchona · digitalis ← foxglove · morphine ← poppy.** **Every extinction destroys an unread experiment.**
6. **A diverse ecosystem is more productive and recovers better from disturbance.**
7. **Causes of loss: HABITAT DESTRUCTION (largest), fragmentation, poaching, over-exploitation, pollution, exotic species, climate change.**
8. **Exotic species harm because they arrive WITHOUT their natural predators** — Lantana, water hyacinth, Prosopis.
9. **A HOTSPOT needs BOTH: ≥1,500 endemic vascular plants AND ≥70% of original habitat lost.** It is a **triage tool**, not a ranking of natural wealth.
10. **India HAS 4 hotspots (Himalaya, Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Sundaland) and IS one of ~17 megadiverse countries.**
11. **IN-SITU (parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, sacred groves) preserves the species AND the system. EX-SITU (zoos, botanical gardens, seed and gene banks) is a backup, not a substitute.**
12. **IUCN Red List: Extinct → EW → CR → EN → VU → NT → LC.** **CITES = trade. Ramsar 1971 = wetlands.**
13. **CBD (Rio 1992) has THREE objectives — conservation, sustainable use, and FAIR AND EQUITABLE BENEFIT SHARING.** **The Nagoya Protocol (2010) gives effect to the third; India's Biological Diversity Act 2002, the NBA and the TKDL are its response to biopiracy.**
