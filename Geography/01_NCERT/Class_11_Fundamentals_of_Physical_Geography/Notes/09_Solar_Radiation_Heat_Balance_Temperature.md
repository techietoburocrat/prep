## 1️⃣ ⭐ Solar Radiation

⭐ **The earth's surface receives most of its energy in short wavelengths.** ⭐ **The energy received by the earth is known as INCOMING SOLAR RADIATION which in short is termed as INSOLATION.**

⭐ **As the earth is a geoid resembling a sphere, the sun's rays fall obliquely at the top of the atmosphere and the earth intercepts a very small portion of the sun's energy.** ⭐ **On an average the earth receives 1.94 calories per sq. cm per minute at the top of its atmosphere.**

⭐ **The solar output received at the top of the atmosphere varies slightly in a year due to the variations in the distance between the earth and the sun.** ⭐ **During its revolution round the sun, the earth is farthest from the sun (152 million km) on 4th July — this position of the earth is called APHELION.** ⭐ **On 3rd January, the earth is the nearest to the sun (147 million km) — this position is called PERIHELION.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Therefore, the annual insolation received by the earth on 3rd January is slightly more than the amount received on 4th July.** ⭐ **However, the effect of this variation in the solar output is masked by other factors like the distribution of land and sea and the atmospheric circulation.** ⭐ **Hence, this variation in the solar output does not have great effect on daily weather changes on the surface of the earth.**

> ⭐⭐ **The perihelion/aphelion facts are asked, AND they demolish the commonest misconception.** ⭐ **The Earth is CLOSEST to the Sun on 3 January (perihelion, 147 mn km) and FARTHEST on 4 July (aphelion, 152 mn km).**
>
> ⭐ **January is northern winter. July is northern summer.** **Distance gives exactly the wrong answer** — which proves seasons are caused by the **axial tilt**, not by distance (Cl.6 Ch.3).

⭐ **The earth receives only a small fraction of the total energy radiated by the sun — a TWO BILLIONTH PART.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Variability of Insolation at the Earth's Surface

⭐ **The amount and the intensity of insolation vary during a day, in a season and in a year.** ⭐ **The factors that cause these variations in insolation are:**

| Factor | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **(i) The rotation of earth on its axis** | ⭐ **The fact that the earth's axis makes an angle of 66.5 degrees with the plane of its orbit round the sun has a greater influence on the amount of insolation received at different latitudes** |
| ⭐ **(ii) The angle of inclination of the sun's rays** | ⭐ **Depends on the latitude of a place.** ⭐ **The higher the latitude the less is the angle they make with the surface of the earth resulting in slant sun rays** |
| ⭐ **(iii) The length of the day** | ⭐ **Longer days mean more insolation** |
| ⭐ **(iv) The transparency of the atmosphere** | ⭐ **Depends upon the presence of clouds, dust particles and water vapour, which reflect, absorb or transmit insolation** |

⭐ **The last two are the ones geography emphasises.** ⭐ **The area covered by slant rays is more than the area covered by vertical rays** — and ⭐ **the slant rays are required to pass through greater depth of the atmosphere resulting in more absorption, scattering and diffusion.**

```
   ⭐ WHY LATITUDE CONTROLS TEMPERATURE — TWO REASONS, NOT ONE

        ☀️ Sun's rays arrive essentially parallel

     ╲╲╲╲     ⭐ 1. SPREAD: the same bundle of rays
      ╲╲╲╲       covers a LARGER AREA at high latitudes
       ╲╲╲╲      → less energy per sq cm
        ╲╲╲╲
    ┌───────╲╲────────────┐
    │        ╲╲           │  ⭐ 2. PATH LENGTH: slant rays pass
    │  ↓↓↓↓↓↓↓↓↓↓         │     through MORE ATMOSPHERE
    │  ↓↓↓↓↓↓↓↓↓↓         │     → more absorption, scattering,
    └─────────────────────┘        diffusion before arrival
```

> ⭐ **Most candidates give only the first reason (spreading). NCERT gives both.** ⭐ **Slant rays also travel a longer path through the atmosphere, losing more energy to absorption and scattering en route.** **Two independent mechanisms, same direction.**

⭐ **There are, however, variations in the amount of insolation received at different latitudes**, with maximum at the equator and decreasing towards the poles.

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Passage of Solar Radiation Through the Atmosphere

⭐ **The atmosphere is largely transparent to short wave solar radiation.** ⭐ **The incoming solar radiation passes through the atmosphere before striking the earth's surface.** ⭐ **Within the troposphere water vapour, ozone and other gases absorb much of the near infrared radiation.**

⭐ **Very small-suspended particles in the troposphere SCATTER visible spectrum both to the space and towards the earth surface.** ⭐ **This process adds colour to the sky.** ⭐ **The red colour of the rising and the setting sun and the blue colour of the sky are the result of scattering of light within the atmosphere.**

> 🌅 ⭐ **Why the sky is blue and sunsets are red — one mechanism, two results, and a genuinely satisfying explanation.**
>
> ⭐ **Scattering is stronger for shorter (blue) wavelengths.** So when sunlight passes through the atmosphere, blue light is scattered in all directions — and reaching your eye from every part of the sky, it makes the sky **blue**.
>
> ⭐ **At sunrise and sunset, the light travels a much longer path through the atmosphere** (the same path-length effect as slant rays above). ⭐ **By then virtually all the blue has been scattered away, leaving only the longer red wavelengths to reach you** — hence the **red** sun.
>
> ⭐ **The blue sky and the red sunset are the same phenomenon seen from two angles.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Heat Budget of the Planet Earth

⭐ **The earth as a whole does not accumulate or lose heat.** ⭐ **It maintains its temperature.** ⭐ **This can happen only if the amount of heat received in the form of insolation equals the amount lost by the earth through terrestrial radiation.**

### ⭐⭐ The exact figures — learn these

⭐ **Consider that the insolation received at the top of the atmosphere is 100 per cent.**

| Stage | ⭐ Amount |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Reflected back by the atmosphere** | ⭐ **35 units** — the **ALBEDO** of the earth |
| ⭐ *— of which: reflected from clouds* | ⭐ **27** |
| ⭐ *— reflected from ice/snow (earth's surface)* | ⭐ **2** |
| ⭐ *— scattered back to space from dust* | ⭐ **6** |
| ⭐ **The remaining 65 units are absorbed** | ⭐ **65** |
| ⭐ *— absorbed by the atmosphere* | ⭐ **14** |
| ⭐ *— absorbed by the earth's surface* | ⭐ **51** |

⭐ **The earth radiates back 51 units in the form of terrestrial radiation:**

| Return path | ⭐ Amount |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Direct radiation to space from the earth** | ⭐ **17** |
| ⭐ **Radiation absorbed by the atmosphere** | ⭐ **34** |
| ⭐ *— of which: through convection and turbulence* | ⭐ **6** |
| ⭐ *— evaporation (latent heat)* | ⭐ **19** |
| ⭐ *— absorbed by atmosphere directly* | ⭐ **9** |

⭐ **Thus, the total radiation returning from the earth and the atmosphere respectively is 17 + 48 = 65 units which balance the total of 65 units received from the sun.** ⭐ **This is termed the HEAT BUDGET or HEAT BALANCE of the earth.**

```
   ⭐⭐ THE HEAT BUDGET — 100 IN, 100 OUT

   INSOLATION 100 units at top of atmosphere
        │
        ├─── ⭐ 35 REFLECTED (ALBEDO) ──────────────► space
        │       ├─ 27 from clouds
        │       ├─  6 scattered by dust
        │       └─  2 from ice/snow
        │
        └─── 65 ABSORBED
                ├─ 14 by the ATMOSPHERE
                └─ ⭐ 51 by the EARTH'S SURFACE
                        │
                        └─► radiated back as TERRESTRIAL (long-wave) radiation
                             ├─ ⭐ 17 direct to space
                             └─ ⭐ 34 absorbed by atmosphere
                                   ├─  6 convection & turbulence
                                   ├─ ⭐ 19 EVAPORATION (latent heat) ← largest
                                   └─  9 direct absorption

   ⭐ OUT = 17 (earth direct) + 48 (atmosphere) = 65 ✅ BALANCED
```

> ⭐⭐ **The single most important number here is 35 — the ALBEDO.** ⭐ **The Earth reflects 35% of incoming solar radiation straight back to space without ever using it.**
>
> 🌱 ⭐ **And this is why melting ice is a self-accelerating problem — the ALBEDO FEEDBACK, which is a top-tier GS-III point:**
> ⭐ **Ice and snow are highly reflective (high albedo). Ocean and bare land are dark (low albedo).** As warming melts ice, **reflective white surface is replaced by absorbent dark surface**, so the region absorbs **more** energy, so it warms **further**, so more ice melts. ⭐ **A positive feedback loop.**
>
> ⭐ **This is precisely why the Arctic is warming roughly two to four times faster than the global average** — a phenomenon called **Arctic amplification.** **Naming the mechanism, not just the fact, is what separates a strong answer.**

### ⭐⭐ Variation in the net heat budget at the Earth's surface

⭐ **As explained earlier, there are variations in the amount of radiation received at the earth's surface.** ⭐ **Some part of the earth has surplus radiation balance while the other part has deficit.**

⭐ **Figure shows that there is a surplus of net radiation balance between 40 degrees north and south, and the regions near the poles have a deficit.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The surplus heat energy from the tropics is redistributed pole wards and as a result the tropics do not get progressively heated up due to the accumulation of excess heat or the high latitudes get permanently frozen due to excess deficit.**

> ⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT SENTENCE IN THE ENTIRE CLIMATE UNIT — and it is the bridge to Chapter 10.**
>
> ⭐ **The heat budget balances GLOBALLY but NOT LOCALLY.** The tropics receive a **surplus**; the poles run a **deficit** — permanently.
>
> ⭐ **If nothing moved that surplus poleward, the tropics would keep heating without limit and the poles would keep freezing.** They don't. Therefore **something must be transporting heat from the equator to the poles, continuously.**
>
> ⭐ **That transport system IS the global wind circulation and the ocean currents.**
>
> 🎯 **So Chapter 10 (atmospheric circulation) and Chapter 14 (ocean currents) are not new topics — they are the ANSWER to the problem this chapter poses.** ⭐ **Winds and currents exist because the heat budget doesn't balance locally.** **State that in a Mains answer and you have framed the entire climate system in one sentence.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐ How Heat Moves — Four Processes

| Process | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **CONDUCTION** | ⭐ **The earth after being heated by insolation transmits the heat to the atmospheric layers near to the earth in long wave form.** ⭐ **The air in contact with the land gets heated slowly and the upper layers in contact with the lower layers also get heated.** ⭐ **This process is called conduction.** ⭐ **Conduction takes place when two bodies of unequal temperature are in contact with one another; there is a flow of energy from the warmer to cooler body.** ⭐ **The transfer of heat continues until both the bodies attain the same temperature or the contact is broken.** ⭐ **Conduction is important in heating the lower layers of the atmosphere** |
| ⭐ **CONVECTION** | ⭐ **The air in contact with the earth rises vertically on heating in the form of currents and further transmits the heat of the atmosphere.** ⭐ **This process of vertical heating of the atmosphere is known as convection.** ⭐ **The convective transfer of energy is confined only to the TROPOSPHERE** |
| ⭐ **ADVECTION** | ⭐ **The transfer of heat through HORIZONTAL movement of air is called advection.** ⭐ **Horizontal movement of the air is relatively more important than the vertical movement.** ⭐ **In middle latitudes, most of diurnal (day and night) variation in daily weather are caused by advection alone.** ⭐ **In tropical regions particularly in northern India during summer season LOCAL WINDS CALLED 'LOO' is the outcome of advection process** |
| ⭐ **TERRESTRIAL RADIATION** | ⭐ **The insolation received by the earth is in short waves forms and heats up its surface.** ⭐ **The earth after being heated itself becomes a radiating body and it radiates energy to the atmosphere in long wave form.** ⭐ **This energy heats up the atmosphere from below.** ⭐ **This process is known as terrestrial radiation** |

> ⭐ **Two examinable points here.** ⭐ **Convection is confined to the TROPOSPHERE only.** ⭐ **And the LOO — north India's hot summer wind — is NCERT's named example of ADVECTION.** Both get asked.

> ⭐ **The direction of heating is the conceptual key: ⭐ THE ATMOSPHERE IS HEATED FROM BELOW, NOT ABOVE.** Sunlight passes through the largely transparent atmosphere, heats the **ground**, and the ground then radiates long-wave heat upward. ⭐ **This is exactly why temperature falls with height in the troposphere (the normal lapse rate)** — you are moving away from the heat source, which is the Earth's surface, not the Sun.

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐ Temperature — Controls and Distribution

### ⭐ The six controls of temperature

⭐ **The temperature of air at any place is influenced by:**

| Control | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **(i) The latitude of the place** | ⭐ **The temperature of a place depends on the insolation received. It has already been explained that the insolation varies according to the latitude, hence the temperature also varies accordingly** |
| ⭐ **(ii) The altitude of the place** | ⭐ **The atmosphere is indirectly heated by terrestrial radiation from below.** ⭐ **Therefore, the places near the sea-level record higher temperature than the places situated at higher elevations.** ⭐ **In other words, the temperature generally decreases with increasing height.** ⭐ **The rate of decrease of temperature with height is termed as the NORMAL LAPSE RATE — 6.5°C per 1,000 m** |
| ⭐ **(iii) Distance from the sea** | ⭐ **Another factor that influences the temperature is the location of a place with respect to the sea.** ⭐ **Compared to land, the sea gets heated slowly and loses heat slowly.** ⭐ **Land heats up and cools down quickly.** ⭐ **Therefore, the variation in temperature over the sea is less compared to land.** ⭐ **The places situated near the sea come under the moderating influence of the sea and land breezes which moderate the temperature** |
| ⭐ **(iv) The air-mass and Ocean currents** | ⭐ **Like the land and sea breezes, the passage of air masses also affects the temperature.** ⭐ **The places, which come under the influence of warm air-masses experience higher temperature and the places that come under the influence of cold air-masses experience low temperature.** ⭐ **Similarly, the places located on the coast where the warm ocean currents flow record higher temperature than the places located on the coast where the cold currents flow** |

### ⭐ Distribution of temperature

⭐ **The global distribution of temperature can well be understood by studying the temperature distribution in January and July.** ⭐ **The temperature distribution is generally shown on the map with the help of ISOTHERMS.**

> ⭐ **ISOTHERMS are lines joining places having equal temperature.**

⭐ **In general the effect of the latitude on temperature is well pronounced on the map, as the isotherms are generally parallel to the latitude.** ⭐ **The deviation from this general trend is more pronounced in January than in July, especially in the northern hemisphere.**

⭐ **In the northern hemisphere the land surface area is much larger than in the southern hemisphere.** ⭐ **Hence, the effects of land mass and the ocean currents are well pronounced.**

⭐ **In January the isotherms deviate to the north over the ocean and to the south over the continent.** ⭐ **This can be seen on the North Atlantic Ocean.** ⭐ **The presence of warm ocean currents, Gulf Stream and North Atlantic drift, make the Northern Atlantic Ocean warmer and the isotherms bend towards the north.**

⭐ **In July the isotherms generally run parallel to the latitude.** ⭐ **The equatorial oceans record warmer temperature, more than 27°C.** ⭐ **Over the land more than 30°C is noticed in the subtropical continental region of Asia, along the 30° N latitude.**

> ⭐ **Why isotherms bend — the rule in one line:** ⭐ **isotherms bend toward the poles over the ocean in winter and toward the equator over land in winter** — because the ocean stays relatively **warm** and the land gets relatively **cold**. **The reverse happens in summer.** ⭐ **The bending of isotherms is a direct visual record of continentality.**

⭐ **The highest range of temperature is more than 60°C over the north-eastern part of Eurasian continent.** ⭐ **This is due to continentality.** ⭐ **The least range of temperature, 3°C, is found between 20° S and 15° N.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐ Inversion of Temperature

⭐ **Normally, temperature decreases with increase in elevation.** ⭐ **It is called the normal lapse rate.** ⭐ **At times, the situations is reversed and the normal lapse rate is inverted. It is called INVERSION OF TEMPERATURE.**

⭐ **Inversion is usually of short duration but quite common nonetheless.** ⭐ **A long winter night with clear skies and still air is ideal situation for inversion.** ⭐ **The heat of the day is radiated off during the night, and by early morning hours, the earth is cooler than the air above.** ⭐ **Over polar areas, temperature inversion is normal throughout the year.**

### ⭐ Conditions favourable for inversion

⭐ **Long nights · clear skies · dry air · still air (calm) · snow-covered surface.**

### ⭐⭐ Inversion in valleys — the important case

⭐ **Surface inversion promotes stability in the lower layer of the atmosphere.** ⭐ **Smoke and dust particles get collected beneath the inversion layer and spread horizontally to fill the lower strata of the atmosphere.** ⭐ **Dense fogs in mornings are common occurrences, especially during winter season.** ⭐ **This inversion commonly lasts for few hours until the sun comes up and beings to warm the earth.**

⭐ **The inversion takes place in hills and mountains due to AIR DRAINAGE.** ⭐ **Cold air at the hills and mountains, produced during night, flows under the influence of gravity.** ⭐ **Being heavy and dense, the cold air acts almost like water and moves down the slope to pile up deeply in pockets and valley bottoms with warm air above.** ⭐ **This is called AIR DRAINAGE.**

⭐ **It protects plants from frost damages.**

> ⭐⭐ **Temperature inversion is one of the most useful concepts in the syllabus because it explains a live policy problem.**
>
> 🌱 ⭐ **Delhi's winter smog is a temperature inversion problem.** On still, clear winter nights, the ground cools rapidly and a layer of **cold air is trapped beneath warmer air above.** ⭐ **Since the cold air cannot rise (it is denser than the air above it), pollutants have nowhere to go — they accumulate in the shallow layer where people breathe.**
>
> ⭐ **Add the Indo-Gangetic Plain's landform trap** — hemmed against the Himalayas with little wind (Cl.6 Ch.6) — **and the same quantity of emissions produces far worse air quality in December than in July.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **The emissions didn't change; the atmosphere's ability to disperse them did.** **That framing — a meteorological problem overlaid on an emissions problem — is a strong, specific GS-III point.**

> ⭐ **The "air drainage" fact has an elegant agricultural consequence NCERT flags:** cold dense air flows downhill like water and **pools in valley bottoms.** ⭐ **This is why orchards and vineyards in hilly regions are planted on the SLOPES rather than the valley floor** — the floor is the frost pocket. **Inversion protects the plants on the slope from frost damage.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Earth receives a two-billionth part of solar output — 1.94 cal/sq cm/min at the top of the atmosphere.**
2. ⭐⭐ **Perihelion 3 January (147 mn km); aphelion 4 July (152 mn km).** Distance gives the *wrong* answer for seasons — **tilt is the cause.**
3. ⭐ **Four insolation factors: rotation/axis angle (66.5°), angle of the sun's rays, length of day, atmospheric transparency.**
4. ⭐⭐ **Latitude matters for TWO reasons: slant rays spread over a larger area AND pass through more atmosphere.**
5. ⭐ **Scattering explains both the blue sky and the red sunset** — same mechanism, two viewing geometries.
6. ⭐⭐ **HEAT BUDGET: 35 reflected (27 clouds + 6 dust + 2 ice) = ALBEDO · 65 absorbed (14 atmosphere + 51 surface).** ⭐ **Returned: 17 direct + 34 to atmosphere (6 convection + 19 evaporation + 9 absorption) = 65.** ⭐ **Evaporation is the largest single return path.**
7. 🌱 ⭐ **Albedo feedback: melting ice replaces reflective white with absorbent dark → more warming → more melt.** This is **Arctic amplification.**
8. ⭐⭐ **Surplus between 40°N and 40°S; deficit near the poles.** ⭐ **The budget balances globally but NOT locally — and that imbalance is the reason winds and ocean currents exist.** This is the bridge to Ch.10 and Ch.14.
9. ⭐ **Four heat-transfer processes: conduction, convection (troposphere only), advection (the LOO), terrestrial radiation.**
10. ⭐ **The atmosphere is heated FROM BELOW** — which is why temperature falls with height.
11. ⭐ **Temperature controls: latitude, altitude, distance from sea, air masses and ocean currents.**
12. ⭐ **Isotherms bend to reveal continentality.** Greatest range >60°C in NE Eurasia; least 3°C between 20°S and 15°N.
13. ⭐⭐ **Temperature inversion: temperature RISES with height.** Needs long nights, clear skies, dry still air, snow cover. 🌱 **It is why Delhi's winter smog is worse than its summer emissions warrant** — and why **orchards are planted on slopes, not valley floors.**
