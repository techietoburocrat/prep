# Chapter 8 — Composition and Structure of Atmosphere 🌱

## 1️⃣ Composition of the Atmosphere

**The atmosphere is composed of GASES, WATER VAPOUR and DUST PARTICLES.**

### Gases

| Gas | % by volume |
|---|---|
| **Nitrogen** | **78.08** |
| **Oxygen** | **20.95** |
| **Argon** | **0.93** |
| **Carbon dioxide** | **0.036** |
| Neon | 0.002 |
| Helium | 0.0005 |
| Ozone | 0.000004 |
| Hydrogen | 0.00005 |

**The proportion of gases changes in the higher layers of the atmosphere in such a way that oxygen will be almost in negligible quantity at the height of 120 km.** **Similarly, carbon dioxide and water vapour are found only up to 90 km from the surface of the earth.**

### Carbon dioxide — why 0.036% matters

**Carbon dioxide is meteorologically a very important gas as it is TRANSPARENT TO THE INCOMING SOLAR RADIATION but OPAQUE TO THE OUTGOING TERRESTRIAL RADIATION.** **It absorbs a part of terrestrial radiation and reflects back some part of it towards the earth's surface.** **It is largely responsible for the GREENHOUSE EFFECT.**

⚠️ **The volume of other gases is constant but the volume of carbon dioxide has been rising in the past few decades mainly because of the burning of fossil fuels.** **This has also increased the temperature of the air.**

> **This sentence is the entire physics of climate change in one line, and it is worth learning verbatim:** **CO₂ is transparent to incoming solar radiation but opaque to outgoing terrestrial radiation.**
>
> **Why?** Incoming sunlight is **short-wave**; the Earth re-radiates it as **long-wave** infrared. **CO₂ lets the short-wave in and blocks the long-wave out.** Energy enters freely and leaves with difficulty — so the system warms until it finds a new balance.
>
> 🚗 **The comparison that fits: a parked car in the Hyderabad sun.** Glass lets short-wave light in; the seats absorb it and re-radiate long-wave heat; glass doesn't let long-wave heat out. **The car gets far hotter inside than out.** **CO₂ determines how tightly the atmosphere's windows are shut.**

### Ozone

**Ozone is another important component of the atmosphere found between 10 and 50 km above the earth's surface and acts as a FILTER and absorbs the ultra-violet rays radiating from the sun and prevents them from reaching the surface of the earth.**

### Water vapour

**Water vapour is a variable gas in the atmosphere, which decreases with altitude.** **In the warm and wet tropics, it may account for FOUR PER CENT of the air by volume, while in the dry and cold areas of desert and polar regions, it may be LESS THAN ONE PER CENT of the air.**

**Water vapour also decreases from the equator towards the poles.**

**It also absorbs parts of the insolation from the sun and preserves the earth's radiated heat.** **It thus, acts like a blanket allowing the earth neither to become too cold nor too hot.** **Water vapour also contributes to the stability and instability in the air.**

### Dust particles

**The atmosphere has a sufficient capacity to keep small solid particles, which may originate from different sources and include sea salts, fine soil, smoke-soot, ash, pollen, dust and disintegrated particles of meteors.**

**Dust particles are generally concentrated in the lower layers of the atmosphere; yet, convectional air currents may transport them to great heights.** **The higher concentration of dust particles is found in subtropical and temperate regions due to dry winds in comparison to equatorial and polar regions.**

**Dust and salt particles act as HYGROSCOPIC NUCLEI around which water vapour condenses to produce clouds.**

> **The dust point is genuinely counter-intuitive and gets asked.** **Without dust and salt particles in the air, water vapour would have far greater difficulty condensing — clouds and rain would be much rarer.** **These are called hygroscopic nuclei (or condensation nuclei).**
>
> **So the "dirtiness" of the air is a precondition for rainfall.** A perfectly clean atmosphere would be a much drier one. **This is also why cloud seeding works** — you add artificial nuclei (silver iodide) to give vapour something to condense on.

## 2️⃣ Structure of the Atmosphere

**The atmosphere consists of different layers with varying density and temperature.** **Density is highest near the surface of the earth and decreases with increasing altitude.** **The column of atmosphere is divided into five different layers depending upon the temperature condition.**

```
   THE FIVE LAYERS

   ▲ 10,000 km
   │  5. EXOSPHERE
   │     The uppermost layer. Very thin.
   │     Gradually merges with outer space.
   │  ─────────────────────────────────────  80 km
   │  4. THERMOSPHERE / IONOSPHERE
   │     80–400 km. Temperature RISES rapidly with height.
   │     Contains electrically charged particles (ions)
   │     RADIO WAVES transmitted from the earth are
   │        reflected back by this layer.
   │  ─────────────────────────────────────  80 km
   │  3. MESOSPHERE
   │     50–80 km. Temperature FALLS with height,
   │        reaching −100°C at 80 km — THE COLDEST.
   │     Upper limit = MESOPAUSE.
   │     METEORS burn up here.
   │  ─────────────────────────────────────  50 km
   │  2. STRATOSPHERE
   │     Above the troposphere, extends up to 50 km.
   │     Contains the OZONE LAYER.
   │     Temperature RISES with height (ozone absorbs UV).
   │     Ideal for FLYING — no clouds, no turbulence.
   │  ═══ TROPOPAUSE ═══════════════════  8 km poles / 18 km equator
   │  1. TROPOSPHERE
   │     Average height 13 km.
   │     8 km at the POLES, 18 km at the EQUATOR.
   │     ALL WEATHER happens here.
   │     Temperature FALLS at 1°C per 165 m
   │        (the NORMAL LAPSE RATE).
   ▼  ─────────────────────────────────────  Sea level
```

### The layers in detail

| Layer | Detail |
|---|---|
| **TROPOSPHERE** | **The lowermost layer.** **Its average height is 13 km and extends roughly to a height of 8 km near the poles and about 18 km at the equator.** **The thickness of the troposphere is greatest at the equator because heat is transported to great heights by strong convectional currents.** **This layer contains dust particles and water vapour.** **All changes in climate and weather take place in this layer.** **The temperature in this layer decreases at the rate of 1°C for every 165 m of height. This is the most important layer for all biological activity** |
| **TROPOPAUSE** | **The zone separating the troposphere from the stratosphere.** **The air temperature at the tropopause is about minus 80°C over the equator and about minus 45°C over the poles.** **The temperature here is nearly constant, and hence, it is called the tropopause** |
| **STRATOSPHERE** | **Found above the tropopause and extends up to a height of 50 km.** **One important feature of the stratosphere is that it contains the OZONE layer.** **This layer absorbs ultra-violet radiation and shields life on the earth from intense, harmful form of energy** |
| **MESOSPHERE** | **Lies above the stratosphere, which extends up to a height of 80 km.** **In this layer, once again, temperature starts decreasing with the increase in altitude and reaches up to minus 100°C at the height of 80 km.** **The upper limit of mesosphere is known as the MESOPAUSE** |
| **IONOSPHERE / THERMOSPHERE** | **Located between 80–400 km above the mesopause.** **It contains electrically charged particles known as ions, and hence, it is known as ionosphere.** **Radio waves transmitted from the earth are reflected back to the earth by this layer.** **Temperature here starts increasing with height** |
| **EXOSPHERE** | **The uppermost layer of the atmosphere above the thermosphere.** **This is the highest layer but very little is known about it.** **Whatever contents are there, these are extremely rarefied in this layer, and it gradually merges with the outer space** |

> **The NORMAL LAPSE RATE is one of the highest-frequency facts in this chapter:** **temperature falls by 1°C for every 165 metres of ascent** in the troposphere. (Equivalently, ~6.5°C per km.) **Know both forms — questions use either.**

> **Why the troposphere is thicker at the equator (18 km) than at the poles (8 km)** — the mechanism matters: **strong convectional currents at the equator carry heat to great heights**, pushing the boundary upward. At the poles there is little convection, so the layer stays shallow. **The troposphere is thickest where the sun works hardest.**

> **Note the alternating temperature pattern — it's a memory aid and a trap detector:**
> **Troposphere: falls ↓ · Stratosphere: rises ↑ · Mesosphere: falls ↓ · Thermosphere: rises ↑**
> **The rises happen where something absorbs energy** — ozone absorbing UV in the stratosphere, ions absorbing solar radiation in the thermosphere. **Each reversal has a physical cause; it isn't arbitrary.**

## 3️⃣ Elements of Weather and Climate

**Weather is the momentary state of the atmosphere while climate refers to the average of the weather conditions over a longer period of time.**

**Weather changes quickly, may be within a day or week but climate changes imperceptibly and may be noted after 50 years or even more.**

**The elements of weather and climate: TEMPERATURE, PRESSURE, WIND, HUMIDITY and PRECIPITATION.**

> 👕 **Weather is what you wear today; climate is what's in your wardrobe.** (Cl.6 Ch.8 — repeated because UPSC exploits the confusion. *"It was cold this winter, so global warming isn't real"* mistakes a weather observation for a climate trend.)

| **Tropopause: −80°C over the equator, −45°C over the poles** ⭐ | Counter-intuitive — colder over the equator |

**Trap 1:** *"The tropopause is warmer over the equator than over the poles."* **The reverse — about −80°C over the equator and −45°C over the poles.** Because the equatorial tropopause is far **higher** (18 km vs 8 km), and temperature falls with height.
**Trap 2:** *"Ozone is in the troposphere."* **10–50 km — the stratosphere.**
**Trap 3:** *"The thermosphere is the coldest layer."* **The MESOSPHERE is (−100°C at 80 km).** The thermosphere is hot.
**Trap 4:** *"Dust in the atmosphere hinders rainfall."* **Dust is REQUIRED** — it provides hygroscopic nuclei for condensation.
**Trap 5:** *"The normal lapse rate is 1°C per 100 m."* **1°C per 165 m.**
**Trap 6:** *"Dust concentration is highest in equatorial regions."* **Subtropical and temperate regions**, because of dry winds.

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Normal lapse rate** | Temperature fall of **1°C per 165 m** in the troposphere |
| **Troposphere** | Lowest layer; all weather; 8 km (poles) to 18 km (equator) |
| **Tropopause** | Boundary of troposphere and stratosphere; near-constant temperature |
| **Stratosphere** | To 50 km; contains **ozone**; ideal for flying |
| **Mesosphere / mesopause** | To 80 km; **coldest (−100°C)**; meteors burn |
| **Ionosphere / thermosphere** | 80–400 km; ions; **reflects radio waves** |
| **Exosphere** | Uppermost, rarefied, merges with space |
| **Hygroscopic nuclei** | Dust/salt particles around which vapour condenses |
| 🌱 **Greenhouse effect** | Trapping of outgoing long-wave terrestrial radiation |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Atmosphere = gases + water vapour + dust.**
2. **N₂ 78.08% · O₂ 20.95% · Ar 0.93% · CO₂ 0.036%.**
3. **CO₂ is transparent to incoming solar radiation but opaque to outgoing terrestrial radiation** — the greenhouse effect in one line.
4. **Oxygen is negligible above 120 km; CO₂ and water vapour only reach 90 km.**
5. **Ozone sits at 10–50 km and filters UV.**
6. **Water vapour: up to 4% in the warm wet tropics, under 1% in deserts and polar regions.** Decreases with altitude and toward the poles. **Acts as a blanket.**
7. **Dust particles are HYGROSCOPIC NUCLEI — without them, condensation and rainfall would be far rarer.** Highest concentration in **subtropical and temperate** regions.
8. **Five layers: troposphere → stratosphere → mesosphere → thermosphere/ionosphere → exosphere.**
9. **NORMAL LAPSE RATE = 1°C per 165 m.**
10. **Troposphere: 8 km at poles, 18 km at equator, average 13 km** — thicker at the equator because of strong convection.
11. **Tropopause is about −80°C over the equator and −45°C over the poles** — colder over the equator because it is far higher.
12. **Mesosphere is the coldest layer (−100°C at 80 km); mesopause is its upper limit.**
13. **Temperature alternates: falls ↓ rises ↑ falls ↓ rises ↑ — and each rise happens where something absorbs energy** (ozone, ions).
