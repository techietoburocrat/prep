## 1️⃣ ⭐ Minerals

> ⭐ **A mineral is a naturally occurring organic and inorganic substance, having an orderly atomic structure and a definite chemical composition and physical properties.**

⭐ **A mineral is composed of two or more elements. However, sometimes single element minerals like sulphur, copper, silver, gold, graphite etc. are found.**

⭐ **The Earth is composed of ~2,000 minerals — but only about SIX are the most common rock-forming minerals**: ⭐ **feldspar, quartz, pyroxene, amphibole, mica and olivine.**

### ⭐ Physical characteristics of minerals

| Property | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **External crystal form** | Determined by the internal arrangement of molecules |
| ⭐ **Cleavage** | ⭐ **Tendency to break in given directions producing relatively plane surfaces** — the result of internal arrangement |
| ⭐ **Fracture** | ⭐ **Internal molecular arrangement so complex there are no planes of molecules; the crystal breaks in an irregular manner, not along a cleavage plane** |
| ⭐ **Lustre** | ⭐ **The appearance of a material without regard to colour** — metallic, silky, glossy |
| ⭐ **Colour** | ⭐ **Sometimes an important diagnostic** — e.g. **chlorite is generally green** |
| ⭐ **Streak** | ⭐ **The colour of the ground powder of any mineral. It may be of the same colour as the mineral or may differ** — e.g. **malachite is green and gives a green streak, whereas fluorite is purple or green but gives a white streak** |
| ⭐ **Transparency** | ⭐ **Transparent** (light rays pass through, object seen clearly) · **translucent** (light passes but rays scattered, object not seen) · **opaque** (light does not pass at all) |
| ⭐ **Structure** | Particular arrangement of individual crystals — fine, medium or coarse grained; fibrous, separable, divergent, radiating |
| ⭐ **Hardness** | ⭐ **Relative resistance to being scratched.** ⭐ **Ten minerals are selected to measure the degree of hardness from 1–10 — the MOHS SCALE** |

### ⭐⭐ The Mohs Scale of Hardness

```
   ⭐ 1. Talc        (softest — scratched by a fingernail)
      2. Gypsum
      3. Calcite
      4. Fluorite
      5. Apatite
      6. Feldspar
      7. Quartz
      8. Topaz
      9. Corundum
   ⭐ 10. Diamond    (hardest known natural substance)
```

> ⭐ **Mohs scale is a Prelims favourite.** ⭐ **Talc = 1 (softest), Diamond = 10 (hardest).** ⚠️ **It is a relative, not absolute, scale** — diamond is far more than ten times harder than talc. **It simply records which mineral scratches which.**

### ⭐ Classification of minerals

| Class | ⭐ Sub-class | ⭐ Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **METALLIC** | ⭐ **Precious metals** | ⭐ **Gold, silver, platinum** |
| | ⭐ **Ferrous metals** | ⭐ **Iron and other metals often mixed with iron to form various kinds of steel** |
| | ⭐ **Non-ferrous metals** | ⭐ **Copper, lead, zinc, tin, aluminium** |
| ⭐ **NON-METALLIC** | — | ⭐ **Sulphur, phosphates, nitrates.** ⭐ **Cement is a mixture of non-metallic minerals** |

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Rocks

> ⭐ **The Earth's crust is composed of rocks. A ROCK is an aggregate of one or more minerals.** ⭐ **Rock may be hard or soft and in varied colours** — e.g. granite is hard, soapstone is soft, gabbro is black and quartzite can be milky white.

⚠️ ⭐ **Rocks do not have a definite composition of mineral constituents.** ⭐ **Feldspar and quartz are the most common minerals found in rocks.**

⭐ **PETROLOGY is the science of rocks.** ⭐ **A petrologist studies rocks in all their aspects — mineral composition, texture, structure, origin, occurrence, alteration and relationship with other rocks.**

### ⭐⭐ The three rock types

| | 🔥 ⭐ **IGNEOUS** | 🏖️ ⭐ **SEDIMENTARY** | 💎 ⭐ **METAMORPHIC** |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **From** | Latin *ignis* = fire | Latin *sedimentum* = settle down | *Meta* = change + *morphic* = form |
| ⭐ **Formation** | ⭐ **Solidification of molten magma** | ⭐ **Denudation → deposition → compaction (LITHIFICATION)** | ⭐ **Existing rocks under great heat and pressure — RECRYSTALLISATION** |
| ⭐ **Called** | ⭐ **PRIMARY ROCKS** — all others form from them | — | — |
| ⭐ **Fossils** | ❌ Never | ⭐ ✅ **Only here** | ❌ Destroyed |
| ⭐ **Layers** | No | ⭐ ✅ **Yes — visible strata** | ⭐ Often **foliated/banded** |
| ⭐ **Examples** | ⭐ **Granite (intrusive), Basalt/Gabbro (extrusive)** | ⭐ **Sandstone, limestone, shale, coal, conglomerate** | ⭐ **Marble, slate, gneiss, schist, quartzite, graphite, diamond** |

### ⭐ Igneous — the sub-classification

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **INTRUSIVE / PLUTONIC** | ⭐ **Cooled and solidified DEEP beneath the earth's surface.** ⭐ **Slow cooling → LARGE crystals.** ⭐ **GRANITE is a typical example.** ⭐ **Named after Pluto, god of the underworld** |
| ⭐ **EXTRUSIVE / VOLCANIC** | ⭐ **Cooled and solidified ON the surface** after volcanic eruption. ⭐ **Rapid cooling → FINE/SMALL crystals.** ⭐ **BASALT is a typical example. The Deccan traps from the Peninsular region are basalt** |

> ⭐ **The crystal-size rule in one sentence, because it is repeatedly asked: SLOW cooling deep down = LARGE crystals (granite). FAST cooling at the surface = FINE crystals (basalt).**
>
> 🍬 **The comparison that fits:** cool sugar syrup slowly and you get **large rock-candy crystals**; cool it fast and you get a smooth, glassy **toffee** with no visible grain. **Same substance, different cooling rate, completely different texture.** Magma does exactly this.

### ⭐ Sedimentary — the three origins

⭐ **Depending upon the mode of formation, sedimentary rocks are classified into three major groups:**

| Origin | ⭐ Detail | ⭐ Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **MECHANICALLY FORMED** | Deposition and compaction of fragments | ⭐ **Sandstone, conglomerate, limestone, shale, loess** |
| ⭐ **ORGANICALLY FORMED** | From the remains of living things | ⭐ **Geyserite, chalk, limestone, coal** |
| ⭐ **CHEMICALLY FORMED** | Precipitation from solution | ⭐ **Chert, limestone, halite, potash** |

⭐ **Sedimentary rocks are formed through deposition, accumulation and compaction of sediments.** ⭐ **These rocks cover 75% of the earth's crust but volumetrically occupy only 5%.**

> ⭐ **That 75% / 5% figure is a classic Prelims item.** Sedimentary rocks form a **thin veneer over most of the surface** — extensive in area, negligible in volume. **They are the wallpaper, not the wall.**

### ⭐ Metamorphic — the two processes

| Process | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **DYNAMIC metamorphism** | ⭐ **Mechanical disruption and reorganisation of the original minerals within rocks due to breaking and crushing without any appreciable chemical changes** |
| ⭐ **THERMAL metamorphism** | ⭐ **Chemical alterations by heat and pressure.** Two sub-types: ⭐ **CONTACT metamorphism** (rocks come in contact with hot intruding magma and lava; the rock material recrystallises at higher temperatures) and ⭐ **REGIONAL metamorphism** (rocks undergo recrystallisation due to deformation caused by tectonic shearing together with high temperature or pressure or both) |

⭐ **In the process of metamorphism in some rocks grains or minerals get arranged in layers or lines. Such an arrangement of minerals or grains in metamorphic rocks is called ⭐ FOLIATION or LINEATION.** ⭐ **Sometimes minerals or materials of different groups are arranged into alternating thin to thick layers appearing in light and dark shades — such a structure in metamorphic rocks is called ⭐ BANDING and rocks displaying banding are called BANDED ROCKS.**

⭐ **Metamorphic pairs to know:**

| Original | → | ⭐ Metamorphic |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Clay / shale** | → | ⭐ **Slate** |
| ⭐ **Limestone** | → | ⭐ **Marble** |
| ⭐ **Sandstone** | → | ⭐ **Quartzite** |
| ⭐ **Coal** | → | ⭐ **Graphite** → **Diamond** |
| ⭐ **Granite** | → | ⭐ **Gneiss** |
| ⭐ **Shale** | → | ⭐ **Schist** |

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Rock Cycle

⭐ **Rocks do not remain in their original form for long but may undergo transformation. ROCK CYCLE is a continuous process through which old rocks are transformed into new ones.**

```
   ⭐ THE ROCK CYCLE

              ┌──── heat & pressure ─────┐
              ▼                           │
        METAMORPHIC ◄──────────────── SEDIMENTARY
              │                           ▲
              │ melting                   │ compaction &
              ▼                           │ cementation
            MAGMA                     SEDIMENTS
              │                           ▲
              │ cooling                   │ weathering &
              ▼                           │ erosion
          ⭐ IGNEOUS ───────────────────┘
         (PRIMARY ROCK)
```

⭐ **IGNEOUS ROCKS ARE PRIMARY ROCKS AND OTHER ROCKS (SEDIMENTARY AND METAMORPHIC) FORM FROM THESE PRIMARY ROCKS.**

⭐ **Sedimentary rocks themselves can turn into fragments and the fragments can be a source for the formation of sedimentary rocks.** ⭐ **The crustal rocks (igneous, metamorphic and sedimentary) once formed may be carried down into the mantle through subduction (heating and cooling) and the same melt-material may contribute to the fresh formation of crustal rocks.**

> ⭐ **The idea to carry: no rock is final.** The Himalayas will one day be silt on an ocean floor; that silt will become sedimentary rock; subduction may melt it into magma; it may erupt as basalt. ⭐ **The same atoms have cycled through this loop for 4.5 billion years.** It is the lithosphere's equivalent of the water cycle — and it connects directly to Ch.4's insight that **ocean floor is never older than 200 million years** because it is continuously recycled.

**Trap 5:** *"Gneiss forms from limestone."* ⭐ **From granite.** Limestone gives **marble**.

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Mineral** | Naturally occurring substance with definite chemical composition and orderly atomic structure |
| ⭐ **Rock** | An aggregate of one or more minerals, with **no** definite composition |
| ⭐ **Petrology** | The science of rocks |
| ⭐ **Cleavage** | Tendency to break along plane surfaces |
| ⭐ **Fracture** | Irregular breakage not along a cleavage plane |
| ⭐ **Lustre** | Appearance of a mineral without regard to colour |
| ⭐ **Streak** | Colour of a mineral's powdered form |
| ⭐ **Mohs scale** | Relative hardness scale, talc (1) to diamond (10) |
| ⭐ **Plutonic / intrusive** | Igneous rock cooled deep underground — large crystals |
| ⭐ **Volcanic / extrusive** | Igneous rock cooled at the surface — fine crystals |
| ⭐ **Lithification** | Compaction of sediments into sedimentary rock |
| ⭐ **Foliation / lineation** | Arrangement of minerals in layers or lines in metamorphic rock |
| ⭐ **Banding** | Alternating light and dark mineral layers |
| ⭐ **Contact metamorphism** | Alteration by contact with intruding magma |
| ⭐ **Regional metamorphism** | Alteration over a wide area by tectonic heat and pressure |
| ⭐ **Rock cycle** | Continuous transformation of one rock type into another |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Mineral = definite chemical composition and orderly atomic structure. Rock = aggregate of minerals with NO definite composition.**
2. ⭐ **~2,000 minerals exist but only six are common rock-formers: feldspar, quartz, pyroxene, amphibole, mica, olivine.** Feldspar and quartz are the commonest in rocks.
3. ⭐ **Properties: crystal form, cleavage, fracture, lustre, colour, streak, transparency, structure, hardness.**
4. ⭐ **Mohs scale: talc (1) → diamond (10). Relative, not absolute.**
5. ⭐ **Streak can differ from colour** — fluorite is purple/green but streaks white.
6. ⭐ **Metallic (precious / ferrous / non-ferrous) vs non-metallic minerals.**
7. ⭐ **Igneous = primary rock.** Intrusive/plutonic = **granite**, large crystals. Extrusive/volcanic = **basalt**, fine crystals (**Deccan Traps**).
8. ⭐ **Sedimentary: three origins — mechanical, organic, chemical.** ⭐ **75% of the surface but only 5% of the volume.** **Fossils occur only here.**
9. ⭐ **Metamorphic: dynamic (mechanical) and thermal (contact + regional).** Textures: **foliation, lineation, banding.**
10. ⭐ **Pairs: clay→slate · limestone→marble · sandstone→quartzite · coal→graphite→diamond · granite→gneiss · shale→schist.**
11. ⭐ **The rock cycle is a closed loop** — no rock is final. The same atoms have cycled for 4.5 billion years.
