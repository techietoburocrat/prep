## 1️⃣ Waves

**Waves are actually the ENERGY, not the water as such, which moves across the ocean surface.** **Water particles only travel in a small circle as a wave passes.**

**Wind provides energy to the waves.** **Wind causes waves to travel in the ocean and the energy is released on shorelines.**

**The motion of the surface water seldom affects the stagnant deep bottom water of the oceans.** **As a wave approaches the beach, it slows down.** **This is due to the friction occurring between the dynamic water and the sea floor.** **And, when the depth of water is less than half the wavelength of the wave, the wave breaks.**

**The largest waves are found in the open oceans.** **Waves continue to grow larger as they move and absorb energy from the wind.**

### Characteristics of waves

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Wave crest and trough** | **The highest and lowest points of a wave respectively** |
| **Wave height** | **The vertical distance from the bottom of a trough to the top of a crest of a wave** |
| **Wave amplitude** | **One-half of the wave height** |
| **Wave period** | **Merely the time interval between two successive wave crests or troughs as they pass a fixed point** |
| **Wavelength** | **The horizontal distance between two successive crests** |
| **Wave speed** | **The rate at which the wave moves through the water, and is measured in knots** |
| **Wave frequency** | **The number of waves passing a given point during a one-second time interval** |

> **The key conceptual point, which is examinable: IT IS THE ENERGY THAT TRAVELS, NOT THE WATER.**
>
> 🌾 **The comparison that fits: wind moving across a wheat field.** **A visible wave crosses the field, but no stalk of wheat travels anywhere** — each simply bends and returns. **The water in an ocean wave behaves the same way: particles move in small circles and return roughly to where they started.** **A floating cork bobs up and down; it does not race toward the shore.**
>
> **This is why the deep bottom water is barely affected by surface waves**, and why waves only "break" when the seabed rises enough to interfere with those circles — **specifically when depth becomes less than half the wavelength.**

## 2️⃣ Tides

> **The periodical rise and fall of the sea level, once or twice a day, mainly due to the attraction of the sun and the moon, is called a TIDE.**

⚠️ **Movement of water caused by meteorological effects (winds and atmospheric pressure changes) are called SURGES.** **Surges are not regular like tides.**

**The moon's gravitational pull to a great extent and to a lesser extent the sun's gravitational pull, are the major causes for the occurrence of tides.** **Another factor is CENTRIFUGAL FORCE, which is the force that acts to counter balance the gravity.**

**Together, the gravitational pull and the centrifugal force are responsible for creating the two major tidal bulges on the earth.**

**On the side of the earth facing the moon, a tidal bulge occurs while on the opposite side though the gravitational attraction of the moon is less as it is farther away, the centrifugal force causes tidal bulge on the other side.**

**The 'tide-generating' force is the difference between these two forces.**

> **The "why are there TWO bulges" question is genuinely asked, and the answer is neat.**
> **The near side bulges because the Moon's gravity pulls the water toward it.**
> **The far side bulges because it is furthest from the Moon and feels the LEAST gravitational pull, while the CENTRIFUGAL FORCE of the Earth–Moon system throws water outward there.**
> **Hence two high tides and two low tides in roughly 24 hours** — the Earth rotates through both bulges each day.

**The moon revolves round the earth. The earth is closer to the sun than the moon.** **Even though the sun is 27 million times larger than the moon, because it is 387 times farther from the earth than is the moon, its tide-generating force is about half that of the moon.**

⚠️ **Since the earth is also rotating, the tidal bulges also move — hence the periodic rise and fall.**

### Types of tides

**Based on frequency:**

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SEMI-DIURNAL tide** | **The most common tidal pattern, featuring TWO high tides and TWO low tides each day.** **The successive high or low tides are approximately of the same height** |
| **DIURNAL tide** | **There is only ONE high tide and ONE low tide during each day.** **The successive high and low tides are approximately of the same height** |
| **MIXED tide** | **Tides having variations in height are known as mixed tides.** **These tides generally occur along the west coast of North America and on many islands of the Pacific Ocean** |

**Based on the sun, moon and earth positions:**

| Type | Geometry | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **SPRING TIDES** | **The sun, the moon and the earth are in the SAME LINE** — at **full moon** and **new moon** | **The tides are HIGHEST.** **These tides are called spring tides and they occur TWICE A MONTH** |
| **NEAP TIDES** | **The sun and moon are at RIGHT ANGLES to each other** — at the **first and last quarter moon** | **The forces of the sun and moon tend to counteract one another.** **The tidal range is LESS than average.** **These occur TWICE A MONTH, seven days after the spring tides** |

```
   SPRING vs NEAP GEOMETRY

   SPRING TIDE (full & new moon)      NEAP TIDE (quarter moons)
   pulls ADD                           pulls CANCEL

   ☀️ ──── 🌍 ──── 🌕                          🌕
       (in line)                                │
                                        ☀️ ─── 🌍
   HIGHEST tidal range                (right angle)
                                        LOWEST tidal range
```

⚠️ **"Spring tide" has NOTHING to do with the spring season.** **It comes from the sense of water "springing up," and spring tides occur twice every month, all year round.** **A standing Prelims trap.**

**Once in a month, when the moon's orbit is closest to the earth (PERIGEE), unusually high and low tides occur.** **During this time the tidal range is greater than normal.** **Two weeks later, when the moon is farthest from earth (APOGEE), the moon's gravitational force is limited and the tidal ranges are less than their average heights.**

**When the earth is closest to the sun (PERIHELION), around 3rd January each year, tidal ranges are also much greater than usual.** **When the earth is farthest from the sun (APHELION), around 4th July each year, tidal ranges are much less than average.**

### Tidal bore

**The time between the high tide and low tide, when the water level is falling, is called the EBB.** **The time between the low tide and high tide, when the tide is rising, is called the FLOW or FLOOD.**

**In a narrow inlet or bay, the tide may rise so rapidly that it produces a wall of water travelling upstream. This is called a TIDAL BORE.**

**Tidal bores occur where the river mouth is narrow and shallow and the tidal range is large.** **In India, the HOOGHLY river and the Bay of Bengal experience tidal bores.**

### Importance of tides

**Since tides are caused by the earth-moon-sun positions which are known accurately, the tides can be predicted well in advance.** **This helps the navigators and fishermen plan their activities.**

**Tidal flows are of great importance in navigation.** **Tidal heights are very important, especially harbours near rivers and within estuaries having shallow 'bars' at the entrance, which prevent ships and boats from entering into the harbour.**

**Tides are also helpful in DESILTING the sediments and in removing polluted water from river estuaries.**

**Tides are used to generate ELECTRICAL POWER.** **A 3 MW tidal power project at Durgaduani in Sunderbans of West Bengal is under way.**

> **The tidal-desilting point is worth noting — it's an ecosystem service most candidates don't mention.** **Tides flush out sediment and pollutants from estuaries twice daily, keeping harbours navigable and estuarine water quality tolerable.** 🌱 **Which is also why barrages and reclamation that block tidal flow degrade estuaries so quickly.**

## 3️⃣ Ocean Currents

> **Ocean currents are like river flow in oceans. They represent a regular volume of water in a definite path and direction.**

**Ocean currents are influenced by two types of forces namely: (i) PRIMARY FORCES that initiate the movement of water; (ii) SECONDARY FORCES that influence the currents to flow.**

### The forces

| Type | Forces |
|---|---|
| **PRIMARY forces** | **Heating by solar energy · wind · gravity · Coriolis force** |
| **SECONDARY forces** | **Differences in water density** |

**Heating by solar energy causes the water to expand.** **That is why, near the equator the ocean water is about 8 cm higher in level than in the middle latitudes.** **This causes a very slight gradient and water tends to flow down the slope.**

**Wind blowing on the surface of the ocean pushes the water to move.** **Friction between the wind and the water surface affects the movement of the water body in its path.**

**Gravity tends to pull the water down the pile and create gradient variation.** **The Coriolis force intervenes and causes the water to move to the right in the northern hemisphere and to the left in the southern hemisphere.**

**These large accumulations of water and the flow around them are called GYRES.** **These produce large circular currents in all the ocean basins.**

### Types of currents

**On the basis of depth:**

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SURFACE currents** | **Constitute about 10 per cent of all the water in the ocean, these waters are the upper 400 m of the ocean** |
| **DEEP WATER currents** | **Make up the other 90 per cent of the ocean water.** **These waters move around the ocean basins due to variations in the density and gravity.** **Deep waters sink into the deep ocean basins at high latitudes, where the temperatures are cold enough to cause the density to increase** |

**On the basis of temperature:**

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **COLD currents** | **Bring cold water into warm water areas.** **These currents are usually found on the WEST COAST of the continents in the low and middle latitudes (true in both hemispheres) and on the EAST COAST in the higher latitudes in the Northern Hemisphere** |
| **WARM currents** | **Bring warm water into cold water areas and are usually observed on the EAST COAST of continents in the low and middle latitudes (true in both hemispheres).** **In the northern hemisphere they are found on the west coasts of continents in high latitudes** |

> **The coast rule is worth learning as a rule, because it lets you predict rather than memorise:**
> **In low and middle latitudes: WARM currents on EAST coasts, COLD currents on WEST coasts.**
> **In high northern latitudes: the reverse.**
>
> **This is why the west coasts of continents in the subtropics have COASTAL DESERTS beside cold currents** — the **Atacama** (Peru/Humboldt current), the **Namib** (Benguela current), **Baja California** (California current). **Cold water cools the air above it, so the air cannot rise or hold much moisture, and it produces fog rather than rain.** **The driest desert on Earth sits beside the ocean.**

### Major ocean currents — learn by ocean

| Ocean | WARM currents | COLD currents |
|---|---|---|
| **NORTH ATLANTIC** | **Gulf Stream · North Atlantic Drift · Norwegian Current · Brazil Current (S. Atlantic)** | **Labrador Current · Canary Current · Greenland Current** |
| **SOUTH ATLANTIC** | **Brazil Current** | **Benguela Current · Falkland Current** |
| **NORTH PACIFIC** | **Kuroshio (Japan) Current · North Pacific Drift · Alaska Current** | **Oyashio (Kurile) Current · California Current** |
| **SOUTH PACIFIC** | **East Australian Current** | **Humboldt (Peru) Current** |
| **INDIAN OCEAN** | **Agulhas Current · Monsoon Current (reverses seasonally)** | **West Australian Current · Somali Current (seasonal)** |
| **SOUTHERN OCEAN** | — | **West Wind Drift (Antarctic Circumpolar Current)** |

> **The Indian Ocean is unique and this gets asked: its northern currents REVERSE DIRECTION WITH THE MONSOON.** **No other ocean does this.** **Because the Indian Ocean is landlocked to the north, the seasonal reversal of monsoon winds reverses the surface circulation with it** — hence the **Monsoon Current** and the seasonal **Somali Current.**

### Effects of ocean currents

**Ocean currents have a number of direct and indirect influences on human activities:**

| Effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Climate moderation** | **West coasts of the continents in tropical and subtropical latitudes (except close to the equator) are bordered by cool waters. Their average temperatures are relatively low with a narrow diurnal and annual ranges.** **There is fog, but generally the areas are arid** |
| **Warm currents warm high latitudes** | **West coasts of the continents in the middle and higher latitudes are bordered by warm waters which cause a distinct marine climate.** **They are characterised by cool summers and relatively mild winters with a narrow annual range of temperatures** |
| **FISHING** | **Mixing of warm and cold currents help to replenish the oxygen and favour the growth of plankton, the primary food for fish population.** **The best fishing grounds of the world exist mainly in these mixing zones** |
| **Navigation** | Currents assist or hinder shipping; also carry icebergs |
| **Fog** | **Mixing zones produce dense fog, a hazard to navigation** |

> **The world's great fishing grounds sit where WARM AND COLD CURRENTS MEET** — and the reason is nutrients:
> - **Grand Banks of Newfoundland** — Gulf Stream meets Labrador Current
> - **Japanese coast** — Kuroshio meets Oyashio
> - **North Sea** — North Atlantic Drift meets colder Arctic water
>
> **Cold currents bring nutrients up from the deep; sunlight in shallow water lets plankton bloom; plankton feeds fish.** ⚠️ **And these zones are also notoriously foggy** — warm moist air over cold water condenses. **The richest fisheries are the most dangerous to sail.**

> 🌱 **UPWELLING is the mechanism to know, and it links this chapter to Ch.10's ENSO.**
> **Where winds push surface water away from a coast, cold nutrient-rich water rises from below to replace it.** **The Peru (Humboldt) coast is the classic case — upwelling supports the world's largest single fishery.**
> **During EL NIÑO, the trade winds weaken, upwelling shuts down, warm nutrient-poor water covers the coast, and the fishery COLLAPSES.** **The plankton and fish loss NCERT lists as El Niño's third consequence is exactly this.**
>
> 🎯 **So a Pacific wind anomaly becomes a Peruvian economic crisis through the thermocline.** **That chain — wind → upwelling → nutrients → plankton → fish → livelihoods — is a strong, specific answer structure for GS-I or GS-III.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Waves carry ENERGY, not water** — particles move in small circles. **A wave breaks when depth < half the wavelength.**
2. **Tides are caused by the moon's gravity plus CENTRIFUGAL FORCE**, producing **two bulges** — hence two high tides daily.
3. **The sun's tide-generating force is about HALF the moon's**, though it is 27 million times larger — because it is 387 times farther away.
4. **Semi-diurnal (most common) · diurnal · mixed (west coast of N. America, Pacific islands).**
5. **SPRING tides: sun–moon–earth in line (full and new moon), HIGHEST range, twice a month.** **NEAP tides: right angles (quarter moons), LOWEST range, seven days after.** ⚠️ **Nothing to do with the season.**
6. **Perigee/apogee and perihelion (3 Jan)/aphelion (4 July) modulate tidal range.**
7. **EBB = falling, FLOW/FLOOD = rising.** **TIDAL BORE on the Hooghly.** **Durgaduani (Sunderbans) tidal power, 3 MW.**
8. **Tides aid navigation, desilt estuaries, flush pollution, and generate power.**
9. **Primary forces: solar heating, wind, gravity, Coriolis. Secondary: density differences.** **GYRES are the large circular systems.**
10. **Surface currents = 10% (upper 400 m); deep water = 90%, driven by density.**
11. **Coast rule: in low/mid latitudes, WARM currents on EAST coasts, COLD on WEST.** **Hence coastal deserts — Atacama, Namib, Baja — beside cold currents.**
12. **Indian Ocean currents REVERSE with the monsoon** — unique among the oceans.
13. **The great fisheries sit where warm and cold currents meet** (Grand Banks, Japan) — nutrients plus sunlight. **These zones are also the foggiest.**
14. 🌱 **UPWELLING off Peru supports the world's largest fishery — and EL NIÑO shuts it down**, which is why a Pacific wind anomaly becomes an economic crisis.
