## 1️⃣ ⭐ Atmospheric Pressure

> ⭐ **The weight of a column of air contained in a unit area from the mean sea level to the top of the atmosphere is called the ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE.**

⭐ **The atmospheric pressure is expressed in units of measurement called the PASCAL, written as Pa.** ⭐ **The weather maps generally show the distribution of atmospheric pressure at sea level.** ⭐ **The pressure measured at stations at different elevations has to be reduced to the sea level for the purpose of comparison** — because pressure varies so strongly with height.

### ⭐ Vertical variation of pressure

⭐ **In the lower atmosphere the pressure decreases rapidly with height.** ⭐ **The decrease amounts to about 1 mb for each 10 m increase in elevation.**

⚠️ ⭐ **It does not always decrease at the same rate. Since the vertical pressure gradient force is much larger than that of the horizontal pressure gradient, it is generally balanced by a nearly equal but opposite gravitational force.** ⭐ **Hence, we do not experience strong upward winds.**

> ⭐⭐ **This is a subtle but genuinely important point most notes skip.** ⭐ **The vertical pressure gradient is FAR stronger than the horizontal one** — pressure drops enormously over a few kilometres upward, but only slightly over hundreds of kilometres sideways.
>
> ⭐ **So why isn't there a permanent violent updraft?** Because **gravity almost exactly cancels it.** The two nearly balance — a state called **hydrostatic equilibrium.**
>
> ⭐ **What's left over is the small horizontal gradient — and THAT is what drives wind.** **Wind is essentially horizontal because the vertical forces have already cancelled.**

### ⭐ Horizontal distribution of pressure — isobars

> ⭐ **ISOBARS are lines connecting places having equal pressure.**

⭐ **Small differences in pressure are highly significant in terms of wind direction and velocity.** ⭐ **Horizontal distribution of pressure is studied by drawing isobars at constant levels.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ World Distribution of Sea Level Pressure — The Four Belts

⭐ **The world distribution of sea level pressure shows the presence of seven pressure belts** *(four types, mirrored across the hemispheres)*:

```
   ⭐⭐ THE PRESSURE BELTS — AND WHY EACH EXISTS

   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 90°N  ⭐ POLAR HIGH             ← THERMAL (cold air sinks)   │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ 60°N  ⭐ SUB-POLAR LOW          ← ⭐ DYNAMIC (air forced up) │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ 30°N  ⭐ SUB-TROPICAL HIGH      ← ⭐ DYNAMIC (air descends)  │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │  0°   ⭐ EQUATORIAL LOW (ITCZ)  ← THERMAL (hot air rises)    │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ 30°S  ⭐ SUB-TROPICAL HIGH      ← DYNAMIC                    │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ 60°S  ⭐ SUB-POLAR LOW          ← DYNAMIC                    │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ 90°S  ⭐ POLAR HIGH             ← THERMAL                    │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Belt | ⭐ Location | ⭐ Origin | ⭐ Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Equatorial Low (ITCZ)** | 0°–5° | ⭐ **THERMAL** | Intense heating → air expands, becomes lighter, **rises** |
| ⭐ **Sub-Tropical High** | 30°N/S | ⭐ **DYNAMIC** | Risen equatorial air, having cooled, **descends** here |
| ⭐ **Sub-Polar Low** | 60°N/S | ⭐ **DYNAMIC** | Warm westerlies and cold polar easterlies meet; warm air **forced up** |
| ⭐ **Polar High** | 90°N/S | ⭐ **THERMAL** | Extreme cold → air contracts, becomes dense, **sinks** |

> ⭐⭐ **THERMAL vs DYNAMIC is the distinction UPSC loves, and most candidates cannot state it.**
>
> ⭐ **THERMALLY induced belts** (equatorial low, polar high) exist because of **temperature** — hot air is light and rises; cold air is dense and sinks. **Straightforward.**
>
> ⭐ **DYNAMICALLY induced belts** (sub-tropical high, sub-polar low) exist because of **AIR MOVEMENT, not local temperature.**
> - ⭐ **The subtropical high at 30° is NOT there because it is cold** — it is one of the hottest latitudes on Earth (the great deserts sit there). ⭐ **It is high pressure because air that rose at the equator has travelled poleward aloft, cooled, become dense, and is now DESCENDING** — piling air onto the surface.
> - ⭐ **The sub-polar low at 60° is NOT there because it is warm.** ⭐ **It is low pressure because converging air masses force air UPWARD**, removing it from the surface.
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **And this explains the world's deserts.** ⭐ **The Sahara, Arabian, Thar, Kalahari, Atacama and Australian deserts all lie near 30°N/S** — under the **descending limb** of the subtropical high. ⭐ **Descending air WARMS and DRIES as it compresses, so it cannot produce rain.** **The great deserts are not where the sun is hottest; they are where the air is sinking.** That is a powerful, compact line for a Mains answer.

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Forces Affecting Wind

⭐ **The wind is driven by three forces.** ⭐ **The air in motion is called wind.**

| Force | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. PRESSURE GRADIENT FORCE (PGF)** | ⭐ **The differences in atmospheric pressure produces a force.** ⭐ **The rate of change of pressure with respect to distance is the pressure gradient.** ⭐ **The pressure gradient is strong where the isobars are CLOSE to each other and is weak where the isobars are APART** |
| ⭐ **2. FRICTIONAL FORCE** | ⭐ **It affects the speed of the wind. It is greatest at the surface and its influence generally extends up to an elevation of 1–3 km.** ⭐ **Over the sea surface the friction is minimal** |
| ⭐ **3. CORIOLIS FORCE** | ⭐ **The rotation of the earth about its axis affects the direction of the wind. This force is called the Coriolis force after the French scientist who described it in 1844.** ⭐ **It deflects the wind to the RIGHT direction in the northern hemisphere and to the LEFT in the southern hemisphere.** ⭐ **The deflection is more when the wind velocity is high** |

### ⭐⭐ The Coriolis force — properly understood

⭐ **The Coriolis force is directly proportional to the angle of latitude.** ⭐ **It is MAXIMUM AT THE POLES and is ABSENT AT THE EQUATOR.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The Coriolis force acts perpendicular to the pressure gradient force.** ⭐ **The pressure gradient force is perpendicular to an isobar.** ⭐ **The higher the pressure gradient force, the more is the velocity of the wind and the larger is the deflection in the direction of wind.**

⭐ **As a result of these two forces operating perpendicular to each other, in the low-pressure areas the wind blows around it.**

> ⭐⭐ **The two Coriolis facts that get tested constantly: ⭐ MAXIMUM AT THE POLES, ZERO AT THE EQUATOR.**
>
> 🎠 **The comparison that fits: throwing a ball on a spinning merry-go-round.** You throw it straight, but by the time it arrives the target has rotated away — so from your rotating point of view, the ball **curved**. ⭐ **Nothing pushed it sideways; the ground beneath moved.** That is why Coriolis is called a *pseudo-force* — it is real in its effects but arises from observing motion on a rotating frame.
>
> ⭐⭐ **And the zero-at-the-equator fact has a huge consequence you must know:** ⭐ **tropical cyclones CANNOT form within about 5° of the equator**, because without Coriolis deflection the converging air cannot begin to **spin**. ⭐ **This is why no cyclone forms on the equator itself** — a favourite Prelims question, and the reason is worth stating, not just the fact.

### ⭐ Pressure and wind relationship — geostrophic wind

⭐ **The velocity and direction of the wind are the net result of the wind generating forces.** ⭐ **The winds in the upper atmosphere, 2–3 km above the surface, are free from frictional effect of the surface and are controlled mainly by the pressure gradient and the Coriolis force.**

⭐ **When isobars are straight and when there is no friction, the pressure gradient force is balanced by the Coriolis force and the resultant wind blows PARALLEL TO THE ISOBAR.** ⭐ **This wind is known as the GEOSTROPHIC WIND.**

> ⭐ **This is genuinely counter-intuitive and therefore examinable.** ⭐ **You would expect wind to blow from high pressure straight to low pressure — directly down the gradient.** ⭐ **Above ~1 km, it doesn't. It blows PARALLEL to the isobars**, because the Coriolis force has deflected it until it exactly balances the pressure gradient. ⭐ **Near the surface, friction spoils the balance, so surface winds DO cross isobars at an angle** — which is why they spiral into a low rather than circling it forever.

### ⭐⭐ Cyclonic and anticyclonic circulation

⭐ **The wind circulation around a low is called CYCLONIC circulation. Around a high it is called ANTICYCLONIC circulation.** ⭐ **The direction of winds around such systems changes according to their location in different hemispheres.**

| System | ⭐ Northern Hemisphere | ⭐ Southern Hemisphere | ⭐ Vertical motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **CYCLONE (Low)** | ⭐ **ANTICLOCKWISE** | ⭐ **CLOCKWISE** | ⭐ **Air RISES (converges at surface)** — cloudy, wet |
| ⭐ **ANTICYCLONE (High)** | ⭐ **CLOCKWISE** | ⭐ **ANTICLOCKWISE** | ⭐ **Air DESCENDS (diverges at surface)** — clear, dry |

⭐ **The wind circulation at the earth's surface around low and high on many occasions is closely related to the wind circulation at higher level.** ⭐ **Generally, over low pressure area the air will converge and rise. Over high pressure area the air will subside from above and diverge at the surface.**

> ⭐ **Memory hook: in the Northern Hemisphere, a cyclone spins ANTI-clockwise. Reverse both words for a Southern Hemisphere cyclone and for an anticyclone.**
>
> ⭐ **And the weather follows from the vertical motion, not the spin.** ⭐ **Low = rising air = cooling = condensation = CLOUD AND RAIN.** ⭐ **High = sinking air = warming = evaporation of cloud = CLEAR SKIES.** **This is why weather forecasts talk about highs and lows at all.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ General Circulation of the Atmosphere — The Three Cells

⭐ **The pattern of planetary winds largely depends on: (i) latitudinal variation of atmospheric heating; (ii) emergence of pressure belts; (iii) the migration of belts following apparent path of the sun; (iv) the distribution of continents and oceans; (v) the rotation of earth.**

⭐ **The pattern of the movement of the planetary winds is called the GENERAL CIRCULATION OF THE ATMOSPHERE.** ⭐ **The general circulation of the atmosphere also sets in motion the ocean water circulation which influences the earth's climate.**

```
   ⭐⭐ THE THREE-CELL MODEL

   90°N  ❄️ POLAR HIGH
         │  ↓ sinking
         │        ⭐ POLAR CELL          ← polar easterlies blow equatorward
         │  ↑ rising
   60°N  🌀 SUB-POLAR LOW  ←─ where warm westerlies meet cold easterlies
         │  ↓                                (the POLAR FRONT)
         │        ⭐ FERREL CELL          ← westerlies blow poleward
         │  ↑
   30°N  ☀️ SUB-TROPICAL HIGH (descending, dry → DESERTS)
         │  ↓ sinking
         │        ⭐ HADLEY CELL          ← trade winds blow equatorward
         │  ↑ rising
    0°   🌧️ EQUATORIAL LOW / ITCZ  (rising, wet → RAINFOREST)
```

### ⭐ The three cells

| Cell | ⭐ Between | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **HADLEY CELL** | ⭐ **Equator and 30°** | ⭐ **The air at the Inter Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) rises because of convection caused by high insolation and a low pressure is created.** ⭐ **The winds from the tropics converge at this low pressure zone.** ⭐ **The converged air rises along with the convective cell. It reaches the top of the troposphere up to an altitude of 14 km and moves towards the poles.** ⭐ **This causes accumulation of air at about 30° N and S.** ⭐ **Part of the accumulated air sinks to the ground and forms a subtropical high.** ⭐ **The cell is called the Hadley Cell** |
| ⭐ **FERREL CELL** | ⭐ **30° and 60°** | ⭐ **In the middle latitudes the circulation is that of sinking cold air that comes from the poles and the rising warm air that blows from the subtropical high.** ⭐ **At the surface these winds are called westerlies and the cell is known as the Ferrel cell** |
| ⭐ **POLAR CELL** | ⭐ **60° and pole** | ⭐ **At polar latitudes the cold dense air subsides at the poles and blows towards middle latitudes as the polar easterlies.** ⭐ **This cell is called the polar cell** |

⭐ **These three cells set the pattern for the general circulation of the atmosphere.** ⭐ **The transfer of heat energy from lower latitudes to higher latitudes maintains the general circulation.**

### ⭐ The planetary winds

| Wind | ⭐ Blows from → to | ⭐ Direction |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **TRADE WINDS** | Subtropical high (30°) → Equatorial low (0°) | ⭐ **NE trades** in N. Hemisphere; ⭐ **SE trades** in S. Hemisphere |
| ⭐ **WESTERLIES** | Subtropical high (30°) → Sub-polar low (60°) | ⭐ **SW** in N. Hemisphere; ⭐ **NW** in S. Hemisphere |
| ⭐ **POLAR EASTERLIES** | Polar high (90°) → Sub-polar low (60°) | ⭐ **NE** in N. Hemisphere; ⭐ **SE** in S. Hemisphere |

> ⭐⭐ **FERREL'S LAW:** ⭐ **winds are deflected to the RIGHT in the Northern Hemisphere and to the LEFT in the Southern Hemisphere** — which is why the trades blow from the north-east rather than straight from the north.
>
> ⭐ **Note the naming convention that trips people up: WINDS ARE NAMED FOR WHERE THEY COME FROM, not where they go.** ⭐ **A "westerly" blows FROM the west.** So westerlies at 30–60° carry weather **eastward** — which is why European weather generally arrives from the Atlantic, and why transatlantic flights are faster going east.

### ⭐ General circulation and the oceans — Walker circulation and ENSO

⭐ **The general atmospheric circulation has a great impact on the oceans.** ⭐ **The large-scale winds of the atmosphere initiate large and slow moving currents of the ocean.** ⭐ **Oceans in turn provide input of energy and water vapour into the air.**

⭐ **These interactions take place rather slowly over a large part of the ocean and atmosphere, and they can lead to abnormal weather conditions.**

⭐ **The pattern of circulation described is called the WALKER CIRCULATION.** ⭐ **Under normal conditions, the eastern Pacific (near Peru) has cold water and higher pressure, while the western Pacific (near Indonesia) has warm water and lower pressure — so the trade winds blow from east to west.**

⭐ **This pattern of low pressure over the eastern Pacific and high pressure over the western Pacific is known as the SOUTHERN OSCILLATION or SO.**

### ⭐⭐ El Niño and La Niña

⭐ **The changes in pressure conditions are connected to the EL NINO.** ⭐ **Hence, the phenomenon is referred to as ENSO (El Nino Southern Oscillations).**

⭐ **EL NINO is a complex weather system that appears once every three to seven years, bringing drought, floods and other weather extremes to different parts of the world.** ⭐ **The system involves oceanic and atmospheric phenomena with the appearance of warm currents off the coast of Peru in the Eastern Pacific and affects weather in many places including India.** ⭐ **El Nino is merely an extension of the warm equatorial current which gets replaced temporarily by cold Peruvian current or Humboldt current.** ⭐ **This current increases the temperature of water on the Peruvian coast by 10°C.** ⭐ **This results in: (i) the distortion of equatorial atmospheric circulation; (ii) irregularities in the evaporation of sea water; (iii) reduction in the amount of planktons which further reduces the number of fish in the sea.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The word El Nino means 'Child Christ' because this current appears around Christmas in December.** ⭐ **December is a summer month in Peru (Southern Hemisphere).**

⭐ **El Nino is used in India for forecasting long range monsoon rainfall.** ⭐ **In 1990–91, a weak El Nino began in May and continued to July of the same year.**

> ⭐⭐ **The El Niño chain, stated so you can reproduce it:**
> ⭐ **Normal:** trade winds push warm water **west** toward Indonesia → **warm, rising, wet in the west; cold, sinking, dry off Peru** → **good Indian monsoon.**
> ⭐ **El Niño:** trade winds weaken or reverse → **warm water sloshes back EAST toward Peru** → the rising branch of the Walker circulation **shifts eastward, away from Asia** → **weakened monsoon over India, floods in Peru.**
> ⭐ **La Niña:** the opposite — stronger-than-normal trades, colder eastern Pacific, **often a good Indian monsoon.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The nuance that scores: El Niño INCREASES THE PROBABILITY of a weak monsoon; it does not guarantee one.** Some El Niño years have had normal Indian rainfall. **Write "strongly associated with, but not deterministic of" rather than an absolute claim.**
>
> 🐟 ⭐ **And note NCERT's third consequence — the collapse of plankton and fish.** The cold Peruvian current normally brings **nutrient-rich upwelling** which feeds the world's richest fishery. ⭐ **El Niño shuts the upwelling off, and the Peruvian anchovy fishery collapses.** **This is why a Pacific temperature anomaly is simultaneously a climate story, a food-security story and an economics story.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐ Seasonal and Local Winds

### ⭐ Seasonal winds

⭐ **The pattern of wind circulation is modified in different seasons due to the shifting of regions of maximum heating, pressure and wind belts.** ⭐ **The most pronounced effect of such a shift is noticed in the MONSOONS, especially over Southeast Asia.**

### ⭐⭐ Local winds

| Wind | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **LAND AND SEA BREEZES** | ⭐ **During the day the land heats up faster and becomes warmer than the sea. Therefore, over the land the air rises giving rise to a low pressure area, whereas the sea is relatively cool and the pressure over sea is relatively high.** ⭐ **Thus, pressure gradient from sea to land is created and the wind blows from the sea to the land as the SEA BREEZE.** ⭐ **In the night the reversal of condition takes place. The land loses heat faster and is cooler than the sea. The pressure gradient is from the land to the sea and hence land breeze results** |
| ⭐ **MOUNTAIN AND VALLEY WINDS** | ⭐ **In mountainous regions, during the day the slopes get heated up and air moves upslope and to fill the resulting gap the air from the valley blows up the valley. This wind is known as the VALLEY BREEZE.** ⭐ **During the night the slopes get cooled and the dense air descends into the valley as the MOUNTAIN WIND.** ⭐ **The cool air, of the high plateaus and ice fields draining into the valley is called KATABATIC WIND.** ⭐ **Another type of warm wind occurs on the leeward side of the mountain ranges. The moisture in these winds, while crossing the mountain ranges condense and precipitate. When it descends down the leeward side of the slope the dry air gets warmed up by adiabatic process. This dry air may melt the snow in a short time** |

> ⭐ **Anabatic (up-valley, daytime) and katabatic (down-slope, night) is the pairing to remember.** ⭐ **Memory: KAta = KAscading down.**

> ⭐ **The warm leeward wind NCERT describes has famous named examples worth knowing:** ⭐ **CHINOOK** (Rockies, North America — "snow eater"), ⭐ **FOEHN** (Alps), ⭐ **BERG** (South Africa). ⭐ **India's LOO** is a hot dry wind but of a different type (advection over heated land).
>
> ⭐ **Why these winds are warm AND dry — the adiabatic mechanism, which is the key idea:** air forced up the windward side **expands, cools, and precipitates its moisture.** Descending the leeward side it is **compressed and warms** — but it has already lost its water. ⭐ **So it arrives hot and dry.** **This is the same process that creates the rain shadow** (Cl.7 Ch.4) — the wind and the dryness are two faces of one mechanism.

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐ Air Masses and Fronts

### ⭐ Air masses

> ⭐ **When the air remains over a homogenous area for a sufficiently longer time, it acquires the characteristics of the area. The homogenous regions can be the vast ocean surface or vast plains. The air with distinctive characteristics in terms of temperature and humidity is called an AIR MASS.**

⭐ **It is defined as a large body of air having little horizontal variation in temperature and moisture.** ⭐ **The homogenous surfaces, over which air masses form, are called the SOURCE REGIONS.**

⭐ **The air masses are classified according to the source regions.** ⭐ **There are five major source regions:**

| Air mass | ⭐ Symbol | ⭐ Character |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Maritime tropical** | ⭐ **mT** | Warm, moist |
| ⭐ **Continental tropical** | ⭐ **cT** | Warm, dry |
| ⭐ **Maritime polar** | ⭐ **mP** | Cold, moist |
| ⭐ **Continental polar** | ⭐ **cP** | Cold, dry |
| ⭐ **Continental arctic** | ⭐ **cA** | Extremely cold, dry |

> ⭐ **The naming logic is systematic: first letter = SURFACE (m = maritime/moist, c = continental/dry); second letter = LATITUDE (T = tropical/warm, P = polar/cold, A = arctic).** ⭐ **So "mT" is warm and moist; "cP" is cold and dry.** Learn the logic, not the list.

### ⭐⭐ Fronts

⭐ **When two different air masses meet, the boundary zone between them is called a FRONT.** ⭐ **The process of formation of the fronts is known as FRONTOGENESIS.**

⭐ **There are four types of fronts:**

| Front | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **COLD FRONT** | ⭐ **When the cold air moves towards the warm air mass, its contact zone is called the cold front.** ⭐ **The cold air, being denser, undercuts the warm air and forces it up STEEPLY.** ⭐ **Produces violent, short-duration precipitation — thunderstorms, cumulonimbus clouds** |
| ⭐ **WARM FRONT** | ⭐ **If the warm air mass moves towards the cold air mass, the contact zone is a warm front.** ⭐ **The warm air rises GENTLY over the cold air.** ⭐ **Produces gentle, prolonged, widespread precipitation — stratus clouds** |
| ⭐ **STATIONARY FRONT** | ⭐ **When the front remains stationary, it is called a stationary front** |
| ⭐ **OCCLUDED FRONT** | ⭐ **When an air mass is fully lifted above the land surface, it is called the occluded front.** ⭐ **The cold front overtakes the warm front and lifts the warm air entirely off the ground** |

⭐ **Fronts occur in middle latitudes and are characterised by steep gradient in temperature and pressure.** ⭐ **They bring abrupt changes in temperature and cause the air to rise to form clouds and cause precipitation.**

> ⭐ **Cold front vs warm front — the mechanical difference and its weather consequence:**
> ⭐ **COLD FRONT: steep slope → rapid, violent uplift → tall cumulonimbus → thunderstorms, heavy but BRIEF rain, then clearing.**
> ⭐ **WARM FRONT: gentle slope → slow, gradual uplift → layered stratus → light, steady, PROLONGED drizzle over a wide area.**
> ⭐ **The angle of the front determines the type of weather.** That single insight makes the four types memorable rather than arbitrary.

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Cyclones

### ⭐⭐ Extra-tropical cyclones

⭐ **The systems developing in the mid and high latitude, beyond the tropics are called the middle latitude or EXTRA TROPICAL CYCLONES.** ⭐ **The passage of front causes abrupt changes in the weather conditions over the area in the middle and high latitudes.**

⭐ **Extra tropical cyclones form along the POLAR FRONT.** ⭐ **Initially, the front is stationary. In the northern hemisphere, warm air blows from the south and cold air from the north of the front.** ⭐ **When the pressure drops along the front, the warm air moves northwards and the cold air move towards south setting in motion an anticlockwise cyclonic circulation.**

⭐ **The cyclonic circulation leads to a well developed extra tropical cyclone, with a warm front and a cold front.** ⭐ **There are pockets of warm air or warm sector wedged between the forward and the rear cold air.** ⭐ **The warm air glides over the cold air and a sequence of clouds appear over the sky ahead of the warm front and cause precipitation.**

⭐ **The cold front approaches the warm air from behind and pushes the warm air up.** ⭐ **As a result, cumulus clouds develop along the cold front.** ⭐ **The cold front moves faster than the warm front ultimately overtaking the warm front.** ⭐ **The warm air is completely lifted up and the front is occluded and the cyclone dissipates.**

### ⭐⭐ Tropical cyclones

⭐ **Tropical cyclones are violent storms that originate over oceans in tropical areas and move over to the coastal areas bringing about large scale destruction caused by violent winds, very heavy rainfall and storm surges.** ⭐ **This is one of the most devastating natural calamities.**

⭐ **They are known as CYCLONES in the Indian Ocean, HURRICANES in the Atlantic, TYPHOONS in the Western Pacific and South China Sea, and WILLY-WILLIES in the Western Australia.**

### ⭐⭐ Conditions for formation — a favourite question

⭐ **Tropical cyclones develop in the late summer months (August to mid-October). The conditions favourable for the formation and intensification of tropical storms are:**

| Condition | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **(i)** | ⭐ **Large sea surface with temperature higher than 27°C** |
| ⭐ **(ii)** | ⭐ **Presence of the Coriolis force** |
| ⭐ **(iii)** | ⭐ **Small variations in the vertical wind speed** |
| ⭐ **(iv)** | ⭐ **A pre-existing weak low-pressure area or low-level-cyclonic circulation** |
| ⭐ **(v)** | ⭐ **Upper divergence above the sea level system** |

⭐ **The energy that intensifies the storm, comes from the condensation process in the towering cumulonimbus clouds, surrounding the centre of the storm.** ⭐ **With continuous supply of moisture from the sea, the storm is further strengthened.**

⚠️ ⭐ **On reaching the land the moisture supply is cut off and the storm dissipates.** ⭐ **The place where a tropical cyclone crosses the coast is called the LANDFALL of the cyclone.**

⭐ **The cyclones, which cross 20° N latitude generally, recurve and they are more destructive.**

> ⭐⭐ **The 27°C threshold and the Coriolis requirement together explain the entire geography of cyclones**, and both are near-certain Prelims content:
> - ⭐ **27°C sea surface temperature** — needed to evaporate enough water to power the storm. **This is why cyclones form only in the tropics, and only in the warm season.**
> - ⭐ **Coriolis force** — needed to make the converging air **rotate**. ⭐ **Since Coriolis is ZERO at the equator, no cyclone can form within ~5° of it.**
>
> ⭐ **And the "dissipates on landfall" fact follows from the same logic:** the storm is a **heat engine fuelled by evaporation from warm water.** ⭐ **Cut off the water and the engine stops.** **This is why cyclones weaken rapidly inland — and why coastal areas bear the damage.**

### ⭐⭐ Structure of a tropical cyclone

⭐ **A mature tropical cyclone is characterised by the strong spirally circulating wind around the centre, called the EYE.** ⭐ **The diameter of the circulating system can vary between 150 and 250 km.**

⭐ **The eye is a region of calm with subsiding air.** ⭐ **Around the eye is the EYE WALL, where there is a strong spiralling ascent of air to greater height reaching the tropopause.** ⭐ **The wind reaches maximum velocity in this region, reaching as high as 250 kmph.**

⭐ **Torrential rain occurs here.** ⭐ **From the eye wall rain bands may radiate and trains of cumulus and cumulonimbus clouds may drift into the outer region.** ⭐ **The diameter of the storm over the Bay of Bengal, Arabian sea and Indian ocean is between 600–1200 km.** ⭐ **The system moves slowly about 300–500 km per day.**

```
   ⭐ CROSS-SECTION OF A TROPICAL CYCLONE

        rain bands        ⭐ EYE WALL        ⭐ EYE        EYE WALL
            ↑↑↑              ↑↑↑↑↑          ↓ calm         ↑↑↑↑↑
         ╱▔▔▔▔▔╲          ╱▔▔▔▔▔▔╲         ┆ subsiding   ╱▔▔▔▔▔▔╲
        ╱ clouds ╲        ╱ TALLEST ╲       ┆ air        ╱ TALLEST ╲
       ╱          ╲      ╱ CLOUDS    ╲      ┆           ╱  CLOUDS   ╲
   ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
                          ⭐ 250 kmph      ⭐ CALM      ⭐ 250 kmph
                          torrential rain   clear sky    torrential rain

   ⭐ Total diameter 150–250 km (system); 600–1,200 km over Bay of Bengal
```

> ⭐⭐ **The eye is the detail that catches people out.** ⭐ **The very centre of the most violent storm on Earth is CALM, with CLEAR SKY and DESCENDING air.** ⭐ **The violence is in the EYE WALL immediately around it, where winds reach 250 kmph.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **This has a lethal practical consequence worth knowing for a disaster-management answer:** as the eye passes over a location, conditions suddenly become **calm and clear** — and people emerge, believing the storm has passed. ⭐ **Then the opposite eye wall arrives, with winds from the reverse direction, and casualties spike.** **The lull is the most dangerous moment.**

### 🌱 Cyclones and India — disaster management

⭐ **The east coast (Bay of Bengal) receives far more cyclones than the west coast (Arabian Sea)** — roughly four times as many. **Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal are the most affected.**

⭐ **Institutional facts for GS-III:** the **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** is the official cyclone-warning agency for the North Indian Ocean; **NDMA** coordinates response; cyclone shelters, early warning dissemination and evacuation are the proven interventions.

> 🌱 ⭐ **The success story worth citing: Odisha.** The 1999 Super Cyclone killed roughly 10,000 people. **Cyclone Phailin in 2013, of comparable intensity, killed fewer than 50** — because of early warning, cyclone shelters and the mass evacuation of nearly a million people. ⭐ **Same hazard, radically different outcome, because of preparedness.**
>
> ⭐ **This is the strongest available Indian example that disaster mortality is a policy variable, not a natural constant** — and it pairs directly with the earthquake argument from Ch.3 (*buildings kill, not quakes*).

### ⭐ Thunderstorms and tornadoes

⭐ **Thunderstorms and tornadoes are severe local storms.** ⭐ **They are of short duration, occurring over a small area but are violent.**

⭐ **Thunderstorms are caused by intense convection on moist hot days.** ⭐ **A thunderstorm is a well-grown cumulonimbus cloud producing thunder and lightening.** ⭐ **When the clouds extend to heights where sub-zero temperature prevails, hails are formed and they come down as hailstorm.** ⭐ **If there is insufficient moisture, a thunderstorm can generate dust-storms.**

⭐ **From severe thunderstorms sometimes spiralling wind descends like a trunk of an elephant with great force, with very low pressure at the centre, causing massive destruction on its way. Such a phenomenon is called a TORNADO.** ⭐ **Tornadoes generally occur in middle latitudes.** ⭐ **The tornado over the sea is called WATER SPOUTS.**

**Trap 1:** *"The subtropical high exists because 30° latitude is cold."* ⭐ **It is DYNAMIC — caused by descending air, not low temperature.** The great deserts sit there.
**Trap 2:** *"The Coriolis force is strongest at the equator."* ⭐ **ZERO at the equator, maximum at the poles.**
**Trap 3:** *"Wind blows directly from high to low pressure."* ⭐ **Above the friction layer it blows PARALLEL to isobars — the geostrophic wind.**
**Trap 4:** *"Cyclones rotate clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere."* ⭐ **Anticlockwise.** Anticyclones are clockwise.
**Trap 5:** *"The eye of a cyclone has the strongest winds."* ⭐ **The eye is CALM; the EYE WALL has the strongest winds.**
**Trap 6:** *"Cyclones can form at the equator."* ⭐ **No — Coriolis is zero there.**
**Trap 7:** *"Tornadoes mainly occur in the tropics."* ⭐ **Middle latitudes.**
**Trap 8:** *"A warm front produces violent thunderstorms."* ⭐ **Gentle prolonged drizzle.** The **cold front** produces the violent weather.
**Trap 9:** *"Willy-willies occur in the Atlantic."* ⭐ **Western Australia.** Atlantic = **hurricanes**.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Pressure falls ~1 mb per 10 m; the vertical gradient is nearly cancelled by gravity, leaving the small horizontal gradient to drive wind.**
2. ⭐⭐ **Four belts: equatorial low (THERMAL) · subtropical high 30° (DYNAMIC) · sub-polar low 60° (DYNAMIC) · polar high (THERMAL).**
3. ⭐⭐ **The world's great deserts sit at 30° under the DESCENDING limb of the subtropical high** — not where the sun is hottest, but where air is sinking.
4. ⭐ **Three forces: pressure gradient, Coriolis, friction (surface to 1–3 km).**
5. ⭐⭐ **Coriolis is MAXIMUM at the poles and ZERO at the equator** — which is why **no cyclone forms within ~5° of the equator.**
6. ⭐ **GEOSTROPHIC WIND blows PARALLEL to isobars** above the friction layer.
7. ⭐ **Cyclone: anticlockwise NH / clockwise SH, air RISES → cloud and rain. Anticyclone: reverse, air DESCENDS → clear and dry.**
8. ⭐ **Three cells: Hadley (0–30°, top at 14 km) · Ferrel (30–60°) · Polar (60–90°).** ⭐ **Ferrel's Law: right in NH, left in SH.** **Winds are named for where they come FROM.**
9. ⭐ **Walker circulation + Southern Oscillation = ENSO.** ⭐ **El Niño = warm eastern Pacific (+10°C off Peru), "Child Christ", appears every 3–7 years** → weak Indian monsoon, **and collapse of the Peruvian fishery** through loss of upwelling.
10. ⭐ **Local winds: sea/land breeze · valley (anabatic) and mountain (katabatic) winds · Chinook and Foehn** (warm, dry, adiabatically heated on the leeward side).
11. ⭐ **Five air masses: mT, cT, mP, cP, cA.** ⭐ **Four fronts: cold (steep, violent, brief) · warm (gentle, prolonged) · stationary · occluded.**
12. ⭐⭐ **Tropical cyclone conditions: SST >27°C · Coriolis · low vertical wind shear · pre-existing low · upper divergence.** ⭐ **Dissipates on landfall because the moisture supply is cut.**
13. ⭐⭐ **Eye = CALM, clear, subsiding. Eye wall = 250 kmph, torrential rain.** ⭐ **The lull as the eye passes is the most dangerous moment.**
14. ⭐ **Cyclone · Hurricane (Atlantic) · Typhoon (W. Pacific) · Willy-willies (W. Australia).**
15. 🌱 ⭐ **Odisha 1999 (~10,000 deaths) vs Phailin 2013 (<50)** — same hazard, different preparedness. **Disaster mortality is a policy variable.**
