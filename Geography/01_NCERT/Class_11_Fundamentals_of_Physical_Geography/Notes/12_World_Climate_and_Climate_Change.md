## 1️⃣ Classification of Climate — Köppen

**The most widely used classification of climate is the empirical climate classification scheme developed by V. KOEPPEN.** **Koeppen identified a close relationship between the distribution of vegetation and climate.** **He selected certain values of temperature and precipitation and related them to the distribution of vegetation and used these values for classifying the climates.**

**It is an EMPIRICAL classification based on mean annual and mean monthly temperature and precipitation data.**

> **The genuinely clever idea worth understanding.** **Köppen did not start with weather data — he started with VEGETATION MAPS, and then worked backwards to find the temperature and rainfall thresholds that explained the boundaries.**
>
> **Why that works: plants integrate climate over decades.** A single hot year tells you nothing; a forest tells you what the last century has been like. **Vegetation is a climate recording instrument that runs continuously and never needs calibrating.**
>
> **This is the whole reason the classification is called EMPIRICAL** — derived from observation of effects, not from theory about causes. **Contrast with a *genetic* classification, which would classify by air masses and circulation, i.e. by cause.**

### Köppen's letter code — decode rather than memorise

**Koeppen used capital and small letters to designate climatic groups and types.**

```
   FIRST LETTER (capital) = THE GROUP
      A — TROPICAL          — average temp of coldest month 18°C or higher
      B — DRY               — potential evaporation exceeds precipitation
      C — WARM TEMPERATE    — coldest month between 18°C and −3°C
      D — COLD SNOW-FOREST  — coldest month below −3°C
      E — COLD (POLAR)      — warmest month below 10°C

   SECOND LETTER (small) = PRECIPITATION
      f — no dry season          m — monsoon
      w — winter dry season      s — summer dry season
      S — semi-arid/steppe       W — arid/desert

   THIRD LETTER (small) = TEMPERATURE DETAIL
      a — hot summer      b — warm summer
      c — cool summer     h — hot (dry climates)
      k — cold (dry)      F — ice cap    T — tundra
```

### The five groups and their principal types

| Group | Type | Code | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — TROPICAL** | Tropical wet | **Af** | **No dry season.** Equatorial — Amazon, Congo, SE Asia |
| | Tropical monsoon | **Am** | **Monsoon, short dry season** — **INDIA**, SE Asia |
| | Tropical wet and dry | **Aw** | **Winter dry season** — savanna |
| **B — DRY** | Subtropical steppe | **BSh** | **Semi-arid, low latitude** — parts of India |
| | Subtropical desert | **BWh** | **Arid, low latitude** — Sahara, **Thar** |
| | Mid-latitude steppe | **BSk** | Semi-arid, mid-latitude |
| | Mid-latitude desert | **BWk** | Arid, mid-latitude — Gobi |
| **C — WARM TEMPERATE** | Humid subtropical | **Cfa** | No dry season, hot summer |
| | **Mediterranean** | **Cs** | **DRY SUMMER** — Mediterranean, California, Chile, Cape, SW Australia |
| | Marine west coast | **Cfb** | No dry season, warm/cool summer |
| **D — COLD SNOW-FOREST** | Humid continental | **Df** | No dry season, severe winter |
| | Subarctic | **Dw** | Winter dry, very severe winter |
| **E — COLD (POLAR)** | **Tundra** | **ET** | **No true summer** |
| | **Ice cap** | **EF** | **Perennial ice** |
| **H — HIGHLAND** | Highland | **H** | **Cold due to elevation** — Himalayas, Andes |

**India falls chiefly in the Am (tropical monsoon), Aw (tropical wet and dry), BSh (semi-arid), BWh (desert) and H (highland) types.**

> **The codes to be certain about: Af = equatorial rainforest · Am = MONSOON (India) · Aw = savanna · BWh = hot desert (Thar, Sahara) · Cs = MEDITERRANEAN (dry summer) · ET = tundra · EF = ice cap.** **These seven cover most of what gets asked.**

> **Note the 'H' group is an addition, not one of Köppen's original five.** Highland climates are classified separately because **altitude, not latitude, is doing the work** — a mountain in the tropics can have a polar climate at its summit (Cl.7 Ch.6's vertical zonation).

## 2️⃣ Climate Change — The Evidence

**Climate in the past has never been stable.** **There is evidence for large-scale climate change in the past.** **The geological records show alternating periods of glacial and inter-glacial periods.**

### How we know climate has changed

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Glacial deposits and landforms** | **Tillite, moraines, U-shaped valleys far from present glaciers** show ice once extended much further |
| **Fossil records** | **Plant and animal fossils of tropical species found in polar regions**, and vice versa |
| **Tree rings (dendrochronology)** | **The width of annual growth rings records the growing conditions of each year** |
| **Ice cores** | **Air bubbles trapped in polar ice preserve samples of the ancient atmosphere**, including its CO₂ content |
| **Pollen analysis** | **Pollen preserved in lake and bog sediments records what vegetation grew there** |
| **Historical records** | Chronicles of harvests, floods, freezes and famines |

> **Ice cores deserve a moment because they are the decisive evidence, and knowing why elevates a climate answer.**
>
> **When snow falls on an ice sheet it traps tiny bubbles of the air of that moment.** Layer upon layer, the ice preserves those bubbles for hundreds of thousands of years. **Drilling a core and analysing the bubbles gives a DIRECT measurement of the past atmosphere — not an inference.**
>
> **What they show:** for the last ~800,000 years, **atmospheric CO₂ oscillated between roughly 180 and 300 ppm** across the glacial cycles. **It is now above 420 ppm** — far outside the entire range of that record, and it rose there in about two centuries rather than over millennia.
>
> 🎯 **This is why "the climate has always changed" is true but beside the point.** **It is not the fact of change that is anomalous; it is the RATE and the CAUSE.** **That single distinction is the strongest sentence you can put in a climate answer.**

### Historical climate episodes

**The most recent glacial period ended about 10,000 years ago.** **Since then the earth has been in an inter-glacial period.**

**Notable episodes:** **the MEDIEVAL WARM PERIOD (about 1000–1300 AD)** and **the LITTLE ICE AGE (about 1550–1850 AD)**, followed by warming from about 1850 onward.

**The 1990s recorded the warmest decade and 1998 the warmest year.**

## 3️⃣ Causes of Climate Change

**The causes for climate change are many.** **They can be grouped into ASTRONOMICAL and TERRESTRIAL causes.**

### Astronomical causes

**The astronomical causes are the changes in solar output associated with SUNSPOT activities.**

**Sunspots are dark and cooler patches on the sun which increase and decrease in a cyclical manner.** **According to some meteorologists, when the number of sunspots increase, cooler and wetter weather and greater storminess occur.** **A decrease in sunspot numbers is associated with warm and drier conditions.** ⚠️ **Yet, these findings are not statistically significant.**

**Another astronomical theory is MILANKOVITCH OSCILLATIONS**, which infer cycles in the variations in the earth's orbital characteristics around the sun, the wobbling of the earth and the changes in the earth's axial tilt.

**All these alter the amount of insolation received from the sun, which in turn, might have a bearing on the climate.**

> **Milankovitch cycles are three separate orbital variations — worth naming:** **ECCENTRICITY** (the shape of Earth's orbit, ~100,000-year cycle), **OBLIQUITY** (the axial tilt varying between ~22.1° and 24.5°, ~41,000-year cycle), and **PRECESSION** (the wobble of the axis, ~26,000-year cycle).
>
> **Together they explain the timing of the ice ages** — and that is a genuine scientific triumph. ⚠️ **But they operate on tens of thousands of years. They cannot explain a change occurring over two centuries.** **Stating that explicitly pre-empts the commonest counter-argument in a climate answer.**

### Terrestrial causes

**VOLCANISM is considered as another cause for climate change.** **Volcanic eruption throws up lots of aerosols into the atmosphere.** **These aerosols remain in the atmosphere for a considerable period of time reducing the sun's radiation reaching the earth's surface.**

**After the recent Pinatubo and El Cion volcanic eruptions, the average temperature of the earth fell to some extent for some years.**

**The most important anthropogenic effect on the climate is the increasing trend in the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere which is likely to cause global warming.**

> 🌋 **The volcanic point is worth carrying because it demonstrates the mechanism in reverse.** **Mount Pinatubo (1991) injected sulphate aerosols into the stratosphere and cooled global average temperature by roughly 0.5°C for about two years.** **A single eruption measurably cooled the whole planet — which proves the atmosphere's radiative balance is genuinely sensitive to what we put into it.** **It is also the basis of proposals for "solar radiation management" geoengineering, which is a live and contested GS-III topic.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Global Warming

**Some gases in the atmosphere allow the incoming shortwave solar radiation to pass through, but absorb the outgoing longwave terrestrial radiation. These gases are known as GREENHOUSE GASES and the phenomenon is called the GREENHOUSE EFFECT.**

**Presence of greenhouse gases keeps the earth's surface warm.** **Without the greenhouse effect the earth's temperature would be much lower and life would not exist.**

### The greenhouse gases

| Gas | Contribution / detail |
|---|---|
| **CARBON DIOXIDE (CO₂)** | **The most important greenhouse gas.** **It contributes about 60% of global warming.** **Concentration has increased from ~280 ppm (pre-industrial) to well over 350 ppm and rising.** **Sources: burning of fossil fuels, deforestation** |
| **CHLOROFLUOROCARBONS (CFCs)** | **Greenhouse gases produced by human activity.** **They also destroy the ozone layer.** **Used in refrigeration, air conditioning, aerosol sprays, foam.** **The Montreal Protocol addresses them** |
| **METHANE (CH₄)** | **Also known as marsh gas.** **Produced from paddy fields, cattle, decay of organic matter, wetlands.** **Its concentration has more than doubled** |
| **NITROUS OXIDE (N₂O)** | **From fertilisers, biomass burning, industrial processes** |
| **Water vapour** | **The most abundant greenhouse gas, though its concentration is controlled by temperature rather than directly by emissions** |

**Increase in the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere may lead to GLOBAL WARMING.**

### Consequences

**An increase in the average temperature of the earth would cause:**
- **Melting of polar ice caps and glaciers, raising sea level**
- **Submergence of low-lying coastal areas and islands**
- **Changes in the pattern of precipitation — some regions wetter, some drier**
- **Increased frequency and intensity of extreme events — droughts, floods, cyclones**
- **Shifts in agricultural zones and crop yields**
- **Loss of biodiversity as species cannot migrate fast enough**
- **Spread of tropical diseases to higher latitudes**

> **The distinction that must be right, repeated from Cl.6 Ch.1 because it is the most common error in environment answers:**
>
> **The GREENHOUSE EFFECT is NATURAL AND ESSENTIAL.** **Without it, Earth's average temperature would be about −18°C instead of ~15°C** — a frozen planet with no liquid water and no life.
>
> **The problem is the ENHANCED greenhouse effect** — the human-driven intensification of a mechanism that was always there.
>
> **Writing "greenhouse effect = bad" costs marks. Writing "the greenhouse effect is essential; its anthropogenic enhancement is the problem" gains them.**

> **And keep OZONE DEPLETION separate from GLOBAL WARMING — the other standing error:**
>
> | | **Ozone depletion** | **Global warming** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Gas** | CFCs | CO₂, methane |
> | **Layer** | **Stratosphere** | **Troposphere** |
> | **Effect** | More UV reaching the surface | Heat trapped, temperature rises |
> | **Treaty** | **Montreal Protocol, 1987** ✅ working | **Paris Agreement, 2015** 🟡 struggling |
>
> **They overlap only in that CFCs happen to be greenhouse gases too.**

### 🌱 The current framework — for GS-III and current affairs

| Instrument | Detail |
|---|---|
| **IPCC** | Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change — the scientific assessment body |
| **UNFCCC, 1992** | The parent convention (from the **Rio Earth Summit**) |
| **Kyoto Protocol, 1997** | Binding targets for developed countries only |
| **Paris Agreement, 2015** | **Limit warming to well below 2°C, pursuing 1.5°C.** Based on **Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)** |
| **CBDR** | **Common But Differentiated Responsibilities** — the principle that all countries share responsibility but not equally, given historical emissions |
| **India's commitments** | **Net zero by 2070; 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; International Solar Alliance (HQ Gurugram)** |

> 🎯 **CBDR is the principle India argues from, and understanding it properly is worth marks.** **The argument: the atmosphere's capacity to absorb carbon is a finite global commons, and developed countries have already used most of it during their industrialisation.** **India's per-capita emissions remain far below the world average.** **So equal present-day obligations would lock in historical inequity.** **This is an argument about equity and historical responsibility, not a refusal to act** — and framing it that way is far stronger than either uncritical alarmism or uncritical defensiveness.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Köppen's classification is EMPIRICAL — he read climate off VEGETATION maps and worked backwards to the thresholds.** Plants integrate climate over decades.
2. **Five groups: A (tropical, coldest month ≥18°C) · B (dry) · C (warm temperate, 18 to −3°C) · D (cold snow-forest, below −3°C) · E (polar, warmest below 10°C), plus H (highland).**
3. **Decode: first letter = group; second = precipitation (f/m/w/s/S/W); third = temperature detail.**
4. **Key codes: Af rainforest · Am MONSOON (India) · Aw savanna · BWh hot desert · Cs MEDITERRANEAN · ET tundra · EF ice cap.**
5. **Evidence of past change: glacial deposits, fossils, tree rings, ICE CORES, pollen, historical records.**
6. **Ice cores show CO₂ stayed between 180 and 300 ppm for 800,000 years. It is now above 420.** **The anomaly is the RATE and the CAUSE, not the fact of change.**
7. **Medieval Warm Period (~1000–1300) and Little Ice Age (~1550–1850); warming since ~1850. The 1990s were the warmest decade recorded at the time of writing.**
8. **Astronomical causes: sunspots (NCERT: "not statistically significant") and MILANKOVITCH oscillations** — eccentricity, obliquity, precession. **These explain ice-age timing but operate over tens of thousands of years.**
9. **Terrestrial causes: volcanism (aerosols COOL — Pinatubo, El Chichón) and anthropogenic greenhouse gases.**
10. **CO₂ contributes ~60% of global warming; 280 ppm pre-industrial. Methane (marsh gas) from paddy, cattle and wetlands has more than doubled. CFCs also destroy ozone.**
11. **The greenhouse effect is NATURAL AND ESSENTIAL — without it Earth would be ~−18°C. The problem is the ENHANCED effect.**
12. **Ozone depletion ≠ global warming** — different gas, layer, effect and treaty (Montreal 1987 vs Paris 2015).
13. 🌱 **Paris 2015: well below 2°C, pursuing 1.5°C, via NDCs, under CBDR.** **India: net zero by 2070, 500 GW non-fossil by 2030, ISA headquartered in Gurugram.**
