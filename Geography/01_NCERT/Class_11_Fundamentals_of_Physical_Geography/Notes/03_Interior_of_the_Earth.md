# Chapter 3 — Interior of the Earth

> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **Nobody has ever been inside the Earth — the deepest hole ever drilled reached about 12 km out of 6,371 km.** Everything we know about the interior comes from **earthquake waves passing through it and being bent, blocked and reflected.** The earthquake is the X-ray, and the shadow it casts is the photograph.

🔴 **HIGH PRIORITY — read carefully, ~1.5 hrs.** Seismic waves and the shadow zones are among the most reliably asked topics in Prelims.

## 1️⃣ ⭐ Sources of Information

### Direct sources

| Source | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Surface rock / mining** | Rocks from mining areas. ⭐ **Gold mines in South Africa are as deep as 3–4 km** |
| ⭐ **Deep ocean drilling / projects** | ⭐ **The "Deep Ocean Drilling Project" and the "Integrated Ocean Drilling Project"** ⭐ **The deepest drill at Kola, in the Arctic Ocean, has so far reached a depth of 12 km** |
| ⭐ **Volcanic eruption** | ⭐ **Magma from the interior reaches the surface — though it is not possible to determine the depth of the source** |

> ⭐ **Note the scale problem, because it's the point of the whole chapter.** The Earth's radius is **6,371 km**. The deepest hole ever drilled is **12 km** — about **0.2%** of the way. **Direct observation is effectively impossible.** Everything below that is inferred.

### ⭐ Indirect sources

| Source | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Temperature, pressure and density** | Rate of change with depth |
| ⭐ **Meteors** | ⭐ **Meteors that reach the earth are solid bodies developed out of materials from the same type as our planet — their composition is comparable** |
| ⭐ **Gravitation** | ⭐ **Gravity is greater near the poles and less at the equator.** ⭐ **Gravity anomaly** — the difference between actual and expected gravity — gives information about the distribution of mass in the crust |
| ⭐ **Magnetic field** | Magnetic surveys give information about the distribution of magnetic materials |
| ⭐⭐ **SEISMIC ACTIVITY** | ⭐ **One of the most important sources of information about the interior** |

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Earthquakes and Seismic Waves

> ⭐ **Earthquake** = the release of energy along a fault, generating waves that travel in all directions.

### ⭐ The vocabulary

| Term | ⭐ Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Focus / Hypocentre** | ⭐ **The point where the energy is released — inside the Earth** |
| ⭐ **Epicentre** | ⭐ **The point on the surface, nearest to the focus** — where waves arrive **first** and damage is greatest |
| ⭐ **Fault** | The fracture along which movement occurs |
| ⭐ **Seismograph** | The instrument recording the waves |

### ⭐⭐ The three wave types — learn every row

| Property | ⭐ **P-WAVES (Primary)** | ⭐ **S-WAVES (Secondary)** | ⭐ **L-WAVES (Surface)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Speed** | ⭐ **Fastest — arrive FIRST** | Slower — arrive second | ⭐ **Slowest — arrive LAST** |
| ⭐ **Also called** | Longitudinal / compressional | Transverse | Surface waves |
| ⭐ **Particle motion** | ⭐ **Parallel to wave direction** — push-pull | ⭐ **Perpendicular (at right angles) to wave direction** | Along the surface |
| ⭐ **Travels through** | ⭐ **SOLIDS, LIQUIDS AND GASES** | ⭐ **SOLIDS ONLY** | Surface only |
| ⭐ **Analogy** | Sound waves | Light/water ripples | — |
| ⭐ **Destructiveness** | Least | Moderate | ⭐ **MOST DESTRUCTIVE** |
| ⭐ **Density effect** | ⭐ Velocity **increases** with density of material | Same | — |

⭐ **Body waves** = P and S waves — they travel **through the body/interior** of the Earth. ⭐ **Surface waves** = generated when body waves reach the surface; **they are the last to report on the seismograph and are the most damaging.**

> ⭐ **Why P-waves outrun S-waves** — the mechanism, worth understanding rather than memorising. **P-waves compress and stretch the material along the direction of travel** (like a push down a slinky). **S-waves shake it side to side.** Compression travels faster through matter than shear does — and crucially, **a liquid has no shear strength at all**, so it simply cannot transmit an S-wave. **That single fact is what lets us see inside the planet.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Shadow Zones — The Key Idea in This Chapter

⭐ **Earthquake waves get recorded in seismographs located at far-off locations. However, there exist some specific areas where the waves are not reported. Such a zone is called the SHADOW ZONE.**

```
   ⭐⭐ THE SHADOW ZONES — HOW WE KNOW THE OUTER CORE IS LIQUID

                    ★ EARTHQUAKE
                    │
          ╱─────────┴─────────╲
        ╱   P & S both arrive   ╲
      ╱                           ╲
    ╱      ╱▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔╲           ╲
   │     ╱   OUTER CORE  ╲          │
   │    │    (LIQUID)     │         │   ⭐ S-WAVES CANNOT
   │    │  ╱▔▔▔▔▔╲        │         │      PASS THROUGH
   │    │ │ INNER │       │         │      → shadow beyond 103°
   │     ╲│ CORE  │      ╱          │
    ╲     ╲╲_____╱     ╱           ╱
      ╲      ╲______╱            ╱
        ╲                      ╱
          ╲──────────────────╱

   ⭐ 105° ←──── P-WAVE SHADOW ZONE ────→ 145°
      (a BAND — P-waves are REFRACTED/bent by the liquid core)

   ⭐ Beyond 103° ─── S-WAVE SHADOW ZONE ───
      (a whole HEMISPHERE — S-waves are BLOCKED entirely)
```

### ⭐⭐ The exact figures — high-frequency Prelims content

| Wave | ⭐ Shadow zone | ⭐ What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **S-WAVES** | ⭐ **Beyond 103° from the epicentre — covering a little over 40% of the Earth's surface** | ⭐ **The outer core is LIQUID** — S-waves cannot pass through liquid |
| ⭐ **P-WAVES** | ⭐ **Between 105° and 145° from the epicentre — a BAND, not a whole zone** | ⭐ **P-waves are REFRACTED (bent) on entering the liquid outer core**, so they emerge elsewhere |

⭐ **The entire zone beyond 103° does not receive S-waves. The shadow zone of P-waves appears as a band around the earth between 105° and 145° away from the epicentre.**

⭐ **The shadow zone of S-waves is much larger than that of P-waves. The shadow zone of P-waves appears as a band around the earth between 105° and 145° away from the epicentre. However, the shadow zone of S-waves is not only larger in extent but it is also a little over 40% of the earth's surface.**

> ⭐⭐ **This is the single most elegant piece of reasoning in physical geography, and it deserves a moment.**
>
> Nobody has been below 12 km. Yet we know with confidence that the outer core is liquid and the inner core is solid. **How?**
>
> 1. **S-waves vanish beyond 103°.** S-waves cannot cross liquid. Therefore **something liquid lies in their path** → the outer core.
> 2. **P-waves disappear between 105° and 145°, then reappear.** They weren't blocked — they were **bent**. Waves bend when they change medium. Therefore there is a **sharp boundary** at a specific depth → the core-mantle boundary.
> 3. **P-waves speed up again in the innermost region** → the inner core is **solid**.
>
> ⭐ **Three deductions about the deep interior of a planet, made entirely from where waves failed to arrive.** The absence of data *was* the data.
>
> 🎯 **This makes an excellent illustration in an essay about scientific method, indirect evidence, or how we know what we know.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Types of Earthquakes

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **TECTONIC** | ⭐ **The most common. Generated due to sliding of rocks along a fault plane** |
| ⭐ **VOLCANIC** | ⭐ **A special class of tectonic earthquake, confined to areas of active volcanoes** |
| ⭐ **COLLAPSE** | ⭐ **In areas of intense mining activity, the roofs of underground mines collapse, causing minor tremors** |
| ⭐ **EXPLOSION** | ⭐ **Caused by the explosion of chemical or nuclear devices** |
| ⭐ **RESERVOIR-INDUCED** | ⭐ **Occur in areas of large reservoirs** |

> ⭐ **Reservoir-induced seismicity connects directly to Cl.10 Ch.3's dam debate.** The weight of impounded water and its seepage into rock fractures can trigger earthquakes. **Koyna (Maharashtra, 1967) is the standard Indian example** — and it is a substantive argument in any answer on large dams.

### ⭐ Measuring earthquakes

| Scale | ⭐ Measures |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **RICHTER scale** | ⭐ **MAGNITUDE — the energy released.** Expressed in **absolute numbers, 0 to 10** |
| ⭐ **MERCALLI scale** | ⭐ **INTENSITY — the visible damage caused.** Range **1 to 12** |

### 🌱 Effects of earthquakes

⭐ **Ground shaking · differential ground settlement · land slides · soil liquefaction · ground lurching · avalanches · ground displacement · floods from dam and levee failures · fires · structural collapse · falling objects · tsunami.**

> ⭐ **Soil liquefaction is worth knowing by name.** In water-saturated loose soil, shaking causes the soil to **temporarily behave like a liquid** — buildings sink or tilt even without structural failure. **This is why building codes care about soil type, not just structure**, and why the Kutch (2001) and Bihar earthquakes caused the damage patterns they did.

> 🌱 **The disaster-management line worth repeating from Cl.7 Ch.3:** **earthquakes do not kill people; buildings do.** The policy answer is **enforcement of seismic building codes**, not prediction — prediction remains scientifically unreliable. India's **Zones II–V** (V highest risk: entire Northeast, parts of J&K/Himachal, Rann of Kutch, Andamans) and the **NDMA** are the institutional facts to cite.

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ Structure of the Earth

```
   ⭐ THE LAYERS, WITH DEPTHS AND DISCONTINUITIES

   ─────────────────────────────────────────  0 km
   ⭐ CRUST
      Oceanic: ~5 km · Continental: ~30 km
      Under mountains: up to 70 km
      Oceanic crust = BASALT (denser, 3.0 g/cm³)
      Continental crust = GRANITE (lighter)
   ═══ ⭐ MOHOROVICIC (MOHO) DISCONTINUITY ═══
   ⭐ MANTLE
      Upper mantle: ⭐ ASTHENOSPHERE (up to 400 km)
        — ⭐ the main source of MAGMA
        — ⭐ density 3.4 g/cm³
      ⭐ LITHOSPHERE = crust + uppermost solid mantle
        — ⭐ 10–200 km thick
      Extends to 2,900 km
   ═══ ⭐ GUTENBERG DISCONTINUITY ═══
   ⭐ CORE
      ⭐ OUTER CORE: LIQUID (2,900–5,150 km)
   ═══ ⭐ LEHMANN DISCONTINUITY ═══
      ⭐ INNER CORE: SOLID (5,150–6,371 km)
      ⭐ Made of NIFE (nickel + ferrous/iron)
      ⭐ Density 5.5 to 13 g/cm³
   ─────────────────────────────────────────  6,371 km
```

| Layer | ⭐ Key facts |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **CRUST** | ⭐ **The outermost solid part; brittle in nature.** ⭐ **~1% of the Earth's volume, 0.5% of its mass.** ⭐ **Thickness varies: ~5 km under oceanic crust, ~30 km under continental.** ⭐ **In major mountain ranges it is much thicker — under the Himalayas about 70 km.** ⭐ **Made of heavier rocks with density 3 g/cm³ (basalt) in oceanic crust; continental crust is made of lighter silicates (granite)** |
| ⭐ **MANTLE** | ⭐ **Extends from just below the Moho to 2,900 km depth.** ⭐ **The upper portion is called the ASTHENOSPHERE, extending up to 400 km.** ⭐ **It is the main source of magma that finds its way to the surface during volcanic eruptions.** ⭐ **Density 3.4 g/cm³.** ⭐ **The mantle is ~84% of the Earth's volume** |
| ⭐ **LITHOSPHERE** | ⭐ **The crust and the uppermost part of the mantle together.** ⭐ **Thickness 10–200 km** |
| ⭐ **CORE** | ⭐ **The core-mantle boundary is at 2,900 km.** ⭐ **The outer core is in LIQUID state while the inner core is SOLID.** ⭐ **Density ranges from 5.5 to 13 g/cm³.** ⭐ **Made up of very heavy material mostly constituted by nickel and iron — often referred to as the NIFE layer** |

⭐ **The discontinuities — a favourite Prelims item:**

| ⭐ Discontinuity | ⭐ Between |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **MOHOROVICIC (Moho)** | **Crust and mantle** |
| ⭐ **GUTENBERG** | **Mantle and outer core** |
| ⭐ **LEHMANN** | **Outer core and inner core** |

> ⭐ **Note the crucial distinction between LITHOSPHERE and CRUST** — regularly confused. ⭐ **The crust is the outermost chemical layer. The lithosphere is a MECHANICAL layer — the crust PLUS the rigid uppermost mantle, behaving as one brittle slab.** **It is the lithosphere, not the crust, that is broken into tectonic plates** (Ch.4). Below it lies the **asthenosphere**, which is plastic enough to flow — which is what allows the plates to move at all.

## 6️⃣ ⭐ Volcanoes and Volcanic Landforms

> ⭐ **A volcano is a place where gases, ashes and/or molten rock material — lava — escape to the ground.**

### ⭐ Types of volcano

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **SHIELD VOLCANOES** | ⭐ **The largest of all volcanoes on the earth (other than flood basalt).** ⭐ **The Hawaiian volcanoes are the most famous examples.** ⭐ **Made up of basalt — very fluid when erupted.** ⭐ **Not steep. They become explosive if somehow water gets into the vent** |
| ⭐ **COMPOSITE VOLCANOES** | ⭐ **Characterised by eruptions of cooler and more viscous lavas than basalt.** ⭐ **Result in explosive eruptions.** ⭐ **Along with lava, large quantities of pyroclastic material and ashes find their way to the ground — building up layers** |
| ⭐ **CALDERA** | ⭐ **The most explosive of the earth's volcanoes.** ⭐ **They are usually so explosive that when they erupt they tend to COLLAPSE ON THEMSELVES rather than building any tall structure.** ⭐ **The collapsed depressions are called calderas** |
| ⭐ **FLOOD BASALT PROVINCES** | ⭐ **Highly fluid lava that flows for long distances.** ⭐ **Some parts of the world are covered by thousands of sq km of thick basalt lava flows.** ⭐ **The DECCAN TRAPS from India, now covering most of the Maharashtra plateau, are a much larger flood basalt province** |
| ⭐ **MID-OCEAN RIDGE VOLCANOES** | ⭐ **Occur in the oceanic areas. There is a system of mid-ocean ridges more than 70,000 km long that stretches through all the ocean basins.** ⭐ **The central portion of this ridge experiences frequent eruptions** |

### ⭐ Intrusive volcanic landforms

| Landform | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **BATHOLITHS** | ⭐ **A large body of magmatic material that cools in the deeper depth of the crust.** ⭐ **They appear on the surface only after the denudational processes remove the overlying materials.** ⭐ **They are granitic bodies and are the cooled portion of magma chambers** |
| ⭐ **LACCOLITHS** | ⭐ **Large dome-shaped intrusive bodies with a level base and connected by a pipe-like conduit from below.** ⭐ **They resemble the surface volcanic domes of composite volcano, only these are located at deeper depths** |
| ⭐ **LAPOLITH** | ⭐ **As the lava moves upwards, a portion may tend to move in a horizontal direction wherever it finds a weak plane. If it develops into a SAUCER SHAPE, concave to the sky, it is called a lapolith** |
| ⭐ **PHACOLITH** | ⭐ **A wavy mass of intrusive rocks, at times, is found at the base of synclines or at the top of an anticline.** ⭐ **These are certain lava which have a definite conduit to source below** |
| ⭐ **SILLS** | ⭐ **The near horizontal bodies of the intrusive igneous rocks.** ⭐ **The thinner ones are called SHEETS; the thick horizontal deposits are called sills** |
| ⭐ **DYKES** | ⭐ **When the lava makes its way through cracks and the fissures developed in the land, it solidifies almost perpendicular to the ground.** ⭐ **It gets cooled in the same position to develop a wall-like structure.** ⭐ **Such structures are called dykes.** ⭐ **These are the most commonly found intrusive forms in the western Maharashtra area. They are considered the feeders for the eruptions that led to the Deccan Traps** |

> ⭐ **Sill vs dyke is the pairing that gets asked: SILL = horizontal, DYKE = vertical.** Memory hook: a **d**yke stands up like a **d**oor; a **s**ill lies flat like a window **s**ill.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **The deepest drill (Kola) reached 12 km of a 6,371 km radius** — everything deeper is **inferred**, mainly from seismic waves.
2. ⭐ **P-waves: fastest, longitudinal, travel through solids, liquids and gases.** ⭐ **S-waves: transverse, SOLIDS ONLY.** ⭐ **L-waves: slowest, most destructive.**
3. ⭐⭐ **S-wave shadow zone: beyond 103°, over 40% of the Earth's surface → proves the outer core is LIQUID.**
4. ⭐⭐ **P-wave shadow zone: a BAND from 105° to 145° → caused by refraction at the core boundary.**
5. ⭐ **The absence of data was the data** — three deductions about the deep interior made purely from where waves failed to arrive.
6. ⭐ **Discontinuities: Moho (crust/mantle) · Gutenberg (mantle/outer core) · Lehmann (outer/inner core).**
7. ⭐ **Crust: ~5 km oceanic (basalt), ~30 km continental (granite), ~70 km under the Himalayas.** ~1% of volume.
8. ⭐ **Asthenosphere: upper mantle to 400 km, main source of magma.** ⭐ **Lithosphere = crust + rigid upper mantle (10–200 km) — and it is the lithosphere, not the crust, that breaks into plates.**
9. ⭐ **Core-mantle boundary at 2,900 km; outer core liquid, inner core solid; NIFE; density 5.5–13.**
10. ⭐ **Five earthquake types: tectonic, volcanic, collapse, explosion, reservoir-induced** (Koyna 1967).
11. ⭐ **Richter = magnitude (0–10, energy). Mercalli = intensity (1–12, damage).**
12. ⭐ **Volcanoes: shield (Hawaii, largest, basalt) · composite (viscous, explosive) · caldera (most explosive, collapses inward) · flood basalt (Deccan Traps) · mid-ocean ridge (70,000 km system).**
13. ⭐ **Intrusive forms: batholith, laccolith, lapolith, phacolith, sill (horizontal), dyke (vertical).** **Dykes in western Maharashtra fed the Deccan Traps.**
