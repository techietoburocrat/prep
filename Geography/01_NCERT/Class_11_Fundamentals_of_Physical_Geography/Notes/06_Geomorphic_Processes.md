# Chapter 6 — Geomorphic Processes 🌱

> **The one-line point:** **Weathering breaks rock in place; erosion carries it away; mass movement lets gravity do the carrying.** Endogenic forces build the land up and create differences in elevation; exogenic forces wear it down and try to level everything. **All exogenic processes are ultimately driven by one thing: gradients created by the endogenic forces.**

## 1️⃣ The Two Families of Process

> **GEOMORPHIC PROCESSES: the endogenic and exogenic forces causing physical stresses and chemical actions on earth materials and bringing about changes in the configuration of the surface of the earth.**

> **A GEOMORPHIC AGENT is a mobile medium (like running water, moving ice masses, wind, waves and currents) which removes, transports and deposits earth materials.**

⚠️ **A process is a force applied on earth materials affecting the same. An agent is a mobile medium which removes, transports and deposits earth materials.** **Running water, groundwater, glaciers, wind, waves and currents can be called geomorphic agents.**

| | **ENDOGENIC** | **EXOGENIC** |
|---|---|---|
| **Energy source** | **Earth's INTERNAL heat** — radioactivity, rotational and tidal friction, primordial heat from Earth's origin | **SOLAR energy** (plus gravity) |
| **Effect** | **BUILD UP** — create relief, elevation differences | **WEAR DOWN** — level, degrade |
| **Processes** | Diastrophism, volcanism | Weathering, mass wasting, erosion, deposition |
| **Also called** | Constructive | **DENUDATION** (collectively) |

> **The crucial causal link, which UPSC likes:** **All exogenic processes are covered under a general term, DENUDATION.** **The word "denude" means to strip off or to uncover.**
>
> **Weathering, mass wasting, erosion and transportation are included in denudation.** And critically: **the endogenic forces continuously elevate parts of the earth's surface and hence the exogenic processes fail to even out the relief variations of the surface of the earth.**
>
> **Without endogenic uplift, exogenic forces would have flattened the entire planet to a featureless plain long ago.** The Earth has mountains only because **building outpaces wearing.** That is the single sentence that unifies the chapter.

**The energy for exogenic processes: the ultimate driving force behind all exogenic processes is derived from SOLAR ENERGY.** **It is through the atmospheric processes that we derive energy from solar radiation. Differences in solar energy received in different latitudes cause differences in temperature, which in turn cause pressure differences. Pressure differences cause winds — and wind and precipitation are the chief geomorphic agents.**

**The variations in temperature and precipitation are responsible for varying geomorphic processes.** **All the exogenic geomorphic processes are covered under a general term, DENUDATION.**

**GRAVITY, along with the gradient, is the main force behind all exogenic processes.**

### Endogenic processes

| Process | Detail |
|---|---|
| **DIASTROPHISM** | **All processes that move, elevate or build up portions of the earth's crust.** Includes: **(i) OROGENIC processes — mountain building through severe folding and affecting long and narrow belts of the earth's crust; (ii) EPEIROGENIC processes — involving uplift or warping of large parts of the earth's crust; (iii) EARTHQUAKES — involving local relatively minor movements; (iv) PLATE TECTONICS — involving horizontal movements of crustal plates** |
| **VOLCANISM** | **The movement of molten rock onto or toward the earth's surface and also formation of many intrusive and extrusive volcanic forms** |

> **Orogenic vs epeirogenic is a standing distinction:** **OROGENIC = mountain building, HORIZONTAL forces, narrow belts, severe folding.** **EPEIROGENIC = continent building, VERTICAL forces, large areas, simple uplift or warping.** Memory: *oro* = mountain (as in orography); *epeiro* = mainland/continent.

## 2️⃣ Weathering

> **WEATHERING is the action of elements of weather and climate over earth materials.** **There are a number of processes within weathering which act either individually or together to affect the earth materials in order to reduce them to fragmental state.**

⚠️ **Weathering is defined as MECHANICAL DISINTEGRATION and CHEMICAL DECOMPOSITION of rocks through the elements of weather.** **Weathering processes are conditioned by many complex geological, climatic, topographic and vegetative factors.** **Climate is of particular importance.**

⚠️ **Weathering processes are site or place specific — they are IN-SITU or ON-SITE processes.** **No transport is involved.**

### The three weathering families

```
   WEATHERING
        │
        ├── CHEMICAL WEATHERING (decomposition)
        │      • Solution
        │      • Carbonation
        │      • Hydration
        │      • Oxidation and Reduction
        │
        ├── PHYSICAL / MECHANICAL WEATHERING (disintegration)
        │      • Unloading and expansion
        │      • Temperature changes and expansion
        │      • Freezing, thawing and frost wedging
        │      • Salt weathering
        │
        └── BIOLOGICAL WEATHERING
               • Plants, animals, humans, microbes
```

### Chemical weathering processes

| Process | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SOLUTION** | **When something is dissolved in water or acids, the water or acid with dissolved contents is called solution.** **Minerals like nitrates, sulphates and potassium are affected.** **Rock salt and gypsum can be dissolved by water and removed in solution** |
| **CARBONATION** | **The reaction of carbonate and bicarbonate with minerals.** **Carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and soil air is absorbed by water to form carbonic acid, which acts as a weak acid.** **Calcium carbonates and magnesium carbonates are dissolved in carbonic acid and are removed in a solution without leaving any residue — resulting in CAVE FORMATION** |
| **HYDRATION** | **The chemical addition of water.** **Minerals take up water and expand — this expansion causes an increase in the volume of the material itself or rock.** **Calcium sulphate takes in water and turns to gypsum.** **This process is reversible, and long continued repetition causes fatigue in the rocks and may lead to their disintegration** |
| **OXIDATION and REDUCTION** | **Oxidation means a combination of a mineral with oxygen to form oxides or hydroxides.** **Oxidation occurs where there is ready access to the atmosphere and oxygenated waters.** **The minerals most commonly involved are iron, manganese, sulphur etc.** **When oxidised minerals are placed in an environment where oxygen is absent, REDUCTION takes place** — such conditions exist usually below the water table, in areas of stagnant water and waterlogged ground. **Red colour of iron upon reduction turns to greenish or bluish grey** |

> **Carbonation is the process behind the entire karst landscape** you'll meet in Chapter 7 — caves, sinkholes, stalactites. **CO₂ dissolves in rainwater → weak carbonic acid → dissolves limestone → caves.** 🌱 **And it is why rising atmospheric CO₂ acidifies rain and ocean water — the same chemistry at planetary scale.**

### Physical weathering processes

| Process | Detail |
|---|---|
| **UNLOADING and EXPANSION** | **Removal of overlying rock load because of continued erosion causes vertical pressure release** — the underlying rocks expand upward, developing **fractures parallel to the surface**. **In due course, sheets of rock separate from the parent body along the fractures and break loose.** **Large, smooth rounded domes called EXFOLIATION DOMES result from this process** |
| **TEMPERATURE CHANGES and EXPANSION** | **Various minerals in rocks possess their own limits of expansion and contraction.** **With rise in temperature, every mineral expands and pushes against its neighbour; as temperature falls, a corresponding contraction takes place.** **Because of diurnal changes in the temperatures, this internal movement among the mineral grains of the superficial layers of rocks takes place regularly.** **This process is most effective in dry climates and high elevations where diurnal temperature changes are drastic.** **In rocks like granites, smooth surfaced and rounded small to big boulders called TORS form due to such exfoliation** |
| **FREEZING, THAWING and FROST WEDGING** | **Frost weathering occurs due to growth of ice within pores and cracks of rocks during repeated cycles of freezing and melting.** **This process is most effective at high elevations in mid-latitudes where freezing and melting is often repeated.** ⚠️ **Glacial areas are subject to frost wedging daily.** **In this process, the rate of freezing is important. Rapid freezing of water causes its sudden expansion and high pressure** |
| **SALT WEATHERING** | **Salts in rocks expand due to thermal action, hydration and crystallisation.** **Many salts like calcium, sodium, magnesium, potassium and barium have a tendency to expand.** **Salt crystals in near-surface pores cause splitting of individual grains within rocks, which eventually fall off — this process of falling off of individual grains may result in granular disintegration or granular foliation.** **Salt weathering is most effective in areas of arid climate and coastal areas** |

> **EXFOLIATION deserves special attention — it is asked often and the mechanism is genuinely interesting.**
>
> **Exfoliation is a result of the differential stresses within rock, not weathering as such.** **It is the result of unloading, temperature changes, or crystal growth — a sheet-like peeling away of the outer layers.**
>
> 🧅 **The comparison that fits: an onion shedding its outer layers.** Successive curved shells peel off, leaving a smooth rounded core.
>
> **Two distinct causes produce it:**
> 1. **UNLOADING** — remove kilometres of overlying rock and the rock beneath, no longer compressed, **expands upward and cracks parallel to the surface.** Gives **EXFOLIATION DOMES**.
> 2. **TEMPERATURE CHANGE** — the outer skin heats and cools faster than the interior, so it expands and contracts differentially and separates. Gives **TORS** in granite.
>
> **Both are physical, not chemical.** And both are most effective in **arid regions with drastic diurnal temperature swings** — where there is little water for chemical weathering to work with.

### Biological weathering

**Biological weathering is contribution to or removal of minerals and ions from the weathering environment and physical changes due to growth or movement of organisms.**

**Burrowing and wedging by organisms like earthworms, termites, rodents etc., help in exposing the new surfaces to chemical attack and assists in the penetration of moisture and air.** **Human beings by disturbing vegetation, ploughing and cultivating soils, also help in mixing and creating new contacts between air, water and minerals in the earth materials.**

**Decaying plant and animal matter helps in the production of humic, carbonic and other acids which enhance decay and solubility of some elements.** **Plant roots exert a tremendous pressure on the earth materials mechanically breaking them apart.**

### Special effects: Exfoliation

**Exfoliation is a result but not a process.** **Flaking off of more or less curved sheets of shells from over rocks or bedrock results in smooth and rounded surfaces.** **Exfoliation can occur due to expansion and contraction induced by temperature changes.** **Exfoliation domes and tors result due to unloading and thermal expansion respectively.**

### Significance of Weathering — a high-value section

**Weathering processes are responsible for breaking down the rocks into smaller fragments and preparing the way for formation of not only regolith and soils, but also erosion and mass movements.**

| Significance | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Soil formation** | **Biomes and biodiversity is basically a result of forests (vegetation) and forests depend upon the depth of weathering mantles** |
| **Erosion enabler** | **Erosion cannot be significant if the rocks are not weathered.** **Weathering aids mass wasting, erosion and reduction of relief and changes in landforms are a consequence of erosion** |
| **ORE DEPOSITS — the economically vital one** | **Weathering is an important process in the formation of soils.** **Weathering is of special significance to man, as it is the first step to the formation of ORE DEPOSITS.** **Enrichment of ores takes place through weathering — a process called LEACHING** |
| **Carbon sink** | **Weathering promotes the sinking of atmospheric carbon dioxide in the form of carbonates. It is a bio-climatically controlled process** |

> **The ore-deposit point is the one that earns marks, and it links three chapters.**
>
> **LEACHING is the process by which weathering ENRICHES ore deposits.** Rain and groundwater dissolve and carry away the soluble, worthless material, **leaving the insoluble valuable minerals concentrated behind.** **This is exactly how BAUXITE forms** (Cl.10 Ch.5 — "decomposition of surface rocks leaves a residual mass") and why **LATERITE soil is nutrient-poor** (Cl.10 Ch.1 — the nutrients were leached out).
>
> **The same process that ruins a soil concentrates an ore.** Whether leaching is good or bad depends entirely on whether you want what stays or what leaves.

> 🌱 **The carbon-sink point is genuinely important and rarely used by candidates.** Chemical weathering of silicate rocks **consumes atmospheric CO₂**, locking it into carbonates that eventually end up in ocean sediment. **Over geological time, rock weathering is one of Earth's principal carbon sinks and a key thermostat on climate.** Mentioning it in a GS-III climate answer — alongside forests and oceans — shows a level of reading beyond the standard.

## 3️⃣ Mass Movements

> **Mass movements transfer the mass of rock debris down the slopes under the direct influence of GRAVITY.** **Air, water or ice do not carry debris with them from place to place but on the other hand the debris may carry with it air, water or ice.**

⚠️ **The movements of mass may range from slow to rapid, affecting shallow to deep columns of materials and include creep, flow, slide and fall.** **Gravity exerts its force on all matter, both bedrock and the products of weathering.**

⚠️ **Mass movements are aided by gravity and NO geomorphic agent like running water, glaciers, wind, waves and currents participate in the process of mass movements.** **That means mass movements do not come under erosion though there is a shift (aided by gravity) of materials from one place to another.**

> **This is a distinction UPSC tests: MASS MOVEMENT IS NOT EROSION.** **Erosion requires a geomorphic agent (water, wind, ice) to transport material. Mass movement is gravity acting alone.** The material moves, but nothing carries it.

### Activating causes

**Weak materials, steep slopes and triggers.** Specifically:
- **Removal of support from below to materials above** — through natural or artificial means
- **Increase in gradient and height of slopes**
- **Overloading through addition of materials naturally or by artificial filling**
- **Overloading due to heavy rainfall, saturation and lubrication of slope materials**
- **Removal of material or load from over the original slope surfaces**
- **Occurrence of earthquakes, explosions or machinery**
- **Excessive natural seepage**
- **Heavy drawdown of water from lakes, reservoirs and rivers leading to slow outflow of water from under the slopes or river banks**
- **Indiscriminate removal of natural vegetation**

### Types of mass movement

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SLOW MOVEMENTS — CREEP** | **Occurs on moderately steep, soil-covered slopes.** **Movement of materials is extremely slow and imperceptible except through extended observation.** **Materials involved can be soil or rock debris.** **Depending upon the type of material involved, several types of creep — soil creep, talus creep, rock creep, rock-glacier creep — may be identified |
| **SOLIFLUCTION** | **The slow downslope flowing soil mass or fine-grained rock debris SATURATED or LUBRICATED WITH WATER.** **This process is quite common in moist temperate areas where surface melting of deeply frozen ground and long continued rain respectively, occur frequently.** **When the upper portions get saturated and when the lower parts are impervious to water percolation, flowing occurs in the upper parts** |
| **RAPID MOVEMENTS — FLOW** | **Mostly prevalent in humid climatic regions and occur over gentle to steep slopes.** **Movement of water-saturated clayey or silty earth materials down low-angle terraces or hillsides is called EARTHFLOW.** **When slopes are steeper, even the bedrock especially of soft sedimentary rocks like shale or deeply weathered igneous rock may slide downslope.** **Debris avalanche is more characteristic of humid regions with or without vegetation and occurs in narrow tracks on steep slopes — this debris avalanche can be much faster than the debris flow.** **Debris avalanche is similar to snow avalanche** |
| **MUDFLOW** | **In the absence of vegetation cover and with heavy rainfall, thick layers of weathered materials get saturated with water and either slowly or rapidly flow down along definite channels.** **It looks like a stream of mud within a valley.** **When there is a heavy rainfall in volcanic areas covered with volcanic ash, mudflows are very common** |
| **LANDSLIDES** | **These are relatively rapid and perceptible movements.** **The materials involved are relatively dry.** **The size and shape of the detached mass depends on the nature of discontinuities in the rock, the degree of weathering and the steepness of the slope** |

### Types of landslide

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SLUMP** | **Slipping of one or several units of rock debris with a backward rotation with respect to the slope over which the movement takes place** |
| **DEBRIS SLIDE** | **Rapid rolling or sliding of earth debris without backward rotation of mass** |
| **DEBRIS FALL** | **Nearly free falling of earth debris from a vertical or overhanging face** |
| **ROCKSLIDE** | **Sliding of individual rock masses down bedding, joint or fault surfaces** |
| **ROCKFALL** | **Free falling of rock blocks over any steep slope keeping itself away from the slope.** **Rockfalls occur from the superficial layers of the rock face, an occurrence which distinguishes it from rockslide which affects materials up to a substantial depth** |

> 🌱 **The disaster-management framing for GS-III:** **Landslides in India are concentrated in the Himalayas, the Western Ghats and the Nilgiris.** The Himalayan case is especially acute because **the Shiwaliks are made of loose, unconsolidated sediment** (Cl.9 Ch.2) in a **young, seismically active** range.
>
> **Notice how many of NCERT's activating causes are HUMAN: removal of support from below, overloading by artificial filling, machinery vibration, heavy drawdown of reservoirs, and indiscriminate removal of natural vegetation.** **This is the basis for the argument that Himalayan landslide risk is being manufactured by road-cutting, hydro projects and unplanned construction — not merely inherited from geology.** That is the point that lifts a landslide answer above description.

## 4️⃣ Erosion, Deposition and Soil Formation

### Erosion and deposition

**EROSION involves acquisition and transportation of rock debris.** **When massive rocks break into smaller fragments through weathering and any other process, erosional geomorphic agents like running water, groundwater, glaciers, wind and waves remove and transport it to other places depending upon the dynamics of each of these agents.**

⚠️ **Abrasion by rock debris carried by these geomorphic agents also aids greatly in erosion.** **By erosion, relief degrades, i.e. the landscape is worn down.** **That means, weathering aids erosion but it is not a pre-condition for erosion to take place.**

**DEPOSITION is a consequence of erosion.** **The erosional agents lose their velocity and hence energy on gentler slopes and the materials carried by them start to settle themselves.** **In other words, deposition is not actually the work of any agent.** **The coarser materials get deposited first and finer ones later.**

> **Note the precision: "weathering aids erosion but it is not a PRE-CONDITION for erosion."** Erosion can act on unweathered rock directly — a glacier plucking bedrock, for instance. **Weathering makes erosion far more effective, but is not required.**

### Soil formation

> **SOIL is a dynamic medium in which many chemical, physical and biological activities go on constantly.** **Soil is a result of decay, it is also the medium for growth.**

**PEDOGENESIS is the process of soil formation.**

**The weathered material, or regolith, is the parent material for soil.** **The first stage of the soil-formation processes is the SOIL PROCESS.** **Weathering is a very important process in the formation of soils.**

### The five soil-forming factors

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **PARENT MATERIAL** | **A passive control factor in soil formation.** **Parent materials can be any in-situ or on-site weathered rock debris (residual soils) or transported deposits (transported soils).** **Soil formation depends upon the texture and structure as well as the mineral and chemical composition of the rock debris/deposits** |
| **TOPOGRAPHY** | **Like parent materials, topography is another passive control factor.** **On steep slopes, soils are thin, as the gravity and running water quickly move materials downslope.** **On flat uplands, where drainage is good, the soils are thick, dark-coloured and rich in organic matter** |
| **CLIMATE** | **An ACTIVE control factor and it plays an important role in soil formation.** **The important climatic elements: (i) moisture in terms of its intensity, frequency and duration of precipitation — evaporation and humidity; (ii) temperature in terms of seasonal and diurnal variations** |
| **BIOLOGICAL ACTIVITY** | **The vegetative cover and organisms that occupy the parent materials from the beginning and also at later stages help in adding organic matter, moisture retention, nitrogen etc.** **Dead plants provide HUMUS, the finely divided organic matter of the soil.** **Bacteria, fungi, earthworms and termites are the chief agents** |
| **TIME** | **The MOST important control factor.** **Time as a factor in soil formation determines the maturation of soil and profile development.** **No specific length of time in absolute terms can be fixed as the time required for the formation of soils** |

> **Note the classification: PARENT MATERIAL and TOPOGRAPHY are PASSIVE factors; CLIMATE and BIOLOGICAL ACTIVITY are ACTIVE factors; TIME determines maturity.** **That active/passive split is examinable and most candidates don't know it.**

**Trap 1:** *"Mass movement is a form of erosion."* **It is not** — erosion needs a geomorphic agent; mass movement is gravity alone.
**Trap 2:** *"Exfoliation is a weathering process."* **It is a RESULT, not a process** — of unloading or thermal expansion.
**Trap 3:** *"Tors form by unloading."* **Thermal expansion.** **Exfoliation domes** form by unloading.
**Trap 4:** *"Weathering is a pre-condition for erosion."* **It aids erosion but is not required.**
**Trap 5:** *"Climate is a passive soil-forming factor."* **ACTIVE.** Parent material and topography are the passive ones.
**Trap 6:** *"Orogenic movements are vertical."* **Horizontal** (mountain building). **Epeirogenic** are vertical.
**Trap 7:** *"Frost weathering is most effective in polar regions."* **At high elevations in MID-LATITUDES**, where freeze–thaw is repeated most often. **Permanently frozen ground doesn't cycle.**

|---|---|

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Endogenic processes run on the Earth's internal heat and BUILD UP; exogenic processes run on SOLAR energy and WEAR DOWN.**
2. **DENUDATION = weathering + mass wasting + erosion + transportation.**
3. **Without endogenic uplift, exogenic forces would have flattened the planet long ago.** Relief exists because building outpaces wearing.
4. **Diastrophism: orogenic (mountain, horizontal, narrow belts) · epeirogenic (continent, vertical, large areas) · earthquakes · plate tectonics.**
5. **Weathering is IN-SITU — no transport.** Three families: **chemical, physical, biological.**
6. **Chemical: solution, carbonation, hydration, oxidation/reduction.** Carbonation makes caves.
7. **Physical: unloading & expansion, temperature change, frost wedging, salt weathering.**
8. **Exfoliation is a RESULT, not a process.** **Exfoliation domes = unloading; tors = thermal expansion.**
9. **LEACHING enriches ore deposits — the same process that makes laterite soil infertile.** Whether it helps depends on whether you want what stays or what goes.
10. 🌱 **Weathering is a carbon sink** — CO₂ locked into carbonates. A geological climate thermostat.
11. **Mass movement is NOT erosion** — no geomorphic agent, gravity alone.
12. **Creep (slowest) → solifluction → earthflow/mudflow/debris avalanche → landslides (slump, debris slide, debris fall, rockslide, rockfall).** **Slump has backward rotation.**
13. 🌱 **Many of NCERT's landslide triggers are human** — removing support, artificial filling, machinery, reservoir drawdown, removing vegetation. **Himalayan landslide risk is partly manufactured.**
14. **Five soil factors: parent material and topography (PASSIVE) · climate and biological activity (ACTIVE) · TIME (most important).**
