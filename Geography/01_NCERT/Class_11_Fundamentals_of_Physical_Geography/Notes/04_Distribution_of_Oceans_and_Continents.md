# Chapter 4 — Distribution of Oceans and Continents

> **The one-line point:** The continents are **moving**, a few centimetres a year, and almost every large-scale feature of the Earth's surface — mountains, ocean trenches, earthquake belts, volcanoes — is a consequence of that motion. ⭐ **Plate tectonics is the single unifying theory of physical geography**, and this chapter is where you learn it.

🔴 **HIGH PRIORITY — read carefully, ~2 hrs.** Plate tectonics is asked in Prelims almost every year and underlies Chapters 6 and 7 entirely.

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ Continental Drift — Wegener, 1912

⭐ **Alfred Wegener, a German meteorologist, put forth the CONTINENTAL DRIFT THEORY in 1912.**

⭐ **He argued that all the continents formed a single continental mass called ⭐ PANGAEA — meaning "all earth" — surrounded by a mega ocean called ⭐ PANTHALASSA — meaning "all water."**

⭐ **He suggested that around 200 million years ago, Pangaea began to split.**

```
   ⭐ THE BREAK-UP SEQUENCE

   PANGAEA (one supercontinent, surrounded by PANTHALASSA)
        │
        │  ~200 million years ago, splits into TWO:
        ▼
   ┌────────────────────┬────────────────────────┐
   │  ⭐ LAURASIA        │  ⭐ GONDWANALAND        │
   │  (northern)        │  (southern)            │
   │                    │                        │
   │  N. America        │  S. America            │
   │  Europe            │  Africa                │
   │  Asia              │  INDIA                 │
   │                    │  Australia             │
   │                    │  Antarctica            │
   └────────────────────┴────────────────────────┘
        │
        │  separated by the ⭐ TETHYS SEA
        ▼
   Further splitting → the present continents
```

⭐ **Pangaea first broke into two large continental masses as Laurasia and Gondwanaland forming the northern and southern components respectively. Subsequently, Laurasia and Gondwanaland kept on breaking into various smaller continents that exist today.**

### ⭐⭐ The evidence — learn every item

| Evidence | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **THE MATCHING OF CONTINENTS (Jig-Saw-Fit)** | ⭐ **The shorelines of Africa and South America facing each other have a remarkable and unmistakable match.** ⭐ **In 1964, Bullard produced a map using a computer programme to find the best fit — it was done at 1,000-fathom line instead of the present shoreline** |
| ⭐ **ROCKS OF SAME AGE ACROSS THE OCEANS** | ⭐ **The radiometric dating methods have helped in correlating the rock formation from different continents across the vast ocean.** ⭐ **The belt of ancient rocks of 2,000 million years from Brazil coast matches with those from western Africa.** ⭐ **The earliest marine deposits along the coastline of South America and Africa are of the Jurassic age — suggesting the ocean did not exist prior to that time** |
| ⭐ **TILLITE** | ⭐ **The sedimentary rock formed out of deposits of glaciers.** ⭐ **The Gondwana system of sediments from India is known to have its counterparts in six different landmasses of the Southern Hemisphere.** ⭐ **The Indian counterpart is known as the Gondwana system of sediments — the base of the system is marked by glacial tillite indicating extensive and prolonged glaciation** |
| ⭐ **PLACER DEPOSITS** | ⭐ **The occurrence of rich placer deposits of GOLD in the Ghana coast and the absolute absence of source rock in the region is an amazing fact.** ⭐ **The gold-bearing veins are in Brazil, and it is obvious that the gold deposits of Ghana are derived from the Brazil plateau when the two continents lay side by side** |
| ⭐ **DISTRIBUTION OF FOSSILS** | ⭐ **When identical species of plants and animals adapted to living on land or in fresh water are found on either side of the marine barriers, a problem arises.** ⭐ **LEMURS occur in India, Madagascar and Africa leading some to consider a contiguous landmass "LEMURIA" linking these three landmasses.** ⭐ **MESOSAURUS was a small reptile adapted to shallow brackish water. The skeletons of these are found only in two localities: the Southern Cape province of South Africa and Iraver formations of Brazil. The two localities presently are 4,800 km apart with an ocean in between them** |

> ⭐ **The Mesosaurus argument is the cleanest, and worth being able to state.** Mesosaurus was a small reptile that lived in **shallow brackish water** — it could not possibly have swum 4,800 km across a deep ocean. Its fossils appear in **exactly two places on Earth: South Africa and Brazil.** ⭐ **Either the same animal independently evolved twice in two identical ways, or the two coastlines were once joined.** The second is vastly more likely.
>
> ⭐ **The Ghana gold argument is equally elegant:** rich **placer gold** deposits on the Ghana coast, with **no source rock anywhere in the region.** The source rock — gold-bearing veins — sits in **Brazil**. ⭐ **The gold was eroded from Brazilian rock and deposited on a coast that is now on the other side of an ocean.**

### ⭐⭐ Why Wegener's theory was rejected — and this is examinable

⭐ **Wegener suggested two forces for the drift:**
- ⭐ **POLE-FLEEING FORCE** — relates to the **rotation of the earth**; the earth is not a perfect sphere but bulges at the equator due to rotation.
- ⭐ **TIDAL FORCE** — due to the **attraction of the moon and the sun** that develops tides in oceanic waters.

⚠️ ⭐ **Wegener believed that these forces would become effective when applied over many million years. However, most of the scholars considered these forces to be TOTALLY INADEQUATE.**

> ⭐⭐ **This is the key insight, and it is what UPSC likes to test.** ⭐ **Wegener's evidence was excellent; his MECHANISM was hopeless.** The forces he proposed — pole-fleeing and tidal — are far too weak to move continents through solid rock.
>
> **So the theory was rejected for fifty years, despite the evidence being right.** ⭐ **What rescued it was the discovery of SEA-FLOOR SPREADING and mantle CONVECTION CURRENTS in the 1960s** — a mechanism strong enough to do the job. **The continents are not ploughing through the ocean floor; they are riding on plates that are being created and destroyed.**
>
> 🎯 **This makes an excellent illustration for an essay on scientific method: a correct hypothesis rejected because it lacked a plausible mechanism, and vindicated only when the mechanism was found.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Convectional Current Theory

⭐ **Arthur Holmes in the 1930s discussed the possibility of CONVECTION CURRENTS operating in the mantle portion.** ⭐ **These currents are generated due to radioactive elements causing thermal differences in the mantle portion.** ⭐ **Holmes argued that there exists a system of such currents in the entire mantle portion. This was an attempt to provide an explanation to the issue of DRIFTING of the continents.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ Sea-Floor Spreading

### ⭐ Mapping the ocean floor

⭐ **Post-war period of the 1950s saw an extensive mapping of the ocean floors. Detailed research revealed some facts:**

| Finding | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Mid-oceanic ridges** | ⭐ **Volcanic eruptions are common along the mid-oceanic ridges and bring huge amounts of lava to the surface in this area** |
| ⭐ **Rock age** | ⭐ **The rocks equidistant on either sides of the crest of mid-oceanic ridges show remarkable similarities in terms of period of formation, chemical compositions and magnetic properties** |
| ⭐ **Age gradient** | ⭐ **Rocks closer to the mid-oceanic ridges have normal polarity and are the youngest. The age of the rocks increases as one moves away from the crest** |
| ⭐ **Ocean crust is young** | ⭐ **The ocean crust rocks are much younger than the continental rocks.** ⭐ **The age of rocks in the oceanic crust is nowhere more than 200 million years old. Some of the continental rock formations are as old as 3,200 million years** |
| ⭐ **Deep trenches** | ⭐ **The sediments on the ocean floor are unexpectedly very thin. Scientists were expecting, if the ocean floors were as old as the continent, to have a complete sequence of sediments for a period of much longer duration. However, nowhere was the sediment column found to be older than 200 million years** |
| ⭐ **Trench seismicity** | ⭐ **The deep trenches have deep-seated earthquake occurrences while in the mid-oceanic ridge areas the quake foci have shallow depths** |

### ⭐ The theory

⭐ **In 1961, HESS put forth the theory of SEA-FLOOR SPREADING.** ⭐ **He argued that constant eruptions at the crest of oceanic ridges cause the rupture of the oceanic crust and the new lava wedges into it, pushing the oceanic crust on either side.** ⭐ **The ocean floor thus spreads.**

⭐ **The younger age of the oceanic crust as well as the fact that the spreading of one ocean does not cause the shrinking of the other, made Hess think about the consumption of the oceanic crust.** ⭐ **He further maintained that the ocean floor that gets pushed due to volcanic eruptions at the crest, sinks down at the oceanic trenches and gets consumed.**

```
   ⭐ SEA-FLOOR SPREADING — CREATED AT RIDGES, DESTROYED AT TRENCHES

   MID-OCEAN RIDGE                          OCEAN TRENCH
   (new crust created)                      (old crust destroyed)
        ▲▲▲                                      │
      ╱  ││  ╲                                   │
   ◄──   ││   ──►  older ────────────────────►   ▼ SUBDUCTION
        magma                                  (sinks into mantle)
        rises

   ⭐ Youngest rock at the ridge; age increases outward
   ⭐ No ocean floor anywhere is older than 200 million years
   ⭐ Some continental rock is 3,200 million years old
```

> ⭐⭐ **The decisive fact worth internalising: no ocean floor anywhere on Earth is older than ~200 million years, while continental rock reaches 3,200 million years.**
>
> **Why?** Because ⭐ **the ocean floor is continuously being manufactured at ridges and recycled at trenches.** It is a **conveyor belt**, not a permanent surface. Continents, being lighter, ride on top and are **not subducted** — so they preserve a far longer record.
>
> ⭐ **This single asymmetry — young oceans, ancient continents — is the strongest evidence for plate tectonics**, and it explains why the Peninsular Plateau (Gondwana fragment) can be billions of years old while every ocean basin is geologically recent.

### ⭐ Palaeomagnetism — the magnetic stripes

⭐ **The rocks closer to the mid-oceanic ridges have normal polarity** and the age increases outward, with the polarity **reversing in alternating bands.** ⭐ **The Earth's magnetic field reverses periodically**, and as new lava solidifies it locks in the field direction at that moment — creating **symmetrical stripes of alternating polarity on both sides of the ridge.**

> 🧲 ⭐ **This is the confirming evidence.** The stripes are **mirror-images either side of the ridge.** ⭐ **That symmetry can only occur if new crust is being created at the ridge centre and pushed outward in both directions simultaneously** — exactly what Hess proposed. **It is effectively a tape recording of the Earth's magnetic history, written outward from the ridge.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Plate Tectonics

⭐ **In 1967, McKenzie and Parker, and later Morgan, outlined the concept of PLATE TECTONICS.**

> ⭐ **A TECTONIC PLATE (also called lithospheric plate) is a massive, irregularly-shaped slab of solid rock, generally composed of both continental and oceanic lithosphere.**

⭐ **Plates move horizontally over the asthenosphere as rigid units.** ⭐ **The lithosphere includes the crust and top mantle with its thickness range varying between 5–100 km in oceanic parts and about 200 km in the continental areas.**

⭐ **A plate may be referred to as the continental plate or oceanic plate depending on which of the two occupy a larger portion of the plate.** ⭐ **The Pacific plate is largely an oceanic plate whereas the Eurasian plate may be called a continental plate.**

### ⭐ The seven major plates

| ⭐ Major plates |
|---|
| ⭐ **1. Antarctica and the surrounding oceanic plate** |
| ⭐ **2. North American (with western Atlantic floor) plate** |
| ⭐ **3. South American (with western Atlantic floor) plate** |
| ⭐ **4. Pacific plate** |
| ⭐ **5. India-Australia-New Zealand plate** |
| ⭐ **6. Africa with the eastern Atlantic floor plate** |
| ⭐ **7. Eurasia and the adjacent oceanic plate** |

⭐ **Some important minor plates:** ⭐ **Cocos plate** (between Central America and Pacific plate) · ⭐ **Nazca plate** (between South America and Pacific) · ⭐ **Arabian plate** (mostly the Saudi Arabian landmass) · ⭐ **Philippine plate** (between the Asiatic and Pacific plate) · ⭐ **Caroline plate** (between the Philippine and Indian plate) · ⭐ **Fuji plate** (north-east of Australia).

### ⭐⭐ The three types of plate boundary

| Boundary | ⭐ Motion | ⭐ Result | ⭐ Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **DIVERGENT (Constructive)** | ⭐ **Plates move AWAY from each other** | ⭐ **New crust is generated.** Mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys | ⭐ **The Mid-Atlantic Ridge** — the American plates separating from the Eurasian and African plates. ⭐ **East African Rift Valley** |
| ⭐ **CONVERGENT (Destructive)** | ⭐ **Plates move TOWARD each other; one goes under the other** | ⭐ **Crust is destroyed — SUBDUCTION.** Mountains, trenches, volcanoes, deep earthquakes | Three sub-types below |
| ⭐ **TRANSFORM (Conservative)** | ⭐ **Plates slide HORIZONTALLY past each other** | ⭐ **Crust is neither produced nor destroyed** | ⭐ **The San Andreas Fault, California** |

⭐ **Transform faults are the planes of separation generally perpendicular to the mid-oceanic ridges.**

### ⭐ Three types of convergence

| Type | ⭐ Result | ⭐ Example |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Oceanic–Continental** | ⭐ Denser oceanic plate subducts; **volcanic mountain range on the continent** | ⭐ **The Andes** (Nazca under South American plate) |
| ⭐ **Oceanic–Oceanic** | One subducts; **island arc of volcanoes** | ⭐ **Japan, Philippines, Aleutians, Andaman & Nicobar** |
| ⭐ **Continental–Continental** | ⭐ **Neither subducts (both are light) — so crust CRUMPLES UPWARD** | ⭐ **THE HIMALAYAS** (Indian plate into Eurasian plate) |

> ⭐⭐ **Why the Himalayas are so exceptionally high — the reasoning, which is genuinely satisfying.**
>
> In **oceanic-continental** convergence, the dense oceanic plate simply **dives under** — the collision is absorbed by subduction, and you get a volcanic range like the Andes.
>
> In **continental-continental** convergence, **neither plate is dense enough to subduct.** Both are light granitic crust. **Neither will go down.** So the collision energy has nowhere to go but **upward and sideways** — the crust thickens, buckles and piles.
>
> ⭐ **This is why the crust under the Himalayas is ~70 km thick (Ch.3) against a normal continental 30 km — it is effectively DOUBLED.** And it is why the collision, which began ~50 million years ago, **is still going**, and the Himalayas are still rising ~5 mm/year while remaining seismically violent.

### ⭐ Rates of plate movement

⭐ **The slowest rate is on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge — the rate of the movement ranges between 2–2.5 cm per year — and the fastest rate is the Nazca plate, more than 15 cm per year.**

⭐ **The magnetic stripes on the sea floor allow the rate of movement to be calculated:** the width of a stripe divided by the known duration of that magnetic epoch gives the spreading rate.

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Indian Plate — Movement and the Himalayas

⭐ **The Indian plate includes Peninsular India and the Australian continental portions.** ⭐ **The subduction zone along the Himalayas forms the northern plate boundary in the form of continent-continent convergence.** ⭐ **In the east, it extends through Rakinyoma Mountains of Myanmar towards the island arc along the Java Trench.** ⭐ **The eastern margin is a spreading site lying to the east of Australia in the form of an oceanic ridge in SW Pacific.** ⭐ **The Western margin follows Kirthar Mountain of Pakistan. It further extends along the Makrana coast and joins the spreading site from the Red Sea rift southeastward along the Chagos Archipelago.** ⭐ **The boundary between India and the Antarctic plate is also marked by oceanic ridge (divergent) running in roughly W-E direction and merging into the spreading site, a little south of New Zealand.**

### ⭐ The journey north

⭐ **India was a large island situated off the Australian coast, in a vast ocean.** ⭐ **The Tethys Sea separated it from the Asian continent till about 225 million years ago.** ⭐ **India is supposed to have started her northward journey about 200 million years ago at the time when Pangaea broke.** ⭐ **India collided with Asia about 40–50 million years ago causing rapid uplift of the Himalayas.**

⭐ **About 140 million years before the present, the subcontinent was located as south as 50°S latitude.** ⭐ **The two major plates were separated by the Tethys Sea and the Tibetan block was closer to the Asiatic landmass.** ⭐ **During the movement of the Indian plate towards the Asiatic plate, a major event that occurred was the outpouring of enormous volumes of lava that formed the DECCAN TRAPS. This started somewhere around 60 million years ago and continued for a long period of time. Note that the subcontinent was still close to the equator.** ⭐ **From 40 million years ago and thereafter, the event of formation of the Himalayas took place. Scientists believe that the process is still continuing and the height of the Himalayas is rising even to this date.**

> ⭐ **The Deccan Traps timing is a lovely connecting fact.** They erupted around **60 million years ago**, while India was **still crossing the equator on its northward journey** — over what is now thought to be the **Réunion hotspot.** ⭐ **So India's two most distinctive physical features — the Deccan basalt and the Himalayas — are both products of the same journey**, roughly 20 million years apart. **And the Deccan Traps' timing coincides closely with the end-Cretaceous mass extinction that killed the dinosaurs**, which is a live scientific debate.

**Trap 1:** *"Wegener's theory was rejected because his evidence was weak."* ⭐ **His evidence was excellent — his proposed MECHANISM (pole-fleeing and tidal forces) was inadequate.**
**Trap 2:** *"The Himalayas formed by oceanic-continental convergence."* ⭐ **Continent-continent** — which is precisely why they are so high.
**Trap 3:** *"Ocean floor rocks are older than continental rocks."* ⭐ **The reverse** — none older than 200 my, against 3,200 my continental.
**Trap 4:** *"The Nazca plate is the slowest-moving."* ⭐ **The fastest (>15 cm/yr).** The **Mid-Atlantic Ridge** is slowest (2–2.5 cm/yr).
**Trap 5:** *"Transform boundaries create new crust."* ⭐ **Neither created nor destroyed** — hence "conservative."
**Trap 6:** *"Pangaea broke into Laurasia and Tethys."* ⭐ **Laurasia and Gondwanaland**, *separated by* the Tethys Sea.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Wegener (1912): Pangaea surrounded by Panthalassa, split ~200 mya into Laurasia (N) and Gondwanaland (S), separated by the Tethys.**
2. ⭐ **Evidence: jig-saw fit (Bullard 1964, 1,000-fathom line) · matching 2,000 my rocks Brazil–W.Africa · tillite · Ghana's placer gold with source rock in Brazil · Mesosaurus in only two places 4,800 km apart · lemurs and "Lemuria."**
3. ⭐⭐ **Wegener was rejected because his forces — pole-fleeing and tidal — were totally inadequate. His evidence was right; his mechanism wasn't.**
4. ⭐ **Holmes (1930s) proposed mantle convection currents driven by radioactive heating.**
5. ⭐ **Hess (1961): SEA-FLOOR SPREADING** — crust created at ridges, consumed at trenches.
6. ⭐⭐ **No ocean floor is older than 200 million years; continental rock reaches 3,200 million.** The ocean floor is a conveyor belt, not a permanent surface.
7. ⭐ **Magnetic stripes are mirror-symmetric about the ridge** — proof that crust is created centrally and pushed outward both ways.
8. ⭐ **McKenzie, Parker and Morgan (1967): plate tectonics.** Seven major plates; minor ones include **Nazca, Cocos, Arabian, Philippine, Caroline, Fuji.**
9. ⭐ **Three boundaries: divergent (constructive, Mid-Atlantic Ridge) · convergent (destructive) · transform (conservative, San Andreas).**
10. ⭐ **Three convergences: ocean-continent (Andes) · ocean-ocean (island arcs: Japan, Andaman) · continent-continent (Himalayas).**
11. ⭐⭐ **The Himalayas are so high because NEITHER plate can subduct** — crust doubles to ~70 km instead of diving.
12. ⭐ **Rates: Mid-Atlantic Ridge slowest (2–2.5 cm/yr); Nazca fastest (>15 cm/yr).**
13. ⭐ **India: separated by the Tethys till ~225 mya · northward journey from ~200 mya · at 50°S ~140 mya · Deccan Traps ~60 mya near the equator · collision 40–50 mya · Himalayas still rising.**
