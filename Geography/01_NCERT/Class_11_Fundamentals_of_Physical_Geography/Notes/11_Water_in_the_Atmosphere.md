# Chapter 11 — Water in the Atmosphere 🌱

> **The one-line point:** **Warm air can hold more water vapour than cold air — and almost everything about clouds, fog and rainfall follows from that single fact.** Cool any parcel of moist air enough and it must give up its water. **The only question is HOW it gets cooled, and that determines whether you get dew, frost, fog, drizzle or a thunderstorm.**

## 1️⃣ Humidity

> **The amount of water vapour present in the atmosphere is known as HUMIDITY.**

**Water is present in the atmosphere in three forms: gaseous, liquid and solid.** **The moisture in the atmosphere is derived from water bodies through evaporation and from plants through transpiration.** **Thus, there is a continuous exchange of water between the atmosphere, the oceans and the continents through the processes of evaporation, transpiration, condensation and precipitation.**

### The four measures

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| **ABSOLUTE HUMIDITY** | **The actual amount of the water vapour present in the atmosphere.** **It is expressed in terms of grams per cubic metre of air.** **The ability of the air to hold water vapour depends entirely on its temperature.** **The absolute humidity differs from place to place on the surface of the earth** |
| **RELATIVE HUMIDITY** | **The percentage of moisture present in the atmosphere as compared to its full capacity at a given temperature.** **With the change of air temperature, the capacity to retain moisture increases or decreases and the relative humidity is also affected.** **It is greater over the oceans and least over the continents** |
| **SPECIFIC HUMIDITY** | **The weight of water vapour per unit weight of the air** |
| **SATURATION / DEW POINT** | **The air containing moisture to its full capacity at a given temperature is said to be SATURATED.** **It means that the air at the given temperature is incapable of holding any additional amount of moisture at that stage.** **The temperature at which saturation occurs in a given sample of air is known as DEW POINT** |

> **Relative humidity is the one people misunderstand, and it is worth getting right.**
>
> **Relative humidity is NOT a measure of how much water is in the air. It is a measure of how FULL the air is, relative to what it could hold at that temperature.**
>
> **So the same air can go from 50% to 100% relative humidity with no water added at all — simply by cooling.** Cooling reduces the *capacity*, so the same absolute quantity becomes a higher *percentage*.
>
> 🥤 **The comparison that fits: a glass of water.** **Absolute humidity is how much water is in it. Relative humidity is how full the glass is.** **Cooling the air shrinks the glass** — so without adding a drop, the glass overflows. **That overflow is condensation.**

**Relative humidity is greater over the OCEANS and least over the CONTINENTS.**

## 2️⃣ Evaporation and Condensation

### Evaporation

**The change of water into water vapour state is called EVAPORATION.** **Evaporation is a cooling process, in that the LATENT HEAT OF VAPORISATION (energy required to change the state of water from liquid to gas) is absorbed from the immediate surroundings.**

**The temperature at which the water starts evaporating is referred to as the LATENT HEAT OF VAPORISATION.**

**Increase in temperature increases water absorption and retention capacity of the given parcel of air.** **Similarly, if the moisture content is low, air has a potentiality of absorbing and retaining moisture.** **Movement of air replaces the saturated layer with the unsaturated layer. Hence, the greater the movement of air, the greater is the evaporation.**

> **Evaporation is a COOLING process** — which is why sweating cools you, why a wet earthen pot keeps water cold, and why 🌱 **evaporation carries 19 of the 51 units in the heat budget** (Ch.9) — the largest single return path. **Latent heat is how the surface exports energy upward without the air ever getting hotter.**

### Condensation

**The transformation of water vapour into water is called CONDENSATION.** **Condensation is caused by the loss of heat.**

**When moist air is cooled, it may reach a level when its capacity to hold water vapour ceases.** **Then, the excess water vapour condenses into liquid form.** **If it directly condenses into solid form, it is known as SUBLIMATION.**

**In free air, condensation results from cooling around very small particles termed as HYGROSCOPIC CONDENSATION NUCLEI.** **Particles of dust, smoke and salt from the ocean are particularly good nuclei because they absorb water.**

**Condensation also takes place when the moist air comes in contact with some colder object and it may also take place when the temperature is close to the dew point.**

**Condensation, therefore, depends upon the amount of cooling and the relative humidity of the air.** **Condensation is influenced by the volume of air, temperature, pressure and humidity.**

**Condensation takes place: (i) when the temperature of the air is reduced to dew point with its volume remaining constant; (ii) when both the volume and the temperature are reduced; (iii) when moisture is added to the air through evaporation.**

⚠️ **However, the most favourable condition for condensation is the DECREASE IN AIR TEMPERATURE.**

**After condensation the water vapour or the moisture in the atmosphere takes one of the following forms — DEW, FROST, FOG and CLOUDS.** **Forms of condensation may be classified on the basis of temperature and location.**

## 3️⃣ Forms of Condensation

| Form | Detail |
|---|---|
| **DEW** | **When the moisture is deposited in the form of water droplets on cooler surfaces of solid objects (rather than nuclei in air above the surface) such as stones, grass blades and plant leaves, it is known as dew.** **The ideal conditions for its formation are clear sky, calm air, high relative humidity, and cold and long nights.** **For the formation of dew, it is necessary that the DEW POINT IS ABOVE THE FREEZING POINT** |
| **FROST** | **Frost forms on cold surfaces when condensation takes place BELOW FREEZING POINT (0°C), i.e. the dew point is at or below the freezing point.** **The excess moisture is deposited in the form of minute ice crystals instead of water droplets.** **The ideal conditions for the formation of white frost are the same as those for the formation of dew, except that the air temperature must be at or below the freezing point** |
| **FOG and MIST** | **When the temperature of an air mass containing a large quantity of water vapour falls all of a sudden, condensation takes place within itself on fine dust particles.** **So, the fog is a cloud with its base at or very near to the ground.** **Because of the fog and mist, the visibility becomes poor to zero.** **In cities and industrial centres SMOKE provides plenty of nuclei which help the formation of fog and mist. Such a condition when fog is mixed with smoke, is described as SMOG** |

**The difference between the mist and fog is that MIST CONTAINS MORE MOISTURE THAN THE FOG.** **In mist each nuclei contains a thicker layer of moisture.** **Mists are frequent over mountains as the rising warm air up the slopes meets a cold surface.** **Fogs are drier than mist and they are prevalent where warm currents of air come in contact with cold currents.** **Fogs are mini clouds in which condensation takes place around nuclei provided by the dust, smoke, and the salt particles.**

> **The dew/frost distinction is purely about ONE number: dew forms when the dew point is ABOVE freezing; frost forms when it is AT OR BELOW freezing.** **Everything else about the conditions is identical** — clear sky, calm air, high relative humidity, long cold nights. **Same process, one threshold.**

> **And note NCERT's specific claim that catches people out: MIST CONTAINS MORE MOISTURE THAN FOG.** Intuitively people assume fog is the wetter one because it is denser-looking. **NCERT says the reverse — in mist each nucleus carries a thicker layer of moisture; fogs are drier.**

> 🌱 **SMOG = smoke + fog**, and the mechanism connects three chapters: **urban smoke supplies abundant condensation nuclei** (Ch.8), **winter temperature inversion traps the layer near the ground** (Ch.9), **and fog forms within it.** **Delhi's winter smog is all three acting together** — which is why it is a meteorological problem as much as an emissions one.

## 4️⃣ Clouds

> **CLOUD is a mass of minute water droplets or tiny crystals of ice formed by the condensation of the water vapour in free air at considerable elevations.**

**As the clouds are formed at some height over the surface of the earth, they take various shapes.** **According to their height, expanse, density and transparency or opaqueness clouds are grouped under four types:**

| Cloud | Height | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **CIRRUS** | **HIGH — 8,000–12,000 m** | **Formed at high altitudes.** **They are thin and detached clouds having a FEATHERY appearance.** **They are always WHITE in colour** |
| **CUMULUS** | **4,000–7,000 m** | **Look like COTTON WOOL.** **They are generally formed at a height of 4,000–7,000 m.** **They exist in patches and can be seen scattered here and there.** **They have a FLAT BASE** |
| **STRATUS** | **LOW** | **As their name implies, are LAYERED clouds covering large portions of the sky.** **These clouds are generally formed either due to loss of heat or the mixing of air masses with different temperatures** |
| **NIMBUS** | **LOW — very near the surface** | **Black or dark gray.** **They form at middle levels or very near to the surface of the earth.** **These are extremely DENSE and OPAQUE to the rays of the sun.** **Sometimes, the clouds are so low that they seem to touch the ground.** **Nimbus clouds are shapeless masses of thick vapour** |

**A combination of these four basic types can give rise to the following types of clouds:** **high clouds — cirrus, cirrostratus, cirrocumulus; middle clouds — altostratus and altocumulus; low clouds — stratocumulus and nimbostratus; and clouds with extensive vertical development — cumulus and cumulonimbus.**

> **The naming system is a code, and learning the code beats memorising the list:**
> **CIRRO- = high · ALTO- = middle · (no prefix) = low**
> **-STRATUS = layered/sheet · -CUMULUS = heaped/puffy · NIMBO-/-NIMBUS = rain-bearing**
> **So "nimbostratus" = a low, layered, rain-bearing cloud. "Cirrocumulus" = high, puffy, no rain.** **Decode rather than memorise.**

> **CUMULONIMBUS is the one to know by name** — the towering thundercloud with enormous vertical development. **It is the cloud of thunderstorms, hail, lightning and tornadoes** (Ch.10), and it is what forms along a **cold front** where warm air is forced up steeply.

## 5️⃣ Precipitation

> **The process of continuous condensation in free air helps the condensed particles to grow in size. When the resistance of the air fails to hold them against the force of gravity, they fall on to the earth's surface. So after the condensation of water vapour, the release of moisture is known as PRECIPITATION.**

**This may take place in liquid or solid form.**

| Form | Detail |
|---|---|
| **RAINFALL** | **The precipitation in the form of water is called rainfall, when the temperature is higher than the freezing point** |
| **SNOWFALL** | **Precipitation in the form of snow takes place when the temperature is less than 0°C.** **Precipitation in the form of snow takes place when the temperature is less than 0° C and the atmosphere is saturated** |
| **SLEET** | **Frozen raindrops and refrozen melted snow-water.** **When a layer of air with the temperature above freezing point overlies a subfreezing layer near the ground, precipitation takes place in the form of sleet** |
| **HAIL** | **Sometimes, drops of rain after being released by the clouds become solidified into small rounded solid pieces of ice and which reach the surface of the earth are called hailstones.** **These are formed by the rainwater passing through the colder layers.** **Hailstones have several concentric layers of ice one over the other** |

> **Hailstones' concentric layers are a nice detail with an explanation:** the stone is carried **up and down repeatedly** by strong updrafts inside a cumulonimbus, **adding a new layer of ice on each circuit** — like an onion. **The number of layers records how many times it went round.** **That is why hail requires violent vertical air movement, and why it is associated with thunderstorms.**

### ⭐ The three types of rainfall — high-frequency content

**On the basis of origin, rainfall may be classified into three main types — the CONVECTIONAL, OROGRAPHIC or RELIEF and the CYCLONIC or FRONTAL.**

| Type | Mechanism | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **CONVECTIONAL** | **The air on being heated, becomes light and rises up in convection currents.** **As it rises, it expands and loses heat and consequently, condensation takes place and cumulus clouds are formed.** **With thunder and lightening, heavy rainfall takes place but this does not last long.** **Such rain is common in the summer or in the hotter part of the day** | **Equatorial regions** and inland areas, particularly northern Europe |
| **OROGRAPHIC (Relief)** | **When the saturated air mass comes across a mountain, it is forced to ascend and as it rises, it expands; the temperature falls, and the moisture is condensed.** **The chief characteristic of this type of rain is that the WINDWARD SLOPES receive greater rainfall.** **After giving rain on the windward side, when these winds reach the other slope, they descend, and their temperature rises. Then their capacity to take in moisture increases and hence, these leeward slopes remain rainless and dry. The area situated on the leeward side, which gets less rainfall is known as the RAIN-SHADOW AREA** | **Western Ghats, Himalayan foothills, Meghalaya** |
| **CYCLONIC (Frontal)** | **Warm and cold air masses meet; the warm air is forced up over the cold, cools, and precipitates along the frontal boundary** | **Temperate regions;** also tropical cyclones |

```
   OROGRAPHIC RAINFALL AND THE RAIN SHADOW

              ☁️☁️☁️  heavy rain
                 ╱▔╲
   moist wind  ╱     ╲   descending air WARMS
   ══════►   ╱  RANGE  ╲    → capacity to hold moisture RISES
           ╱             ╲   → no condensation → DRY
   ────────                 ────────────────
   WINDWARD: WET          LEEWARD: RAIN SHADOW
   Mumbai, Mangaluru,        Pune, interior Karnataka,
   Mawsynram                 LADAKH (cold desert)
```

> **Orographic rainfall is the single most useful rainfall type for Indian geography.** It explains:
> - **Why the Konkan coast is drenched and interior Karnataka semi-arid at the same latitude**
> - **Why Mawsynram is among the wettest places on Earth** — the funnel-shaped Khasi Hills force air sharply up
> - **Why LADAKH is a cold DESERT** — it sits behind the world's highest wall, and the air arriving has already lost its moisture (Cl.7 Ch.9)
> - **Why Rajasthan is dry despite the Arabian Sea branch passing over it** — the Aravallis run **parallel** to the wind, so the air is never forced to rise
>
> **Whenever a question asks "why does place X get so much / so little rain," orographic effect and rain shadow should be your first thought.**

### World distribution of rainfall

**Different places on the earth's surface receive different amounts of rainfall in a year and that too in different seasons.**

**In general, as we proceed from the equator towards the poles, RAINFALL GOES ON DECREASING STEADILY.**

**The coastal areas of the world receive greater amounts of rainfall than the interior of the continents.**

**The rainfall is more over the oceans than on the landmasses of the world because of being great sources of water.**

**Between the latitudes 35° and 40° N and S of the equator, the rain is heavier on the eastern coasts and goes on decreasing towards the west.** **But, between 45° and 65° N and S of equator, due to the westerlies, the rainfall is first received on the western margins of the continents and it goes on decreasing towards the east.**

**Wherever mountains run parallel to the coast, the rain is greater on the coastal plain, on the windward side and it decreases towards the leeward side.**

### The rainfall zones

| Zone | Amount | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Heavy rainfall** | **Over 200 cm** | Equatorial belt, windward slopes of the western coasts in cool temperate zone, coastal areas of monsoon land |
| **Moderate rainfall** | **100–200 cm** | Interior continental areas and coastal areas |
| **Low rainfall** | **50–100 cm** | Central parts of tropical land and eastern and interior parts of temperate lands |
| **Very low / inadequate** | **Less than 50 cm** | **Areas lying in the rain shadow zone of the interior of the continents and high latitudes** |

> **The east-coast/west-coast reversal between 35–40° and 45–65° is a subtle but examinable point:**
> **At 35–40°: rain is heavier on EASTERN coasts** (these lie in the trade-wind/monsoon belt).
> **At 45–65°: rain is heavier on WESTERN coasts** (these face the **westerlies**).
> **The reversal happens because the prevailing wind reverses direction between the two latitude bands** — trades blow east-to-west, westerlies blow west-to-east. **Wind direction decides which coast gets wet.**

**Trap 1:** *"Fog contains more moisture than mist."* **NCERT says the reverse — MIST contains more moisture; fogs are drier.**
**Trap 2:** *"High relative humidity means a lot of water in the air."* **It means the air is close to FULL for its temperature.** Cold air at 100% RH holds far less water than warm air at 50%.
**Trap 3:** *"Frost forms whenever dew forms in winter."* **Only when the dew point is AT OR BELOW freezing.**
**Trap 4:** *"Cirrus clouds bring rain."* **Thin, feathery, always white — high altitude, no rain.** **Nimbus** clouds bring rain.
**Trap 5:** *"Convectional rainfall is typical of temperate regions."* **Equatorial.** Cyclonic/frontal is the temperate type.
**Trap 6:** *"Evaporation warms the surroundings."* **It COOLS them** — latent heat is absorbed.
**Trap 7:** *"Rainfall increases from the equator to the poles."* **It decreases steadily poleward.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Warm air holds more water vapour than cold air** — the fact everything else follows from.
2. **Absolute humidity (g/m³) · relative humidity (% of capacity) · specific humidity (per unit weight of air).** **RH is greater over oceans, least over continents.**
3. **Relative humidity measures how FULL the air is, not how much water it holds.** Cooling shrinks the glass — hence condensation without adding water.
4. **DEW POINT = the temperature at which saturation occurs.**
5. **Evaporation is a COOLING process** (latent heat absorbed). **Condensation is caused by loss of heat**; the most favourable condition is **decrease in air temperature.**
6. **Condensation needs HYGROSCOPIC NUCLEI** — dust, smoke, ocean salt.
7. **Dew vs frost differ ONLY by whether the dew point is above or at/below freezing.** All other conditions identical.
8. **MIST CONTAINS MORE MOISTURE THAN FOG.** **SMOG = smoke + fog.**
9. **Four clouds: cirrus (8,000–12,000 m, feathery, always white) · cumulus (4,000–7,000 m, cotton wool, flat base) · stratus (layered) · nimbus (dark, dense, opaque).** **Decode the names: cirro-/alto- = height; -stratus/-cumulus = form; nimbo- = rain.**
10. **Precipitation forms: rain · snow (<0°C) · sleet (frozen raindrops) · hail (concentric layers from repeated updraft circuits).**
11. **Three rainfall types: CONVECTIONAL (equatorial, afternoon, brief but heavy) · OROGRAPHIC (windward wet, leeward RAIN SHADOW) · CYCLONIC/FRONTAL (temperate).**
12. **Orographic rainfall explains the Konkan/Deccan contrast, Mawsynram, Ladakh's cold desert, and Rajasthan's dryness.**
13. **Rainfall decreases steadily from equator to poles**; more over oceans and coasts than interiors. **At 35–40° the east coasts are wetter; at 45–65° the WEST coasts are** — because the prevailing wind reverses.
