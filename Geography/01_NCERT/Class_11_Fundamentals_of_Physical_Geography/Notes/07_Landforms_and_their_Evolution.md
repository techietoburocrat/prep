# Chapter 7 — Landforms and their Evolution

> **The one-line point:** **Five agents — running water, groundwater, glaciers, waves and wind — each carve a distinctive set of landforms, and each set follows the same two-part logic: EROSIONAL upstream/upslope where energy is high, DEPOSITIONAL downstream/downslope where energy is lost.** Learn the pattern once and the fifty landform names organise themselves.

## 🎬 The Organising Principle

```
   EVERY AGENT FOLLOWS THE SAME TWO-PART LOGIC

   HIGH ENERGY ZONE              →       LOW ENERGY ZONE
   (steep, fast, upstream)               (gentle, slow, downstream)
        ↓                                      ↓
   EROSION dominates                  DEPOSITION dominates
   (carving, cutting, plucking)          (dropping, building, filling)
        ↓                                      ↓
   V-valleys, gorges, potholes           Floodplains, deltas, dunes
   cirques, sea caves, yardangs          moraines, beaches, loess
```

> **Learn the LOGIC and you can derive the landforms.** An agent erodes while it has energy and deposits when it loses energy. **Everything else is naming.**

## 1️⃣ Running Water (Fluvial Landforms)

**In humid regions, which receive heavy rainfall, running water is considered the most important of the geomorphic agents in bringing about the degradation of the land surface.**

**There are two components of running water: one, OVERLAND FLOW on general land surface as a sheet; two, LINEAR FLOW as streams and rivers in valleys.**

**The gradational work of running water is greatly influenced by the volume and velocity of water.** **Most of the erosional forms created by running water are of destructional nature.**

### The three stages of a river

| Stage | Character |
|---|---|
| **YOUTH** | **Streams are few during youth with poor integration and flow over original slopes showing shallow V-shaped valleys with no floodplains or with very narrow floodplains along trunk streams.** **Streams divides are broad and flat with marshes, swamp and lakes.** **Meanders if present develop over these broad upland surfaces** |
| **MATURITY** | **Streams are plenty with good integration.** **The valleys are still V-shaped but deep; trunk streams are broad enough to have wider floodplains within which streams may flow in meanders confined within the valley.** **The flat and broad inter-stream areas and swamps and marshes of youth disappear and the stream divides turn sharp** |
| **OLD** | **Smaller tributaries during old age are few with very gentle gradients.** **Streams meander freely over vast floodplains showing natural levees, oxbow lakes etc.** **Divides are broad and flat with lakes, swamps and marshes.** **Most of the landscape is at or slightly above sea level** |

### Erosional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **VALLEYS** | **Valleys start as small and narrow rills; the rills will gradually develop into long and wide GULLIES; the gullies will further deepen, widen and lengthen to give rise to VALLEYS.** **Depending upon dimensions and shape, many types of valleys can be recognised: V-shaped valley, GORGE, CANYON** |
| **GORGE vs CANYON** | **A gorge is a deep valley with very steep to straight sides** and **a canyon is characterised by steep step-like side slopes and may be as deep as a gorge.** **A gorge is almost equal in width at its top as well as at its bottom.** **In contrast, a canyon is wider at its top than at its bottom.** **In fact, a canyon is a variant of gorge.** **Canyon commonly forms in horizontal bedded sedimentary rocks and gorges form in hard rocks** |
| **POTHOLES** | **More or less circular depressions over the rocky beds of hill-streams.** **Once a small and shallow depression forms, pebbles and boulders get collected in those depressions and get rotated by flowing water and consequently the depressions grow in dimensions** |
| **PLUNGE POOLS** | **Large, deep holes at the base of waterfalls.** **They are formed because of sheer impact of water and rotation of boulders** |
| **INCISED / ENTRENCHED MEANDERS** | **In streams that flow rapidly over steep gradients, normally erosion is concentrated on the bottom of the stream channel.** **Very deep and wide meanders found cut in hard rocks.** **Such meanders can be found in Rewa in Madhya Pradesh** |
| **RIVER TERRACES** | **Surfaces marking old valley floor or floodplain levels.** **They may be bedrock surfaces without any alluvial cover or alluvial terraces.** **When they occur at the same elevation on either side of the river, they are called PAIRED TERRACES.** **When a terrace is present only on one side with none on the other or one at quite a different elevation on the other side, they are called UNPAIRED TERRACES** |

### Depositional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **ALLUVIAL FANS** | **Formed when streams flowing from higher levels break into foot slope plains of low gradient.** **Normally very coarse load is carried by streams flowing over mountain slopes. This load becomes too heavy for the streams to be carried over gentler gradients and gets dumped and spread as a broad low to high cone shaped deposit** |
| **DELTAS** | **Deltas are like alluvial fans but develop at a different location.** **The load carried by the rivers is dumped and spread into the sea.** **If this load is not carried away far into the sea or distributed along the coast, it spreads and accumulates as a low cone** |
| **FLOODPLAINS, NATURAL LEVEES and POINT BARS** | **Deposition develops a floodplain just as erosion makes valleys.** **Floodplain is a major landform of river deposition.** **Large sized materials are deposited first when stream channel breaks into a gentle slope. Thus, normally, fine sized materials like sand, silt and clay are carried by relatively slow moving waters in gentler channels usually found in the plains and deposited over the bed and when the water spills over the banks during flooding above the bed** |
| **NATURAL LEVEES** | **Found along the banks of large rivers.** **They are low, linear and parallel ridges of coarse deposits along the banks of rivers, quite often cut into individual mounds** |
| **POINT BARS** | **Also known as MEANDER BARS.** **They are found on the concave side of meanders of large rivers and are sediments deposited in a linear fashion by flowing waters along the bank** |
| **MEANDERS** | **In large flood and delta plains, rivers rarely flow in straight courses. Loop-like channel patterns called meanders develop over flood and delta plains.** **Meander is not a landform but is only a type of channel pattern** |
| **OX-BOW LAKES** | **As meanders grow into deep loops, the same may get cut-off due to erosion at the inflection points and are left as ox-bow lakes** |
| **BRAIDED CHANNELS** | **When the lateral erosion of banks is severe, the deposition of sediments in the channel forms bars and islands, and the channel divides into many.** **The bars (calcareous sandy accumulations) and islands are known as BRAIDED CHANNELS** |

> ⚠️ **"Meander is not a landform but only a type of CHANNEL PATTERN."** NCERT says this explicitly, and it is exactly the kind of precision a Prelims statement can turn on.

> **Why meanders form — the mechanism, worth knowing:** on the **outer** bank of a bend the water flows faster, so it **erodes** (creating a river cliff); on the **inner** bank it flows slower, so it **deposits** (creating a point bar). **Erosion and deposition happen simultaneously on opposite sides of the same bend** — which is why the loop grows steadily more exaggerated until it is cut off as an ox-bow lake.

## 2️⃣ Groundwater — Karst Topography

**Any limestone or dolomitic region showing typical landforms produced by the action of groundwater through the processes of solution and deposition is called KARST TOPOGRAPHY** — **after the typical topography developed in limestone rocks of Karst region in the Balkans adjacent to Adriatic sea.**

**The karst topography is also characterised by erosional and depositional landforms.**

### Erosional (surface and subsurface) landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **POOLS, SINKHOLES, LAPIES, LIMESTONE PAVEMENTS** | **Small to medium sized round to sub-rounded shallow depressions called SWALLOW HOLES form on the surface of limestones through solution** |
| **SINKHOLES** | **A sinkhole is an opening more or less circular at the top and funnel-shaped towards the bottom with sizes varying in area from a few sq. m to a hectare and with depth from a less than half a metre to thirty metres or more.** **Some of these form solely through solution action (SOLUTION SINKS) and others might start as solution forms first and if the bottom of a sinkhole forms the roof of a void or cave underground, it might collapse leaving a large hole opening into a cave below (COLLAPSE SINKS)** |
| **DOLINE** | **Quite often the surface run-off simply goes down swallow and sink holes and flow as underground streams and re-emerge at a distance downstream through a cave opening.** **When sink holes and dolines join together due to slumping of materials along their margins or due to roof collapse of caves, long, narrow to wide trenches called VALLEY SINKS or UVALAS form** |
| **LAPIES** | **Gradually, most of the surface of the limestone is eaten away by these small pits, leaving it as a bare rock with distinct edges and ridges — this weathered lime stone surface is called LAPIE.** **Over a period of time, lapie field turns into somewhat smooth LIMESTONE PAVEMENTS** |
| **CAVES** | **In areas where there are alternating beds of rocks (shales, sandstones, quartzites) with limestones or dolomites in between or in areas where limestones are dense, massive and occurring as thick beds, cave formation is prominent.** **Caves normally have an opening through which cave streams are discharged.** **Caves having openings at both the ends are called TUNNELS** |

### Depositional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **STALACTITES** | **Hang as icicles of different diameters. Normally they are broad at their bases and taper towards the free ends showing up in a variety of forms.** **They hang from the CEILING** |
| **STALAGMITES** | **Rise up from the floor of the caves.** **In fact, stalagmites form due to dripping water from the surface or through the thin pipe, of the stalactite, immediately below it.** **Stalagmites may take the shape of a column, a disc, with either a smooth, rounded bulging end or a miniature crater like depression** |
| **PILLARS / COLUMNS** | **The stalagmite and stalactites eventually fuse to give rise to columns and pillars of different diameters** |

> **The memory hook everyone needs: stalaC**tites come down from the **C**eiling; **stalaG**mites come up from the **G**round. **When they meet, they form a PILLAR or COLUMN.**

> **The whole karst landscape is one chemical reaction, from Ch.6.** **CO₂ + rainwater → carbonic acid → dissolves limestone (carbonation).** The water carries the dissolved calcium carbonate downward; where it drips and evaporates in a cave, the mineral **precipitates back out** — building stalactites and stalagmites. **Solution above, deposition below — the same chemistry running in both directions.**

**In India, karst features are found in the **Bastar region (Chhattisgarh)**, **Borra caves (Andhra Pradesh)**, **Meghalaya (Mawsmai, Siju)**, and parts of **Uttarakhand and Himachal**.

## 3️⃣ Glaciers

**Masses of ice moving as sheets over the land (CONTINENTAL GLACIER or piedmont glacier if a vast sheet of ice is spread over the plains at the foot of mountains) or as linear flows down the slopes of mountains in broad trough-like valleys (MOUNTAIN and VALLEY GLACIERS) are called glaciers.**

**Glaciers move basically because of the force of gravity.** **Erosion by glaciers is tremendous because of friction caused by sheer weight of the ice.** **The material plucked from the land by glaciers get dragged along the floors or sides of the valleys and cause great damage through abrasion and plucking.** **Glaciers can cause significant damage to even un-weathered rocks and can reduce high mountains into low hills and plains.**

### Erosional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **CIRQUE** | **The most common of landforms in glaciated mountains.** **Deep, long and wide troughs or basins with very steep concave to vertically dropping high walls at its head as well as sides.** **A lake of water can be seen quite often within the cirques after the glacier disappears — such lakes are called CIRQUE or TARN LAKES** |
| **HORNS and SERRATED RIDGES** | **Horns form through head-ward erosion of the cirque walls.** **If three or more radiating glaciers cut headward until their cirques meet, high, sharp pointed and steep sided peaks called HORNS form.** **The divides between cirque side walls or head walls get narrow because of progressive erosion and turn into serrated or saw-toothed ridges sometimes referred to as ARÊTES with very sharp crest and a zig-zag outline** |
| **GLACIAL VALLEYS / TROUGHS** | **Glaciated valleys are trough-like and U-shaped with broad floors and relatively smooth, and steep sides.** **The valleys may contain littered debris or debris shaped as moraines with swampy appearance.** **There may be lakes gouged out of rocky floor or formed by debris within the valleys.** **There can be HANGING VALLEYS at an elevation on one or both sides of the main glacial valley.** **The faces of divides or spurs of such hanging valleys opening into main glacial valleys are quite often truncated to give them an appearance like a TRUNCATED SPUR** |
| **FJORDS** | **Very deep glacial troughs filled with sea water and making up shorelines (in high latitudes) are called fjords/fiords** |

### Depositional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **MORAINES** | **Long ridges of deposits of glacial till.** **TERMINAL MORAINES are long ridges of debris deposited at the end (toe) of the glaciers.** **LATERAL MORAINES form along the sides parallel to the glacial valleys.** **The lateral moraines may join a terminal moraine forming a horse-shoe shaped ridge.** **GROUND MORAINES: the moraine in the centre of the glacial valley flanked by lateral moraines** |
| **ESKERS** | **When glaciers melt in summer, the water flows on the surface of the ice or seeps down along the margins or even moves through holes in the ice.** **These waters accumulate beneath the glacier and flow like streams in a channel beneath the ice.** **Very coarse materials like boulders and blocks along with some minor fractions of rock debris carried into this stream settle in the valley of ice beneath the glacier and after the ice melts can be found as a sinuous ridge called ESKER** |
| **OUTWASH PLAINS** | **The plains at the foot of the glacial mountains or beyond the limits of continental ice sheets are covered with glacio-fluvial deposits in the form of broad flat alluvial fans which may join to form an outwash plain** |
| **DRUMLINS** | **Smooth oval shaped ridge-like features composed mainly of glacial till with some masses of gravel and sand.** **The long axes of drumlins are parallel to the direction of ice movement.** **They may measure up to 1 km in length and 30 m or so in height.** **One end of the drumlins facing the glacier called the STOSS END is blunter and steeper than the other end called TAIL.** **The drumlins form due to dumping of rock debris beneath heavily loaded ice through fissures in the glacier** |

> **Drumlin orientation is a favourite question: the STOSS END (blunt, steep) faces the direction the ice CAME FROM; the TAIL points the way it went.** **So a field of drumlins is a fossil record of ice-flow direction** — you can read the vanished glacier's path from the shape of the hills it left.

## 4️⃣ Waves and Currents — Coastal Landforms

**Coastal processes are the most dynamic and hence most destructive.** **Some of the changes along the coasts take place very fast.** **At the same time, it may be noted that the same coast may be subjected to deposition and/or erosion.**

**There are two broad categories of coasts: (i) HIGH ROCKY COASTS (submerged coasts) and (ii) LOW SEDIMENTARY COASTS (emerged coasts).**

### Erosional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **CLIFFS, TERRACES, CAVES and STACKS** | **Wave-cut cliffs and terraces are two forms usually found where erosion is the dominant shore process.** **Almost all sea cliffs are steep and may range from a few m to 30 m or even more.** **At the foot of such cliffs there may be a flat or gently sloping platform covered by rock debris derived from the sea cliff behind — such platforms occurring at elevations above the average height of waves is called a WAVE-CUT TERRACE** |
| **SEA CAVES → ARCHES → STACKS** | **When cavities are formed at the base of the cliff, they enlarge into sea caves.** **The hollows in the cliff, if enlarged, form sea caves. When such caves cut across the headland, sea arches form.** **When the roof of the arch collapses, a portion of the headland is isolated and forms a STACK** |

**The sequence: CLIFF → CAVE → ARCH → STACK.** **Learn it as a temporal sequence, not four separate facts** — one landform becoming the next.

### Depositional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **BEACHES and DUNES** | **Beaches are characteristic of shorelines that are dominated by deposition, but may occur as patches along even the rugged shores.** **Most of the sediment making up the beaches comes from land carried by the streams and rivers or from wave erosion.** **Beaches are temporary features.** **The sandy beach which appears so permanent may be reduced to a very narrow strip of coarse pebbles in some other season.** **Just behind the beach, the sands lifted and winnowed from over the beach surfaces will be deposited as sand dunes** |
| **BARS, BARRIERS and SPITS** | **A ridge of sand and shingle formed in the sea in the offshore zone (from the position of low tide waterline to seaward) lying parallel to the coast is called an OFFSHORE BAR.** **An offshore bar which is exposed due to further addition of sand is termed a BARRIER BAR.** **The barrier bar which get keyed up to the headland of a bay is called a SPIT.** **When the ends of a bar projects into the sea and are hooked or curved inward, they are called HOOKS.** **When such barrier bars and spits form at the mouth of a bay and block it, a LAGOON forms** |

**The lagoons would gradually get filled up by sediments from the land giving rise to a coastal plain.** **Chilika and Pulicat are Indian examples.**

## 5️⃣ Winds — Aeolian Landforms

**Winds are one of the two dominant agents in hot deserts.** **The desert floors get heated up too much and too quickly because of being dry and barren.** **The heated floors heat up the air directly above them and result in convection currents creating film of air pressure differences.** **Deserts have a great range of diurnal temperature.**

⚠️ **It is not that deserts lack rainfall, but they lack water retention.** **Winds and running water are the two dominant agents in hot deserts — and RUNNING WATER, though scarce, does more work than wind.**

> **This is a genuine correction to the popular picture, and it's examinable.** **NCERT is explicit that in deserts, running water is a more significant agent than wind**, despite the rarity of rain. When it does rain in a desert, there is **no vegetation to hold the soil and no infiltration**, so flash floods move enormous quantities of material very quickly. **Wind gets the reputation; water does the work.**

### Erosional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **PEDIMENTS and PEDIPLAINS** | **Landscape evolution in deserts is primarily concerned with the formation and extension of pediments.** **Gently inclined rocky floors close to the mountains at their foot with or without a thin cover of debris, are called PEDIMENTS.** **Once, pediments are formed with a steep wash slope followed by cliff or free face above it, the steep wash slope and free face retreat backwards.** **This method of erosion is termed as PARALLEL RETREAT OF SLOPES through backwasting.** **So, through parallel retreat of slopes, the pediments extend backwards at the expense of mountain front, and gradually, the mountain gets reduced leaving an inselberg which is a remnant of the mountain.** **That's how the high relief in desert areas is reduced to low featureless plains called PEDIPLAINS** |
| **PLAYAS** | **Plains are by far the most prominent landforms in the deserts.** **In basins with mountains and hills around and along, the drainage is towards the centre of the basin and due to gradual deposition of sediments from basin margins, a nearly level plain forms at the centre of the basin.** **In times of sufficient water, this plain is covered up by a shallow water body — such types of shallow lakes are called PLAYAS where water is retained only for short duration** |
| **SALINAS** | **The playa plain covered up by salts is called ALKALI FLATS** |
| **DEFLATION HOLLOWS and CAVES** | **Weathered mantle from over the rocks or bare soil, gets blown out by persistent movement of wind currents in one direction.** **This process of erosion is called DEFLATION.** **Where sand grains are removed, shallow depressions called BLOWOUTS form** |
| **MUSHROOM, TABLE and PEDESTAL ROCKS** | **Many rock-outcrops in the deserts easily susceptible to wind deflation and abrasion are worn out quickly leaving some remnants of resistant rocks polished beautifully in the shape of mushroom with a slender stalk and a broad and rounded pear shaped cap above.** **Sometimes, the top surface is broad like a table top and thus is called a table rock** |

> 🍄 **Why mushroom rocks have that shape** — the mechanism, which explains itself: **wind carries sand, and sand is heavy, so most of it travels close to the ground.** The lower part of a rock is therefore sandblasted far harder than the upper part. **Over time the base is eaten away while the top survives** — a narrow stem and a broad cap. **The landform is a record of where the abrasive was.**

### Depositional landforms

| Landform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SAND DUNES** | **Dry hot deserts are good places for sand dune formation.** **Obstacles to initiate dune formation are equally important.** **There can be a great variety of dune forms** |
| **BARCHANS** | **Crescent shaped dunes called barchans with the horns of the crescent facing away from the wind direction form where the wind direction is constant and moderate and where the original ground surface over which sand is moving is almost uniform** |
| **PARABOLIC DUNES** | **Form when sandy surfaces are partially covered with vegetation.** **That means parabolic dunes form when the ground surface has patches of vegetation. These are U-shaped and the horns point INTO the wind — the opposite of barchans** |
| **SEIF** | **Similar to barchan with a small difference. Seif has only one wing or point.** **This happens when there is shift in wind conditions** |
| **LONGITUDINAL DUNES** | **Form when supply of sand is poor and wind direction is constant.** **They appear as long ridges of considerable length but low in height** |
| **TRANSVERSE DUNES** | **Aligned perpendicular to wind direction.** **These dunes form when the wind direction is constant and the source of sand is an elongated feature at right angles to the wind direction** |

> **Barchan vs parabolic is the pairing that gets tested — and the difference is elegant.**
> **BARCHAN: horns point AWAY from (downwind of) the wind.** The centre is heavier and moves more slowly; the thin edges race ahead.
> **PARABOLIC: horns point INTO the wind.** Because vegetation anchors the horns while the unvegetated centre is blown forward — **exactly the reverse geometry, for exactly the opposite reason.**
> **Vegetation is the whole difference: barchans form on bare ground, parabolic dunes where there are vegetation patches.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Every agent: EROSION where energy is high, DEPOSITION where energy is lost.** Learn the logic, derive the names.
2. **River stages: youth (shallow V, broad flat divides) → maturity (deep V, sharp divides, wider floodplains) → old (free meanders, levees, ox-bows, broad flat divides).**
3. **Gorge = equal width top and bottom, hard rock. Canyon = wider at top, horizontal sedimentary beds.**
4. **"Meander is not a landform but a channel pattern."** Erosion on the outer bank, deposition on the inner — hence ox-bow lakes.
5. **Alluvial fan and delta are the same process at different locations** — foot slope vs sea.
6. **KARST (named after the Balkan region): swallow holes, sinkholes (solution and collapse), uvalas, lapies, limestone pavements, caves, tunnels.**
7. **StalaCtite = Ceiling · stalaGmite = Ground · fused = PILLAR.** **Solution above, deposition below — the same chemistry both ways.**
8. **Glacial erosional: cirque/tarn, horn (3+ cirques meeting), arête, U-valley, hanging valley, truncated spur, fjord.**
9. **Glacial depositional: moraines (terminal, lateral, ground), esker (subglacial stream), outwash plain, drumlin (stoss end blunt, facing the ice).**
10. **Coastal erosion sequence: cliff → cave → arch → stack.** **Deposition: offshore bar → barrier bar → spit → hook → lagoon** (Chilika, Pulicat).
11. **In deserts, RUNNING WATER does more work than wind** — no vegetation, no infiltration, so flash floods move enormous loads.
12. **Desert erosional: pediment → parallel retreat (backwasting) → inselberg → pediplain. Also playas, alkali flats, deflation hollows, mushroom rocks.**
13. **Barchan horns point AWAY from the wind (bare ground); parabolic horns point INTO it (vegetation anchors them).**
