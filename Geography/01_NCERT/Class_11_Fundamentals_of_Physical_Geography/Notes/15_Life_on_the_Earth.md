> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **All energy in every ecosystem on Earth entered as sunlight, and it flows in ONE DIRECTION — producers → consumers → decomposers — losing about 90% at every step.** ⭐ **Nutrients, by contrast, CYCLE endlessly. Energy flows through; matter goes round.** That single asymmetry explains ecological pyramids, food chain length and why the biosphere works at all.

## 1️⃣ ⭐ Ecology and the Ecosystem

⭐ **The relation between the organisms and their environment is studied under the discipline called ECOLOGY.** ⭐ **The term ecology is derived from the Greek word 'oikos' meaning 'house', combined with the word 'logy' meaning the 'science of' or 'the study of'.**

⭐ **Ecology is the study of the habitats of organisms.** ⭐ **Ernst Haeckel, a German zoologist, used the term ecology in 1869.**

> ⭐ **ECOSYSTEM is a system consisting of biotic and abiotic components interacting with each other.** ⭐ **It is the totality of the physical, chemical and biological factors in an area.**

⭐ **All ecosystems have four components: (i) abiotic substances; (ii) producers; (iii) consumers; (iv) decomposers.**

### ⭐ The nesting hierarchy

```
   ⭐ ORGANISM → POPULATION → COMMUNITY → ECOSYSTEM → BIOME → BIOSPHERE

   ⭐ ORGANISM   — a single individual
   ⭐ POPULATION — all individuals of ONE species in an area
   ⭐ COMMUNITY  — all populations of ALL species in an area (living only)
   ⭐ ECOSYSTEM  — community ⭐ PLUS its non-living environment
   ⭐ BIOME      — a very large ecosystem, defined by dominant vegetation
   ⭐ BIOSPHERE  — the sum of all life zones on earth
```

> ⭐ **The distinction that gets tested: ⭐ a COMMUNITY is the living component only; an ECOSYSTEM is the community PLUS the abiotic environment PLUS the interactions.** ⭐ **And a BIOME is simply a very large ecosystem, classified by its dominant plant life.**

### ⭐ Types of ecosystems

⭐ **Ecosystems are broadly divided into TERRESTRIAL and AQUATIC ecosystems.**

| Type | ⭐ Sub-types |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **TERRESTRIAL** | ⭐ **Further divided into BIOMES.** ⭐ **A biome is a plant and animal community that covers a large geographical area** |
| ⭐ **AQUATIC** | ⭐ **Divided into FRESHWATER (lakes, ponds, streams, marshes, bogs) and MARINE (oceans, estuaries, coral reefs, salt marshes)** |

⭐ **The boundaries of biomes on land are determined mainly by climate.** ⭐ **Therefore, biomes are characterised as the total assemblage of plant and animal species interacting within specific conditions.**

⭐ **These include rainfall, temperature, humidity and soil conditions.** ⭐ **Some of the major biomes of the world are: forest, grassland, desert and tundra biomes.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Food Chains and Trophic Levels

⭐ **The autotrophs (producers) and the heterotrophs (consumers) are the two basic categories.**

| Category | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **PRODUCERS (Autotrophs)** | ⭐ **Green plants which manufacture their own food through photosynthesis** |
| ⭐ **CONSUMERS (Heterotrophs)** | ⭐ **Organisms which depend on other organisms for food** |
| ⭐ **DECOMPOSERS** | ⭐ **Bacteria and fungi which break down dead organic matter and return nutrients to the soil** |

⭐ **The producers, consumers and decomposers are linked in a FOOD CHAIN.**

⭐ **Energy at each level of the food chain is called a TROPHIC LEVEL.**

```
   ⭐⭐ TROPHIC LEVELS AND THE 10% RULE

   ☀️ SUN
    │  energy captured by photosynthesis
    ▼
   🌿 T1 — PRODUCERS (green plants)             100%
    │  ⭐ ~90% LOST as heat/respiration
    ▼
   🐛 T2 — PRIMARY CONSUMERS (herbivores)        10%
    │  ⭐ ~90% LOST
    ▼
   🐍 T3 — SECONDARY CONSUMERS (carnivores)       1%
    │  ⭐ ~90% LOST
    ▼
   🦅 T4 — TERTIARY CONSUMERS (top carnivores)  0.1%

   🍄 DECOMPOSERS act at EVERY level, returning
      nutrients to the soil for the producers
```

> ⭐⭐ **The 10% rule is the single most consequential idea in ecology, and it explains several things at once:**
>
> ⭐ **Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level passes to the next.** The other 90% is lost as heat, used in respiration, or simply not eaten.
>
> ⭐ **Three consequences that follow logically — and any of them makes a strong answer:**
> 1. ⭐ **Food chains are SHORT** — rarely more than four or five links. **By the fifth level there is simply not enough energy left to support a population.**
> 2. ⭐ **Top predators are always FEW.** A forest supports thousands of deer but only a handful of tigers — **not because tigers breed slowly, but because the energy arithmetic permits no more.**
> 3. 🌱 ⭐ **A vegetarian diet feeds far more people from the same land** than a meat diet, because eating at trophic level 2 instead of 3 avoids a 90% loss. **This is a genuinely useful point for GS-III food-security answers.**

### ⭐ Two kinds of food chain

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **GRAZING food chain** | ⭐ **Starts with green plants (producers) and passes to herbivores and then carnivores.** ⭐ **Grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk** |
| ⭐ **DETRITUS food chain** | ⭐ **Starts with DEAD ORGANIC MATTER, which is consumed by decomposers and detritivores** |

⭐ **FOOD WEB: the interlocking pattern of several food chains is known as a food web.**

> ⭐ **In most real ecosystems the DETRITUS chain carries MORE energy than the grazing chain** — most plant material is never eaten alive; it dies and is decomposed. ⭐ **Decomposers are the least glamorous and most essential part of the system**, and without them nutrient cycling would stop and the biosphere would run down within a few decades.

> ⭐ **Why food WEBS matter more than food CHAINS:** a chain is a simplification. ⭐ **In reality species eat many things and are eaten by many things, so removing one species reverberates through multiple pathways.** ⭐ **This is why extinction effects are hard to predict — and the argument for protecting whole ecosystems rather than individual species.**

### ⭐ Ecological pyramids

⭐ **The trophic structure and function at successive trophic levels can be shown graphically by means of ECOLOGICAL PYRAMIDS.**

| Pyramid | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Pyramid of NUMBERS** | ⭐ **Shows the number of organisms at each trophic level.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Can be INVERTED** — e.g. one tree supports thousands of insects |
| ⭐ **Pyramid of BIOMASS** | ⭐ **Shows the total dry weight of organisms at each level.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Can also be inverted in aquatic ecosystems** — phytoplankton have low standing biomass but reproduce very rapidly |
| ⭐ **Pyramid of ENERGY** | ⭐ **Shows energy flow at each level.** ⭐ **ALWAYS UPRIGHT — never inverted** |

> ⭐⭐ **"The pyramid of energy is always upright" is a favourite Prelims statement, and the reason is thermodynamic:** ⭐ **energy is lost at every transfer and cannot be created, so a higher level can never contain more energy than the level below it.** ⭐ **Numbers and biomass can invert because they measure standing stock at a moment; energy measures flow over time, and flow can only decrease.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ Biogeochemical Cycles

> ⭐ **The chemical elements including all the essential elements of protoplasm, tend to circulate in the biosphere in characteristic paths from environment to organisms and back to the environment.** ⭐ **These more or less circular paths are known as BIOGEOCHEMICAL CYCLES.**

⭐ **Basically, there are two types of biogeochemical cycles: (i) the GASEOUS and (ii) the SEDIMENTARY cycle.**

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **GASEOUS cycle** | ⭐ **The main reservoir of nutrients is the ATMOSPHERE and the OCEAN.** ⭐ **Examples: carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, water** |
| ⭐ **SEDIMENTARY cycle** | ⭐ **The main reservoir is the SOIL and the SEDIMENTARY and other rocks of the earth's crust.** ⭐ **Examples: phosphorus, sulphur, calcium** |

> ⭐ **The distinction that gets asked: ⭐ GASEOUS cycles have an ATMOSPHERIC reservoir and are therefore FAST and globally well-mixed. ⭐ SEDIMENTARY cycles have a ROCK/SOIL reservoir and are therefore SLOW and locally variable.**
>
> 🌱 ⭐ **This has a real consequence: phosphorus, being sedimentary, cannot be replenished from the air.** ⭐ **Phosphate rock is a finite, mined resource, and "peak phosphorus" is a genuine long-term food-security concern.** **Nitrogen fertiliser can in principle be made from air; phosphate cannot.**

### ⭐⭐ The water cycle

⭐ **All living organisms, the atmosphere and the lithosphere maintain between them a circulation of water in solid, liquid or gaseous form referred to as the WATER or HYDROLOGICAL CYCLE.**

⭐ **The total amount of water on Earth does not change; it merely changes form and location.**

### ⭐⭐ The carbon cycle

⭐ **Carbon is one of the basic elements of all living organisms.** ⭐ **It forms the basic constituent of all the organic compounds.**

⭐ **The biosphere contains over half a million carbon compounds in them.** ⭐ **The carbon cycle is mainly the conversion of carbon dioxide.** ⭐ **This conversion is initiated by the fixation of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere through photosynthesis.**

⭐ **Such conversion results in the production of carbohydrate, glucose that may be converted to other organic compounds such as sucrose, starch, cellulose etc.** ⭐ **Here, some of the carbohydrates are utilised directly by the plant itself. During this process, more carbon dioxide is generated and is released through its leaves or roots during the day.**

⭐ **The remaining carbohydrates not being utilised by the plant become part of the plant tissue.** ⭐ **Plant tissues are either being eaten by the herbivorous animals or get decomposed by the micro-organisms.** ⭐ **The herbivores convert some of the consumed carbohydrates into carbon dioxide for release into the air through respiration.**

⭐ **The micro-organisms decompose the remaining carbohydrates after the animal dies.** ⭐ **The carbohydrates that are decomposed by the micro-organisms then get oxidised into carbon dioxide and are returned to the atmosphere.**

> 🌱 ⭐⭐ **The point that connects this chapter to climate change:** the carbon cycle was **approximately balanced** for millennia — as much CO₂ returned to the atmosphere as was removed.
>
> ⭐ **Fossil fuels represent carbon that was REMOVED from the cycle and locked into rock over hundreds of millions of years.** ⭐ **Burning them returns that carbon to the atmosphere in decades rather than geological time.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **Climate change is therefore best described as a DISRUPTION OF THE CARBON CYCLE, not simply "pollution."** **That framing — the cycle was balanced, we opened a locked reservoir and released it far faster than the cycle can absorb — is a far stronger opening than a generic statement about emissions.**

### ⭐⭐ The nitrogen cycle

⭐ **Nitrogen is a major constituent of the atmosphere comprising about 79% of the atmospheric gases.** ⭐ **It is also an essential constituent of different organic compounds such as the amino acids, nucleic acids, proteins, vitamins and pigments.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The atmospheric nitrogen cannot be utilised directly by the majority of the organisms.**

⭐ **Only a few forms of blue-green algae and bacteria are capable of utilising it directly.**

⭐ **The nitrogen is fixed in three ways:**

| Process | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **(i) LIGHTNING / atmospheric fixation** | ⭐ **The energy of lightning breaks nitrogen molecules and enables their atoms to combine with oxygen in the air, forming nitrogen oxides.** ⭐ **These dissolve in rain, forming nitrates that are carried to the earth's surface** |
| ⭐ **(ii) BIOLOGICAL fixation** | ⭐ **Nitrogen fixing bacteria and blue-green algae are present in the soil and water.** ⭐ **Some of these bacteria live in association with LEGUMINOUS PLANTS — in the ROOT NODULES of legumes such as peas, beans, gram** |
| ⭐ **(iii) Industrial fixation** | ⭐ **Manufacture of nitrogen fertiliser (Haber process)** |

⭐ **Plants absorb nitrates from the soil and convert them into amino acids and proteins.** ⭐ **The animals feeding on plants get their nitrogen.** ⭐ **On the death of plants and animals, DENITRIFYING bacteria convert the nitrates back into free nitrogen, returning it to the atmosphere.**

> ⭐⭐ **The nitrogen paradox is worth stating, because it makes the whole cycle memorable:**
> ⭐ **The atmosphere is 79% nitrogen. Plants desperately need nitrogen. And plants cannot use it.**
> ⭐ **They are starving in the middle of a nitrogen ocean** — because atmospheric N₂ has an extremely strong triple bond that most organisms cannot break.
>
> ⭐ **Only lightning, a few bacteria and blue-green algae, and (since 1909) industrial chemistry can break it.** 🌱 ⭐ **The invention of the Haber process is arguably the single most consequential chemical development in history** — roughly half the nitrogen in your body passed through it. **It also enabled the population growth and the fertiliser runoff/eutrophication problem that follows.**

### ⭐ The oxygen cycle

⭐ **Oxygen is the byproduct of photosynthesis.** ⭐ **It is involved in the oxidation of carbohydrates with the release of energy, carbon dioxide and water.**

⭐ **The oxygen is combined with nitrogen to form nitrates and with other minerals such as iron to form oxides.** ⭐ **The cycling of oxygen is a highly complex process.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Ecological Balance

> ⭐ **ECOLOGICAL BALANCE is a state of dynamic equilibrium within a community of organisms in which genetic, species and ecosystem diversity remain relatively stable, subject to gradual changes through natural succession.**

⭐ **It also implies a balance between production, consumption and decomposition.**

⭐ **Ecological balance may be disturbed due to the introduction of new species, sudden death or extinction of some species, natural hazards or man-made causes.**

⚠️ ⭐ **A perfect ecological balance also means that the population size of any species does not increase or decrease drastically.**

🌱 ⭐ **Human activities that disturb ecological balance:** ⭐ **rapid population growth · deforestation · industrialisation and urbanisation · pollution · overexploitation of resources · introduction of exotic species.**

> ⭐ **Note the phrase "DYNAMIC equilibrium," which is doing important work.** ⭐ **Ecological balance is not stasis** — populations fluctuate, species succeed one another, disturbance is normal. ⭐ **What matters is that the system returns toward equilibrium after disturbance rather than collapsing into a different state.** **That capacity is called RESILIENCE, and losing it — not any single species loss — is what makes an ecosystem fail.**

**Trap 2:** *"Plants absorb nitrogen directly from the air."* ⭐ **They cannot** — it must be fixed by lightning, bacteria/algae, or industry.
**Trap 3:** *"Phosphorus follows a gaseous cycle."* ⭐ **SEDIMENTARY** — its reservoir is rock, not air.

**Trap 5:** *"Food chains can be very long."* ⭐ **Rarely more than 4–5 links**, because of the 10% rule.
**Trap 6:** *"Decomposers are unimportant compared with producers."* ⭐ **Without them nutrient cycling stops** — and in most ecosystems the **detritus** chain carries more energy than the grazing chain.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Ecology — Ernst Haeckel, 1869**, from Greek *oikos* (house).
2. ⭐ **Four ecosystem components: abiotic substances, producers, consumers, decomposers.**
3. ⭐ **Hierarchy: organism → population → community (living only) → ECOSYSTEM (living + abiotic) → biome → biosphere.**
4. ⭐⭐ **The 10% rule: only ~10% of energy passes to the next trophic level.** ⭐ **Hence food chains are short (4–5 links), top predators are always few, and a plant-based diet feeds more people per hectare.**
5. ⭐ **Grazing food chain starts with living plants; DETRITUS chain starts with dead matter — and usually carries MORE energy.**
6. ⭐ **A food WEB is the realistic picture**, which is why removing one species has unpredictable effects.
7. ⭐ **Pyramid of ENERGY is ALWAYS UPRIGHT.** Numbers and biomass **can invert**.
8. ⭐⭐ **GASEOUS cycles (C, N, O, water) have atmospheric/ocean reservoirs — fast. SEDIMENTARY cycles (P, S, Ca) have rock/soil reservoirs — slow.** 🌱 **Phosphorus cannot be made from air, hence "peak phosphorus".**
9. 🌱 ⭐ **Climate change is best framed as a DISRUPTION OF THE CARBON CYCLE** — burning in decades what was locked away over geological time.
10. ⭐⭐ **Nitrogen paradox: 79% of the atmosphere, essential to life, and unusable directly.** ⭐ **Three fixation routes: lightning, biological (legume root nodules, blue-green algae), industrial.** ⭐ **Denitrifying bacteria complete the loop.**
11. ⭐ **Oxygen is a byproduct of photosynthesis.**
12. ⭐ **Ecological balance is a DYNAMIC equilibrium, not stasis.** **Resilience — the capacity to recover — is what matters.**
