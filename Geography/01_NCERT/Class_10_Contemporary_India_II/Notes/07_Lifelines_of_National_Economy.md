## 1️⃣ Roadways

**India has one of the largest road networks in the world.** **Road transport is more important than rail in India** because:
- **Construction cost is much lower than railways**
- **Roads can traverse dissected and undulating topography and steeper gradients** — so they can cross mountains like the Himalayas
- **Economical for small numbers of people and small amounts of goods over short distances**
- **Provides door-to-door service**, so loading and unloading costs are lower
- **Acts as a feeder to other modes** — it links railway stations, airports and seaports

### The six categories of road

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **GOLDEN QUADRILATERAL SUPER HIGHWAYS** | **6-lane super highways linking Delhi–Kolkata–Chennai–Mumbai–Delhi.** **North-South corridor: Srinagar to Kanniyakumari.** **East-West corridor: Silchar (Assam) to Porbandar (Gujarat).** **Implemented by the National Highway Authority of India (NHAI).** Major objective: **reduce time and distance between mega cities** |
| **NATIONAL HIGHWAYS (NH)** | **Link extreme parts of the country.** **Constructed and maintained by the Central Public Works Department (CPWD).** **The historical Sher-Shah Suri Marg is called NH-1 between Delhi and Amritsar** |
| **STATE HIGHWAYS** | **Link a state capital with district headquarters.** **Constructed and maintained by the State Public Works Department (PWD)** |
| **DISTRICT ROADS** | **Connect district headquarters with other places of the district.** **Maintained by the Zila Parishad** |
| **RURAL / OTHER ROADS** | **Link rural areas and villages with towns.** **Received special impetus under the PRADHAN MANTRI GRAMA SADAK YOJANA** — under which every village is linked to a major town by an all-season motorable road |
| **BORDER ROADS** | **Constructed and maintained by the BORDER ROADS ORGANISATION (BRO), a Government of India undertaking established in 1960.** **Develops roads of strategic importance in northern and north-eastern border areas.** **Has improved accessibility in areas of difficult terrain and helped economic development** |

**Roads are also classified by the material used: METALLED (cement, concrete, bitumen — all-weather) and UNMETALLED (go out of use in the rainy season).**

⚠️ **Road density = length of road per 100 sq km of area.** **Highly uneven: Kerala has the highest, Jammu & Kashmir the lowest.**

## 2️⃣ Railways

**The principal mode of transportation for freight and passengers.** **Railways also make it possible to conduct multifarious activities like business, sightseeing, pilgrimage and the transportation of goods over longer distances.**

**The Indian Railways is the largest public sector undertaking in the country.**

**Distribution influenced by physiographic, economic and administrative factors:**

| Region | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Northern plains** | **Vast level land, high population density and rich agricultural resources** made them **most favourable** for railways. However, **a large number of rivers requiring bridges** made it challenging |
| **Hilly terrains of the peninsula** | Railways connected through **low hills, gaps or tunnels** |
| **Himalayan mountainous regions** | **Unfavourable** — sparse population, lack of economic opportunities and technical difficulties |
| **Sandy plain of western Rajasthan, Gujarat swamps, Chhotanagpur forested tracts** | **Not favourable** |
| **Konkan railway** | **Along the western coast — has facilitated movement, though it faces problems like sinking of tracks and landslides** |

---

## 3️⃣ Pipelines

**A new arrival on the transportation map.** **Used for transporting crude oil, petroleum products and natural gas from oil and natural gas fields to refineries, fertiliser factories and big thermal power plants.** **Solids can also be transported through a pipeline when converted into slurry.**

**Advantages:** **Initial cost of laying is high but subsequent running costs are minimal. It rules out trans-shipment losses and delays.**

### India's three important pipeline networks

| Pipeline | Route |
|---|---|
| **1** | **From the upper Assam oilfield to Kanpur (UP)**, via Guwahati, Barauni and Allahabad. Branches connect **Barauni to Haldia**, via Rajbandh; **Rajbandh to Maurigram**; and **Guwahati to Siliguri** |
| **2** | **From Salaya (Gujarat) to Jalandhar (Punjab)**, via Viramgam, Mathura, Delhi and Sonipat. Branches to **Koyali (Gujarat), Chakshu and other places** |
| **3** | **The 1,700 km Hazira–Vijaipur–Jagdishpur (HVJ) cross-country gas pipeline** — links **Mumbai High and Bassein** with fertiliser, power and industrial complexes in western and northern India |

## 4️⃣ Waterways

**Waterways are the CHEAPEST means of transport.** **They are most suitable for carrying heavy and bulky goods. It is a fuel-efficient and environment friendly mode of transport.**

**India has inland navigation waterways of 14,500 km in length. Only 3,700 km of major rivers are navigable by mechanised vessels.**

### The National Waterways

| Waterway | Route |
|---|---|
| **N.W. No. 1** | **The Ganga river between Allahabad and Haldia (1,620 km)** |
| **N.W. No. 2** | **The Brahmaputra river between Sadiya and Dhubri (891 km)** |
| **N.W. No. 3** | **The West-Coast Canal in Kerala (Kottapuram–Kollam, Udyogamandal and Champakkara canals — 205 km)** |
| **Other waterways** | **The Godavari and Krishna rivers; the Buckingham Canal; the Barak river; the backwaters of Kerala; the Sunderbans; Mandavi, Zuari and Cumberjua** |

### Major Sea Ports

**India has 12 major ports and 200 notified non-major (intermediate and minor) ports. India's trade with foreign countries is carried from the ports located along the coast — 95% of the country's trade volume (68% in terms of value) is moved by sea.**

| Port | Specialisation |
|---|---|
| **Kandla (Gujarat)** | **Renamed DEENDAYAL PORT.** A **tidal port**. Developed as a major port to relieve pressure on Mumbai after Partition. Caters to convenient handling of exports and imports of highly productive granary and industrial belts of Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP |
| **Mumbai** | **The biggest port with a spacious natural and well-sheltered harbour** |
| **Jawaharlal Nehru Port (Nhava Sheva)** | **Developed as a satellite port to relieve the pressure at Mumbai. The largest container port in India** |
| **Marmagao (Goa)** | **The premier IRON ORE exporting port**, accounting for about 50% of India's iron ore export |
| **New Mangalore (Karnataka)** | **Caters to the export of IRON ORE from Kudremukh mines** |
| **Kochchi (Kerala)** | **The extreme south-western port, located at the entrance of a lagoon with a natural harbour** |
| **Tuticorin (Tamil Nadu)** | On the east coast; **has a natural harbour and rich hinterland.** Handles a wide variety of cargoes to Sri Lanka, Maldives and coastal regions |
| **Chennai** | **One of the OLDEST ARTIFICIAL ports.** Ranks next to Mumbai in terms of volume of trade and cargo |
| **Vishakhapatnam** | **The DEEPEST LANDLOCKED and well-protected port** |
| **Paradwip (Odisha)** | **Specialises in the export of IRON ORE** |
| **Kolkata** | **An inland riverine port with a tidal port. Requires constant dredging of the Hooghly** |
| **Haldia** | **Developed as a subsidiary port to relieve growing pressure on Kolkata** |

> **The three "relief" ports are a favourite pairing:** **Kandla relieves Mumbai** (post-Partition), **Jawaharlal Nehru/Nhava Sheva relieves Mumbai** (container traffic), **Haldia relieves Kolkata.** Learn them as pairs.

## 5️⃣ Airways

**The fastest, most comfortable and prestigious mode of transport.** **It can cover very difficult terrains like high mountains, dreary deserts, dense forests and long oceanic stretches with great ease.**

⚠️ **Air travel is not within the reach of the common people. It is only in the north-eastern states that special provisions are made to extend the services to the common people.**

**Air transport was nationalised in 1953.** **Pawanhans Helicopters Ltd provides helicopter services to ONGC in its off-shore operations, to inaccessible areas and difficult terrains like the north-eastern states and the interior parts of Jammu and Kashmir.**

## 6️⃣ Communication

**Personal and mass communication.** **India has one of the largest postal networks in the world.**

**Cards and envelopes are considered first-class mail and are airlifted between stations covering both land and air.** **The second-class mail includes book packets, registered newspapers and periodicals — carried by surface mail, covering land and water transport.**

**To facilitate quick delivery of mail in large towns and cities, six mail channels have been introduced. They are called RAJDHANI CHANNEL, METRO CHANNEL, GREEN CHANNEL, BUSINESS CHANNEL, BULK MAIL CHANNEL and PERIODICAL CHANNEL.**

**India has the largest telecom network in Asia after China.** **STD facility is available in almost the entire country. Uniform rates have been introduced throughout the country due to the initiative of the government to make the facility available to all.**

**Mass communication includes radio, television, newspapers, magazines, books and films.** **All India Radio (Akashwani) broadcasts a variety of programmes.** **Doordarshan is one of the largest terrestrial networks in the world.** **India publishes a large number of newspapers and periodicals annually — in about 100 languages and dialects. Hindi newspapers have the largest circulation, followed by English and Urdu.** **India is the largest producer of feature films in the world.**

## 7️⃣ International Trade

**Trade between two countries is called international trade. It may take place through sea, air or land routes.**

**The exchange of goods among people, states and countries is referred to as trade. The market is the place where such exchanges take place. Trade between two countries is called international trade.**

**Advancement of international trade of a country is an index to its economic prosperity. It is considered THE ECONOMIC BAROMETER for a country.**

**Export and import are the components of trade. The balance of trade of a country is the difference between its export and import.** **When the value of export exceeds the value of imports, it is called a FAVOURABLE balance of trade. On the contrary, if the value of imports exceeds the value of exports, it is termed as an UNFAVOURABLE balance of trade.**

**India has trade relations with all major trading blocks and all geographical regions of the world.** **Commodities exported: gems and jewellery, chemicals and related products, agriculture and allied products.** **Commodities imported: petroleum and petroleum products, gems and jewellery, chemicals, base metals, electronic items, machinery, agricultural and allied products.**

**International trade has undergone a sea change in the last fifteen years. Exchange of commodities and goods has been superseded by the exchange of information and knowledge. India has emerged as a software giant at the international level and is earning large foreign exchange through the export of information technology.**

### Tourism as a trade

**Tourism in India has grown substantially over the last three decades.** **More than 15 million people are directly engaged in the tourism industry.** **Tourism also promotes national integration, provides support to local handicrafts and cultural pursuits. It also helps in the development of international understanding about our culture and heritage.**

**Foreign tourists visit India for heritage tourism, eco-tourism, adventure tourism, cultural tourism, medical tourism and business tourism.** **There is a vast potential for the development of tourism in all parts of the country.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Roads matter more than railways in India** — cheaper to build, cross difficult terrain, door-to-door, and act as feeders to other modes.
2. **Six road categories: Golden Quadrilateral · NH (CPWD) · State Highways (State PWD) · District Roads (Zila Parishad) · Rural (PMGSY) · Border Roads (BRO, 1960).**
3. **N-S corridor: Srinagar–Kanniyakumari. E-W corridor: Silchar–Porbandar. NH-1 = Sher-Shah Suri Marg (Delhi–Amritsar).**
4. **Road density: Kerala highest, J&K lowest.**
5. **Railways favoured the northern plains** (level land, dense population, rich agriculture) but faced **river-bridging challenges**; the **Konkan railway** suffers track sinking and landslides.
6. **Pipelines: high initial cost, minimal running cost, no trans-shipment losses.** **HVJ (1,700 km) links Mumbai High and Bassein to western and northern India.**
7. **Waterways are the cheapest and most fuel-efficient.** **NW-1 Ganga (Allahabad–Haldia, 1,620 km) · NW-2 Brahmaputra (Sadiya–Dhubri, 891 km) · NW-3 West-Coast Canal, Kerala (205 km).**
8. **12 major ports; 95% of trade volume (68% of value) moves by sea.**
9. **Kandla (Deendayal, tidal) and JNPT relieve Mumbai; Haldia relieves Kolkata. Marmagao = iron ore (~50%). Vishakhapatnam = deepest landlocked. Chennai = oldest artificial.**
10. **Air transport nationalised 1953; Pawanhans serves ONGC and difficult terrain.**
11. **Six mail channels: Rajdhani, Metro, Green, Business, Bulk Mail, Periodical.** India has Asia's largest telecom network after China and is the **largest producer of feature films.**
12. **Trade is the "economic barometer." Favourable balance = exports exceed imports.** **Tourism employs 15+ million directly.**
