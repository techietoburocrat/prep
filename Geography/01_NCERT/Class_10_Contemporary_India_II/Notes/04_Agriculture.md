## 1️⃣ ⭐ Types of Farming

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **PRIMITIVE SUBSISTENCE** | ⭐ **Slash-and-burn**, on small patches, with primitive tools (hoe, dao, digging sticks) and family labour. Depends on **monsoon and natural soil fertility.** ⭐ **Called *jhumming* in NE India, *bewar/dahiya* in MP, *podu/penda* in AP, *pama dabi/koman* in Odisha, *kumari* in Western Ghats, *valre/waltre* in SE Rajasthan, *khil* in the Himalayas** |
| ⭐ **INTENSIVE SUBSISTENCE** | Practised in areas of **high population pressure**. **Labour-intensive**, high doses of biochemical inputs and irrigation. ⭐ **The 'right of inheritance' leading to land division makes holdings uneconomical** |
| ⭐ **COMMERCIAL** | **High doses of modern inputs** — HYV seeds, fertilisers, insecticides. ⭐ **The same crop can be commercial in one region and subsistence in another** — e.g. **rice is commercial in Punjab and Haryana but subsistence in Odisha.** ⭐ **Plantation** is a type of commercial farming — single crop, large area, capital-intensive, needs good transport |

⭐ **Plantation crops:** tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane, banana. **Tea in Assam and North Bengal; coffee in Karnataka.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Three Cropping Seasons

**Very frequently asked — learn months and crops together.**

| Season | ⭐ Sown | ⭐ Harvested | ⭐ Crops | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **RABI** | **Winter — Oct to Dec** | **Summer — April to June** | ⭐ **Wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard** | ⭐ **Punjab, Haryana, HP, J&K, Uttarakhand, UP** — success helped by **precipitation from western disturbances** |
| ⭐ **KHARIF** | ⭐ **With the monsoon onset — June/July** | **Sept–Oct** | ⭐ **Paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, tur (arhar), moong, urad, cotton, jute, groundnut, soyabean** | Assam, WB, Odisha, AP, Telangana, TN, Kerala, Maharashtra, UP, Bihar |
| ⭐ **ZAID** | ⭐ **Short season BETWEEN rabi and kharif — summer months** | | ⭐ **Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder crops. Sugarcane takes almost a year to grow** | |

⚠️ ⭐ **Some states grow three crops of paddy a year: *aus, aman* and *boro*** (West Bengal). A frequently asked detail.

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Major Crops — The Master Table

### Food grains

| Crop | ⭐ Conditions | ⭐ Regions / Producers |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **RICE** | Kharif. **Temp above 25°C, high humidity, annual rainfall above 100 cm.** Below 100 cm needs irrigation | ⭐ **Northern & north-eastern plains, coastal areas, deltaic regions.** Punjab, Haryana, western UP, Rajasthan grow it **with irrigation.** India is the **2nd largest producer after China** |
| ⭐ **WHEAT** | Rabi. ⭐ **Cool growing season + BRIGHT SUNSHINE at ripening. 50–75 cm annual rainfall, evenly distributed** | ⭐ **Two zones: the Ganga-Satluj plains (NW) and the black soil region of the Deccan.** Punjab, Haryana, UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, MP |
| ⭐ **MILLETS — Jowar** | ⭐ **Rain-fed, moist areas; little irrigation** | ⭐ **3rd most important food crop.** Maharashtra (largest), Karnataka, AP, MP |
| ⭐ **MILLETS — Bajra** | ⭐ **Sandy soils and shallow black soil** | ⭐ **Rajasthan (largest)**, UP, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Haryana |
| ⭐ **MILLETS — Ragi** | ⭐ **Dry regions; red, black, sandy, loamy and shallow black soils.** ⭐ **Very rich in iron, calcium, other micronutrients and roughage** | ⭐ **Karnataka (largest)**, TN, Himachal, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Jharkhand, Arunachal |
| ⭐ **MAIZE** | ⭐ **Both food AND fodder. Kharif.** Temp 21–27°C; **old alluvial soil.** In Bihar it is also grown in rabi | Karnataka, Maharashtra, MP, Telangana, Bihar, UP |
| ⭐ **PULSES** | ⭐ **India is the largest producer AND largest consumer.** Major source of protein in a vegetarian diet. ⭐ **Being leguminous, they help restore soil fertility by fixing nitrogen — except arhar** | ⭐ **MP, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, UP, Karnataka.** Tur, urad, moong, masur, peas, gram |

> ⭐ **The pulses-nitrogen point is examined and is worth understanding.** Pulses are **leguminous** — their root nodules host bacteria that **fix atmospheric nitrogen into the soil**. This is why they are grown in **rotation** with cereals: they restore what wheat and rice deplete. ⚠️ **NCERT specifies arhar (tur) is the exception.** This links to Cl.7 Ch.4 — plants can't use atmospheric nitrogen directly, so this bacterial partnership is how it enters the food chain.

> 🌾 **Millets are the underrated crop** (as in Cl.8 Ch.4). Drought-hardy, poor-soil tolerant, more nutritious than rice or wheat. ⭐ **Ragi is exceptionally rich in iron, calcium and micronutrients.** They are simultaneously a **climate-adaptation**, **nutrition** and **groundwater** strategy. **UN International Year of Millets 2023; branded "Shree Anna" in India.**

### Food and non-food crops

| Crop | ⭐ Conditions | ⭐ Producers |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **SUGARCANE** | ⭐ **Tropical AND subtropical. 21–27°C, 75–100 cm rainfall.** Can grow on many soils; needs **manual labour** | ⭐ **India is the 2nd largest producer after Brazil.** UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka, TN, AP, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana. **Source of sugar, gur (jaggery), khandsari, molasses** |
| ⭐ **OILSEEDS** | ⭐ **India is among the largest producers.** ⭐ **~12% of total cropped area** | ⭐ **Groundnut** — kharif; **Gujarat largest**. ⭐ **Mustard, sesamum (til)** — kharif in north, rabi in south. ⭐ **Linseed & mustard = rabi; sesamum = kharif in north, rabi in south; castor = both** |
| ⭐ **TEA** | ⭐ **Plantation. Tropical & subtropical. Deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in HUMUS and ORGANIC MATTER. Warm, moist frost-free climate all year. Frequent showers evenly distributed** | ⭐ **Assam, hills of Darjeeling & Jalpaiguri (WB), TN, Kerala, HP, Uttarakhand, Meghalaya, AP, Tripura.** India a leading producer and exporter |
| ⭐ **COFFEE** | ⭐ **Arabica variety, initially brought from YEMEN** | ⭐ **Baba Budan Hills** — the original site. **Karnataka (largest), Kerala, Tamil Nadu** |
| ⭐ **HORTICULTURE** | | ⭐ **India is 2nd largest producer of fruits and vegetables after China.** Mangoes (Maharashtra, AP, UP, WB), oranges (Nagpur, Cherrapunjee), bananas (Kerala, Mizoram, Maharashtra, TN), litchi & guava (UP, Bihar), pineapples (Meghalaya), grapes (AP, Telangana, Maharashtra), apples/pears/apricots/walnuts (J&K, HP) |
| ⭐ **RUBBER** | ⭐ **Equatorial crop, also grown in tropical/subtropical. Moist humid climate, rainfall above 200 cm, temperature above 25°C** | ⭐ **Kerala, TN, Karnataka, Andaman & Nicobar, Garo Hills (Meghalaya)** |

### ⭐ Fibre crops

| Crop | ⭐ Conditions | ⭐ Producers |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **COTTON** | ⭐ **BLACK SOIL of the Deccan plateau. High temperature, light rainfall or irrigation, 210 FROST-FREE DAYS, bright sunshine.** ⭐ **A kharif crop requiring 6–8 months to mature** | ⭐ **India believed to be the original home of the cotton plant.** Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP, Karnataka, AP, Telangana, Punjab, Haryana, UP |
| ⭐ **JUTE** | ⭐ **"The GOLDEN FIBRE." Well-drained fertile soils in FLOOD PLAINS where soils are renewed every year. High temperature during growth** | ⭐ **West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, Meghalaya.** Used for gunny bags, mats, ropes, yarn, carpets, artefacts. ⚠️ **Losing market to synthetic fibres and packing materials, especially nylon** |

⭐ **Four natural fibres in India: cotton, jute, hemp and natural silk.** ⭐ **Silk is obtained from cocoons of silkworms fed on mulberry leaves — called *sericulture*.**

## 4️⃣ Technological and Institutional Reforms

| Reform | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Land reforms** | **Collectivisation, consolidation of holdings, cooperation, abolition of zamindari** — the main focus of the First Five Year Plan |
| ⭐ **Green Revolution** | 1960s — HYV seeds, fertilisers, irrigation |
| ⭐ **White Revolution / Operation Flood** | Milk — Verghese Kurien |
| ⭐ **Institutional support** | **Kissan Credit Card (KCC)**, **Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS)**, crop insurance, **Grameen banks**, cooperative societies |
| ⭐ **Government support** | **Minimum Support Price (MSP)**, subsidies on inputs, **Public Distribution System (PDS)** |
| ⭐ **Media & information** | Special weather bulletins and agricultural programmes on radio and TV |
| ⭐ **Bhoodan–Gramdan** | ⭐ **Vinoba Bhave's "blood-less revolution."** After Gandhi's death he walked across India; at Pochampalli (Telangana) some farmers offered land for the landless. ⭐ **Ram Chandra Reddy offered 80 acres for 80 landless villagers — called Bhoodan.** Later some zamindars offered whole villages — **Gramdan** |

> ⭐ **The Bhoodan–Gramdan detail (Pochampalli, Ram Chandra Reddy, 80 acres) is specific enough to be a Prelims answer**, and it connects your Geography notes to Modern History and to Ethics (voluntary redistribution as a moral rather than legal instrument).

## 5️⃣ 🌱 Agriculture's Challenges

| Challenge | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Declining share in GDP** | ⭐ Agriculture's share of GDP has fallen steadily while **employment share has not fallen proportionately** — the core structural problem |
| ⭐ **Small and fragmented holdings** | Inheritance divides land; mechanisation becomes uneconomic |
| **Competition from imports** | Post-liberalisation, Indian farmers compete with subsidised foreign produce |
| ⭐ **Reduced public investment** | In irrigation, power, rural roads, market and mandi facilities |
| ⭐ **Subsidy on fertilisers decreased** | Raising input costs |
| ⭐ **Groundwater depletion, soil degradation, salinisation** | The Green Revolution's ecological bill |
| **Monsoon dependence** | ~60% of net sown area is still rain-fed |

> 🌱 **The sustainable-agriculture answer** — worth carrying as a package: **organic and natural farming, crop diversification (especially towards millets and pulses), micro-irrigation (drip and sprinkler), soil health cards, watershed development, agroforestry, and better storage and marketing (e-NAM, FPOs).** ⭐ **The goal is a second transition that keeps the Green Revolution's yields while repairing its ecological damage.**

**Trap 1:** *"All pulses fix nitrogen."* ⭐ **Except arhar (tur)** — NCERT specifies this.
**Trap 2:** *"Bajra is grown mainly in Karnataka."* ⭐ **Rajasthan.** Ragi is Karnataka's.
**Trap 3:** *"Zaid is the winter season."* ⭐ **A short summer season between rabi and kharif.**
**Trap 4:** *"Coffee's Arabica variety came from Brazil."* ⭐ **Yemen**, introduced via the Baba Budan Hills.
**Trap 5:** *"Rice can only be grown in high-rainfall areas."* Also grown with **irrigation** in Punjab, Haryana, western UP and Rajasthan.
**Trap 6:** *"Jute grows best on old alluvium."* ⭐ **Flood plains, where soil is renewed each year.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Three farming types: primitive subsistence (jhumming), intensive subsistence, commercial** — and **the same crop can be commercial in Punjab and subsistence in Odisha.**
2. ⭐ **Rabi (sown Oct–Dec, harvested Apr–Jun; wheat, barley, gram, mustard) · Kharif (monsoon; paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, cotton, jute) · Zaid (short summer; melons, vegetables).**
3. ⭐ **Rice: >25°C, >100 cm rain. Wheat: cool season + bright sunshine at ripening, 50–75 cm.**
4. ⭐ **Millets: Jowar–Maharashtra · Bajra–Rajasthan · Ragi–Karnataka.** Ragi is rich in iron and calcium.
5. ⭐ **Pulses fix nitrogen — except arhar.** India is the largest producer **and** consumer.
6. ⭐ **Cotton: black soil, 210 frost-free days, 6–8 months.** ⭐ **Jute: "golden fibre", flood plains, West Bengal.**
7. ⭐ **Coffee: Arabica from Yemen via the Baba Budan Hills; Karnataka leads.** ⭐ **Rubber: >200 cm rain, Kerala leads.**
8. ⭐ **Sugarcane: India 2nd after Brazil** — gur, khandsari, molasses.
9. ⭐ **Four natural fibres: cotton, jute, hemp, natural silk (sericulture).**
10. ⭐ **Bhoodan–Gramdan: Vinoba Bhave; Pochampalli; Ram Chandra Reddy's 80 acres.**
11. ⭐ **The structural problem: agriculture's GDP share has fallen but its employment share has not** — the definition of disguised unemployment.
12. 🌱 **The second transition:** natural farming, millets and pulses, micro-irrigation, soil health cards, e-NAM, FPOs — keeping the yields while repairing the ecology.
