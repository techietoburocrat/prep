The chapter then turns to energy, where India's central tension is that its abundant fuel (coal) is the one it most needs to abandon.

## 1️⃣ Modes of Occurrence

| Occurrence | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Igneous & metamorphic rocks** | In **cracks, crevices, faults, joints.** Smaller = **veins**; larger = **lodes**. Tin, copper, zinc, lead |
| **Sedimentary rocks** | In **beds or layers** — coal, iron ore, gypsum, potash, sodium salt |
| **Decomposition of surface rocks** | Leaves a residual mass — **bauxite** |
| **Alluvial / placer deposits** | In **valley floors and hill base sands.** **Gold, silver, tin, platinum — minerals NOT corroded by water** |
| **Ocean waters** | **Common salt, magnesium, bromine.** Ocean beds rich in **manganese nodules** |

> **The placer-deposit logic (again, because it's asked):** gold, silver, tin and platinum are **not corroded by water.** When gold-bearing rock weathers, other minerals dissolve or break down, but these survive as dense particles, are washed downstream and settle in riverbeds. **The river does the sorting for you.**

**Rat-hole mining:** in Meghalaya (Jowai, Cherrapunjee), minerals are mined by family members through **long narrow tunnels** — an unregulated and extremely dangerous practice. **Frequently asked, and a live GS-III/GS-II issue given NGT restrictions.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Ferrous Minerals

### Iron ore — the four belts

**Magnetite is the finest iron ore, with up to 70% iron content and excellent magnetic qualities. Haematite is the most important industrial ore in terms of quantity used, with 50–60% iron.**

| Belt | Where |
|---|---|
| **Odisha–Jharkhand belt** | **Badampahar** (Mayurbhanj & Kendujhar, Odisha); **Gua and Noamundi** (Singhbhum, Jharkhand) |
| **Durg–Bastar–Chandrapur belt** | **Chhattisgarh & Maharashtra.** **Bailadila hills (Dantewada)** — very high-grade haematite; **exported to Japan and South Korea via Vishakhapatnam** |
| **Bellary–Chitradurga–Chikkamagaluru–Tumakuru belt (Karnataka)** | **Kudremukh mines** — 100% export unit; **a Western Ghats biodiversity site**; ore transported as **slurry through a pipeline to Mangaluru** |
| **Maharashtra–Goa belt** | **Ratnagiri (Maharashtra) and Goa.** Ore not of highest quality but efficiently exploited; exported through **Marmagao port** |

> **Kudremukh is worth knowing from two angles at once** — it is a mineral fact *and* a conservation controversy: a 100%-export mining operation inside a Western Ghats biodiversity hotspot, whose slurry pipeline to Mangaluru was itself an environmental issue. **Mining was eventually halted.** A compact example of the minerals-vs-forests conflict.

### Manganese

**Used mainly in manufacturing steel and ferro-manganese alloy — about 10 kg of manganese per tonne of steel.** Also used in bleaching powder, insecticides, paints.

**Producers: Odisha (largest), Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Telangana.**

## 3️⃣ Non-Ferrous Minerals

| Mineral | Uses | Producers |
|---|---|---|
| **COPPER** | **Malleable, ductile, good conductor** — electrical cables, electronics, chemical industries | **Balaghat (MP), Khetri (Rajasthan), Singhbhum (Jharkhand).** India's reserves and production are **critically low** |
| **BAUXITE** | **Ore of ALUMINIUM** — combines the strength of metals with lightness, good conductivity and malleability | **Odisha (largest — Panchpatmali deposits in Koraput district)**, Amarkantak plateau, Maikal hills, Bilaspur-Katni |

> **Note the distinction UPSC likes:** **bauxite is not aluminium — it is the ore from which aluminium is extracted.** Bauxite forms by the **decomposition of a wide variety of rocks rich in aluminium silicates** (Cl.7 Ch.2's weathering process).

## 4️⃣ Non-Metallic Minerals

| Mineral | Detail | Producers |
|---|---|---|
| **MICA** | **In the form of plates or leaves; can be split into very thin sheets. Excellent di-electric strength, low power loss factor, insulating properties, resistance to high voltage** — indispensable in **electric and electronic industries** | **Chota Nagpur plateau (Jharkhand-Bihar belt — Koderma-Gaya-Hazaribagh is the leading producer), Rajasthan (Ajmer), Andhra Pradesh (Nellore mica belt)** |
| **LIMESTONE** | **Found in sedimentary rocks. Basic raw material for the CEMENT industry; essential for smelting IRON ORE in blast furnaces** | Widely distributed |

## 5️⃣ 🌱 Conservation of Minerals

**The problem:** mineral resources are **finite and non-renewable**, formed over millions of years. **The rate of formation is far smaller than the rate of consumption.** Continued extraction leads to **increasing costs as depth increases and quality decreases.**

**Measures:** planned and sustainable use, **recycling of metals**, use of **scrap and substitutes**, improved technology to allow low-grade ore use at low cost.

## 6️⃣ Energy Resources

### Conventional sources

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| **COAL** | **India's most abundant fossil fuel; provides a substantial part of the nation's energy needs.** Grades: **PEAT** (low carbon, high moisture, low heating capacity) → **LIGNITE** (low-grade brown coal, soft, high moisture — **Neyveli, Tamil Nadu**) → **BITUMINOUS** (most popular for commercial use; **metallurgical coal** used in blast furnaces) → **ANTHRACITE** (highest quality, hard coal) |
| **Coalfields** | **Gondwana coal (over 200 million years old): Damodar valley (WB-Jharkhand) — Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro; Godavari, Mahanadi, Son, Wardha valleys.** **Tertiary coal (~55 million years old): Meghalaya, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland** |
| **PETROLEUM** | **Found in anticlines and fault traps in rock formations of the tertiary age.** **Mumbai High produces 63%; Gujarat 18%; Assam 16%.** **Ankleshwar (Gujarat); Digboi, Naharkatiya, Moran-Hugrijan (Assam)** |
| **NATURAL GAS** | **Clean fuel; used as an industrial raw material in petrochemicals.** **Krishna-Godavari basin has large reserves.** Also Mumbai High, Gulf of Khambhat, Andaman & Nicobar. **The Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur (HVJ) pipeline** links Mumbai High and Bassein with the western and northern markets |
| **ELECTRICITY** | **Two types: HYDRO (from fast-flowing water, renewable) and THERMAL (from coal, petroleum, natural gas — non-renewable).** **India has over 300 thermal power plants** |

> **Coal grade sequence, learnt in one line: PEAT → LIGNITE → BITUMINOUS → ANTHRACITE**, increasing in carbon content and heating value. **Bituminous is most used commercially; anthracite is the highest quality; Neyveli in Tamil Nadu is the lignite reference.**

> **The Gondwana/tertiary distinction matters:** **Gondwana coal is over 200 million years old** and provides **metallurgical coal** — found in the Damodar valley. **Tertiary coal is ~55 million years old** and is found in the **north-eastern states.** A frequently asked pairing.

### Non-conventional sources

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| **NUCLEAR / ATOMIC** | **From altering the structure of atoms — releases heat energy.** **Uranium and Thorium, available in Jharkhand and the Aravalli ranges of Rajasthan.** **Monazite sands of Kerala are rich in THORIUM** |
| **SOLAR** | **Photovoltaic technology converts sunlight directly into electricity.** India is a tropical country with huge potential. **The largest solar plant is at Madhapur near Bhuj** — solar energy sterilises milk cans |
| **WIND** | **India is now the world's largest wind energy... a major producer.** **The largest wind farm cluster is from Nagarcoil to Madurai (Tamil Nadu).** Also Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Gujarat, Kerala, Maharashtra, Lakshadweep. **Nagarcoil and Jaisalmer are well known** |
| **BIOGAS** | **From shrubs, farm waste, animal and human waste.** **Higher thermal efficiency than kerosene, dung cake and charcoal.** **Plants using cattle dung are called "GOBAR GAS PLANTS"** — provide energy **and** improved manure |
| **TIDAL** | **Floodgate dams built across inlets.** **The Gulf of Khambhat, Gulf of Kachchh (Gujarat) and the Gangetic delta in the Sunderbans have potential** |
| **GEOTHERMAL** | **Heat from the Earth's interior.** **Two experimental projects: the Parvati valley near Manikaran (Himachal Pradesh) and the Puga Valley (Ladakh)** |

> **Nearly every one of the bolded place names above has appeared in a Prelims question**: Madhapur near Bhuj (solar), Nagarcoil to Madurai (wind), Manikaran and Puga (geothermal), Neyveli (lignite), Bailadila and Kudremukh (iron ore), Panchpatmali (bauxite), Khetri (copper), Koderma (mica). **Learn them as place–resource pairs, and locate each on the atlas.**

### 🌱 Conservation of energy

**India is one of the largest energy consumers in the world.** Sustainable development requires **judicious use**: using public transport, switching off unused electricity, using power-saving devices, and **using non-conventional sources**.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Minerals occur in veins/lodes (igneous), beds (sedimentary), residual masses (bauxite), placer deposits (gold, silver, tin, platinum) and ocean water.**
2. **Magnetite = finest (up to 70% Fe, magnetic); haematite = most used industrially (50–60%).**
3. **Four iron ore belts: Odisha–Jharkhand · Durg–Bastar–Chandrapur (Bailadila) · Bellary–Chitradurga (Kudremukh) · Maharashtra–Goa.**
4. **Manganese: Odisha largest.** ~10 kg per tonne of steel.
5. **Copper: Balaghat, Khetri, Singhbhum — India's reserves are critically low.** **Bauxite: Panchpatmali, Koraput (Odisha).**
6. **Mica: Koderma-Gaya-Hazaribagh, Ajmer, Nellore** — indispensable for electronics.
7. **Coal grades: peat → lignite (Neyveli) → bituminous (most used) → anthracite (best).**
8. **Gondwana coal (>200 mn yrs, Damodar valley) vs tertiary coal (~55 mn yrs, NE states).**
9. **Petroleum: Mumbai High 63%, Gujarat 18%, Assam 16%.** **HVJ pipeline** carries gas west and north.
10. **Thorium in Kerala's monazite sands; uranium in Jharkhand and the Aravallis.**
11. **Solar: Madhapur near Bhuj. Wind: Nagarcoil to Madurai. Geothermal: Manikaran (HP) and Puga (Ladakh). Tidal: Gulfs of Khambhat and Kachchh, Sunderbans.**
12. 🌱 **Minerals form far slower than we consume them** — hence recycling, substitutes and low-grade ore technology.
