## 1️⃣ Classification of Resources

```
   RESOURCES
      │
      ├── ON THE BASIS OF ORIGIN
      │      BIOTIC (living) · ABIOTIC (non-living)
      │
      ├── ON THE BASIS OF EXHAUSTIBILITY
      │      RENEWABLE (solar, wind, water, forest)
      │      NON-RENEWABLE (minerals, fossil fuels)
      │
      ├── ON THE BASIS OF OWNERSHIP
      │      INDIVIDUAL   — private land, houses, wells
      │      COMMUNITY    — village ponds, grazing grounds, public parks
      │      NATIONAL     — all resources within political boundaries;
      │                     oceanic resources up to 12 nautical miles
      │                       (territorial waters) + the EEZ
      │      INTERNATIONAL — regulated by international institutions;
      │                     ocean beyond 200 nautical miles of the EEZ
      │
      └── ON THE BASIS OF STATUS OF DEVELOPMENT
             POTENTIAL · DEVELOPED · STOCK · RESERVE
```

**The four development categories — a favourite distinction:**

| Category | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Potential** | Exists but not yet used | Solar and wind in Rajasthan and Gujarat |
| **Developed** | Surveyed, quality determined, **in use** | Coal in Jharkhand |
| **Stock** | Materials with potential to satisfy needs, but **we lack the technology** to access them | **Hydrogen from water** — a rich energy source we cannot yet use easily |
| **Reserve** | A **subset of stock** which **can be used with existing technology**, but use has **not yet started** | **Water in dams for hydel power**; forest reserves |

> **Stock vs reserve is the pairing that gets tested.** **Stock = we don't have the technology. Reserve = we have the technology but haven't started.** The classic example: **hydrogen in water is stock** (we can't cheaply split it at scale); **water held behind a dam is a reserve** (we absolutely could generate power from it, we just haven't yet).

## 2️⃣ Development of Resources

**The problem:** resource use driven purely by short-term greed produces **depletion, concentration in few hands, and ecological crisis** — global warming, ozone depletion, pollution, land degradation.

### Sustainable development and the Rio Summit

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit** | **June 1992** — the UN Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED) |
| **Attended by** | 100+ heads of state |
| **Purpose** | Address global environmental problems and socio-economic development |
| **Outcomes** | **Declaration on Global Climate Change and Biological Diversity**; endorsed **Agenda 21** |
| **Agenda 21** | A declaration aiming at global sustainable development, to be achieved through **local action** — hence *"Think Global, Act Local."* Signatories to prepare their own **Local Agenda 21** |

> 🎯 **Agenda 21's core aim** is worth stating precisely: **to combat environmental damage, poverty and disease through global cooperation on shared interests, mutual needs and shared responsibilities.** The number "21" refers to the 21st century.

### Resource planning in India — three stages

1. **Identification and inventory** of resources across the country — surveying, mapping, qualitative and quantitative estimation.
2. **Evolving a planning structure** with appropriate technology, skill and institutional set-up for implementation.
3. **Matching resource development plans with overall national development plans.**

> **Why India specifically needs planning — the argument, with examples:**
> - **Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh** are rich in minerals and coal but **short of infrastructure, water and industry.**
> - **Rajasthan** has abundant solar and wind potential but **lacks water.**
> - **Arunachal Pradesh** has enormous water resources but **lacks infrastructure.**
> - **Ladakh** is culturally rich but **short of water and key minerals.**
>
> **The lesson: resource endowment alone doesn't produce development.** Technology, institutions and human capital are equally necessary — **which is why some resource-rich regions remain poor and resource-poor countries like Japan prosper.** That is the "resource curse" idea in embryo, and a strong line for an Economy or GS-III answer.

## 3️⃣ Land Resources

### Relief distribution

| Relief | Share |
|---|---|
| **Plains** | **43%** — provide facilities for agriculture and industry |
| **Mountains** | **30%** — perennial rivers, tourism, ecological value |
| **Plateaus** | **27%** — minerals, fossil fuels, forests |

### Land use pattern — the key facts

| Category | Note |
|---|---|
| **Net sown area** | ~**46%** of total reporting area. **Highest in Punjab and Haryana (~80%+)**; very low in Arunachal, Mizoram, Manipur, Andaman & Nicobar |
| **Forest area** | ~**23%** — **far short of the 33% recommended by the National Forest Policy 1952** |
| **Waste land** | Rocky, arid, desert land |
| **Land put to non-agricultural uses** | Buildings, roads, industry |
| **Culturable waste land** | Left uncultivated for **more than 5 agricultural years** |
| **Fallow land** | **Current fallow** — left uncultivated for one or less than one agricultural year. **Other than current fallow** — uncultivated for 1 to 5 agricultural years |

> ⚠️ **The 33% forest target from the National Forest Policy 1952 is a standing Prelims fact** — and India is still at ~23–25%. Note the policy specified **33% overall, and 60% in hilly regions**, to prevent erosion and maintain ecological balance.

### 🌱 Land degradation and conservation

| Cause | Where |
|---|---|
| **Deforestation** | Nationwide |
| **Overgrazing** | **Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Maharashtra** |
| **Mining** | **Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, MP, Odisha** — deep scars, over-burden dumping |
| **Over-irrigation** | **Punjab, Haryana, western UP** — waterlogging and salinity/alkalinity |
| **Mineral processing** | Cement dust, ceramic industry — settles on land, retards infiltration |
| **Industrial effluents** | Water and land pollution |

**Conservation measures:** afforestation, proper grazing management, **shelter belts of plants**, control of overgrazing, **stabilising sand dunes with thorny bushes**, management of waste land, control of mining, and proper discharge/disposal of industrial effluents after treatment.

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Soils of India — The Highest-Yield Section

Soil is a **living system** — it takes millions of years to form up to a few centimetres. Formed by **parent rock, climate, relief, flora/fauna, and time** (Cl.8 Ch.2).

### The six major types

| Soil | Where | Formation | Character | Crops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ALLUVIAL** | **Entire northern plains; also eastern coastal plains, deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri** | Deposited by the **Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra** | **Most widespread and most fertile.** Rich in **potash, phosphoric acid and lime**; poor in humus/nitrogen. Sub-types: **Khadar (new, fertile)** and **Bhangar (old, kankar)** | **Sugarcane, paddy, wheat, pulses, cereals** |
| **BLACK (Regur)** | **Deccan plateau — Maharashtra, Saurashtra, MP, Chhattisgarh, and along the Godavari and Krishna valleys** | From **Deccan Trap basalt** (lava) | **Holds moisture extremely well**; rich in **calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash, lime**; poor in phosphoric content. **Develops deep cracks when dry — which self-aerates the soil** | **COTTON** ("black cotton soil") |
| **RED AND YELLOW** | **Eastern and southern Deccan plateau; parts of Odisha, Chhattisgarh, southern Ganga plain, Western Ghats piedmont** | Weathering of **crystalline igneous rock** in **low-rainfall** areas | **Red because of iron diffusion; yellow when hydrated** | Wheat, rice, cotton, pulses, millets |
| **LATERITE** | **Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Assam hills** | **High temperature + heavy rainfall → intense leaching** washes out lime and silica | **Humus removed by bacteria in high heat.** Low fertility, but responds to fertiliser. **Used as building brick** (the name comes from Latin *later* = brick) | **Tea, coffee, cashew nut** |
| **ARID (Desert)** | **Western Rajasthan** | Arid climate, high evaporation | **Sandy, saline. Low humus and moisture.** **Kankar (calcium carbonate) horizon** below restricts water infiltration | Drought-resistant crops after irrigation |
| **FOREST/MOUNTAIN** | **Hilly and mountainous areas with sufficient rain forest** | Weathering in situ | **Loamy and silty in valleys; coarse-grained on upper slopes.** Acidic with low humus in snow-covered areas | Tea, spices, tropical fruits in lower valleys |

> **Black soil's crack behaviour is worth understanding, not just memorising.** Regur is rich in clay, so it **swells when wet and shrinks when dry**, producing deep cracks in summer. Those cracks let air deep into the soil — a **natural self-aeration** and self-ploughing process. Combined with its exceptional moisture retention, this is exactly what cotton needs in a region with a marked dry season. **The soil suits the crop for a mechanical reason, not just a chemical one.**

> **Laterite is the counter-intuitive one.** It forms in the **wettest** regions — yet it is **infertile**, because the very heavy rainfall **leaches away** the soluble nutrients (lime and silica) and high temperatures let bacteria destroy the humus. **Heavy rain produces poor soil.** This is the same principle as Amazon rainforest soil (Cl.7 Ch.8), and it explains why laterite regions grow **plantation crops (tea, coffee, cashew) with added fertiliser** rather than food grains.

### Soil erosion and conservation

| Erosion type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Gully erosion** | Running water cuts deep channels → **BADLAND**; in the **Chambal basin** these are called **RAVINES** |
| **Sheet erosion** | Water flows as a **sheet** over large areas down a slope, removing the topsoil evenly |
| **Wind erosion** | Wind blows loose soil off flat or sloping land |
| **Defective farming** | **Ploughing up and down the slope** creates channels for water |

**Conservation methods:**

| Method | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Contour ploughing** | Ploughing **along contour lines** decelerates water flow down slopes |
| **Terrace farming** | Steps cut into slopes — **western and central Himalayas** |
| **Strip cropping** | Large fields divided into strips; **strips of grass left between crops** to break wind force |
| **Shelter belts** | Rows of trees planted to break wind — **contributed significantly to stabilising sand dunes in western India** |

**Trap 4:** *"Stock and reserve mean the same."* **Stock = no technology. Reserve = technology exists, use not started.**
**Trap 5:** *"The 33% forest target comes from the Forest Conservation Act 1980."* **National Forest Policy 1952.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Resources classified by origin, exhaustibility, ownership and status of development.**
2. **Stock = no technology (hydrogen from water). Reserve = technology exists, use not begun (dam water).**
3. **Ownership: individual, community, national (12 nm territorial waters + EEZ), international (beyond 200 nm).**
4. **Rio Earth Summit 1992 → Agenda 21** — "Think Global, Act Local."
5. **Resource planning has three stages: inventory → planning structure → matching with national plans.**
6. **Endowment alone doesn't produce development** — Jharkhand has minerals but not water or infrastructure; Japan has neither and prospers.
7. **Relief: plains 43%, mountains 30%, plateaus 27%. Net sown area ~46%; forest ~23% against the 33% target of the National Forest Policy 1952.**
8. **Six soils: Alluvial** (most widespread, most fertile, Khadar/Bhangar) · **Black/Regur** (Deccan basalt, **cotton**, cracks when dry) · **Red & Yellow** (crystalline rock, iron gives colour) · **Laterite** (leached, infertile, **tea/coffee/cashew**, used as brick) · **Arid** (sandy, saline, **kankar horizon**) · **Forest**.
9. **Laterite is the counter-intuitive one: heavy rainfall produces poor soil**, because leaching removes nutrients and heat destroys humus.
10. **Erosion: gully (→ ravines/badland, Chambal), sheet, wind, defective ploughing.**
11. **Conservation: contour ploughing, terrace farming, strip cropping, shelter belts.**
