## 1️⃣ 🌱 Water Scarcity

**India receives adequate total rainfall — yet faces scarcity.** Why?

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Large and growing population** | More people, more food, more water for irrigation |
| **Over-exploitation of groundwater** | Especially for irrigation; falling water tables in Punjab, Haryana, western UP |
| **Industrial and urban demand** | Industrialisation and urbanisation multiply demand; hydroelectric power |
| **Unequal access** | Water may be available but **not accessible to all social groups** |
| **Pollution** | Domestic sewage, industrial effluent, agricultural chemicals make water **unfit** — **scarcity of *usable* water, not of water** |
| **Seasonal and spatial variation** | 75% of rain in four months; Mawsynram vs Jaisalmer |

> **The distinction that matters: water scarcity is often a problem of quality and access, not quantity.** A river can flow past a village whose people have no safe water — because the river is polluted, or because a dominant group controls the tank. **"Scarcity amid plenty" is the accurate framing**, and it converts a GS-III answer from generic to precise.

## 2️⃣ Multipurpose River Projects

> **Multipurpose project** = a dam built to serve **several purposes at once** — irrigation, electricity, flood control, water supply, navigation, fisheries, recreation.

**Nehru called dams the "temples of modern India"** — because they would integrate development of agriculture, the village economy, industry and the urban economy.

### Major projects — learn river + state

| Project | River | State(s) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhakra Nangal** | **Satluj–Beas** | Punjab/Himachal |
| **Hirakud** | **Mahanadi** | Odisha |
| **Damodar Valley Corporation** | **Damodar** | Jharkhand/West Bengal |
| **Sardar Sarovar** | **Narmada** | Gujarat (also MP, Maharashtra, Rajasthan) |
| **Nagarjuna Sagar** | **Krishna** | Telangana/Andhra Pradesh |
| **Tehri** | **Bhagirathi** | Uttarakhand |
| **Rana Pratap Sagar / Gandhi Sagar** | **Chambal** | Rajasthan/MP |
| **Salal** | **Chenab** | J&K |
| **Tungabhadra** | **Tungabhadra** | Karnataka/AP |
| **Mettur** | **Kaveri** | Tamil Nadu |
| **Koyna** | **Koyna** | Maharashtra |

### The dam debate — both sides

| ✅ **The case for** | ❌ **The case against** |
|---|---|
| **Irrigation** — enabled the Green Revolution | **Regulates natural flow**, causing **excessive sedimentation at the reservoir bottom** |
| **Hydroelectric power** — clean, renewable | **Rockier stream beds and poorer habitats** for aquatic life |
| **Flood control** | **Fragments rivers**, making **fish migration difficult** — spawning is disrupted |
| **Water supply** to cities and industry | **Displacement of local communities** — large-scale, often tribal, often poorly compensated |
| **Inland navigation** | **Submergence of forest and farmland** |
| **Fisheries, recreation, tourism** | **Induced earthquakes** and waterborne diseases; pests |
| Integrated regional development | **Interstate water disputes** — Krishna–Godavari, Kaveri, Satluj-Yamuna |
| | **Have caused floods** — sediment build-up reduces reservoir capacity; 2006 Maharashtra/Gujarat floods worsened by releases from overfull reservoirs |
| | **Benefits often flow to the landowning and industrial elite**, not the displaced |

> **The most damning specific charge — worth knowing precisely:** irrigation from dams has **changed cropping patterns towards water-intensive commercial crops**, causing **salinisation of soil** and **widening the gap between landowners and landless.** **The dam intended to reduce inequality often increased it.**

### Narmada Bachao Andolan (NBA)

An NGO movement mobilising **tribal people, farmers, environmentalists and human-rights activists** against the **Sardar Sarovar Dam** on the Narmada.

- Originally focused on **environmental issues** — forest submergence.
- Later focused on **the rehabilitation of displaced people**.
- **Argued that local people should get benefits — such as full costs of resettlement and rehabilitation — from the very projects that displace them.**

> 🎯 **The Sardar Sarovar case is one of the most useful in the whole syllabus.** It appears in GS-I (geography), GS-II (governance, tribal rights, judicial review — the Supreme Court ruled on it), GS-III (environment, development) and Ethics (whose costs, whose benefits; intergenerational and intragenerational equity). **Learn it once, deploy it in four papers.**
>
> **And the balanced verdict:** the dams *did* deliver irrigation, drinking water and power to millions in Gujarat and Rajasthan. The costs *were* borne overwhelmingly by displaced tribal communities. **The question is not "were dams good or bad" but "who decided, who benefited, and who paid"** — which is precisely the framing that scores.

## 3️⃣ Rainwater Harvesting — India's Traditional Systems

**A high-value, frequently asked section.** These are region-specific, so learn them as pairs.

| System | Region | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Guls / Kuls** | **Western Himalayas** | Diversion channels for agriculture |
| **Khadins & Johads** | **Rajasthan** | Rainfed storage structures for agriculture |
| **Tankas** | **Bikaner, Phalodi, Barmer (Rajasthan)** | Underground tanks in houses, connected by pipes to rooftops. **Water stored till the next rainfall** |
| **Bamboo drip irrigation** | **Meghalaya** | 200-year-old system diverting stream and spring water via bamboo pipes |
| **Inundation channels** | **Bengal floodplains** | To irrigate fields |
| **Rooftop rainwater harvesting** | **Shillong (Meghalaya)**; also **Rajasthan** | Common household practice |
| **Gendathur, Mysuru (Karnataka)** | **Karnataka** | A village where nearly **200 households installed rooftop harvesting**, achieving near self-sufficiency |
| **Tamil Nadu** | — | **The FIRST and ONLY state in India to make rooftop rainwater harvesting structures COMPULSORY for all houses** |

> **Two facts here are near-certain Prelims material:**
> 1. **Tamil Nadu is the first state to make rooftop rainwater harvesting compulsory.**
> 2. **Bamboo drip irrigation is from Meghalaya** — a ~200-year-old system.

> 💧 **Why "tankas" matter beyond the fact:** in Rajasthan, the first spell of rain was traditionally **not collected** — it cleaned the roof and pipes. Only from the **second spell** was water stored. **A built-in filtration protocol, developed centuries before water-treatment engineering.** That detail makes the point that traditional systems were sophisticated, not merely quaint.

> 🌱 **The argument these support:** traditional harvesting was **decentralised, locally maintained, and matched to local rainfall.** Their replacement by centralised canals and borewells is a real cause of groundwater collapse. **"Reviving traditional water harvesting" is therefore a substantive policy recommendation, not nostalgia** — and this section gives you the named evidence to back it.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **India's water scarcity is about quality, access and seasonality — not total quantity.** "Scarcity amid plenty."
2. **Causes: population, groundwater over-extraction, industry/urban demand, unequal access, pollution.**
3. **Nehru: dams are the "temples of modern India."**
4. **Learn project + river + state:** Bhakra Nangal–Satluj · Hirakud–Mahanadi · Sardar Sarovar–Narmada · Nagarjuna Sagar–Krishna · Tehri–Bhagirathi · Salal–Chenab · Mettur–Kaveri · DVC–Damodar.
5. **Dams deliver irrigation, power, flood control — and cause sedimentation, blocked fish migration, displacement, submergence, induced seismicity and interstate disputes.**
6. **Irrigation changed cropping to water-intensive commercial crops → salinisation and greater inequality.** The instrument meant to reduce inequality often widened it.
7. **Narmada Bachao Andolan** — Sardar Sarovar; shifted focus from forest submergence to **rehabilitation of the displaced.**
8. 🎯 **The right framing is not "dams good or bad" but "who decided, who benefited, who paid."**
9. **Traditional systems: guls/kuls (W. Himalayas) · khadins & johads (Rajasthan) · tankas (Bikaner, Phalodi, Barmer) · bamboo drip (Meghalaya) · inundation channels (Bengal) · rooftop (Shillong, Gendathur).**
10. **Tamil Nadu is the first and only state to make rooftop rainwater harvesting compulsory.**
11. **Rajasthan's tankas discarded the first spell of rain to clean the roof** — built-in filtration, centuries before modern treatment.
