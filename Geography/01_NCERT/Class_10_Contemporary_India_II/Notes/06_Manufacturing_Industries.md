## 1️⃣ Importance of Manufacturing

> **Manufacturing** = production of goods in large quantities after processing raw materials into more valuable products.

⭐ **Manufacturing is considered the backbone of development** because:
- It **reduces heavy dependence on agriculture** by providing jobs in secondary and tertiary sectors.
- It **eradicates unemployment and poverty.**
- **Export of manufactured goods expands trade and brings in foreign exchange.**
- Countries with high manufacturing shares are **prosperous**.

⭐ **Manufacturing's share of GDP has stagnated at around 17%** over the past two decades, while total GDP grew at 5–7%. ⭐ **The desired growth rate for manufacturing is 12% per annum.**

⭐ **The National Manufacturing Competitiveness Council (NMCC)** was set up to improve productivity.

> ⭐ **The structural point worth carrying into an Economy answer:** India **skipped the manufacturing phase** — moving from agriculture largely into services (IT). Every other major economy industrialised first. **This is why "Make in India", PLI schemes and manufacturing-led job creation are persistent policy priorities:** services alone cannot absorb the workforce leaving agriculture, because IT employs relatively few people per rupee of output.

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Classification of Industries

| Basis | Categories |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Source of raw material** | **AGRO-BASED** (cotton, woollen, jute, silk textiles, rubber, sugar, tea, coffee, edible oil) · **MINERAL-BASED** (iron and steel, cement, aluminium, machine tools, petrochemicals) |
| ⭐ **Main role** | **BASIC / KEY INDUSTRIES** — supply raw material to other industries (iron and steel, copper smelting, aluminium smelting) · **CONSUMER INDUSTRIES** — produce goods for direct consumption (sugar, toothpaste, paper, sewing machines, fans) |
| ⭐ **Capital investment** | **SMALL SCALE** — ⭐ **investment up to ₹1 crore** · **LARGE SCALE** — above it |
| ⭐ **Ownership** | **PUBLIC SECTOR** (BHEL, SAIL) · **PRIVATE SECTOR** (TISCO, Bajaj Auto, Dabur) · **JOINT SECTOR** (⭐ **Oil India Ltd**) · **COOPERATIVE SECTOR** (⭐ **AMUL, sugar industry in Maharashtra, coir industry in Kerala**) |
| ⭐ **Bulk and weight** | **HEAVY INDUSTRIES** (iron and steel) · **LIGHT INDUSTRIES** (electrical goods) |

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Agro-Based Industries

### ⭐ Textiles

⭐ **The textile industry is the ONLY industry in the country self-reliant and complete in the value chain — from raw material to the highest value-added products.** It contributes significantly to industrial production, employment and foreign exchange.

| Textile | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **COTTON** | ⭐ **In ancient India, cotton textiles were produced with hand spinning and handloom weaving.** ⭐ **The first successful mill was established in MUMBAI in 1854.** Mumbai and Ahmedabad dominate — ⭐ **Maharashtra and Gujarat** — because of black cotton soil, moist climate, port facilities, capital, labour and market. ⭐ **India has a large share of world trade in cotton YARN but only 4% in garments** |
| ⭐ **JUTE** | ⭐ **India is the largest producer of raw jute and jute goods, and 2nd largest exporter after Bangladesh.** ⭐ **The first jute mill was set up near Kolkata at RISHRA in 1855.** ⭐ **Most mills are along the Hooghly in a narrow belt (98 km long, 3 km wide).** ⭐ **The National Jute Policy was formulated in 2005** |

> ⭐ **Why jute mills cluster on the Hooghly** — the classic industrial-location reasoning, and a favourite question: **proximity to the jute-producing areas, inexpensive water transport, a network of railways/roadways/waterways, abundant water for processing, cheap labour from Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal, and Kolkata as a port with banking, insurance and market facilities.** **Six factors converging in one narrow belt.**

> ⭐ **The cotton yarn vs garments contrast is the single sharpest economic point in this chapter.** India has a **large share of world trade in cotton yarn** but only about **4% in garments.** ⭐ **The reason: India exports the low-value-added product and imports back or forgoes the high-value-added one.** The weaving, processing and garment-making stages remain fragmented and under-capitalised. **This is exactly the "moving up the value chain" problem** — a strong, specific line for GS-III.

### ⭐ Sugar

⭐ **India is the 2nd largest producer of sugar but 1st in gur and khandsari.** ⭐ **Concentrated in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, AP, Telangana, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana, MP.**

⭐ **Sugar mills are shifting SOUTH to Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and AP** — because ⭐ **the cane produced there has a higher sucrose content, the cooler climate ensures a longer crushing season, and cooperatives are more successful there.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Sugarcane is a weight-losing crop** — its **sucrose content begins to dry up if harvesting is delayed or transport is slow.** **This is why mills must be near the cane fields**, and it is the textbook example of raw-material-oriented location.

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ Mineral-Based Industries

### ⭐ Iron and Steel

⭐ **The basic industry — all other industries depend on it** for machinery, construction, transport and defence.

| Fact | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Ratio of raw materials** | **Iron ore, coking coal and limestone in the ratio of approximately 4 : 2 : 1** |
| ⭐ **Production rank** | India is among the **largest crude steel producers**, but ⭐ **per capita consumption is low** |
| ⭐ **Integrated steel plants** | ⭐ **Bhilai (Chhattisgarh), Durgapur (WB), Burnpur (WB), Rourkela (Odisha), Bokaro (Jharkhand), Jamshedpur/TISCO (Jharkhand), Vishakhapatnam (AP), Vijaynagar (Karnataka), Salem (TN)** |
| ⭐ **Concentration** | ⭐ **Chhotanagpur plateau region** — because of low-cost iron ore, high-grade raw materials in proximity, cheap labour and a vast home market |

⭐ **Problems of the iron and steel industry:** **high costs and limited availability of coking coal, lower productivity of labour, irregular energy supply, and poor infrastructure.**

> ⭐ **Why Vishakhapatnam and Vijaynagar differ from the rest** — a useful nuance. Most Indian steel plants sit in the Chhotanagpur belt, at the raw material. ⭐ **Vishakhapatnam is a port-based plant** — it can import coking coal, which India is short of. **The location logic shifts from "near the ore" to "near the port" once a key input has to be imported.** That is the weight-losing/weight-gaining principle from Cl.8 Ch.5 in action.

### ⭐ Aluminium Smelting

⭐ **The 2nd most important metallurgical industry in India.** ⭐ **Light, resistant to corrosion, a good conductor of heat, malleable, and becomes strong when mixed with other metals.** Used to replace steel, copper, zinc and lead in many industries.

⭐ **Bauxite is the raw material — a bulky, dark reddish rock.** ⭐ **Regular supply of electricity and an assured source of raw material at minimum cost are the two prime factors for location.** ⭐ **Odisha is the largest producer.**

### ⭐ Chemical Industries

⭐ **Fast-growing; contributes ~3% of GDP. Third largest in Asia; 12th in the world by size.** ⭐ **Comprises both large and small scale.** ⭐ **The chemical industry is its own largest consumer.**

| Type | Products |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Inorganic chemicals** | Sulphuric acid (fertilisers, synthetic fibres, plastics), nitric acid, alkalies, soda ash (glass, soaps, detergents, paper), caustic soda |
| ⭐ **Organic chemicals** | Petrochemicals — synthetic fibres, synthetic rubber, plastics, dye-stuffs, drugs, pharmaceuticals. ⭐ **Organic chemical plants are located near oil refineries or petrochemical plants** |

### ⭐ Fertiliser Industry

⭐ **Centred on nitrogenous fertilisers (mainly urea), phosphatic fertilisers, ammonium phosphate (DAP) and complex fertilisers.** ⭐ **Note: India lacks mineral deposits of commercial potash/potassium compounds — so potash is entirely imported.**

⭐ **After the Green Revolution the industry expanded in several parts.** Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, UP, Punjab and Kerala contribute about half of India's fertiliser production.

### ⭐ Cement Industry

⭐ **Essential for construction — houses, factories, bridges, roads, airports, dams.** ⭐ **Requires bulky and heavy raw materials: limestone, silica, alumina and gypsum. Coal and electric power are needed apart from rail transport.**

⭐ **The industry has strategically located plants in Gujarat, which has suitable access to the market in the Gulf countries.** ⭐ **The first plant was set up in Chennai in 1904.**

### ⭐ Automobile and IT

| Industry | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **AUTOMOBILE** | ⭐ **After liberalisation, new and contemporary models stimulated demand.** Located around **Delhi, Gurugram, Mumbai, Pune, Chennai, Kolkata, Lucknow, Indore, Hyderabad, Jamshedpur, Bengaluru** |
| ⭐ **IT AND ELECTRONICS** | ⭐ **Bengaluru is the electronic capital of India.** Other centres: Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Kolkata, Lucknow, Coimbatore. ⭐ **18 software technology parks provide single-window service and high data communication facility.** ⭐ **A major impact has been on employment generation — up to 31 March 2005 over one million people were employed; ⭐ 30% of the people employed in this sector are WOMEN.** ⭐ **This industry has been a major foreign exchange earner due to its fast-growing Business Processes Outsourcing (BPO) sector** |

## 5️⃣ 🌱 Industrial Pollution and Environmental Degradation

⭐ **Industry is responsible for four types of pollution: AIR, WATER, LAND and NOISE.**

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **AIR POLLUTION** | ⭐ **Caused by the presence of high proportions of undesirable gases such as sulphur dioxide and carbon monoxide.** ⭐ **Airborne particulate materials contain both solid and liquid particles — dust, sprays, mist and smoke.** ⭐ **Smoke is emitted by chemical and paper factories, brick kilns, refineries and smelting plants, and burning fossil fuels.** ⭐ **Toxic gas leaks can be REALLY DISASTROUS with long-term effects** |
| ⭐ **WATER POLLUTION** | ⭐ **Organic and inorganic industrial wastes and effluents discharged into rivers.** ⭐ **The main culprits: paper, pulp, chemical, textile and dyeing, petroleum refineries, tanneries and electroplating industries** that let out dyes, detergents, acids, salts, heavy metals like **lead and mercury**, pesticides, fertilisers, synthetic chemicals with carbon, plastics and rubber. ⭐ **Fly ash, phosphogypsum and iron and steel slags are the major solid wastes** |
| ⭐ **THERMAL POLLUTION** | ⭐ **Occurs when hot water from factories and thermal plants is drained into rivers and ponds before cooling.** ⭐ **Adversely affects aquatic life** |
| ⭐ **NOISE POLLUTION** | ⭐ **Causes irritation and anger, can cause hearing impairment, increased heart rate and blood pressure.** ⭐ **Sources: industrial and construction machinery, factory equipment, generators, saws, pneumatic and electric drills** |

⭐ **Radioactive waste from nuclear plants** causes cancers, birth defects and miscarriages. **Soil and water pollution are closely related** — dumping wastes on land makes it useless, and rain washes pollutants down to contaminate groundwater.

### ⭐ Control of environmental degradation — the measures list

| Measure | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Minimise water use** | **Reusing and recycling water in two or more successive stages** |
| ⭐ **Rainwater harvesting** | To meet water requirements |
| ⭐ **Treat hot water and effluents** | **Before releasing into rivers and ponds** |
| ⭐ **Three-stage effluent treatment** | ⭐ **(a) PRIMARY treatment by mechanical means — screening, grinding, flocculation and sedimentation.** ⭐ **(b) SECONDARY treatment by biological process.** ⭐ **(c) TERTIARY treatment by biological, chemical and physical processes — recycling of wastewater** |
| ⭐ **Groundwater** | **Overdrawing must be legally regulated** |
| ⭐ **Particulate matter** | **Smoke can be reduced by fitting smoke stacks to factories with electrostatic precipitators, fabric filters, scrubbers and inertial separators** |
| ⭐ **Smoke** | **Use oil or gas instead of coal in factories** |
| ⭐ **Noise** | **Machinery and equipment can be fitted with silencers; noise-absorbing material; generators should be fitted with silencers; earplugs and earphones for workers** |

> ⭐ **The NTPC example is worth knowing** — NCERT holds it up as a model of environmentally responsible industry: **NTPC is a major power provider with an ISO 14001 certification for environmental management.** Its proactive approach includes **optimum utilisation of equipment, minimising waste generation, providing green belts, reducing environmental pollution through ash pond management and ash water recycling, ecological monitoring, and afforestation.**

> 🌱 **The Bhopal link:** Cl.8 Ch.5 covers the disaster itself — **2–3 December 1984, methyl isocyanate, the world's worst industrial disaster** — and the legislation it produced (**Environment Protection Act 1986**, later the **NGT 2010**). **Read the two chapters together for a complete industrial-pollution answer.**

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Manufacturing** | Production of goods in large quantities from raw materials |
| ⭐ **Basic / key industry** | Supplies raw material to other industries (iron and steel) |
| ⭐ **Consumer industry** | Produces goods for direct consumption |
| ⭐ **Agglomeration economies** | Benefits firms gain by locating near each other |
| ⭐ **NMCC** | National Manufacturing Competitiveness Council |
| ⭐ **Integrated steel plant** | Handles everything from raw material to finished steel |
| ⭐ **Mini steel plant** | Smaller, uses steel scrap and sponge iron; electric furnaces |
| ⭐ **Thermal pollution** | Hot water discharged into water bodies, harming aquatic life |
| ⭐ **Particulate matter** | Airborne solid and liquid particles |
| ⭐ **Electrostatic precipitator / scrubber** | Devices to reduce smoke and particulates |
| ⭐ **Fly ash / phosphogypsum / slag** | Major industrial solid wastes |
| **BPO** | Business Process Outsourcing |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Manufacturing is stuck at ~17% of GDP; target growth is 12% p.a.** India skipped the manufacturing phase — the root of its jobs problem.
2. ⭐ **Five classification bases: raw material, role, capital, ownership, bulk.**
3. ⭐ **First cotton mill Mumbai 1854; first jute mill Rishra 1855; first cement plant Chennai 1904.**
4. ⭐ **Textiles are the only fully self-reliant value chain** — yet India has a large share in cotton **yarn** and only ~4% in **garments.** The value-chain problem in one statistic.
5. ⭐ **Jute mills cluster on the Hooghly** — cane proximity, water transport, water for processing, cheap labour, rail links, Kolkata's port and banking.
6. ⭐ **Sugar: 2nd in sugar, 1st in gur/khandsari. Mills shifting south** — higher sucrose, longer crushing season, better cooperatives. **Cane is weight-losing, so mills must be near the fields.**
7. ⭐ **Iron ore : coking coal : limestone = 4 : 2 : 1.** Plants concentrate on the **Chhotanagpur plateau**; **Vishakhapatnam is port-based** because coking coal is imported.
8. ⭐ **Aluminium is the 2nd metallurgical industry** — needs cheap power and assured bauxite; **Odisha largest.**
9. ⭐ **Chemical industry is its own largest consumer.** ⭐ **Potash is entirely imported.**
10. ⭐ **Bengaluru is the electronic capital; 18 software technology parks; ~30% of IT employees are women; BPO is a major forex earner.**
11. 🌱 ⭐ **Four pollution types: air, water, land, noise — plus thermal and radioactive.**
12. 🌱 ⭐ **Three-stage effluent treatment: primary (mechanical) → secondary (biological) → tertiary (biological, chemical, physical + recycling).** **NTPC (ISO 14001) is the model.**
