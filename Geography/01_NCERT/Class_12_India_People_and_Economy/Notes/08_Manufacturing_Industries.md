## 1️⃣ ⭐ Types of Industries

⭐ **Industries are classified on the basis of SIZE, CAPITAL INVESTMENT and LABOUR FORCE; on the basis of RAW MATERIAL; on the basis of OWNERSHIP; and on the basis of the NATURE OF THE MANUFACTURED PRODUCTS.**

| Basis | ⭐ Categories |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **By raw material** | ⭐ **AGRO-BASED (cotton, woollen, jute, silk textile, rubber, sugar, tea, coffee, edible oil) · MINERAL-BASED (iron and steel, cement, aluminium, machine tools, petrochemicals)** |
| ⭐ **By output/product** | ⭐ **BASIC (whose products feed other industries — iron and steel) · CONSUMER GOODS (direct consumption — sugar, toothpaste, paper, sewing machines, fans)** |
| ⭐ **By size** | ⭐ **SMALL SCALE · LARGE SCALE** |
| ⭐ **By ownership** | ⭐ **PUBLIC SECTOR (BHEL, SAIL) · PRIVATE SECTOR (TISCO, Bajaj Auto, Dabur) · JOINT SECTOR (Oil India Ltd) · COOPERATIVE SECTOR (AMUL, sugar mills in Maharashtra, coir in Kerala)** |

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Major Industries

### ⭐⭐ Iron and Steel Industry

⭐ **The iron and steel industry is a BASIC INDUSTRY since all other industries — heavy, medium and light, depend on it for their machinery.**

⭐ **Steel is needed to manufacture a variety of engineering goods, construction material, defence, medical, telephonic, scientific equipment and a variety of consumer goods.**

⭐ **Production and consumption of steel is often regarded as the index of a country's development.**

⭐ **The iron and steel industry is a HEAVY INDUSTRY because all the raw materials as well as finished goods are heavy and bulky entailing heavy transportation costs.**

⭐ **IRON ORE, COKING COAL and LIMESTONE are required roughly in the ratio 4 : 2 : 1.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Some quantities of manganese, are also required to harden the steel.**

⭐ **Since the weight loss of raw material is very high in the process of steel manufacturing, the location of the steel plant is generally decided on the basis of PROXIMITY OF RAW MATERIAL.**

### ⭐ The integrated steel plants

| Plant | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **TISCO (Tata Iron and Steel Company), JAMSHEDPUR** | ⭐ **Set up in 1907 at SAKCHI, near the confluence of the rivers SUBARNAREKHA and KHARKAI in Jharkhand.** ⭐ **Sakchi was chosen for its proximity to iron ore, coal and manganese deposits as well as its proximity to Kolkata, which provided a large market.** ⭐ **TISCO gets coal from Joda mines in Odisha, and water from the Subarnarekha and Kharkai rivers.** ⭐ **TISCO is the only steel plant in the PRIVATE SECTOR** |
| ⭐ **IISCO (Indian Iron and Steel Company)** | ⭐ **Set up its first factory at HIRAPUR and later on another at KULTI.** ⭐ **In 1937, the Steel Corporation of Bengal was constituted in association with IISCO and set up another iron and steel producing unit at BURNPUR (West Bengal).** ⭐ **All the three plants under IISCO are located very close to Damodar valley coal fields (Raniganj, Jharia, and Ramgarh).** ⭐ **Iron ore comes from Singhbhum in Jharkhand.** ⭐ **Water is obtained from the Barakar River, a tributary of the Damodar** |
| ⭐ **VISVESVARAYA IRON AND STEEL WORKS LTD (VISL)** | ⭐ **The third integrated steel plant, initially called the MYSORE IRON AND STEEL WORKS, is located close to an iron ore producing area of KEMANGUNDI in the BABA BUDAN HILLS.** ⭐ **Limestone and manganese are also locally available.** ⭐ **But this region has NO COAL.** ⭐ **At the initial stage, CHARCOAL obtained by burning wood from nearby forests was used as fuel till 1951.** ⭐ **Afterwards, electric furnaces were installed which use hydroelectricity from the Jog Falls hydel power project** |
| ⭐ **ROURKELA STEEL PLANT** | ⭐ **Set up in 1959 in the SUNDARGARH district of Odisha in collaboration with GERMANY.** ⭐ **The plant was located on the basis of proximity to raw materials, thus, minimising the cost of transporting weight losing raw material.** ⭐ **This plant has a unique locational advantage, as it receives coal from Jharia and hydel power from the Hirakud project** |
| ⭐ **BHILAI STEEL PLANT** | ⭐ **Established with the RUSSIAN collaboration in DURG District of Chhattisgarh and started production in 1959.** ⭐ **The iron ore comes from Dalli-Rajhara mine, coal comes from Korba and Kargali coal fields and the water comes from the Tanduladam.** ⭐ **The entire product is supplied to the Hindustan Shipyard at Vishakhapatnam** |
| ⭐ **DURGAPUR STEEL PLANT** | ⭐ **Set up in West Bengal, in collaboration with the government of the UNITED KINGDOM and started production in 1962** |
| ⭐ **BOKARO STEEL PLANT** | ⭐ **Set up in 1964 at Bokaro with RUSSIAN collaboration.** ⭐ **This plant was set up on the principle of "TRANSPORTATION COST MINIMISATION" by creating Bokaro-Rourkela combine.** ⭐ **It receives iron ore from the Rourkela region and the wagons which take coal from Bokaro get iron ore to Rourkela** |
| ⭐ **Other steel plants** | ⭐ **VIJAYNAGAR (Karnataka) using indigenous technology; SALEM (Tamil Nadu); VISHAKHAPATNAM (Andhra Pradesh) — the first PORT-BASED plant** |

> ⭐⭐ **The foreign-collaboration pairings are near-certain Prelims material:**
> ⭐ **ROURKELA — GERMANY (1959)**
> ⭐ **BHILAI — RUSSIA/USSR (1959)**
> ⭐ **DURGAPUR — UNITED KINGDOM (1962)**
> ⭐ **BOKARO — RUSSIA/USSR (1964)**
>
> ⭐ **And note the elegant logic of BOKARO: it was set up on the principle of ⭐ "TRANSPORTATION COST MINIMISATION" via the BOKARO-ROURKELA COMBINE.** ⭐ **Wagons carrying coal from Bokaro to Rourkela return carrying iron ore from Rourkela to Bokaro.** ⭐ **Neither leg runs empty.** **A genuinely clever piece of industrial planning, and a memorable example.**

> ⭐ **VISL is the instructive exception: ⭐ it had iron ore, limestone and manganese locally BUT NO COAL.** ⭐ **So it used CHARCOAL from nearby forests until 1951, then switched to electric furnaces powered by JOG FALLS hydel power.** ⭐ **When you lack one input, you substitute the process** — the south-western belt's coal absence (Ch.7) forced a technological workaround.

### ⭐⭐ Cotton Textile Industry

⭐ **Cotton textile industry has THREE SUB-SECTORS i.e. HANDLOOM, POWERLOOM and MILL SECTOR.**

⭐ **Handloom sector is LABOUR-INTENSIVE and provides employment to semi-skilled workers.** ⭐ **It requires small capital investment.**

⭐ **The powerloom sector introduces better technology.** ⭐ **The mill sector is CAPITAL INTENSIVE and produces fine and superfine varieties.**

⭐ **The first modern cotton mill was established in 1854 in MUMBAI.**

⭐ **This city had several advantages as a cotton textile manufacturing centre.** ⭐ **It was very close to the cotton producing areas of Gujarat and Maharashtra.** ⭐ **Raw cotton used to be brought to Mumbai port to be transported to England.** ⭐ **Moreover, Mumbai even then was the financial centre and the capital needed to start an industry was available there.**

⭐ **As a large town, providing employment, it attracted the labour in large number.** ⭐ **Hence, cheap and abundant labour too was available locally.** ⭐ **The machinery required for a cotton textile mill could be directly imported from England.**

⭐ **Moreover, Mumbai's humid climate too was well-suited for spinning and weaving.**

⭐ **Cotton textile industry in India can be broadly divided into TWO CATEGORIES: MILL SECTOR and DECENTRALISED SECTOR (powerlooms and handlooms).**

⚠️ ⭐ **This industry has close links with agriculture and provides a living to farmers, cotton boll pluckers and workers engaged in ginning, spinning, weaving, dyeing, designing, packaging, tailoring and sewing.**

⭐ **It supports many other industries such as chemicals and dyes, mill stores, packaging materials and engineering works.**

### ⭐ Sugar Industry

⭐ **The sugar industry is the SECOND LARGEST agro-based industry in the country.**

⭐ **India is the largest producer of both sugarcane and cane sugar and contributes about 8 per cent of the total sugar production in the world.**

⭐ **Besides, khandsari and gur or jaggery are also prepared from sugarcane.**

⭐ **This industry provides employment for more than 4 lakh persons directly and a large number of farmers indirectly.**

⭐ **Sugar industry is a SEASONAL INDUSTRY because of the seasonality of raw materials.**

⭐ **Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Gujarat are the major producers.**

⚠️ ⭐ **In recent years, there is a tendency for the industry to SHIFT AND CONCENTRATE IN THE SOUTHERN AND WESTERN STATES, especially in MAHARASHTRA.**

⭐ **This is because the cane produced here has a HIGHER SUCROSE CONTENT.** ⭐ **The cooler climate also ensures a LONGER CRUSHING SEASON.** ⭐ **Moreover, the COOPERATIVES are more successful in these states.**

> ⭐ **Sugarcane is the classic WEIGHT-LOSING, PERISHABLE raw material: ⭐ its SUCROSE CONTENT BEGINS TO DRY UP if harvesting is delayed or transport is slow.** ⭐ **So mills must be near the fields** — which is why the industry is dispersed across the cane belt rather than concentrated.
>
> ⭐ **The three reasons for the southward shift are examinable: ⭐ HIGHER SUCROSE CONTENT · LONGER CRUSHING SEASON (cooler climate) · MORE SUCCESSFUL COOPERATIVES.**

### ⭐ Petrochemical Industries

⭐ **This group of industries has been growing fast.**

⭐ **A variety of items are produced in this industry — POLYMERS, SYNTHETIC FIBRES, ELASTOMERS and SURFACTANT INTERMEDIATE.**

⭐ **Mumbai is the hub of the petrochemical industries.**

⭐ **Cracker units are also located in Auraiya (Uttar Pradesh), Jamnagar, Gandhinagar and Hazira (Gujarat), Nagothane, Ratnagiri (Maharashtra), Haldia (West Bengal) and Vishakhapatnam (Andhra Pradesh).**

⭐ **Three organisations are working in the petrochemical sector under the administrative control of the Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals:**
- ⭐ **INDIAN PETROCHEMICAL CORPORATION LIMITED (IPCL)** — a public sector undertaking
- ⭐ **PETROFILS COOPERATIVE LIMITED (PCL)** — a joint venture of the Government of India and Gujarat State Cooperative Ltd.
- ⭐ **CENTRAL INSTITUTE OF PLASTIC ENGINEERING AND TECHNOLOGY (CIPET)** — a national institute

### ⭐⭐ Knowledge-Based Industries

⭐ **The advancement in information technology has had a profound influence on the country's economy.**

⭐ **The Information Technology (IT) revolution opened up new possibilities of economic and social transformation.**

⭐ **The IT and IT enabled business process outsourcing (ITES-BPO) services continue to be on a robust growth path.**

⭐ **Indian software industry has emerged as one of the fastest growing sectors in the economy.**

⭐ **The Software Technology Park of India came into existence in 1991.** ⭐ **The STPs provide single window service and high data communication facility to software experts.**

⚠️ ⭐ **A major impact of this growth has been on employment created.** ⭐ **The IT software and services industry account for a large number of jobs.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Another notable feature is the growing number of WOMEN employees.** ⭐ **The IT-BPO sector employs a significant proportion of women.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ Industrial Regions in India

⭐ **Industries are not evenly distributed in the country.**

⭐ **They tend to concentrate at certain locations because of the favourable locational factors.**

⭐ **INDUSTRIAL REGIONS emerge when a number of industries locate close to each other and share the benefits of their closeness and enjoy the AGGLOMERATION ECONOMIES.**

⭐ **India has EIGHT MAJOR INDUSTRIAL REGIONS:**

| Region | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. MUMBAI-PUNE REGION** | ⭐ **The opening of the Suez Canal in 1869 gave a great impetus to the growth of Mumbai as a port.** ⭐ **Cotton textile industry developed here.** ⭐ **Machinery, engineering, petrochemicals, chemicals, electronics, films |
| ⭐ **2. HUGLI REGION** | ⭐ **Extending along the Hugli river.** ⭐ **KOLKATA-HAORA form the nucleus.** ⭐ **Jute industry is the dominant one.** ⭐ **Cotton textiles, paper, engineering, chemicals |
| ⭐ **3. BANGALORE-TAMIL NADU REGION** | ⭐ **Rapid industrial development.** ⭐ **This region is away from the coal fields.** ⭐ **Its development is dependent on the HYDROELECTRICITY from Pykara.** ⭐ **Cotton textiles, aircraft, machine tools, telephones, IT |
| ⭐ **4. GUJARAT REGION** | ⭐ **The nucleus of this region lies between AHMEDABAD and VADODARA.** ⭐ **Cotton textiles, petrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, dairy |
| ⭐ **5. CHOTANAGPUR REGION** | ⭐ **This region extends over Jharkhand, northern Odisha and western West Bengal.** ⭐ **HEAVY METALLURGICAL INDUSTRIES — iron and steel |
| ⭐ **6. VISHAKHAPATNAM-GUNTUR REGION** | ⭐ **Extends from Vishakhapatnam district to Kurnool and Prakasam districts in the south.** ⭐ **Shipbuilding, petroleum refinery, steel |
| ⭐ **7. GURUGRAM-DELHI-MEERUT REGION** | ⭐ **Located far away from the mineral and power resources.** ⭐ **The industries are LIGHT AND MARKET-ORIENTED.** ⭐ **Electronics, light engineering, electrical goods, cotton, woollen, synthetic fabrics |
| ⭐ **8. KOLLAM-THIRUVANANTHAPURAM REGION** | ⭐ **This industrial region is spread over Thiruvananthapuram, Kollam, Alwaye, Ernakulam and Allapuzha districts.** ⭐ **PLANTATION AGRICULTURE and HYDROPOWER provide the industrial base.** ⭐ **Agricultural processing and market-oriented light industries |

> ⭐⭐ **Two regions demonstrate the shift in location logic and are worth contrasting:**
> ⭐ **CHOTANAGPUR is the RAW-MATERIAL region** — heavy metallurgy located on the mineral belt.
> ⭐ **GURUGRAM-DELHI-MEERUT is the MARKET region** — ⭐ **"located far away from the mineral and power resources," with LIGHT and MARKET-ORIENTED industries.**
>
> ⭐ **BANGALORE-TAMIL NADU is the third model: ⭐ "away from the coal fields," developed on HYDROELECTRICITY** — and later on skilled labour for IT.
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **Three regions, three location logics: raw material, market, and power-plus-skill.** **That framing organises the whole list.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ Industrial Policy and Liberalisation

⭐ **The NEW INDUSTRIAL POLICY was announced in 1991.**

⭐ **The major objectives of this policy were to build on the gains already made, correct the distortions or weaknesses that have crept in, maintain a sustained growth in productivity and gainful employment and attain international competitiveness.**

⭐ **The measures initiated:**

| Measure | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **ABOLITION OF INDUSTRIAL LICENSING** | ⭐ **Industrial licensing was abolished for all project except for a short list of industries** |
| ⭐ **FREE ENTRY TO FOREIGN TECHNOLOGY** | ⭐ **Automatic permission for foreign technology agreements in high priority industries** |
| ⭐ **FOREIGN INVESTMENT POLICY** | ⭐ **Automatic approval for direct foreign investment up to 51 per cent equity in high priority industries** |
| ⭐ **ACCESS TO CAPITAL MARKET** | ⭐ **Liberalised access to capital markets |
| ⭐ **OPEN TRADE** | ⭐ **Reduction of tariffs and removal of quantitative restrictions |
| ⭐ **ABOLITION OF PHASED MANUFACTURING PROGRAMME** | ⭐ **Which had required progressive indigenisation |
| ⭐ **LIBERALISED INDUSTRIAL LOCATION PROGRAMME** | ⭐ **Industries could locate more freely |

⭐ **The policy has three dimensions: LIBERALISATION, PRIVATISATION and GLOBALISATION (LPG).**

⚠️ ⭐ **The industrial licensing was abolished, and the role of the public sector was reduced.**

> ⭐⭐ **The 1991 reforms are conventionally summarised as ⭐ LPG — LIBERALISATION, PRIVATISATION, GLOBALISATION.** ⭐ **From the geography point of view, the most consequential change was the ⭐ LIBERALISED INDUSTRIAL LOCATION POLICY** — industries could locate where economics dictated rather than where licensing permitted.
>
> 🌱 ⭐ **The consequence, worth stating in a GS-III answer: ⭐ liberalisation ACCELERATED REGIONAL CONCENTRATION.** ⭐ **Freed from location controls, investment flowed to states with better infrastructure, ports and skilled labour — widening the gap between industrialised western and southern states and the eastern hinterland.** ⭐ **Market-driven location is efficient but not equalising** — which is precisely the argument for regional planning in Ch.9.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Iron and steel is a BASIC and HEAVY industry; raw materials in the ratio 4 : 2 : 1** (iron ore : coking coal : limestone), plus manganese.
2. ⭐ **TISCO (1907, Sakchi, at the Subarnarekha-Kharkai confluence) is the only PRIVATE-sector integrated plant.** ⭐ **IISCO: Hirapur, Kulti, Burnpur.**
3. ⭐⭐ **VISL had iron ore, limestone and manganese but NO COAL** — so it used charcoal until 1951, then Jog Falls hydel power. **Lacking one input, it changed the process.**
4. ⭐⭐ **Collaborations: Rourkela–GERMANY (1959) · Bhilai–RUSSIA (1959) · Durgapur–UK (1962) · Bokaro–RUSSIA (1964).**
5. ⭐⭐ **The BOKARO-ROURKELA COMBINE embodies "transportation cost minimisation"** — wagons carry coal one way and iron ore the other, so neither leg runs empty.
6. ⭐ **First cotton mill: MUMBAI, 1854** — port, capital, labour, nearby cotton, and a humid climate suited to spinning.
7. ⭐ **Sugar is the SECOND largest agro-based industry and a SEASONAL one.** ⭐ **Its southward shift is explained by higher sucrose content, a longer crushing season and stronger cooperatives.**
8. ⭐ **Software Technology Parks came into existence in 1991**; the IT-BPO sector employs a notable proportion of women.
9. ⭐⭐ **Eight industrial regions — and three location logics: RAW MATERIAL (Chotanagpur) · MARKET (Gurugram-Delhi-Meerut, "far from mineral and power resources") · POWER AND SKILL (Bangalore-TN, on Pykara hydroelectricity).**
10. ⭐ **New Industrial Policy 1991 = LPG.** Licensing abolished; automatic FDI up to 51% in high-priority industries; **liberalised industrial location.**
11. 🌱 ⭐ **Liberalisation ACCELERATED REGIONAL CONCENTRATION** — market-driven location is efficient but not equalising, which is the case for the regional planning of Ch.9.
