## 1️⃣ ⭐ India and Human Development

⭐ **The National Human Development Report of India uses THREE INDICATORS: ECONOMIC ATTAINMENT, EDUCATIONAL ATTAINMENT and HEALTH.**

| Indicator | ⭐ Basis |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **ECONOMIC ATTAINMENT** | ⭐ **Access to resources — measured in terms of per capita consumption expenditure, incidence of poverty, etc.** |
| ⭐ **EDUCATIONAL ATTAINMENT** | ⭐ **Measured through literacy rates and intensity of formal education** |
| ⭐ **HEALTH** | ⭐ **Measured through life expectancy at age one and infant mortality rate** |

> ⭐ **Note the specific health indicators NCERT uses: ⭐ LIFE EXPECTANCY AT AGE ONE (not at birth) and the INFANT MORTALITY RATE.** ⭐ **"At age one" is a deliberate choice** — it strips out infant deaths so the two measures capture different things rather than double-counting.

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Regional Variation in India's Human Development

⭐ **There are wide-ranging regional variations in the levels of human development in India.**

| Group | ⭐ States |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **HIGH human development** | ⭐ **KERALA, followed by Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Haryana** |
| ⭐ **MEDIUM human development** | ⭐ **The majority of Indian states** |
| ⭐ **LOW human development** | ⭐ **Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Assam** |

⭐ **KERALA occupies the TOP RANK with the HIGHEST value of HDI in the country.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Its rank is high because of its impressive performance in ACHIEVING NEAR UNIVERSAL LITERACY, enhanced life expectancy and low infant mortality rate.**

> ⭐⭐ **THE KERALA MODEL is the single most important thing in this chapter, and it deserves to be understood rather than merely cited.**
>
> ⭐ **Kerala achieves human development outcomes comparable to far richer societies, at a modest per capita income.** How?
> - ⭐ **Sustained public investment in EDUCATION, especially FEMALE education, over generations**
> - ⭐ **A dense network of primary health care**
> - ⭐ **Land reform and relatively low inequality**
> - ⭐ **High social mobilisation and political competition around welfare delivery**
>
> ⭐ **The results: near-universal literacy, high life expectancy, low infant mortality, ⭐ the ONLY Indian state with a sex ratio above 1,000, and a TFR below replacement decades before the national average.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **The conclusion to state in a Mains answer: ⭐ HUMAN DEVELOPMENT IS A POLICY CHOICE, NOT AN INCOME EFFECT.** **Kerala domestically, and Sri Lanka internationally, both make the same point.**

> ⚠️ ⭐ **The honest counterpoint worth including:** ⭐ **Kerala's high human development has NOT been matched by comparable industrial or employment growth** — which is why it exports labour heavily and depends substantially on remittances. **The "Kerala model" solved human development without fully solving employment.** **Acknowledging that makes an answer more credible, not less.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ Population, Environment and Development

⭐ **The concern for human development has emerged as a reaction to the earlier notions of development.**

⭐ **The world of development thinking has evolved:**

| Phase | ⭐ View |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Post-war (Growth view)** | ⭐ **Development = ECONOMIC GROWTH.** Measured by GDP and per capita income |
| ⭐ **1970s onward** | ⭐ **Questioning: growth was not reaching people; poverty, illiteracy and ill-health persisted** |
| ⭐ **Human development** | ⭐ **People-centred; enlarging choices; capability approach** |

⭐ **The concern for HUMAN WELL-BEING and QUALITY OF LIFE gave rise to the concept of human development.**

### ⭐ The environmental dimension

⭐ **The relationship between POPULATION, ENVIRONMENT and DEVELOPMENT is complex.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The neo-Malthusian, environmentalists and radical ecologists believe that for a happy and peaceful social life, it is important to maintain a balance between population and resources.**

⭐ **Empirical evidences suggest that in developing countries, a large section of the population is dependent on natural resources for their sustenance.**

> 🌱 ⭐ **The framing worth carrying: ⭐ POVERTY IS BOTH A CAUSE AND A CONSEQUENCE OF ENVIRONMENTAL DEGRADATION.**
> ⭐ **The poor depend most directly on natural resources — fuelwood, fodder, common lands, groundwater — so degradation hits them first and hardest.**
> ⭐ **And having no alternative, they may be forced into practices that degrade further.**
> ⭐ **Breaking that loop requires providing alternatives, not merely restricting access.** **This is the argument behind LPG (Ujjwala), community forest rights and MGNREGA-linked natural resource works.**

**Trap 2:** *"Kerala's HDI rank reflects its high per capita income."* ⭐ **It reflects literacy, life expectancy and low IMR** achieved at modest income — the point of the Kerala model.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **NHDR India uses THREE indicators: economic attainment, educational attainment, health.**
2. ⭐ **Health is measured by LIFE EXPECTANCY AT AGE ONE and the INFANT MORTALITY RATE** — "at age one" deliberately avoids double-counting infant deaths.
3. ⭐ **Economic attainment: per capita consumption expenditure and incidence of poverty.**
4. ⭐ **KERALA ranks first**, on near-universal literacy, high life expectancy and low infant mortality. **High: Kerala, Punjab, TN, Maharashtra, Haryana. Low: Bihar, UP, MP, Odisha, Assam.**
5. ⭐⭐ **The Kerala model: sustained public investment in education (especially female), dense primary health care, land reform, low inequality, high social mobilisation.** ⭐ **Result: the only state with a sex ratio above 1,000, and sub-replacement fertility decades early.**
6. ⭐ **The conclusion: HUMAN DEVELOPMENT IS A POLICY CHOICE, NOT AN INCOME EFFECT.**
7. ⚠️ ⭐ **The honest caveat: Kerala's human development was not matched by employment growth**, hence heavy labour export and remittance dependence.
8. 🌱 ⭐ **Poverty is both a cause and a consequence of environmental degradation** — breaking the loop needs alternatives, not restrictions.
