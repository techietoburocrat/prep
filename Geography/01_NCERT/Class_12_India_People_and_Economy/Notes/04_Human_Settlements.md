## 1️⃣ ⭐ Rural Settlements

⭐ **Rural settlements are those settlements where the people are largely engaged in PRIMARY ACTIVITIES.**

⭐ **On the basis of the shape and size, rural settlements in India can be classified into FOUR TYPES** *(as in [Cl.12 *Fundamentals* Ch.10](../../Class_12_Fundamentals_of_Human_Geography/Notes/10_Human_Settlements.md))*:

| Type | ⭐ Where in India |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **CLUSTERED, agglomerated or nucleated** | ⭐ **Fertile alluvial plains and the north-eastern states.** ⭐ **For DEFENCE in Bundelkhand and Nagaland; for WATER SCARCITY in Rajasthan** |
| ⭐ **SEMI-CLUSTERED or fragmented** | ⭐ **Gujarat plain and parts of Rajasthan** |
| ⭐ **HAMLETED** | ⭐ **Middle and lower Ganga plain, Chhattisgarh and lower valleys of the Himalayas.** ⭐ **Units called PANNA, PARA, PALLI, NAGLA, DHANI** |
| ⭐ **DISPERSED or isolated** | ⭐ **Meghalaya, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh and Kerala** |

⭐ **Factors affecting the location of rural settlements: ⭐ WATER SUPPLY · LAND · UPLAND · BUILDING MATERIAL · DEFENCE · PLANNED SETTLEMENTS.**

> ⭐ **Note the specific factor NCERT lists as UPLAND: ⭐ in flood-prone plains, settlements are located on higher ground or artificially raised mounds** — the "levee settlements" of the Ganga plain and Bengal. ⭐ **The village is placed above the flood line, and the fields below it.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Urban Settlements

### ⭐⭐ The evolution of towns in India

⭐ **Towns flourished since prehistorical times in India.** ⭐ **Even at the time of Indus valley civilisation, towns like Harappa and Mohenjodaro were in existence.**

⭐ **The following period has witnessed evolution of towns.** ⭐ **It continued with periodic ups and downs until the arrival of Europeans in India in the eighteenth century.**

⭐ **On the basis of their evolution in different periods, Indian towns may be classified as:**

| Period | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **ANCIENT TOWNS** | ⭐ **There are number of towns in India having historical background spanning over 2000 years.** ⭐ **Most of them developed as religious and cultural centres.** ⭐ **VARANASI is one of the important towns among these.** ⭐ **Prayag (Allahabad), Pataliputra (Patna), Madurai are some other examples** |
| ⭐ **MEDIEVAL TOWNS** | ⭐ **About 100 of the existing towns have their roots in the medieval period.** ⭐ **Most of them developed as headquarters of principalities and kingdoms.** ⭐ **These are FORT TOWNS which came up on the ruins of ancient towns.** ⭐ **Important among them are Delhi, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Lucknow, Agra and Nagpur** |
| ⭐ **MODERN TOWNS** | ⭐ **The British and other Europeans have developed a number of towns in India.** ⭐ **Starting their foothold on coastal locations, they first developed some trading ports such as SURAT, DAMAN, GOA, PONDICHERRY, etc.** ⭐ **The British later consolidated their hold around three principal nodes — MUMBAI (Bombay), CHENNAI (Madras) and KOLKATA (Calcutta) — and built them in the British style.** ⭐ **Rapidly extending their principalities, they established their administrative centres, hill-towns as summer resorts, and added new civil, administrative and military areas to them.** ⭐ **British and other Europeans have developed many industrial centres such as JAMSHEDPUR, MODINAGAR, DALMianagar, ROURKELA, BHILAI, SINDRI, BARAUNI.** ⭐ **A number of administrative towns such as CHANDIGARH, BHUBANESWAR, GANDHINAGAR, DISPUR, etc. and industrial towns have also been developed after Independence** |

> ⭐ **The three-period classification (ancient, medieval, modern) with examples is examinable.** ⭐ **Note the pattern: ancient towns are RELIGIOUS/CULTURAL (Varanasi, Prayag, Madurai) · medieval towns are FORT/ADMINISTRATIVE (Delhi, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Lucknow, Agra, Nagpur) · modern towns are COLONIAL-COMMERCIAL then INDUSTRIAL then PLANNED-ADMINISTRATIVE (Chandigarh, Bhubaneswar, Gandhinagar, Dispur).**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Urbanisation in India — statutory vs census towns

⭐ **The level of urbanisation is measured in terms of PERCENTAGE OF URBAN POPULATION TO TOTAL POPULATION.**

⭐ **The level of urbanisation in India in 2011 was 31.16 per cent, which is quite low in comparison to developed countries.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The Census of India, 2011 defines URBAN SETTLEMENTS as:**

| Category | ⭐ Definition |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **STATUTORY TOWNS** | ⭐ **All places with a MUNICIPALITY, CORPORATION, CANTONMENT BOARD or NOTIFIED TOWN AREA COMMITTEE, etc. are known as urban units** |
| ⭐ **CENSUS TOWNS** | ⭐ **All other places which satisfy the following criteria simultaneously: (i) a MINIMUM POPULATION OF 5,000; (ii) at least 75 PER CENT OF THE MALE MAIN WORKING POPULATION engaged in NON-AGRICULTURAL pursuits; and (iii) a DENSITY OF POPULATION OF AT LEAST 400 PERSONS PER SQ KM** |

> ⭐⭐ **The STATUTORY vs CENSUS TOWN distinction is one of the most reliably asked items in Indian urban geography, and the three census-town criteria must be known exactly:**
> ⭐ **(i) population ≥ 5,000 · (ii) ≥75% of MALE MAIN WORKERS in non-agricultural work · (iii) density ≥ 400 persons/sq km — ALL THREE SIMULTANEOUSLY.**
>
> ⭐ **The difference in kind: a STATUTORY town is urban because it has an urban LOCAL BODY (a legal-administrative fact). A CENSUS town is urban because it MEETS DEMOGRAPHIC AND ECONOMIC CRITERIA (a statistical fact) — but is still governed as a village panchayat.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **This produces a real governance problem worth stating in a Mains answer: ⭐ census towns are functionally urban but administratively rural.** ⭐ **They receive rural funds and rural governance while facing urban problems — waste, water, congestion.** ⭐ **A large share of India's recent "urban growth" has come from new census towns rather than from existing cities expanding.** **That is a live urban-policy issue.**

### ⭐ Classification of towns on the basis of population size

⭐ **Census of India classifies urban centres into SIX CLASSES:**

| Class | ⭐ Population |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **CLASS I** | ⭐ **100,000 and above** |
| ⭐ **CLASS II** | ⭐ **50,000 – 99,999** |
| ⭐ **CLASS III** | ⭐ **20,000 – 49,999** |
| ⭐ **CLASS IV** | ⭐ **10,000 – 19,999** |
| ⭐ **CLASS V** | ⭐ **5,000 – 9,999** |
| ⭐ **CLASS VI** | ⭐ **Less than 5,000** |

⭐ **Urban centres with population of MORE THAN ONE MILLION are called MILLION PLUS CITIES / METROPOLITAN CITIES.**

⭐ **Cities with population size of MORE THAN 5 MILLION are called MEGA CITIES.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Interestingly, more than 60 per cent of urban population in India lives in CLASS I towns.**

⭐ **Out of 468 cities, 53 cities/urban agglomerations are METROPOLITAN CITIES.** ⭐ **Six of them are MEGA CITIES.**

⭐ **More than one-fifth (21.0%) of urban population lives in these mega cities.**

⚠️ ⭐ **GREATER MUMBAI is the largest agglomeration with 18.4 million people.**

> ⭐ **The concentration figure is striking and examinable: ⭐ MORE THAN 60% OF INDIA'S URBAN POPULATION LIVES IN CLASS I TOWNS**, and **more than one-fifth lives in just six mega cities.** ⭐ **India's urbanisation is not spread evenly across a hierarchy of towns — it is concentrated at the top.** **That is why big-city infrastructure strain is the dominant urban problem.**

### ⭐⭐ Functional classification of towns

⭐ **Besides their role, the towns can also be classified on the basis of their FUNCTIONS:**

| Function | ⭐ Examples |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **ADMINISTRATIVE TOWNS** | ⭐ **Towns supporting administrative headquarters of higher order are administrative towns, such as CHANDIGARH, NEW DELHI, BHOPAL, SHILLONG, GUWAHATI, IMPHAL, SRINAGAR, GANDHINAGAR, JAIPUR, CHENNAI, etc.** |
| ⭐ **INDUSTRIAL TOWNS** | ⭐ **Industries constitute prime motive force of these cities such as MUMBAI, SALEM, COIMBATORE, MODINAGAR, JAMSHEDPUR, HOOGHLY, BHILAI, etc.** |
| ⭐ **TRANSPORT CITIES** | ⭐ **They may be ports primarily engaged in export and import activities such as KANDLA, KOCHCHI, KOZHIKODE, VISAKHAPATNAM, etc. or hubs of inland transport such as AGRA, DHULIA, MUGHAL SARAI, ITARSI, KATNI, etc.** |
| ⭐ **COMMERCIAL TOWNS** | ⭐ **Towns and cities specialising in trade and commerce are kept in this class.** ⭐ **KOLKATA, SAHARANPUR, SATNA, etc. are some examples** |
| ⭐ **MINING TOWNS** | ⭐ **These towns have developed in mineral rich areas such as RANIGANJ, JHARIA, DIGBOI, ANKLESHWAR, SINGRAULI, etc.** |
| ⭐ **GARRISON CANTONMENT TOWNS** | ⭐ **These towns emerged as garrison towns such as AMBALA, JALANDHAR, MHOW, BABINA, UDHAMPUR, etc.** |
| ⭐ **EDUCATIONAL TOWNS** | ⭐ **Starting as centres of education, some of the towns have grown into major campus towns such as ROORKEE, VARANASI, ALIGARH, PILANI, ALLAHABAD etc.** |
| ⭐ **RELIGIOUS AND CULTURAL TOWNS** | ⭐ **VARANASI, MATHURA, AMRITSAR, MADURAI, PURI, AJMER, PUSHKAR, TIRUPATI, KURUKSHETRA, HARIDWAR, UJJAIN came to prominence due to their religious/cultural significance** |
| ⭐ **TOURIST TOWNS** | ⭐ **NAINITAL, MUSSOORIE, SHIMLA, PACHMARHI, JODHPUR, JAISALMER, UDAGAMANDALAM (Ooty), MOUNT ABU are some of the tourist destinations** |

⚠️ ⭐ **Cities are NOT STATIC in their function.** ⭐ **They change their functions rapidly with the passage of time.** ⭐ **They acquire new functions and shed old ones as they grow into MULTIFUNCTIONAL cities.**

> ⭐ **The final observation is the analytically useful one: ⭐ cities become MULTIFUNCTIONAL as they grow.** ⭐ **A functional label describes a city's origin far better than its present.** **Mumbai began as a port, became a textile city, then a financial capital, and is now also an entertainment hub.**

## 3️⃣ 🌱 Smart Cities Mission

⭐ **In order to look into urban development, the Government of India has launched the SMART CITIES MISSION.**

⭐ **The purpose is to drive economic growth and improve the quality of life of people by enabling local area development and harnessing technology, especially technology that leads to smart outcomes.**

⭐ **The core infrastructure elements in a smart city would include: adequate water supply, assured electricity supply, sanitation including solid waste management, efficient urban mobility and public transport, affordable housing especially for the poor, robust IT connectivity and digitalisation, good governance especially e-governance and citizen participation, sustainable environment, safety and security of citizens particularly women, children and the elderly, and health and education.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Four rural types: clustered (fertile plains; DEFENCE in Bundelkhand/Nagaland; WATER in Rajasthan) · semi-clustered (Gujarat, Rajasthan) · hamleted (Ganga plain, Chhattisgarh; panna/para/palli/nagla/dhani) · dispersed (Meghalaya, Uttarakhand, HP, Kerala).**
2. ⭐ **Location factors include UPLAND** — settlements placed above the flood line in flood-prone plains.
3. ⭐ **Towns by period: ANCIENT (religious/cultural — Varanasi, Prayag, Pataliputra, Madurai) · MEDIEVAL (fort/administrative — Delhi, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Lucknow, Agra, Nagpur) · MODERN (colonial ports → Mumbai/Chennai/Kolkata → industrial → planned capitals).**
4. ⭐ **Urbanisation level 2011: 31.16%.**
5. ⭐⭐ **STATUTORY TOWN = has a municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area committee.** ⭐ **CENSUS TOWN = population ≥5,000 AND ≥75% of MALE MAIN workers non-agricultural AND density ≥400/sq km, all three together.**
6. ⭐ **Census towns are functionally urban but administratively rural** — a genuine governance gap, and a large share of recent urban growth.
7. ⭐ **Six size classes: Class I (≥100,000) to Class VI (<5,000).** ⭐ **Million-plus = metropolitan; >5 million = MEGA CITY.**
8. ⭐ **>60% of urban Indians live in Class I towns; 53 metropolitan cities; 6 mega cities holding 21% of urban population.** ⭐ **Greater Mumbai is the largest agglomeration (18.4 million).**
9. ⭐ **India's urbanisation is concentrated at the TOP of the hierarchy**, not spread evenly — which is why big-city infrastructure strain dominates.
10. ⭐ **Nine functional types — and cities become MULTIFUNCTIONAL as they grow**, so a functional label describes origin better than present.
