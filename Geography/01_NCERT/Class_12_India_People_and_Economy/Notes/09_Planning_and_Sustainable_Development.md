# Chapter 9 — Planning and Sustainable Development in Indian Context 🌱🌱

## 1️⃣ Types of Planning

**Planning is a widely accepted strategy for economic development.**

**There are TWO APPROACHES to planning:**

| Approach | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SECTORAL PLANNING** | **Means formulation and implementation of the sets of schemes or programmes aimed at development of various sectors of the economy such as agriculture, irrigation, manufacturing, power, construction, transport, communication, social infrastructure and services** |
| **REGIONAL PLANNING** | **There are geographical units which lag behind in development, and there are others which are more developed.** **This is due to the differences in resource endowment, historical background and level of technology.** **The planning to reduce the regional imbalance in development is known as REGIONAL PLANNING** |

⚠️ **The economic development of a country or a region is INCOMPLETE if the benefits of development are not shared by all sections of the population and all regions.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Target Area Planning

**Economic development at the macro level often FAILS TO REACH the poorer sections and lagging regions.**

**In order to arrest the accentuation of regional and social disparities, the PLANNING COMMISSION introduced the "TARGET AREA" and "TARGET GROUP" APPROACHES to planning.**

| Approach | Examples |
|---|---|
| **TARGET AREA programmes** | **COMMAND AREA DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME · DROUGHT PRONE AREA DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME · DESERT DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME · HILL AREA DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME** |
| **TARGET GROUP programmes** | **SMALL FARMERS DEVELOPMENT AGENCY (SFDA) · MARGINAL FARMERS DEVELOPMENT AGENCY (MFDA)** |
| **Special area programmes** | **TRIBAL AREA DEVELOPMENT · Programmes for backward classes |

⚠️ **In the EIGHTH FIVE YEAR PLAN, special area programmes were designed to develop infrastructure in HILL AREAS, NORTH-EASTERN STATES, TRIBAL AREAS and BACKWARD AREAS.**

> **The distinction between TARGET AREA and TARGET GROUP is the key conceptual move, and it is genuinely important:**
> **TARGET AREA planning identifies a PLACE that is lagging** — a drought-prone district, a desert, a hill region — and directs resources there.
> **TARGET GROUP planning identifies a CATEGORY OF PEOPLE who are lagging** — small farmers, marginal farmers — regardless of where they live.
>
> **Both exist because AGGREGATE GROWTH DOES NOT REACH EVERYONE.** **A rising national average can conceal a district falling behind and a class of people getting poorer within a prospering district.**
>
> 🎯 **The framing for Mains: growth is spatially and socially selective, so planning must be spatially and socially targeted.** **This is the geographical version of the argument for affirmative action.**

### Hill Area Development Programmes

**HILL AREA DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMMES were initiated during the FIFTH FIVE YEAR PLAN covering 15 districts comprising all the hilly districts of Uttar Pradesh (present Uttarakhand), Mikir Hill and North Cachar hills of Assam, Darjeeling district of West Bengal and Nilgiri district of Tamil Nadu.**

**The NATIONAL COMMITTEE ON THE DEVELOPMENT OF BACKWARD AREA in 1981 recommended that all the hill areas in the country having height above 600 M and not covered under tribal sub-plan be treated as backward hill areas.**

**The detailed plans for the development of hill areas were drawn keeping in view their TOPOGRAPHICAL, ECOLOGICAL, SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC CONDITIONS.**

**These programmes aimed at HARNESSING THE INDIGENOUS RESOURCES of the hill areas through development of HORTICULTURE, PLANTATION AGRICULTURE, ANIMAL HUSBANDRY, POULTRY, FORESTRY and SMALL-SCALE AND VILLAGE INDUSTRY.**

### Drought Prone Area Programme

**This programme was initiated during the FOURTH FIVE YEAR PLAN with the objectives of PROVIDING EMPLOYMENT to the people in drought prone areas and CREATING PRODUCTIVE ASSETS.**

**Initially this programme laid emphasis on the construction of LABOUR-INTENSIVE CIVIL WORKS.**

⚠️ **But later on, it emphasised on IRRIGATION PROJECTS, LAND DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMMES, AFFORESTATION, GRASSLAND DEVELOPMENT and CREATION OF BASIC RURAL INFRASTRUCTURE such as electricity, roads, market, credit and services.**

**The National Committee on Development of Backward Area reviewed the performance of DPAP.**

⚠️ **It found that this programme is mainly confined to the development of agriculture and allied sectors with SPECIAL FOCUS ON SOIL AND MOISTURE CONSERVATION.**

**This programme is applicable in the DROUGHT PRONE AREAS OF INDIA — the drought prone areas of Rajasthan, Gujarat, Western Madhya Pradesh, Marathwada region of Maharashtra, Rayalseema and Telangana plateaus of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka plateau and highlands and interior parts of Tamil Nadu.**

⚠️ **These drought prone areas need PLANNING for the development of alternative agricultural systems that could sustain in the low rainfall conditions and REDUCED DEPENDENCE ON WATER-INTENSIVE CROPPING.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Case Study 1 — Bharmaur Tribal Region (Integrated Tribal Development Project)

**BHARMAUR TRIBAL AREA comprises BHARMAUR and HOLI TEHSILS of CHAMBA district of HIMACHAL PRADESH.**

**It is a NOTIFIED TRIBAL AREA since 21 November 1975.**

**It is inhabited by "GADDI", a tribal community who used to practise TRANSHUMANCE and speak a distinct dialect called "GADDIALI".**

**The Bharmaur tribal area has a harsh climate condition, low resource base and fragile environment.**

⚠️ **These factors have adversely affected the economic and social development of the ethnic "Gaddi" community.**

**The historical and cultural roots of the "GADDI" tribal community can be traced back to the pre-Aryan period.**

### The physical setting

**The BUDHIL AND RAVI RIVERS drain the region.**

**The region is surrounded by lofty mountains — the PIR PANJAL in the north and DHAULADHAR in the south.**

**The topography is rugged and the region has a harsh climate.**

**Agriculture is limited by the terrain — small terraced fields, limited soil, short growing season.**

### The Integrated Tribal Development Project

**The process of development of tribal area of Bharmaur started in 1970s when GADDIS were included among "scheduled tribes".**

**Under the FIFTH FIVE YEAR PLAN, the tribal sub-plan was introduced in 1974 and Bharmaur was designated as one of the FIVE INTEGRATED TRIBAL DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS (ITDP) in Himachal Pradesh.**

**This area development plan was aimed at IMPROVING THE QUALITY OF LIFE of the Gaddis and NARROWING DOWN THE GAP in the level of development between Bharmaur and other areas of Himachal Pradesh.**

**The specific programmes run under Bharmaur I.T.D.P. include development of TRANSPORT AND COMMUNICATION, AGRICULTURE AND ALLIED ACTIVITIES, and SOCIAL AND COMMUNITY SERVICES.**

### The results — and what they show

**The BENEFITS of these programmes are:**

| Outcome | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Social benefits** | **The most significant achievement of ITDP is the DEVELOPMENT OF INFRASTRUCTURE in terms of schools, health care facilities, potable water, roads, communication and electricity.** **But the BENEFITS OF SOCIAL SERVICES ARE VERY UNEVENLY DISTRIBUTED** |
| **Literacy** | **The transformation is very conspicuous in the increase in LITERACY RATE, improvement in sex ratio and decline in child marriage.** **The female literacy rate in the region increased from 1.88 per cent in 1971 to 65 per cent in 2011** |
| ⚠️ **Uneven benefit** | **The BENEFITS OF THE SOCIAL SERVICES ARE VERY UNEVENLY DISTRIBUTED and are concentrated in Bharmaur and Holi areas** |
| **Economic change** | **In the economic sphere, Gaddis have DIVERSIFIED their economic base.** **The traditional economy was based on subsistence agriculture and TRANSHUMANT PASTORALISM.** **Now there is a shift to CASH CROPS — the cultivation of PULSES and other CASH CROPS has increased** |
| ⚠️ **The pastoralism decline** | **The proportion of pastoral population has RECORDED A DECLINE** |

> **The Bharmaur female literacy figure is remarkable and directly quotable: FROM 1.88% IN 1971 TO 65% IN 2011.**
>
> **That is roughly a thirty-fold increase in forty years, in a remote, high-altitude tribal region with harsh terrain.** **It is one of the strongest available Indian examples that TARGETED AREA PLANNING CAN WORK.**
>
> ⚠️ **But note the two honest caveats NCERT includes, and a good answer must include both:**
> **(1) The benefits are UNEVENLY DISTRIBUTED, concentrated in the Bharmaur and Holi areas** — the periphery of the target area remains behind.
> **(2) The proportion of PASTORAL POPULATION has DECLINED** — development changed a way of life, not only living standards.
>
> 🎯 **The balanced verdict for a Mains answer: ITDP delivered measurable human development, but unevenly, and at the cost of a traditional pastoral livelihood.** **"Development succeeded and transformed" is a more honest formulation than "development succeeded."**

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Case Study 2 — The Indira Gandhi Canal (Nahar) Command Area

**The INDIRA GANDHI CANAL, formerly known as the RAJASTHAN CANAL, is one of the largest canal systems in India.**

**Conceived by KANWAR SAIN in 1948, the canal project was launched on 31 MARCH 1958.**

**The canal originates at HARIKE BARRAGE in Punjab and runs parallel to Pakistan border at an average distance of 40 km in Thar Desert (Marusthali) of Rajasthan.**

**The total planned length of the canal system is 9,060 km catering to the irrigation needs of a total culturable command area of 19.63 lakh hectares.**

### The transformation

**The introduction of canal irrigation in this dry land has brought about a PERCEPTIBLE TRANSFORMATION in its ECOLOGY, ECONOMY and SOCIETY.**

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ecological** | **The availability of soil moisture for a longer period of time and various afforestation and pasture development programmes under CAD (Command Area Development) have led to a GREENING OF THE AREA** |
| **Reduced wind erosion** | **It has also helped in REDUCING WIND EROSION and shifting of sand dunes** |
| ⚠️ **THE PROBLEM** | **The intensive irrigation has led to the emergence of TWIN ENVIRONMENTAL PROBLEMS OF WATERLOGGING AND SOIL SALINITY** |
| **Economic** | **The cropping pattern has shifted from gram, bajra and jowar to WHEAT, COTTON, GROUNDNUT AND RICE.** **Intensive irrigation has increased the agricultural production and productivity** |
| **Social** | **The colonisation of land in command area has led to migration of people and settlement** |

### Measures for sustainable development

**The following measures are suggested to promote sustainable development in the Indira Gandhi Canal Command Area:**

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1.** | **The FIRST AND FOREMOST REQUIREMENT is strict implementation of WATER MANAGEMENT POLICY.** **The canal project envisages protective irrigation in Stage-I and extensive irrigation of crops and pasture development in Stage-II** |
| **2.** | **In general, the CROPPING PATTERN SHALL NOT INCLUDE WATER INTENSIVE CROPS.** **It shall be adhered to and people shall be encouraged to grow PLANTATION CROPS such as citrus fruits** |
| **3.** | **The CAD programmes such as LINING OF WATERCOURSES, land development and levelling and WARABANDI SYSTEM (equal distribution of canal water in the command area of outlet) shall be effectively implemented to reduce the CONVEYANCE LOSS of water** |
| **4.** | **The AFFORESTATION and development of PASTURE and SHELTERBELT PLANTATION are necessary particularly in the fragile environment of Stage-II** |
| **5.** | **The CULTIVABLE WASTELAND shall be developed and allotted to landless labourers.** **The economic sustainability in the region cannot be attained only through development of agriculture and animal husbandry** |
| **6.** | **The ECOLOGICAL SUSTAINABILITY: the region requires the development of agriculture along with other sectors.** **This shall lead to diversification of economic base and establishment of functional linkages between basic villages, agro-service centres and market centres** |

> **WARABANDI is the term to know precisely: the EQUAL DISTRIBUTION OF CANAL WATER in the command area of an outlet, by a fixed rotational schedule.** **It is a rationing system — each farmer gets water for a fixed period on a fixed day, whatever their crop.** **Its purpose is both equity and discipline: it prevents head-reach farmers from taking everything and leaving tail-enders dry.**

> ⭐ **THE INDIRA GANDHI CANAL IS THE BEST SINGLE CASE STUDY IN THE ENTIRE NCERT SERIES FOR THE DEVELOPMENT-VS-SUSTAINABILITY QUESTION, and it should be learned as a complete argument:**
>
> **THE ACHIEVEMENT: canal irrigation greened a desert, reduced wind erosion, stabilised shifting sand dunes, raised agricultural production and productivity dramatically, and enabled settlement of a near-uninhabitable region.**
>
> ⚠️ **THE COST: intensive irrigation in an arid zone produced WATERLOGGING AND SOIL SALINITY** — the same capillary-action mechanism as Cl.11 *India: Physical Environment* Ch.6. **And the cropping pattern shifted from drought-hardy gram, bajra and jowar to WATER-INTENSIVE wheat, cotton, groundnut and RICE — rice, in a desert.**
>
> 🎯 **THE LESSON: THE PROJECT SOLVED WATER SCARCITY AND THEN INVITED FARMERS TO BEHAVE AS IF WATER WERE ABUNDANT.** **The recommended remedies — no water-intensive crops, warabandi, lined watercourses, plantation crops like citrus — are all attempts to restore the discipline that the canal removed.**
>
> **This is Griffith Taylor's traffic-light argument** (Cl.12 *Fundamentals* Ch.1) **in physical form: the canal gave a green light, and the ecology is now enforcing a red one.**

## 5️⃣ Sustainable Development

**The concept of SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT was popularised by the BRUNDTLAND COMMISSION REPORT, "OUR COMMON FUTURE" (1987).**

> **SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.**

**It aims at satisfying the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of the future generations to satisfy their needs.**

⚠️ **The concept of sustainable development takes into consideration the ECOLOGICAL, SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC ASPECTS.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Two approaches: SECTORAL planning (by economic sector) and REGIONAL planning (to reduce spatial imbalance).**
2. **TARGET AREA programmes (Command Area Development, DPAP, Desert Development, Hill Area Development) target PLACES; TARGET GROUP programmes (SFDA, MFDA) target PEOPLE.** **Both exist because aggregate growth reaches neither automatically.**
3. **Hill Area Development: FIFTH Plan, 15 districts.** **The 1981 committee defined backward hill areas as those above 600 M not under the tribal sub-plan.**
4. **DPAP: FOURTH Plan; employment plus productive assets; later focused on soil and moisture conservation.** **Its regions need alternative agricultural systems and reduced dependence on water-intensive cropping.**
5. **BHARMAUR: Bharmaur and Holi tehsils, Chamba district, Himachal Pradesh; notified tribal area 21 Nov 1975; the GADDI tribe, speaking GADDIALI, traditionally practising TRANSHUMANCE; drained by the Budhil and Ravi; between the Pir Panjal and Dhauladhar.**
6. **ITDP began under the Fifth Plan (tribal sub-plan, 1974); Bharmaur was one of FIVE ITDPs in Himachal.**
7. ⭐ **Bharmaur's female literacy rose from 1.88% (1971) to 65% (2011)** — one of India's strongest demonstrations that targeted area planning works. ⚠️ **But benefits were unevenly distributed, and the pastoral population declined.** **Development succeeded AND transformed.**
8. **INDIRA GANDHI CANAL: conceived by KANWAR SAIN (1948), launched 31 March 1958, originates at HARIKE BARRAGE in Punjab, runs ~40 km parallel to the Pakistan border, planned length 9,060 km, command area 19.63 lakh hectares.**
9. **Benefits: greening, reduced wind erosion, stabilised dunes, higher production, settlement.**
10. ⚠️ **Costs: WATERLOGGING and SOIL SALINITY**, plus a cropping shift from gram/bajra/jowar to wheat/cotton/groundnut/**RICE — in a desert.**
11. **Remedies: water management policy, no water-intensive crops, WARABANDI (equal rotational distribution), lined watercourses, afforestation and shelterbelts, wasteland allotment to the landless, economic diversification.**
12. 🎯 **The lesson: the project solved scarcity and then invited behaviour as if water were abundant.** **Every remedy is an attempt to restore the discipline the canal removed.**
13. **Sustainable development — Brundtland Commission, "Our Common Future" (1987).**
