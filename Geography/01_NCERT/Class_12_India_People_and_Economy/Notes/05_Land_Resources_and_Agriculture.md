# Chapter 5 — Land Resources and Agriculture 🌱

> **The one-line point:** **India feeds 17.5% of humanity from 2.4% of the world's land — and it does so on holdings that are shrinking, with water that is running out, using a cropping pattern that no longer suits its water budget.** **The chapter's real subject is not how much India grows but whether the way it grows can continue.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐ Land-Use Categories

**The land-use categories as maintained in the Land Revenue Records are as follows:**

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| **1. FORESTS** | **It is important to note that AREA UNDER ACTUAL FOREST COVER IS DIFFERENT FROM AREA CLASSIFIED AS FOREST.** **The latter is the area which the Government has identified and demarcated for forest growth.** **The land revenue records are consistent with the latter definition** |
| **2. LAND PUT TO NON-AGRICULTURAL USES** | **Land under settlements (rural and urban), infrastructure (roads, canals, etc.), industries, shops, etc. are included in this category.** **An increase in this category is at the cost of wastelands and agricultural land** |
| **3. BARREN AND WASTELANDS** | **The land which may be classified as a wasteland such as barren hilly terrains, desert lands, ravines, etc. normally cannot be brought under cultivation with the available technology** |
| **4. AREA UNDER PERMANENT PASTURES AND GRAZING LANDS** | **Most of this type of land is owned by the village "PANCHAYAT" or the Government.** **Only a small proportion of this land is privately owned.** **The land owned by the village panchayat comes under "COMMON PROPERTY RESOURCES"** |
| **5. AREA UNDER MISCELLANEOUS TREE CROPS AND GROVES** | **The land under orchards and fruit trees are included in this category.** **Much of this land is PRIVATELY OWNED** |
| **6. CULTURABLE WASTELAND** | **Any land which is left FALLOW (uncultivated) for MORE THAN FIVE YEARS is included in this category.** **It can be brought under cultivation after improving it through reclamation practices** |
| **7. CURRENT FALLOW** | **This is the land which is left without cultivation for ONE OR LESS THAN ONE agricultural year.** **Fallowing is a cultural practice adopted for giving the land rest.** **The land recoups the lost fertility through natural processes** |
| **8. FALLOW OTHER THAN CURRENT FALLOW** | **This is also a CULTURABLE LAND which is left uncultivated for MORE THAN A YEAR BUT LESS THAN FIVE YEARS.** **If the land is left uncultivated for more than five years, it would be categorised as CULTURABLE WASTELAND** |
| **9. NET AREA SOWN** | **The physical extent of land on which crops are sown and harvested is known as net sown area** |

> **The FALLOW LAND definitions are the most reliably asked item in this chapter. Learn the three thresholds exactly:**
> **CURRENT FALLOW — one year or less**
> **FALLOW OTHER THAN CURRENT FALLOW — more than one year but less than five**
> **CULTURABLE WASTELAND — more than five years**
>
> **And note the important distinction in category 1: AREA UNDER ACTUAL FOREST COVER ≠ AREA CLASSIFIED AS FOREST.** **Revenue records use the latter** — land demarcated for forest growth, whether or not trees are on it. **(The same forest area vs forest cover point as Cl.11 *India: Physical Environment* Ch.5.)**

### Land-use changes in India

**Three types of changes that an economy undergoes, which affect land-use are:**

**(i) The size of the economy grows over time as a result of increasing population, change in income levels, available technology and associated factors.** **As a consequence, the pressure on land will increase with time and marginal lands would come under use.**

**(ii) Secondly, the composition of the economy would undergo a change over time.** **In other words, the secondary and the tertiary sectors usually grow much faster than the primary sector.** **This type of change is common in developing countries like India.**

⚠️ **This type of change would lead to a gradual DECLINE IN THE SHARE OF AGRICULTURAL ACTIVITIES IN THE ECONOMY.**

**(iii) Though the contribution of the agricultural activities reduces over time, the pressure on land does not decrease.**

**The reasons are: (a) the additional personsupported by the land would have to be absorbed either in the agricultural sector or in the non-agricultural sector; (b) though the share of agricultural activities decreases over time, the a**sec**tor continues to support the majority.**

### The key trends

| Trend | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Share of area under FORESTS** | **Has increased marginally.** **The increase is more on paper than in reality — the actual increase in forest cover has been small** |
| **AREA UNDER NON-AGRICULTURAL USES** | **Has INCREASED, due to expansion of settlements, transport, industry.** **This expansion has come at the cost of both wastelands and agricultural land |
| **BARREN AND WASTELAND** | **Has DECLINED** |
| **AREA UNDER PASTURES AND GRAZING LANDS** | **Has DECLINED — a serious concern for livestock** |
| **CULTURABLE WASTELAND** | **Has DECLINED** |
| **NET AREA SOWN** | **Has shown a marginal increase and then stabilised** |
| **FALLOW LANDS** | **Fluctuate with rainfall — good monsoon reduces fallow, poor monsoon increases it** |

> 🌱 **The decline in PERMANENT PASTURES is a specific, under-used point.** **India has one of the world's largest livestock populations and a shrinking area of common grazing land.** **The pressure is displaced onto forests and crop residues — increasing degradation and conflict over commons.** **Good for GS-III on land degradation.**

## 2️⃣ Cropping Seasons in India

**There are THREE distinct crop seasons in the northern and interior parts of country, namely KHARIF, RABI and ZAID.**

| Season | Sowing | Harvesting | Major crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KHARIF** | **June–July** | **September–October** | **Rice, jowar, bajra, maize, cotton, groundnut, jute, hemp, tobacco, sugarcane, soyabean, tur** |
| **RABI** | **October–December** | **February–April** | **Wheat, barley, gram, mustard, linseed, masur, peas** |
| **ZAID** | **March–April** | **June–July** | **Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder crops** |

⚠️ **In southern parts of the country, there is NO CLEAR-CUT DISTINCTION IN CROPPING SEASONS.**

**The same crops can be grown thrice in an agricultural year due to the presence of higher temperature which allows growing of tropical crops for all the 12 months provided sufficient soil moisture is available.**

**Therefore, in the southern states the terms KHARIF and RABI are not used.** **Rather, the cropping periods are named after the months in which the crops are sown.**

> **The southern exception is examinable: THE KHARIF/RABI DISTINCTION DOES NOT APPLY IN SOUTHERN INDIA**, because temperatures permit year-round tropical cropping if soil moisture allows. **The seasons are simply named after sowing months.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Major Crops

### Food grains — cereals

**Cereals occupy about 54 per cent of total cropped area in India.**

**India produces about 11 per cent cereals of the world and ranks THIRD in production after China and U.S.A.**

**The cereals are classified as FINE GRAINS (rice, wheat) and COARSE GRAINS (jowar, bajra, maize, ragi).**

| Crop | Conditions | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **RICE** | **A staple food crop of majority of Indian people.** **India is the SECOND LARGEST producer of rice in the world after China.** **Grown in the plains of north and north-eastern India and coastal and deltaic regions** | **West Bengal, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar.** **In Punjab and Haryana rice is grown as a result of IRRIGATION** **In states like Assam, West Bengal and Odisha, TWO OR THREE crops of rice are raised in a year** |
| **WHEAT** | **The second most important cereal crop in India after rice.** **India produces about 12 per cent of total wheat production of the world.** **It is primarily a crop of TEMPERATE ZONE, hence its cultivation is undertaken during WINTER** | **Concentrated in TWO BROAD ZONES: the NORTH-WEST (Punjab, Haryana, western UP, Rajasthan) and the BLACK SOIL REGION of the Deccan.** **About 85 per cent of total area under this crop is concentrated in north and central regions.** **UTTAR PRADESH, PUNJAB, HARYANA, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Bihar** |
| **JOWAR** | **The third most important food crop with respect to area and production.** **It is a RAIN-FED crop mostly grown in the moist areas which hardly needs irrigation** | **MAHARASHTRA alone produces more than half of the total jowar production of the country.** **Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh** |
| **BAJRA** | **Sown in HOT AND DRY CLIMATIC conditions in north-western and western parts of the country.** **It is a hardy crop which resists frequent dry spells and drought.** **This crop is sown alone as well as part of mixed cropping** | **RAJASTHAN is the largest producer**, followed by Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat and Haryana |
| **MAIZE** | **A food as well as FODDER crop grown under semi-arid climatic conditions and over inferior soils.** **This crop occupies only about 3.6 per cent of total cropped area** | **Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh** |

### Pulses

**Pulses are a very important INGREDIENT OF VEGETARIAN FOOD as these are rich sources of PROTEINS.**

**These are LEGUMINOUS crops and increase the natural fertility of soils through NITROGEN FIXATION.**

**India is a LEADING PRODUCER of pulses and accounts for about ONE-FIFTH of the total production of pulses in the world.**

**The cultivation of pulses in the country is largely concentrated in the drylands of Deccan and central plateaus and north-western parts of the country.**

⚠️ **Pulses occupy about 11 per cent of the total cropped area in the country.**

| Pulse | Detail |
|---|---|
| **GRAM** | **Cultivated in SUBTROPICAL AREAS.** **It is a RABI crop.** **It is a RAIN-FED crop cultivated during winter** **Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and Maharashtra** |
| **TUR (ARHAR)** | **The SECOND most important pulse crop in the country.** **It is also known as RED GRAM or PIGEON PEA.** **It is cultivated over MARGINAL LANDS and under RAIN-FED conditions.** **MAHARASHTRA alone contributes about one-third of the total production** |

### Oilseeds

**The oilseeds are produced for extracting EDIBLE OILS.** **Drylands of Malwa plateau, Marathwada, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Telangana, Rayalseema region of Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka plateau are oilseeds growing regions of India.**

**These crops together occupy about 14 per cent of total cropped area in the country.**

| Oilseed | Detail |
|---|---|
| **GROUNDNUT** | **India is the SECOND LARGEST producer of groundnut after China.** **Groundnut is largely a RAIN-FED KHARIF crop.** **GUJARAT, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Maharashtra** |
| **RAPESEED AND MUSTARD** | **Comprise several oilseeds as rai, sarson, toria and taramira.** **These are SUBTROPICAL crops cultivated during RABI season.** **These are FROST SENSITIVE crops.** **Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, West Bengal and Madhya Pradesh** |
| **OTHER OILSEEDS** | **Soyabean and sunflower** — Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra (soyabean); Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra (sunflower) |

### Fibre crops

| Crop | Conditions | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **COTTON** | **A tropical crop grown in KHARIF season.** **India lost a large proportion of cotton growing area to Pakistan during partition.** **India grows both SHORT STAPLE (Indian) cotton as well as LONG STAPLE (American) cotton called "NARMA" in north-western parts of the country** | **Cotton requires CLEAR SKY during flowering stage.** **MAHARASHTRA, GUJARAT, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana** |
| **JUTE** | **A cash crop in the eastern parts of the country, mainly West Bengal.** **India lost large jute-growing areas to East Pakistan (Bangladesh) at the time of partition.** **At present, India produces about THREE-FIFTHS of jute production of the world** | **WEST BENGAL, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, Meghalaya** |

> **NARMA is the name worth knowing: LONG STAPLE (American) cotton grown in north-western India.** **And note the specific requirement: cotton needs a CLEAR SKY during the flowering stage.**

### Other crops

| Crop | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SUGARCANE** | **A crop of TROPICAL AREAS.** **Under rain-fed conditions, it is cultivated in sub-humid and humid climates.** **But it is largely an IRRIGATED CROP in India.** **In Indo-Gangetic plain, its cultivation is largely concentrated in Uttar Pradesh.** **UTTAR PRADESH, MAHARASHTRA, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh** |
| **TEA** | **A PLANTATION crop used as beverage.** **Black tea leaves are fermented whereas green tea leaves are unfermented.** **Tea leaves have rich content of caffeine and tannin.** **It is an indigenous crop to hills in northern China.** **It is grown over UNDULATING TOPOGRAPHY of hilly areas and WELL-DRAINED soils in HUMID and SUB-HUMID tropics and sub-tropics.** **ASSAM, West Bengal (Darjeeling and Jalpaiguri), Tamil Nadu, Kerala** |
| **COFFEE** | **A TROPICAL PLANTATION crop.** **Its seeds are roasted, ground and are used for preparing a beverage.** **There are THREE VARIETIES of coffee i.e. ARABICA, ROBUSTA and LIBERICA.** **India mostly grows superior quality coffee, ARABICA, which is in great demand in international market.** **But India produces only about 3.2 per cent coffee of the world.** **KARNATAKA alone accounts for more than two-thirds of total production**, followed by Kerala and Tamil Nadu |

> **The three coffee varieties — ARABICA, ROBUSTA, LIBERICA — are examinable, and the note that India mostly grows the superior ARABICA.** **Also: black tea leaves are FERMENTED, green tea leaves are UNFERMENTED.**

## 4️⃣ Agricultural Development in India

### The problems

| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| **DEPENDENCE ON ERRATIC MONSOON** | **Irrigation covers only about 33 per cent of the cultivated area in India.** **The crop production in rest of the cultivated land directly depends on rainfall** |
| **LOW PRODUCTIVITY** | **The yield of the crops in the country is LOW in comparison to the international level.** **Per hectare output of most of the crops such as rice, wheat, cotton and oilseeds in India is much lower than that of U.S.A., Russia and Japan** |
| **CONSTRAINTS OF FINANCIAL RESOURCES AND INDEBTEDNESS** | **The inputs of modern agriculture are very expensive.** **This resource intensive approach has become unmanageable for marginal and small farmers as they have very little or no saving to invest in agriculture** |
| **LACK OF LAND REFORMS** | **Indian peasantry had been exploited for a long time as there had been unequal distribution of land.** **Among the three revenue systems operational during British period, i.e. Mahalwari, Ryotwari and Zamindari, the last one was most exploitative** |
| **SMALL FARM SIZE AND FRAGMENTATION OF LANDHOLDINGS** | **There are large numbers of marginal and small farmers in the country.** **More than 60 per cent of the ownership holdings have a size smaller than one hectare** |
| **LACK OF COMMERCIALISATION** | **A large number of farmers produce crops for self-consumption.** **These farmers do not have enough land resources to produce more than their requirement** |
| **VAST UNDER-EMPLOYMENT** | **There is a massive under-employment in the agricultural sector in India, particularly in the un-irrigated tract.** **In these areas, there is a seasonal unemployment ranging from 4 to 8 months** |
| **DEGRADATION OF CULTIVABLE LAND** | **One of the serious problems that arises out of faulty strategy of irrigation and agricultural development is degradation of land resources.** **This is serious because it may lead to LOSS OF FERTILE TOP SOIL and lowering of its productivity** |

> **The three colonial revenue systems are worth knowing by name: MAHALWARI, RYOTWARI and ZAMINDARI — and NCERT's judgement that ZAMINDARI WAS THE MOST EXPLOITATIVE.** **This connects directly to your Modern History notes.**

> **The two figures to have at hand:**
> **IRRIGATION covers only about 33% of cultivated area** — so two-thirds of Indian cropping is monsoon-dependent.
> **MORE THAN 60% of ownership holdings are SMALLER THAN ONE HECTARE.**
>
> **Those two facts together explain almost every problem in the list.** **A farmer on under one hectare, dependent on rain, cannot mechanise economically, cannot absorb a crop failure, and cannot invest in improvement.** **The constraint is structural, not cultural.**

> 🌱 **And note NCERT's own phrase: "faulty strategy of irrigation and agricultural development" causing land degradation.** **The textbook is naming the Green Revolution's costs directly** — waterlogging, salinisation, groundwater depletion and loss of topsoil.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Nine land-use categories.** **The three fallow thresholds: CURRENT FALLOW ≤1 year · FALLOW OTHER THAN CURRENT 1–5 years · CULTURABLE WASTELAND >5 years.**
2. **Forest AREA (classified, in revenue records) ≠ forest COVER (actual canopy).**
3. **Permanent pastures are COMMON PROPERTY RESOURCES owned by panchayats — and their area is declining**, squeezing India's huge livestock population.
4. **Three seasons: KHARIF (Jun–Jul → Sep–Oct) · RABI (Oct–Dec → Feb–Apr) · ZAID (Mar–Apr → Jun–Jul).** **The distinction does not apply in southern India.**
5. **Cereals ~54% of cropped area; India ranks 3rd in cereals after China and the USA.** Fine grains (rice, wheat) vs coarse (jowar, bajra, maize, ragi).
6. **Rice: India 2nd after China; Punjab and Haryana grow it by IRRIGATION.** **Wheat: two zones — NW plains and Deccan black soil; 85% in north and central India.**
7. **Jowar: Maharashtra >half. Bajra: Rajasthan largest, a hardy drought-resistant crop.** **Maize is food AND fodder, ~3.6% of cropped area.**
8. **Pulses ~11% of cropped area, India ~1/5 of world production; leguminous, fixing nitrogen.** **Tur = red gram/pigeon pea; Maharashtra ~1/3.**
9. **Oilseeds ~14%. Groundnut: India 2nd after China, Gujarat leading, rain-fed kharif.** **Rapeseed/mustard are FROST-SENSITIVE rabi crops.**
10. **NARMA = long staple American cotton in the north-west; cotton needs CLEAR SKY at flowering.** **Jute: India ~3/5 of world production; West Bengal.**
11. **Coffee: Arabica, Robusta, Liberica — India grows mostly ARABICA; only ~3.2% of world; Karnataka >2/3.** **Black tea fermented, green tea unfermented.**
12. **The two structural facts: IRRIGATION covers only ~33% of cultivated area, and MORE THAN 60% of holdings are under ONE HECTARE.** **Together they explain nearly every problem in Indian agriculture.**
13. **Colonial revenue systems: Mahalwari, Ryotwari, ZAMINDARI — the last most exploitative.** **Seasonal unemployment of 4–8 months in un-irrigated tracts.**
