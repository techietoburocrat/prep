## 1️⃣ ⭐ India's Three Mineral Belts

**Minerals are generally concentrated in three broad belts in India:**

| Belt | Extent | Minerals |
|---|---|---|
| **1. THE NORTH-EASTERN PLATEAU REGION** | **Covers CHOTA NAGPUR (Jharkhand), Odisha Plateau, West Bengal and parts of Chhattisgarh** | **Has a variety of minerals viz. IRON ORE, COAL, MANGANESE, BAUXITE, MICA** |
| **2. THE SOUTH-WESTERN PLATEAU REGION** | **Extends over KARNATAKA, GOA and contiguous TAMIL NADU uplands and KERALA** | **Rich in FERROUS METALS and BAUXITE.** **It also contains high grade IRON ORE, MANGANESE and LIMESTONE.** ⚠️ **This belt PACKS IN COAL DEPOSITS EXCEPT NEYVELI LIGNITE.** **This belt does not have as diversified mineral deposits as the north-eastern belt.** **Kerala has deposits of MONAZITE and THORIUM, BAUXITE CLAY.** **Goa has IRON ORE deposits** |
| **3. THE NORTH-WESTERN REGION** | **Extends along ARAVALI in RAJASTHAN and part of GUJARAT** | **Minerals are associated with Dharwar system of rocks.** **COPPER, ZINC have been major minerals.** **Rajasthan is rich in building stones i.e. SANDSTONE, GRANITE, MARBLE.** **GYPSUM and FULLER'S EARTH deposits are also extensive.** **DOLOMITE and LIMESTONE provide raw materials for cement industry.** **Gujarat is known for its PETROLEUM deposits.** **Gujarat and Rajasthan both have rich sources of SALT** |

⚠️ **The HIMALAYAN BELT is another mineral belt where COPPER, LEAD, ZINC, COBALT and TUNGSTEN are known to occur.** **They occur on both the eastern and western parts.**

⚠️ **Assam valley has mineral oil deposits.** **Besides oil resources are also found in off-shore-areas near Mumbai Coast (Mumbai High).**

> **The three-belt structure is the most useful organising device in this chapter, and it maps directly onto geology:**
> **NORTH-EASTERN (Chota Nagpur–Odisha–WB–Chhattisgarh) — the MOST DIVERSIFIED belt; iron, coal, manganese, bauxite, mica.**
> **SOUTH-WESTERN (Karnataka–Goa–TN–Kerala) — ferrous metals and bauxite, but ALMOST NO COAL except Neyveli lignite.**
> **NORTH-WESTERN (Aravalli–Rajasthan–Gujarat) — non-ferrous (copper, zinc), building stones, gypsum, and Gujarat's PETROLEUM.**
>
> **The key contrast to state: THE SOUTH-WESTERN BELT LACKS COAL.** **That single absence is why India's iron and steel industry concentrated in the north-east rather than the south**, despite the south having high-grade iron ore. **Ore without coking coal cannot make steel economically.**

## 2️⃣ Major Minerals

| Mineral | Detail |
|---|---|
| **IRON ORE** | **India is endowed with fairly abundant resources of iron ore.** **It has the LARGEST RESERVE OF IRON ORE IN ASIA.** **The two main types of ore found in our country are HAEMATITE and MAGNETITE.** **It has great demand in international market due to its SUPERIOR QUALITY.** **The iron ore mines occur in close proximity to the coal fields in the north-eastern plateau region of the country which adds to their advantage.** **About 95 per cent of total reserves of iron ore is located in the States of ODISHA, JHARKHAND, CHHATTISGARH, KARNATAKA, GOA, TELANGANA, ANDHRA PRADESH and TAMIL NADU** |
| **MANGANESE** | **An important raw material for smelting of iron ore and also used for manufacturing FERRO ALLOYS.** **ODISHA is the leading producer** of Manganese |
| **BAUXITE** | **Bauxite is a clay-like substance from which alumina and later aluminium is obtained.** **Bauxite is found mainly in tertiary deposits and is associated with LATERITIC ROCKS occurring extensively as plateau and hill ranges of PENINSULAR INDIA and also in the coastal tracts of the country.** **ODISHA happens to be the largest producer of Bauxite.** **KALAHANDI and SAMBALPUR are the leading producers** |
| **COPPER** | **India is CRITICALLY DEFICIENT in the reserve and production of copper.** **Copper is malleable, ductile and a good conductor of electricity, it is mainly used in electrical cables, electronics and chemical industries.** **The BALAGHAT mines in Madhya Pradesh produce 52 per cent of India's copper.** **The SINGHBHUM district of Jharkhand is also a leading producer of copper.** **The KHETRI mines in Rajasthan** |

> **Two facts worth having precisely: INDIA HAS THE LARGEST IRON ORE RESERVE IN ASIA**, and **95% of it is in Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Goa, Telangana, AP and Tamil Nadu.** **And in sharp contrast, INDIA IS CRITICALLY DEFICIENT IN COPPER** — with Balaghat alone supplying 52%.
>
> **Note the locational advantage NCERT highlights: India's iron ore mines occur CLOSE TO THE COAL FIELDS in the north-eastern plateau.** **That proximity is the single reason India's steel industry could develop where it did.**

**NON-METALLIC MINERALS: MICA is the most important one.** **India is a leading producer.** **JHARKHAND, ANDHRA PRADESH and RAJASTHAN are the leading producers.**

## 3️⃣ Energy Resources

**Mineral fuels are essential for generation of power, required by agriculture, industry, transport and other sectors of the economy.**

**Mineral fuels like COAL, PETROLEUM AND NATURAL GAS (fossil fuels), NUCLEAR ENERGY MINERALS, are the CONVENTIONAL SOURCES of energy.**

**These sources of energy are EXHAUSTIBLE.**

### Conventional energy sources

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| **COAL** | **Coal is a critical source of energy for a majority of the countries in the world.** **In India, coal is the most abundantly available fossil fuel.** **It provides a substantial part of the nation's energy needs.** **It is used for POWER GENERATION, to supply energy to industry as well as for domestic needs.** **India is highly dependent on coal for meeting its commercial energy requirements** |
| **Coal deposits** | **In India coal occurs in rock series of two main geological ages, namely GONDWANA and TERTIARY deposits.** **About 80 per cent of the coal deposits in India is of BITUMINOUS type and is of NON-COKING GRADE.** **The most important Gondwana coal fields of India are located in DAMODAR VALLEY.** **They lie in Jharkhand-Bengal coal belt and the important coal fields in this region are JHARIA, RANIGANJ, BOKARO, GIRIDIH, KARANPURA.** **Jharia is the LARGEST coal field followed by Raniganj.** **The other river valleys associated with coal are GODAVARI, MAHANADI and SONE.** **TERTIARY coals occur in the north eastern states of Meghalaya, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh and Nagaland** |
| **PETROLEUM** | **Crude petroleum occurs in SEDIMENTARY ROCKS of the tertiary period.** **Oil exploration and production was systematically taken up after the Oil and Natural Gas Commission was set up in 1956.** **Till then, the DIGBOI in Assam was the only oil producing region.** **The major OIL PRODUCING AREAS are: MUMBAI HIGH, GUJARAT and ASSAM.** **ANKLESHWAR is the most important field of GUJARAT.** **Assam is the oldest oil producing state of India.** **DIGBOI, NAHARKATIYA and MORAN are important oil producing areas in the state** |
| **NATURAL GAS** | **Natural gas is an important CLEAN ENERGY resource found in association with or without petroleum.** **It is used as a source of energy as well as an industrial raw material in the petrochemical industry.** **Natural gas is considered an environment friendly fuel because of low carbon dioxide emissions and is therefore, the FUEL FOR THE PRESENT CENTURY.** **Large reserves of natural gas have been discovered in the KRISHNA-GODAVARI basin.** **Along the west coast the reserves of the Mumbai High and allied fields are supplemented by finds in the Gulf of Cambay.** **Andaman and Nicobar Islands is also an important area having large reserves of natural gas** |

> **The coal facts most reliably asked:**
> **~80% of India's coal is BITUMINOUS and of NON-COKING grade.**
> **JHARIA is the LARGEST coalfield, followed by RANIGANJ.**
> **Gondwana coalfields in the DAMODAR VALLEY (Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro, Giridih, Karanpura); also Godavari, Mahanadi and Sone valleys.**
> **TERTIARY coal in the NORTH-EAST (Meghalaya, Assam, Arunachal, Nagaland).**
>
> ⚠️ **The "non-coking" point matters enormously and is under-used:** **India has abundant coal but is short of COKING coal — the metallurgical grade needed for steel-making.** **This is why India imports coking coal despite being a major coal producer, and why Vishakhapatnam's steel plant is port-based** (Cl.10 Ch.6). **Abundance of the wrong grade is not abundance.**

> **ONGC was set up in 1956; before that DIGBOI (Assam) was India's ONLY oil-producing region.** **Assam is the OLDEST oil-producing state.** **Natural gas is called the "FUEL FOR THE PRESENT CENTURY."**

### Non-conventional energy sources

**The conventional sources of energy are generally EXHAUSTIBLE and in some cases POLLUTING.**

**The non-conventional energy sources like SOLAR, WIND, HYDRO GEOTHERMAL and BIOMASS are RENEWABLE.**

**These sources are more equitably distributed and environmental friendly.**

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SOLAR ENERGY** | **Sun rays tapped in photovoltaic cells can be converted into energy known as solar energy.** **The two effective processes considered to be very effective to tap solar energy are PHOTOVOLTAICS and SOLAR THERMAL TECHNOLOGY.** **Solar thermal technology has some relative advantages over all other non-renewable energy sources.** **It is COST COMPETITIVE, ENVIRONMENT FRIENDLY and EASY TO CONSTRUCT.** **Solar energy is 7 per cent more effective than coal or oil based plants and 10 per cent more effective than nuclear plants.** **WESTERN INDIA (Rajasthan and Gujarat) has greater potential** |
| **WIND ENERGY** | **Wind energy is absolutely POLLUTION FREE, INEXHAUSTIBLE source of energy.** **The permanent wind systems such as trade winds, westerlies and seasonal wind like monsoon have been used as source of energy.** **In India, the Ministry of Non-conventional Energy Sources is developing wind energy.** **RAJASTHAN, GUJARAT, MAHARASHTRA and KARNATAKA have favourable conditions for wind energy** |
| **TIDAL AND WAVE ENERGY** | **Ocean currents are the STORE-HOUSE OF INFINITE ENERGY.** **In India, the GULF OF KHAMBHAT, THE GULF OF KACHCHH and the GANGETIC DELTA in Sunderban regions of West Bengal provide ideal conditions for utilising tidal energy** |
| **GEOTHERMAL ENERGY** | **When the magma from the interior of earth, comes out on the surface, tremendous heat is released.** **This heat energy can successfully be tapped and converted to electrical energy.** **In India, geothermal energy sites: PARVATI VALLEY near MANIKARAN in HIMACHAL PRADESH and PUGA VALLEY in LADAKH** |
| **BIO-ENERGY** | **Bio-energy refers to energy derived from biological products which includes agricultural residues, municipal, industrial and other wastes.** **Bio-energy is a potential source of energy conversion.** **It can be converted into electrical energy, heat energy or gas for cooking.** **It will also process the waste and garbage and produce energy.** **This will improve economic life of rural areas in developing countries, reduce environmental pollution, enhance self-reliance and reduce pressure on fuel wood.** **One such project converting municipal waste into energy is OKHLA in DELHI** |

> **The specific figures for solar are examinable: solar energy is 7% more effective than coal- or oil-based plants and 10% more effective than nuclear plants.** **And the named geothermal sites: PARVATI VALLEY near MANIKARAN (Himachal) and PUGA VALLEY (Ladakh).** **The bio-energy example: OKHLA, DELHI.**

## 4️⃣ 🌱 Conservation of Mineral Resources

**The economic development of any country is dependent on the availability of mineral resources.**

⚠️ **The mineral resources in general are FINITE, NON-RENEWABLE and EXHAUSTIBLE.**

**Hence, sustained efforts have to be made for their conservation.**

**Measures include:**
- **Use of scrap metals and other substitutes**
- **Recycling of metals**
- **Using alternative renewable substitutes**
- **Export of strategic and scarce minerals must be reduced, so that the existing reserve may be used for a longer period**
- **Technology should be upgraded to allow use of low grade ores at low costs**

> 🌱 **The point about restricting export of STRATEGIC AND SCARCE minerals is worth carrying into a GS-III answer on critical minerals.** **India has recently identified a list of 30 CRITICAL MINERALS (lithium, cobalt, rare earths and others) essential for the energy transition and defence, and has set up KABIL and the National Critical Mineral Mission.** **A current-affairs extension of exactly this NCERT point.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Three mineral belts: NORTH-EASTERN (Chota Nagpur–Odisha–WB–Chhattisgarh — the most diversified: iron, coal, manganese, bauxite, mica) · SOUTH-WESTERN (Karnataka–Goa–TN–Kerala — ferrous metals and bauxite but ALMOST NO COAL except Neyveli lignite) · NORTH-WESTERN (Aravalli–Rajasthan–Gujarat — copper, zinc, building stones, gypsum, Gujarat petroleum).**
2. **The Himalayan belt has copper, lead, zinc, cobalt and tungsten.**
3. **India has the LARGEST IRON ORE RESERVE IN ASIA** — haematite and magnetite, 95% in eight states. **Its mines sit close to the north-eastern coalfields**, which is why the steel industry located there.
4. **Odisha leads both MANGANESE and BAUXITE (Kalahandi, Sambalpur).** **Bauxite is associated with LATERITIC rocks.**
5. **India is CRITICALLY DEFICIENT in copper** — Balaghat (MP) alone supplies 52%.
6. **~80% of India's coal is BITUMINOUS and NON-COKING** — so India must import coking coal despite being a major producer. **Abundance of the wrong grade is not abundance.**
7. **JHARIA is the largest coalfield, then RANIGANJ; both in the Damodar valley.** **Tertiary coal is in the north-east.**
8. **ONGC founded 1956; before that DIGBOI was India's only producer.** **Assam is the oldest oil state; Ankleshwar is Gujarat's most important field.**
9. **Natural gas: KRISHNA-GODAVARI basin, Mumbai High, Gulf of Cambay, Andaman & Nicobar.** **Called the "fuel for the present century."**
10. **Solar is 7% more effective than coal/oil plants and 10% more than nuclear.** **Geothermal: Manikaran (HP) and Puga (Ladakh).** **Tidal: Gulfs of Khambhat and Kachchh, Sunderbans.** **Bio-energy: Okhla, Delhi.**
11. 🌱 **Conservation: recycling, substitutes, technology for low-grade ores, and RESTRICTING EXPORT of strategic and scarce minerals** — the NCERT root of today's critical minerals policy.
