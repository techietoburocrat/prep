# Chapter 1 — Population: Distribution, Density, Growth and Composition

> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **India holds about 17.5% of the world's people on 2.4% of its land** — and that population is distributed with extreme unevenness, is growing more slowly than it once did, and is composed in ways that will determine the country's economic trajectory for the next three decades.

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ Distribution of Population

⭐ **India's population as per 2011 census is 1,210.6 MILLION.**

⭐ **India's population is 17.5 PER CENT of the world's population, spread over 2.4 PER CENT of its land area.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The distribution is HIGHLY UNEVEN.**

| Fact | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Most populous state** | ⭐ **UTTAR PRADESH — about 16% of India's population** |
| ⭐ **Least populous state** | ⭐ **SIKKIM (0.05%)** |
| ⭐ **Least populous UT** | ⭐ **LAKSHADWEEP (0.01%)** |
| ⭐ **Concentration** | ⭐ **Almost HALF of India's population lives in just FIVE STATES — Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Bihar, West Bengal and Madhya Pradesh** |
| ⭐ **Rajasthan's anomaly** | ⭐ **Rajasthan, the largest state in terms of area, has only 5.5 per cent of the total population of India** |

> ⭐ **The Rajasthan point is the analytically useful one: ⭐ AREA AND POPULATION ARE NOT CORRELATED — HABITABILITY IS.** ⭐ **Rajasthan is India's largest state and holds only 5.5% of its people, because most of it is the Thar Desert.** **Land is not the same as liveable land.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Density of Population

> ⭐ **DENSITY OF POPULATION = number of persons per unit area.**

⭐ **The density of population in India (2011) is 382 PERSONS PER SQ KM.**

| Rank | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Highest density (state)** | ⭐ **BIHAR (1,106)** |
| ⭐ **Next highest** | ⭐ **West Bengal (1,028), Kerala (860), Uttar Pradesh (829)** |
| ⭐ **Lowest density (state)** | ⭐ **ARUNACHAL PRADESH (17)** |
| ⭐ **Highest density (UT)** | ⭐ **DELHI (11,320)** |
| ⭐ **Lowest density (UT)** | ⭐ **Andaman & Nicobar Islands (46)** |

⚠️ ⭐ **DO NOT confuse the two questions: ⭐ UTTAR PRADESH is the MOST POPULOUS state; BIHAR has the HIGHEST DENSITY.** ⭐ **And ARUNACHAL PRADESH has the lowest density while SIKKIM has the smallest population.** **Four different answers to four different questions.**

### ⭐ Physiological and agricultural density

⭐ **The density of population, as discussed in the earlier chapter, is a crude measure of human and land relationship.**

⭐ **To get a better insight into the human-land ratio in terms of pressure of population on total cultivable land, the DENSITIES OF POPULATION are refined:**

| Measure | ⭐ Formula |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **PHYSIOLOGICAL DENSITY** | ⭐ **Total Population ÷ NET CULTIVATED AREA** |
| ⭐ **AGRICULTURAL DENSITY** | ⭐ **Total AGRICULTURAL population ÷ NET CULTIVABLE AREA** |

⭐ **AGRICULTURAL POPULATION includes cultivators and agricultural labourers and their family members.**

> ⭐ **These refined measures matter because arithmetic density can mislead badly.** ⭐ **A state with much uncultivable land (desert, mountain, forest) has a low arithmetic density and a very high physiological density** — meaning genuine pressure on the land that actually feeds people.

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Growth of Population

> ⭐ **GROWTH OF POPULATION is the change in the number of people living in a particular area between two points of time.**

⭐ **Its rate is expressed in percentage.**

| Term | ⭐ Definition |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **NATURAL GROWTH** | ⭐ **Analysed by assessing the crude birth and death rates.** ⭐ **NATURAL INCREASE = BIRTHS − DEATHS** |
| ⭐ **ACTUAL GROWTH** | ⭐ **BIRTHS − DEATHS + IN-MIGRATION − OUT-MIGRATION** |
| ⭐ **POSITIVE GROWTH** | ⭐ **When the birth rate is more than the death rate, or people from other countries migrate permanently to a region** |
| ⭐ **NEGATIVE GROWTH** | ⭐ **When the birth rate falls below the death rate, or people migrate to other countries** |

### ⭐⭐ The four phases of India's population growth

⭐ **The growth of population in India can be studied under FOUR PHASES:**

| Phase | ⭐ Period | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **PHASE I** | ⭐ **1901–1921** | ⭐ **STAGNANT PHASE of growth of India's population.** ⭐ **The growth rate was very low, even recording a NEGATIVE growth rate during 1911–1921.** ⭐ **Both the birth rate and death rate were high, keeping the rate of increase low.** ⭐ **Poor health and medical services, illiteracy of people at large and inefficient distribution system of food and other basic necessities were largely responsible for a high birth and death rates in this period.** ⭐ **THE YEAR 1921 IS CALLED THE YEAR OF GREAT DIVIDE** |
| ⭐ **PHASE II** | ⭐ **1921–1951** | ⭐ **STEADY POPULATION GROWTH.** ⭐ **An overall improvement in health and sanitation throughout the country brought down the mortality rate.** ⭐ **At the same time better transport and communication system improved distribution system.** ⭐ **The crude birth rate remained high in this period leading to higher growth rate than the previous phase.** ⭐ **This is impressive at the backdrop of the Great Economic Depression, 1920s and World War II** |
| ⭐ **PHASE III** | ⭐ **1951–1981** | ⭐ **Referred to as the period of POPULATION EXPLOSION in India, which was caused by a rapid fall in the mortality rate but a high fertility rate of population in the country.** ⭐ **The average annual growth rate was as high as 2.2 per cent.** ⭐ **It is in this period, after the Independence, that developmental activities were introduced through a centralised planning process and economy started showing up ensuring the improvement of living condition of people at large.** ⭐ **Consequently, there was a high natural increase and higher growth rate.** ⭐ **Besides, increased international migration bringing in Tibetans, Bangladeshis, Nepalis and even people from Pakistan contributed to the high growth rate** |
| ⭐ **PHASE IV** | ⭐ **1981 – present** | ⭐ **Since 1981, the growth rate started DECLINING gradually.** ⭐ **During this period, downward trend of crude birth rate is held responsible for such a population growth.** ⭐ **This was, in turn, affected by increase in the mean age at marriage, improved quality of life particularly education of females in the country** |

> ⭐⭐ **1921 — "THE YEAR OF GREAT DIVIDE" — is one of the most reliably asked facts in Indian population geography.** ⭐ **It is the LAST YEAR India's population DECLINED** (influenza pandemic and famine). ⭐ **Every year since has seen growth.**

> ⭐⭐ **The insight that most candidates get wrong, and it applies to Phase III precisely:** ⭐ **the population explosion was caused by a RAPID FALL IN MORTALITY, not a rise in fertility.** ⭐ **Fertility stayed high; deaths collapsed.** **The gap between the two is the explosion.**

> 📉 ⭐ **The current position, which changes how a population answer should be framed:** ⭐ **India's Total Fertility Rate is now about 2.0 (NFHS-5) — BELOW the replacement level of 2.1.** ⭐ **Population is still growing only because of POPULATION MOMENTUM: a very large cohort is already of childbearing age.** ⭐ **India has, in effect, completed its fertility transition.** **The question has shifted from "control" to "ageing and skills."**

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Regional Variation in Population Growth

⭐ **The growth rate of population is not uniform across the country.**

⭐ **The states with the HIGHEST growth rates are generally the northern and central states — Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan.**

⭐ **The states with the LOWEST growth rates are the southern states — Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka — and Goa.**

⚠️ ⭐ **It is important to note that though the annual growth rate of population in India is decreasing, still the number of people being added every year is very large.**

> ⭐⭐ **The north–south divergence is the important structural fact, and it has real political consequences:**
> ⭐ **The southern states have completed their demographic transition; the northern states have not.**
> ⭐ **So ⭐ INDIA WILL EXPERIENCE A DEMOGRAPHIC DIVIDEND AND A DEMOGRAPHIC BURDEN SIMULTANEOUSLY, IN DIFFERENT STATES** — young Bihar and UP, ageing Kerala and Tamil Nadu.
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **This connects directly to DELIMITATION (states that controlled population would lose parliamentary seats if seats were reallocated by population), FISCAL DEVOLUTION (Finance Commission formulas), and INTERNAL MIGRATION.** **A high-value GS-II point that few candidates make.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ Population Composition

### ⭐ Rural–urban composition

⭐ **India is a country of villages.** ⭐ **According to Census 2011, about 68.8 PER CENT of the total population lives in villages, and 31.2 PER CENT in urban areas.**

⭐ **The proportion of the rural population is declining and that of the urban population is increasing.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The size of rural population differs from state to state.** ⭐ **Himachal Pradesh and Bihar have very high proportion of rural population, whereas Goa and Maharashtra have larger proportion of urban population.**

### ⭐⭐ Linguistic composition

⭐ **India is a land of linguistic diversity.** ⭐ **According to Grierson (Linguistic Survey of India, 1903–1928), there were 179 LANGUAGES and as many as 544 DIALECTS in the country.**

⭐ **In the contemporary India, there are 22 SCHEDULED LANGUAGES and a number of non-scheduled languages.**

⭐ **The languages of India belong to four language families:**

| Family | ⭐ Speech areas | ⭐ Share |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **AUSTRIC (Nishada)** | ⭐ **Meghalaya, Nicobar Islands, West Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra** | ⭐ **1.38%** |
| ⭐ **DRAVIDIAN (Dravida)** | ⭐ **South India (Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh), Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Maharashtra** | ⭐ **20%** |
| ⭐ **SINO-TIBETAN (Kirata)** | ⭐ **Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, West Bengal** | ⭐ **0.85%** |
| ⭐ **INDO-EUROPEAN (Aryan)** | ⭐ **Jammu and Kashmir, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa** | ⭐ **73%** |

> ⭐ **The four families with their traditional names are examinable: ⭐ AUSTRIC (Nishada) · DRAVIDIAN (Dravida) · SINO-TIBETAN (Kirata) · INDO-EUROPEAN (Aryan).** ⭐ **And the shares: Indo-European ~73%, Dravidian ~20%, Austric ~1.38%, Sino-Tibetan ~0.85%.**
>
> ⭐ **Grierson's Linguistic Survey (1903–1928) recorded 179 languages and 544 dialects** — another reliably asked pairing.

### ⭐ Religious composition

⭐ **Religion is one of the most dominant forces affecting the cultural and political life of the majority of Indians.**

⭐ **The spatial distribution of religious communities in the country shows that there are certain states and districts having large share of particular religions:**

⭐ **HINDUS** are distributed as a major group in many states except Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Mizoram, and Lakshadweep.

⭐ **MUSLIMS**, the largest religious minority, are concentrated in Jammu & Kashmir, certain districts of West Bengal and Kerala, many districts of Uttar Pradesh, in and around Delhi and in Lakshadweep.

⭐ **CHRISTIANS** are concentrated in the states of Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Goa, Kerala.

⭐ **SIKHS** are concentrated in Punjab, Haryana, Delhi.

⭐ **JAINS and BUDDHISTS** are concentrated in Maharashtra, Rajasthan (Jains); Maharashtra, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Ladakh (Buddhists).

### ⭐⭐ Composition of working population

⭐ **The population of India according to their economic status is divided into three groups, namely: MAIN WORKERS, MARGINAL WORKERS and NON-WORKERS.**

| Category | ⭐ Definition |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **MAIN WORKER** | ⭐ **A person who works for at least 183 DAYS (or six months) in a year** |
| ⭐ **MARGINAL WORKER** | ⭐ **A person who works for LESS THAN 183 DAYS (or six months) in a year** |
| ⭐ **NON-WORKER** | ⭐ **A person who does not participate in any economically productive activity** |

⚠️ ⭐ **The proportion of workers (both main and marginal) is only about 39 PER CENT (2011) leaving a vast majority of 61 per cent as non-workers.**

⭐ **This indicates an economic status in which there is a larger proportion of dependent population, further indicating possible SOCIO-ECONOMIC PROBLEMS.**

### ⭐ Occupational categories

⭐ **The occupational composition of India's population shows a LARGE PROPORTION OF PRIMARY SECTOR WORKERS compared to secondary and tertiary sectors.**

⭐ **About 54.6 PER CENT of the total working population are CULTIVATORS AND AGRICULTURAL LABOURERS**, whereas only 3.8% of workers are engaged in household industries and 41.6 % are other workers including non-household industries, trade, commerce, construction and repair and other services.

⚠️ ⭐ **As far as the occupation of country's male and female population is concerned, male workers out-number female workers in all the three sectors.**

> ⭐⭐ **The 183-day threshold for main vs marginal workers is a precise, examinable definition.**

> ⭐⭐ **The structural problem in one comparison:** ⭐ **agriculture engages about 54.6% of the workforce but contributes a far smaller share of GDP.** ⭐ **That gap IS disguised unemployment** — too many people on too little land, each producing too little value. ⭐ **Moving people out of agriculture into productive non-farm work is the central challenge of Indian development.**

> ⚠️ ⭐ **And the 39% work participation rate hides a gender story:** ⭐ **male workers outnumber female workers in all three sectors**, and India's female labour force participation is low and has been falling. ⭐ **Much women's work — unpaid domestic and care work, and unpaid family labour on farms — is not counted as "work" at all.** **A strong point for GS-I society and for any answer on the demographic dividend.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **2011: 1,210.6 million — 17.5% of world population on 2.4% of land. Density 382/sq km.**
2. ⭐ **UP most populous (~16%); Sikkim smallest population; BIHAR highest density (1,106); ARUNACHAL lowest (17); Delhi highest UT (11,320).**
3. ⭐ **Half of India lives in five states: UP, Maharashtra, Bihar, WB, MP.** ⭐ **Rajasthan is largest by area with only 5.5% of people — habitability, not area, drives population.**
4. ⭐ **Physiological density = total population ÷ net cultivated area. Agricultural density = agricultural population ÷ net cultivable area.**
5. ⭐⭐ **Four growth phases: 1901–21 STAGNANT (⭐ 1921 = YEAR OF GREAT DIVIDE, the last decline) · 1921–51 STEADY · 1951–81 EXPLOSION (2.2% p.a.) · 1981–present DECLINING.**
6. ⭐ **The explosion was caused by MORTALITY FALLING, not fertility rising.** ⭐ **Phase IV's decline came from a later mean age at marriage and better female education.**
7. 📉 ⭐ **India's TFR is now ~2.0 — below replacement.** Growth continues through **population momentum**.
8. ⭐⭐ **North–south divergence: young northern states, ageing southern ones** — so India will have a dividend and a burden simultaneously. **Connects to delimitation and fiscal devolution.**
9. ⭐ **68.8% rural, 31.2% urban (2011).**
10. ⭐ **Grierson recorded 179 languages and 544 dialects; 22 are scheduled today.** ⭐ **Families: Indo-European/Aryan 73% · Dravidian/Dravida 20% · Austric/Nishada 1.38% · Sino-Tibetan/Kirata 0.85%.**
11. ⭐⭐ **MAIN worker = 183+ days; MARGINAL = fewer.** ⭐ **Workers 39%, non-workers 61%.**
12. ⭐ **Cultivators and agricultural labourers = 54.6% of workers** — far above agriculture's GDP share. **That gap is disguised unemployment.** ⭐ **Male workers outnumber female in all three sectors.**
