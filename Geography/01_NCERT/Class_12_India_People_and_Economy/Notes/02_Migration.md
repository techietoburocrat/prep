> **The one-line point:** **Migration is the third component of population change, and the only one people choose.** **In India it is overwhelmingly RURAL-TO-URBAN for men and MARRIAGE-DRIVEN for women — and that single gendered split explains almost every pattern in the data.**

## 1️⃣ What Migration Is

**In India, people move from one place to another in search of better opportunities.**

> **MIGRATION is the third component of population change, the other two being BIRTH RATE and DEATH RATE.**

⚠️ **When people move from one place to another, the place they move FROM is called the PLACE OF ORIGIN and the place they move TO is called the PLACE OF DESTINATION.**

**The place of origin shows a DECREASE in population while the population increases in the place of destination.**

**Migration may be interpreted as a spontaneous effort to achieve a better balance between population and resources.**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **IMMIGRATION** | **Migrants who move INTO a new place are called Immigrants** |
| **EMIGRATION** | **Migrants who move OUT of a place are called Emigrants** |
| **IN-MIGRATION** | **Migrants who come to a place within the country** |
| **OUT-MIGRATION** | **Migrants who leave a place within the country** |

⚠️ **INTERNAL migration uses in-migration/out-migration; INTERNATIONAL migration uses immigration/emigration.**

**In the Census of India migration is enumerated on TWO BASES:**

| Basis | Detail |
|---|---|
| **PLACE OF BIRTH** | **If the place of birth is different from the place of enumeration (known as LIFE TIME MIGRANT)** |
| **PLACE OF RESIDENCE** | **If the place of last residence is different from the place of enumeration (known as MIGRANT BY PLACE OF LAST RESIDENCE)** |

> **The two Census bases are examinable: LIFE TIME MIGRANT (place of birth ≠ place of enumeration) and MIGRANT BY PLACE OF LAST RESIDENCE.** **The second captures more recent movement; the first captures the whole lifetime.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Streams of Migration

**Under INTERNAL MIGRATION, there are FOUR STREAMS:**

```
   THE FOUR INTERNAL MIGRATION STREAMS

   1. RURAL TO RURAL  (R–R)
   2. RURAL TO URBAN  (R–U)
   3. URBAN TO URBAN  (U–U)
   4. URBAN TO RURAL  (U–R)
```

### The gendered split — the key finding

⚠️ **In India, during the last few decades, the RURAL TO RURAL stream dominated among FEMALES, and RURAL TO URBAN among MALES.**

**Among the FEMALE migrants, the R–R stream dominates because of MARRIAGE.**

**Among MALE migrants, the R–U stream is prominent because of EMPLOYMENT.**

> **This is the single most important finding in the chapter, and it explains nearly everything else:**
>
> **WOMEN migrate mainly RURAL-TO-RURAL, for MARRIAGE.** In a patrilocal society, a woman moves to her husband's village on marriage — and Census records this as migration.
> **MEN migrate mainly RURAL-TO-URBAN, for WORK.**
>
> **Consequences that follow directly:**
> - **Marriage is the single largest reported reason for migration in India, because women outnumber men among migrants overall.**
> - **Cities become MALE-HEAVY; villages become FEMALE-HEAVY** — the reverse of the Western pattern (Cl.12 *Fundamentals* Ch.3).
> - **Female migration statistics MASK the fact that most women's migration is not economic.** **When "migration for employment" is disaggregated by sex, women almost vanish from it.**
>
> 🎯 **In a GS-I Society answer, noting that India's migration data is dominated by marriage migration — and that this reflects patrilocality rather than economic opportunity — is a genuinely sharp observation.**

**In international migration, people move OUT of the country (emigration) or move INTO the country (immigration).**

## 3️⃣ Spatial Variation in Migration

### Largest streams and states

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Largest number of IN-MIGRANTS (intra-state)** | **MAHARASHTRA** occupied first place in the list, followed by **Delhi, Gujarat and Haryana** |
| **Largest number of OUT-MIGRANTS** | **UTTAR PRADESH** followed by **BIHAR** |
| **Net in-migration states** | **Maharashtra, Delhi, Gujarat, Haryana** |
| **Net out-migration states** | **Uttar Pradesh, Bihar** |

**Among the urban agglomerations, GREATER MUMBAI received the higher number of in-migrants.**

**Intra-state migration constitutes the largest number of migrants.**

> **The UP–Bihar → Maharashtra–Delhi–Gujarat–Haryana axis is the spine of Indian internal migration**, and it maps almost exactly onto the demographic and economic divergence from Ch.1. **Population-surplus, employment-deficit states send; industrialised states receive.**

### International migration

**India experiences all the three types of INTERNATIONAL MIGRATION: immigration, emigration and refugees.**

**India has a large number of INDIAN DIASPORA spread across the world.**

**There are two broad types of emigration from India:**
- **The FIRST WAVE was of INDENTURED LABOURERS to colonies — Mauritius, Caribbean islands (Trinidad, Tobago and Guyana), Fiji and South Africa** under the **GIRMIT ACT (Indian Emigration Act)**.
- **The SECOND WAVE comprised PROFESSIONALS — doctors, engineers, software engineers, management consultants, financial experts, media persons — to USA, Canada, UK, Australia, New Zealand and Germany.**
- **The THIRD WAVE was of semi-skilled and unskilled labourers to the OIL-RICH WEST ASIAN COUNTRIES after the oil boom of the 1970s.**

> **The GIRMIT ACT is worth knowing by name** — the Indian Emigration Act under which indentured labourers were taken to colonial plantations. **The word "girmitiya" derives from "agreement."** **The Indian diaspora in Mauritius, Fiji, Trinidad, Guyana and South Africa dates from this.**

**There are also a large number of immigrants from BANGLADESH, NEPAL and PAKISTAN.**

## 4️⃣ Causes of Migration

**People, generally are EMOTIONALLY ATTACHED to their place of birth.** **But millions of people leave their places of birth and residence.**

**There could be a variety of reasons.** **These reasons can be put into two broad categories:**

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **PUSH FACTORS** | **These cause people to leave their place of residence or origin** |
| **PULL FACTORS** | **Which attract the people from different places** |

### The Indian pattern

**In India, people MIGRATE FROM RURAL TO URBAN AREAS mainly due to POVERTY, HIGH POPULATION PRESSURE ON THE LAND, LACK OF BASIC INFRASTRUCTURAL FACILITIES LIKE HEALTH CARE, EDUCATION, ETC.**

⚠️ **Apart from these factors, natural disasters such as FLOOD, DROUGHT, CYCLONIC STORMS, EARTHQUAKE, TSUNAMI, WARS AND LOCAL CONFLICTS also give extra push to migrate.**

**On the other hand, there are PULL FACTORS which attract the people from rural areas to cities.** **The MOST IMPORTANT PULL FACTOR for majority of the rural migrants to urban areas is the BETTER OPPORTUNITIES, availability of regular work and relatively higher wages.**

**Better opportunities for education, better health facilities and sources of entertainment, etc., are also quite important pull factors.**

### Reported reasons for migration — with the gender split

| Reason | Detail |
|---|---|
| **WORK/EMPLOYMENT** | **The MAIN reason for MALE migration** |
| **MARRIAGE** | **The MAIN reason for FEMALE migration.** **Nearly every married woman in rural areas has to migrate to her husband's place of residence.** **Hence, the entire female population of India is affected by this phenomenon of migration |
| **EDUCATION** | **Rural areas, by and large, lack educational facilities, especially those of higher education and rural people have to migrate to the urban centres for this purpose.** **Many of them settle down in the cities for earning a livelihood after completing their education** |
| **BUSINESS** | **Movement for trade and commerce** |
| **MOVED AFTER BIRTH / MOVED WITH HOUSEHOLD** | **Dependent migration** |

⚠️ **Interestingly, the census records "MARRIAGE" as the reason for a very large share of female migration, but this is not an economic decision — it is a social institution recorded as a demographic event.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐ Consequences of Migration

**Migration is a response to the uneven distribution of opportunities over space.**

**People tend to move from a place of LOW OPPORTUNITY and LOW SAFETY to a place of HIGHER OPPORTUNITY and BETTER SAFETY.**

**This, in turn, creates both benefits and problems for the areas, people migrate from and migrate to.**

**Consequences can be observed in ECONOMIC, SOCIAL, CULTURAL, POLITICAL and DEMOGRAPHIC terms.**

### Economic consequences

| Consequence | Detail |
|---|---|
| **REMITTANCES — the major benefit** | **A major benefit for the source region is the REMITTANCE sent by migrants.** **Remittances from the international migrants are one of the MAJOR SOURCES OF FOREIGN EXCHANGE.** **In 2002, India received US$ 11 billion as remittances from international migrants.** **PUNJAB, KERALA and TAMIL NADU receive very significant amount from their international migrants** |
| **Remittances used for** | **Food, repayment of debts, treatment, marriages, children's education, agricultural inputs, construction of houses, etc.** **For thousands of the poor villages of Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Odisha, etc. remittance works as LIFE BLOOD FOR THEIR ECONOMY** |
| **UNPLANNED URBAN GROWTH — the cost** | **Uncontrolled migration to metropolitan cities of India has caused OVERCROWDING** |
| **Development of slums** | **Development of slums in industrially developed states such as Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu is a negative consequence of unregulated migration within the country** |

### Demographic consequences

**Migration leads to the REDISTRIBUTION OF THE POPULATION within a country.**

**Rural urban migration is one of the important factors contributing to the population growth of cities.**

⚠️ **AGE AND SKILL SELECTIVE OUT MIGRATION from the rural area have adverse effect on the rural demographic structure.**

**However, high out migration from Uttarakhand, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Eastern Maharashtra have brought SERIOUS IMBALANCES IN AGE AND SEX COMPOSITION in these states.**

⚠️ **Similar imbalances are also brought in the recipients states.**

> **"AGE AND SKILL SELECTIVE" is the crucial phrase, and NCERT chooses it carefully.**
>
> **Migration does not remove a random sample of the population. It removes the YOUNG, the ABLE-BODIED and often the BETTER-EDUCATED.**
>
> **So the source region is left with a population skewed toward the very young, the elderly and women** — with the productive cohort removed. **The village loses precisely the people who would have driven local development.**
>
> 🎯 **This is why out-migration can be simultaneously a lifeline (remittances) and a trap (loss of human capital).** **Stating both sides is what a good Mains answer does.**

### Social consequences

| Consequence | Detail |
|---|---|
| **AGENTS OF SOCIAL CHANGE** | **Migrants act as agents of social change.** **The new ideas related to new technologies, family planning, girl's education, etc. get diffused from urban to rural areas through them** |
| **INTERMIXING OF CULTURES** | **Migration leads to intermixing of people from diverse cultures.** **It has positive contribution such as EVOLUTION OF COMPOSITE CULTURE and BREAKING THROUGH THE NARROW CONSIDERATIONS AND WIDENS UP THE MENTAL HORIZON of the people at large** |
| ⚠️ **ANONYMITY AND SOCIAL VOID** | **But it also has serious negative consequences such as ANONYMITY, which creates SOCIAL VACUUM and SENSE OF DEJECTION among individuals.** **Continued feeling of dejection may motivate people to fall in the trap of ANTI-SOCIAL ACTIVITIES like crime and drug abuse** |

### Environmental consequences

**Overcrowding of people due to rural-urban migration has put PRESSURE ON THE EXISTING SOCIAL AND PHYSICAL INFRASTRUCTURE in the urban areas.**

⚠️ **This ultimately leads to UNPLANNED GROWTH OF URBAN SETTLEMENT and formation of SLUMS SHANTY COLONIES.**

**Apart from this, excessive pressure on the natural resources leads to DEPLETION OF GROUND WATER, AIR POLLUTION, DISPOSAL OF SEWAGE and MANAGEMENT OF SOLID WASTES.**

### Others — the gendered burden

⚠️ **Migration (even excluding the marriage migration) affects the STATUS OF WOMEN directly or indirectly.**

**In the rural areas, male selective out-migration leaving their wives behind puts ADDITIONAL PHYSICAL AS WELL AS MENTAL PRESSURE ON THE WOMEN.**

**Migration of "SEMI-SKILLED" (or unskilled) male workers to the urban areas increases the vulnerability of the women.**

> **This is one of the sharpest points in the chapter and it is easy to miss:**
>
> **When men migrate and women stay, the women left behind take on the ENTIRE agricultural workload, household responsibility and childcare — usually WITHOUT any corresponding increase in decision-making power or land rights.**
>
> **This is often called the "FEMINISATION OF AGRICULTURE" — women do a rising share of farm work while land titles, credit and extension services remain in men's names.**
>
> 🎯 **Strong for GS-I Society, for women's empowerment questions, and for agricultural policy answers.**

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|

| **Immigration / emigration** | Moving into / out of a country |
| **In-migration / out-migration** | Moving into / out of a place within a country |
| **Life time migrant** | Place of birth differs from place of enumeration |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Migration is the third component of population change** — and the only one people choose.
2. **Two Census bases: LIFE TIME MIGRANT (place of birth) and MIGRANT BY PLACE OF LAST RESIDENCE.**
3. **Four internal streams: R–R, R–U, U–U, U–R.**
4. **THE KEY FINDING: women dominate RURAL-TO-RURAL migration because of MARRIAGE; men dominate RURAL-TO-URBAN because of EMPLOYMENT.** **This makes marriage the largest reported reason for migration in India, and makes cities male-heavy and villages female-heavy.**
5. **Maharashtra receives the most in-migrants (then Delhi, Gujarat, Haryana); UP and Bihar send the most.** **Greater Mumbai leads among urban agglomerations.** **INTRA-state migration is the largest category.**
6. **Three emigration waves: indentured labour under the GIRMIT ACT → professionals to the West → semi/unskilled workers to West Asia after the 1970s oil boom.**
7. **Remittances: US$ 11 billion in 2002; Punjab, Kerala and Tamil Nadu are major recipients; "life blood" for poor villages of Bihar, UP and Odisha.**
8. **Out-migration is AGE AND SKILL SELECTIVE** — it removes the young, able-bodied and better-educated, distorting age and sex composition in Uttarakhand, Rajasthan, MP and eastern Maharashtra.
9. **Out-migration is simultaneously a LIFELINE (remittances) and a TRAP (loss of human capital).** Both sides belong in an answer.
10. **Social consequences cut both ways: migrants are agents of social change and build composite culture — but ANONYMITY creates a social vacuum and can lead to anti-social activity.**
11. 🌱 **Environmental: overcrowding, unplanned growth, slums, groundwater depletion, air pollution, sewage and solid waste.**
12. **Male-selective out-migration puts additional physical and mental pressure on the women left behind** — the **feminisation of agriculture**, with rising workload but unchanged land rights.
