## 1️⃣ What International Trade Is

**Exchange of goods and services among countries across national boundaries is known as INTERNATIONAL TRADE.**

**Trade is essentially an exchange of goods and services.** **It may take place between different regions of the same country or between different countries.**

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **LOCAL trade** | Within a locality |
| **NATIONAL / INTERNAL trade** | Within a country |
| **INTERNATIONAL / EXTERNAL trade** | Between countries |

**International trade is considered to be an INDEX OF THE ECONOMIC PROSPERITY of a country.**

**In the ancient times, trade was conducted through BARTER SYSTEM.**

---

## 2️⃣ India's International Trade — the historical shift

**India has been a trading nation since ancient times.**

⚠️ **But the trade pattern underwent a drastic change during the COLONIAL PERIOD.**

**During colonial rule, India was reduced to a supplier of RAW MATERIALS and AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTS to Britain, and an IMPORTER of finished manufactured goods.**

**After Independence, India has diversified both its composition and direction of trade.**

> **The colonial reversal is the historically important point, and it connects directly to your Modern History notes.**
>
> **Before colonial rule, India was a major EXPORTER OF MANUFACTURES — cotton textiles above all — with a favourable balance of trade.**
> **Colonial policy inverted this: India became a supplier of RAW COTTON and an importer of British MILL CLOTH.**
> **The de-industrialisation of Indian handicrafts is exactly this inversion.**
>
> 🎯 **This is why the composition of trade matters more than the volume (Cl.12 *Fundamentals* Ch.9): the same country trading the same total value can be either enriched or impoverished depending on WHICH END of the value chain it occupies.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Changing Pattern of the Composition of India's Exports

**The composition of commodities in India's international trade has been UNDERGOING A CHANGE OVER THE YEARS.**

| Category | Trend |
|---|---|
| **AGRICULTURE AND ALLIED PRODUCTS** | **Share has DECLINED** |
| **ORES AND MINERALS** | **Share has broadly declined/fluctuated** |
| **MANUFACTURED GOODS** | **Share has INCREASED — the dominant category** |
| **CRUDE PETROLEUM AND PETROLEUM PRODUCTS** | **Share has INCREASED substantially** |
| **OTHER COMMODITIES** | **Fluctuating** |

**The share of AGRICULTURE AND ALLIED PRODUCTS has declined, whereas shares of PETROLEUM AND CRUDE PRODUCTS and other commodities have increased.**

**The decline in the share of agriculture and allied products is due to the fact that there is an increase in domestic demand of agricultural products.**

**The share of ORES AND MINERALS has remained constant.**

**The share of MANUFACTURED GOODS has shown an increase.**

**The major items of manufactured goods are ENGINEERING GOODS, GEMS AND JEWELLERY, CHEMICALS AND ALLIED PRODUCTS, TEXTILES.**

⚠️ **GEMS AND JEWELLERY contribute a larger share of India's foreign trade.**

> **The reason NCERT gives for agriculture's declining export share is worth noting because it is not the obvious one: it is due to INCREASED DOMESTIC DEMAND**, not to falling production. **India is eating more of what it grows.** **That reframing matters in an answer.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Changing Pattern of India's Imports

**India's imports have also undergone a change.**

**India faced a serious food shortage during 1950s and 1960s.**

**The dominant items of imports were FOODGRAINS, CAPITAL GOODS, MACHINERY AND EQUIPMENT.**

⚠️ **The SUCCESS OF THE GREEN REVOLUTION helped India to become SELF-SUFFICIENT IN FOODGRAINS**, and the imports of foodgrains declined.

**But the energy crisis of the 1970s pushed up the prices of PETROLEUM and this item became the LEADING IMPORT ITEM.**

**The main imports are: PETROLEUM AND PETROLEUM PRODUCTS, GEMS AND JEWELLERY (uncut gems for processing and re-export), CHEMICALS AND RELATED PRODUCTS, BASE METALS, ELECTRONIC GOODS, MACHINERY, AGRICULTURE AND ALLIED PRODUCTS.**

**PETROLEUM AND PETROLEUM PRODUCTS account for the largest share of India's imports.**

> **The import story is a clean two-stage narrative, and it should be told that way in an answer:**
>
> **STAGE 1 (1950s–60s): FOODGRAINS dominated imports.** India could not feed itself.
> **THE GREEN REVOLUTION removed that dependence** — foodgrain imports collapsed.
> **STAGE 2 (1970s onward): PETROLEUM took its place** as the leading import, after the energy crisis pushed prices up.
>
> **So India traded one dependence for another: FOOD SECURITY WAS ACHIEVED, ENERGY SECURITY WAS NOT.**
>
> 🎯 **That single sentence is a strong opening for a GS-III answer on energy security, and it explains why India's trade deficit tracks the global oil price so closely.**

> **Note the GEMS AND JEWELLERY anomaly: it appears in BOTH the export and import lists.** **India imports UNCUT gems, processes them (Surat is the world centre for diamond cutting and polishing), and RE-EXPORTS them.** **The value added is the labour of cutting and polishing** — a genuine example of India occupying a processing niche in a global value chain.

## 5️⃣ Direction of Trade

**India has trade relations with most of the countries and major trading blocks of the world.**

**India's major trading partners include: ASIA AND ASEAN (the largest share), EUROPE, AMERICA, AFRICA.**

**India's major trading partner countries are U.S.A., CHINA, U.A.E., SAUDI ARABIA, SWITZERLAND, GERMANY, HONG KONG, IRAQ, SINGAPORE.**

**The bulk of India's trade is with ASIA AND ASEAN countries.**

> **The direction of India's trade has shifted decisively toward ASIA**, which reflects both the rise of China and West Asia's role as India's oil supplier. **U.A.E., Saudi Arabia and Iraq appear on the partner list primarily because of PETROLEUM** — the same import that dominates the trade bill.

## 6️⃣ Sea Ports as Gateways of International Trade

**India is surrounded by sea from three sides and is bestowed with a NATURAL GIFT OF WATER.**

**After Independence, the maritime trade of India grew.**

**India has TWELVE MAJOR PORTS and 185 MINOR OR INTERMEDIATE PORTS.**

**These major ports handle 95 per cent of India's foreign trade.**

### The major ports

| Port | Detail |
|---|---|
| **KANDLA (Deendayal Port)** | **A TIDAL PORT, situated at the head of Gulf of Kachchh.** **It was developed as a major port to cater to the needs of western and north western part of the country and also to reduce the pressure at Mumbai port.** **This port is specially designed to receive large quantities of PETROLEUM AND PETROLEUM PRODUCTS and FERTILISER** |
| **MUMBAI** | **A NATURAL HARBOUR and the BIGGEST PORT of the country.** **It has the advantage of its location — it lies closest to the general routes from the countries of Middle East, Mediterranean countries, North Africa, North America and Europe where the major share of country's overseas trade is carried out.** **The port is 20 km long and 6-10 km wide with 54 berths** |
| **JAWAHARLAL NEHRU PORT (NHAVA SHEVA)** | **Was developed as a satellite port to relieve the pressure at the Mumbai port.** **It is the LARGEST CONTAINER PORT in India** |
| **MARMAGAO PORT** | **Situated at the entrance of the Zuari estuary, is a natural harbour in Goa.** **It gained significance after its remodelling in 1961 to handle IRON-ORE exports to Japan.** **Much of the ore comes from the Chitradurg, Bellary, Hospet region** |
| **NEW MANGALORE PORT** | **Located in the state of Karnataka.** **This port caters to the export of IRON-ORE and IRON-CONCENTRATES.** **It also handles fertilisers, petroleum products, edible oils, coffee, tea, wood pulp, yarn, granite stone, molasses, etc.** |
| **KOCHCHI PORT** | **Situated at the head of Vembanad Kayal, popularly known as the QUEEN OF THE ARABIAN SEA.** **It is a NATURAL HARBOUR.** **It has the advantage of its location being closer to the Suez-Colombo route.** **It caters to the needs of Kerala, southern-Karnataka and southern Tamil Nadu** |
| **KOLKATA PORT** | **Situated on the Hugli river, 128 km inland from the Bay of Bengal.** **Like the Mumbai port, this port was also developed by the BRITISH.** **Kolkata had the initial advantage of being the capital of British India.** **The port has lost its significance considerably on account of the diversion of exports to the other ports such as Vishakhapatnam, Paradwip and its satellite port HALDIA** |
| **HALDIA** | **Located 105 km downstream from Kolkata.** **It has been constructed to reduce the congestion at Kolkata port** |
| **PARADWIP PORT** | **Situated in the Jagatsinghpur district of Odisha.** **It has the DEEPEST HARBOUR specially suited to handle very large vessels.** **It has been developed to handle large scale export of IRON ORE** |
| **VISHAKHAPATNAM PORT** | **In Andhra Pradesh.** **Here the LAND-LOCKED HARBOUR is connected to the sea by a channel cut through solid rock and sand.** **This port was designed to handle IRON-ORE exports** |
| **CHENNAI PORT** | **One of the OLDEST PORTS on the eastern coast.** **It is an ARTIFICIAL HARBOUR built in 1859.** **It is not much suitable for large ships because of shallow waters near the coast** |
| **ENNORE** | **A newly developed port in Tamil Nadu, has been constructed 25 km north of Chennai to relieve the pressure at Chennai port** |
| **TUTICORIN PORT** | **Also developed in Tamil Nadu to relieve the pressure of Chennai port.** **It deals with a variety of cargo including coal, salt, food grains, edible oils, sugar, chemicals and petroleum products** |

> **The "relief port" pairings are the most reliably asked pattern — learn them as pairs:**
> **KANDLA relieves MUMBAI** (and serves the north-west)
> **JAWAHARLAL NEHRU / NHAVA SHEVA relieves MUMBAI** (containers)
> **HALDIA relieves KOLKATA**
> **ENNORE relieves CHENNAI**
> **TUTICORIN relieves CHENNAI**

> **The distinctive descriptors, each of which has been asked:**
> **KANDLA — TIDAL port** · **MUMBAI — natural harbour, BIGGEST port** · **JNPT — LARGEST CONTAINER port** · **MARMAGAO — iron ore to JAPAN, remodelled 1961** · **KOCHCHI — "QUEEN OF THE ARABIAN SEA," natural harbour on Vembanad Kayal** · **PARADWIP — DEEPEST harbour** · **VISHAKHAPATNAM — LAND-LOCKED harbour connected by a channel cut through solid rock** · **CHENNAI — oldest on the east coast, ARTIFICIAL harbour built 1859, shallow water**

> **Note the Kolkata decline: 128 km inland on the Hugli, it required constant dredging and has LOST significance to Vishakhapatnam, Paradwip and its own satellite Haldia.** **A port built for a colonial capital lost its rationale when the capital moved and ships grew larger.**

**Trap 1:** *"Foodgrains remain India's largest import."* **PETROLEUM.** Foodgrain imports ended after the Green Revolution.
**Trap 2:** *"Chennai has a natural harbour."* **ARTIFICIAL, built 1859**, and unsuitable for large ships due to shallow water.
**Trap 3:** *"Vishakhapatnam is the deepest harbour."* **PARADWIP has the deepest harbour.** Vishakhapatnam is the **land-locked** one.
**Trap 4:** *"Agriculture's export share fell because production fell."* **Because DOMESTIC DEMAND rose.**
**Trap 5:** *"Mumbai is India's largest container port."* **Jawaharlal Nehru Port (Nhava Sheva).** Mumbai is the **biggest port** overall.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Colonial rule reversed India's trade: from exporting manufactures to supplying raw materials and importing finished goods.** **The composition of trade, not its volume, determines who benefits.**
2. **Export trend: AGRICULTURE DOWN (because DOMESTIC DEMAND rose) · MANUFACTURED GOODS UP · PETROLEUM PRODUCTS UP.**
3. **Manufactured exports: engineering goods, gems and jewellery, chemicals, textiles.**
4. **Import story in two stages: FOODGRAINS dominated in the 1950s–60s → the GREEN REVOLUTION ended that dependence → PETROLEUM took its place after the 1970s energy crisis.** **India achieved FOOD security but not ENERGY security.**
5. **Gems and jewellery appear in both exports and imports** — uncut stones imported, cut and polished (Surat), re-exported.
6. **Direction: the bulk of trade is with ASIA and ASEAN.** Partners include USA, China, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Switzerland, Germany, Hong Kong, Iraq, Singapore — several of them oil suppliers.
7. **12 major and 185 minor ports; the majors handle 95% of foreign trade.**
8. **Relief ports: KANDLA and JNPT relieve Mumbai; HALDIA relieves Kolkata; ENNORE and TUTICORIN relieve Chennai.**
9. **Descriptors: Kandla TIDAL · Mumbai BIGGEST and natural · JNPT LARGEST CONTAINER · Marmagao IRON ORE TO JAPAN (remodelled 1961) · Kochchi "QUEEN OF THE ARABIAN SEA" · Paradwip DEEPEST · Vishakhapatnam LAND-LOCKED, channel cut through solid rock · Chennai OLDEST east-coast, ARTIFICIAL (1859).**
10. **Kolkata sits 128 km inland on the Hugli and has declined; Haldia lies 105 km downstream.** **A colonial-capital port outlived its rationale.**
