## 1️⃣ Environmental Pollution

> **ENVIRONMENTAL POLLUTION results from the RELEASE OF SUBSTANCES AND ENERGY from waste products of human activities.**

**Pollution may be defined as an UNDESIRABLE CHANGE IN THE PHYSICAL, CHEMICAL OR BIOLOGICAL CHARACTERISTICS of air, land and water that may harmfully affect the life or create a potential health hazard of any living organism.**

**Pollution is thus DIRECT OR INDIRECT CHANGES in any component of the biosphere that is harmful to the living component(s), and in particular undesirable for man, adversely affecting the industrial progress, cultural and natural assets or general environment of living society.**

**On the basis of medium through which pollutants are transported and diffused, pollution is classified as: (i) AIR POLLUTION, (ii) WATER POLLUTION, (iii) LAND POLLUTION, and (iv) NOISE POLLUTION.**

---

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Water Pollution

**Water is a UNIVERSAL SOLVENT.**

**When unwanted or excess amount of substances get mixed with water, it results in WATER POLLUTION.**

⚠️ **Human beings are responsible for the pollution of water bodies to a great extent.**

**The main sources of water pollution are:**

| Source | Detail |
|---|---|
| **INDUSTRIAL** | **Industries produce several undesirable products including industrial wastes, polluted waste water, poisonous gases, chemical residuals, numerous heavy metals, dust, smoke, etc.** **Most of the industrial wastes are disposed off in running water or lakes.** **Consequently, poisonous elements reach the reservoirs, rivers and other water bodies, which destroy the bio-system of these waters** |
| **AGRICULTURAL** | **In agriculture, the use of insecticides, pesticides, fertilisers, herbicides and other chemicals is on the rise for higher yield.** **These chemicals reach the water bodies through rainwater** |
| **CULTURAL** | **In India, cultural activities such as religious fairs, rituals, etc. also contribute to water pollution.** **Immersion of idols, dumping of ashes and unburnt corpses** |
| **DOMESTIC / MUNICIPAL** | **Sewage disposal** |

⚠️ **Water borne diseases: the most common water-borne diseases are DIARRHOEA, INTESTINAL WORMS, HEPATITIS, etc.**

**The World Health Organisation says that about ONE-FOURTH of the communicable diseases in India are water-borne.**

### The Ganga and Yamuna — the most polluted

**The GANGA and the YAMUNA are the two most polluted rivers in the country.**

**The GANGA ACTION PLAN and YAMUNA ACTION PLAN were launched to clean these rivers.**

> **The WHO figure is directly quotable and powerful: ABOUT ONE-FOURTH OF INDIA'S COMMUNICABLE DISEASES ARE WATER-BORNE.**
>
> **That converts water pollution from an environmental issue into a PUBLIC HEALTH issue** — and public health arguments generally carry more political weight than ecological ones.
>
> 🎯 **A GS-III answer that opens with the disease burden rather than with river quality indices is framing the problem where it actually bites.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Air Pollution

**Air pollution refers to the presence of contaminants or pollutant substances in the air that INTERFERE WITH HUMAN HEALTH OR WELFARE, or produce other harmful environmental effects.**

**Air pollution occurs by the increase in concentration of harmful gases, such as CARBON DIOXIDE, SULPHUR DIOXIDE, CARBON MONOXIDE, NITROGEN OXIDE, etc.**

**Combustion of fossil fuels, mining and industries are the main sources of air pollution.**

**These processes release oxides of sulphur and nitrogen, hydrocarbons, dust, fumes and other trace gases.**

⚠️ **Air pollution causes various diseases related to RESPIRATORY, NERVOUS AND CIRCULATORY SYSTEMS.**

**SMOKY FOG over cities called as URBAN SMOG is caused by SMOKE FROM THE VEHICLES, INDUSTRIES AND OTHER SOURCES.**

**It has very harmful effects on human health.**

⚠️ **Air pollution also causes ACID RAIN.** **It causes damage to buildings, monuments, crops and other living organisms.**

**The TAJ MAHAL in Agra is affected by acid rain due to the emissions from the Mathura refinery.**

> **The Taj Mahal example is worth carrying** — acid rain from Mathura refinery emissions attacking the marble. **It led to the creation of the TAJ TRAPEZIUM ZONE, a court-mandated protected area within which polluting industries were restricted.** **A rare case where a monument's protection drove regional industrial policy.**

## 4️⃣ Noise Pollution

**Noise pollution refers to the state of UNBEARABLE AND UNCOMFORTABLE TO HUMAN BEINGS caused by noise from different sources.**

⚠️ **The main sources of noise pollution are various factories, mechanised construction and demolition works, automobiles and aircrafts, etc.**

**In fact, the most SERIOUS SOURCE OF NOISE POLLUTION in urban areas is the TRAFFIC.**

**The industrial and household noise is also a source of noise pollution.**

**Sometimes, the noise pollution is also caused by NATURAL FACTORS, such as thunder, lightning, etc.**

⚠️ **The MAGNITUDE OF NOISE POLLUTION varies from place to place.** **The areas which are close to airports, railway stations, bus stands, industrial areas, etc. are more affected.**

**Noise pollution causes IRRITATION, ANGER, DEAFNESS, HYPERTENSION, and other diseases.**

> **Note the point NCERT emphasises: TRAFFIC is the most serious source of urban noise pollution** — not industry. **And unlike other pollutants, noise leaves no residue; it is a pollutant that exists only while being produced.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐ Urban Waste Disposal

**Urban waste disposal is a serious problem in India.**

**Massive garbage in cities has become a serious health hazard.**

**The solid waste generated in cities poses a serious problem for the municipal authorities.**

**The SOLID WASTES refer to a variety of old and used articles, for instance stained small pieces of metals, broken glasswares, plastic containers, polythene bags, ash, floppies, CDs, etc. dumped at different places.**

⚠️ **These discarded materials are also termed as REFUSE, GARBAGE and RUBBISH, etc. and are disposed off from two sources: (i) HOUSEHOLD OR DOMESTIC ESTABLISHMENTS, and (ii) INDUSTRIAL OR COMMERCIAL ESTABLISHMENTS.**

### The consequences

**Untreated wastes ferment slowly and release toxic biogas to the atmosphere, including methane.**

**The dumping of industrial wastes into rivers leads to WATER POLLUTION.**

**Concentration of industrial units in and around urban centres, and lack of enforcement of environmental legislation, results in serious pollution.**

⚠️ **The most CONCENTRATED AND WORST FORM OF POLLUTION is in the metropolitan cities.**

⚠️ **Untreated wastes ferment slowly and release toxic biogas to the atmosphere, including METHANE.**

**The dumping of wastes in low-lying areas leads to the DEGRADATION OF LAND and CONTAMINATION OF GROUNDWATER.**

**In cities and towns, the wastes are collected and dumped in open low-lying areas outside the city.**

⚠️ **It is important to note that PEOPLE LIVING IN SLUMS AND UNAUTHORISED COLONIES ARE THE WORST AFFECTED, as they lack the basic amenities.**

**The most affected are those who live in the vicinity of the dumping grounds.**

> 🌱 **NCERT's observation about who bears the cost is the most important line in this section:** **"PEOPLE LIVING IN SLUMS AND UNAUTHORISED COLONIES ARE THE WORST AFFECTED."**
>
> **Waste is generated disproportionately by the affluent and disposed of disproportionately near the poor.** **The dumping grounds are always at the urban periphery, where land is cheap and residents lack political voice.**
>
> 🎯 **This is ENVIRONMENTAL INJUSTICE stated plainly, and it is the single strongest framing available for a GS-III answer on urban waste.** **The environmental burden and the environmental benefit fall on different people.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐ Rural-Urban Migration and Slums

**The high rate of in-migration to metropolitan cities has been a major issue.**

**The rural-urban migration has been an important factor in the growth of urban population.**

**However, the cities are UNABLE TO ABSORB THE MIGRANTS.**

### Problems of slums

**The migrants are unable to afford proper housing.**

**They live in SLUMS — the residential areas of the LEAST CHOICE.**

**Slums are:**

| Characteristic | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Housing** | **DILAPIDATED HOUSES, POOR VENTILATION, ACUTE OVERCROWDING, faulty alignment of streets, INADEQUATE LIGHTING, PAUCITY OF SAFE DRINKING WATER, water facilities and SANITATION** |
| **Structure** | **These are the areas where houses are structurally weak, and have poor infrastructure** |
| **Economy** | **The people living in slums are the POOREST and are engaged in LOW-PAID, HIGH-RISK-PRONE, UNORGANISED SECTORS of the urban economy** |
| **Health** | **Consequently, they are UNDERNOURISHED, PRONE TO VARIOUS TYPES OF DISEASES AND ILLNESS and cannot afford to give proper education to their children** |
| **Social** | **The poverty makes them vulnerable to DRUG ABUSE, ALCOHOLISM, CRIME, VANDALISM, ESCAPISM, APATHY and ultimately SOCIAL EXCLUSION** |

> **The phrase "RESIDENTIAL AREAS OF THE LEAST CHOICE" is NCERT's own, and it is precise.** **Nobody chooses a slum; a slum is what remains when every other option is unaffordable.**
>
> **And note the economic characterisation carefully: slum dwellers are engaged in LOW-PAID, HIGH-RISK, UNORGANISED work.** **They are NOT unemployed — they are employed on adverse terms, doing work the city depends on.**
>
> 🎯 **The framing that avoids the standard moralising: the problem is not that slum dwellers are outside the urban economy but that they are INSIDE it without any of its protections.** **This is why the policy answer is tenure security, in-situ upgradation and labour protection — not demolition.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐ Land Degradation

**There is pressure on agricultural land not only because the per capita availability of land is low, but also there is a constant degradation of land.**

> **LAND DEGRADATION refers to a TEMPORARY OR PERMANENT DECLINE IN THE PRODUCTIVE CAPACITY OF THE LAND.**

⚠️ **Land is considered as a degraded land when it suffers from any of the following: LOSS OF NATURAL VEGETATION, SOIL EROSION, WATERLOGGING, SALINITY AND ALKALINITY, etc.**

### Types of degraded land

**Broadly, the degraded lands can be classified into TWO CATEGORIES:**

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **(i) LANDS DEGRADED BY NATURAL AGENTS** | **Gullied/ravinous land, land with or without scrub, sandy areas, barren rocky areas, steep sloping area, and glacial areas** |
| **(ii) LANDS DEGRADED BY HUMAN ACTIVITIES** | **Surface water logging, sub-surface water logging, marsh, saline, alkaline, degraded pastures, degraded forests, degraded land under plantation crop, mining and industrial wastelands, and shifting cultivation areas** |

**In India, the degraded land can be classified into three broad categories on the basis of the process of land degradation:**

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **PERMANENT DEGRADATION** | **Barren rocky areas, steep sloping area and glacial areas — cannot be brought under cultivation |
| **WATER EROSION** | **Gullied and ravinous land — the largest category |
| **WIND EROSION** | **Sandy areas |
| **SALINITY/ALKALINITY AND WATERLOGGING** | **From over-irrigation |

> **The two-way classification — NATURAL agents vs HUMAN activities — is the examinable structure, and the human list is the longer one.**
>
> **Note what appears under HUMAN causes: surface and sub-surface waterlogging, salinity and alkalinity, degraded pastures, degraded forests, MINING AND INDUSTRIAL WASTELANDS, and SHIFTING CULTIVATION areas.**
>
> ⚠️ **Two of these connect directly to earlier chapters: WATERLOGGING AND SALINITY are precisely what the Indira Gandhi Canal produced (Ch.9), and MINING WASTELANDS are what Ch.7's mineral belts leave behind.** **The development described in Chapters 5–9 is the cause of the degradation described in Chapter 12.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Pollution = an undesirable change in the physical, chemical or biological characteristics of air, land and water.** **Four types: air, water, land, noise.**
2. **Water pollution sources: INDUSTRIAL · AGRICULTURAL (fertilisers, pesticides via rainwater) · CULTURAL (religious fairs, idol immersion) · DOMESTIC.**
3. **WHO: about ONE-FOURTH of India's communicable diseases are WATER-BORNE.** **Water pollution is a public health problem, not merely an ecological one.**
4. **Ganga and Yamuna are the most polluted rivers; Ganga and Yamuna Action Plans were launched.**
5. **Air pollution from fossil fuel combustion, mining and industry; causes respiratory, nervous and circulatory disease, URBAN SMOG and ACID RAIN.** **The TAJ MAHAL is damaged by acid rain from Mathura refinery emissions.**
6. **TRAFFIC is the most serious source of urban noise pollution** — causing irritation, anger, deafness and hypertension.
7. **Untreated urban waste ferments and releases toxic biogas including METHANE; dumping degrades land and contaminates groundwater.**
8. 🌱 **People in SLUMS AND UNAUTHORISED COLONIES ARE WORST AFFECTED by waste** — waste is generated by the affluent and dumped near the poor. **Environmental injustice.**
9. **Slums are "residential areas of the LEAST CHOICE."** **Their residents are in LOW-PAID, HIGH-RISK, UNORGANISED work — inside the urban economy without its protections.**
10. **Consequences: undernourishment, disease, lost schooling, and vulnerability to drug abuse, alcoholism, crime, vandalism, escapism, apathy and SOCIAL EXCLUSION.**
11. **Land degradation splits into NATURAL agents (gullies, ravines, scrub, sandy, barren rocky, steep slopes, glacial) and HUMAN activities (waterlogging, salinity/alkalinity, degraded pastures and forests, MINING and industrial wastelands, shifting cultivation).**
12. 🎯 **The book's closing argument: the waterlogging and salinity of Ch.9's canal, and the mining wastelands of Ch.7's mineral belts, appear here as DEGRADATION.** **Development creates the problems that development must then solve.**
