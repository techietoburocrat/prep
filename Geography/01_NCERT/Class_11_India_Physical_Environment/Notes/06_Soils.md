## 1️⃣ What Soil Is

> **SOIL is the mixture of rock debris and organic materials which develop on the earth's surface.**

**The major factors affecting the formation of soil are RELIEF, PARENT MATERIAL, CLIMATE, VEGETATION and other life-forms and TIME.** **Besides these, human activities also influence it to a large extent.**

**Components of the soil are MINERAL PARTICLES, HUMUS, WATER and AIR.**

⚠️ **The actual amount of each of these depend upon the type of soil.** **Some soils are deficient in one or more of these, while there are some others that have varied combinations.**

> **Restating the crucial asymmetry (Cl.8 Ch.2): soil takes CENTURIES TO MILLENNIA to form a single centimetre, and can be lost in ONE MONSOON.** **Slow to build, fast to lose — that is the entire argument for soil conservation, and it belongs at the start of any answer on the subject.**

## 2️⃣ Classification of Soils

**On the basis of genesis, colour, composition and location, the soils of India have been classified into:**

**The ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) has classified the soils of India into 8 categories:**

```
   1. ALLUVIAL SOILS
   2. BLACK SOILS
   3. RED AND YELLOW SOILS
   4. LATERITE SOILS
   5. ARID SOILS
   6. SALINE SOILS
   7. PEATY SOILS
   8. FOREST SOILS
```

## 3️⃣ ⭐ The Eight Soils — The Master Table

### 1. ALLUVIAL SOILS

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Extent** | **The most widespread soil group. They cover about 40% of the total area of the country** |
| **Where** | **The entire Northern Plains.** **They also occur in eastern coastal plains particularly in the deltas of the Mahanadi, the Godavari, the Krishna and the Kaveri rivers** |
| **Formed by** | **Depositional soils — transported and deposited by rivers and streams** |
| **Texture** | **Varies from sandy loam to clay.** **They are generally rich in potash but poor in phosphorous** |
| **Colour** | **Light grey to ash grey** |
| **Sub-types** | **KHADAR (new alluvium, deposited by floods annually, enriches the soil by depositing fine silts) and BHANGAR (older alluvium, clayey, contains KANKAR — lime nodules)** |
| **Crops** | **Wheat, rice, sugarcane, pulses — intensively cultivated** |

### 2. BLACK SOIL (REGUR)

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Where** | **Covers most of the DECCAN PLATEAU** — parts of Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh and some parts of Tamil Nadu |
| **Formed from** | **The Deccan Trap — climatic conditions along with the parent rock material** |
| **Colour** | **Black; also called REGUR** |
| **Texture** | **Generally clayey, deep and impermeable** |
| **Water** | **They SWELL and become STICKY when wet and SHRINK when dried.** **So, during the dry season, these soil develop WIDE CRACKS.** **Thus, there occurs a kind of "SELF PLOUGHING"** |
| **Nutrients** | **Rich in lime, iron, magnesia and alumina.** **They also contain potash.** **But they LACK in phosphorous, nitrogen and organic matter** |
| **Crops** | **COTTON** ("black cotton soil") |

> **The "self-ploughing" property is the detail worth knowing.** **Regur is rich in clay, so it swells when wet and shrinks when dry, producing deep cracks in summer.** **Those cracks let air deep into the soil — a NATURAL AERATION AND SELF-PLOUGHING process.** **Combined with exceptional moisture retention, this is precisely what cotton needs in a region with a marked dry season.** **The soil suits the crop for a mechanical reason, not just a chemical one.**

### 3. RED AND YELLOW SOILS

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Where** | **Develop on CRYSTALLINE IGNEOUS ROCKS in areas of LOW RAINFALL in the eastern and southern part of the Deccan Plateau** |
| **Also found** | **Along the piedmont zone of the Western Ghat, in parts of Odisha and Chhattisgarh and in the southern parts of the middle Ganga plain** |
| **Colour** | **RED because of the DIFFUSION OF IRON in crystalline and metamorphic rocks.** **It looks YELLOW when it occurs in a HYDRATED form** |
| **Texture** | **The fine-grained red and yellow soils are normally fertile, whereas coarse-grained soils found in dry upland areas are poor in fertility** |
| **Nutrients** | **Generally poor in nitrogen, phosphorous and humus** |

### 4. LATERITE SOILS

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Name from** | **Latin word "LATER" which means BRICK** |
| **Develops in** | **Areas with HIGH TEMPERATURE and HIGH RAINFALL** |
| **Result of** | **INTENSE LEACHING due to tropical rains.** **With rain, lime and silica are leached away, and soils rich in iron oxide and aluminium compound are left behind** |
| **Humus** | **Humus content of the soil is REMOVED FAST BY BACTERIA that thrives well in high temperature** |
| **Nutrients** | **Poor in organic matter, nitrogen, phosphate and calcium, while iron oxide and potash are in excess.** **Hence, laterites are NOT SUITABLE FOR CULTIVATION** |
| **With manure** | **However, application of manures and fertilisers are required for making the soils fertile for cultivation** |
| **Where** | **Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Madhya Pradesh and the hilly areas of Odisha and Assam** |
| **Crops** | **After adopting appropriate soil conservation techniques particularly in the hilly areas of Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, this soil is very useful for growing PLANTATION CROPS like TEA, COFFEE, RUBBER, CINCHONA, COCONUT, ARECANUT etc.** |
| **Other use** | **Used as BUILDING BRICKS** |

> **Laterite is the counter-intuitive soil and the one most often asked.** **It forms in the WETTEST regions — and it is INFERTILE.**
>
> **Why? Two mechanisms working together:**
> 1. **LEACHING** — heavy rain dissolves and carries away the soluble nutrients (lime and silica), leaving only insoluble iron and aluminium oxides.
> 2. **BACTERIAL DECOMPOSITION** — high temperatures let bacteria destroy humus faster than it accumulates.
>
> **Heavy rain produces poor soil.** **This is the same principle as Amazon rainforest soil (Cl.7 Ch.8) and the same reason Cherrapunji has bare rock (Ch.2).**

### 5. ARID SOILS

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Colour** | **Range from red to brown** |
| **Texture** | **Generally sandy in structure and SALINE in nature.** **In some areas, the salt content is so high that common salt is obtained by evaporating the saline water** |
| **Climate** | **Due to the dry climate, high temperature, evaporation is faster and the soil lacks moisture and humus** |
| **Nutrients** | **Nitrogen is insufficient and the phosphate content is normal** |
| **Lower horizons** | **Lower horizons of the soil are occupied by KANKAR layers because of the increasing calcium content downwards.** **The kankar layer formation in the bottom horizons RESTRICTS THE INFILTRATION OF WATER** |
| **Where** | **Western Rajasthan** |
| **With irrigation** | **When irrigated these soils become cultivable as has been in the case of western Rajasthan** |

### 6. SALINE SOILS

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Also known as** | **USARA soils** |
| **Contain** | **A larger proportion of sodium, potassium and magnesium, and thus, they are INFERTILE, and do not support any vegetative growth** |
| **Nutrients** | **They have more salts, largely because of dry climate and poor drainage.** **They occur in arid and semi-arid regions, and in waterlogged and swampy areas.** **Their structure ranges from sandy to loamy.** **They LACK IN NITROGEN AND CALCIUM** |
| **Where** | **Western Gujarat, deltas of the eastern coast and in Sunderban areas of West Bengal** |
| **Cause** | **In the RANN OF KACHCHH, the South-West Monsoon brings salt particles and deposits there as a crust.** **Seawater intrusions in the deltas promote the occurrence of saline soils** |
| **Human cause** | **In the areas of intensive cultivation with excessive use of irrigation, especially in areas of green revolution, the fertile alluvial soils are becoming saline.** **Excessive irrigation with dry climatic conditions promotes CAPILLARY ACTION, which results in the deposition of salt on the top layer of the soil** |
| **Remedy** | **In such areas, especially in Punjab and Haryana, farmers are advised to add GYPSUM to solve the problem of alkalinity in the soil** |

> **The salinisation-by-irrigation mechanism is a top-tier GS-III point, and NCERT explains it precisely.**
>
> **Excessive irrigation in a dry climate promotes CAPILLARY ACTION** — water is drawn upward through the soil, evaporates at the surface, and **leaves its dissolved salts behind as a crust.**
>
> **So the very irrigation that made the Green Revolution possible is now degrading the soil it fed.** **Punjab and Haryana — the Green Revolution heartland — are the affected areas, and the remedy is GYPSUM.**
>
> 🎯 **"The Green Revolution's irrigation is salinising the soil it enriched" is a compact, evidence-based line for any answer on sustainable agriculture.**

### 7. PEATY SOILS

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Found in** | **The areas of HEAVY RAINFALL and HIGH HUMIDITY, where there is a good growth of vegetation** |
| **Formation** | **Thus, large quantity of dead organic matter accumulates in these areas, and this gives a rich humus and organic content to the soil** |
| **Organic matter** | **Organic matter in these soils may go even up to 40–50 per cent** |
| **Character** | **These soils are normally HEAVY and BLACK in colour** |
| **Where** | **At many places, they are ALKALINE also.** **It occurs widely in the northern part of Bihar, southern part of Uttaranchal and the coastal areas of West Bengal, Odisha and Tamil Nadu** |

### 8. FOREST SOILS

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Where** | **Formed in the forest areas where sufficient rainfall is available** |
| **Texture** | **The soils vary in structure and texture depending on the mountain environment where they are formed.** **They are LOAMY AND SILTY ON VALLEY SIDES and COARSE-GRAINED IN THE UPPER SLOPES** |
| **Snow-covered areas** | **In the snow-bound areas of the Himalayas, they experience DENUDATION, and are ACIDIC WITH LOW HUMUS CONTENT** |
| **Lower valleys** | **The soils found in the lower valleys are FERTILE** |

## 4️⃣ Soil Degradation and Erosion

**In a broader sense, SOIL DEGRADATION can be defined as the decline in soil fertility, when the nutritional status declines and depth of the soil goes down due to erosion and misuse.**

**Soil degradation is the main factor leading to the depleting soil resource base in India.**

### Soil erosion

> **The destruction of the soil cover is described as SOIL EROSION.**

**The soil forming processes and the erosional processes of running water and wind go on simultaneously.** **But generally, there is a balance between these two processes.** **Sometimes, such a balance is disturbed by natural or human factors, leading to a greater rate of removal of soil.**

### Types of erosion

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Water erosion** | **More serious and occurs extensively in different parts of India** |
| **SHEET EROSION** | **The finer and more fertile top soil is removed.** **Takes place on level lands after a heavy shower and the soil removal is not easily noticeable.** **But it is harmful since it removes the finer and more fertile top soil** |
| **GULLY EROSION** | **Common on steep slopes.** **Gullies deepen with rainfall, cut the agricultural lands into small fragments and make them unfit for cultivation.** **A region with a large number of deep gullies or ravines is called a BADLAND TOPOGRAPHY** |
| **RAVINES** | **Ravines are widespread, in the CHAMBAL BASIN.** **Besides this, they are also found in Tamil Nadu and West Bengal** |
| **Wind erosion** | **Significant in arid and semi-arid regions.** **Wind blows loose soil off flat or sloping land known as wind erosion** |
| **Human causes** | **DEFORESTATION is one of the major causes of soil erosion.** **Besides, faulty methods of agriculture, overgrazing, mining and quarrying, construction of roads and railways etc. accelerate soil erosion** |

⚠️ **Ploughing along the wrong direction, i.e., up and down the slope form channels for the quick flow of water leading to soil erosion.** **Faulty methods of agriculture, overgrazing, mining and quarrying accelerate the process.**

## 5️⃣ Soil Conservation

> **SOIL CONSERVATION is a methodology to maintain soil fertility, prevent soil erosion and exhaustion, and improve the degraded condition of the soil.**

### The measures

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Check on RUNNING WATER** | **Erosion of soil by running water can be checked by CONTOUR BUNDING, CONTOUR TERRACING, regulated forestry, controlled grazing, cover cropping, mixed farming and crop rotation** |
| **CONTOUR BUNDING / TERRACING** | **Involves the construction of bunds/terraces ALONG THE CONTOURS, checking the flow of water down the slope** |
| **GULLY plugging** | **The gullies should be plugged with BRUSHWOOD and BOULDERS to check erosion** |
| **STRIP CROPPING** | **Large fields can be divided into strips.** **Strips of grass are left to grow between the crops.** **This breaks up the force of the wind** |
| **SHELTER BELTS** | **Planting lines of trees to create shelter.** **These rows of trees also act as WIND BREAKS** |
| **Regulated forestry** | **Forests must be maintained; felling regulated** |
| **Controlled grazing** | **Prevents removal of vegetative cover** |
| **Crop rotation** | **Restores fertility, particularly through leguminous crops that fix nitrogen** |
| **Gypsum for alkalinity** | **In Punjab and Haryana, gypsum is added to counter salinity/alkalinity** |

**In India, the CENTRAL SOIL CONSERVATION BOARD, set up by the Government of India, has prepared a number of plans for soil conservation in different parts of the country.**

**These plans are based on the SOIL TYPES, TERRAIN and FARMING CAPABILITY.**

⚠️ **Very little emphasis has been laid on the sustainable use of soil resources in India — the need is to make a survey of soil types and cultivate as per the capability of the soil.**

> **Why contour ploughing works — the mechanism, worth stating in an answer:** **Water runs downhill. Furrows running UP AND DOWN the slope are channels ACCELERATING water downhill, carrying soil with it. Furrows running ACROSS the slope are tiny DAMS that catch and slow the water, letting it soak in.** **Same field, same plough, opposite outcome — decided entirely by direction.**

**Trap 1:** *"Laterite soil is fertile because it forms in high-rainfall areas."* **The opposite — leaching and bacterial humus destruction make it infertile.**
**Trap 2:** *"Black soil is rich in nitrogen and phosphorous."* **It LACKS phosphorous, nitrogen and organic matter.** It is rich in lime, iron, magnesia, alumina and potash.
**Trap 3:** *"Alluvial soil is rich in phosphorous."* **Rich in POTASH, poor in PHOSPHOROUS.**
**Trap 4:** *"Saline soils occur only naturally."* **Excessive irrigation in dry climates causes them, via capillary action.**
**Trap 5:** *"Bhangar is the newer alluvium."* **Khadar is newer.**
**Trap 6:** *"Sheet erosion is easily noticeable."* **It is NOT easily noticeable — which is what makes it dangerous.** It removes the finest, most fertile topsoil.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Soil = rock debris + organic material. Factors: relief, parent material, climate, vegetation, time — plus human activity.**
2. **ICAR's EIGHT types: Alluvial · Black · Red & Yellow · Laterite · Arid · Saline · Peaty · Forest.**
3. **ALLUVIAL: ~40% of India, most widespread; rich in POTASH, poor in PHOSPHOROUS; Khadar (new) and Bhangar (old, kankar).**
4. **BLACK/REGUR: from the Deccan Trap; clayey; SWELLS wet, CRACKS dry → SELF-PLOUGHING; ideal for COTTON.** Lacks phosphorous, nitrogen and organic matter.
5. **RED from iron diffusion; YELLOW when hydrated. On crystalline rock in LOW rainfall areas.**
6. **LATERITE ("later" = brick): high temperature + high rainfall → LEACHING removes lime and silica; bacteria destroy humus. INFERTILE — but good for tea, coffee, rubber, cinchona, coconut, arecanut with fertiliser. Used as building brick.**
7. **ARID: sandy, saline, KANKAR layer restricting infiltration; western Rajasthan; cultivable when irrigated.**
8. **SALINE (USARA): excessive irrigation in dry climates → CAPILLARY ACTION → salt crust. Punjab and Haryana affected. Remedy: GYPSUM.** **The Green Revolution's irrigation is salinising the soil it enriched.**
9. **PEATY: organic matter 40–50%; heavy, black, often alkaline; N. Bihar, S. Uttarakhand, coastal WB/Odisha/TN.**
10. **FOREST: loamy/silty in valleys, coarse on upper slopes; acidic with low humus where snow-bound.**
11. **Erosion: SHEET (not easily noticeable, removes the finest topsoil) · GULLY (steep slopes → ravines → BADLAND, Chambal) · WIND.**
12. **Conservation: contour bunding and terracing, gully plugging with brushwood and boulders, strip cropping, shelter belts, controlled grazing, crop rotation, gypsum.** **Central Soil Conservation Board.**
