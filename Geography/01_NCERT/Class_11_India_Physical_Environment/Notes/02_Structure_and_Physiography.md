## 1️⃣ ⭐ Geological Structure — Three Divisions

⭐ **The geological history of India can be divided into three broad divisions:**

| Division | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **THE PENINSULAR BLOCK** | ⭐ **The oldest.** A **fragment of Gondwanaland**, composed of **ancient gneisses and granites**. ⭐ **Since the Cambrian period, the Peninsula has been standing like a rigid block** with the exception of some of its western coast which is submerged beneath the sea and some other parts changed due to tectonic activity. ⭐ **Thus, as compared to the Himalayan and Northern plains, it has been a stable land mass** |
| ⭐ **THE HIMALAYAS AND OTHER PENINSULAR MOUNTAINS** | ⭐ **Young, weak and flexible in their geological structure unlike the rigid and stable Peninsular Block.** ⭐ **Consequently, they are still subjected to the interplay of exogenic and endogenic forces, resulting in the development of faults, folds and thrust plains.** ⭐ **These mountains are tectonic in origin, dissected by fast-flowing rivers which are in their youthful stage** |
| ⭐ **INDO-GANGETIC PLAIN** | ⭐ **The third geological division of India comprises the plains formed by the river Indus, the Ganga and the Brahmaputra.** ⭐ **Originally, it was a geo-synclinal depression which attained its maximum development during the third phase of the Himalayan mountain formation approximately about 64 million years ago.** ⭐ **Since then, it has been gradually filled by the sediments brought by the Himalayan and Peninsular rivers** |

⭐ **The average depth of alluvial deposits in these plains ranges from 1,000–2,000 m.**

> ⭐⭐ **The three divisions are three DIFFERENT AGES, and this single fact explains almost everything else in the chapter:**
> ⭐ **The Peninsula is RIGID and STABLE** — hence broad shallow valleys, mature rivers, mineral wealth from long erosion (Cl.6 Ch.6), and low seismicity.
> ⭐ **The Himalayas are YOUNG and FLEXIBLE** — hence deep gorges, youthful fast rivers, active faulting, and severe earthquakes.
> ⭐ **The Plains are a FILLED TROUGH** — hence 1,000–2,000 m of alluvium, extraordinary fertility, and the densest population on Earth.
>
> ⭐ **Rigid old block · young flexible mountains · a trough filled with the mountains' debris.** That is the whole geological story of India in one line.

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Northern and North-Eastern Mountains

⭐ **The northern and north-eastern mountains consist of the Himalayas and the North-eastern hills.** ⭐ **The Himalayas consist of a series of parallel mountain ranges.**

### ⭐⭐ The longitudinal division — three parallel ranges

| Range | ⭐ Also called | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **HIMADRI** | **Greater / Inner Himalaya** | ⭐ **The most continuous and highest range** — average altitude **6,000 m**. ⭐ **Perennially snowbound**; source of the great glaciers. ⭐ **All the loftiest peaks** |
| ⭐ **HIMACHAL** | **Lesser Himalaya** | ⭐ **Altitude 3,700–4,500 m; average width 50 km.** ⭐ **Highly compressed and altered rocks.** ⭐ **Ranges: PIR PANJAL (the longest), DHAULADHAR, MAHABHARAT.** ⭐ **Famous valleys: KASHMIR, KANGRA, KULLU.** ⭐ **Hill stations** |
| ⭐ **SHIWALIK** | **Outer Himalaya** | ⭐ **Altitude 900–1,100 m; width 10–50 km.** ⭐ **Composed of UNCONSOLIDATED SEDIMENTS** brought down by rivers from the main Himalayan ranges. ⭐ **DUNS lie between the Shiwalik and the Himachal — Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun** |

> ⭐ **"Hi-Hi-Shi," north to south.** Height decreases southward, and so does age. ⚠️ ⭐ **The Shiwaliks' unconsolidated sediment composition is the reason Himalayan landslides are so severe** — it is loose material on steep slopes in a seismically active belt (Ch.7).

### ⭐⭐ The regional (west→east) division

⭐ **The Himalayas have also been divided on the basis of regions from west to east, demarcated by river valleys:**

| Region | ⭐ Between | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Kashmir / Punjab / Himachal Himalaya** | ⭐ **Indus ↔ Satluj** | ⭐ **Karakoram, Ladakh, Zaskar and Pir Panjal ranges.** ⭐ **Cold desert of Ladakh.** ⭐ **KARE WA formations** — thick deposits of glacial clay in the Kashmir valley, used for **saffron** cultivation. ⭐ **Important passes: Zoji La (Great Himalaya), Banihal (Pir Panjal), Photu La (Zaskar), Khardung La (Ladakh).** ⭐ **Famous freshwater lake Dal; saltwater lakes Pangong Tso and Tso Moriri** |
| ⭐ **Kumaon Himalaya** | ⭐ **Satluj ↔ Kali** | ⭐ **The Ganga and the Yamuna have their sources here.** ⭐ **Duns: Chandigarh-Kalka dun, Nalagarh dun, Dehra Dun, Harike dun, Kota dun** |
| ⭐ **Central / Nepal Himalaya** | ⭐ **Kali ↔ Teesta** | ⭐ **The tallest peaks — Mt. Everest, Kanchenjunga.** ⭐ **Two distinctive features: the "SHIWALIK" here is much reduced, and the Himalayas are at their tallest** |
| ⭐ **Eastern / Assam Himalaya** | ⭐ **Teesta ↔ Dihang** | ⭐ **Kanchenjunga, Namcha Barwa.** ⭐ **The Brahmaputra makes a sharp U-turn around Namcha Barwa** |

### ⭐ The Purvanchal (North-Eastern Hills)

⭐ **The Brahmaputra marks the eastern-most boundary of the Himalayas.** ⭐ **Beyond the Dihang gorge, the Himalayas bend sharply to the south and spread along the eastern boundary of India.**

⭐ **They are known as the PURVANCHAL or the Eastern hills and mountains.** ⭐ **These hills running through the north-eastern states are mostly composed of strong SANDSTONES which are sedimentary rocks.** ⭐ **Covered with dense forests, they mostly run as parallel ranges and valleys.**

⭐ **The Purvanchal comprises the ⭐ PATKAI BUM, the NAGA HILLS, the MANIPUR HILLS and the MIZO (LUSHAI) HILLS.**

### ⭐ Key peaks and passes

| Peak | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Mt. Everest (8,848 m)** | World's highest — in **Nepal** |
| ⭐ **K2 / Godwin-Austen (8,611 m)** | 2nd highest in the world; in the **Karakoram**, PoK |
| ⭐ **Kanchenjunga (8,586 m)** | ⭐ **Highest peak wholly within India** (Sikkim) |
| ⭐ **Nanda Devi, Kamet, Namcha Barwa, Nanga Parbat** | Other major peaks |

⭐ **Important passes: ZOJI LA (Ladakh) · BANIHAL (Pir Panjal) · SHIPKI LA (Himachal) · NATHU LA (Sikkim) · BOMDI LA (Arunachal) · ROHTANG (Himachal) · KHARDUNG LA (Ladakh) · PHOTU LA (Zaskar).**

> ⭐ **The KAREWA formations are a favourite specific:** ⭐ **thick deposits of glacial clay and other materials embedded with moraines in the Kashmir valley, famous for SAFFRON cultivation.** **A precise, small fact of exactly the kind Prelims uses.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Northern Plain

⭐ **The northern plain is formed by the alluvial deposits brought by three river systems — the INDUS, the GANGA and the BRAHMAPUTRA.** ⭐ **It spreads over an area of 7 lakh sq km — about 2,400 km long and 240–320 km broad.**

⭐ **It is one of the most densely populated regions of the world.**

### ⭐⭐ The four relief belts, north to south

| Belt | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **BHABAR** | ⭐ **A narrow belt, 8–16 km wide, running parallel to the Shiwaliks.** ⭐ **The rivers deposit PEBBLES here.** ⭐ **All the streams DISAPPEAR in this bhabar belt** |
| ⭐ **TARAI** | ⭐ **South of the bhabar, 10–20 km wide.** ⭐ **All the streams RE-EMERGE and create a wet, swampy and marshy region.** ⭐ **Originally a thickly forested region full of wildlife; much has been cleared for agriculture** |
| ⭐ **BHANGAR** | ⭐ **The OLDER alluvium, forming alluvial terraces above the level of the flood plains.** ⭐ **Contains calcareous deposits locally known as KANKAR** |
| ⭐ **KHADAR** | ⭐ **The NEWER, YOUNGER deposits of the flood plains.** ⭐ **They are RENEWED ALMOST EVERY YEAR and so are FERTILE, thus, ideal for intensive agriculture** |

> ⭐⭐ **Bhabar and Tarai are cause and effect, which is how to remember them.** ⭐ **Rivers leaving the mountains hit a belt of coarse PEBBLES so porous that the water SINKS out of sight (Bhabar).** ⭐ **A little further south, where the sediment is finer and less permeable, that water COMES BACK UP (Tarai) — creating swamp.** ⭐ **One belt swallows the rivers; the next spits them out.**

> ⭐ **Bhangar vs Khadar — the pairing most often asked.** ⭐ **KHADAR = newer, lower, flooded and renewed almost every year, MOST FERTILE.** ⭐ **BHANGAR = older, higher terraces, above the flood level, contains KANKAR nodules, less fertile.**

⭐ **The rivers in their lower course split into numerous channels due to the deposition of silt.** ⭐ **These channels are known as DISTRIBUTARIES.**

⭐ **The northern plains are divided into three sections: the PUNJAB PLAINS (formed by the Indus and its tributaries; the larger part lies in Pakistan; dominated by DOABS), the GANGA PLAINS (from Haryana to West Bengal), and the BRAHMAPUTRA PLAINS (mainly Assam).**

⭐ **MAJULI, in the Brahmaputra, is the world's largest river island.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Peninsular Plateau

⭐ **Rising from the height of 150 m above the river plains up to an elevation of 600–900 m is the irregular triangle known as the PENINSULAR PLATEAU.**

⭐ **Delhi ridge in the northwest (extension of Aravalis), the Rajmahal hills in the east, Gir range in the west and the Cardamom hills in the south constitute the outer extent of the Peninsular plateau.**

⭐ **This is one of the oldest and the most stable landmass of India.** ⭐ **The general elevation of the plateau is from the west to the east, which is also proved by the pattern of the flow of rivers.**

### ⭐ Distinctive features

⭐ **Some of the important physiographic features of this region are TORS, BLOCK MOUNTAINS, RIFT VALLEYS, SPURS, BARE ROCKY STRUCTURES, SERIES OF HUMMOCKY HILLS and WALL-LIKE QUARTZITE DYKES offering natural sites for water storage.**

⭐ **The western and northwestern part of the plateau has an emphatic presence of black soil.**

### ⭐⭐ The three broad groups

⭐ **On the basis of the prominent relief features, the Peninsular plateau can be divided into three broad groups:**

| Group | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **THE DECCAN PLATEAU** | ⭐ **Bordered by the Western Ghats in the west, Eastern Ghats in the east and the Satpura, Maikal range and Mahadeo hills in the north.** ⭐ **Western Ghats are locally known by different names such as SAHYADRI in Maharashtra, NILGIRI hills in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu and ANAIMALAI hills and CARDAMOM hills in Kerala.** ⭐ **Western Ghats are comparatively higher in elevation and more continuous than the Eastern Ghats.** ⭐ **Their average elevation is about 1,500 m with the height increasing from north to south.** ⭐ **ANAIMUDI (2,695 m), the highest peak of Peninsular plateau is located on the Anaimalai hills of the Western Ghats followed by DODABETTA (2,637 m) on the Nilgiri hills** |
| ⭐ **THE CENTRAL HIGHLANDS** | ⭐ **Bounded to the west by the Aravali range.** ⭐ **The Satpura range is formed by a series of seven mountains.** ⭐ **The general elevation of the Central Highlands ranges between 700–1,000 m above the mean sea level and it slopes towards the north and northeastern directions.** ⭐ **Most of the tributaries of the river Yamuna have their origin in the Vindhyan and Kaimur ranges.** ⭐ **Banas is the only significant tributary of the river Chambal that originates from the Aravalli in the west.** ⭐ **An eastern extension of the Central Highland is formed by the Rajmahal hills, to the south of which lies a large reserve of mineral resources in the CHHOTANAGPUR PLATEAU** |
| ⭐ **THE NORTH-EASTERN PLATEAU** | ⭐ **In fact it is an extension of the main Peninsular plateau.** ⭐ **It is believed that due to the force exerted by the northeastward movement of the Indian plate at the time of the Himalayan origin, a huge fault was created between the Rajmahal hills and the Meghalaya plateau.** ⭐ **Later, this depression got filled up by the deposition activity of the numerous rivers.** ⭐ **Today, Meghalaya and Karbi Anglong plateau stand detached from the main Peninsular Block.** ⭐ **The Meghalaya plateau is further sub-divided into three: (i) The GARO Hills; (ii) The KHASI Hills; (iii) The JAINTIA Hills, named after the tribal groups inhabiting this region.** ⭐ **An extension of this is also seen in the Karbi Anglong hills of Assam** |

⭐ **Similar to the Chotanagpur Plateau, the Meghalaya plateau is also rich in mineral resources like coal, iron ore, sillimanite, limestone and uranium.** ⭐ **This area receives maximum rainfall from the south west monsoon. As a result, the Meghalaya plateau has a highly eroded surface. CHERRAPUNJI displays a bare rocky surface devoid of any permanent vegetation cover.**

> ⭐ **The Meghalaya plateau's detachment is a nice piece of connected reasoning:** ⭐ **the northeastward movement of the Indian plate created a FAULT between the Rajmahal hills and the Meghalaya plateau, which later filled with sediment.** ⭐ **So Meghalaya is a piece of the Peninsular Block that has been separated from it by tectonics and buried sediment** — geologically peninsular, geographically north-eastern.

> ⚠️ ⭐ **The Cherrapunji paradox is worth noting:** ⭐ **the wettest place on Earth has a BARE ROCKY SURFACE devoid of permanent vegetation.** ⭐ **Extreme rainfall has leached and eroded the soil away** — the same leaching logic that makes laterite infertile (Cl.10 Ch.1). **Abundant water and water scarcity coexist there, because there is nothing to hold it.**

### ⭐ Western vs Eastern Ghats

| | ⭐ **WESTERN GHATS** | ⭐ **EASTERN GHATS** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Continuity** | ⭐ **Continuous** — crossed only through **passes** | ⭐ **Discontinuous and irregular** — dissected by rivers draining into the Bay of Bengal |
| ⭐ **Elevation** | ⭐ **Higher — average ~1,500 m**, increasing north to south | ⭐ **Average 600 m** |
| ⭐ **Highest peaks** | ⭐ **Anaimudi (2,695 m)**, then **Dodabetta (2,637 m)** | ⭐ **Mahendragiri (1,501 m)** |
| ⭐ **Passes** | ⭐ **Thal Ghat, Bhor Ghat, Pal Ghat** | — |
| ⭐ **Local names** | ⭐ **Sahyadri · Nilgiri · Anaimalai · Cardamom hills** | — |

> ⭐ **Why the difference? The peninsula tilts EAST**, so the major rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) flow east and have **sawn the Eastern Ghats into detached hill groups.** ⭐ **The Western Ghats, with no large rivers crossing them, stayed continuous.** **Drainage explains the relief.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐ The Indian Desert

⭐ **To the northwest of the Aravali hills lies the Great Indian desert.** ⭐ **It is a land of undulating topography dotted with longitudinal dunes and barchans.**

⭐ **This region receives low rainfall below 150 mm per year; hence, it has arid climate with low vegetation cover.** ⭐ **It is because of these characteristic features that this is also known as MARUSTHALI.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Though the underlying rock structure of the desert is an extension of the Peninsular plateau, yet, due to extreme arid conditions, its surface features have been carved by physical weathering and wind actions.**

⭐ **Some of the well pronounced desert land features present here are MUSHROOM ROCKS, SHIFTING DUNES and OASIS (mostly in its southern part).**

⭐ **On the basis of the orientation, the desert can be divided into two parts: the northern part is sloping towards Sindh and the southern towards the Rann of Kachchh.** ⭐ **Most of the rivers in this region are ephemeral.** ⭐ **The LUNI river flowing in the southern part of the desert is of some significance.** ⭐ **Low precipitation and high evaporation makes it a water deficit region.**

> ⭐ **The important structural point: ⭐ the Thar's underlying rock is an EXTENSION OF THE PENINSULAR PLATEAU.** ⭐ **It is not a separate geological entity — only its SURFACE features were carved by aridity.** **Structure is peninsular; physiography is desert.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐ The Coastal Plains

⭐ **On the basis of the location and active geomorphological processes, it can be broadly divided into two: (i) the western coastal plains; (ii) the eastern coastal plains.**

| | ⭐ **WESTERN COASTAL PLAINS** | ⭐ **EASTERN COASTAL PLAINS** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Type** | ⭐ **An example of SUBMERGED coastal plain** | ⭐ **An EMERGENT coast** |
| ⭐ **Width** | ⭐ **Narrow** | ⭐ **Broad** |
| ⭐ **Deltas** | ⭐ **No major deltas** — rivers form ESTUARIES | ⭐ **Well-developed deltas** — Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri |
| ⭐ **Divisions** | ⭐ **KACHCHH and KATHIAWAR coast (Gujarat) · KONKAN coast (Maharashtra) · GOA coast · MALABAR coast (Karnataka & Kerala)** | ⭐ **NORTHERN CIRCAR (north) · COROMANDEL coast (south)** |
| ⭐ **Features** | ⭐ **BACKWATERS (KAYALS) in Kerala — used for fishing, inland navigation and tourism (Nehru Trophy Vallamkali boat race in Punnamada Kayal, Alappuzha)** | ⭐ **LAGOONS — Chilika (largest), Pulicat** |
| ⭐ **Ports** | ⭐ **Because it is a submerged coastal plain, it provides natural conditions for the development of PORTS AND HARBOURS** — Kandla, Mazagaon, JLN port Nhava Sheva, Marmagao, Mangalore, Cochin | ⭐ **Being an emergent coast, it has less number of ports and harbours.** ⭐ **The continental shelf extends up to 500 km into the sea, which makes it difficult for the development of good ports and harbours** |

> ⭐⭐ **Submerged vs emergent is the key distinction, and it has a direct economic consequence:**
> ⭐ **The WESTERN coast is SUBMERGED** — the sea has drowned river valleys, creating deep, sheltered inlets. ⭐ **Perfect for ports.**
> ⭐ **The EASTERN coast is EMERGENT** — the land has risen, and the continental shelf extends up to **500 km** offshore, making the water shallow far out. ⭐ **Poor for ports.**
>
> ⭐ **This is why India's great natural harbours — Mumbai, Marmagao, Mangalore, Kochi — are nearly all on the WEST coast.** **Geology decided where India's ports could be.**

## 8️⃣ ⭐ The Islands

⭐ **There are two major island groups in India — one in the Bay of Bengal and the other in the Arabian Sea.**

| | ⭐ **ANDAMAN & NICOBAR** | ⭐ **LAKSHADWEEP** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Sea** | **Bay of Bengal** | **Arabian Sea** |
| ⭐ **Origin** | ⭐ **Volcanic / tectonic — elevated portions of submarine mountains** | ⭐ **CORAL origin** |
| ⭐ **Number** | ⭐ **~572 islands/islets** | ⭐ **36 islands, of which 11 are inhabited** |
| ⭐ **Divided** | ⭐ **Andaman (north) and Nicobar (south), by the TEN DEGREE CHANNEL** | ⭐ **The entire island group is built of coral deposits** |
| ⭐ **Capital** | ⭐ **Port Blair** | ⭐ **Kavaratti** |
| ⭐ **Notable** | ⭐ **BARREN ISLAND — India's only ACTIVE VOLCANO.** ⭐ **INDIRA POINT (Great Nicobar) — India's southernmost point.** ⭐ **Saddle Peak (North Andaman) is the highest peak** | ⭐ **MINICOY is the largest island.** ⭐ **PITTI island, uninhabited, has a bird sanctuary** |

⭐ **The Ten Degree Channel separates the Andaman from the Nicobar.** ⭐ **These islands lie close to the equator and experience equatorial climate and have thick forest cover.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Do not swap these: LAKSHADWEEP = Arabian Sea + CORAL. ANDAMAN & NICOBAR = Bay of Bengal + VOLCANIC.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Three geological divisions: the Peninsular Block (Gondwana fragment, rigid and stable since the Cambrian) · the Himalayas and Peninsular mountains (young, weak, flexible, still faulting) · the Indo-Gangetic Plain (a geo-synclinal depression filled from ~64 mya).**
2. ⭐ **Alluvium in the plains is 1,000–2,000 m deep.**
3. ⭐ **Himalayas longitudinally: Himadri (6,000 m) > Himachal (3,700–4,500 m; Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mahabharat; Kashmir, Kangra, Kullu valleys) > Shiwalik (900–1,100 m, unconsolidated sediment).** **Duns between the last two.**
4. ⭐ **Regionally west→east: Kashmir/Punjab (Indus–Satluj) · Kumaon (Satluj–Kali) · Nepal (Kali–Teesta) · Assam (Teesta–Dihang).** Then the **Purvanchal**.
5. ⭐ **KAREWA formations in Kashmir grow SAFFRON.**
6. ⭐ **Bhabar swallows the streams (pebbles, 8–16 km); Tarai spits them back out (swamp, 10–20 km).** ⭐ **Khadar is newer and most fertile; Bhangar is older with kankar.**
7. ⭐ **Peninsular plateau: Deccan · Central Highlands · North-Eastern (Meghalaya) plateau.** ⭐ **Anaimudi (2,695 m) is the highest peak; Dodabetta second; Mahendragiri highest in the Eastern Ghats.**
8. ⭐ **Meghalaya was detached from the Peninsular Block by a FAULT** created during the Indian plate's northeastward movement. **Garo, Khasi, Jaintia hills.**
9. ⚠️ ⭐ **Cherrapunji — the wettest place — has a BARE ROCKY SURFACE**, its soil leached and eroded away.
10. ⭐ **The Thar (Marusthali) sits on a Peninsular rock extension; only its surface is desert. Luni is the only significant river.**
11. ⭐⭐ **Western coast SUBMERGED → deep sheltered inlets → India's great ports.** ⭐ **Eastern coast EMERGENT → shelf extends 500 km → shallow water → few good harbours.**
12. ⭐ **A&N: ~572 islands, volcanic, split by the Ten Degree Channel; Barren Island (only active volcano), Saddle Peak, Indira Point.** ⭐ **Lakshadweep: 36 islands (11 inhabited), coral, Minicoy largest, Pitti bird sanctuary.**
