# Chapter 3 — Drainage System 🌱

> **The one-line point:** **India has two river families with opposite personalities** — Himalayan rivers that are **perennial, antecedent, still cutting their valleys**, and Peninsular rivers that are **seasonal, superimposed, and long since settled into fixed courses in hard rock.** **Almost every question about Indian rivers tests whether you know which family a river belongs to and why that matters.**

## 1️⃣ Basic Concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Drainage** | **The flow of water through well-defined channels** |
| **Drainage system** | **The network of such channels** |
| **Drainage basin / catchment area** | **An area drained by a river and its tributaries** |
| **WATERSHED** | **The boundary line separating one drainage basin from the other** |
| **Water divide** | **The upland separating two drainage basins** |

⚠️ **The difference between a river basin and a watershed is one of SCALE.** **The catchments of large rivers are called RIVER BASINS while those of small rivulets and rills are often referred to as WATERSHEDS.** **Watersheds are small in area while the basins cover larger areas.**

### Drainage patterns

| Pattern | Detail |
|---|---|
| **DENDRITIC** | **The drainage pattern resembling the branches of a tree is known as "dendritic."** **The examples of which are the rivers of northern plain** |
| **RADIAL** | **When the rivers originate from a hill and flow in all directions.** **The rivers originating from the AMARKANTAK range present a good example** |
| **TRELLIS** | **When the primary tributaries of rivers flow parallel to each other and secondary tributaries join them at right angles** |
| **CENTRIPETAL** | **When the rivers discharge their waters from all directions in a lake or depression** |

## 2️⃣ Antecedent and Superimposed Drainage — the key concept

**On the basis of the mode of origin, the drainage may also be classified.**

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **ANTECEDENT (Inherited) drainage** | **A river that MAINTAINS ITS ORIGINAL COURSE and pattern DESPITE the changes in underlying rock topography — i.e. the land rose, and the river cut down through it fast enough to keep its course.** **Examples: the INDUS, SATLUJ, BRAHMAPUTRA and other Himalayan rivers that CUT THROUGH the Himalayas, and are OLDER THAN THE MOUNTAINS THEMSELVES** |
| **SUPERIMPOSED / EPIGENETIC drainage** | **A river that has cut down through the overlying younger rocks into the older underlying rock structure, so that its pattern reflects the ROCKS THAT ARE NO LONGER PRESENT.** **Example: the DAMODAR, SUBARNAREKHA, CHAMBAL, BANAS and rivers flowing over the Chotanagpur plateau** |

> **ANTECEDENT DRAINAGE is one of the most striking ideas in Indian geography, and it deserves to be understood, not memorised.**
>
> **The Indus, Satluj and Brahmaputra rise on the TIBETAN side of the Himalayas — north of the range — and cut clean THROUGH it in deep gorges to reach India.**
>
> **How is that possible? Because the rivers were there FIRST.** **They were flowing when the Himalayas were still low. As the mountains rose — slowly, over millions of years — the rivers cut downward at least as fast as the land rose beneath them**, sawing their gorges deeper and holding their course.
>
> **These rivers are OLDER THAN THE MOUNTAINS THEY CROSS.** **The gorges — the Brahmaputra's at Namcha Barwa is among the deepest on Earth — are a record of a race between uplift and erosion in which erosion just kept pace.**
>
> 🎯 **In Mains, "the Himalayan rivers are antecedent — they predate the mountains and maintained their courses by downcutting as the range rose" is exactly the sort of precise, mechanism-based sentence that distinguishes a strong answer.**

## 3️⃣ The Two River Families

| Property | **HIMALAYAN RIVERS** | **PENINSULAR RIVERS** |
|---|---|---|
| **Place of origin** | **Himalayan mountain covered with glaciers** | **Peninsular plateau and central highland** |
| **Nature of flow** | **PERENNIAL — receive water from glacier and rainfall** | **SEASONAL — dependent on monsoon rainfall** |
| **Type of drainage** | **ANTECEDENT and consequent leading to dendritic pattern in plains** | **SUPERIMPOSED, rejuvenated resulting in trellis, radial and rectangular patterns** |
| **Nature of river** | **Long course, flowing through the rugged mountains experiencing head ward erosion and river capturing; in plains meandering and shifting of course** | **Smaller, fixed course with well-adjusted valleys** |
| **Catchment area** | **Very large basins** | **Relatively smaller basin** |
| **Age of the river** | **YOUTHFUL, active and deepening in the valleys** | **OLD rivers with graded profile, and have almost reached their base levels** |

> **"Graded profile" and "reached base level" mean the Peninsular rivers have finished most of their downcutting** — they are in the **old stage** (Cl.11 Fundamentals Ch.7). **Himalayan rivers are still in the youthful stage, actively deepening.** **This is why Himalayan valleys are deep V-shaped gorges and Peninsular valleys are broad and shallow.**

## 4️⃣ The Himalayan Drainage

**The Himalayan drainage system has evolved through a long geological history.** **It mainly includes the Ganga, the Indus and the Brahmaputra river systems.**

**Since these are fed both by melting of snow and precipitation, rivers of this system are PERENNIAL.** **These rivers pass through the giant gorges carved out by the erosional activity carried on simultaneously with the uplift of the Himalayas.**

**Besides deep gorges, these rivers also form V-shaped valleys, rapids and waterfalls in their mountainous course.** **While entering the plains, they form depositional features like flat valleys, ox-bow lakes, flood plains, braided channels, and deltas near the river mouth.**

**In the Himalayan reaches, the course of these rivers is highly tortuous, but over the plains they display a strong meandering tendency and shift their courses frequently.** **RIVER KOSI, also known as the "SORROW OF BIHAR", has been notorious for frequently changing its course.**

### The Indus System

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Source** | **A glacier near BOKHAR CHU (31°15′ N latitude and 81°40′ E longitude) in the Tibetan region at an altitude of 4,164 m in the KAILASH Mountain range.** **In Tibet, it is known as "SINGI KHAMBAN" or Lion's mouth** |
| **Length** | **~2,880 km in total; in India ~1,114 km** |
| **Course** | **Flows north-west; enters Indian territory in Jammu and Kashmir; forms a picturesque gorge in this part.** **Enters Pakistan near Chillar in the Dardistan region** |
| **Basin in India** | **Covers J&K, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan and Chandigarh** |
| **Himalayan tributaries** | **Shyok, Gilgit, Zaskar, Hunza, Nubra, Shigar, Gasting, Dras** |
| **Main tributaries (Punjab rivers)** | **JHELUM, CHENAB, RAVI, BEAS, SATLUJ** |

**Details of the five Punjab rivers:**

| River | Detail |
|---|---|
| **JHELUM** | **Rises from a spring at VERINAG at the foot of the Pir Panjal in the south-eastern part of the valley of Kashmir.** **Flows through Srinagar and the Wular lake before entering Pakistan** |
| **CHENAB** | **The LARGEST tributary of the Indus.** **Formed by two streams, the CHANDRA and the BHAGA, which join at Tandi near Keylong in Himachal Pradesh.** **Hence, it is also known as CHANDRABHAGA** |
| **RAVI** | **Rises west of the Rohtang pass in the Kullu hills of Himachal Pradesh and flows through the Chamba valley** |
| **BEAS** | **Originates from the Beas Kund near the ROHTANG PASS at an elevation of 4,000 m above the mean sea level.** **Enters the Punjab plains and joins the Satluj near Harike** |
| **SATLUJ** | **Originates in the RAKAS LAKE near Mansarovar at an altitude of 4,555 m in Tibet where it is known as LANGCHEN KHAMBAB.** **An ANTECEDENT river.** **It is a very important tributary as it feeds the canal system of the Bhakra Nangal project** |

> **The Tibetan names are examinable specifics:** **Indus = SINGI KHAMBAN (Lion's mouth) · Satluj = LANGCHEN KHAMBAB · Brahmaputra = TSANGPO.**

> ⚖️ **The INDUS WATERS TREATY (1960)**, brokered by the World Bank: **the three EASTERN rivers — RAVI, BEAS, SATLUJ — to India; the three WESTERN rivers — INDUS, JHELUM, CHENAB — largely to Pakistan.** **India may use about 20% of the total water.** **A standing Prelims fact and a live GS-II issue.**
> 🧠 **Memory: "Eastern rivers to India" — R-B-S go to Bharat.**

### The Ganga System

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Source** | **The BHAGIRATHI, fed by the GANGOTRI Glacier, joined by the ALAKNANDA** |
| **Confluence** | **They join at DEVPRAYAG in Uttarakhand — from here it is known as the GANGA** |
| **Enters plains at** | **HARIDWAR** |
| **Length** | **~2,525 km** |
| **Shared by** | **Uttarakhand (110 km), Uttar Pradesh (1,450 km), Bihar (445 km) and West Bengal (520 km)** |
| **Basin** | **Covers about 8.6 lakh sq km area in India alone** |

**Left-bank (Himalayan) tributaries and right-bank (Peninsular) tributaries:**

| **LEFT BANK (Himalayan)** | **RIGHT BANK (Peninsular)** |
|---|---|
| **RAMGANGA** — rises in the Garhwal hills near Gairsain | **YAMUNA** — the WESTERNMOST and LONGEST tributary; originates from the **YAMUNOTRI** glacier on the western slopes of Banderpunch range |
| **GHAGHARA** — originates in the glaciers of Mapchachungo | **CHAMBAL** — rises near Mhow in the Malwa plateau of MP; known for its **BADLAND topography, the CHAMBAL RAVINES** |
| **GANDAK** — comprises two streams, Kaligandak and Trishulganga | **SON** — a large south-bank tributary, originating in the Amarkantak plateau |
| **KOSI** — an ANTECEDENT river with its source to the north of Mount Everest in Tibet. **"SORROW OF BIHAR"** | **BETWA, KEN, DAMODAR** |
| **SARDA / SARYU** — rises in the Milam glacier in the Nepal Himalayas | **DAMODAR** — flows through a rift valley; **the "SORROW OF BENGAL"** |
| **MAHANANDA** — the LAST left-bank tributary, joining in West Bengal | |

**The Ganga's distributaries:** **The Ganga bifurcates at Farakka; the BHAGIRATHI-HOOGHLY flows south into West Bengal, while the PADMA flows into Bangladesh.** **In Bangladesh the Ganga (Padma) is joined by the Brahmaputra (Jamuna), and the combined stream is called the MEGHNA.** **Together they form the SUNDERBANS DELTA — the world's largest delta.**

> **Two "sorrow" rivers — a favourite pairing:** **KOSI = "Sorrow of Bihar"** (shifts course, catastrophic floods, antecedent). **DAMODAR = "Sorrow of Bengal"** (flows through a rift valley; controlled by the Damodar Valley Corporation).

### The Brahmaputra System

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Source** | **The CHEMAYUNGDUNG glacier of the Kailash range near the MANSAROVAR lake** — very close to the sources of the Indus and Satluj |
| **Length** | **Slightly longer than the Indus** |
| **Names** | **TSANGPO (Tibet) → DIHANG (Arunachal) → BRAHMAPUTRA (Assam) → JAMUNA (Bangladesh)** |
| **Course** | **Flows EAST through southern Tibet, parallel to the Himalayas; on reaching NAMCHA BARWA (7,755 m) it takes a U-turn and enters India in Arunachal Pradesh through a gorge** |
| **In Assam** | **A BRAIDED river with numerous riverine islands.** **MAJULI is the world's largest river island** |
| **Tributaries** | **In Arunachal: DIBANG, LOHIT, KENULA.** **In Assam: SUBANSIRI, KAMENG, MANAS, SANKOSH (north bank); BURHI DIHING, DHANSARI (south bank)** |
| **Character** | **Known for FLOODS, channel shifting and bank erosion.** **This is due to the fact that most of its tributaries are large, and bring large quantity of sediments owing to heavy rainfall in its catchment area** |

> **The Brahmaputra's U-turn explains its dual personality, and this is worth being able to state.**
>
> **In Tibet it flows east for ~1,700 km through a COLD, DRY region, receiving little water and carrying little silt — placid.** **Then it swings sharply around Namcha Barwa and plunges into India, where it meets TORRENTIAL RAINFALL** — picking up a colossal volume of water and sediment.
>
> **That abrupt change is why the same river is calm in Tibet and violently flood-prone in Assam.**
>
> 🇨🇳 **Strategic note: because it rises in Tibet, Chinese dam-building on the Tsangpo is a standing Indian concern.** **GS-II.**

## 5️⃣ The Peninsular Drainage

**The Peninsular drainage system is OLDER than the Himalayan one.** **This is evident from the broad, largely-graded shallow valleys, and the maturity of the rivers.**

**The WESTERN GHATS running close to the western coast act as the WATER DIVIDE between the major Peninsular rivers, discharging their water in the Bay of Bengal and as small rivulets joining the Arabian Sea.**

**Most of the major Peninsular rivers except Narmada and Tapi flow from west to east.**

### East-flowing rivers (Bay of Bengal) — form DELTAS

| River | Source | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **MAHANADI** | **Rises near Sihawa in Raipur district of Chhattisgarh** | Runs through Odisha; **Hirakud Dam** |
| **GODAVARI** | **Rises in NASIK district of Maharashtra** | **The LARGEST Peninsular river system.** **Also called DAKSHIN GANGA.** **Tributaries: PENGANGA, INDRAVATI, PRANHITA, MANJRA** |
| **KRISHNA** | **Rises near MAHABALESHWAR in Sahyadri** | **The SECOND largest east-flowing Peninsular river.** **Tributaries: KOYNA, TUNGABHADRA, BHIMA** |
| **KAVERI** | **Rises in BRAHMAGIRI hills (1,341 m) of Kogadu district in Karnataka** | **Tributaries: KABINI, BHAVANI, AMARAVATI** |

### West-flowing rivers (Arabian Sea) — form ESTUARIES

| River | Source | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **NARMADA** | **Originates on the western flank of the AMARKANTAK plateau at a height of ~1,057 m** | **Flows in a RIFT VALLEY between the Satpura in the south and the Vindhyan range in the north.** **Creates the "MARBLE ROCKS" near Jabalpur and the "DHUANDHAR FALLS"** |
| **TAPI** | **Originates from MULTAI in the Betul district of Madhya Pradesh** | **The other important west-flowing river; also flows in a RIFT VALLEY parallel to the Narmada but is much shorter** |
| **Other west-flowing** | **Sabarmati, Mahi, Bharathpuzha, Periyar, Luni** | |

> **Why Narmada and Tapi form ESTUARIES, not deltas — this is asked repeatedly and the REASON is what makes it stick:**
>
> **Both flow WEST through RIFT VALLEYS — steep-sided trenches between faults.** **The gradient is steep and the channel confined, so the rivers carry their sediment STRAIGHT OUT TO SEA instead of depositing it in a fan at the mouth.**
>
> **No deposition, no delta — just a funnel-shaped estuary.**
>
> ⚠️ **And note they flow WEST despite the peninsula tilting EAST — because they follow pre-existing FAULT LINES, not the general slope.** **Geology overrides topography here.**

## 6️⃣ River Regimes

> **The pattern of flow of water in a river channel over a year is known as its REGIME.**

**The variability in the flow of water is because of the variability in the rainfall received during the different months of the year.**

| Regime | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Himalayan rivers** | **Fed by snowmelt AND rainfall.** **Peak flow in summer (snowmelt) and monsoon.** **Never dry up — PERENNIAL** |
| **Peninsular rivers** | **Fed by rainfall only.** **Very high discharge during the monsoon, drastically reduced afterwards — some almost dry up** |

**The Ganga has its minimum flow during the January–June period.** **The maximum flow is attained either in August or in September.** **After September, there is a steady fall in the flow.**

**The rivers of the Deccan plateau show a great deal of variation, with peak flow during the monsoon months and much reduced flow thereafter.**

> **The practical consequence is worth stating:** **the perennial regime of Himalayan rivers is why the northern plains support year-round irrigation and three cropping seasons.** **Peninsular rivers' seasonal regime is why southern India relies far more on tanks, wells and reservoirs — and why interstate water disputes (Cauvery, Krishna) are far sharper there.** **The regime, not the volume, drives the politics.**

## 7️⃣ 🌱 River Pollution and Contemporary Issues

**The growing domestic, municipal, industrial and agricultural demand for water has led to a decline in river water quality.**

**A lot of urban waste water is dumped into the rivers.** **This has affected the quality of water.**

| Programme | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ganga Action Plan (GAP)** | **1985** — the first major effort |
| **NAMAMI GANGE** | **2014** — integrated approach: sewage treatment, riverfront development, afforestation, biodiversity |
| **National Ganga Council** | Apex body, chaired by the **Prime Minister** |
| **NRCP** | National River Conservation Plan — covers other rivers |

> 🌱 **The insight worth carrying (from Cl.9 Ch.3):** **SEWAGE, not industry, is the dominant pollution load** — and **reduced flow removes the river's own ability to dilute what is discharged.** **A river needs a minimum "ENVIRONMENTAL FLOW" to clean itself.** **So water abstraction and water pollution are the same problem.**

> 🌱 **River interlinking (NRLP):** transfers from "surplus" to "deficit" basins. **For:** flood control, drought mitigation, irrigation, navigation. **Against:** cost, displacement, ecological disruption, forest submergence, the contested premise that any basin is truly "surplus", and interstate disputes. **KEN–BETWA is the first link approved.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Watershed = boundary between basins.** River basin vs watershed is a matter of **scale**.
2. **Patterns: dendritic (northern plains) · radial (Amarkantak) · trellis · centripetal.**
3. **ANTECEDENT: Indus, Satluj, Brahmaputra are OLDER THAN THE HIMALAYAS** — they cut down as fast as the range rose. **SUPERIMPOSED: Damodar, Subarnarekha, Chambal, Banas.**
4. **Himalayan: perennial, antecedent, youthful, large basins, meandering. Peninsular: seasonal, superimposed, old and graded, smaller basins, fixed courses.**
5. **Indus rises at Bokhar Chu (Kailash, 4,164 m), called SINGI KHAMBAN.** **Chenab is its largest tributary (Chandra + Bhaga = Chandrabhaga).** **Jhelum rises at Verinag; Satluj at Rakas Lake (Langchen Khambab).**
6. **IWT 1960: Ravi, Beas, Satluj to India; India uses ~20% of the total.**
7. **Bhagirathi (Gangotri) + Alaknanda meet at DEVPRAYAG = the Ganga; enters the plains at Haridwar; 2,525 km; UP has the longest stretch (1,450 km).**
8. **Yamuna is the longest and westernmost tributary (Yamunotri).** **KOSI = Sorrow of Bihar; DAMODAR = Sorrow of Bengal.** **Chambal = badland ravines.**
9. **Brahmaputra: Chemayungdung glacier → Tsangpo → U-turn at Namcha Barwa → Dihang → Brahmaputra → Jamuna → joins the Ganga as the MEGHNA.** **Braided; Majuli is the world's largest river island.**
10. **Sunderbans = world's largest delta.**
11. **Godavari (Nasik) = largest Peninsular river, DAKSHIN GANGA. Krishna (Mahabaleshwar) = second. Kaveri = Brahmagiri hills.**
12. **Narmada (Amarkantak) and Tapi (Multai) flow WEST through RIFT VALLEYS and form ESTUARIES** — geology overriding the eastward tilt. **Marble Rocks and Dhuandhar Falls on the Narmada.**
13. **Ganga regime: minimum flow Jan–June, maximum Aug–Sept.** **The perennial/seasonal split explains why southern water disputes are sharper.**
14. 🌱 **Sewage dominates river pollution, and reduced flow removes the river's ability to dilute** — abstraction and pollution are one problem.
