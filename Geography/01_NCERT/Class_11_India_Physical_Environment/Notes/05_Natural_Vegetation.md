## 1️⃣ ⭐ Types of Forests

⭐ **On the basis of certain common features such as predominant vegetation type and climatic regions, Indian forests can be divided into the following groups:**

### ⭐⭐ The master table

| Type | ⭐ Rainfall / conditions | ⭐ Where | ⭐ Trees |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **TROPICAL EVERGREEN AND SEMI EVERGREEN** | ⭐ **Rainfall over 200 cm, mean annual temperature above 22°C.** ⭐ **Warm and humid areas** | ⭐ **Western slope of the Western Ghats, hills of the northeastern region and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands** | ⭐ **Rosewood, mahogony, aini, ebony.** ⭐ **Semi evergreen: white cedar, hollock and kail** |
| ⭐ **TROPICAL DECIDUOUS** | ⭐ **The MOST WIDESPREAD forests in India.** ⭐ **Also called the MONSOON FORESTS** | ⭐ **Spread over regions which receive rainfall between 70–200 cm** | ⭐ **MOIST deciduous (100–200 cm): teak, sal, shisham, hurra, mahua, amla, semul, kusum, sandalwood.** ⭐ **DRY deciduous (70–100 cm): tendu, palas, amaltas, bel, khair, axlewood** |
| ⭐ **TROPICAL THORN** | ⭐ **Areas which receive rainfall LESS THAN 50 cm** | ⭐ **Semi-arid areas of south west Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh** | ⭐ **Babool, ber, wild date palm, khair, neem, khejri, palas.** ⭐ **Tussocky grass grows up to a height of 2 m as the under growth** |
| ⭐ **MONTANE FORESTS** | ⭐ **Vary with altitude** | ⭐ **Himalayas and other hills** | ⭐ **See the altitudinal sequence below** |
| ⭐ **LITTORAL AND SWAMP** | ⭐ **Wetlands and tidal areas** | ⭐ **Deltas of the Ganga, Mahanadi, Krishna, Godavari and Kaveri** | ⭐ **MANGROVES — the SUNDARI tree gives the SUNDERBANS its name** |

⚠️ ⭐ **Note the difference from Class 10:** ⭐ **Class 11 gives tropical thorn as UNDER 50 cm** (Class 10 said under 70 cm). **Use the Class 11 figure — it is the more rigorous text.**

### ⭐ Montane forests — the altitudinal sequence

| Altitude | ⭐ Vegetation |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Up to 1,000–2,000 m** | ⭐ **Evergreen broad leaf trees such as oak and chestnut** |
| ⭐ **1,000–3,000 m** | ⭐ **Coniferous — CHIR, PINE, DEODAR, SILVER FIR, SPRUCE and CEDAR** |
| ⭐ **3,000–4,000 m** | ⭐ **Alpine — SILVER FIR, JUNIPER, PINE, BIRCH and RHODODENDRONS** |
| ⭐ **Above 4,000 m** | ⭐ **Alpine grasslands / tundra vegetation — MOSSES and LICHENS** |
| ⭐ **Southern montane** | ⭐ **In the southern hills (Nilgiri, Anaimalai, Palani), the temperate forests are called SHOLAS** |

> ⭐ **The SHOLAS are worth knowing by name** — ⭐ **temperate montane forests of the Nilgiri, Anaimalai and Palani hills**, occurring as **patches of forest in valleys separated by rolling grassland.** ⭐ **A distinctive and threatened ecosystem, and precisely the kind of specific term that distinguishes an answer.**

> ⭐ **The altitudinal sequence is the LATITUDINAL sequence turned vertical** (Cl.7 Ch.6). ⭐ **Climbing 4,000 m in the Himalayas takes you through the same series of vegetation zones as travelling from the tropics to the Arctic.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Forest Cover in India

⭐ **According to state records, the forest area covers 23.28 per cent of the total land area of the country.** ⭐ **It is important to note that the forest area and the actual forest cover are not the same.**

⚠️ ⭐ **THE FOREST AREA IS THE AREA NOTIFIED AND RECORDED AS THE FOREST LAND IRRESPECTIVE OF THE EXISTENCE OF TREES, WHILE THE ACTUAL FOREST COVER IS THE AREA OCCUPIED BY FORESTS WITH CANOPY.**

⭐ **The former is based on the records of the State Revenue Department, while the latter is based on aerial photographs and satellite imageries.**

> ⭐⭐ **The forest area vs forest cover distinction is examinable AND analytically important.**
> ⭐ **FOREST AREA = legally notified land, whether or not trees grow on it** (revenue records).
> ⭐ **FOREST COVER = land actually under tree canopy** (satellite imagery).
> ⭐ **The two can diverge sharply** — notified forest that has been degraded still counts as forest *area*, and tree plantations outside notified land count as forest *cover*.
>
> 🌱 ⭐ **This is why claims that "India's forest cover is increasing" need scrutiny:** ⭐ **plantations and orchards register as cover, but a eucalyptus monoculture has a fraction of the biodiversity of natural forest.** **The India State of Forest Report distinguishes them for exactly this reason — and knowing that distinction is a genuine differentiator in a GS-III answer.**

### ⭐ Classification by administrative status

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **RESERVED FORESTS** | ⭐ **More than half of the total forest land has been declared reserved forests.** ⭐ **Reserved forests are regarded as the most valuable as far as the conservation of forest and wildlife resources are concerned** |
| ⭐ **PROTECTED FORESTS** | ⭐ **Almost one-third of the total forest area is protected forest, as declared by the Forest Department.** ⭐ **This forest land are protected from any further depletion** |
| ⭐ **UNCLASSED FORESTS** | ⭐ **Other forests and wastelands belonging to both government and private individuals and communities** |

⭐ **Reserved and protected forests are also referred to as PERMANENT FOREST ESTATES maintained for the purpose of producing timber and other forest produce, and for protective reasons.**

⭐ **MADHYA PRADESH has the largest area under permanent forests, constituting 75 per cent of its total forest area.**

⭐ **Jammu and Kashmir, Andhra Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, and Maharashtra have large percentages of reserved forests, whereas Bihar, Haryana, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Odisha and Rajasthan have a bulk of it under protected forests.** ⭐ **All North-eastern states and parts of Gujarat have a very high percentage of their forests as UNCLASSED FORESTS managed by local communities.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Forest Conservation

⭐ **Forests have an intrinsic value, besides being important for us for a number of reasons.** ⭐ **Forests are a renewable resource and play a major role in enhancing the quality of environment.**

⭐ **They modify local climate, control soil erosion, regulate stream flow, support a variety of industries, provide livelihood for many communities and offer panoramic or scenic view for recreation.** ⭐ **It controls wind force and temperature and causes rainfall. It provides humus to the soil and shelter to the wildlife.**

### ⭐⭐ The National Forest Policy

⭐ **India is one of the few countries which has a FOREST POLICY since 1894.** ⭐ **It was revised in 1952 and further in 1988.**

⭐ **The main aim of the 1952 policy was to maintain ONE-THIRD (33%) of the total land area under forest** — ⭐ **and in the hills and mountainous regions, the aim was to keep TWO-THIRD (67%) of the area under forests.**

⭐ **The MAIN OBJECTIVES OF THE NATIONAL FOREST POLICY 1988 are:**

| Objective | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **(i)** | ⭐ **Maintenance of environmental stability through preservation and restoration of ecological balance** |
| ⭐ **(ii)** | ⭐ **Conservation of natural heritage** |
| ⭐ **(iii)** | ⭐ **Check on soil erosion, extension of the desert lands and reduction of floods and droughts** |
| ⭐ **(iv)** | ⭐ **Check on soil erosion and denudation in catchment areas of rivers, lakes and reservoirs** |
| ⭐ **(v)** | ⭐ **Increase in forest cover through SOCIAL FORESTRY and afforestation on degraded land** |
| ⭐ **(vi)** | ⭐ **Increase the productivity of forests to make timber, fuel, fodder and food available to rural population dependent on forests** |
| ⭐ **(vii)** | ⭐ **Encouragement of the substitution of wood** |
| ⭐ **(viii)** | ⭐ **Creating of massive people's movement involving women to encourage planting of trees, stop felling of trees and thus, reduce pressure on the existing forest** |

> ⭐ **The 33% / 67% targets from the 1952 policy are a standing Prelims fact.** ⭐ **India is still at around 23–25%.** ⚠️ **Note the hill-region target is TWO-THIRDS, which many candidates forget.**

### ⭐⭐ Social Forestry

> ⭐ **SOCIAL FORESTRY means the management and protection of forests and afforestation on barren lands with the purpose of helping in the environmental, social and rural development.**

⭐ **The NATIONAL COMMISSION ON AGRICULTURE (1976) has classified social forestry into three categories:**

| Category | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **URBAN FORESTRY** | ⭐ **Pertains to the raising and management of trees on public and privately owned lands in and around urban centres such as green belts, parks, roadside avenues, industrial and commercial green belts** |
| ⭐ **RURAL FORESTRY** | ⭐ **Lays emphasis on promotion of AGRO-FORESTRY and COMMUNITY-FORESTRY.** ⭐ **AGRO-FORESTRY is the raising of trees and agriculture crops on the same land inclusive of waste patches.** ⭐ **It combines forestry with agriculture, thus, altering the simultaneous production of food, fodder, fuel, timber and fruit.** ⭐ **COMMUNITY FORESTRY involves the raising of trees on public or community land such as the village pasture and temple land, roadside, canal bank, strips along railway lines, and schools etc.** ⭐ **Community forestry programme aims at providing benefits to the community as a whole** |
| ⭐ **FARM FORESTRY** | ⭐ **A term applied to the process under which farmers grow trees for commercial and non-commercial purposes on their farm lands.** ⭐ **Forest departments of various states distribute seedlings of trees free of cost to small and medium farmers.** ⭐ **Several lands such as the margins of agricultural fields, grasslands and pastures, land around homes and cow sheds may be used for raising trees under farm forestry** |

> ⭐ **The 1976 National Commission on Agriculture classification (urban / rural / farm forestry) is a precise, examinable structure.** ⭐ **And within rural forestry, know the AGRO-FORESTRY vs COMMUNITY FORESTRY split** — trees with crops on the same land, vs trees on public/community land.

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Wildlife and Protected Areas

⭐ **India is one of the countries with the greatest biological diversity.**

⭐ **Wildlife conservation instruments:**

| Instrument | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** | ⭐ **The core statute; created protected areas and schedules of protected species** |
| ⭐ **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** | ⭐ **Restricts diversion of forest land for non-forest use** |
| ⭐ **Project Tiger, 1973** | ⭐ **Launched with 9 tiger reserves** |
| ⭐ **Project Elephant, 1992** | ⭐ **Elephant and migration corridors** |
| ⭐ **National Parks / Sanctuaries** | ⭐ **In-situ conservation** |
| ⭐ **BIOSPHERE RESERVES** | ⭐ **India has 18** — zoned **core / buffer / transition** |

### ⭐ Biosphere reserves to know

⭐ **NILGIRI (India's first, 1986) · NANDA DEVI · NOKREK · GREAT NICOBAR · GULF OF MANNAR · MANAS · SUNDERBANS · SIMLIPAL · DIHANG-DIBANG · PACHMARHI · KHANGCHENDZONGA · AGASTHYAMALAI · ACHANAKMAR-AMARKANTAK · KACHCHH · COLD DESERT · SESHACHALAM · PANNA.**

⭐ **India's 4 biodiversity hotspots: THE HIMALAYA · THE WESTERN GHATS & SRI LANKA · INDO-BURMA · SUNDALAND.**

**Trap 1:** *"Forest area and forest cover mean the same thing."* ⭐ **Area = notified land regardless of trees; Cover = actual canopy from satellite imagery.**
**Trap 2:** *"Tropical evergreen is India's most widespread forest."* ⭐ **Tropical DECIDUOUS.**
**Trap 3:** *"The 1952 policy targeted 33% in all regions."* ⭐ **33% overall but 67% (two-thirds) in hills and mountains.**
**Trap 4:** *"Thorn forests occur where rainfall is 70–100 cm."* ⭐ **Under 50 cm** in the Class 11 text.
**Trap 5:** *"Farm forestry is raising trees on community land."* ⭐ **That is COMMUNITY forestry.** Farm forestry is on farmers' own land.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **India's vegetation map is its rainfall map.** ⭐ **>200 cm evergreen · 70–200 cm deciduous (MOST WIDESPREAD) · <50 cm thorn · montane · littoral/swamp.**
2. ⭐ **Evergreen: rosewood, mahogony, aini, ebony** — Western Ghats western slope, NE hills, Andaman & Nicobar.
3. ⭐ **Moist deciduous (100–200 cm): teak, sal, shisham, sandalwood. Dry deciduous (70–100 cm): tendu, palas, amaltas, khair.**
4. ⭐ **Thorn: babool, ber, khejri, palas; tussocky grass to 2 m.**
5. ⭐ **Montane sequence: oak/chestnut → conifers → alpine (juniper, birch, rhododendron) → mosses and lichens.** ⭐ **SHOLAS in the southern hills.**
6. ⭐⭐ **FOREST AREA (notified, revenue records) ≠ FOREST COVER (actual canopy, satellite).** 🌱 **Plantations register as cover but are not natural forest.**
7. ⭐ **Forest area = 23.28% of land. Reserved > half · Protected ~one-third · Unclassed the rest.** ⭐ **MP has the largest permanent forest area (75% of its forest).** ⭐ **NE states are largely unclassed, community-managed.**
8. ⭐ **India has had a forest policy since 1894; revised 1952 and 1988.** ⭐ **1952 target: 33% overall, 67% in hills.** Still at ~23–25%.
9. ⭐ **National Forest Policy 1988's eight objectives** — including social forestry, substitution of wood, and a **people's movement involving women.**
10. ⭐ **Social forestry (NCA 1976): URBAN · RURAL (agro-forestry + community forestry) · FARM forestry.**
11. ⭐ **18 biosphere reserves; Nilgiri was the first (1986).** ⭐ **4 biodiversity hotspots.**
