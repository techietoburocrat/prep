# Chapter 5 — Water 🌱

> **The one-line point:** The ocean is never still, and it moves in **three quite different ways** — **waves** (wind on the surface), **tides** (the Moon's pull), and **currents** (vast rivers within the sea). Confusing these three is the commonest error in this chapter; keeping them straight is most of the marks.

## 1️⃣ Distribution of Water

```
   ████████████████████████████████████████████  97%  SALT (oceans)
   ██                                             3%  FRESH
                                                       ├─ ~69% glaciers & ice caps
                                                       ├─ ~30% groundwater
                                                       └─ ~1%  rivers, lakes, air
```

**Only ~3% of Earth's water is fresh, and most of that is frozen or deep underground.** Under **1%** is readily usable.

> 🌱 **This is the foundation of water security** — the same point as Cl.6 Ch.5, restated because it underlies everything from Cauvery disputes to Punjab's falling water table. **India is the world's largest user of groundwater**, and extraction exceeds recharge across much of the northwest.

## 2️⃣ The Water Cycle

```
        ☀️ solar energy drives the whole loop

              ☁️ CONDENSATION
               ▲          │
       EVAPORATION    PRECIPITATION
       (+ transpiration    │
          from plants)     ▼
               ▲     ═══════════
               └──── RUN-OFF & INFILTRATION ────┘
```

**The key property: the total amount of water on Earth is fixed.** It is neither created nor destroyed — only moved and changed in state. The water in your glass has been through dinosaurs, oceans and clouds.

> 🔑 **The consequence that matters for policy:** we never "use up" water — **we degrade it and move it.** Pollution and mislocation are the real problems, not absolute quantity. That reframing is worth a line in a water-security answer.

## 3️⃣ The Three Ocean Movements — Keep These Separate

| | 🌊 **WAVES** | 🌕 **TIDES** | 🔄 **CURRENTS** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Caused by** | **WIND** blowing over the surface | **Gravitational pull of the MOON** (and Sun) | Differences in **temperature and salinity**, plus prevailing winds |
| **Scale** | Surface, local | Whole ocean, coastal effect | Vast, continuous, ocean-wide |
| **Timing** | Continuous, irregular | **Twice daily**, predictable | Constant, flowing year-round |
| **Motion** | Up-and-down of water | Rise and fall of sea level | **Horizontal flow** — like a river in the sea |

> 🔑 **The one-line discriminator: waves = wind · tides = Moon · currents = temperature.** Almost every question in this chapter is testing whether you can keep those three apart.

## 4️⃣ 🌕 Tides

> **Tide** = the rhythmic **rise and fall of sea water**, twice a day, caused by the **gravitational pull of the Moon** (and to a lesser extent the Sun).

**High tide** = water rises and covers more shore. **Low tide** = water falls and exposes more shore.

### Spring tides vs neap tides

| | 🌊 **SPRING TIDE** | 🌙 **NEAP TIDE** |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | **Full moon** and **new moon** | **First and third quarter** moon |
| **Geometry** | Sun, Moon and Earth in a **STRAIGHT LINE** | Sun and Moon at **RIGHT ANGLES** to Earth |
| **Pulls** | **Combine** — reinforce each other | **Oppose** — partially cancel |
| **Result** | **HIGHEST high tides, lowest lows** | **Lowest high tides** — smallest range |

```
   SPRING TIDE (twice a month)          NEAP TIDE (twice a month)
   pulls ADD together                    pulls CANCEL partially

   ☀️ ──── 🌍 ──── 🌕                          🌕
       (aligned)                                │
                                        ☀️ ─── 🌍
   Sun + Moon pull the same way              (right angle)
   → biggest tidal range                  → smallest tidal range
```

> ⚠️ **"Spring tide" has nothing to do with the spring season.** It comes from the sense of water *springing up*. **Spring tides happen twice every month, all year round.** This is a favourite trap.

**Why tides matter practically:**
- **Navigation** — ships enter and leave harbours at high tide
- **Fishing** — fish come closer to shore
- **Tidal energy** — a renewable source (India has potential in the **Gulf of Khambhat** and **Gulf of Kachchh**)
- **Salt pans** — seawater trapped at high tide, evaporated

## 5️⃣ 🔄 Ocean Currents

> **Ocean current** = a large mass of surface water moving continuously in a definite direction — effectively **a river flowing within the ocean**.

| | **WARM CURRENT** | **COLD CURRENT** |
|---|---|---|
| **From** | Equatorial regions → toward the poles | Polar regions → toward the equator |
| **Effect on coasts** | **Warms** them | **Cools** them |
| **Examples** | **Gulf Stream**, North Atlantic Drift, Kuroshio | **Labrador Current**, Humboldt (Peru) Current, Benguela |

### Why currents matter — three real consequences

**1. They control coastal climate.**

> 🌡️ **The North Atlantic Drift (warm) is why Britain and Norway are habitable.** London sits at roughly the same latitude as Newfoundland in Canada — but London has mild, ice-free winters while Newfoundland freezes. **The warm current is the entire difference.** Norwegian ports above the Arctic Circle stay ice-free year-round for the same reason.

**2. Where warm and cold currents meet, you get the world's best fishing grounds.**

> 🐟 **Why:** cold currents carry **nutrients** up from the deep ocean; the mixing zone is rich in plankton; plankton feeds fish. The **Grand Banks of Newfoundland** (Gulf Stream meets Labrador Current) and the **Japanese coast** (Kuroshio meets Oyashio) are the classic examples. ⚠️ **But those meeting zones also produce dense fog**, which historically made them dangerous to navigate.

**3. They redistribute planetary heat.**

Currents move enormous quantities of warmth from the tropics toward the poles — a global circulation system that moderates temperature worldwide.

> 🌱 **Climate-change link worth tagging:** melting Greenland ice is diluting the North Atlantic with fresh water, which may weaken the circulation that drives the Gulf Stream. **If it weakened substantially, north-west Europe could get colder even as the planet warms.** A striking, specific example for a GS-III answer — and a good illustration that "global warming" does not mean "everywhere warms uniformly."

## 6️⃣ 🌱 Tsunami

> **Tsunami** = a series of enormous ocean waves generated by a **sudden displacement of the seabed** — usually an **undersea earthquake**, sometimes a volcanic eruption or landslide.

| | **Ordinary wave** | **Tsunami** |
|---|---|---|
| **Caused by** | Wind | **Seabed displacement** (earthquake) |
| **Depth affected** | Surface only | **The entire water column** |
| **Wavelength** | Short | Extremely long (100+ km) |
| **Speed in deep ocean** | Slow | **~800 km/hr** — as fast as a jet |
| **Height in deep ocean** | Visible | **Often under 1 m — barely noticeable** |
| **On reaching shallow water** | — | **Slows, and height builds enormously** |

> ⚠️ **"Tidal wave" is a wrong and misleading name.** A tsunami has **nothing to do with tides** — tides are lunar, tsunamis are seismic. The word *tsunami* is Japanese for "harbour wave," which is more accurate: it is barely detectable in the open sea and devastating at the coast.

> 🌊 **Why it grows at the shore** — the mechanism is worth understanding. In deep water the energy is spread through a huge column of water moving fast. As the sea shallows, the front slows down while the water behind keeps coming — so the energy is compressed into a shorter, taller mass. **The wave doesn't gain energy; it gains height by losing room.**

**The 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami** (26 December 2004) — triggered by a magnitude ~9.1 earthquake off Sumatra, it killed ~230,000 people across 14 countries, including in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

> 🌱 **Two lessons that regularly appear in GS-III:**
> 1. **India had no tsunami warning system in 2004.** It now does — the **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC)** at INCOIS, Hyderabad, operational since 2007.
> 2. **Coasts with intact mangroves and coral reefs suffered measurably less damage** than cleared stretches nearby. **Natural barriers work.** This is the strongest available argument for mangrove conservation, and it links straight back to Cl.6 Ch.8.

## ⚡ UPSC FOCUS

| Topic | The specific thing |
|---|---|
| **Waves / tides / currents** ⭐ | **Wind / Moon / temperature** — keep the causes separate |
| **Spring vs neap tide** ⭐ | Straight line (highest range) vs right angles (lowest range) |
| **Spring tide timing** ⭐ | **Full moon and new moon** — twice a month, not seasonal |
| **Warm vs cold currents** ⭐ | Gulf Stream (warm) · Labrador, Humboldt (cold) |
| **Fishing grounds** ⭐ | Where **warm and cold currents meet** — Grand Banks, Japan coast |
| **North Atlantic Drift** | Why NW Europe is far milder than its latitude |
| **Tsunami ≠ tidal wave** ⭐ | Seismic, not lunar. ~800 km/hr, low in deep water |
| **2004 tsunami** | 26 Dec 2004; ITEWC established at INCOIS Hyderabad |
| **Mangroves reduced tsunami damage** | The conservation argument |
| **Water distribution** | 97% salt / 3% fresh; under 1% usable |

**Trap 1:** *"Spring tides occur in the spring season."* **No** — at full and new moon, twice every month, all year.
**Trap 2:** *"Tsunamis are caused by tides or by strong winds."* **No — by seabed displacement**, usually an undersea earthquake.
**Trap 3:** *"Ocean currents are caused by the Moon."* **No — that's tides.** Currents are driven by temperature, salinity and wind.
**Trap 4:** *"Tsunamis are tall in the open ocean."* **Often under a metre** — ships at sea may not notice one pass beneath them.
**Trap 5:** *"The Gulf Stream is a cold current."* **Warm.** Labrador and Humboldt are the cold ones.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **97% salt, 3% fresh — and under 1% readily usable.**
2. **The water cycle moves a fixed total.** We don't consume water; we degrade and relocate it.
3. **Waves = wind. Tides = Moon. Currents = temperature.** Never mix these three.
4. **Spring tide:** Sun–Moon–Earth **aligned** (full/new moon) → **greatest** range. **Neap tide:** **right angles** (quarter moons) → **least** range.
5. **"Spring tide" is not seasonal** — it happens twice a month, year-round.
6. **Warm currents** (Gulf Stream) **warm** coasts; **cold currents** (Labrador, Humboldt) **cool** them.
7. **The North Atlantic Drift is why London is mild and Newfoundland freezes at the same latitude.**
8. **Best fishing grounds are where warm and cold currents meet** — nutrient upwelling feeds plankton. Also produces dense fog.
9. 🌱 **A tsunami is seismic, not tidal.** ~800 km/hr, under 1 m high in deep ocean, builds enormously in shallow water.
10. 🌱 **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami → India built the ITEWC (2007).** **Intact mangroves measurably reduced damage** — the conservation argument.
