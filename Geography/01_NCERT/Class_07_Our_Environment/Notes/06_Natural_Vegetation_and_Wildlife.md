# Chapter 6 — Natural Vegetation and Wildlife 🌱

> **The one-line point:** Vegetation is not scattered randomly across the world — it forms **belts that run with latitude**, because **latitude sets temperature and rainfall, and those two set what can grow.** Learn the belts once and you can predict the vegetation of anywhere on Earth from its coordinates.

## 🎬 Start Here: Why Vegetation Comes in Belts

```
   VEGETATION FOLLOWS THE HEAT ZONES (Cl.6 Ch.2)

   90°N  ❄️  ICE / TUNDRA               ← too cold for trees
   66½°  🌲  CONIFEROUS (TAIGA)         ← cold, needle leaves
   50°   🍂  TEMPERATE DECIDUOUS        ← four seasons
   40°   🌿  MEDITERRANEAN / GRASSLAND  ← dry summers
   30°   🌵  DESERT / THORNY SCRUB      ← subtropical high pressure
   23½°  🌳  TROPICAL DECIDUOUS         ← monsoon, seasonal rain
    0°   🌴  TROPICAL EVERGREEN         ← hot + wet all year
         (and the whole pattern MIRRORS south of the equator)
```

> 🔑 **The chain, in one line: latitude → sunlight angle → temperature → rainfall → vegetation → wildlife.** This is the payoff of everything in Class 6 Ch.2 and Class 7 Ch.4. **Once you see vegetation as the *output* of climate rather than as a separate list to memorise, this chapter becomes almost self-generating.**

> ⛰️ **The same sequence appears vertically.** Climb a tall tropical mountain and you pass through rainforest → temperate forest → coniferous → alpine meadow → snow — **the same order as travelling from equator to pole**, compressed into a few kilometres of altitude. That is why mountains hold such concentrated biodiversity (Cl.6 Ch.6).

## 1️⃣ ⭐ Forests

### The master table

| Type | Where | Rainfall/Climate | Trees | Animals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🌴 **TROPICAL EVERGREEN (Rainforest)** | Near the **equator** — Amazon, Congo, SE Asia, Western Ghats | Hot, **>200 cm**, rain all year | **Rosewood, ebony, mahogany**; dense, multi-layered canopy | Monkeys, apes, sloths, jaguars, elephants, snakes, birds |
| 🌳 **TROPICAL DECIDUOUS (Monsoon)** | India, N. Australia, C. America | **100–200 cm**, marked dry season | ⭐ **Sal, teak, neem, shisham** | **Tiger, elephant, lion, deer, monkeys** |
| 🌾 **TEMPERATE EVERGREEN** | Mid-latitude **east coasts** — SE USA, SE China, SE Brazil | Moderate rain all year | Oak, pine, eucalyptus | — |
| 🍂 **TEMPERATE DECIDUOUS** | NE USA, China, NZ, Europe | Cold winters, moderate rain | **Oak, ash, beech** | Deer, fox, wolves, bears |
| 🫒 **MEDITERRANEAN** | ⭐ Around the Mediterranean, plus **California, SW Africa, SW Australia, central Chile** | **Hot dry summers, mild wet winters** | ⭐ **Citrus — oranges, figs, olives, grapes** | — |
| 🌲 **CONIFEROUS (TAIGA)** | High northern latitudes — **Siberia, Canada, Scandinavia** | Very cold | ⭐ **Chir, pine, cedar** — softwood, needle leaves, conical | Silver fox, mink, polar bear |

> ⭐ **The Mediterranean type is a favourite Prelims item** for a specific reason: it occurs in **five widely separated places** on the west coasts of continents around 30–40° latitude — the Mediterranean basin, California, central Chile, South Africa's Cape, and south-west Australia. **Same climate, same vegetation, five different continents.** And it is the world's orchard: citrus, olives, grapes, figs. **"Mediterranean = citrus fruits" is the association to lock in.**

> 🌲 **Why taiga trees are softwood and conical** — the mechanism, so you don't have to memorise it:
> - **Conical shape + sloping branches** → snow slides off instead of accumulating and snapping them
> - **Needle-shaped leaves** → minimal surface area, so minimal water loss (important because frozen ground makes water hard to draw up even where it's plentiful)
> - **Evergreen** → no energy wasted regrowing leaves in a very short growing season
>
> **Taiga is the world's largest land biome**, and commercially it supplies most of the world's softwood, paper and pulp.

> 🌴 **Why rainforests are layered** — worth knowing because it explains their biodiversity. Competition for light produces distinct storeys: emergent trees, canopy, understorey, forest floor. **Each layer is a different habitat with different species.** One hectare of Amazon rainforest can hold more tree species than all of Europe. And because the canopy intercepts most light, the forest floor is surprisingly **dark and open** — not the impenetrable tangle of popular imagination.
>
> ⚠️ **A counter-intuitive fact worth carrying:** rainforest **soil is poor**. Nutrients are held in the living vegetation, not the ground, and are recycled fast by decomposers. **Clear the forest and the soil is exhausted within a few years** — which is exactly why Amazon cattle ranching keeps advancing into new forest rather than reusing cleared land.

## 2️⃣ ⭐ Grasslands — The Highest-Frequency Prelims Item

Grasslands occur where there is **too little rain for forest, too much for desert.**

### Tropical grasslands (Savanna type)

| Name | Region |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Savanna** | **Africa** |
| ⭐ **Campos** | **Brazil** |
| ⭐ **Llanos** | **Venezuela** |

**Character:** tall grass (up to 3–4 m), scattered trees, hot climate. **Animals:** elephant, zebra, giraffe, deer, leopard — Africa's great herds.

### Temperate grasslands

| Name | Region |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Prairie** | **North America** |
| ⭐ **Pampas** | **Argentina** |
| ⭐ **Steppe** | **Central Asia / Russia** |
| ⭐ **Veld** | **South Africa** |
| ⭐ **Downs** | **Australia** |

**Character:** shorter grass, very few trees. **Animals:** wild buffalo, bison, antelope.

> 🧠 **This table is asked more often than almost anything else in Class 7.** A memory hook:
> - **P**rairie — **P**North America… try instead: *"**P**rairies and **P**ampas are the two **P**s of the **Americas**"* (Prairie = North, Pampas = South)
> - **Steppe** — think *steppes of Central Asia*, the horse-riding steppe nomads of your History notes
> - **Veld** — Afrikaans word → **South Africa**
> - **Downs** — English word → **Australia** (British settlement)
> - **Savanna** (Africa), **Campos** (Brazil), **Llanos** (Venezuela) — the three tropical ones

> 🌾 **Why temperate grasslands became the world's breadbaskets.** Deep, fertile grassland soils plus flat terrain plus a suitable growing season made the Prairies, Pampas and Steppes the great wheat and cattle regions. **Almost none of the original grassland survives** — it has been ploughed. **The temperate grassland is arguably the most completely destroyed biome on Earth**, and it gets far less attention than rainforest loss because farmland doesn't look like destruction. A sharp, contrarian point for a GS-III answer.

## 3️⃣ Thorny Bushes and Tundra

| | 🌵 **THORNY BUSHES / SCRUB** | ❄️ **TUNDRA** |
|---|---|---|
| **Where** | Dry desert regions — Sahara, Thar, Arabia | **Polar regions** — northern Canada, Siberia, Greenland |
| **Climate** | Very low rainfall, extreme temperatures | Extremely cold; ground frozen (**permafrost**) |
| **Vegetation** | Cactus, acacia, date palm; **leaves reduced to spines**, long roots, thick stems | ⭐ **Mosses, lichens, sedges** — **no trees** |
| **Animals** | Camel, desert fox, snakes, lizards | **Polar bear, reindeer, seal, walrus, musk ox, arctic fox** |

> 🌵 **Every desert-plant feature is a water strategy:** spines instead of leaves (minimal surface for evaporation, plus defence against grazing), thick waxy stems (water storage), very long or very wide root systems (reaching deep water or catching brief rain over a large area).

> ❄️ **Why tundra has no trees** — the actual reason is the **permafrost**, not merely the cold. Permanently frozen subsoil means roots cannot penetrate deep enough to anchor a tree or reach water. So vegetation stays low and shallow-rooted.
>
> 🌱 **And this is now a serious climate issue:** thawing permafrost releases **methane**, a greenhouse gas far more potent than CO₂ over the short term. That warming causes more thaw, which releases more methane — a **positive feedback loop**. **Feedback loops are exactly the kind of specific mechanism that lifts a GS-III answer above the generic.**

## 4️⃣ 🌱 Threats and Conservation

### Why vegetation and wildlife are disappearing

| Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Deforestation** | Clearing for agriculture, settlement, industry, mining, roads |
| **Habitat fragmentation** | Roads and railways cut habitat into patches too small to sustain populations |
| **Poaching** | Ivory, horn, skin, bone, pangolin scales |
| **Forest fires** | Natural and human-caused |
| **Pollution** | Air, water, soil, pesticides |
| **Invasive species** | Outcompete natives — *Lantana*, water hyacinth |
| **Climate change** | Shifts habitat belts faster than species can migrate |

### 🌱 Conservation responses

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Protected areas** | National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves (Cl.6 Ch.8) |
| **Species projects** | Project Tiger (1973), Project Elephant (1992) |
| **Afforestation** | Planting new forest; social forestry |
| **Legal protection** | ⭐ **Wildlife Protection Act 1972**, **Forest Conservation Act 1980** |
| **International** | **CITES** (trade in endangered species), **Ramsar** (wetlands), **CBD** (biodiversity) |
| **Awareness** | **Vanamahotsav**, community conservation, sacred groves |

> 🌱 **The specific fact that scores:** *"forest cover is increasing"* claims often rest on **plantations counting as forest**. A monoculture eucalyptus or teak plantation has a fraction of the biodiversity of natural forest, though both register as "forest cover" in the statistics. **The India State of Forest Report distinguishes forest cover from tree cover for this reason.** Knowing that distinction is a genuine differentiator in an environment answer.

**Trap 1:** *"Pampas is in North America."* **No — Argentina.** **Prairie** is North America.
**Trap 2:** *"Veld is in Australia."* **No — South Africa.** **Downs** is Australia.
**Trap 3:** *"Tundra has coniferous trees."* **No trees at all** — mosses and lichens. Taiga is the coniferous one, just south of tundra.
**Trap 4:** *"Rainforests have rich soil because they're so lush."* **The opposite** — soil is poor; nutrients are locked in living biomass.
**Trap 5:** *"Mediterranean vegetation is found only around the Mediterranean Sea."* **Five regions**, including California, Chile, South Africa and SW Australia.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Vegetation forms belts because latitude sets temperature and rainfall.** The same sequence repeats vertically with altitude.
2. **Six forest types:** tropical evergreen · tropical deciduous · temperate evergreen · temperate deciduous · Mediterranean · coniferous.
3. ⭐ **Mediterranean = citrus**, hot dry summers, and occurs in **five** separate world regions.
4. ⭐ **Taiga (coniferous) is the world's largest land biome** — Siberia, Canada, Scandinavia. Conical shape sheds snow; needles cut water loss.
5. 🌱 **Rainforest soil is poor** — nutrients live in the vegetation. Clear it and it exhausts in a few years.
6. ⭐ **Tropical grasslands:** Savanna (Africa), Campos (Brazil), Llanos (Venezuela).
7. ⭐ **Temperate grasslands:** Prairie (N. America), Pampas (Argentina), Steppe (C. Asia), Veld (S. Africa), Downs (Australia).
8. 🌾 **Temperate grasslands became the world's breadbaskets** — and are arguably the most completely destroyed biome, because ploughed land doesn't look like destruction.
9. **Thorny scrub:** spines, thick stems, long roots — every feature a water strategy.
10. ❄️ **Tundra has no trees because of permafrost**, not merely cold. Thawing permafrost releases **methane** — a positive feedback loop.
11. 🌱 **Habitat loss is the biggest driver of extinction**, compounded by fragmentation.
12. 🌱 **Plantations count as "forest cover" but are not natural forest** — know the distinction.
