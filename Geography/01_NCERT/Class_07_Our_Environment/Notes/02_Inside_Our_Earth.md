# Chapter 2 — Inside Our Earth

> **The one-line point:** The Earth is layered like an onion — **crust, mantle, core** — and its outermost skin is made of **three kinds of rock** that endlessly convert into one another. That conversion, the **rock cycle**, is why the same atoms have been recycled through the planet for four billion years.

## 1️⃣ The Earth's Interior

```
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  🍎 THE EARTH IS LAYERED LIKE AN ONION              │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

         ╱▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔╲
       ╱   CRUST  (thin skin)  ╲     ← 5 km (ocean) to 35 km (land)
      │  ╱▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔▔╲    │        Si + Al = SIAL (continental)
      │ │                  │   │        Si + Mg = SIMA (oceanic)
      │ │     MANTLE       │   │     ← to 2,900 km
      │ │                  │   │
      │ │    ╱▔▔▔▔▔▔╲      │   │
      │ │   │  CORE  │     │   │     ← to 6,400 km (centre)
      │ │   │ Ni+Fe  │     │   │        NIFE. Outer = liquid,
      │ │    ╲______╱      │   │        Inner = solid
      │  ╲________________╱    │
       ╲______________________╱
```

| Layer | Depth | Made of | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CRUST** | **5 km** under oceans · **35 km** under continents | **SIAL** (silica + alumina) = continental · **SIMA** (silica + magnesium) = oceanic | **The thinnest layer** — and the only one we live on or have ever drilled into |
| **MANTLE** | To **2,900 km** | Silicate rock, semi-molten | The thickest layer. Convection here drives plate movement |
| **CORE** | To **6,400 km** (centre) | **NIFE** — **Ni**ckel + **Fe**rrum (iron) | **Outer core liquid, inner core solid.** Temperatures and pressures are extreme |

> 🥚 **The comparison that fits: a boiled egg.** Shell = crust (brittle, thin, cracks into plates). White = mantle (thick, the bulk of the volume). Yolk = core (dense, at the centre). It's not a perfect analogy — the mantle isn't liquid and the shell doesn't move — but for the **proportions** it is exactly right, and proportion is what people get wrong. **The crust is proportionally thinner than an eggshell.**

> **Why the core is iron and nickel** — worth understanding. When the young Earth was molten, the densest materials sank to the centre and the lighter ones floated up. Iron and nickel are heavy; silica and aluminium are light. **The Earth sorted itself by density.** That's also why the core generates the magnetic field that gives us the compass (Cl.6 Ch.4) and shields us from solar radiation.

## 2️⃣ Rocks — The Three Types

> **Rock** = any natural mass of mineral matter making up the Earth's crust.
> **Mineral** = a naturally occurring substance with a definite chemical composition. **Rocks are made of minerals.**

### The master table — highest-value content in the chapter

| | 🔥 **IGNEOUS** | 🏖️ **SEDIMENTARY** | 💎 **METAMORPHIC** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **From** | *ignis* = fire | *sedimentum* = settling | *meta* = change + *morph* = form |
| **How it forms** | **Molten magma/lava cools and solidifies** | **Sediments compress** over long periods | **Existing rock changed by heat and pressure** |
| **Also called** | **Primary rock** — the original rock | — | — |
| **Layered?** | No | ✅ **Yes — in visible layers/strata** | Often banded/foliated |
| **Fossils?** | ❌ Never | ✅ **Yes — the only rock with fossils** | ❌ Destroyed by the change |
| **Examples** | **Granite** (intrusive), **Basalt** (extrusive) | **Sandstone, limestone, shale, coal** | **Marble, slate, diamond, quartzite, graphite** |

### 🔥 Igneous — two sub-types

| | **INTRUSIVE** | **EXTRUSIVE** |
|---|---|---|
| **Cools** | **Inside** the Earth, slowly | **On the surface**, fast |
| **Crystals** | **Large** (slow cooling = time to grow) | **Very fine/small** (rapid cooling) |
| **Example** | **GRANITE** | **BASALT** |

> 🍫 **Why cooling speed decides crystal size — the comparison that fits.** Cool sugar syrup slowly and you get large rock-candy crystals; cool it fast and you get a smooth, glassy toffee. **Same substance, different cooling rate, completely different texture.** Magma behaves identically: slow underground cooling gives granite's visible crystals; fast surface cooling gives basalt's fine grain.

> 🇮🇳 **Basalt matters to India specifically.** The **Deccan Trap** — the vast lava plateau of central and western India — is basalt. Weathered basalt produces **black soil (regur)**, which retains moisture and is ideal for **cotton**. **A rock type determines a soil type determines a crop determines a regional economy.** This chain appears again in Class 8 and 10.

### 🏖️ Sedimentary — and why fossils live here

Rocks at the surface are broken down by wind, water and ice into **sediments** — small fragments. These are transported, deposited in layers, and compressed over millions of years into rock.

> **Why only sedimentary rock has fossils** — this is the reasoning UPSC likes:
> Fossils form when a dead organism is **buried gently in accumulating sediment** and preserved. Igneous rock forms from molten material at 1,000°C+ — nothing organic survives that. Metamorphic rock is existing rock **cooked and crushed** — any fossils present are destroyed in the process.
> **So the fossil record — the entire history of life on Earth — is written in sedimentary rock only.**

**Coal** is a special case worth noting: it's sedimentary rock formed from compressed ancient plant matter. This is the literal sense in which fossil fuels are "ancient sunlight" (Cl.6 Ch.1).

### 💎 Metamorphic — rock that changed

Existing igneous or sedimentary rock, subjected to **great heat and pressure**, transforms without melting.

| Original rock | → | Metamorphic result |
|---|---|---|
| **Clay** | → | **Slate** |
| **Limestone** | → | **Marble** |
| **Sandstone** | → | Quartzite |
| **Coal** | → | Graphite → (extreme conditions) **Diamond** |

> 🍞 **The comparison that fits: dough becoming bread.** Heat and time change the structure completely — you cannot get the dough back — but nothing was melted and re-poured. It's the same material, reorganised. **Marble is limestone that has been baked.**

> 🏛️ **Cross-link to Art & Culture:** the **Taj Mahal** is marble (metamorphic), **Red Fort** and much Mughal architecture is red sandstone (sedimentary), and South Indian temples are largely granite (igneous). **Which rock a monument uses depends on what the local geology offered** — a genuinely useful point in a heritage answer.

## 3️⃣ The Rock Cycle

The three types are not permanent categories. They **continuously become one another.**

```
                    ┌──── heat & pressure ────┐
                    ▼                          │
              METAMORPHIC ◄──────────────── SEDIMENTARY
                    │                          ▲
                    │ melts                    │ compaction
                    ▼                          │ & cementation
                  MAGMA                    SEDIMENTS
                    │                          ▲
                    │ cools                    │ weathering
                    ▼                          │ & erosion
                IGNEOUS ──────────────────────┘
```

**Read it as a loop:** magma cools into igneous rock → weathering breaks it into sediments → sediments compress into sedimentary rock → heat and pressure convert that into metamorphic rock → deeper heat melts it back into magma → and round again.

> 🔑 **The idea worth carrying: no rock is final.** The Himalayas will one day be sediment on an ocean floor; that sediment will become rock; that rock may be melted and erupted. **The same atoms have cycled through this loop for 4.5 billion years.** It is the lithosphere's equivalent of the water cycle — and it is why Class 6's "landforms are processes, not objects" holds at every scale.

## 4️⃣ Why Rocks Matter

| Use | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Minerals and ores** | Iron, copper, bauxite, gold — the basis of industry |
| **Fuels** | Coal, petroleum (in sedimentary basins) |
| **Construction** | Granite, marble, sandstone, slate |
| **Soil formation** | All soil is weathered rock — basalt → black soil |
| **Tools, historically** | The entire Stone Age (cross-link: your History Cl.6 Ch.2) |

> ⛏️ **Where minerals are found follows directly from rock type**, which is why Class 6 Ch.6 said plateaus are mineral storehouses. Old, hard, deeply eroded igneous and metamorphic rock exposes ore bodies. **India's Chhotanagpur Plateau is the case study**, and it recurs in Class 8, Class 10 and Economy.

| **Fossils only in sedimentary** ⭐ | And the reason why |
| **Intrusive vs extrusive** ⭐ | **Granite** (inside, large crystals) vs **Basalt** (surface, fine crystals) |
| **Metamorphic pairs** ⭐ | Limestone→marble · Clay→slate · Coal→graphite/diamond · Sandstone→quartzite |
| **Layers of the Earth** | Crust (5–35 km) · Mantle (to 2,900 km) · Core (to 6,400 km) |
| **SIAL / SIMA / NIFE** | Continental crust / oceanic crust / core |
| **Igneous = primary rock** | The original rock; all others derive from it |
| **Rock cycle** | A closed loop; no rock type is permanent |

**Trap 1:** *"Fossils are found in metamorphic rock."* **No** — heat and pressure destroy them. **Sedimentary only.**
**Trap 2:** *"Granite is extrusive."* **No — intrusive.** Basalt is the extrusive one. (Mnemonic: **B**asalt = **B**ahar/outside.)
**Trap 3:** *"Marble is sedimentary."* **No — metamorphic**, from limestone. Limestone is the sedimentary one.
**Trap 4:** *"The crust is the thickest layer."* **The thinnest.** The mantle is by far the thickest.
**Trap 5:** *"Diamond is igneous."* Classified as **metamorphic** in NCERT (carbon under extreme heat and pressure).

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Crust** | Outermost thin layer; 5 km (oceanic) to 35 km (continental) |
| **SIAL** | Continental crust — **si**lica + **al**umina |
| **SIMA** | Oceanic crust — **si**lica + **ma**gnesium |
| **Mantle** | Layer beneath the crust, to 2,900 km |
| **Core** | Innermost layer; **NIFE** (nickel + iron); outer liquid, inner solid |
| **Rock** | Natural mass of mineral matter forming the crust |
| **Mineral** | Naturally occurring substance of definite chemical composition |
| **Igneous rock** | Formed from cooled magma/lava. **Primary rock** |
| **Intrusive** | Cooled **inside** the Earth — **large** crystals (granite) |
| **Extrusive** | Cooled **on the surface** — **fine** crystals (basalt) |
| **Sedimentary rock** | Formed from compressed sediments. **Contains fossils** |
| **Metamorphic rock** | Formed from existing rock changed by heat and pressure |
| **Fossil** | Preserved remains of ancient life, found only in sedimentary rock |
| **Rock cycle** | The continuous conversion of one rock type into another |
| **Magma / Lava** | Molten rock **below** / **above** the surface |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Three layers: crust (thinnest, 5–35 km), mantle (thickest, to 2,900 km), core (NIFE, to 6,400 km).**
2. **SIAL = continental crust, SIMA = oceanic crust, NIFE = core.** Outer core liquid, inner core solid.
3. **The Earth sorted itself by density** when molten — heavy iron and nickel sank to the centre.
4. **Three rock types: igneous (fire), sedimentary (settling), metamorphic (changed).**
5. **Igneous is primary rock.** Intrusive = **granite** (large crystals, slow cooling). Extrusive = **basalt** (fine crystals, fast cooling).
6. **Fossils occur only in sedimentary rock** — igneous is too hot, metamorphic destroys them.
7. **Metamorphic pairs: limestone→marble, clay→slate, coal→graphite/diamond, sandstone→quartzite.**
8. **The rock cycle is a closed loop.** No rock type is permanent.
9. 🇮🇳 **Deccan basalt → black soil → cotton.** Rock type decides soil decides crop decides economy.
