# Chapter 8 — Transport and Communication

> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **Transport moves things; communication moves information — and the great change of the modern era is that the two have SEPARATED.** ⭐ **For most of history a message travelled only as fast as the messenger; today information moves instantly while goods still move at the speed of a ship.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐ Transport

⭐ **TRANSPORT is a service or facility by which people, materials and manufactured goods are physically carried from one location to another.**

⭐ **It is an organised industry created to satisfy man's basic need of MOBILITY.**

⭐ **Modern society requires speedy and efficient transport systems to assist in the production, distribution and consumption of goods.**

⭐ **At every stage in this complex system, the value of the material is significantly enhanced by transportation.**

### ⭐ Key terms

| Term | ⭐ Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **TRANSPORT NETWORK** | ⭐ **Several places (NODES) joined together by a series of routes (LINKS) to form a pattern** |
| ⭐ **NODES** | ⭐ **The places joined by routes** |
| ⭐ **LINKS** | ⭐ **The routes joining the nodes** |
| ⭐ **ACCESSIBILITY** | ⭐ **The ease of reaching a place** |

⭐ **The four major modes of transport are LAND, WATER, AIR and PIPELINES.**

⭐ **The factors influencing the choice: the DISTANCE in terms of time and cost, the NATURE OF THE COMMODITY (perishability, bulk, value), and the availability of the mode.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Land Transport

### ⭐ Roads

⭐ **The world's TOTAL MOTORABLE ROAD LENGTH is only about 15 million km, of which North America accounts for 33 per cent.**

⭐ **The highest road density and the highest number of vehicles are registered in this continent compared to Western Europe.**

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Traffic flows** | ⭐ **Roads are cheaper to construct and maintain than railways.** ⭐ **Road transport is economical for short distances** |
| ⭐ **QUALITY OF ROADS** | ⭐ **In developed countries, good quality roads are universal and provide long-distance links in the form of MOTORWAYS, AUTOBAHN (Germany), and INTERSTATE HIGHWAYS (USA)** |
| ⭐ **UNMETALLED ROADS** | ⭐ **In under-developed countries most roads are unmetalled and unsuitable for vehicular traffic** |

### ⭐⭐ Highways — the world's great routes

⭐ **HIGHWAYS are metalled roads connecting distant places.** ⭐ **They are constructed in a manner for unobstructed vehicular movement.**

| Highway | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **In North America** | ⭐ **Highway density is high, about 0.65 km per sq km.** ⭐ **Every place is within 20 km distance from a highway.** ⭐ **Cities located on the Pacific coast (west) are well-connected with those of the Atlantic coast (east)** |
| ⭐ **TRANS-CANADIAN HIGHWAY** | ⭐ **Links Vancouver in British Columbia (West Coast) to St. John's City in Newfoundland (East Coast)** |
| ⭐ **ALASKAN HIGHWAY** | ⭐ **Links Edmonton (Canada) to Anchorage (Alaska)** |
| ⭐ **PAN-AMERICAN HIGHWAY** | ⭐ **A large portion of this planned highway connecting the countries of South America, Central America and USA-Canada, has been constructed** |
| ⭐ **In Europe** | ⭐ **Highly developed, dense network** |
| ⭐ **In Russia** | ⭐ **A dense highway network in the industrialised region west of the Urals with Moscow as the hub.** ⭐ **The important Moscow-Vladivostok Highway serves the region to the east** |
| ⭐ **In China** | ⭐ **A large highway network which criss-crosses the country connecting all major cities such as Tsungtso (near Vietnam boundary), Shanghai (central China), Guangzhou (south) and Beijing (north)** |
| ⭐ **In India** | ⭐ **The Border Roads Organisation constructs and maintains roads in strategic border areas** |

### ⭐⭐ Railways

⭐ **RAILWAYS are a mode of land transport for BULKY GOODS and PASSENGERS OVER LONG DISTANCES.**

⭐ **Commuter trains are very popular in UK, USA, Japan and India.** ⭐ **These carry millions of passengers daily to and fro in the city.**

⭐ **There are about 13 lakh km of railways open for traffic in the world.**

| Region | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **EUROPE** | ⭐ **Has one of the DENSEST RAIL NETWORKS in the world.** ⭐ **There are about 4,40,000 km of railways, most of which is double or multiple-tracked.** ⭐ **Belgium has the highest density of 1 km of railway for every 6.5 sq km area** |
| ⭐ **RUSSIA** | ⭐ **Accounts for about 90 per cent of the country's total transport with a very dense network west of the Urals.** ⭐ **Moscow is the hub with 11 lines radiating to different parts of the region** |
| ⭐ **NORTH AMERICA** | ⭐ **Has one of the most extensive rail networks accounting for nearly 40 per cent of the world's total.** ⭐ **In contrast to many European countries, the railways are used more for LONG-DISTANCE BULKY FREIGHT like ores, grains, timbers and machinery than for passengers** |
| ⭐ **AUSTRALIA** | ⭐ **About 40 per cent of the total railways of Australia are found in New South Wales alone.** ⭐ **The west-east Australian National Railway line runs across the country from Perth to Sydney** |
| ⭐ **SOUTH AMERICA** | ⭐ **Only Brazil and Argentina have a fairly dense rail network.** ⭐ **Nearly 40 per cent of the total railways in South America are found in Brazil alone** |
| ⭐ **ASIA** | ⭐ **Rail network is the densest in Japan, China and India** |
| ⭐ **AFRICA** | ⭐ **Despite being the second largest continent, has only 40,000 km of railways.** ⭐ **South Africa alone contains 18,000 km due to a highly developed gold mining industry** |

### ⭐⭐ Trans-continental railways

⭐ **TRANS-CONTINENTAL RAILWAYS run across the continent and link its two ends.**

| Railway | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **TRANS-SIBERIAN RAILWAY** | ⭐ **The MOST IMPORTANT route in Asia and the LONGEST DOUBLE-TRACKED and ELECTRIFIED trans-continental railway in the world.** ⭐ **It runs from ST. PETERSBURG in the west to VLADIVOSTOK on the Pacific Coast in the east, passing through Moscow, Ufa, Novosibirsk, Irkutsk, Chita and Khabarovsk.** ⭐ **It is the most important route in Asia and the longest (9,332 km) double-tracked and electrified trans-continental railway in the world.** ⭐ **It has helped in opening up its Asian region to West European markets.** ⭐ **It runs across the Ural Mountains Ob and Yenisei rivers.** ⭐ **Chief regional branch lines include connections to Odessa (Ukraine), Baku (on the Caspian Coast), Tashkent (Central Asia), Ulan Bator (Mongolia) and Beijing (China)** |
| ⭐ **TRANS-CANADIAN RAILWAYS** | ⭐ **This 7,050 km long rail-line in Canada runs from HALIFAX in the east to VANCOUVER on the Pacific Coast passing through Montreal, Ottawa, Winnipeg and Calgary.** ⭐ **It was constructed in 1886, initially as a part of an agreement to make British Columbia a part of the Canadian Federation.** ⭐ **This rail-line is the ECONOMIC ARTERY of Canada.** ⭐ **Wheat and meat are the important export items** |
| ⭐ **THE UNION AND PACIFIC RAILWAY** | ⭐ **This rail-line connects NEW YORK on the Atlantic Coast to SAN FRANCISCO on the Pacific Coast passing through Cleveland, Chicago, Omaha, Evans, Ogden and Sacramento.** ⭐ **The most valuable exports on this route are ores, grain, paper, chemicals and machinery** |
| ⭐ **THE AUSTRALIAN TRANS-CONTINENTAL RAILWAY** | ⭐ **Runs west-east across the southern part of the continent from PERTH on the west coast, to SYDNEY on the east coast, covering a distance of 3,961 km.** ⭐ **Another major north-south line connects Adelaide and Alice Spring and to be joined further to the Darwin-Birdum line** |
| ⭐ **THE ORIENT EXPRESS** | ⭐ **Runs from PARIS to ISTANBUL passing through Strasbourg, Munich, Vienna, Budapest and Belgrade.** ⭐ **The journey time from London to Istanbul is now reduced to 96 hours as against 10 days by sea.** ⭐ **The chief exports on this rail-route are cheese, bacon, oats, wine, fruits and machinery** |

> ⭐⭐ **The Trans-Siberian is the single most examinable railway fact:** ⭐ **the LONGEST double-tracked and electrified trans-continental railway in the world, 9,332 km, ST. PETERSBURG to VLADIVOSTOK.** ⭐ **Branch lines to Odessa, Baku, Tashkent, Ulan Bator and Beijing.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ Water Transport

⭐ **One of the great advantages of water transportation is that it does not require route construction.**

⭐ **The sea and oceans are linked with each other and are negotiable with ships of various sizes.**

⚠️ ⭐ **All that is needed is to provide port facilities at the two ends.**

⭐ **It is much CHEAPER because the friction of water is far less than that of land.**

⭐ **The energy cost of water transportation is lower.**

⭐ **Water transport is divided into SEA ROUTES and INLAND WATERWAYS.**

### ⭐⭐ Important sea routes

| Route | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **THE NORTHERN ATLANTIC SEA ROUTE** | ⭐ **This links North-eastern U.S.A. and North-western Europe, the two industrially developed regions of the world.** ⭐ **The foreign trade over this route is greater than that of the rest of the world combined.** ⭐ **One fourth of the world's foreign trade moves on this route.** ⭐ **It is, therefore, the BUSIEST IN THE WORLD and otherwise, called the BIG TRUNK ROUTE** |
| ⭐ **THE MEDITERRANEAN–INDIAN OCEAN SEA ROUTE** | ⭐ **This sea route passes through the heart of the Old World and serves more countries and people than any other route.** ⭐ **Port Said, Aden, Mumbai, Colombo and Singapore are some of the important ports on this route.** ⭐ **The construction of the Suez Canal has greatly reduced the distance and time as compared to the earlier route through the Cape of Good Hope** |
| ⭐ **THE CAPE OF GOOD HOPE SEA ROUTE** | ⭐ **This is another important route across the Atlantic Ocean which connects West European and West African countries with Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay in South America** |
| ⭐ **THE SOUTH PACIFIC SEA ROUTE** | ⭐ **This route connects Western Europe and North America with Australia, New Zealand and the scattered Pacific islands via the Panama Canal.** ⭐ **This route is also used for reaching Hong Kong, Philippines and Indonesia.** ⭐ **The distance covered between Panama and Sydney is 12,000 km** |
| ⭐ **THE NORTH PACIFIC SEA ROUTE** | ⭐ **Links the ports on the west-coast of North America with those of Asia** |

### ⭐⭐ Shipping canals

| Canal | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **THE SUEZ CANAL** | ⭐ **Constructed in 1869 in Egypt between PORT SAID in the north and PORT SUEZ in the south linking the MEDITERRANEAN SEA and the RED SEA.** ⭐ **It gives Europe a NEW GATEWAY to the Indian Ocean and reduces direct sea-route distance between Liverpool and Colombo compared to the Cape of Good Hope route.** ⭐ **It is a SEA-LEVEL CANAL WITHOUT LOCKS which is about 160 km and 11 to 15 m deep.** ⭐ **About 100 ships travel daily and each ship takes 10–12 hours to cross this canal.** ⭐ **The tolls are so heavy that some find it cheaper to go by the longer Cape Route** |
| ⭐ **THE PANAMA CANAL** | ⭐ **Connects the ATLANTIC OCEAN in the east to the PACIFIC OCEAN in the west.** ⭐ **It has been constructed across the Panama Isthmus between Panama City and Colon.** ⭐ **This canal from Gulf of Panama in the Pacific Ocean to Colon in the Caribbean Sea provides a SHORT ROUTE.** ⭐ **The canal is about 72 km long and involves a DEEP CUTTING for a length of 12 km.** ⭐ **It has a SERIES OF LOCKS.** ⭐ **Its impact is more regional than global** |

> ⭐⭐ **The Suez vs Panama contrast is a favourite:**
> ⭐ **SUEZ (1869): Mediterranean ↔ Red Sea; ~160 km; SEA-LEVEL, NO LOCKS; global impact.**
> ⭐ **PANAMA: Atlantic ↔ Pacific; ~72 km; has a SERIES OF LOCKS; regional impact.**
>
> ⭐ **NCERT's blunt observation about Suez tolls is worth carrying: they are so heavy that some ships still find the longer Cape route cheaper.** ⭐ **A shorter route is not automatically a cheaper one.**

### ⭐ Inland waterways

⭐ **Rivers, canals, lakes and coastal areas have been important waterways since time immemorial.**

⭐ **The development of inland waterways is dependent on the NAVIGABILITY WIDTH AND DEPTH OF THE CHANNEL, CONTINUITY IN THE WATER FLOW, and TRANSPORT TECHNOLOGY in use.**

| Waterway | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **THE RHINE WATERWAYS** | ⭐ **The Rhine flows through GERMANY and the NETHERLANDS.** ⭐ **It is navigable for 700 km from Rotterdam, at its mouth in the Netherlands to Basel in Switzerland.** ⭐ **Ocean-going vessels can reach up to Cologne.** ⭐ **This waterway is the WORLD'S MOST HEAVILY USED.** ⭐ **In one year, over 20,000 ocean-going ships and 2,00,000 inland vessels exchange their cargoes** |
| ⭐ **THE DANUBE WATERWAY** | ⭐ **This important inland waterway serves EASTERN EUROPE.** ⭐ **The Danube river rises in the Black Forest and flows eastwards through many countries.** ⭐ **It is navigable up to Taurna Severin.** ⭐ **The chief export items are wheat, maize, timber and machinery** |
| ⭐ **THE VOLGA WATERWAY** | ⭐ **RUSSIA has a large number of developed waterways, of which the Volga is one of the most important.** ⭐ **It provides a navigable waterway of 11,200 km and drains into the Caspian Sea.** ⭐ **The Volga-Moscow Canal connects it with the Moscow region and the Volga-Don Canal with the Black Sea** |
| ⭐ **THE GREAT LAKES–ST. LAWRENCE SEAWAY** | ⭐ **The Great Lakes of North America — Superior, Huron, Erie and Ontario — are connected by SOO CANAL and WELLAND CANAL to form an inland waterway.** ⭐ **The channel is called the ST. LAWRENCE SEAWAY.** ⭐ **Large ocean-going vessels can reach up to the interior of the continent** |
| ⭐ **THE MISSISSIPPI WATERWAYS** | ⭐ **The Mississippi-Ohio waterway connects the interior part of U.S.A. with the Gulf of Mexico in the south.** ⭐ **Large steamers can go through this route up to Minneapolis** |

> ⭐ **The RHINE is the fact most often asked: ⭐ THE WORLD'S MOST HEAVILY USED INLAND WATERWAY** — over 20,000 ocean-going ships and 200,000 inland vessels annually.

## 4️⃣ ⭐ Air Transport and Pipelines

### ⭐ Air transport

⭐ **AIR TRANSPORT is the FASTEST means of transportation, but it is very COSTLY.**

⭐ **Being fast, it is preferred by passengers for long-distance travel.**

⭐ **Valuable cargo can be moved rapidly on a worldwide scale.** ⭐ **It is often the ONLY MEANS to reach INACCESSIBLE AREAS.**

⭐ **Air transport has brought about a REVOLUTION IN THE MODE OF TRANSPORT because of its SPEED.**

⭐ **The frequency of air services is more in Northern Hemisphere.** ⭐ **NORTH AMERICA, WESTERN EUROPE and SOUTHEAST ASIA have a dense network of air routes.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The NORTHERN HEMISPHERE has a distinct east-west belt of inter-continental air routes.** ⭐ **Africa, Asiatic part of Russia and South America lack air services.** ⭐ **There are limited air services between 10–35 latitudes in the Southern hemisphere.**

### ⭐ Pipelines

⭐ **PIPELINES are used extensively to transport LIQUIDS and GASES such as water, petroleum and natural gas for an uninterrupted flow.**

⭐ **Water supplied through pipelines is familiar to all.** ⭐ **Cooking gas or LPG is supplied through pipelines in many parts of the world.**

⭐ **In New Zealand, milk is supplied from farms to factories through pipelines.**

⭐ **In U.S.A. there is a dense network of oil pipelines from the producing areas to the consuming areas.** ⭐ **BIG INCH is one such famous pipeline, which carries petroleum from the oil wells of the Gulf of Mexico to the North-eastern States.**

⭐ **About 17 per cent of all freight per tonne-km is carried through pipelines in U.S.A.**

⭐ **In Europe, Russia, West Asia and India pipelines are used to connect oil wells to refineries, and to ports or domestic markets.**

⭐ **TURKMENISTAN in central Asia has extended pipelines to Iran and also to parts of China.**

⭐ **The proposed IRAN-INDIA VIA PAKISTAN INTERNATIONAL GAS PIPELINE will be the LONGEST IN THE WORLD and Will Deliver 2.2 billion cubic feet of gas per day.**

> ⭐ **BIG INCH is the named pipeline to know** — carries petroleum from the Gulf of Mexico oil wells to the north-eastern states of the USA. ⭐ **And New Zealand's MILK pipeline is the memorable oddity NCERT includes.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ Communication

⭐ **Human beings have used different methods of long distance communications of which the most common was the WRITTEN MESSAGE.**

⭐ **In fact, the carrier of the messages has always been an important person in human society.**

⭐ **With the development of technology, mobile telephony and satellite communication have revolutionised communication.**

### ⭐ Telecommunications

⭐ **The use of telecommunications is linked to the development of modern technology.**

⭐ **It has revolutionised communications because of the speed with which messages are sent.**

⭐ **The time is reduced from weeks to minutes.**

⭐ **Mobile telephony has made communications easier.** ⭐ **The developments in telecommunication technology have brought about a revolution.**

### ⭐⭐ Satellite communication

⭐ **SATELLITE COMMUNICATION is one of the most sophisticated forms.**

⭐ **Satellites are an integral part of the communication system.**

⭐ **They regulate the worldwide communication, and the entire world can be linked through it.**

⭐ **In India, ARYABHAT was launched on 19 April 1979, BHASKAR-I in 1979 and ROHINI in 1980.**

⭐ **On 18 June 1981, APPLE (Ariane Passenger Payload Experiment) was launched through Ariane rocket.**

⭐ **INSAT-IA (National Satellite System) was commissioned in 1983.**

⭐ **Satellite images can be used for weather forecast, monitoring of natural calamities, surveillance of border areas, etc.**

### ⭐⭐ Cyber space — Internet

> ⭐ **CYBER SPACE is the world of ELECTRONIC COMPUTERISED SPACE.**

⭐ **It is encompassed by the INTERNET such as the World Wide Web (www).**

⭐ **In simple words, it is the ELECTRONIC DIGITAL WORLD for communicating or accessing information over computer networks without physical movement of the sender and the receiver.** ⭐ **It is also referred to as INTERNET.**

⭐ **Cyberspace exists everywhere.** ⭐ **It may be in an office, sailing boat, flying plane and virtually anywhere.**

⭐ **The internet users number in 2000 grew from 1,50,000 to more than 260 million by 2000.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The countries with a large number of internet users are U.S.A., U.K., Germany, Japan, China and India.**

⭐ **Now, in the age of INFORMATION SOCIETY, the entire world is a GLOBAL VILLAGE.**

⭐ **Cyberspace will expand the contemporary economic and social space of humans through E-MAIL, E-COMMERCE, E-LEARNING and E-GOVERNANCE.**

⭐ **The internet together with fax, television and radio will be accessible to more and more people cutting across place and time.**

⭐ **It is these modern communication systems, more than transportation, that has made the concept of GLOBAL VILLAGE a reality.**

> ⭐⭐ **NCERT's closing claim is the point of the whole chapter, and it is worth quoting:** ⭐ **"It is these modern communication systems, MORE THAN TRANSPORTATION, that has made the concept of global village a reality."**
>
> ⭐ **This is the great separation.** ⭐ **For all of history before the telegraph, a message could travel no faster than a person could carry it** — information and goods moved at the same speed.
>
> ⭐ **Now information is INSTANT and effectively free, while goods still move at the speed of a container ship.** ⭐ **That divergence is what makes outsourcing (Ch.7), global finance and remote work possible** — you can move the *work* without moving the *worker* or the *product*.
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **"Globalisation is possible because information decoupled from transport" is a compact and genuinely strong line for an Economy or Essay answer.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Transport networks = NODES joined by LINKS.** Four modes: land, water, air, pipelines.
2. ⭐ **World roads ~15 million km; North America has 33%.** **Autobahn (Germany), Interstate Highways (USA).**
3. ⭐ **Highways: Trans-Canadian (Vancouver–St. John's) · Alaskan (Edmonton–Anchorage) · Pan-American.**
4. ⭐⭐ **TRANS-SIBERIAN: longest double-tracked and electrified trans-continental railway; 9,332 km; St. Petersburg to Vladivostok.** Branches to Odessa, Baku, Tashkent, Ulan Bator, Beijing.
5. ⭐ **Trans-Canadian Railway (1886, Halifax–Vancouver, 7,050 km) is Canada's ECONOMIC ARTERY.** ⭐ **Orient Express: Paris–Istanbul, 96 hours vs 10 days by sea.**
6. ⭐ **Belgium has the densest rail network (1 km per 6.5 sq km). North America has ~40% of world rail, used mainly for FREIGHT. Africa has only 40,000 km.**
7. ⭐⭐ **The NORTHERN ATLANTIC route is the BIG TRUNK ROUTE — the busiest, carrying one-fourth of world foreign trade.**
8. ⭐⭐ **SUEZ (1869): Mediterranean–Red Sea, ~160 km, SEA-LEVEL, NO LOCKS, global impact.** ⭐ **PANAMA: Atlantic–Pacific, ~72 km, SERIES OF LOCKS, regional impact.** ⭐ **Suez tolls are heavy enough that some ships still round the Cape.**
9. ⭐ **The RHINE is the world's most heavily used inland waterway.** ⭐ **The St. Lawrence Seaway links the Great Lakes via the Soo and Welland canals.**
10. ⭐ **BIG INCH pipeline: Gulf of Mexico to north-eastern USA.** New Zealand pipes MILK.
11. ⭐ **Indian satellites: Aryabhat (1979), Bhaskar-I (1979), Rohini (1980), APPLE (1981), INSAT-IA (1983).**
12. ⭐⭐ **CYBER SPACE made the GLOBAL VILLAGE real — "more than transportation."** ⭐ **Information decoupled from transport, which is what makes outsourcing and global finance possible.**
