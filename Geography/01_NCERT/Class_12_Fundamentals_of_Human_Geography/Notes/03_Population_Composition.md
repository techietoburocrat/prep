## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ Sex Composition

> ⭐ **The ratio between the number of women and men in the population is called the SEX RATIO.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Note the two conventions — and this is a genuine trap:**

| Convention | ⭐ Formula |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **INDIA** | ⭐ **Number of FEMALES per 1,000 MALES** |
| ⭐ **Most other countries** | ⭐ **Number of MALES per 1,000 FEMALES** — **Sex Ratio = (Male Population ÷ Female Population) × 1000** |

⭐ **A higher sex ratio (in the international convention) is unfavourable to females; a low ratio is favourable.**

### ⭐ World pattern

⭐ **The world population reflects a sex ratio of 102 males per 100 females** *(i.e. slightly more men)*.

⭐ **The highest sex ratio in the world has been recorded in LATVIA where there are 85 males per 100 females** *(i.e. far more women)*.

⭐ **On the other extreme is QATAR where there are 311 males per 100 females.**

⭐ **The world pattern of sex ratio does not exhibit variations in the developed countries.** ⭐ **The ratio is favourable for females in 139 countries of the world and unfavourable for them in the remaining 72 countries.**

⚠️ ⭐ **In general, ASIA has a low sex ratio.** ⭐ **Countries like China, India, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, Afghanistan have a lower sex ratio.**

⭐ **On the other extreme is greater part of Europe (including Russia) where males are in minority.** ⭐ **A deficit of males in the populations of many European countries is attributed to better status of women, and an excessively male-dominated out-migration to different parts of the world in the past.**

> ⭐⭐ **The Qatar and Latvia extremes both have explanations worth knowing:**
> ⭐ **QATAR (311 males per 100 females)** — an extreme male surplus caused by **massive male labour migration** into Gulf construction and services.
> ⭐ **LATVIA (85 males per 100 females)** — an extreme female surplus, reflecting **higher male mortality** and the demographic legacy of war.
>
> ⭐ **Neither extreme is about births.** **Both are about migration and mortality** — which is exactly why sex ratio is a *composition* measure, not a fertility one.

> ⭐ **Note NCERT's explanation for Europe's female surplus: BETTER STATUS OF WOMEN and historical MALE-DOMINATED OUT-MIGRATION.** ⭐ **The reasons a country has "too many" women and a country has "too few" are entirely different in kind — one is about longevity, the other about son preference.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Age Structure

> ⭐ **AGE STRUCTURE represents the number of people of different age groups.**

⭐ **This is an important indicator of population composition, since a large size of population in the age group of 15–59 indicates a large working population.**

⭐ **A greater proportion of population above 60 years represents an AGEING POPULATION which requires more expenditure on health care facilities.**

⭐ **Similarly, high proportion of young population would mean that the region has a high birth rate and the population is YOUTHFUL.**

### ⭐⭐ The Age-Sex Pyramid

> ⭐ **The age-sex structure of a population refers to the number of females and males in different age groups.** ⭐ **A POPULATION PYRAMID is used to show the age-sex structure of the population.**

⭐ **The SHAPE of the population pyramid reflects the characteristics of the population.**

⭐ **The LEFT SIDE shows the percentage of MALES while the RIGHT SIDE shows the percentage of WOMEN in each age group.**

```
   ⭐⭐ THE THREE SHAPES

   ⭐ 1. EXPANDING            ⭐ 2. CONSTANT           ⭐ 3. DECLINING
      (triangular)              (bell-shaped)           (narrow base)

        ▁▁▁                      ▁▁▁▁▁                   ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁
       ▁▁▁▁▁                    ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                 ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁
      ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                  ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁
     ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                 ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                 ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁
    ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                ▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁                  ▁▁▁▁▁

   ⭐ HIGH birth rates,       ⭐ Birth ≈ death,        ⭐ LOW birth rate,
     population growing        population stable        population DECLINING
     e.g. NIGERIA,             e.g. AUSTRALIA           e.g. JAPAN
     BANGLADESH, MEXICO
```

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **EXPANDING POPULATIONS** | ⭐ **The age-sex pyramid is TRIANGULAR SHAPED with a WIDE BASE.** ⭐ **It is representative of developing countries.** ⭐ **These have larger populations in lower age groups due to high birth rates.** ⭐ **Examples: NIGERIA, BANGLADESH, MEXICO** |
| ⭐ **CONSTANT POPULATION** | ⭐ **BELL SHAPED and TAPERED TOWARDS THE TOP.** ⭐ **This shows birth and death rates are almost equal leading to a near constant population.** ⭐ **Example: AUSTRALIA** |
| ⭐ **DECLINING POPULATIONS** | ⭐ **NARROW BASE and a TAPERED TOP** showing low birth and death rates. ⭐ **The population growth in developed countries is usually zero or negative.** ⭐ **Example: JAPAN** |

> ⭐⭐ **How to read a pyramid in three seconds:** ⭐ **look at the BASE.** ⭐ **Wide base = many children = growing population. Narrow base = few children = ageing, shrinking population.** ⭐ **The TOP tells you life expectancy** — a broad top means many elderly survive.

## 3️⃣ ⭐ Rural–Urban Composition

⭐ **The division of population into rural and urban is based on the residence.** ⭐ **This division is necessary because rural and urban life styles differ from each other in terms of their livelihood and social conditions.**

⭐ **The age-sex-occupational structure, density of population and level of development vary between rural and urban areas.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The criteria for differentiating rural and urban population varies from country to country.**

⭐ **In general terms rural areas are those where people are engaged in PRIMARY ACTIVITIES and urban areas are those when majority of the working population is engaged in NON-PRIMARY ACTIVITIES.**

> ⭐ **An interesting sex-ratio observation NCERT makes:** ⭐ **In Western countries, males outnumber females in rural areas and females outnumber males in urban areas.** ⭐ **In countries like Nepal, Pakistan and India, the case is reverse — the sex ratio in urban areas is lower than rural areas.**
>
> ⭐ **The explanation:** ⭐ **in the West, farming is highly mechanised and men dominate it, while women move to towns for service employment.** ⭐ **In South Asia, MEN migrate to cities for work leaving families behind** — producing male-heavy cities and female-heavy villages. ⭐ **The same variable, opposite pattern, for entirely different social reasons.** **A good, specific point for a GS-I society answer on migration.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ Literacy

⭐ **Proportion of literate population of a country is an indicator of its socio-economic development as it reveals the standard of living, social status of females, availability of educational facilities and policies of government.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Level of economic development is both a cause and consequence of literacy.**

⭐ **In India, literacy rate denotes the percentage of population above 7 years of age, who is able to READ, WRITE and have the ability to do ARITHMETIC CALCULATIONS with understanding.**

> ⭐ **Note the Indian definition precisely: ⭐ aged 7 and above, able to READ AND WRITE WITH UNDERSTANDING** (Class 12 adds arithmetic ability). ⚠️ **Merely being able to sign one's name does not count.**

> ⭐ **The "cause and consequence" phrasing is worth carrying:** ⭐ **literacy causes development AND development causes literacy** — a reinforcing loop. ⭐ **This is why interventions in female education have such disproportionate effects: they enter the loop at its most leveraged point** (Cl.9 Ch.6 — female education is the strongest predictor of falling fertility).

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ Occupational Structure

⭐ **The working population (i.e. women and men of the age group 15 to 59) take part in various occupations ranging from agriculture, forestry, fishing, manufacturing construction, commercial transport, services, communication and other unclassified services.**

### ⭐ The classification

| Sector | ⭐ Activities |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **PRIMARY** | ⭐ **Agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining, quarrying** — extraction from nature |
| ⭐ **SECONDARY** | ⭐ **Manufacturing industry, building and construction work** |
| ⭐ **TERTIARY** | ⭐ **Transport, communications, commerce, administration and other services** |
| ⭐ **QUATERNARY** | ⭐ **Intellectual activities — education, research, information, knowledge-based work** |

⭐ **The PROPORTION of the working population engaged in these four sectors is a good indicator of the levels of economic development of a nation.**

⚠️ ⭐ **This is because only developed economies are able to generate more employment in secondary, tertiary and quaternary sectors.**

> ⭐⭐ **The development sequence, and India's anomaly:**
> ⭐ **The normal path is PRIMARY → SECONDARY → TERTIARY.** ⭐ **Every industrialised economy moved labour out of agriculture into manufacturing, and then into services.**
> ⭐ **INDIA JUMPED SUBSTANTIALLY FROM PRIMARY TO TERTIARY**, without a large manufacturing phase.
> ⭐ **Why this matters: services — especially IT — generate high output per worker but employ relatively few people.** ⭐ **Manufacturing absorbs large numbers of moderately skilled workers, which is exactly what a country leaving agriculture needs.**
> 🎯 ⭐ **This is the structural reason behind "Make in India", PLI schemes and the persistent concern about jobless growth.** **A high-value point for both GS-III Economy and any answer on the demographic dividend.**

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Sex ratio** | Ratio of females to males (India) or males to females (international) |
| ⭐ **Age structure** | Distribution of population across age groups |
| ⭐ **Age-sex pyramid** | Graphic of population by age group and sex |
| ⭐ **Expanding / constant / declining pyramid** | Triangular / bell-shaped / narrow-based |
| ⭐ **Ageing population** | Greater proportion above 60 years |
| ⭐ **Literacy rate (India)** | % aged 7+ able to read, write and calculate with understanding |
| ⭐ **Occupational structure** | Distribution of workers across sectors |
| ⭐ **Primary / secondary / tertiary / quaternary** | Extraction / manufacturing / services / knowledge work |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐⭐ **Sex ratio: INDIA counts females per 1,000 males; internationally it is often males per 1,000 females.** Check which convention a question uses.
2. ⭐ **World ratio ~102 males per 100 females.** ⭐ **Latvia lowest (85), Qatar highest (311).** ⭐ **Favourable to females in 139 countries, unfavourable in 72.**
3. ⭐ **Both extremes are about MIGRATION and MORTALITY, not births** — Qatar's male labour influx, Latvia's higher male mortality.
4. ⭐ **Europe's female surplus reflects better status of women and historical male out-migration.** ⭐ **Asia's deficit reflects son preference.**
5. ⭐⭐ **Three pyramid shapes: EXPANDING/triangular (Nigeria, Bangladesh, Mexico) · CONSTANT/bell (Australia) · DECLINING/narrow base (Japan).** ⭐ **Read the BASE first.**
6. ⭐ **Rural–urban sex ratio reverses between the West and South Asia** — mechanised male farming vs male labour migration to cities.
7. ⭐ **Indian literacy: aged 7+, able to read, write and do arithmetic WITH UNDERSTANDING.**
8. ⭐ **Literacy is both a CAUSE and a CONSEQUENCE of development** — a reinforcing loop, which is why female education is so leveraged.
9. ⭐⭐ **Four sectors: primary, secondary, tertiary, QUATERNARY (intellectual/knowledge).** ⭐ **The proportion in each indicates the level of development.**
10. ⭐⭐ **India jumped from primary to tertiary without a large manufacturing phase** — which is why services-led growth has not absorbed the workforce leaving agriculture.
