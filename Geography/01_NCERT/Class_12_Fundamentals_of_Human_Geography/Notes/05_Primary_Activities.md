## 1️⃣ What Primary Activities Are

**PRIMARY ACTIVITIES are directly dependent on environment as these refer to utilisation of earth's resources such as land, water, vegetation, building materials and minerals.**

**It, thus includes, HUNTING AND GATHERING, PASTORAL ACTIVITIES, FISHING, FORESTRY, AGRICULTURE, and MINING AND QUARRYING.**

⚠️ **People engaged in primary activities are called RED-COLLAR WORKERS due to the outdoor nature of their work.**

> **The "collar" colours are examinable across Chapters 5–7:** **RED-COLLAR = primary (outdoor)** · **BLUE-COLLAR = secondary (manufacturing)** · **WHITE-COLLAR = tertiary (office/services)** · **GOLD-COLLAR = quaternary (high-skill professionals)**. **Learn them as a set.**

## 2️⃣ Hunting and Gathering

**The earliest human beings depended on their immediate environment for their sustenance.** **They subsisted on animals which they hunted and the edible plants which they gathered from forests in the vicinity.**

**In modern times some primitive societies depend on wild animals and they are found in LOW LATITUDE ZONES.**

| Region | Groups |
|---|---|
| **Northern Canada, northern Eurasia and southern Chile** | Primitive gathering |
| **Amazon Basin, tropical Africa, Northern fringe of Australia and interior parts of Southeast Asia** | Gathering in tropical regions |

**GATHERING is practised in regions with harsh climatic conditions.** **It often involves primitive societies, who extract both plants and animals to satisfy their needs for food, shelter and clothing.**

**This type of activity requires a small amount of capital investment and operates at very LOW LEVEL OF TECHNOLOGY.** **The yield per person is very low and little or no surplus is produced.**

**Gathering has little chance of becoming important at the global level.** **Products of such societies are not their competitors in world market.**

**In modern times some of the plant products gathered by these people are used as MEDICINES: quinine, bark of cinchona; bark of the chinchona tree used for making quinine; leaves, bark of plants and roots are used in medicines.** **Gathering is also done for GUMS, RESINS, BARKS, NUTS, FRUITS, LEAVES and other products** — e.g. **chicle from chico tree** for chewing gum, **balata** for golf balls, and **tannin** for leather.

⚠️ **Hunting has been BANNED in India and many other countries.**

## 3️⃣ Pastoralism (Herding)

**At some stage in history, with the realisation that hunting is an unsustainable activity, human beings might have thought of domestication of animals.**

**Depending on the geographical factors, and technological development, animal rearing today is practised either at SUBSISTENCE or at COMMERCIAL level.**

### Nomadic herding

**NOMADIC HERDING or PASTORAL NOMADISM is a SUBSISTENCE activity, in which the herders rely on animals for food, clothing, shelter, tools and transport.**

**They move from one place to another along with their livestock, depending on the amount and quality of pastures and water.**

**Each nomadic community occupies a well-identified territory as a matter of tradition.**

**A wide variety of animals is kept in different regions.** **In tropical Africa, CATTLE are the most important livestock, while in Sahara and Asiatic deserts, SHEEP, GOATS and CAMEL are reared.** **In the mountainous areas of Tibet and Andes, YAK and LLAMA and in the Arctic and sub Arctic areas, REINDEER are reared.**

### TRANSHUMANCE — the key term

> **TRANSHUMANCE: the SEASONAL movement of herders and their livestock between mountain pastures in summer and lower valleys in winter.**

**In mountain regions, such as Himalayas, GUJJARS, BAKARWALS, GADDIS and BHOTIYAS migrate from plains to the mountains in summers and to the plains from the high altitude pastures in winters.** **Similarly, in the tundra regions, the nomadic herders move from south to north in summers and from north to south in winters.**

⚠️ **Movement in search of pastures is undertaken either seasonally or otherwise.** **The pastoral nomadism is associated with three important regions: the core region extends from the Atlantic shores of North Africa eastwards across the Arabian peninsula into Mongolia and Central China; the second region extends over the tundra region of Eurasia; the third region is found in the southern hemisphere — southwest Africa and on the island of Madagascar.**

> **Distinguish NOMADIC HERDING from TRANSHUMANCE:** **Nomadic herding is movement in search of pasture generally; TRANSHUMANCE is specifically the SEASONAL, REGULAR, ALTITUDINAL movement between summer highland and winter lowland pastures.** **Transhumance is patterned and predictable; nomadism need not be.**

### Commercial livestock rearing

**Commercial livestock rearing is more organised and capital intensive.** **It is essentially associated with WESTERN CULTURES and is practised on PERMANENT RANCHES.**

**These ranches cover large areas and are divided into a number of parcels, which are fenced to regulate the grazing.** **When the grass of one parcel is grazed, animals are moved to another parcel.**

**The main products are MEAT, WOOL, HIDES and SKIN, which are processed and packed scientifically and exported to different world markets.**

**Rearing of animals in ranching is organised on a scientific basis.** **The main emphasis is on breeding, genetic improvement, disease control and health care of the animals.**

**NEW ZEALAND, AUSTRALIA, ARGENTINA, URUGUAY and UNITED STATES OF AMERICA are important countries where commercial livestock rearing is practised.**

## 4️⃣ Agriculture

**Agriculture is practised under multiple combinations of physical and socio-economic conditions, which gives rise to different types of agricultural systems.**

### Subsistence agriculture

> **SUBSISTENCE AGRICULTURE is one in which the farming areas consume all, or nearly so, of the products locally grown.**

**It can be grouped in two categories: PRIMITIVE SUBSISTENCE AGRICULTURE and INTENSIVE SUBSISTENCE AGRICULTURE.**

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **PRIMITIVE SUBSISTENCE AGRICULTURE / SHIFTING CULTIVATION** | **Widely practised by many tribes in the tropics, especially in Africa, in tropical South and Central America, and southeast Asia.** **The vegetation is usually cleared by FIRE, and the ashes add to the fertility of the soil.** **Thus, it is also called SLASH AND BURN AGRICULTURE.** **The cultivated patches are very small and cultivation is done with very primitive tools.** **After sometime (3 to 5 years) the soil loses its fertility and the farmer shifts to another parts and clears other patch of the forest for cultivation.** **The former field is left fallow for many years to regain its fertility naturally.** **This process of preparing land is called SLASH AND BURN.** **The most important crops are yams, potatoes, cassava, maize, millets, upland rice, bananas, sugarcane, vegetables** |
| **INTENSIVE SUBSISTENCE AGRICULTURE** | **This type of agriculture is largely found in DENSELY POPULATED regions of monsoon Asia.** **Basically, there are two types: (i) **Intensive subsistence agriculture DOMINATED BY WET PADDY CULTIVATION** — the land holdings are very small due to the high density of population. Farmers work with the help of family labour leading to intensive use of land. Use of machinery is limited and most of the agricultural operations are done by manual labour. Farm yard manure is used to maintain soil fertility. In this type of agriculture, the yield per unit area is high but per labour productivity is low. (ii) **Intensive subsistence agriculture DOMINATED BY CROPS OTHER THAN PADDY** — due to the difference in relief, climate, soil and some other geographical factors, it is not practical to grow paddy in many parts of monsoon Asia. Wheat, soyabean, barley and sorghum are grown in northern China, Manchuria, North Korea and North Japan. In India wheat is grown in western parts of the Indo-Gangetic plains and millets are grown in dry parts of western and southern India |

**Shifting cultivation is known by different names in different regions:** **JHUMING in north-eastern states of India, MILPA in central America and Mexico, LADANG in Indonesia and Malaysia.**

> **The intensive-subsistence contrast worth learning: HIGH YIELD PER UNIT AREA, but LOW YIELD PER PERSON.** **Enormous labour on a tiny plot produces a lot per hectare and very little per worker.** **Commercial grain farming is the exact reverse — low per hectare, very high per person.** **Different things are being maximised: LAND productivity vs LABOUR productivity.**

### Plantation agriculture

**Plantation agriculture was introduced by the EUROPEANS in colonies situated in the tropics.**

**Some of the important characteristic features of this type of farming are:** **LARGE ESTATES or PLANTATIONS, LARGE CAPITAL INVESTMENT, MANAGERIAL AND TECHNICAL SUPPORT, SCIENTIFIC METHODS OF CULTIVATION, SINGLE CROP SPECIALISATION, CHEAP LABOUR, and a GOOD SYSTEM OF TRANSPORTATION which links the estates to the factories and markets for the export of the products.**

| Plantation | Where |
|---|---|
| **Tea** | **The French established cocoa and coffee plantations in west Africa.** **The British set up large tea gardens in India and Sri Lanka** |
| **Rubber** | **Rubber in Malaysia** |
| **Sugarcane and banana** | **Sugarcane and banana in West Indies** |
| **Coffee** | **Coffee in Brazil** *(FAZENDAS)* |

**Many of the plantations have now been TAKEN OVER BY THE GOVERNMENT or the LOCAL PEOPLE.**

### Extensive commercial grain cultivation

**Commercial grain cultivation is practised in the interior parts of semi-arid lands of the mid-latitudes.**

**WHEAT is the principal crop, though other crops like corn, barley, oats and rye are also grown.**

**The size of the farm is very large; therefore entire operations of cultivation from ploughing to harvesting are MECHANISED.**

⚠️ **There is a low yield per acre but high yield per person.**

**This type of agriculture is best developed in EURASIAN STEPPES, the CANADIAN and AMERICAN PRAIRIES, the PAMPAS of Argentina, the VELDS of South Africa, the AUSTRALIAN DOWNS and the CANTERBURY PLAINS of New Zealand.**

### Mixed farming

**This form of agriculture is found in the highly developed parts of the world, e.g. NORTH-WESTERN EUROPE, EASTERN NORTH AMERICA, parts of EURASIA and the temperate latitudes of SOUTHERN CONTINENTS.**

**Mixed farms are moderate in size and usually the crops associated with it are WHEAT, BARLEY, OATS, RYE, MAIZE, FODDER and ROOT CROPS.**

**Fodder crops are an important component of mixed farming.** **CROP ROTATION and INTER CROPPING play an important role in maintaining soil fertility.**

**Equal emphasis is laid on crop cultivation and animal husbandry.** **Animals like cattle, sheep, pigs and poultry provide the main income along with crops.**

**Mixed farming is characterised by HIGH CAPITAL EXPENDITURE on farm machinery and building, extensive use of chemical fertilisers and green manures and also by the skill and expertise of the farmers.**

### Dairy farming

**DAIRY is the most advanced and efficient type of rearing of milch animals.**

**It is highly capital intensive.** **Animal sheds, storage facilities for fodder, feeding and milching machines add to the cost of dairy farming.**

**Special emphasis is laid on cattle breeding, health care and veterinary services.**

**It is highly labour intensive as it involves rigorous care in feeding and milching.** **There is no off season during the year as in the case of crop raising.**

**It is practised mainly near URBAN AND INDUSTRIAL CENTRES which provide neighbourhood market for fresh milk and dairy products.**

**THE MOST IMPORTANT REGIONS: North Western Europe is the leading region followed by Canada, and the second is around the region of Southeast Australia, New Zealand and Tasmania.**

> **Note why dairy locates near cities: MILK IS PERISHABLE.** **This is the classic **market-oriented** location (weight-gaining/perishable-output logic from Cl.8 Ch.5).** **The advent of refrigeration, pasteurisation and other preservatives has increased the duration of storage — which is precisely why dairying can now occur somewhat further from markets than it once could.**

### Mediterranean agriculture

**Mediterranean agriculture is highly SPECIALISED commercial agriculture.**

**It is practised in the countries on either side of the Mediterranean sea in Europe and in north Africa from Tunisia to Atlantic coast, southern California, central Chile, south western parts of South Africa and south and south western parts of Australia.**

**This region is an important supplier of CITRUS FRUITS.**

**VITICULTURE or GRAPE CULTIVATION is a speciality of the Mediterranean region.**

**Best quality wines in the world with distinctive flavours are produced from high quality grapes in various countries of this region.** **The inferior grapes are dried into raisins and currants.**

**This region also produces olives and figs.** **The advantage of Mediterranean agriculture is that more valuable crops such as fruits and vegetables are grown in winters when there is great demand in European and North American markets.**

> **VITICULTURE = grape cultivation.** **A frequently asked term.** **And note the commercial insight NCERT gives: the Mediterranean region's advantage is producing fruit and vegetables in WINTER, when northern markets have no supply of their own.** **The timing, not just the crop, is the advantage.**

### Market gardening and horticulture

**Market gardening and horticulture specialise in the cultivation of HIGH VALUE CROPS such as VEGETABLES, FRUITS and FLOWERS, solely for the URBAN MARKETS.**

**Farms are SMALL and are located where there are good transportation links with the urban centre where high income group of consumers is located.**

**It is both LABOUR AND CAPITAL INTENSIVE and lays emphasis on the use of irrigation, HYVs, fertilisers, insecticides, greenhouses and artificial heating in colder regions.**

⚠️ **This type of agriculture is well developed in densely populated industrial districts of north west Europe, north eastern United States of America and the Mediterranean regions.**

**THE NETHERLANDS specialises in growing flowers and horticultural crops especially TULIPS, which are flown to all major cities of Europe.**

**The regions where farmers specialise in vegetables only, the farming is known as TRUCK FARMING.** **The distance of truck farms from the market is governed by the distance that a truck can cover overnight, hence the name truck farming.**

> **TRUCK FARMING is a lovely bit of etymology and it gets asked:** **the name comes from the distance a truck can travel OVERNIGHT to reach the market with fresh produce.** **The technology of transport literally defined the size of the farming region.**

### Factory farming

**In addition to market gardening, a modern development in the industrial regions of western Europe and North America is FACTORY FARMING.**

**Livestock rearing (mainly cattle and poultry) is done in stalls and pens, fed on manufactured feedstuff and carefully supervised against diseases.**

**This requires HEAVY CAPITAL INVESTMENT in terms of building, machinery for various operations, veterinary services and heating and lighting.**

**One of the important features of poultry farming is BREED SELECTION AND HEALTHCARE PROGRAMMES.**

### Co-operative and collective farming

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **CO-OPERATIVE FARMING** | **A group of farmers form a co-operative society by pooling in their resources VOLUNTARILY for more efficient and profitable farming.** **Individual farms remain intact and farming is a matter of INDIVIDUAL INITIATIVE.** **The society helps by procuring important inputs of farming, selling the produce at the most favourable terms and helps in processing quality products at cheaper rates.** **Co-operative movement originated over a century ago and has been successful in many western European countries like DENMARK, NETHERLANDS, BELGIUM, SWEDEN, ITALY.** **In DENMARK, the movement has been so successful that practically every farmer is a member of a co-operative** |
| **COLLECTIVE FARMING** | **Based on SOCIAL OWNERSHIP of the means of production and COLLECTIVE LABOUR.** **Collective farming or the model of KOLKHOZ was introduced in erstwhile SOVIET UNION to improve upon the inefficiency of the previous methods of agriculture and to boost agricultural production for self-sufficiency.** **The farmers used to pool in all their resources like land, livestock and labour.** **However, they were allowed to retain very small plots to grow crops in order to meet their daily requirements** |

> **CO-OPERATIVE vs COLLECTIVE is a crucial distinction:** **CO-OPERATIVE = VOLUNTARY pooling; individual farms REMAIN INTACT; individual initiative retained.** **COLLECTIVE (KOLKHOZ) = SOCIAL OWNERSHIP of the means of production; land is pooled, not retained.**
>
> **Denmark is the co-operative exemplar** (practically every farmer a member); **the Soviet KOLKHOZ is the collective one.** **India's AMUL follows the CO-OPERATIVE model** (Cl.8 Ch.5).

## 5️⃣ Mining

**The discovery of minerals in the history of human development is reflected in the different stages of civilisation — the Stone Age, the Bronze Age and the Iron Age.**

**The Industrial Revolution which took place in Europe in the 18th and 19th centuries was based on coal and iron.**

### Factors affecting mining activity

**The profitability of mining operations thus depends on two main factors:**

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **(i) PHYSICAL FACTORS** | **Include the size, grade and the mode of occurrence of the deposits** |
| **(ii) ECONOMIC FACTORS** | **Such as the demand for the mineral, technology available and used, capital to develop infrastructure and the labour and transport costs** |

### Methods of mining

| Method | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SURFACE MINING / OPEN-CAST MINING** | **The CHEAPEST way of mining minerals that occur close to the surface.** **Overhead costs such as safety precautions and equipment is relatively low in this method.** **The output is both large and rapid** |
| **UNDERGROUND / SHAFT MINING** | **When the ore lies DEEP below the surface, underground mining method (shaft method) has to be used.** **In this method, VERTICAL SHAFTS have to be sunk, from where GALLERIES radiate to reach the mineral deposits.** **Minerals are extracted and transported to the surface through these passages.** **It requires specially designed lifts, drills, haulage vehicles, ventilation system for safety and efficient movement of people and material.** **This method is RISKY.** **Poisonous gases, fires, floods and caving in lead to fatal accidents** |

**Developed economies are retreating from mining, processing and refining stages of production due to HIGH LABOUR COSTS, while developing countries with large labour force and striving for higher standard of living are becoming more important.**

⚠️ **Several countries of Africa and few of south America and Asia have over-dependence on mining.**

> 🌱 **The over-dependence point is worth developing:** **countries whose economies rest on mineral exports are vulnerable to price volatility, and mineral wealth often correlates with weak institutions and conflict** — the **"resource curse."** **This connects directly to Cl.10 Ch.1's observation that endowment alone does not produce development.**

**Trap 1:** *"Intensive subsistence agriculture has high yield per person."* **High per unit AREA, LOW per person.**
**Trap 2:** *"Collective farming allows individual farms to remain intact."* **That is CO-OPERATIVE farming.** Collective (kolkhoz) is based on social ownership.

**Trap 4:** *"Viticulture means olive cultivation."* **Grape cultivation.**
**Trap 5:** *"Shaft mining is the cheapest method."* **Open-cast is cheapest.** Shaft mining is deep, expensive and risky.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **Primary activities extract directly from nature. Workers are RED-COLLAR.** (Blue = secondary, White = tertiary, Gold = quaternary.)
2. **Gathering yields quinine (cinchona), chicle (chewing gum), balata (golf balls), tannin (leather).** Hunting is banned in India.
3. **TRANSHUMANCE = seasonal altitudinal movement.** **Gujjars, Bakarwals, Gaddis, Bhotiyas in the Himalayas.**
4. **Herd animals by region: cattle (tropical Africa) · sheep, goat, camel (Sahara, Asian deserts) · yak and llama (Tibet, Andes) · reindeer (Arctic).**
5. **Commercial livestock rearing on ranches: NZ, Australia, Argentina, Uruguay, USA.**
6. **Shifting cultivation: JHUMING (NE India) · MILPA (Central America, Mexico) · LADANG (Indonesia, Malaysia).** Fallow 3–5 years.
7. **Intensive subsistence: HIGH yield per unit AREA, LOW per PERSON.** **Extensive commercial grain: LOW per acre, HIGH per person.** Different things maximised.
8. **Plantations: European colonial origin; large estates, single crop, cheap labour, good transport.** Many now government or locally owned.
9. **Commercial grain regions: Eurasian steppes, Prairies, Pampas, Velds, Downs, Canterbury Plains.**
10. **Dairy locates near cities because MILK IS PERISHABLE.** Refrigeration extended the range.
11. **VITICULTURE = grape cultivation.** **The Mediterranean's real advantage is supplying WINTER fruit to northern markets.**
12. **TRUCK FARMING is named for the distance a truck can cover OVERNIGHT.** Netherlands specialises in tulips.
13. **CO-OPERATIVE = voluntary pooling, farms intact (Denmark). COLLECTIVE = social ownership, KOLKHOZ (USSR).**
14. **Mining: open-cast (cheapest, near surface) vs shaft (deep, galleries, risky).** 🌱 **Over-dependence on mining is the "resource curse."**
