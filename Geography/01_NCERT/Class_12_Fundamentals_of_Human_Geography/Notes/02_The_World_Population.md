# Chapter 2 — The World Population: Distribution, Density and Growth

> **The one-line point:** **World population is distributed extremely unevenly — about 90% of people live on 10% of the land** — and the explosive growth of the last two centuries happened because **death rates collapsed while birth rates lagged behind.** **The Demographic Transition Model explains the whole story in five stages.**

## 1️⃣ Patterns of Population Distribution

**Population distribution refers to the way people are spaced over the earth's surface.**

**Broadly, 90 PER CENT OF THE WORLD POPULATION LIVES IN ABOUT 10 PER CENT OF ITS LAND AREA.**

**The 10 most populous countries of the world contribute about 60 per cent of the world's population.** **Of these 10 countries, 6 are located in Asia.**

### Factors influencing distribution

| Category | Factors |
|---|---|
| **GEOGRAPHICAL** | **AVAILABILITY OF WATER** — the most important; people prefer to live in areas where fresh water is easily available. **LANDFORMS** — people prefer living on flat plains and gentle slopes. **CLIMATE** — an extreme climate discourages settlement. **SOILS** — fertile soils support intensive agriculture |
| **ECONOMIC** | **MINERALS** — areas with mineral deposits attract industries and settlement. **URBANISATION** — cities offer better employment, education and services. **INDUSTRIALISATION** — industrial belts provide job opportunities |
| **SOCIAL AND CULTURAL** | **Places with religious or cultural significance attract people.** **Conversely, people move away from places with social and political unrest** |

> **Water is the first requirement — that is why the river valleys of the world are among the most densely populated areas** (Cl.6 Ch.6's causal chain, at world scale). **The Ganga, Nile, Yangtze and Rhine valleys are all population concentrations.**

## 2️⃣ Density of Population

> **DENSITY OF POPULATION = Population ÷ Area**, expressed as **persons per unit area** (usually per sq km).

**Density of population is the ratio between the number of people to the size of land.** **It is usually measured in persons per sq km.**

⚠️ **Note the difference between DISTRIBUTION (the pattern of spread) and DENSITY (the ratio).** **They are different questions.**

**ARITHMETIC DENSITY divides total population by total area.** **PHYSIOLOGICAL DENSITY divides population by the AREA OF CULTIVABLE LAND — a more meaningful measure for agrarian economies.** **AGRICULTURAL DENSITY divides the agricultural population by the cultivable area.**

> **The physiological density point is worth carrying:** **Egypt's arithmetic density looks moderate, but almost its entire population lives on the Nile strip** — so its physiological density is enormous. **Arithmetic density can conceal extreme concentration.**

## 3️⃣ Population Growth

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **GROWTH OF POPULATION** | **The change in the number of inhabitants of a territory during a specific period of time.** **This change may be positive as well as negative.** **It can be expressed either in terms of absolute numbers or in terms of percentage** |
| **GROWTH RATE OF POPULATION** | **When the change in population is expressed in percentage** |
| **NATURAL GROWTH OF POPULATION** | **The population increased by difference between births and deaths in a particular region between two points of time.** **NATURAL GROWTH = BIRTHS − DEATHS** |
| **ACTUAL GROWTH OF POPULATION** | **BIRTHS − DEATHS + IN MIGRATION − OUT MIGRATION** |
| **POSITIVE GROWTH OF POPULATION** | **Happens when the birth rate is more than the death rate between two points of time or when people from other countries migrate permanently to a region** |
| **NEGATIVE GROWTH OF POPULATION** | **If the population decreases between two points of time it is known as negative growth of population.** **It occurs when the birth rate falls below the death rate or people migrate to other countries** |

### Components of population change

| Component | Detail |
|---|---|
| **CRUDE BIRTH RATE (CBR)** | **CBR = (Bi ÷ P) × 1000**, where **Bi = live births during the year, P = mid-year population of the area.** **Expressed as number of live births per thousand persons** |
| **CRUDE DEATH RATE (CDR)** | **CDR = (D ÷ P) × 1000**, where **D = number of deaths, P = estimated mid-year population** |
| **MIGRATION** | **The third component.** **When people move from one place to another, the place they move FROM is called the PLACE OF ORIGIN and the place they move TO is called the PLACE OF DESTINATION** |

⚠️ **Largely, mortality and fertility are responsible for the population change.**

### Migration

**Migration may be interpreted as a response to the ECONOMIC, SOCIAL, CULTURAL and POLITICAL factors.**

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| **IMMIGRATION** | **Migrants who move INTO a new place are called immigrants** |
| **EMIGRATION** | **Migrants who move OUT of a place are called emigrants** |
| **Internal / international** | Within a country / between countries |
| **Permanent / temporary / seasonal** | By duration |

### Push and pull factors

| **PUSH FACTORS** | **PULL FACTORS** |
|---|---|
| **Unemployment, poor living conditions, political turmoil, unpleasant climate, natural disasters, epidemics and socio-economic backwardness** | **Better job opportunities and living conditions, peace and stability, security of life and property and pleasant climate** |

> **The push/pull framework is the standard analytical tool for any migration question, and it generalises well:** **push factors make the origin unattractive; pull factors make the destination attractive.** **Most real migration is driven by BOTH simultaneously** — and the poorest often cannot migrate at all, because migration itself requires resources. **That last point is a good nuance for a GS-I society answer.**

## 4️⃣ Trends in Population Growth

**The population on the earth is more than SEVEN BILLION.** **It has grown to this size over centuries.**

**In the early periods population of the world grew very slowly.** **It is only during the last few hundred years that human population has increased at an alarming rate.**

### The historical pattern

| Period | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Early period** | **Population grew very slowly — high birth rates matched by high death rates** |
| **After 1750 (Industrial Revolution)** | **The world population exploded.** **It took more than a million years for the human population to attain the one billion mark. But it took only 12 years for it to rise from 5 billion to 6 billion** |
| **Doubling time** | **The time taken by any population to double itself at its current annual growth rate.** **Note the alarming rate: developing countries take far less time to double** |

**The main reasons for the growth:**
- **Expansion of world trade in the sixteenth and seventeenth century**
- **The steam engine replaced human and animal energy and also provided mechanised energy of water and wind — this increased agricultural and industrial production**
- **Better transport made possible the migration of people from crowded areas**
- **Innovations in the field of medicine — a large number of epidemics were controlled**

> **The insight that most candidates get wrong, and it is worth stating plainly:** **the population explosion was caused by DEATH RATES FALLING, not by birth rates rising.**
>
> **Better food supply, clean water and sanitation, vaccines and antibiotics meant people stopped dying young.** **Birth rates, being culturally embedded, took generations to adjust downward — and the gap between the two is the explosion.**
>
> **"People started having too many children" is simply false as an explanation.** **Getting this right transforms a population answer.**

**Spatial pattern of population change:** **The population growth is not uniform.** **DEVELOPED countries are taking more time to double their population as compared to DEVELOPING countries.** **Most of the population growth is occurring in the developing world, where population is exploding.**

### Impact of population change

**A small increase in population is desirable in a growing economy.** **However, population growth beyond a certain level leads to problems.**

**DEPLETION OF RESOURCES is the most serious consequence of population growth.** **Population decline is also a matter of concern.** **It indicates that resources that had supported a population earlier are now insufficient to maintain the population.**

⚠️ **The extreme case of population decline is the spread of AIDS/HIV and other epidemics.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐ The Demographic Transition Theory

**DEMOGRAPHIC TRANSITION THEORY can be used to describe and predict the future population of any area.** **The theory tells us that population of any region changes from HIGH BIRTHS AND HIGH DEATHS to LOW BIRTHS AND LOW DEATHS as society progresses from rural agrarian and illiterate to urban industrial and literate society.**

**These changes occur in stages which are collectively known as the DEMOGRAPHIC CYCLE.**

```
   THE THREE-STAGE MODEL

   ┌──────────────┬──────────────────┬─────────────────┐
   │  STAGE 1   │   STAGE 2     │   STAGE 3     │
   ├──────────────┼──────────────────┼─────────────────┤
   │ HIGH birth   │ DEATH RATE     │ LOW birth       │
   │ HIGH death   │   FALLS FAST     │ LOW death       │
   │              │ Birth rate       │                 │
   │ Population   │ still HIGH       │ Population      │
   │ STABLE/slow  │        ↓         │ STABLE / may    │
   │              │ POPULATION     │ DECLINE         │
   │              │   EXPLOSION      │                 │
   ├──────────────┼──────────────────┼─────────────────┤
   │ Rural,       │ Improving        │ Urban,          │
   │ agrarian,    │ health, food,    │ industrial,     │
   │ illiterate   │ sanitation       │ literate        │
   └──────────────┴──────────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

### The three stages in detail

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **FIRST STAGE** | **HIGH FERTILITY AND HIGH MORTALITY** because people reproduce more to compensate for the deaths due to epidemics and variable food supply. **The population growth is SLOW and most of the people are engaged in agriculture where large families are an asset.** **Life expectancy is low, people are mostly illiterate and have low levels of technology.** **Two hundred years ago all the countries of the world were in this stage** |
| **SECOND STAGE** | **FERTILITY REMAINS HIGH IN THE BEGINNING BUT IT DECLINES WITH TIME.** **This is accompanied by REDUCED MORTALITY RATE.** **Improvements in sanitation and health conditions lead to decline in mortality.** **Because of this gap the NET ADDITION TO POPULATION IS HIGH** |
| **LAST STAGE** | **BOTH FERTILITY AND MORTALITY DECLINE CONSIDERABLY.** **The population is either STABLE or GROWS SLOWLY.** **The population becomes URBANISED, LITERATE and has HIGH TECHNICAL KNOW-HOW and deliberately controls the family size** |

⚠️ **This shows that human beings are extremely flexible and are able to adjust their fertility.**

> **The crucial mechanism of Stage 2 — worth stating precisely because it explains the whole explosion:**
>
> **Death rates fall QUICKLY** (medicine, sanitation and food supply can improve within a generation). **Birth rates fall SLOWLY** (they are embedded in culture, family structure, women's education and old-age security — all of which take generations to change).
>
> **The gap between the two falling curves IS the population explosion.** **Every country passes through it; the only question is how long Stage 2 lasts.**
>
> **India is now in late Stage 2 / early Stage 3** — TFR has fallen to about 2.0, below replacement, and growth continues only through **population momentum** (Cl.9 Ch.6).

### Population control measures

**FAMILY PLANNING is the spacing or preventing the birth of children.** **Access to family planning services is a significant factor in limiting population growth and improving women's health.**

**PROPAGANDA, free availability of contraceptives and tax disincentives for large families are some of the measures which can help population control.**

> **THOMAS MALTHUS is the reference worth knowing.** **In his theory (1798), Malthus stated that the number of people would increase FASTER than the food supply.** **Any further increase would result in a POPULATION CRASH caused by famine, disease and war.** **He suggested that human beings limit their population.**
>
> ⚠️ **The historical verdict:** **Malthus was wrong about the outcome but right about the arithmetic he described.** **He did not anticipate that agricultural TECHNOLOGY — the Green Revolution above all — would raise food production faster than population.** **This is the classic case for the "technology loosens environmental shackles" argument from Ch.1.**
>
> 🌱 **But the neo-Malthusian counter is also worth stating: the Green Revolution bought time at an ecological cost** (groundwater, soil, biodiversity — Cl.10 Ch.4). **So the question is not whether Malthus was refuted, but whether he was merely postponed.** **That framing is genuinely strong for an essay.**

**Trap 1:** *"The population explosion was caused by rising birth rates."* **By FALLING DEATH RATES.** Birth rates lagged behind.
**Trap 2:** *"Natural growth includes migration."* **It does not** — that is **actual** growth.
**Trap 3:** *"Immigration means moving out of a country."* **Moving IN.** Emigration is moving out.
**Trap 4:** *"Malthus was proved entirely right."* **He did not anticipate agricultural technology.** Whether he was refuted or merely postponed is a live question.
**Trap 5:** *"Distribution and density mean the same thing."* **Distribution = pattern of spread; density = ratio of people to area.**

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Population distribution** | The pattern of spread of people over space |
| **Population density** | Persons per unit area |
| **Physiological density** | Population per unit of **cultivable** land |

| **Actual growth** | Births − deaths + in-migration − out-migration |

| **Demographic cycle** | The sequence of demographic transition stages |
| **Malthusian theory** | Population grows faster than food supply, leading to a crash |

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. **~90% of the world's people live on ~10% of the land.** The 10 most populous countries hold ~60%; **6 are in Asia.**
2. **Distribution factors: geographical (WATER first, landforms, climate, soils) · economic (minerals, urbanisation, industrialisation) · social and cultural.**
3. **Density = population ÷ area.** **Physiological density (per cultivable land) is more meaningful for agrarian economies.**
4. **Natural growth = births − deaths. ACTUAL growth = births − deaths + in-migration − out-migration.**
5. **CBR = (Bi ÷ P) × 1000; CDR = (D ÷ P) × 1000.**
6. **Push factors drive people out; pull factors draw them in.** **The poorest often cannot migrate at all** — migration requires resources.
7. **It took over a million years to reach 1 billion, and only 12 years to go from 5 to 6 billion.**
8. **Demographic Transition: Stage 1 high births/high deaths (slow growth) → Stage 2 DEATHS FALL FAST while births lag (EXPLOSION) → Stage 3 both low (stable or declining).**
9. **The gap between the two falling curves IS the explosion.** **Death rates fall in a generation; birth rates take generations, because they are embedded in culture, women's education and old-age security.**
10. **India is now in late Stage 2 / early Stage 3** — TFR ~2.0, below replacement, growing on momentum.
11. **MALTHUS (1798): population outruns food → crash.** **Agricultural technology refuted him — or merely postponed him, at ecological cost.**
