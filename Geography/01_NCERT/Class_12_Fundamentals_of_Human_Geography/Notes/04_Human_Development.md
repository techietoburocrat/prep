# Chapter 4 — Human Development

> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **Growth is quantitative and value-neutral; development is qualitative and value-positive.** ⭐ **A country can grow richer while its people live worse — which is why Mahbub-ul-Haq and Amartya Sen built a measure of development around what people are actually able to DO and BE, not around what their economy produces.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ Growth vs Development

| Term | ⭐ Definition |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **GROWTH** | ⭐ **QUANTITATIVE and VALUE NEUTRAL.** ⭐ **It may have a POSITIVE OR A NEGATIVE SIGN.** ⭐ **It means that the change may be either positive (showing increase) or negative (indicating decrease) |
| ⭐ **DEVELOPMENT** | ⭐ **QUALITATIVE and VALUE POSITIVE.** ⭐ **Development means a QUALITATIVE CHANGE which is ALWAYS VALUE POSITIVE.** ⭐ **This means that development cannot take place unless there is an increment or addition to the existing conditions.** ⭐ **Development occurs when positive growth takes place** |

⚠️ ⭐ **Yet, positive growth does not always lead to development.** ⭐ **Development occurs when there is a positive change in quality.**

> ⭐⭐ **The distinction, stated so you can deploy it:** ⭐ **GROWTH CAN BE NEGATIVE; DEVELOPMENT CANNOT.** ⭐ **Growth is a number going up or down. Development is an improvement in quality.**
>
> ⭐ **A city's population growing is growth. A city's people living longer and better is development.** ⭐ **An economy expanding while inequality widens and rivers die is growth WITHOUT development.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **This single distinction is worth using in almost any essay on economic policy.**

### ⭐ The history of the idea

⭐ **The concept of DEVELOPMENT is dynamic and it originated in the second half of the twentieth century.**

⭐ **The idea of development gained momentum in the post Second World War period.** ⭐ **It was in the 1970s that scholars started questioning the whole idea of development.**

⭐ **The GROWTH-BASED VIEW dominated first: development was equated with ECONOMIC GROWTH, measured by GDP and per capita income.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The problem: many countries showed high growth but their people continued to live in poverty, poor health and illiteracy.** ⭐ **Growth was not reaching people.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Concept of Human Development

> ⭐ **HUMAN DEVELOPMENT is a process where in the PEOPLE'S CHOICES ARE ENLARGED and their WELL-BEING is enhanced.**

⭐ **The idea of human development was introduced by DR MAHBUB-UL-HAQ.** ⭐ **He has described human development as development that ENLARGES PEOPLE'S CHOICES AND IMPROVES THEIR LIVES.**

⭐ **People are central to all development under this concept.** ⭐ **These choices are not fixed but keep on changing.**

⭐ **The basic goal of development is to CREATE CONDITIONS WHERE PEOPLE CAN LIVE MEANINGFUL LIVES.** ⭐ **A meaningful life is not just a long one. It must be a life with some purpose.** ⭐ **This means that people must be HEALTHY, be able to DEVELOP THEIR TALENTS, PARTICIPATE IN SOCIETY and be FREE TO ACHIEVE THEIR GOALS.**

⭐ **Dr Mahbub-ul-Haq and PROF. AMARTYA SEN worked together.** ⭐ **Prof. Sen saw an increase in FREEDOM (or decrease in unfreedom) as the main objective of development.**

> ⭐⭐ **The two founders, and what each contributed — this pairing is examinable:**
> ⭐ **MAHBUB-UL-HAQ (Pakistani economist)** created the **Human Development Index** and the **Human Development Report** (1990). ⭐ **His formulation: development ENLARGES PEOPLE'S CHOICES.**
> ⭐ **AMARTYA SEN (Indian economist, Nobel 1998)** provided the philosophical foundation — the **CAPABILITY APPROACH**. ⭐ **His formulation: development is the EXPANSION OF FREEDOM, or the removal of "unfreedoms."**
>
> ⭐ **Sen's insight is that what matters is not what people HAVE but what they are ABLE TO DO AND BE.** ⭐ **Two people with identical incomes are not equally developed if one is disabled, illiterate or excluded — because the same income buys them different capabilities.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **This is why development must be measured in outcomes (life expectancy, education) rather than resources (income).** **And it works powerfully in an Ethics answer on justice or in an essay on inequality.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Four Pillars of Human Development

⭐ **The idea of human development is supported by the concepts of EQUITY, SUSTAINABILITY, PRODUCTIVITY and EMPOWERMENT.**

| Pillar | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **EQUITY** | ⭐ **Refers to making EQUAL ACCESS to opportunities available to everybody.** ⭐ **The opportunities available to people must be equal irrespective of their gender, race, income and in the case of India, caste.** ⭐ **Yet this is very often not the case and happens in almost every society** |
| ⭐ **SUSTAINABILITY** | ⭐ **Means CONTINUITY IN THE AVAILABILITY OF OPPORTUNITIES.** ⭐ **To have sustainable human development, each generation must have the same opportunities.** ⭐ **All environmental, financial and human resources must be used keeping in mind the future.** ⭐ **Misuse of any of these resources will lead to fewer opportunities for future generations** |
| ⭐ **PRODUCTIVITY** | ⭐ **Means HUMAN LABOUR PRODUCTIVITY or productivity in terms of human work.** ⭐ **Such productivity must be constantly enriched by building capabilities in people.** ⭐ **Ultimately, it is people who are the real wealth of nations.** ⭐ **Therefore, efforts to increase their knowledge, or provide better health facilities ultimately leads to better work efficiency** |
| ⭐ **EMPOWERMENT** | ⭐ **Means to have the POWER TO MAKE CHOICES.** ⭐ **Such power comes from increasing freedom and capability.** ⭐ **Good governance and people-oriented policies are required to empower people.** ⭐ **The empowerment of socially and economically disadvantaged groups is of special importance** |

> ⭐ **Memory hook: E-S-P-E — Equity, Sustainability, Productivity, Empowerment.** ⭐ **Note that EQUITY is about access NOW; SUSTAINABILITY is about access ACROSS GENERATIONS.** ⭐ **They are the same principle applied to space and to time — intragenerational and intergenerational equity.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ Approaches to Human Development

| Approach | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **INCOME APPROACH** | ⭐ **This is one of the OLDEST approaches to human development.** ⭐ **Human development is seen as being linked to income.** ⭐ **The idea is that the level of income reflects the level of freedom an individual enjoys.** ⭐ **Higher the level of income, the higher is the level of human development** |
| ⭐ **WELFARE APPROACH** | ⭐ **This approach looks at HUMAN BEINGS AS BENEFICIARIES OR TARGETS of all development activities.** ⭐ **The approach argues for higher government expenditure on education, health, social secondary and amenities.** ⭐ **People are not participants in development but only PASSIVE RECIPIENTS.** ⭐ **The government is responsible for increasing levels of human development by maximising expenditure on welfare** |
| ⭐ **BASIC NEEDS APPROACH** | ⭐ **This approach was initially proposed by the INTERNATIONAL LABOUR ORGANISATION (ILO).** ⭐ **SIX BASIC NEEDS i.e.: health, education, food, water supply, sanitation, and housing were identified.** ⭐ **The question of human choices is IGNORED and the emphasis is on the provision of basic needs of defined sections** |
| ⭐ **CAPABILITY APPROACH** | ⭐ **This approach is associated with PROF. AMARTYA SEN.** ⭐ **Building human capabilities in the areas of HEALTH, EDUCATION and ACCESS TO RESOURCES is the key to increasing human development** |

> ⭐⭐ **The four approaches form a progression worth understanding:**
> ⭐ **INCOME** — money is the measure. ⭐ **WELFARE** — people are passive recipients of state spending. ⭐ **BASIC NEEDS** — provide a defined list, but ignore choice. ⭐ **CAPABILITY** — build what people are ABLE to do, and let them choose.
>
> ⭐ **Notice the direction: each step gives people more AGENCY.** ⭐ **The capability approach is the only one that treats people as agents rather than as targets or beneficiaries.** ⭐ **That is precisely why it won out.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The ILO's SIX basic needs are examinable: health, education, food, water supply, sanitation, housing.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Measuring Human Development

⭐ **A composite index called the HUMAN DEVELOPMENT INDEX (HDI) was created and the first HUMAN DEVELOPMENT REPORT was published in 1990** by the **United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)**.

### ⭐⭐ The three dimensions

| Dimension | ⭐ Indicator |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. HEALTH — a long and healthy life** | ⭐ **LIFE EXPECTANCY AT BIRTH** |
| ⭐ **2. EDUCATION — access to knowledge** | ⭐ **ADULT LITERACY RATE and GROSS ENROLMENT RATIO** *(now: mean years of schooling and expected years of schooling)* |
| ⭐ **3. RESOURCES — a decent standard of living** | ⭐ **GROSS NATIONAL PRODUCT / GNI PER CAPITA at purchasing power parity (PPP) in US dollars** |

⭐ **Each dimension is given a weightage of 1/3.**

⭐ **HDI is a sum total of the weights assigned to all these dimensions.** ⭐ **The closer a score is to ONE, the greater is the level of human development.** ⭐ **Therefore, a score of 0.983 would be considered VERY HIGH while a score of 0.268 would mean a VERY LOW level of human development.**

### ⭐ HDI categories

| Category | ⭐ Score range |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **VERY HIGH human development** | ⭐ **0.800 and above** |
| ⭐ **HIGH human development** | ⭐ **0.700 – 0.799** |
| ⭐ **MEDIUM human development** | ⭐ **0.550 – 0.699** |
| ⭐ **LOW human development** | ⭐ **Below 0.550** |

⚠️ ⭐ **India generally falls in the MEDIUM human development category.**

### ⭐ Human Poverty Index (HPI)

⭐ **The HUMAN POVERTY INDEX is related to the human development index.** ⭐ **This index measures the SHORTFALL in human development.**

⭐ **It is a NON-INCOME MEASURE.**

⭐ **The probability of not surviving till the age of 40, the adult illiteracy rate, the number of people who do not have access to clean water, and the number of small children who are underweight are all taken into account to show the shortfall in human development in any region.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Often the HPI is more revealing than the HDI.** ⭐ **Looking at both these measures of human development together gives an accurate picture of the human development situation in a country.**

> ⭐⭐ **Why HPI matters and this is a genuinely good point:** ⭐ **HDI reports the AVERAGE; HPI reports the SHORTFALL.** ⭐ **A country can have a respectable average HDI while a large minority is severely deprived — the average conceals them.**
>
> ⭐ **NCERT says so directly: "often the HPI is MORE REVEALING than the HDI."** ⭐ **Averages hide distribution.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **This connects directly to the Inequality-adjusted HDI (IHDI) and the Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)** — the modern successors. **NITI Aayog publishes India's own MPI; India's rapid MPI decline is a live current-affairs point.**

### ⭐ Gender-related measures

| Index | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **GDI — Gender-related Development Index** | ⭐ **HDI adjusted for gender disparity in the three basic dimensions** |
| ⭐ **GEM — Gender Empowerment Measure** | ⭐ **Measures women's participation in economic and political decision-making** |

*(Current UNDP reports use the **Gender Inequality Index (GII)** and **Gender Development Index**; know both the NCERT terms and the current ones.)*

## 6️⃣ ⭐ International Comparisons

⭐ **International comparisons of human development are interesting.** ⭐ **Size and per capita income are not directly related to human development.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Often smaller countries have done better than larger ones in human development.**

⭐ **Similarly, a higher income country may have lower human development than a lower income country.**

⭐ **SRI LANKA, TRINIDAD AND TOBAGO have a higher rank in human development than India despite lower per capita income.**

> ⭐⭐ **This is the empirical proof of Sen's argument, and it deserves emphasis:** ⭐ **income and human development are NOT the same thing.**
>
> ⭐ **Sri Lanka has long had higher life expectancy and literacy than India at lower per capita income** — because it invested early and consistently in public health and education.
>
> ⭐ **Within India, KERALA is the domestic version of the same lesson:** ⭐ **high literacy (especially female), high life expectancy, low infant mortality and a favourable sex ratio, achieved without correspondingly high per capita income.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **"Development is a policy choice, not merely an income effect" — that sentence, backed by Sri Lanka and Kerala, is one of the strongest available in any answer on human development.**

### ⭐ Political freedom and human development

⭐ **Countries with high levels of human development invest more in the social sectors and are generally free from political turmoil and instability.**

⭐ **The pattern of governmental expenditure on social sectors gives a fair idea about the human development situation.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Countries with higher human development have low military expenditure and high social sector expenditure.**

> ⭐ **NCERT's observation that high-HDI countries have LOW MILITARY and HIGH SOCIAL spending is a pointed one, and useful in an essay** on development priorities or the guns-versus-butter trade-off.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐⭐ **GROWTH is quantitative and value-neutral and CAN BE NEGATIVE. DEVELOPMENT is qualitative and ALWAYS value-positive.** ⭐ **Positive growth does not always produce development.**
2. ⭐ **MAHBUB-UL-HAQ introduced human development and created the HDI; the first Human Development Report was published in 1990 by UNDP.**
3. ⭐⭐ **AMARTYA SEN's CAPABILITY APPROACH: development is the expansion of FREEDOM — what people are ABLE TO DO AND BE, not what they have.**
4. ⭐⭐ **Four pillars: EQUITY (access now) · SUSTAINABILITY (access across generations) · PRODUCTIVITY (building capability) · EMPOWERMENT (power to choose).**
5. ⭐⭐ **Four approaches, in increasing order of human agency: INCOME → WELFARE (passive recipients) → BASIC NEEDS (ILO's SIX: health, education, food, water, sanitation, housing; ignores choice) → CAPABILITY (Sen).**
6. ⭐⭐ **HDI has THREE dimensions, each weighted 1/3: life expectancy · education · GNI per capita at PPP.** ⭐ **Closer to 1 = higher development.**
7. ⭐ **Categories: ≥0.800 very high · 0.700–0.799 high · 0.550–0.699 medium · <0.550 low.** India is generally **medium**.
8. ⭐⭐ **HPI measures the SHORTFALL and is a NON-INCOME measure** — probability of not surviving to 40, adult illiteracy, no clean water, underweight children. ⭐ **"Often more revealing than HDI"** because averages hide distribution.
9. ⭐ **GDI adjusts HDI for gender disparity; GEM measures women's participation in decision-making.**
10. ⭐⭐ **Sri Lanka and Trinidad & Tobago rank above India despite lower per capita income** — and **Kerala is the domestic version of the same lesson.** ⭐ **Development is a policy choice, not merely an income effect.**
11. ⭐ **High-HDI countries have LOW military and HIGH social-sector expenditure.**
