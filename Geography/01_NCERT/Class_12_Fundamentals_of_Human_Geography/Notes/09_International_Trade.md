# Chapter 9 — International Trade

> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **Trade exists because no place produces everything it needs, and every place produces something better than someone else can.** ⭐ **But the composition of a country's trade — what it sells versus what it buys — reveals more about its development than the volume ever does.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐ What International Trade Is

> ⭐ **INTERNATIONAL TRADE is the exchange of goods and services among countries across national boundaries.**

⭐ **Countries need to trade to obtain commodities, they cannot produce themselves or they can purchase elsewhere at a lower price.**

⭐ **In the past, TRANSPORTATION AND STORAGE WERE EXPENSIVE, so trade was restricted to local markets.**

⭐ **Even today, trade in perishable goods is limited by cost of transport and storage.**

⭐ **Advancement in transport and communication has increased the volume of trade.**

⭐ **In ancient times, trade was conducted through BARTER SYSTEM — exchange of goods for goods.**

⭐ **Later, MONEY was used as a medium of exchange.**

> ⭐ **Trade is fundamentally about SPECIALISATION.** ⭐ **A country that produces everything itself produces everything badly.** ⭐ **The gains from trade come from each place doing what it does comparatively best and exchanging the surplus.** *(This is Ricardo's theory of comparative advantage, which Economy will develop formally.)*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Basis of International Trade

| Basis | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **DIFFERENCE IN NATIONAL RESOURCES** | ⭐ **The world's national resources are unevenly distributed because of differences in their physical make up i.e. GEOLOGY, RELIEF, SOIL AND CLIMATE.** ⭐ **(a) GEOLOGICAL STRUCTURE determines the mineral resource base and topographical differences ensure diversity of crops and animals raised. ⭐ (b) MINERAL RESOURCES are unevenly distributed the world over. The availability of mineral resources provides the basis for industrial development. ⭐ (c) CLIMATE influences the type of flora and fauna that can survive in a given region. It also ensures diversity in the range of various products, e.g. wool production can take place in cold regions, bananas, rubber and cocoa can grow in tropical regions** |
| ⭐ **POPULATION FACTORS** | ⭐ **The size, distribution and diversity of people between countries affect the type and volume of goods traded.** ⭐ **(a) CULTURAL FACTORS: Distinctive forms of art and craft develop in certain cultures which are valued the world over, e.g. China produces the finest porcelains and brocades. Carpets of Iran are famous while Indonesia produces the best batik cloth. ⭐ (b) SIZE OF POPULATION: Densely populated countries have large volume of internal trade but little external trade because most of the agricultural and industrial production is consumed in the local markets. Standard of living of the population determines the demand for better quality imported products** |
| ⭐ **STAGE OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT** | ⭐ **At different stages of economic development of countries, the nature of items traded undergo changes.** ⭐ **In agriculturally important countries, agro products are exchanged for manufactured goods whereas industrialised nations export machinery and finished products and import food grains and other raw materials** |
| ⭐ **EXTENT OF FOREIGN INVESTMENT** | ⭐ **Foreign investment can boost trade in developing countries which lack in capital required for the development of mining, oil drilling, heavy engineering, lumbering and plantation agriculture.** ⭐ **By developing such capital intensive industries in developing countries, the industrial nations ensure import of food stuffs, minerals and create markets for their finished products** |
| ⭐ **TRANSPORT** | ⭐ **In olden times, lack of adequate and efficient means of transport restricted trade to local areas.** ⭐ **Only high value items, e.g. gems, silk and spices were traded over long distances.** ⭐ **With the expansion of rail, ocean and air transport, better means of refrigeration and preservation, trade has experienced spatial expansion** |

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ Important Aspects of International Trade

### ⭐⭐ Volume, composition, direction and balance

| Aspect | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **VOLUME OF TRADE** | ⭐ **The actual tonnage of goods traded.** ⭐ **However, services traded cannot be measured in tonnage.** ⭐ **Therefore, the total value of goods and services traded is considered to be a better indicator of the volume of trade** |
| ⭐ **COMPOSITION OF TRADE** | ⭐ **The nature of goods and services traded is called the composition of trade.** ⭐ **In the beginning of the twentieth century, PRIMARY PRODUCTS such as food, raw materials, ores and minerals accounted for the bulk of world trade.** ⭐ **In the first half of the twentieth century, MANUFACTURED GOODS gained importance.** ⭐ **Since the 1970s, world trade in MANUFACTURED GOODS and SERVICES has expanded, and the share of primary products has declined.** ⭐ **Today, agricultural products account for a low proportion of world trade whereas the share of the service sector has increased** |
| ⭐ **DIRECTION OF TRADE** | ⭐ **In the nineteenth century, the developing countries used to export valuable goods, artefacts to developed countries.** ⭐ **After European colonisation of Asia and Africa, the direction of trade got reversed — developed countries began exporting manufactured goods to colonies and importing raw materials** |
| ⭐ **BALANCE OF TRADE** | ⭐ **Records the volume of goods and services imported as well as exported by a country to other countries.** ⭐ **If the value of imports is more than the value of a country's exports, the country has NEGATIVE OR UNFAVOURABLE balance of trade.** ⭐ **If the value of exports is more than the value of imports, then the country has a POSITIVE OR FAVOURABLE balance of trade** |

⚠️ ⭐ **A NEGATIVE balance would mean that the country spends more on buying goods than it can earn by selling its goods.** ⭐ **This would ultimately lead to exhaustion of its financial reserves.**

> ⭐⭐ **COMPOSITION matters more than VOLUME, and this is the analytically important point:**
> ⭐ **A country exporting raw materials and importing manufactures is in a structurally weak position**, because primary product prices are volatile and value-addition happens elsewhere.
> ⭐ **A country exporting manufactures and services is capturing the value.**
>
> ⭐ **This connects directly to Cl.10 Ch.6's statistic: India exports cotton YARN (large world share) but garments (only ~4%).** ⭐ **Same fibre, different position in the value chain.** ⭐ **The volume of trade tells you how much moves; the composition tells you who is getting rich.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ Types of International Trade

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **BILATERAL TRADE** | ⭐ **Done by two countries with each other.** ⭐ **They enter into agreement to trade specified commodities amongst them.** ⭐ **For example, country A may agree to trade some raw material with agreed quantity of processed/manufactured goods with country B** |
| ⭐ **MULTILATERAL TRADE** | ⭐ **Conducted with many trading countries.** ⭐ **The same country can trade with a number of other countries.** ⭐ **The country may also grant the status of the "MOST FAVOURED NATION" (MFN) on some of the trading partners** |

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ Free Trade vs Protection

> ⭐ **FREE TRADE or TRADE LIBERALISATION is the act of opening up economies for trading.**

⭐ **This is done by BRINGING DOWN TRADE BARRIERS like tariffs.**

⭐ **Free trade or trade liberalisation is the act of opening up economies for trading.** ⭐ **All the trade barriers are removed which allows the markets to be opened up to foreign companies.**

⚠️ ⭐ **However, free trade should not only be free but should also be FAIR.**

⭐ **The developed countries have been trying to protect their domestic producers.**

⭐ **DUMPING: The practice of selling a commodity in two different countries at a price which differs for reasons not related to costs is called dumping.**

> ⭐⭐ **DUMPING is the term to learn precisely:** ⭐ **selling the same commodity in two countries at prices differing for reasons NOT related to costs** — typically selling abroad below the home-market price or below cost to capture market share.
>
> ⭐ **Anti-dumping duties are the standard remedy, and India is among the world's most frequent users of them.** **A live current-affairs point.**

> ⭐⭐ **The "free but also FAIR" line is NCERT's own and it is worth reproducing.** ⭐ **The developing-country critique of trade liberalisation is not that trade is bad, but that the rules are asymmetric:** ⭐ **developed countries demand open markets for their manufactures and services while heavily SUBSIDISING their own agriculture** — which is precisely the deadlock at the WTO.
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **India's position — food security, the Peace Clause and Public Stockholding — rests on exactly this argument. Strong for GS-II and GS-III.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐ The World Trade Organisation

⭐ **In 1948, the GENERAL AGREEMENT FOR TARIFFS AND TRADE (GATT) was formed by some countries.**

⭐ **This was transformed into the WORLD TRADE ORGANISATION from 1 JANUARY 1995.**

⭐ **The WTO is the ONLY INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATION DEALING WITH THE GLOBAL RULES OF TRADE BETWEEN NATIONS.**

⭐ **It sets the rules for the global trading system and resolves disputes between its member nations.**

⭐ **WTO also covers trade in services, such as telecommunication and banking, and others issues such as intellectual property rights.**

⚠️ ⭐ **However, the WTO has been criticised and opposed by demonstrators and critics who feel that FREE TRADE AND ECONOMIC GLOBALISATION HAS INCREASED INEQUALITY, made rich countries richer, ignored environment and labour issues, and given rise to social unrest.**

⭐ **THE HEADQUARTERS OF WTO IS AT GENEVA, SWITZERLAND.**

⭐ **INDIA IS A FOUNDING MEMBER OF WTO.**

> ⭐ **The GATT → WTO transition dates are examinable: ⭐ GATT formed 1948; WTO from 1 JANUARY 1995; HQ GENEVA; India a FOUNDING MEMBER.**
>
> ⭐ **And note that NCERT itself records the criticism** — that free trade has increased inequality, favoured rich countries, and ignored environmental and labour concerns. **A textbook acknowledging the critique of its own subject is unusual and worth citing.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐ Regional Trade Blocs

⭐ **REGIONAL TRADE BLOCS have come up in order to encourage trade between countries with geographical proximity, similarity and complementarities in trading items and to curb restrictions on trade of the developing world.**

⭐ **Today, 120 regional trade blocs generate 52 per cent of the world trade.**

⭐ **These trading blocs developed as a response to the failure of the global organisations to speed up intra-regional trade.**

## 8️⃣ ⭐ Concerns Related to International Trade

⭐ **International trade can prove to be BENEFICIAL to countries only up to a point.**

⭐ **Over dependence on trade at any time is fraught with danger.**

⭐ **Trade in items that lead to environmental degradation and destruction of resources should be discouraged.**

⭐ **The problem of continuously exporting a resource may lead to its exhaustion.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Exports of valuable natural resources may lead to their exhaustion and environmental degradation.**

⭐ **A country should be careful in dealing with import of items which may be detrimental to health.**

> 🌱 ⭐ **The environmental point NCERT makes is worth developing:** ⭐ **when a country exports a natural resource continuously, it is exporting its future.** ⭐ **And in practice the ecological cost stays in the exporting country while the benefit accrues to the importer** — **"ecologically unequal exchange."**
>
> ⭐ **This connects to the mining "resource curse" (Ch.5) and to the argument about India's mineral belt (Cl.10 Ch.5).**

## 9️⃣ ⭐ Gateways of International Trade

⭐ **PORTS are the chief gateways of the world of international trade.**

⭐ **The cargoes and travellers pass from one part of the world to another through these ports.**

⭐ **The ports provide facilities of docking, loading, unloading and the storage facilities for cargo.**

### ⭐ Types of ports

**⭐ Types of port on the basis of CARGO handled:**

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **INDUSTRIAL PORTS** | ⭐ **Specialise in bulk cargo — like grain, sugar, ore, oil, chemicals and similar materials** |
| ⭐ **COMMERCIAL PORTS** | ⭐ **Handle general cargo — packaged products and manufactured goods.** ⭐ **These ports also handle passenger traffic** |
| ⭐ **COMPREHENSIVE PORTS** | ⭐ **Handle bulk and general cargo in large volumes.** ⭐ **Most of the world's great ports are classified as comprehensive ports** |

**⭐ Types of port on the basis of LOCATION:**

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **INLAND PORTS** | ⭐ **Located away from the sea coast.** ⭐ **They are linked with the sea through a river or a canal.** ⭐ **Such ports are accessible to flat bottom ships or barges.** ⭐ **For example, Manchester is linked with a canal; Memphis is located on the river Mississippi; Rhine has several ports like Mannheim and Duisburg; and Kolkata is located on the river Hooghly, a branch of the river Ganga** |
| ⭐ **OUT PORTS** | ⭐ **Deep water ports built away from the actual ports.** ⭐ **These serve the parent ports by receiving those ships which are unable to approach them due to their large size.** ⭐ **Classic combination, for example, is Athens and its out port Piraeus in Greece** |

**⭐ Types of port on the basis of SPECIALISED FUNCTIONS:**

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **OIL PORTS** | ⭐ **Deal in the processing and shipping of oil.** ⭐ **Some of these are TANKER PORTS and some are REFINERY PORTS.** ⭐ **Maracaibo in Venezuela, Esskhira in Tunisia, Tripoli in Lebanon are tanker ports. Abadan on the Gulf of Persia is a refinery port** |
| ⭐ **PORTS OF CALL** | ⭐ **The ports which originally developed as calling points on main sea routes where ships used to anchor for refuelling, watering and taking food items.** ⭐ **Later on, they developed into commercial ports.** ⭐ **Aden, Honolulu and Singapore are good examples** |
| ⭐ **PACKET STATION** | ⭐ **Also known as FERRY PORTS.** ⭐ **These are exclusively concerned with the transportation of passengers and mail across water bodies covering short distances.** ⭐ **These stations occur in pairs located in such a way that they face each other across the water body, e.g. Dover in England and Calais in France across the English Channel** |
| ⭐ **ENTREPOT PORTS** | ⭐ **These are collection centres where the goods are brought from different countries for export.** ⭐ **Singapore is an entrepot for Asia.** ⭐ **Rotterdam for Europe, and Copenhagen for the Baltic region** |
| ⭐ **NAVAL PORTS** | ⭐ **These are ports which have only strategic importance.** ⭐ **These ports serve warships and have repair workshops for them.** ⭐ **Kochi and Karwar are examples of such ports in India** |

> ⭐ **The port typology is dense but examinable.** ⭐ **The ones most often asked: ⭐ ENTREPOT (Singapore, Rotterdam, Copenhagen) · PORT OF CALL (Aden, Honolulu, Singapore) · PACKET STATION/FERRY (Dover–Calais) · OUT PORT (Piraeus for Athens) · NAVAL (Kochi, Karwar).**

**Trap 1:** *"A favourable balance of trade means imports exceed exports."* ⭐ **EXPORTS exceed imports.**
**Trap 2:** *"WTO was established in 1948."* ⭐ **GATT was formed in 1948; WTO from 1 January 1995.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Trade rests on SPECIALISATION** — no country produces everything well.
2. ⭐ **Basis: national resources (geology, minerals, climate) · population factors (cultural specialities, size) · stage of development · foreign investment · transport.**
3. ⭐⭐ **Four aspects: VOLUME · COMPOSITION · DIRECTION · BALANCE.**
4. ⭐ **Composition has shifted: primary products → manufactured goods → SERVICES (since the 1970s).**
5. ⭐⭐ **COMPOSITION matters more than VOLUME** — exporting raw material and importing manufactures is a structurally weak position. **India's yarn-vs-garments gap is the case.**
6. ⭐ **BALANCE: exports > imports = FAVOURABLE; imports > exports = UNFAVOURABLE**, leading to exhaustion of financial reserves.
7. ⭐ **BILATERAL (two countries) vs MULTILATERAL (many, with MFN status).**
8. ⭐⭐ **DUMPING = selling in two countries at prices differing for reasons NOT related to costs.**
9. ⭐ **NCERT's own line: "free trade should not only be free but should also be FAIR."** The developing-country critique is about asymmetric rules, not about trade itself.
10. ⭐⭐ **GATT 1948 → WTO from 1 JANUARY 1995; HQ GENEVA; India a FOUNDING MEMBER.** ⭐ **NCERT records the criticism: inequality, rich-country bias, environment and labour ignored.**
11. ⭐ **120 regional blocs generate 52% of world trade.** ASEAN, CIS, EU, LAIA, NAFTA, OECD, OPEC, SAFTA.
12. ⭐ **Ports: industrial · commercial · comprehensive · inland (Kolkata) · out port (Piraeus) · oil (tanker/refinery) · port of call (Aden, Honolulu, Singapore) · packet/ferry (Dover–Calais) · ENTREPOT (Singapore, Rotterdam, Copenhagen) · naval (Kochi, Karwar).**
