# Chapter 7 — Tertiary and Quaternary Activities

> **The one-line point:** ⭐ **Tertiary activities sell EXPERTISE and SERVICE rather than goods** — and as economies develop, employment shifts decisively into them. ⭐ **Beyond services lie the QUATERNARY sector (knowledge) and the QUINARY sector (the highest decision-making) — a hierarchy that describes where power in a modern economy actually sits.**

🟠 **HIGH PRIORITY — ~1.5 hrs.** ⭐ **Heavy Economy overlap; outsourcing and the knowledge economy are live current-affairs topics.**

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ Tertiary Activities

⭐ **TERTIARY ACTIVITIES are related to the SERVICE SECTOR.**

⭐ **Manpower is an important component of service sector as most of the tertiary activities are performed by SKILLED LABOUR, PROFESSIONALLY TRAINED EXPERTS and CONSULTANTS.**

⭐ **Tertiary activities involve the COMMERCIAL OUTPUT OF SERVICES rather than the production of tangible goods.**

⭐ **They are not directly involved in the processing of physical raw materials.** ⭐ **Common examples are the work of a plumber, electrician, technician, launderer, barber, shopkeeper, driver, cashier, teacher, doctor, lawyer and publisher etc.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The main difference between secondary activities and tertiary activities is that the EXPERTISE PROVIDED BY SERVICES relies more on SPECIALISED SKILLS, EXPERIENCE AND KNOWLEDGE of the workers rather than on the production techniques, machinery and factory processes.**

⭐ **Workers are called WHITE-COLLAR WORKERS.**

### ⭐ Types of tertiary activities

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **TRADE AND COMMERCE** | ⭐ **Trade is essentially BUYING AND SELLING of items produced elsewhere.** ⭐ **All the services in retail and wholesale trading or commerce are specifically intended for profit.** ⭐ **The towns and cities where all these works take place are known as TRADING CENTRES** |
| ⭐ **RETAIL TRADING** | ⭐ **This is the business activity concerned with the SALE OF GOODS DIRECTLY TO THE CONSUMERS.** ⭐ **Most of the retail trading takes place in fixed establishments or stores solely devoted to selling.** ⭐ **Street peddling, handcarts, trucks, door-to-door, mail-order, telephone, automatic vending machines and internet are examples of NON-STORE RETAIL TRADING** |
| ⭐ **WHOLESALE TRADING** | ⭐ **Constitutes BULK BUSINESS through numerous intermediary merchants and supply houses and not through retail stores.** ⭐ **Some large stores including chain stores are able to buy directly from the manufacturers.** ⭐ **However, most retail stores procure supplies from an intermediary source.** ⭐ **Wholesalers often extend credit to retail stores** |
| ⭐ **TRANSPORT** | ⭐ **A SERVICE or FACILITY by which people, materials and manufactured goods are physically carried from one location to another.** ⭐ **It is an organised industry created to satisfy man's basic need of mobility |
| ⭐ **COMMUNICATION** | ⭐ **Communication services involve the TRANSMISSION OF WORDS AND MESSAGES, facts and ideas** |
| ⭐ **SERVICES** | ⭐ **Services occur at many different levels.** ⭐ **Some are geared to industry, some to people; and some to both** |

### ⭐ Trading centres

| Type | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **RURAL MARKETING CENTRES** | ⭐ **Cater to nearby settlements.** ⭐ **These are QUASI-URBAN CENTRES.** ⭐ **They serve as trading centres of the most rudimentary type.** ⭐ **Here, personal and professional services are not well-developed.** ⭐ **These form local collecting and distributing centres.** ⭐ **Most of these have MANDIS (wholesale markets) and also retailing areas.** ⭐ **They are not urban centres per se but are significant centres for making available goods and services which are most frequently demanded by rural folk.** ⭐ **PERIODIC MARKETS in rural areas are found where there are no regular markets and local periodic markets are organised at different temporal intervals.** ⭐ **These may be weekly, bi-weekly markets from where people from the surrounding areas meet their variable demands** |
| ⭐ **URBAN MARKETING CENTRES** | ⭐ **Have more widely specialised urban services.** ⭐ **They provide ordinary goods and services as well as many of the specialised goods and services required by people.** ⭐ **Urban centres, therefore, offer manufactured goods as well as many specialised markets develop, e.g. markets for labour, housing, semi or finished products.** ⭐ **Services of educational institutions and professionals such as teachers, lawyers, consultants, physicians, dentists and veterinary doctors are available** |

### ⭐ Services

⭐ **Services are provided to individual consumers who can purchase them (e.g. GARDENERS, WASHERMEN and BARBERS), and to businesses.**

⭐ **LOW-ORDER SERVICES, such as grocery shops and laundries, are more common and widespread than HIGH-ORDER SERVICES or more specialised ones like those of doctors, banks, insurance, computer specialists, lawyers, and business consultants.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The service sector employment has grown enormously.**

> ⭐ **The LOW-ORDER vs HIGH-ORDER distinction is important for Ch.10 (settlements) too:** ⭐ **low-order services are frequently needed and therefore widespread; high-order services are rarely needed and therefore concentrated in larger settlements.** ⭐ **This is the basis of the settlement hierarchy — the more specialised the service, the larger the settlement needed to support it.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Quaternary Activities

⭐ **The economy of the developed countries has been transformed into a service-based economy.**

> ⭐ **QUATERNARY ACTIVITIES centre around RESEARCH, DEVELOPMENT and may be seen as an advanced form of services involving specialised knowledge and technical skills.**

⭐ **The quaternary sector involves the COLLECTION, PRODUCTION AND DISSEMINATION OF INFORMATION or even the production of information.**

⭐ **Quaternary activities involve some of the following: the collection, production and dissemination of information or even the production of information.**

⭐ **Quaternary services include: education, information, research and development, and all the other white-collar professions.**

⭐ **Like some of the tertiary functions, quaternary activities can also be OUTSOURCED.** ⭐ **They are not tied to resources, affected by the environment, or necessarily localised by market.**

⭐ **Personnel working in office buildings, elementary schools and university classrooms, hospitals and doctors' offices, theatres, accounting and brokerage firms all belong to this category of services.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ Quinary Activities

⭐ **QUINARY ACTIVITIES are services that focus on the CREATION, RE-ARRANGEMENT AND INTERPRETATION OF NEW AND EXISTING IDEAS; DATA INTERPRETATION AND THE USE AND EVALUATION OF NEW TECHNOLOGIES.**

⭐ **Often referred to as 'GOLD COLLAR' PROFESSIONS, they represent another subdivision of the tertiary sector representing SPECIAL AND HIGHLY PAID SKILLS OF SENIOR BUSINESS EXECUTIVES, GOVERNMENT OFFICIALS, RESEARCH SCIENTISTS, FINANCIAL AND LEGAL CONSULTANTS, etc.**

⭐ **Their importance in the structure of advanced economies far outweighs their numbers.**

⚠️ ⭐ **The highest level of decision makers or policy makers perform quinary activities.**

> ⭐⭐ **The five-sector hierarchy, in order — worth learning as a ladder:**
>
> ⭐ **PRIMARY (red-collar)** — extract from nature
> ⭐ **SECONDARY (blue-collar)** — manufacture
> ⭐ **TERTIARY (white-collar)** — provide services
> ⭐ **QUATERNARY** — produce and disseminate KNOWLEDGE
> ⭐ **QUINARY (GOLD-collar)** — CREATE and INTERPRET ideas; the highest decision-makers
>
> ⭐ **NCERT's key line about the quinary sector: "their importance in the structure of advanced economies FAR OUTWEIGHS THEIR NUMBERS."** ⭐ **A very small number of people making decisions with disproportionate consequences.** **That observation is genuinely useful in an essay about inequality or the structure of modern economies.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Outsourcing

> ⭐ **OUTSOURCING or CONTRACTING OUT is giving work to an outside agency to improve efficiency and to reduce costs.**

⭐ **When outsourcing involves transferring work to overseas locations, it is described by the term OFF-SHORING**, although both off-shoring and outsourcing are used together.

⭐ **Business activities that are outsourced include INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY (IT), HUMAN RESOURCES, CUSTOMER SUPPORT AND CALL CENTRE SERVICES and at times MANUFACTURING AND ENGINEERING.**

⭐ **Data processing is an IT-related service which can easily be carried out in Asian, East European and African countries.** ⭐ **In these countries the wages are relatively low, which reduces the cost of outsourcing.**

⭐ **Overhead costs are also much lower in these regions.**

⭐ **This is the main reason for outsourcing.** ⭐ **Companies outsource specific business functions to reduce costs.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Outsourcing is coming to those countries where cheap and skilled workers are available.** ⭐ **These are ADVANTAGEOUS TO COMPANIES LOCATED IN DEVELOPED COUNTRIES.**

⭐ **There has been an increase in outsourcing to India, China and many Eastern European countries.**

### ⭐ Effects of outsourcing

⭐ **New trends in quaternary services include KNOWLEDGE PROCESSING OUTSOURCING (KPO) and 'HOME SHORING', the latter as an alternative to outsourcing.**

⭐ **The KPO industry is distinct from BUSINESS PROCESS OUTSOURCING (BPO) as it involves highly skilled workers.**

⭐ **It is aimed at cost reduction and saving resources of the parent company.** ⭐ **KPO provides its services in the fields of RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT, BUSINESS AND TECHNICAL ANALYSIS, ANIMATION AND DESIGN, PHARMACEUTICALS AND BIOTECHNOLOGY, MEDICAL AND LEGAL PROCESSES and I.T., etc.**

⭐ **Examples of KPOs include E-LEARNING, PUBLISHING, MARKET RESEARCH (MR), etc.**

> ⭐⭐ **BPO vs KPO is a frequently examined distinction:**
> ⭐ **BPO (Business Process Outsourcing)** — routine, process-driven work; call centres, data entry, transaction processing. **Lower skill.**
> ⭐ **KPO (Knowledge Process Outsourcing)** — ⭐ **involves HIGHLY SKILLED WORKERS.** R&D, business and technical analysis, animation and design, pharmaceuticals and biotechnology, medical and legal processes, IT. ⭐ **Examples: e-learning, publishing, market research.**
>
> ⭐ **The move from BPO to KPO is India's attempt to climb the value chain** — from selling cheap labour hours to selling expertise. **A strong, current point for an Economy answer.**

> ⭐ **'HOME SHORING' is worth noting** — ⭐ **an ALTERNATIVE to outsourcing, in which work is done from home in the parent country rather than sent abroad.** **The pandemic accelerated it substantially, which is a good current-affairs link.**

⚠️ ⭐ **There is a fear among the people in the developed countries that outsourcing is taking away jobs from them.** ⭐ **Some countries are trying to protect jobs and are opposing outsourcing.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐ The Digital Divide

⭐ **Opportunities emerging from the information and communication technology-based development is unevenly distributed across the globe.**

⭐ **There are wide-ranging economic, political and social differences among countries.**

⭐ **How quickly countries can provide ICT access and benefits to its citizens is a deciding factor, whereas access to it and its use is decided by economic, political and social factors.**

⭐ **DIGITAL DIVIDE is the gap between the countries and communities which have access to information and communication technology and those which do not.**

⚠️ ⭐ **Digital divide is conspicuous at both global and regional levels.**

⭐ **Developed countries in general have surged forward and the developing countries have lagged behind.**

> 🎯 ⭐ **The DIGITAL DIVIDE is a term worth carrying into GS-II and GS-III answers.** ⭐ **It exists at three levels: BETWEEN countries, BETWEEN regions within a country (India's urban/rural), and BETWEEN social groups (gender, income, caste).**
>
> ⭐ **India's response: Digital India, BharatNet, common service centres, UPI and the India Stack.** ⭐ **But the gender digital divide in India remains substantial** — a specific, current point.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐ **Tertiary activities sell EXPERTISE rather than goods; workers are WHITE-COLLAR.** The difference from secondary is reliance on **skills, experience and knowledge** rather than machinery.
2. ⭐ **Retail = direct to consumers. Wholesale = bulk through intermediaries; wholesalers extend CREDIT to retailers.** ⭐ **Non-store retail includes street peddling, mail-order, vending machines and the internet.**
3. ⭐ **Rural marketing centres are QUASI-URBAN, with MANDIS; PERIODIC MARKETS operate where there is no regular market.**
4. ⭐ **LOW-ORDER services (groceries, laundries) are widespread; HIGH-ORDER services (doctors, banks, consultants) are concentrated.** This underpins the settlement hierarchy.
5. ⭐⭐ **QUATERNARY = collection, production and dissemination of INFORMATION** — R&D, education, white-collar professions. ⭐ **Not tied to resources or market, and therefore outsourceable.**
6. ⭐⭐ **QUINARY = GOLD COLLAR — creation and interpretation of ideas; the highest decision-makers.** ⭐ **"Their importance far outweighs their numbers."**
7. ⭐ **The five-sector ladder: primary (red) → secondary (blue) → tertiary (white) → quaternary → quinary (gold).**
8. ⭐⭐ **OUTSOURCING = contracting out. OFF-SHORING = transferring work OVERSEAS.** Driven by lower wages and overheads.
9. ⭐⭐ **BPO = routine processes. KPO = HIGHLY SKILLED work** — R&D, business and technical analysis, animation and design, pharma and biotech, medical and legal, IT. ⭐ **Examples: e-learning, publishing, market research.**
10. ⭐ **HOME SHORING is the alternative to outsourcing.** Developed countries increasingly resist outsourcing on job-loss grounds.
11. ⭐ **The DIGITAL DIVIDE separates those with ICT access from those without** — between countries, within countries, and between social groups.
