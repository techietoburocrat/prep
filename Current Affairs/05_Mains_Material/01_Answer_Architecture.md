# 🏗️ Answer Architecture — the structural half of Mains

**Most marks lost in GS are lost to structure, not to knowledge.** Two candidates who know the same material score 6 and 11 on the same question, and the difference is almost never content. It's that one answered the directive that was asked and one answered the topic that was mentioned.

This file is short on purpose. Learn it once, apply it 20 times a paper.

---

## 1. The directive verb decides the shape

Read the verb **first**, before you think about the topic. It is an instruction, not decoration.

| Directive | What it demands | Fatal mistake |
|---|---|---|
| **Discuss** | Multiple facets, balanced, no strong verdict needed | Writing only the positive side |
| **Critically examine / analyse** | All facets **plus an explicit judgement** | Thinking "critically" means "criticise." It means **arrive at a verdict** |
| **Examine** | Investigate in detail — causes, mechanisms, effects | Listing facts without showing *why* |
| **Analyse** | Break into components, show how they relate | Describing instead of decomposing |
| **Evaluate / Assess** | Weigh merits against demerits, then judge | Weighing and then not judging |
| **To what extent / How far** | **Quantify the degree explicitly** | Not answering "to what extent." The answer must contain a phrase like *"largely, but not in respect of…"* |
| **Comment** | Your view, justified, concise | Writing a full essay |
| **Elucidate / Explain** | Make it clear, with illustration | Adding criticism nobody asked for |
| **Substantiate** | Marshal evidence for a claim already given | Arguing against the claim |
| **Illustrate** | Examples are **mandatory**, not optional | Abstract answer with zero examples |
| **Compare and contrast** | Similarities **and** differences | Only differences |
| **Enumerate** | List, brief, structured | Long prose |

> 🔑 **The two highest-frequency errors in the whole exam:** treating *critically* as "attack it," and not stating the extent in a *to what extent* question. Fix those two and you have moved a band.

## 2. The budget

| Marks | Words | Time | Reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | ~150 | 7–8 min | ~4 body points |
| 15 | ~250 | 11 min | ~6 body points |
| Full paper | 20 Q | 180 min | **9 min per question, including thinking** |

**You cannot think for three minutes and write for six.** The structure below exists so that thinking takes 60 seconds — because the shape is already decided before you read the question.

---

## 3. The shape

```
INTRO      2–3 lines   ~15%
BODY       headed      ~70%
CONCLUSION 2–3 lines   ~15%
```

### Intro — pick one of four, never a fifth

| Type | Example opening |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | *"Ecologically Sensitive Areas are notified under s.3 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986…"* |
| **Data** | *"With 56,825 sq km across six states proposed as ESA…"* |
| **Constitutional/legal anchor** | *"Article 82 mandates readjustment of constituencies after every census…"* |
| **Context/paradox** | *"India froze Lok Sabha seats in 1976 to reward population control; fifty years on, that reward has become a grievance…"* |

**Never open with:** "India is a country of vast diversity," "Since time immemorial," "In today's world." These signal a weak answer before the examiner reaches the body.

### Body — headings, always

Use **bold sub-headings**. They cost nothing and they make the examiner's job easy, which is the entire game in a 90-second evaluation.

Each point: **claim → one line of elaboration → example or data.** A claim with no example is an opinion; an example with no claim is trivia.

### Conclusion — forward-looking, never a summary

Repeat nothing. Land on one of:
- **Way forward** — a committee recommendation, a named reform
- **Balanced verdict** — for *critically* questions, this is where the verdict goes
- **Constitutional/values anchor** — cooperative federalism, Directive Principles, SDG target

---

## 4. Diagrams, tables, flowcharts

A small table or flow diagram earns marks that the same content in prose does not. Use one per paper minimum. **Especially** for compare-and-contrast questions, where a two-column table *is* the answer.

---

## 5. What separates a 6 from an 11

| 6/15 | 11/15 |
|---|---|
| Correct but generic | Correct **and specific** — named article, named committee, named scheme |
| One-sided | Both sides, then a verdict |
| No examples | Every point carries an example or a number |
| Prose block | Headed, scannable |
| Repeats the question in the intro | Defines or anchors in the intro |
| Ends by summarising | Ends by proposing |

Content gets you to 6. **Specificity and structure get you to 11.** This is good news: specificity and structure are trainable in weeks, whereas content takes months.
