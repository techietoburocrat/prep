# 🔭 The Eight Lenses — how to generate an answer instead of hoping for one

**This file replaces years of reading editorials.**

What editorials actually teach — slowly, indirectly, by osmosis — is that any public issue can be looked at from a fixed set of angles, and that a good argument names several of them. You don't have to absorb that over three years of newspapers. You can learn the list in ten minutes and drill it.

> **The method:** run the issue through all eight lenses in 60 seconds. Discard the four that don't apply. The remaining four *are* your body paragraphs, already ordered.
>
> That is the whole technique. It converts "I don't know what to write" into "which four of these eight."

---

## The eight

| # | Lens | The question it asks | Trigger words in your answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⚖️ **Constitutional / Legal** | Which Article, Act, or judgment governs this? | *Article 82 · s.3 of EPA 1986 · Kesavananda · basic structure* |
| 2 | 🏛️ **Federal** | Centre or State? Which List? Who pays, who implements? | *Seventh Schedule · Concurrent List · cooperative federalism · fiscal transfer* |
| 3 | 💰 **Economic** | What does it cost, who bears it, what incentives does it create? | *fiscal burden · crowding out · perverse incentive · opportunity cost* |
| 4 | 👥 **Social equity** | Who gains, who loses — by class, caste, gender, region, tribe, age? | *exclusion error · last mile · intersectional · asymmetric burden* |
| 5 | 🏗️ **Institutional capacity** | *Can the implementing body actually do this?* Staff, data, enforcement | *implementation deficit · vacancy · regulatory capture · capacity constraint* |
| 6 | 🌏 **Environmental / sustainability** | Resource cost, climate, intergenerational equity | *carrying capacity · intergenerational equity · ecosystem services* |
| 7 | 🌐 **International / comparative** | How do other countries handle this? Any treaty obligation? | *the US Senate model · Nordic experience · Paris commitments · WTO-compatible* |
| 8 | 🧭 **Ethical** | Rights vs utility. Means vs ends. Which values collide? | *procedural vs substantive · consent · dignity · public trust* |

## Worked example — delimitation *(this week's issue)*

Watch how fast a full answer appears:

| Lens | What it generates |
|---|---|
| ⚖️ **Constitutional** | Art. 81, 82; 42nd Amendment froze on 1971; 84th (2001) extended to "first census after 2026"; **Art. 368 special majority** — which is exactly why the 131st Bill failed with 298 votes |
| 🏛️ **Federal** | States that controlled population lose weight. LS:RS ratio 2.2:1 → 3.3:1 weakens the states' chamber. Strikes at the federal bargain |
| 💰 **Economic** | Southern states contribute disproportionately to central revenue while facing reduced political weight — the "fiscal representation" grievance |
| 👥 **Social equity** | One-person-one-vote is *currently* violated: a UP and a Kerala voter are not equally represented. Cuts the *other* way from the federal lens |
| 🏗️ **Institutional** | 850 seats needs physical capacity, new constituency machinery, a Delimitation Commission; the women's-reservation linkage adds sequencing risk |
| 🌏 **Environmental** | ❌ Not relevant — **discard** |
| 🌐 **Comparative** | US Senate: equal state representation regardless of population, precisely to protect small states. The standard counter-model |
| 🧭 **Ethical** | Punishing successful policy compliance is a fairness problem — it breaks the promise the 1976 freeze implicitly made |

**Six usable lenses in under a minute.** Pick four for 250 words. Note that lenses 2 and 4 point in *opposite* directions — that tension **is** the answer to a "critically examine" question, and it's precisely what a one-sided answer misses.

## The counter-argument reflex

For every claim you write, ask: **"What is the strongest case against this?"** Not the weakest — the strongest.

This single habit is what "critically" is testing, and it is the hardest thing to learn from reading, because published editorials usually argue *one* side well. You'd have to read two papers with opposite politics on the same day to get what this reflex gives you in one question.

**Drill it:** on any Issue of the Week, write your four points, then write the best objection to each. If an objection is stronger than your point, swap them.

---

## Three structural tensions that generate most GS answers

Almost every GS-II/III question is one of these three arguments wearing a costume. Recognise which, and you know the shape before you know the content:

1. **Efficiency vs Equity** — growth vs distribution, targeting vs universality, merit vs reservation
2. **Liberty vs Security/Order** — privacy vs surveillance, speech vs public order, due process vs preventive detention
3. **Centralisation vs Autonomy** — Centre vs states, national standards vs local fit, uniformity vs diversity

Delimitation is #3. Cash-vs-kind transfers is #1. UAPA is #2.

---

## How to practise this without extra time

**Sunday, 20 min, inside the existing slot.** The Issue of the Week now gives you the tension and the data, and asks *you* to write 150 words. My version stays collapsed until you've written yours. Then compare — not for style, but for **which lenses you missed**.

Missing a lens is a repeatable, fixable error. Vague writing is not. That's why the comparison is lens-by-lens and not line-by-line.
