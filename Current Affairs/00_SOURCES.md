# 🏛️ Source Registry — official & primary sources

**Why this file exists.** A newspaper tells you a thing happened. The ministry's own page tells you **the thing UPSC asks about**: the exact scheme name, the implementing ministry, the launch year, the beneficiary definition, the rank and the denominator.

UPSC sets questions from **official wording**. Coaching material is a lossy copy of these pages, and the loss is always in the columns that get tested.

> **Operating rule:** every compiled item should trace to a primary source where one exists. News reports are the *pointer*; the ministry/body page is the *fact*. When they disagree, the official page wins.

## 📕 Government publications worth reading directly

| Publication | Cadence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic Survey** | Annual, pre-Budget | The single best GS-III document published. Chapter summaries are enough |
| **Union Budget speech + highlights** | Annual, 1 Feb | Read the highlights, never the full documents |
| **India Year Book** | Annual | Reference, not a read-through |
| **Yojana / Kurukshetra** | Monthly | One themed issue = one Mains topic done properly. Phase B onward |
| **Annual Reports** of MoEFCC, MHA, MEA | Annual | Skim only when that theme is being revised |

## ⚠️ Two cautions

**1. Domains drift.** Ministries reorganise and URLs move — this list is a map of *which body owns which fact*, and that part doesn't change. The exact domain is resolved at fetch time; if a link 404s, the body name is what to search for.

**2. Official ≠ neutral.** A ministry page states what a scheme intends, never what it achieved. For **outcomes** you need CAG reports, MoSPI data, or parliamentary committee reports — and that gap between intent and outcome is exactly what good Mains answers are built on. Capture the official version as fact; capture the audit as argument.
