### 1. 🏞️ **Glaw Lake — India's 101st Ramsar Site, and Arunachal Pradesh's first**

The single biggest Prelims item of the day.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ramsar number** | **101st in India** — **Arunachal Pradesh's first** |
| Location | **Lohit district**, near Wakro town |
| Sits inside | **Kamlang Tiger Reserve & Wildlife Sanctuary** |
| Type | High-altitude **freshwater** lake · **1,168 m** · **8 sq km**, 5 km circumference |
| Hydrology | **Headwater source of the perennial Kamlang River** |
| Origin | **Tectonic** — land-block warping typical of the Eastern Himalayan fold belt |
| Communities | **Miju Mishmi, Digaro Mishmi, Idu Mishmi** |

**Biodiversity — and this is the part that makes it examinable:**
- **All four big cats found in India's east: Bengal Tiger, Indian Leopard, Clouded Leopard, Snow Leopard**
- Primates: **Hoolock Gibbon** *(India's only ape)*, Slow Loris, Capped Langur
- Alpine mammals: **Mishmi Takin**, **Red Panda**
- 150+ tree species; **49 wild orchid species**

> 📖 **The story behind it.** "Ramsar" is not an acronym — it's a **town in Iran**, on the Caspian coast, where the Convention on Wetlands was signed in **1971**. That's why the name looks like it should stand for something and doesn't. The convention is also notable for being **the oldest of the modern global environmental treaties**, predating Stockholm 1972. A "Ramsar Site" is a wetland of *international importance*; the strictest listing is the **Montreux Record**, for sites where ecological character has changed or is threatened — India has **two on it: Keoladeo (Rajasthan) and Loktak (Manipur)**.

> **Why it's asked:** new Ramsar sites are asked almost every year — the reliable questions are **which state** and **is it the state's first**. Both are notable here. **Kamlang Tiger Reserve** is worth knowing separately, and the **four-big-cats** fact is a gift of a distinguishing detail. Also link **Mishmi Takin** — the state animal of Arunachal Pradesh, IUCN **Vulnerable**.

**Theme:** Environment · **Static hook:** Ramsar Convention, protected areas, Eastern Himalaya biodiversity hotspot · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/04/upsc-current-affairs-4-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 2. **Critical minerals — the strategic-power story**

Policy analysis on critical minerals as the foundation of clean energy, defence and technology capability.

**The concentration problem, in numbers:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Top 3 refining nations | **86%** of global market share |
| China's share of **rare earth** processing | **90%** |
| China's share of **cobalt** processing | **75%** |
| China's share of **lithium chemicals** | **70%** |
| Projected **copper** shortfall by 2035 | **30%** |
| India's cumulative demand under **Net-Zero 2070** | **~169 million tonnes** |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The phrase "critical mineral" isn't about scarcity in the ground — it's about **concentration of *processing***. Cobalt and lithium exist in many countries; the refining does not. A mineral becomes "critical" when your economy needs it and someone else controls the step that makes it usable. That's why the policy response is about **refining, recycling and overseas acquisition**, not just mining.

> 🏛️ **Bodies involved**
>
> | Body | What it is |
> |---|---|
> | **NCMM** — National Critical Mineral Mission | The umbrella mission |
> | **KABIL** — Khanij Bidesh India Ltd | A **joint-venture PSU** to acquire mineral assets **abroad**. Has secured **15,703 hectares in Argentina** (lithium) |
> | **MMDR Amendment Act** | Lets the **Centre** auction critical mineral blocks — **24** notified |
> | **Quad Critical Minerals Initiative** | India · US · Japan · Australia |
> | **India–US Strategic Critical Minerals Cooperation Framework** | May 2026 |
>
> Plus a **₹1,500 crore recycling incentive scheme**.

> **Why it's asked:** **KABIL is the name to know** — expand it (Khanij Bidesh India Limited), and know its job is **overseas acquisition**, which distinguishes it from domestic bodies like GSI (surveys) and IBM (leases). Note the MMDR amendment shifted critical-mineral auction power **from states to the Centre** — a federalism point for Mains.

### 3. **MSME Development (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — passed by Parliament**

Aimed at easing liquidity and speeding up dispute resolution.

| Change | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Flexible classification** | Removes fixed investment thresholds — the Centre can reclassify by **notification**, on investment *and* turnover |
| **Voluntary digital registration** | Registration becomes voluntary; central digital platform, states may run portals |
| **TReDS mandate** | **CPSEs must settle all MSME procurement invoices through TReDS** |
| **Mediation: 90 days** | Before **MSME Facilitation Councils**, from first hearing |
| **Arbitration: 30 / 90** | Must begin within 30 days if mediation fails; award within 90 days of pleadings |
| **50% interim relief** | If court proceedings exceed 6 months, at least **50%** of the award must be paid to the MSME supplier |
| **Decriminalisation** | Criminal penalties → graded warnings and monetary penalties |
| **Escalating penalties** | Minimum penalties rise **10% every three years** |
| **Adjudication** | **Development Commissioner (MSME)**; appeal to the **MSME Secretary** |

> **Why it's asked:** **TReDS** is the term to own — **Trade Receivables Discounting System**, an **RBI-regulated** electronic platform where MSMEs discount their receivables from large buyers to get paid early. Delayed payments are *the* structural MSME problem, and TReDS is the standard answer. The **decriminalisation** thread also links to the **Jan Vishwas Act** family — the same reform philosophy.

**Theme:** Economy · **Static hook:** MSME policy, ease of doing business · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/04/upsc-current-affairs-4-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 4. **e-Pratyaropan — national organ donation and transplant platform**

Unveiled on the **16th Indian Organ Donation Day**.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** *Pratyaropan* is Sanskrit for **transplantation** — literally "re-planting". The problem it solves is grim and specific: organs are allocated locally and on paper, so a heart available in one city may go unused while a matched patient waits elsewhere, and opacity in allocation is exactly the gap that illegal organ trade exploits. A single national registry with **algorithmic allocation** removes both the delay and the discretion.

| | |
|---|---|
| Ministry | **Health & Family Welfare** |
| Implementing agency | **NOTTO** — National Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisation |
| Legal basis | **THOTA** — Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act |
| Features | Unified national registry (waiting lists, donors, tissue banks, pledges) · **protocol-based algorithmic allocation** · procedure logging within **48 hours** · real-time tracking along **green corridors** |

> **Why it's asked:** **NOTTO** is the body — under the **Directorate General of Health Services, MoHFW**, with ROTTO (regional) and SOTTO (state) tiers beneath it. That three-tier structure is the question. **"Green corridor"** — a cleared traffic route for time-critical organ transport — is a term worth knowing.

**Theme:** Social Issues / Health · **Static hook:** Health governance, THOTA · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/04/upsc-current-affairs-4-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 5. 🗺️ **Nauru is now the Republic of Naoero**

The Pacific island nation officially changed its name.

> 📖 **The story behind it — and this is one of the more remarkable entries you'll meet.** "Nauru" is the anglicised form; **Naoero** is the name in the indigenous **dei-Naoero** language, and the change is a deliberate act of linguistic preservation for a critically endangered tongue.
>
> But the island's real story is **phosphate**. Nauru sits on a raised coral atoll capped with phosphate rock — bird guano compressed over millennia. Twentieth-century **open-cast mining** stripped the interior and left roughly 80% of the island as a moonscape of **jagged limestone pinnacles**, uninhabitable and unfarmable. For a brief period Nauru had among the **highest per-capita incomes on earth**; when the phosphate ran down, that vanished. It is the standard cautionary example of a **resource curse** and of **irreversible environmental degradation for short-term gain** — and it is now among the states most exposed to sea-level rise.

| | |
|---|---|
| Size rank | **Third-smallest sovereign republic** by land area — after Vatican City and Monaco |
| Population | ~**12,000** |
| Capital | **None officially** — government offices at **Yaren District** |
| Location | SW Pacific, **southeastern Micronesia**, ~40 km south of the Equator |
| Geology | Raised **coral limestone** atoll; central phosphate plateau 30–65 m |
| Water | **No rivers or streams** — rainwater catchment and imported freshwater |
| Inland water | **Buada Lagoon** — shallow, brackish |

> **Why it's asked:** "no capital city" and "third-smallest republic" are precisely the oddities Prelims likes. For **Mains**, Nauru is an unusually efficient example — it carries *resource curse*, *irreversible ecological damage*, *small-island climate vulnerability*, and now *language preservation* in one name.

**Theme:** IR / Geography / Environment · **Static hook:** Small island developing states, resource curse · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/04/upsc-current-affairs-4-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 6. 🛕 **UNESCO Intangible Heritage tag sought for the Puri Rath Yatra**

Odisha's CM urged the Centre to nominate the **Shree Jagannath Rath Yatra** for UNESCO's **Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity**. Proposed by the **Shree Jagannatha Temple Administration (SJTA)**.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The astonishing detail is that **the chariots are built new every single year** and then dismantled — nothing is reused. Specific timber (**Phassi** and **Dhausa**) is floated down from **Dasapalla** along the **Mahanadi**, and **hereditary artisan guilds** build them to the **Shilpa Shastras**. So the "heritage" being protected is not an object at all — it is **a set of skills that must be performed annually or lost**. That is exactly what *intangible* cultural heritage means, and why this nomination is the right category.

**The three chariots — learn these, they're directly askable:**

| Chariot | Deity |
|---|---|
| **Nandighosha** | **Lord Jagannath** |
| **Taladhwaja** | **Lord Balabhadra** |
| **Darpadalana** | **Goddess Subhadra** |

**The sequence:** *Pahandi Bije* (ceremonial procession of the deities) along the **Bada Danda** → **seven-day stay at the Gundicha Temple** → **Bahuda Yatra** (return), pausing at the **Mausi Maa Temple** for the **Podo Pitha** offering. Timing: bright fortnight (**Shukla Paksha**) of **Ashadha** (June–July). Textual references in the **Brahma, Padma and Skanda Puranas** and the *Kapila Samhita*.

> **Why it's asked:** **Tangible vs Intangible is the distinction being tested.** A World Heritage *Site* is a place (Sarnath, 2 Aug); Intangible Cultural Heritage is a *practice*. India's ICH entries already include **Kutiyattam, Vedic chanting, Ramlila, Chhau, Kalbelia, Yoga, Kumbh Mela, Durga Puja and Garba** — that list is worth memorising as a set.

**Theme:** Art & Culture · **Static hook:** [History/](../../../History/) — temple traditions, UNESCO ICH · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/04/upsc-current-affairs-4-august-2026/) ⚠️

The President unveiled a statue at **Lajpat Bhawan, New Delhi**.

| | |
|---|---|

That is a genuinely important Polity/History fact, and Gopabandhu Das is how it gets asked.

### 8. **AI and cyber — Agentic AI against Zero Trust**

Analysts warned that autonomous **Agentic AI** is beginning to challenge **Zero Trust** security models.

- Shift from **Generative** AI (produces content) to **Agentic** AI (takes independent operational decisions)
- AI systems discovering and exploiting **zero-day** vulnerabilities faster than human teams
- Autonomous agents bypassing identity checks by **simulating legitimate user behaviour** — which is precisely what Zero Trust assumes it can detect
- Responses proposed: **National AI Governance Guidelines**, mandatory **synthetic content labelling**, Cyber Crisis Management Plans, **human-in-the-loop** requirements, AI red-teaming

> **Why it's asked:** define the two terms cleanly. **Zero Trust** = "never trust, always verify" — no user or device is trusted by default, even inside the network. **Zero-day** = a vulnerability unknown to the vendor, so there is no patch. These get conflated because both start with "zero"; they are unrelated. As a software engineer this is your home ground — but note that UPSC will ask it **shallowly and definitionally**, so resist over-reading.

**Theme:** S&T / Security · **Static hook:** Cybersecurity, AI governance · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/04/upsc-current-affairs-4-august-2026/) ⚠️
