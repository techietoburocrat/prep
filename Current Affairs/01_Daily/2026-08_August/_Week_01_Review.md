## 🧠 Issue of the Week — *Delimitation and the 1971 freeze*

> ✍️ **A writing drill, not a reading item.** Read the brief, run the [eight lenses](../../05_Mains_Material/02_The_Eight_Lenses.md), then **write 150 words by hand on a 12-minute timer.** My version is collapsed at the bottom — opening it first makes the exercise worthless, because you will recognise every point and conclude you knew it. You didn't.

### The question

> **"The freeze on Lok Sabha seats has outlived the purpose it was created for." Critically examine.** *(10 marks, 150 words)*

**Note the directive.** *Critically examine* = all facets **plus an explicit verdict**. An answer that lays out both sides and declines to judge scores badly regardless of content. See [Answer Architecture §1](../../05_Mains_Material/01_Answer_Architecture.md).

### The brief — facts you may use

**The tension.** Lok Sabha seats have been frozen on **1971** population since the **42nd Amendment (1976)**, so states that successfully controlled population would not lose political weight. Unfreezing restores **one-person-one-vote**. Staying frozen protects the **federal bargain**. Both are legitimate constitutional principles and they are irreconcilable — which is why this is a *critically examine* question and not a *discuss* one.

**What happened.** The **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026**, introduced in Lok Sabha on **16 April 2026** by Law Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal, sought to raise the Lok Sabha ceiling from **550 to 850** seats and operationalise the **one-third women's reservation** through a fresh delimitation. It was **negatived on 17 April 2026** ⚠️*(reported 298 for / 230 against)*.

> 🔑 **The detail that makes this a gift.** A bill with a clear *simple* majority still **failed**. **Art. 368** requires a **special majority** — a majority of the *total membership* of the House **and** two-thirds of those *present and voting*. 298 of 528 voting is ~56%, short of the ~352 needed. This is the cleanest live illustration of Art. 368 you will ever get.

**Facts available to you**
- 42nd Amendment (1976) froze seats on the 1971 census; **84th Amendment (2001)** extended the freeze to *"the first census taken after 2026"* — which is why **2026 is the deadline year**
- Proposed ceiling **850** — states 815, UTs 35 ⚠️
- LS:RS ratio would move from ~**2.2:1 to ~3.3:1**
- **UP** gains; **Tamil Nadu, Kerala** lose relative weight

**Static hooks:** Laxmikanth — Parliament (Art. 81, 82), Amendment of the Constitution (Art. 368), Federalism · [Polity/02_Laxmikanth](../../../Polity/02_Laxmikanth/00_INDEX.md)

**Model answer (150 words)**

*Article 82 mandates readjustment of constituencies after every census, but the 42nd Amendment (1976) froze allocation on the 1971 census — a deliberate assurance to states pursuing population control. The 84th Amendment extended this to "the first census after 2026."*

***The freeze has outlived its logic in one respect.*** *Representation now violates one-person-one-vote at scale: constituencies of vastly unequal population return one member each, an inequality no democracy can indefinitely defend on fifty-five-year-old data.*

***It retains force in another.*** *Unfreezing penalises states that complied with national policy — Tamil Nadu and Kerala lose relative weight to Uttar Pradesh. Raising the ceiling to 850 would move the Lok Sabha–Rajya Sabha ratio from 2.2:1 to 3.3:1, weakening the chamber designed to balance states. The 131st Amendment Bill's defeat in April 2026, short of Article 368's special majority, shows this consent does not exist.*

***The purpose has not expired; it has changed.*** *The remedy is to unfreeze totals while capping any state's relative loss — as the US Senate protects small states — rather than to choose one principle over the other.*

**Why this scores:** verdict given in the last paragraph (**directive obeyed**) · both sides run at equal strength (**both sides**) · four named articles and two amendments (**specificity**) · three numbers (**evidence**) · anchored intro, headed body, proposing conclusion (**structure**). That's the [five-criterion rubric](../../05_Mains_Material/03_Issue_Bank.md#the-rubric-i-mark-against).

**Note the conclusion.** *"The purpose has not expired; it has changed"* — a *critically examine* verdict is rarely "yes" or "no". It is usually a **reframe**. That move is worth stealing.
