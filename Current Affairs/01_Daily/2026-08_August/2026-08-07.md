### 1. ⚖️ **The Money Bill controversy — and the irony is the story**

The **Rajya Sabha passed the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026** as a **Money Bill**, raising sanctioned judicial strength from **34 to 38** (including the CJI). This reignited the long-running fight over the Money Bill route.

**The constitutional machinery:**

| Provision | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Art. 110(1)** | Defines a Money Bill — taxation, borrowing, appropriation from the **Consolidated Fund**, financial obligations |
| **Art. 110(1)(g)** | **"any matter incidental to"** the above — *the contested clause* |
| **Art. 110(3)** | **The Lok Sabha Speaker's certification is final** |
| **Art. 109** | The special procedure for Money Bills |

**What the Rajya Sabha cannot do with a Money Bill:** reject it · amend it · trigger a joint sitting. It gets **14 days** to return it with recommendations the Lok Sabha may ignore. Presidential recommendation is required before introduction.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The Money Bill route exists for a sound reason: the Lok Sabha is directly elected, so control of the public purse belongs to it — the Rajya Sabha, which is indirectly elected, should not block supply. **Art. 110(1)(g)** — "any matter incidental to" — was meant as a small housekeeping clause.
>
> It has become the crack in the wall. Because the Speaker's certification is **final** under Art. 110(3), classifying a bill as a Money Bill is close to unreviewable in practice, and legislation with substantial non-financial content has been routed this way — the **Aadhaar Act, 2016**, **PMLA amendments**, and the **Tribunals Reforms** framework are all pending before the Supreme Court on exactly this ground. **Justice D. Y. Chandrachud's dissent in the Aadhaar case called the misuse "a fraud on the Constitution."** A **seven-judge Constitution Bench** is now tasked with settling the test.
>
> **And the irony here is exquisite:** a bill about *the Supreme Court's own strength* has been passed by the route the Supreme Court is currently examining.

> **Why it's asked:** Money Bill vs **Financial Bill** vs Ordinary Bill is a permanent Prelims favourite. The distinguishing facts: **only the Speaker certifies**, **only Lok Sabha can introduce**, **Rajya Sabha has 14 days and no veto**, **no joint sitting**. The defence of *this* bill is that judges' salaries are charged on the **Consolidated Fund** — so there is a genuine financial nexus. The criticism is that raising judicial strength is a structural change wearing a financial costume. **Both sides belong in your answer.**

### 2. ⛈️ **Heatwaves and lightning added to the notified natural calamities list**

The **Ministry of Home Affairs** included both in the **SDRF/NDRF operational guidelines for 2026–31** — expanding the list from **12 to 14** categories.

| | |
|---|---|
| Lightning deaths | **2,000–2,500 a year** — around **35% of all natural-disaster deaths in India** |
| **Rural share of lightning deaths** | **96%** |
| Heatstroke cases | **4,853+** reported March–July 2026 |
| Ex-gratia cost | **₹12–15 crore** a year per affected state |
| Lightning-prone | **Odisha, Jharkhand, Bihar, West Bengal** |
| Heatwave-prone | **Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, UP, MP** |
| Law | **Disaster Management Act, 2005** |
| Funds | **SDRF — 80% response, 20% mitigation**; NDRF as backup |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** Being on this list is not symbolic — it is the switch that releases money. A "notified" calamity is one for which a state may draw on the **SDRF** for relief and ex-gratia; anything not notified must be paid for from the state's own budget. Lightning has killed more Indians annually than floods or cyclones for years, and yet sat outside the list because it kills **one or two people at a time, in rural fields, spread across the country** — it never produces the single visible catastrophe that drives policy. The change is a recognition that **diffuse, chronic killers deserve the same machinery as spectacular ones.**

> **Why it's asked:** the **12 → 14** expansion is directly askable. Know the **SDRF/NDRF distinction**: SDRF is the state's first-response fund (Centre contributes **75%** for general states, **90%** for NE and Himalayan states); NDRF supplements it when SDRF is inadequate. Both flow from the **DM Act, 2005**, with **NDMA chaired by the Prime Minister**.

### 3. 👵 **NITI Aayog's "Reimagining Care" report**

A landmark report proposing professionalisation of India's caregiving workforce, framed to **Viksit Bharat@2047**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Elderly population | **149 million (10.5%)** in 2022 → projected **347 million (20.8%) by 2050** |
| Persons with disabilities | **26.8 million (2.21%)** |
| **Unpaid care work** | **Women 299 min/day vs men 97 min/day** |
| Care market | **USD 29.62 bn** (2023) → **USD 72.31 bn** by 2030, **13.76% CAGR** |
| Key proposal | **National Caregiver Council (NCC)** under **Social Justice & Empowerment** |
| Standardisation | Align with **NSQF** and **Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)** |
| Models cited | **Japan** (Long-Term Care Insurance, SSW visa) · **Germany** (dual financing, family leave) · **China** (8-level certification, AI) |
| Related law | **RPwD Act, 2016** |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The number to sit with is **299 minutes against 97**. Indian women do roughly **three times** the unpaid care work men do — and because it is unpaid it appears in **no GDP figure, no employment statistic, and no pension entitlement**. That invisibility is the actual policy problem: you cannot fund, train or regulate work the statistical system does not see. Professionalising care converts invisible household labour into counted, paid, skilled employment — which is simultaneously an **ageing** policy, a **women's workforce participation** policy, and a **jobs** policy. Very few interventions do three at once.

> **Why it's asked:** **NITI Aayog is an executive body** — a Cabinet resolution of 2015, not statutory *(see [00_INSTITUTIONS](../../00_INSTITUTIONS.md) §3)*. Its reports are recommendations, not directions. The **299 vs 97** figure and the **10.5% → 20.8%** ageing projection are among the most quotable numbers you will collect this month.

### 4. ♻️ **GOBARdhan approved as a national circular bioenergy scheme**

Cabinet approved **GOBARdhan** — **G**alvanizing **O**rganic **B**io-**A**gro **R**esources **Dhan** — unifying the compressed biogas value chain.

| | |
|---|---|
| Ministry | **Petroleum & Natural Gas** |
| Outlay | **₹23,731 crore** |
| Period | **10 years** — FY 2026-27 to 2035-36 |
| **Subsumes** | **SATAT**, Market Development Assistance, Biomass Aggregation Machinery, National Bioenergy Programme |
| **CBG blending targets** | **3%** (FY27) → **4%** (FY28) → **5%** (FY29 onwards) |
| Administered CBG price | **₹2,110 per MMBTU**, held 10 years |
| Capital assistance | up to **₹2 crore per tonne-per-day** of capacity |
| Feedstock | Agri residues, **cattle dung**, press mud, municipal solid waste |
| Outputs | **CBG** + **Fermented Organic Manure (FOM)** |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The scheme's design solves a problem that killed earlier biogas efforts: **nobody would build a plant without a guaranteed buyer and a stable price.** By fixing the CBG price for ten years *and* mandating a rising blending percentage, the government has manufactured both. Note also the **two outputs** — the plant sells gas *and* fermented organic manure, so the same cattle dung generates energy and returns nutrients to soil. That closed loop is why it's called a **circular** bioenergy scheme, and it's the reason the item belongs as much to agriculture as to energy.

> **Why it's asked:** **the ministry is the trap** — a scheme about cattle dung sits under **Petroleum & Natural Gas**, not Agriculture or Jal Shakti. *(GOBARdhan previously ran under the Department of Drinking Water & Sanitation — the shift is itself the news.)* Know that it **subsumes SATAT** — "which scheme replaced X" is a standing question format. **CBG** = Compressed Bio-Gas, chemically near-identical to CNG.

### 5. ✈️ **Sqn Ldr Bhawana Kanth — India's first woman Fighter Combat Leader**

Completed the **Fighter Combat Leader (FCL)** course at **TACDE, Gwalior**.

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **2016** | Among India's **first three women fighter pilots** |
| **2019** | **First woman** qualified for day fighter combat missions |
| **2026** | **First woman Fighter Combat Leader** |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** **TACDE** — the Tactics and Air Combat Development Establishment — is often described as the IAF's equivalent of Top Gun, and the **FCL** qualification is not a flying licence but a *leadership* one: it certifies you to plan and lead a mission package of multiple aircraft into contested airspace. So this is a step beyond "can fly a fighter" to "can command others in combat." That is why the 2019 and 2026 milestones are genuinely different achievements rather than the same story told twice.

> **Why it's asked:** "first woman to…" is standard, but the more useful thread is the **policy timeline** — women were inducted into the fighter stream on an experimental basis in **2016**, made permanent later, and the **Supreme Court's rulings on permanent commission for women officers** (2020) sit alongside this. That is the Mains angle: individual achievement as evidence of institutional change.

### 6. 🐅 **Satpura–Melghat tiger corridor — railway line cleared**

The **Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife (SC-NBWL)** gave in-principle clearance to a **Central Railway third-line** project (Jujharpur–Chichonda) needing **200+ hectares** of forest inside the corridor.

| | |
|---|---|
| Corridor size | **7,325 sq km** |
| States | **MP** (Betul, Narmadapuram) · **Maharashtra** (Amravati) |
| MP protected areas | **Satpura NP**, Bori Sanctuary, Pachmarhi Sanctuary |
| Maharashtra | **Gugamal NP**, Wan, Narnala, Ambabarwa Sanctuaries |
| Natural habitat | **60%+** tropical dry and moist deciduous forest |
| Wildlife | Tiger, leopard, sloth bear, striped hyena, **forest owlet** |
| Communities | ~**54%** local population — **Korku and Gond** tribes |
| Function | Prevents **genetic inbreeding**; inter-landscape connectivity |

> **Why it's asked:** the **NBWL** is the body — **statutory** under the **Wildlife Protection Act, 1972**, **chaired by the Prime Minister**, with its **Standing Committee** chaired by the Environment Minister doing the actual clearances. That structure is directly askable. The **forest owlet** is worth knowing separately: *Athene blewitti*, rediscovered in 1997 after being presumed extinct for over a century, and largely confined to this Satpura–Melghat landscape. **Corridors, not reserves, are the current conservation frontier** — a reserve protects animals, a corridor protects their gene flow.

### 7. 🗿 **A hero stone dates a Hoysala king's death to 11 December 1141**

A 12th-century **veeragallu** unearthed at **Neeragunda village, Arsikere taluk, Karnataka**, carries an Old Kannada inscription fixing **King Vishnuvardhana's** death — resolving a long scholarly debate.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** **Hero stones** are among the most human artefacts in Indian archaeology. A *veeragallu* (Kannada; also **nadukal** in Tamil, *nandikal* elsewhere) commemorates someone who **died protecting the community** — and the categories are startlingly specific: **Gograhana** (defending cattle from raiders), **Turugol** (rescuing livestock), **Penbuyyal** (protecting women). They were raised at village borders, sacred groves, tank bunds and temple precincts, and they run from the **Iron Age and Sangam period (3rd century BCE)** to a peak in the **5th–14th centuries CE**.
>
> Their **three-panel grammar** is consistent enough to read like a sentence: **bottom** — the battle in which he died; **middle** — celestial *apsaras* carrying his soul upward; **top** — the hero worshipping a deity, having arrived. **Sun and moon at the apex** mean "as long as sun and moon endure" — eternal glory.
>
> This stone also records the **Garuda tradition** — royal bodyguards who took their own lives on their king's death.

> **Why it's asked:** hero stones appear in Art & Culture as a recurring category, and the **regional naming** (veeragallu / nadukal) plus the **dynasties** (Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Gangas, Kadambas, **Hoysalas**) are the askable parts. The **Hoysalas** are separately important — **Vishnuvardhana** built the **Chennakeshava temple at Belur**, and Hoysala temples at **Belur, Halebidu and Somanathapura** were inscribed as a **UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023**.

### 8. 🗺️ **Ceuta — Spain's North African enclave, and migration as leverage**

A large migrant influx at **Ceuta** triggered military deployment and renewed EU debate on border security and asylum.

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | **Autonomous city and exclave of Spain**, on the **North African coast** |
| Borders | **Morocco** |
| Position | At the **Strait of Gibraltar** — the Mediterranean–Atlantic junction |
| Area | **~5 sq km** · population **~85,000** |
| Terrain | **La Almina peninsula**, **Mount Hacho** |
| Border | **8 km** militarised fence — sensors, razor wire, watchtowers |
| **Special status** | **Free port, outside the EU Customs Union — but inside Schengen** |
| Communities | Spanish Christians · Arab Muslims (**Ceuta Darija**) · Sephardic Jews · **Sindhi Hindus** |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** Ceuta is a piece of Europe **on the African mainland** — one of two Spanish enclaves there, with **Melilla**. That makes its 8 km fence not a national border but **the EU's land frontier with Africa**, which is why a local crowd crossing becomes a continental political event. And because Morocco controls the approach, relaxing or tightening its own policing there functions as diplomatic pressure on Madrid — the reason "**weaponisation of migration**" is the phrase attached to Ceuta.

> **Why it's asked:** the **Strait of Gibraltar** is the map anchor — it separates **Spain from Morocco** and links the **Atlantic to the Mediterranean**. Ceuta's oddity — **in Schengen but outside the EU customs union** — is exactly the kind of exception options are built on. Note the **Sindhi Hindu** community: a small, genuinely surprising Indian-diaspora link.
