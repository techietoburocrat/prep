## ⏪ First — your Pitch Black question, answered

You asked why it's called that. Good instinct, and the answer is a real one:

> 📖 **Exercise Pitch Black** was first held in **June 1981** from **RAAF Base Williamtown, New South Wales** — a modest **three-day** air defence exercise. The name refers to its founding emphasis on **night flying over vast unpopulated terrain**. *(A common embellishment says it was flown on moonless nights — that part is not true.)* From three days it has grown into the RAAF's largest multinational exercise, now **biennial** and based at **Darwin**, Northern Territory.
>
> **Why this helps you:** you now have a hook. **Pitch Black = darkness = night flying = Australia.** You will not confuse it with a naval exercise again — which is exactly the option-swap UPSC uses.

**Source:** [RAAF](https://www.airforce.gov.au/our-work/exercises/exercise-pitch-black)

## 🔴 TIER 1 — must know

### 1. **Europe's wildfires — "climate whiplash" and fire-made thunderstorms**

Intense wildfires across **Greece, Spain, France, Portugal and Italy** destroyed hundreds of thousands of hectares and triggered record peacetime evacuations.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** Two ideas here are worth more than the event itself.
> **Climate whiplash** — rapid swings from very wet seasons to severe drought. The wet season grows dense vegetation; the drought turns it into fuel. The *sequence* is the danger, not either condition alone.
> **PyroCb (pyrocumulonimbus)** — a thunderstorm *made by the fire itself*. Heat drives a plume to **10–15 km**, which builds a storm cloud that generates its own **lightning and erratic winds** — starting new fires and steering the old one unpredictably. A fire that makes its own weather.
> **The fire-suppression paradox** — decades of successfully putting out *small* fires let fuel accumulate, so the fire that finally escapes is far worse. Good intentions, worse outcome.

| | |
|---|---|
| Cost to the EU | **€2.5 billion** annually ⚠️ |
| Class | "Sixth-generation" wildfires — those that generate their own weather systems |
| Contributing causes | Atmospheric warming, rural land abandonment, unmanaged fuel loads |

> **Why it's asked:** **PyroCb** is a named, definable phenomenon — exactly the kind of term Prelims likes ("consider the following statements about pyrocumulonimbus clouds"). And the **fire-suppression paradox** is a ready-made Mains point about unintended consequences of well-meant policy.

**Theme:** Environment · **Static hook:** GS-I geophysical phenomena, climate change · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/03/upsc-current-affairs-3-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 3. **SBSTTA-28 concludes in Nairobi**

The **28th session of the Subsidiary Body on Scientific, Technical and Technological Advice** concluded **1 August 2026** in **Nairobi, Kenya**, ahead of **CBD COP17 in Yerevan, Armenia**.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** A treaty needs somewhere for the *science* to be argued before the *politics* happens. SBSTTA is that room. The Convention on Biological Diversity created it in its own text — **Article 25** — as an open-ended, multidisciplinary scientific advisory body reporting to the COP. When you read "SBSTTA recommended…", read it as: *the scientists have prepared the option; the parties will now decide.* This session covered **synthetic biology** risk-benefit, biodiversity finance, and implementation gaps.

> 🏛️ **Bodies involved** — the five fields
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Full form** | Subsidiary Body on Scientific, Technical and Technological Advice |
> | **Type + instrument** | Treaty body — **Article 25 of the CBD** |
> | **Parent** | **CBD Secretariat**, with **UNEP** |
> | **Reports to** | The **Conference of the Parties (COP)** |
> | **Distinguisher** | Advises; does **not** decide. The COP decides |
>
> **Siblings, and don't mix them up:** **IPBES** is the *independent* science-policy platform on biodiversity — often called "the IPCC for biodiversity" — and is **not** a CBD body. **SBSTA** *(no second T)* is the equivalent body under the **UNFCCC**, for climate. One letter apart, different treaties.

**Framework in play:** the **Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework** — **23 targets by 2030**, adopted at COP15.

> **Why it's asked:** the CBD family is asked regularly, and the reliable trap is **SBSTTA vs SBSTA vs IPBES**. Also know the CBD's protocols: **Cartagena** (biosafety, living modified organisms) and **Nagoya** (access and benefit-sharing).

**Theme:** Environment / IR · **Static hook:** CBD, biodiversity conventions · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/03/upsc-current-affairs-3-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 4. **Exercise ROTOR CLAP III — counter-drone, at Pokhran**

The IAF concluded a week-long **rotary-wing counter-drone** exercise at the **Pokhran Field Firing Range, Jaisalmer, Rajasthan**.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** Read the name literally: **"Rotor"** = rotary-wing, i.e. helicopters, not fighter jets. **"Clap"** = the swatting-down of something small. It is a **third-edition, India-only** drill — no foreign participants — built for one specific and very current problem: **cheap drones crossing the western border** carrying contraband, weapons, or surveillance payloads. Fighter jets are useless against a slow object at 200 feet. Helicopters are not.

| | |
|---|---|
| Theme | **Counter-UAS** (Counter-Unmanned Aerial Systems) |
| Platforms | **Apache**, **Prachand** (indigenous LCH), Mi-25/35 gunships; **Chinook** transport |
| Location | Pokhran range, western desert sector |
| Scope | **Indigenous only** — no foreign participation |

> **Why it's asked:** contrast it with **Pitch Black** above and the pattern locks in — *host, service branch, and whether it's multinational or national*. ROTOR CLAP is **India-only, air force, rotary-wing**. **Prachand** is worth knowing separately: India's indigenous **Light Combat Helicopter**, built by **HAL**, the only attack helicopter that can operate at Siachen altitudes.

**Theme:** Security · **Static hook:** Defence exercises, drone warfare · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/03/upsc-current-affairs-3-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 5. **SHANTI framework — a rules-based maritime order in the Bay of Bengal**

> **Why it's asked:** the vehicle is the question. **BIMSTEC** = Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation — **seven members**: India, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand. **Secretariat in Dhaka.** The classic option tests who is *not* a member — **Pakistan and Maldives are not**, and that absence is precisely why India favours BIMSTEC over SAARC. ### 6. 🗺️ **India–Rwanda Joint Trade Committee — first session, New Delhi**

An institutional mechanism established for bilateral commerce.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** Rwanda is called the **"Land of a Thousand Hills"** — and that isn't tourism copy, it's geology. The country sits on the **Albertine Rift**, the western branch of the **East African Rift System**, where the continent is slowly pulling apart. That rifting produced the hills, the volcanoes and the deep lakes.

| | |
|---|---|
| Location | East/Central Africa, just south of the Equator · **landlocked** |
| Capital | **Kigali** |
| Neighbours | **Uganda · Tanzania · Burundi · DR Congo** |
| Highest point | **Mount Karisimbi, 4,507 m** — in the **Virunga** volcanic range, mountain gorilla habitat |
| The divide | The **Congo–Nile divide** runs north–south through it: eastern waters go to the **Nile** via the **Kagera**; western waters to the **Congo** via **Lake Kivu** |
| Strategic | Supplier of **3T minerals** — **tin, tungsten, tantalum (coltan)** — for electronics and clean energy |

> **Why it's asked:** two high-value facts. **Lake Kivu** is one of only three lakes on Earth with dissolved **methane** at depth — a limnic-eruption hazard and an energy resource at once. And **coltan/tantalum** puts Rwanda directly inside the **critical minerals** supply-chain debate, which is one of the most examinable themes going. Also: Rwanda sits in **EAC, COMESA and AfCFTA**.

**Theme:** IR / Geography · **Static hook:** African geography, critical minerals · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/03/upsc-current-affairs-3-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 7. **Fresh survey identifies zero manual scavengers**

The government told Parliament that a nationwide survey under the **Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013** identified **no** manual scavengers.

| | |
|---|---|
| Validated instead | **89,915 Sewer and Septic Tank Workers (SSWs)** |
| Scheme | **NAMASTE** — PPE kits, safety training, **ERSUs** (Emergency Response Support Units), mechanised equipment |
| Support | Health insurance, capital subsidy for machinery, backing for **PSSOs** (Private Sanitation Service Operators) |
| Related | **SBM-U 2.0** — mechanised sewer and septic-tank cleaning |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The law draws a line most people miss. **"Manual scavenger"** under the 2013 Act means someone manually handling *untreated human excreta* — that is prohibited outright. **"Sewer and septic tank worker"** is a different, *legal* category, provided the work is mechanised and protected. So "zero manual scavengers, 89,915 SSWs" is a statement about **definitions** as much as about reality — which is exactly why it's contested.

> **Why it's asked:** for **Mains**, this is a gift — the gap between a legal category and a lived reality, with the state's own numbers on both sides. It's a strong GS-II welfare-delivery and GS-IV dignity-of-labour example. Deaths continue to be reported in sewer cleaning even where no manual scavengers are officially identified: *that tension is the answer.*

**Theme:** Social Issues · **Static hook:** Social justice, Art. 17 (untouchability), dignity of labour · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/03/upsc-current-affairs-3-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 8. 🏛️ **Geological Survey of India — the full institutional picture**

Following Varsha Aglawe's appointment as 54th DG *(see [2 Aug](2026-08-02.md))*, here is the body itself — and your first worked example of the [five-field capture](../../00_INSTITUTIONS.md#4️⃣-the-capture-habit--five-fields-every-time).

> 📖 **The story behind it.** GSI exists because of **coal and railways**. The East India Company needed domestic coal to run the railways it was laying, so from **1836–1848** it ran coal investigations, formally named the **Geological Survey of India** in **1848** and established it on **4 March 1851** under **Sir Thomas Oldham**, its first Director. An organisation created to find fuel for trains is now, 176 years on, the body assessing India's **lithium, cobalt and rare-earth** reserves — the fuel of the next transition. Same mandate, different century.

> 🏛️ **The five fields**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Full form** | Geological Survey of India |
> | **Type + instrument** | **Attached office** — not statutory, not constitutional |
> | **Parent** | **Ministry of Mines** |
> | **Head** | Director General — **Varsha Ashok Aglawe**, 54th, first woman in 176 years |
> | **Distinguisher** | **Surveys and maps.** It does not grant leases — that's IBM |
>
> **Siblings:** **IBM** (Indian Bureau of Mines) regulates mining leases and conservation, also under Mines. **Survey of India** does *topographic* mapping and sits under the **DST**, not Mines — a standing trap.

**Founded** 1851 · **HQ** Kolkata · **Now specialising in** polar studies, palaeontology, climate-change geology, **critical minerals**

**Theme:** Institutions / Economy · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/03/upsc-current-affairs-3-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 9. **RBI's Monetary Policy Committee begins its three-day meeting**

Began **3 August**; decision announced **5 August**. Reviews the repo rate against inflation, liquidity and growth.

> **Why it's asked:** the *decision* changes; the **structure** doesn't, and the structure is what's tested. **MPC = 6 members** — 3 from the RBI (Governor, a Deputy Governor, one RBI officer) and **3 appointed by the Centre**. The **Governor has the casting vote**. It must meet **at least four times a year**. The inflation target is **4%, band ±2%**, set by the Centre in consultation with the RBI every five years.

**Theme:** Economy · **Static hook:** Monetary policy, RBI Act 1934 · **Source:** [search roundup](https://www.freejobalert.com/articles/daily-current-affairs-3-august-2026-10360) ⚠️

### 10. **Project SARATHI reviewed for nationwide expansion**

The Union Health Minister chaired a review of **Project SARATHI** across central government hospitals.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** **SARATHI** = **S**tudents' **A**lliance for **R**esponsible **A**ction to **T**ransform **H**ealthcare **I**nstitutes — and the acronym is doing deliberate work. A *sarathi* is a **charioteer**: the one who guides, not the one who fights. That's precisely the design — student volunteers help patients **navigate** a large public hospital (finding the OPD, registering, locating the lab, creating an **ABHA** health ID) and are **explicitly barred from any clinical, medical or administrative role**. It is a wayfinding service, not free labour.

**Volunteers** from NSS units and nursing trainees, routed via the **MY Bharat** portal *(Viksit Bharat Youth Network)*.

> **Why it's asked:** the **ministry split** is the trap — the health project runs through **MY Bharat**, which sits under the **Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports**, not Health. Also worth knowing: **ABHA** (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) is the health-ID layer of the **Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission**, distinct from **PM-JAY**, the insurance scheme.

**Theme:** Social Issues & Schemes · **Static hook:** Health governance, Ayushman Bharat · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/03/upsc-current-affairs-3-august-2026/) ⚠️
