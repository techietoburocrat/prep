## 🔎 A source conflict caught — and it's instructive

Two compilations disagree on **where Glaw Lake is** *(the 101st Ramsar site, from [4 Aug](2026-08-04.md))*:

| Source | Claim |
|---|---|
| Insights | **Lohit district** |
| AffairsCloud | **Tawang district** |

**Go with Lohit.** Both sources agree the lake lies inside **Kamlang Tiger Reserve**, and Kamlang is in the **Lohit/Namsai belt of eastern Arunachal**, near Wakro. **Tawang is in the extreme west of the state**, hundreds of kilometres away — the two cannot both be true, and the tiger reserve anchors it. ⚠️ *Still worth confirming against the Ramsar Secretariat listing when it publishes.*

> 🔑 **Why I'm showing you this rather than quietly picking one.** This is the exact failure mode the ⚠️ flags exist for. A wrong district in a map question is a full negative mark, and the aggregators copy each other's errors. **When two sources disagree, the tiebreaker is the fact that can't move** — here, the tiger reserve's location. Learn that habit; it will save you more than once.

### 1. **RBI holds the repo rate at 5.25% — unanimously**

The MPC's decision, announced 5 August.

| Rate | Level |
|---|---|
| **Repo rate** | **5.25%** — unchanged, **4th consecutive pause** |

| **Stance** | **Neutral**, retained |

Governor **Sanjay Malhotra**: the RBI is *"neither dovish nor hawkish"* and will continue to be guided by **headline inflation**. Backdrop: global uncertainty linked to the West Asia conflict.

Note also that a **unanimous** vote is itself notable; split votes (4-2, 5-1) are common and get reported.

| | |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The EPI is produced by **Yale and Columbia universities** — it is an **academic index, not a UN or governmental one**, and India has publicly disputed its methodology in past editions, arguing the indicators are weighted toward wealthy-country priorities and penalise developing economies for their emissions trajectory rather than their historical contribution. > **Why it's asked:** **the publisher is the question** — Yale/Columbia, not UNEP. Rank *and* denominator both matter: "176 of 177" is the fact; "176th" alone is useless. Pair it with India's counter-argument for a balanced Mains paragraph rather than just quoting the rank.

### 3. 🦋 **India's Lepidoptera catalogue completed — 22 volumes**

India's first comprehensive catalogue of moths and butterflies.

| | |
|---|---|
| Volumes | **22** |
| Total species | **13,703** |
| Butterflies | **1,417** |
| Moths | **12,286** |
| Share of global Lepidoptera diversity | **8.25%** |
| Largest moth superfamily | **Geometroidea** — 2,205 species |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** Look at the split: **moths outnumber butterflies roughly nine to one.** Almost everyone assumes the reverse, because butterflies are diurnal and conspicuous and moths are not. Taxonomically, butterflies are simply a *daytime subset* of Lepidoptera — there is no deep evolutionary wall between them. A catalogue like this takes decades because most of those 12,286 moths were described piecemeal across colonial-era and post-Independence literature, in journals scattered across continents; the work is as much *archival consolidation* as field discovery.

> **Why it's asked:** the compiling body is almost certainly the **Zoological Survey of India (ZSI)** — Kolkata, under **MoEFCC**, founded **1916**. Its siblings: **BSI** (Botanical Survey, plants, 1890) and **FSI** (Forest Survey, forest cover, Dehradun). That trio recurs; see [00_INSTITUTIONS](../../00_INSTITUTIONS.md). The **8.25% of global diversity** figure is quotable in any biodiversity answer. ⚠️ *Attribution to ZSI is inference — confirm.*

**Theme:** Environment · **Static hook:** Biodiversity, survey institutions · **Source:** [AffairsCloud](https://affairscloud.com/current-affairs-5-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 4. 🐆 **Clouded Leopard Conservation Action Plan**

Identifies **14 priority conservation landscapes** across **Northeast India**. Monitoring via **eDNA**, camera traps and genetic sampling; focus on habitat restoration and community engagement.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The clouded leopard is the **evolutionary bridge between big cats and small cats** — it has the longest canine teeth relative to skull size of any living cat, and it can **climb down a tree head-first**, rotating its ankles like a squirrel. It is neither a true "big cat" (it cannot roar) nor a small one. **Meghalaya's state animal**, and the reason India can claim four cat species in one landscape — as at **Glaw Lake**, above.

> **Why it's asked:** **eDNA** — *environmental DNA*, genetic material recovered from soil or water without ever seeing the animal — is a term worth owning; it's increasingly how surveys are done. IUCN status **Vulnerable**; **WPA Schedule I**. Do not confuse it with the **snow leopard** (Project Snow Leopard, Himalayan) or the **Indian leopard** (widespread).

**Theme:** Environment · **Static hook:** Wildlife conservation, WPA 1972 · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/05/upsc-current-affairs-5-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 5. 🐖 **India's indigenous African Swine Fever vaccine**

Developed by **ICAR-NIHSAD, Bhopal**.

- **First MA-104 cell-line-based live-attenuated vaccine** for **ASFV Genotype II**
- Substantially lower manufacturing cost than foreign alternatives

> 📖 **The story behind it.** African Swine Fever has **no cure and near-100% mortality** in pigs; outbreaks are controlled by culling entire herds, which is economically devastating in the Northeast where pig rearing is a mainstay of tribal household income. It does **not** infect humans — the damage is purely economic and nutritional. A vaccine has been extraordinarily hard to make because the virus is enormous and complex compared with typical vaccine targets, which is why "first cell-line-based" is the newsworthy phrase.

> 🏛️ **The body:** **NIHSAD** = National Institute of High Security Animal Diseases, Bhopal — India's **highest-containment animal disease lab (BSL-4)**, under **ICAR**. It is the designated national referral lab for exotic and emerging animal diseases. ⚠️

> **Why it's asked:** ICAR institutes come up regularly, and NIHSAD is the one to know for animal disease. Note the pattern: **ICAR** (agriculture) · **ICMR** (medical) · **CSIR** (industrial/scientific) — three research councils, three parent ministries.

**Theme:** S&T / Agriculture · **Static hook:** Animal husbandry, research councils · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/05/upsc-current-affairs-5-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 6. 🌌 **COSPAR Vikram Sarabhai Medal 2026 — Prof. Annapurni Subramaniam**

Awarded to **Prof. Annapurni Subramaniam**, **Indian Institute of Astrophysics** — the **first Indian woman** to receive it.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** **COSPAR** = the **Committee on Space Research**, formed in **1958** under the International Council for Science, in the immediate aftermath of Sputnik — created so that space science could continue as an international scientific enterprise across the Cold War divide. The **Vikram Sarabhai Medal** is its award for **outstanding space research in a developing country**, established jointly with ISRO in honour of the father of India's space programme. So the medal itself encodes an idea: that space science is not the preserve of wealthy states.

> **Why it's asked:** "first Indian woman to…" is standard awards-table material. Also fix **Vikram Sarabhai** himself: founder of **ISRO** and of **PRL (Physical Research Laboratory), Ahmedabad**; distinguish him from **Homi Bhabha** (nuclear programme, TIFR/BARC). The **Indian Institute of Astrophysics** is in **Bengaluru**, an autonomous institute under the **DST**.

**Theme:** S&T / Awards · **Static hook:** Space science, scientific institutions · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/05/upsc-current-affairs-5-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 7. 💧 **Water bodies geo-tagged; Atal Bhujal Yojana progress**

| | |
|---|---|
| Water bodies identified and **geo-tagged** | **24.2 lakh** |
| **Atal Bhujal Yojana** coverage | **8,203 water-stressed Gram Panchayats** across **80 districts** |
| Water conservation works completed | **over 2.0 crore**, through scheme convergence |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** Atal Bhujal Yojana is unusual in Indian water policy because it is a **demand-side** scheme. Most water programmes build supply — dams, tanks, recharge structures. Atal Bhujal instead makes **gram panchayats prepare their own water budgets** and pays them for *measured reductions in extraction*. India is the world's largest groundwater user, and the binding constraint is behaviour, not infrastructure. Funding is **World Bank-assisted**, and disbursement is tied to results.

> **Why it's asked:** the distinguishing fact is exactly that — **demand-side, community-led, incentive-linked**, under the **Ministry of Jal Shakti**. Contrast it with **Jal Jeevan Mission** (household tap connections — supply-side) and the **Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation, AMRUT** (urban infrastructure). Three "Atal/Jal" names that get swapped in options.

**Theme:** Environment / Governance · **Static hook:** Water resources, Jal Shakti · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/05/upsc-current-affairs-5-august-2026/) ⚠️
