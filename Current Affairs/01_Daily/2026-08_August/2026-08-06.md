### 1. 🏛️ **The 16th Finance Commission report — the year's biggest federalism story**

FC-16's recommendations for **2026–31** have opened a serious Centre–State debate.

| | FC-15 | **FC-16** |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertical devolution** (states' share of the divisible pool) | 41% | **41% — maintained** |
| **Grants-in-aid share** | 19.4% | **8.3% — more than halved** |
| Total recommended grants | — | **₹9.47 lakh crore** |
| Local body funding | — | **₹7.2 lakh crore**, with performance-linked conditions |

**The horizontal formula changed too** — how the states' pool is split *between* states:
- **New: 10% weight for a state's contribution to GDP** — rewarding economic performance
- **Income distance weight cut from 45% to 42.5%** — reducing the weight given to being poorer

**Result: eight states face a simultaneous fall in both devolution and grants.**

> 📖 **The story behind it.** A Finance Commission does two separate things, and confusing them is the standard error. **Vertical devolution** = how much of the central tax pool goes to states *collectively*. **Horizontal distribution** = how that pool is divided *among* states. The headline "41% maintained" sounds like continuity — but the **grants-in-aid halving** and the **new GDP-contribution weight** together shift real money from poorer states to richer ones. That is the whole controversy: the Commission moved toward **efficiency** and away from **equity**.

> **Why it's asked:** **Article 280** constitutes the Finance Commission — it is a **constitutional body**, appointed by the President every five years *(contrast NITI Aayog: executive — see [00_INSTITUTIONS](../../00_INSTITUTIONS.md))*. **Article 275** covers grants-in-aid. Know that grants-in-aid are *distinct from* tax devolution, and that only devolution comes from the **divisible pool**. **Cesses and surcharges are outside the divisible pool** — which is the single most-cited state grievance and the most reliable exam angle.

### 2. 🦁 **The Asiatic lion — a conservation success now facing a mining proposal**

A proposal to divert **75 hectares** of reserved forest in **Jafrabad Taluka (Babarkot corridor)** for **limestone mining** has raised alarm.

| | |
|---|---|
| Species | ***Panthera leo persica*** |
| Population | ~100–150 (1960s) → **891** *(16th Lion Census, 2025)* |
| **Outside protected areas** | **56% — 507 individuals** |
| Range | 30,000 sq km (2020) → **35,000+ sq km** (2025) |
| **IUCN status** | **Upgraded from Critically Endangered → Endangered** |
| Core habitat | **Gir Wildlife Sanctuary**, established **1965** |
| Satellite populations | **9** |
| 2018 setback | **Canine Distemper Virus** killed 20+ lions |
| Proposed second home | **Kuno National Park, Madhya Pradesh** |
| Facilities | National Wildlife Referral Centre, **Junagadh** · **Vanya Prani Mitras** (local trackers) |

> 📖 **The story behind it — and the paradox is the point.** The Asiatic lion recovered from near-extinction to 891 animals. But **56% now live outside protected areas**, on farmland, near villages, beside roads and railway lines. The conservation success has *created* the next problem: a single population in a single landscape is one epidemic away from catastrophe — as the 2018 distemper outbreak showed — and the Supreme Court has long directed a **second home at Kuno**, which Gujarat has resisted. Then Kuno received **African cheetahs** instead. So the lion's second home is now occupied.

> **Why it's asked:** the **Kuno** thread is examinable in itself — ordered for lions, used for cheetahs. **Gir 1965**, **IUCN Endangered** (recently upgraded — an uncommon *good news* status change worth remembering), and the **56% off-reserve** figure. Distinguish clearly from the **African lion** (*Panthera leo leo*).

> `GS-III conservation` · `GS-III environment vs development` · `GS-III disaster/epidemic risk in wildlife` · `GS-II Centre–State friction (Gujarat vs SC order)` · `GS-I human–wildlife coexistence` · `Essay: development at what cost` · `Essay: success creates new problems` · `Interview: mining vs a wildlife corridor — decide`

### 3. 🏦 **RBI resumes on-tap licensing for Urban Cooperative Banks — after 25 years**

| | |
|---|---|
| Legal basis | **Section 22 read with Section 56, Banking Regulation Act, 1949** |
| Suspension | **June 2001 → August 2026** |
| What went wrong before | **820+** UCB licences issued 1993–2001 — **31% became insolvent** |
| New minimum net worth | **₹300 crore** |
| Track record required | **10 years** operating · **5 years** strong financials |
| **CRAR** | minimum **12%** |
| **Net NPAs** | maximum **3%** |
| Framework | Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, **2020**; **4-tier prudential framework** since Dec 2022 |
| Umbrella body | **NUCFDC** — National Urban Cooperative Finance and Development Corporation, est. **2024** |
| UCB gross NPAs FY26 | **₹21,769 crore** — a six-year low |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The 2001 freeze wasn't arbitrary. Licensing was liberalised in 1993, 820+ new UCBs opened, and **nearly a third failed** — culminating in the **Madhavpura Mercantile Co-operative Bank** collapse, which wiped out depositors and exposed the core problem: cooperative banks had **dual regulation**, answering to the RBI *and* to state Registrars of Cooperative Societies, so nobody owned the failure. The **2020 amendment** finally brought UCBs properly under RBI supervision — and only *after* that fix, 25 years later, is licensing reopening. The sequence is the lesson.

> **Why it's asked:** **dual regulation of cooperative banks** is a classic Prelims and Mains point, and the **2020 amendment** is the resolution. Know **CRAR** = Capital to Risk-weighted Assets Ratio. Note that **DICGC** insures deposits up to **₹5 lakh**, which is what protects UCB depositors.

> 🎯 **Where you can use this**
> `GS-III banking sector reform` · `GS-III financial inclusion` · `GS-II regulatory governance` · `GS-II cooperatives (Ministry of Cooperation)` · `Essay: regulation and the cost of its absence` · `Interview: should small banks be allowed to fail?`

**Theme:** Economy · **Static hook:** Banking regulation, RBI, cooperatives · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/06/upsc-current-affairs-6-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 4. ⚖️ **Bankers' Books Evidence Bill, 2026 — replacing an 1891 Act**

Passed by Lok Sabha; introduced **3 August** by the Finance Minister.

- Replaces the **Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891** — **135 years old**, colonial-era
- Modernises evidence standards for **digital, virtual and cloud-based** banking records
- Electronic records admissible if **integrity** is demonstrated — true representation, no unauthorised change, no system tampering
- Requires a **technical certificate** describing the generating system, signed by the branch/office head
- Protects bank officers from **compulsory court attendance** unless a judge finds "special cause"
- Centre may extend provisions to other financial entities by notification

> 📖 **The story behind it.** The 1891 Act existed because bank ledgers were enormous bound volumes that physically could not be carried to court, and a bank could not spare the officer who kept them. So the law allowed a *certified copy* to stand as evidence. That logic survived intact for 135 years — and broke completely once "the books" became a cloud database with no original to copy. This bill is what happens when a sensible rule outlives its physical assumption.

> **Why it's asked:** part of the wider **colonial-law replacement** project — sits alongside the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita / Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita / Sakshya Adhiniyam** (which replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act 1872). "Which colonial-era law was replaced by X?" is a live question format.

> 🎯 **Where you can use this**
> `GS-II governance & legal reform` · `GS-II decolonising the statute book` · `GS-III cyber/digital evidence` · `GS-III banking` · `Essay: law must keep pace with technology` · `Interview: why replace colonial laws?`

**Theme:** Polity / Economy · **Static hook:** Evidence law, legal reform · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/06/upsc-current-affairs-6-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 5. ⚓ **'Seafarer-First' initiative — after attacks in the Strait of Hormuz**

Launched by the Minister of **Ports, Shipping and Waterways** after attacks on merchant vessels **MT Al Bahiyah** and **MT Mombasa**, both carrying Indian crew.

| | |
|---|---|
| Nodal ministry | **MoPSW** |
| Area | West Asia — **Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz, Gulf of Oman** |
| Core tool | **Directorate General of Shipping** live vessel-tracking dashboard |
| Support | 24×7 emergency lines — toll-free, international, WhatsApp, email |
| Rules | **RPSL** (Recruitment and Placement Service Licence) compliance; **no forced sailing in high-risk zones** |
| Requirement | Pre-voyage threat assessment for affected waters |

> 📖 **The story behind it.** India supplies a strikingly large share of the world's merchant seafarers — Indian nationals crew vessels flying every flag, which means an attack anywhere in the Gulf is very likely to involve Indian citizens on a ship India does not own. That is a genuinely unusual diplomatic position: the state has a duty to citizens it cannot protect by protecting its own vessels. Hence a *whole-of-government* response built around tracking and consular reach rather than naval escort alone.

> **Why it's asked:** the **Strait of Hormuz** is the chokepoint to own — it links the **Persian Gulf** to the **Gulf of Oman**, is bounded by **Iran and Oman**, and roughly a fifth of global oil passes through it. **India imports the bulk of its crude through Hormuz**, so it's an energy-security question as much as a diaspora one.

> 🎯 **Where you can use this**
> `GS-II Indian diaspora & welfare of citizens abroad` · `GS-II India and West Asia` · `GS-III energy security` · `GS-III maritime chokepoints` · `GS-I world geography — straits` · `Essay: interdependence and vulnerability` · `Interview: how would you protect Indians abroad?`

**Theme:** IR / Security · **Static hook:** Maritime chokepoints, diaspora policy · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/06/upsc-current-affairs-6-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 6. 💧 **Inter-state river waters — Tamil Nadu asserts its share**

The TN Finance Minister said the state will protect its rightful share of inter-state river waters.

**The constitutional architecture — learn this as a block:**

| Provision | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Article 262(1)** | Parliament **may enact law** for adjudication of inter-state river disputes |
| **Article 262(2)** | Parliament may **bar the Supreme Court and all courts** from jurisdiction |
| **Entry 17, State List** | Water — supply, irrigation, canals, drainage, embankments, water storage |
| **Entry 56, Union List** | Regulation of **inter-state rivers** in the public interest |
| **ISRWD Act, 1956** | The law made under Art. 262 — creates **ad-hoc tribunals** |
| **River Boards Act, 1956** | Provides for **River Boards** — advisory on basin management, flood control, soil conservation |

Tribunal awards carry the **force of a Supreme Court decree**.

> 📖 **The story behind it.** Article 262 is one of the very few places where the Constitution lets Parliament **exclude the Supreme Court entirely** — a deliberate choice, because the framers judged that water disputes between states were political-technical problems that courts would only prolong. The irony is that it hasn't worked: tribunals have taken decades, and litigants have repeatedly reached the Supreme Court anyway through **Article 136 (special leave)**. The **River Boards Act, 1956** is even starker — **not a single river board has ever been constituted under it** in seventy years. A law that exists entirely on paper is itself an examinable fact.

> **Why it's asked:** the **Entry 17 vs Entry 56** split is the classic question — water is a *State* subject, *except* inter-state rivers, which the Union may regulate. And **Art. 262(2)** as a rare ouster of Supreme Court jurisdiction. Major tribunals worth knowing: **Cauvery, Krishna, Godavari, Ravi-Beas, Mahanadi, Vansadhara**.

> 🎯 **Where you can use this**
> `GS-II federalism & inter-state disputes` · `GS-II constitutional provisions` · `GS-I water resources geography` · `GS-III water management` · `Essay: federal cooperation and conflict` · `Interview: Cauvery-type disputes, how would you resolve them?`

**Theme:** Polity · **Static hook:** Laxmikanth — Inter-State Relations, Art. 262 · **Source:** [Insights](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/08/06/upsc-current-affairs-6-august-2026/) ⚠️

### 7. 🗺️ **The Danube falls to record lows**

Water levels dropped to record lows across **Hungary, Slovakia, Romania and Bulgaria** after severe summer heatwaves and drought.

| | |
|---|---|
| Rank | **Europe's second-longest river** — after the **Volga** |
| Length | ~**2,850 km** |
| Source | **Black Forest**, western Germany — confluence of the **Breg** and **Brigach** |
| Mouth | **Black Sea**, via the **Danube Delta** (Romania/Ukraine), UNESCO-protected |
| **Countries** | **10** — Germany, Austria, Slovakia, Hungary, Croatia, Serbia, Bulgaria, Romania, Moldova, Ukraine |
| **Capitals on it** | **Vienna · Bratislava · Budapest · Belgrade** |
| Tributaries | 300+, of which 30+ navigable — Inn, Drava, Sava, Tisza, Prut, Siret |
| Delta | **4,300 sq km** wetland — major migratory bird habitat |
| Infrastructure | **Iron Gate I & II** (Serbia–Romania) · **Gabčíkovo** (Slovakia) |

> **Why it's asked:** **the Danube flows through more countries than any other river in the world (10)** — that is the single most askable fact about it, and it flows past **four national capitals**, more than any other river. Both are option-ready. Note it flows **west to east**, unusual for a major European river, and that the **Iron Gate** gorge separates the Carpathians from the Balkans.

### 8. 💻 **Financial fraud — I4C and the recovery gap**

| | |
|---|---|
| Highest **value** of fraud | **Maharashtra — ₹1.20 lakh crore** |
| Top states by **case count** | Maharashtra, Delhi, **Haryana** |
| Haryana | **84,378 cases**, ₹1,336.40 crore *(FY 2023-24 to 2025-26)* |
| **Haryana recovery rate** | **38%** — against a national average of **8%** |
| Haryana disposal rate | **97%** on the Samanvaya Portal |
| Threshold | **e-Zero FIR** for cases above **₹1 lakh** |

> **Why it's asked:** **I4C** — the **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre**, under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** — is the body, along with the **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal**. The **8% national recovery rate** is a brutal, quotable number for any cyber-crime answer, and Haryana's 38% shows the gap is *administrative*, not technological. **e-Zero FIR** automatically converts a cyber-fraud complaint above ₹1 lakh into an FIR — worth knowing by name.
