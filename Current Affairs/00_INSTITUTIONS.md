# 🏛️ The Institutions Map — how to stop drowning in bodies

**The problem you named:** there are hundreds of ministries, departments, commissions, authorities, boards, councils, tribunals and PSUs, and every day's news adds more.

**The good news, and it is genuinely good:** *UPSC almost never asks you to list them.* It asks three questions, over and over:

| The question | What it's really testing |
|---|---|
| "Which of these is a **constitutional** body?" | The **classification ladder** |
| "X functions under which **ministry**?" | The **parent** |
| "Consider the following about X…" | Its **creating instrument** and **one distinguishing function** |

So you don't need to *know hundreds of bodies*. You need **one ladder**, **one capture habit**, and **the sibling sets where options come from**. That's the whole skill, and it's learnable in a week.

---

## 1️⃣ The ladder — every body sits on exactly one rung

Ask one question of any new body: **what created it?**

| Rung | Created by | Can be abolished by | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Constitutional** | The **Constitution itself** — named in an Article | Only a constitutional amendment | Election Commission (Art. 324) |
| **Statutory** | An **Act** of Parliament or a state legislature | Repealing that Act | NHRC (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993) |
| **Executive / non-statutory** | A **Cabinet resolution or executive order** | Another executive order — *instantly* | NITI Aayog (2015 resolution) |
| **Regulatory** | Usually statutory — a *function*, not a separate rung | — | SEBI (SEBI Act, 1992) |
| **Autonomous body / registered society** | Registered under the Societies Act, funded by a ministry | — | ICAR, CSIR, NCERT |
| **PSU / CPSE** | Incorporated under the Companies Act | — | SECI, NSIL, HSL, NIACL |
| **Attached / subordinate office** | An arm of a ministry, no separate legal identity | — | GSI, ASI, IMD, Survey of India |

> 🔑 **The single highest-yield consequence.** A **constitutional** body's existence is protected — abolishing it needs an amendment. An **executive** body can be dissolved by the same government that made it, overnight. That difference is *why* UPSC asks the question at all, and it's the sentence that turns a rote fact into an answer-worthy point in Mains.

## 3️⃣ The traps — where the marks are actually lost

These recur because the intuitive answer is wrong:

| Body | Intuition | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| **NITI Aayog** | Statutory — it replaced the Planning Commission | ❌ **Executive.** 2015 Cabinet resolution. *So was the Planning Commission* |
| **CBI** | Non-statutory | ❌ **Statutory** — Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, **1946** |
| **NHRC** | Constitutional — it sounds fundamental | ❌ **Statutory** — Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 |
| **NCBC** | Statutory | ❌ **Constitutional since 2018** (102nd Amendment). Was statutory before — *the year is the question* |
| **GST Council** | Statutory — created by the GST Acts | ❌ **Constitutional** — Art. 279A |
| **Solicitor General** | Constitutional, like the AG | ❌ **Not constitutional.** Only the **Attorney General** (Art. 76) is |
| **Delimitation Commission** | Constitutional, like the EC | ❌ **Statutory** — under a Delimitation Act |
| **CVC** | Always statutory | ❌ **Executive 1964 → statutory 2003** (CVC Act) |
| **Lokpal** | Constitutional | ❌ **Statutory** — Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 |
| **Survey of India** | Under Ministry of Mines, like GSI | ❌ Under the **Dept. of Science & Technology** |

## 4️⃣ The capture habit — five fields, every time

When a body appears in a daily brief, capture exactly these. **Five fields or it doesn't go in** — a half-captured body is worse than none, because you'll trust it.

```
1. FULL FORM        — expand the acronym, always
2. TYPE + INSTRUMENT — constitutional (Article) / statutory (Act + year) / executive / PSU / society
3. PARENT           — ministry or department
4. HEAD             — who chairs it, and who appoints them
5. THE DISTINGUISHER — the one function that separates it from its siblings
```

Field 5 is the one everyone skips and the one that scores. "GSI does geology" is useless. "**GSI** maps and surveys; **IBM** regulates mining leases and conservation" is an answer.

## 5️⃣ Sibling sets — where options are actually built

**This is the most useful section on the page.** UPSC rarely asks about a body in isolation. It asks you to tell apart bodies that sound alike and sit in the same domain. Learn them as *sets*, never singly.

### 🚀 Space — under the Department of Space
| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **ISRO** | Builds and flies. The agency |
| **IN-SPACe** | **Authorises and regulates** private activity. Non-statutory, created 2020 |
| **NSIL** | The **commercial arm** — a PSU |
| **Antrix** | The *older* commercial arm |

### 💳 Payments
| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **RBI** | The **regulator** (statutory — RBI Act, 1934) |
| **NPCI** | Umbrella organisation for retail payments — a **not-for-profit company**, not a regulator |
| **NIPL** | NPCI's **international** arm — takes UPI abroad |

### 💰 Financial regulators — all statutory
**RBI** banking · **SEBI** securities (1992) · **IRDAI** insurance (1999) · **PFRDA** pensions (2013) · **IFSCA** GIFT City (2019) · **CCI** competition (Competition Act, 2002)

### 🌿 Environment — under MoEFCC
| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **CPCB** | Statutory — Water Act, **1974**. Pollution standards |
| **NGT** | A **tribunal**, statutory — NGT Act, **2010** |
| **NBA** | National Biodiversity Authority — Biological Diversity Act, 2002 |
| **NTCA** | Tiger conservation — statutory, under the Wildlife Protection Act |
| **WII · ZSI · BSI · FSI** | Research/survey institutes, **not** regulators |

### 🌊 Disaster
**NDMA** — policy, statutory (Disaster Management Act, **2005**), chaired by the **PM** · **NDRF** — the response *force* · **NIDM** — training

### 🎓 Education
**UGC** (1956 Act) · **AICTE** (1987) · **NCTE** (1993) · **NMC** — replaced the MCI · **NCERT** — an **autonomous society**, not statutory · **NTA** — conducts exams

### ⛏️ Mines & geoscience
**GSI** — surveys and maps, Ministry of **Mines**, est. **1851** · **IBM** (Indian Bureau of Mines) — regulates leases · **Survey of India** — topographic mapping, under **DST** *(the trap)*

## 7️⃣ How to actually use this

1. **This week:** memorise §2 (thirteen constitutional bodies) and §3 (the ten traps). That alone is worth more than a hundred body names
2. **Every daily brief:** any new body gets the five fields (§4) and gets hung on its ministry branch (§6)
3. **When a body appears:** immediately ask *"who are its siblings?"* — if you can't name one, that's the gap. Go to §5
4. **In Phase C:** revise §2, §3 and §5 only. The trees are for building understanding; the ladder and the sibling sets are what get tested

> 🔑 **The reframe worth holding onto.** You are not memorising an encyclopedia of institutions. You are learning **one classification skill** and applying it to whatever walks in. A body you've never seen is answerable if you can ask "what created it, whose child is it, and who are its siblings?"
