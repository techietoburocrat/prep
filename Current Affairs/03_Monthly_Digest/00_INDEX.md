# 🗜️ 03_MONTHLY_DIGEST — the compression layer

**One file per month.** Written on the **last Sunday of the month**, ~2 hrs borrowed from the subject block.

By April 2027 this folder plus [04_Prelims_Factsheets](../04_Prelims_Factsheets/00_INDEX.md) is the *entire* current-affairs revision. Nine files, an afternoon each. That is the whole point of the funnel — and it only works if each month actually gets compressed, on time, while you still remember which items mattered.

## What a digest contains

Roughly **two pages. Not more.** A month produces ~150 captured items; the digest keeps maybe 40. Compression *is* the work — a digest that keeps everything is just a slower copy of the theme files.

```markdown
# <Month Year> — digest

## The five stories that mattered
   (running stories, one short paragraph each — the Mains fodder)

## By theme
   (one line per surviving item, grouped under the 8 themes)

## New to the factsheets this month
   (schemes / reports / appointments / places / species added — names only, links to the sheets)

## Questions this month could produce
   (5–8 self-written Prelims-style questions. Writing the question is
    the best test of whether you actually understood the item)

## Dropped
   (what looked important this month and turned out to be noise —
    one line. This is how the filter gets better)
```

The **Dropped** section is not filler. Reading three months of it back-to-back is the fastest way to learn what news is worth capturing, and it's the section that shrinks the daily 45 minutes over time.

---

## The quarterly pass

Every three months, read the three digests back-to-back in one sitting (~2 hrs) and **delete** what has since proved to be noise. Digests are living files — a month that looked significant in August often reads as nothing by November, and cutting it then is cheaper than carrying it to May.
