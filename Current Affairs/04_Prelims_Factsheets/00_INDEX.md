# 🎯 04_PRELIMS_FACTSHEETS — the cram layer

**Five running tables of pure factual bait.** No analysis, no context, no paragraphs. These are the files you read in the last week before Prelims 2027.

Where `02_Themes/` explains *why* something is asked, this folder holds the **things that are asked directly** — the rows that turn into options.

---

## The five sheets

| Sheet | What goes in | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| [01_Schemes](01_Schemes.md) | Every government scheme in the news | Ministry attribution is asked relentlessly and guessed wrong |
| [02_Reports_and_Indices](02_Reports_and_Indices.md) | Every report, index, ranking | "Which organisation publishes X?" is a near-annual question |
| [03_Appointments_and_Awards](03_Appointments_and_Awards.md) | Who heads what, who won what | Cheap marks, forgotten without a list |
| [04_Places_in_News](04_Places_in_News.md) | Any place that appeared for any reason | Map-based questions are the highest-accuracy Prelims questions available |
| [05_Species_in_News](05_Species_in_News.md) | Any organism in the news | Environment is the biggest CA-driven Prelims section |

---

## The rule

**A row goes in the moment the fact appears — not on Sunday.** These sheets are the one exception to the batch-on-Sunday rule, because a table row costs fifteen seconds and a forgotten fact costs the mark.

Every sheet has a fixed column set. **Fill every column, or don't add the row.** A half-filled row is exactly the kind of note that feels productive and answers nothing: the question will be about the column you left blank.
