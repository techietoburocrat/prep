## 🧭 How each file is built

Every chapter follows the same six-part shape, so your brain learns where to look:

1. **The one-line point** — if you remember nothing else, remember this
2. **The story** — plain-language explanation, the way you'd explain it to a school student
3. **Real incident** — an actual case or event where this provision was *used*, because a rule you've seen in action is a rule you don't forget
4. **Memory hooks** — mnemonics, comparison tables, number traps
5. **Expected Mains questions** — with a 4-line answer skeleton each
6. **60-second revision** — the block to re-read the night before

## ⏱️ A realistic study plan for a working professional

You have roughly 90 minutes on a weekday evening and longer on weekends. Don't read linearly.

**Weeks 1–2 · The foundation**
Ch 1 → 2 → 3 → 4. These four are connected: history produced the Assembly, the Assembly produced the features, the features are summarised in the Preamble. Read them as one arc, not four separate topics.

**Weeks 3–4 · The rights block**
Ch 7 (split over 3 sittings — it's the biggest), then Ch 8, then Ch 9. Read Fundamental Rights, DPSP and Duties **together**. The exam constantly tests the tension between them (*Minerva Mills*, *Kesavananda*), and reading them separately hides that tension.

**Week 5 · The rest**
Ch 5 → 6 → 10.

**Every weekend:** open the matching year files in [`../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/`](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/00_INDEX.md) and attempt the questions on what you just read. Reading without attempting PYQs is the most common way aspirants waste a year.

## ⚠️ One honest note on sourcing

These notes are written from standard, stable constitutional knowledge — Articles, landmark judgments, amendment numbers, commission reports. This material doesn't change year to year, and I've kept to well-established facts.

Two things to watch:
- **Anything dated after mid-2024** (recent judgments, new amendments) — verify against a current source before writing it in an exam.
- **Exact numbers** (how many articles, how many amendments to date) drift as amendments pass. I've flagged these in place with ⚠️ where relevant.

Where I say a case "held X", that's the ratio of a well-known judgment. Where I illustrate with a historical incident, it's a documented event. Nothing here is invented for effect.
