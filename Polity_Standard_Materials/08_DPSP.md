# Chapter 8 — Directive Principles of State Policy

[← Chapter 7](07_Fundamental_Rights.md) · [Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 9 →](09_Fundamental_Duties.md)

**Read time ~14 min** · Part IV, Articles 36–51 · Prelims: 1–2 every year · Mains: the FR-vs-DPSP conflict is a recurring theme

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **Fundamental Rights tell the State what it must NOT do. Directive Principles tell the State what it SHOULD do.** One is a prohibition you can enforce in court; the other is a promise you can only enforce at the ballot box.

---

## 📖 The story

Imagine two documents handed to a new government:

- **Document A (Part III):** *"You may not detain people without cause. You may not discriminate. If you break these, citizens will drag you to court."*
- **Document B (Part IV):** *"You should provide work for everyone, a living wage, free legal aid, village panchayats, a uniform civil code. We can't take you to court over these — but we will judge you by them."*

Document B is the DPSP. Ambedkar called them **"novel features"** and Granville Austin called Part III and Part IV together the **"conscience of the Constitution."**

> 💡 **Why make them unenforceable at all?** Because in 1950 India simply did not have the money. A right to work or free healthcare that the state cannot fund would be a lie in the statute book. So the framers wrote them as **goals with a moral obligation**, to be achieved as resources allowed.
>
> **Article 37 says it plainly:** the principles are **"fundamental in the governance of the country"** and it shall be the **duty of the State to apply them in making laws** — but they **shall not be enforceable by any court.**

> 🔑 [Prelims 2013 Q6](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2013.md) asked which are "fundamental for the governance of the country." Answer: **DPSP** — that phrase comes straight from Article 37.

**Borrowed from:** the **Irish** Constitution (which itself took the idea from Spain).

---

## PART A — The three-fold classification

The Constitution itself does **not** classify them. This grouping is a scholarly convention — but it's how the exam thinks.

### 1. SOCIALISTIC principles — the welfare state

| Article | Directive |
|---|---|
| **38** | Promote the welfare of the people; **minimise inequalities** in income, status, facilities and opportunities *(38(2) added by 44th Amendment, 1978)* |
| **39** | Secure: (a) adequate **means of livelihood**; (b) equitable **distribution of material resources**; (c) prevent **concentration of wealth**; (d) **equal pay for equal work**; (e) protect health of workers and children; (f) opportunities for healthy child development |
| **39A** | **Equal justice and free legal aid** — *added by 42nd Amendment, 1976* |
| **41** | Right to **work, education and public assistance** in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement |
| **42** | Just and humane conditions of work and **maternity relief** |
| **43** | **Living wage** and a decent standard of life for all workers |
| **43A** | **Participation of workers in management** of industries — *added by 42nd Amendment, 1976* |
| **47** | Raise the level of **nutrition and public health**; prohibit intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health |

> 🔑 **Article 39(c) — "concentration of wealth"** was tested directly in [Prelims 2021 Q1](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2021.md). Answer: it violates the **DPSP**, not the Right to Equality.
> **Article 39A** is the constitutional basis for **NALSA** — see [Prelims 2013 Q1](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2013.md), [2020 Q8](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2020.md) and [Mains 2023 GS-II Q2](../UPSC_Mains_Polity_PYQs/2023.md).

### 2. GANDHIAN principles — the village and the marginalised

| Article | Directive |
|---|---|
| **40** | Organise **village panchayats** as units of self-government |
| **43** | Promote **cottage industries** on an individual or co-operative basis in rural areas |
| **43B** | Promote **co-operative societies** — *added by 97th Amendment, 2011* |
| **46** | Promote educational and economic interests of **SCs, STs and other weaker sections** |
| **47** | **Prohibition** of intoxicating drinks and drugs |
| **48** | Prohibit slaughter of **cows, calves and other milch and draught cattle**, and improve breeds |

> 💡 **Article 40 is the ancestor of the 73rd Amendment (1992).** For 42 years panchayats were only a directive; then they were given constitutional status. **A directive became structure.**

### 3. LIBERAL-INTELLECTUAL principles

| Article | Directive |
|---|---|
| **44** | **Uniform Civil Code** for all citizens |
| **45** | **Early childhood care and education for children below 6 years** *(changed by 86th Amendment, 2002 — see below)* |
| **48** | Organise **agriculture and animal husbandry** on modern scientific lines |
| **48A** | Protect and improve the **environment**, safeguard forests and wildlife — *added by 42nd Amendment, 1976* |
| **49** | Protect **monuments, places and objects of national importance** |
| **50** | **Separate the judiciary from the executive** in the public services of the State |
| **51** | Promote **international peace and security**, respect for international law and treaty obligations, settlement of disputes by arbitration |

> 🔑 Two very frequently asked:
> - **Article 50 — separation of judiciary from executive → [Prelims 2020 Q4](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2020.md).** Answer: a **Directive Principle**, not the Preamble or Seventh Schedule.
> - **Article 51 — promotion of international peace → [Prelims 2014 Q3](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2014.md).** Answer: **DPSP**, not the Preamble or Fundamental Duties.

> 💡 **Article 44 (UCC) is the most politically live directive.** It was [Mains 2015 GS-II Q1](../UPSC_Mains_Polity_PYQs/2015.md), and became live again when **Uttarakhand enacted a UCC**. Expect it back.

## PART B — New directives added by amendment ⭐

| Amendment | Added |
|---|---|
| **42nd, 1976** | **39A** (free legal aid) · **43A** (workers' participation) · **48A** (environment) |
| **44th, 1978** | **38(2)** — minimise inequalities in income and status |
| **86th, 2002** | Changed **Article 45** — early childhood care for under-6s *(because education 6–14 was promoted to **Article 21A**, a Fundamental Right)* |
| **97th, 2011** | **43B** — promotion of co-operative societies |

> 🧠 **Mnemonic for the 42nd's three: "Legal aid, Labour, Land"** → 39A legal aid, 43A workers, 48A environment.

> 🔑 **The Article 45 → 21A story is the single best illustration in this chapter:** free and compulsory education for 6–14 year olds began life as a *non-enforceable directive*. In 2002 it was **promoted into a Fundamental Right (Article 21A)**, and in 2009 the **RTE Act** gave it teeth.
>
> **A promise became a right.** That is exactly what the framers hoped would happen over time — and it proves the DPSP are not decorative.

## PART C — Directives outside Part IV

Three directives sit elsewhere in the Constitution but are equally non-justiciable:

| Article | Directive |
|---|---|
| **335** | Claims of **SCs and STs** to services must be considered consistently with **efficiency of administration** |
| **350A** | Instruction in the **mother tongue** at the primary stage for linguistic minority children |
| **351** | Promotion and development of the **Hindi language** |

> 🔑 **Article 335 was tested in [Prelims 2023 Q9](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2023.md)** — Statement-II wrongly claimed Article 335 *defines* "efficiency of administration." It does not.

## PART D — The FR vs DPSP war ⭐⭐

**This is where the Mains marks are.** Track it as a five-round fight.

| Round | Event | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **1. *Champakam Dorairajan*** (1951) | Caste-based reservation in Madras challenged | **FR prevail over DPSP.** DPSP must run subsidiary to FR |
| **2. Parliament responds** | **1st Amendment, 1951** | Inserted Article 15(4) to allow reservation — Parliament amends its way around the Court |
| **3. *Golaknath*** (1967) | Can Parliament amend FR at all? | **No** — Parliament cannot abridge Fundamental Rights |
| **4. Parliament responds** | **24th Amendment (1971)** — Parliament *can* amend any part. **25th Amendment (1971)** — inserted **Article 31C**: laws implementing **Articles 39(b) and 39(c)** cannot be struck down for violating Articles 14 or 19 | DPSP given primacy over some FR |
| **5. *Kesavananda Bharati*** (1973) | The landmark | Parliament **can** amend any part **but cannot destroy the BASIC STRUCTURE**. Upheld the first part of Art 31C, struck down the clause barring judicial review |
| **6. 42nd Amendment (1976)** | Expanded Article 31C to cover **ALL** Directive Principles | DPSP would have completely overridden Articles 14 and 19 |
| **7. *Minerva Mills*** (1980) | The correction | **Struck down that expansion.** Held that **"the Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between Parts III and IV. To give absolute primacy to one over the other is to disturb the harmony of the Constitution."** This harmony is itself part of the basic structure |

> 💡 **The *Minerva Mills* line is the single most valuable quotation in this chapter.** It resolves the whole conflict: **neither Part III nor Part IV is supreme — the BALANCE between them is.**

**Where the law stands today:**
- Article 31C survives **only** in its original, narrower form — laws implementing **Article 39(b) and (c)** get protection from Articles 14 and 19
- Everywhere else, courts read FR and DPSP **harmoniously**, and interpret Fundamental Rights *in the light of* Directive Principles

> 🔑 **This is how Article 21 grew so large** (Chapter 7). Rights to health, education, livelihood and a clean environment were read into Article 21 **by importing Directive Principles into it**. The DPSP became enforceable *through the back door of Article 21*.

## PART E — Criticisms and defence

| Criticism | Who said it / the counter |
|---|---|
| **No legal force** | K.T. Shah called them *"pious superfluities"*; **Nasiruddin** said they are *"no better than the new year's resolutions"* |
| **Illogically arranged** | Sir Ivor Jennings — not arranged on any consistent philosophy |
| **Conservative** | Jennings called them based on 19th-century English political philosophy |
| **Constitutional conflict** | K. Santhanam — they create conflict between Centre and states, President and PM, Governor and CM |

**The defence:**
- They have **educative value** — they remind every government of its obligations
- They are a **yardstick for the electorate** to judge performance
- They are a **stabilising factor** — successive governments of different ideologies still work towards the same goals
- They **supplement Fundamental Rights** and fill the socio-economic gap in Part III
- Courts use them as **interpretive aids** — which is how they became partly enforceable in practice

> 💡 **A strong closing line for any DPSP answer:** *the Directive Principles are not weak rights — they are the constitutional statement of what the State exists to achieve. Part III restrains the State; Part IV gives it purpose.*

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### FR vs DPSP — the master table

| | **Fundamental Rights** | **Directive Principles** |
|---|---|---|
| Part | **III** (12–35) | **IV** (36–51) |
| Source | USA | **Ireland** |
| Nature | **Negative** — restrain the State | **Positive** — direct the State to act |
| Enforceable | **Yes**, justiciable | **No**, non-justiciable |
| Aim | **Political democracy** | **Social and economic democracy** |
| Sanction | **Legal** | **Political / moral** |
| Against whom | Mostly the State (some against private persons) | Only the State |

> 🧠 **One line to hold it all:** *"Rights are what the State cannot do to you. Directives are what the State should do for you."*

### The amendment map
> **42nd Amd (1976)** gave DPSP its three new members (**39A, 43A, 48A**) *and* tried to make DPSP supreme via Art 31C — **Minerva Mills (1980) stopped it.**
> **86th Amd (2002)** moved education **out** of Part IV and **into** Part III as Art 21A.
> **97th Amd (2011)** added **43B**.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between Parts III and IV." Discuss with reference to case law. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* *Champakam* (FR prevail) → 1st Amendment → *Golaknath* → 24th & 25th Amendments and Art 31C → *Kesavananda* (basic structure) → 42nd Amendment expansion → ***Minerva Mills*** restores balance. Conclude with the *Minerva Mills* quotation and the modern practice of harmonious construction, illustrated by Article 21's expansion.

**2. Discuss the possible factors that inhibit India from enacting a Uniform Civil Code as provided in the DPSP. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2015 GS-II Q1](../UPSC_Mains_Polity_PYQs/2015.md).*
> *Skeleton:* Article 44. Factors — religious diversity and personal-law plurality; minority apprehension about cultural erosion; Article 25 and 29 concerns; political cost; diversity even *within* communities; tribal customary law and Article 371 exemptions. Note *Shah Bano*, *Sarla Mudgal*, the Law Commission's 2018 view that a UCC is "neither necessary nor desirable at this stage", and Uttarakhand's recent enactment. Balanced conclusion — reform of personal laws first, codification later.

**3. "Directive Principles are not weak rights but unenforced obligations." Critically examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Article 37 language. Criticisms (Shah's "pious superfluities", Jennings). Counter — legislative record: MGNREGA from Art 41, RTE from Art 45→21A, Panchayati Raj from Art 40, Environment Protection Act from Art 48A, NALSA from Art 39A. Judicial route — DPSP read into Art 21. Conclude: unenforceable in court, but demonstrably operative in law-making.

**4. "The Directive Principles reflect the unfinished agenda of the Indian Republic." Comment. (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Use Ambedkar's "life of contradictions" warning. Achievements — panchayats, RTE, legal aid, environmental law. Gaps — living wage (Art 43), UCC (Art 44), prohibition (Art 47), full separation of judiciary and executive (Art 50). Conclude on DPSP as a standing audit of the State.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- **Part IV, Articles 36–51.** From **Ireland**. **Non-justiciable** (Art 37) but *"fundamental in the governance of the country"*
- **Socialistic:** 38, 39, **39A** (free legal aid), 41, 42, 43, **43A**, 47 · **Gandhian:** **40** (panchayats), 43, **43B**, 46, 47, 48 (cattle) · **Liberal-intellectual:** **44** (UCC), 45, 48, **48A** (environment), 49, **50** (separate judiciary), **51** (international peace)
- **Added by amendment:** 42nd → **39A, 43A, 48A** · 44th → 38(2) · 86th → changed Art 45 (education 6–14 moved to **Art 21A**) · 97th → **43B**
- **Outside Part IV:** Art **335** (SC/ST claims + efficiency), **350A** (mother tongue), **351** (Hindi)
- **The conflict:** *Champakam* 1951 (FR prevail) → 1st Amd → *Golaknath* 1967 → 24th & 25th Amd (**Art 31C**) → ***Kesavananda* 1973 (basic structure)** → 42nd Amd expands 31C → ***Minerva Mills* 1980 strikes it down — "balance between Parts III and IV is the bedrock"**
- Today: **Art 31C protects only laws under Art 39(b) and (c)**; elsewhere, **harmonious construction**, and DPSP read into Article 21
- Criticisms: "pious superfluities" (K.T. Shah), "new year's resolutions" (Nasiruddin), illogically arranged (Jennings), constitutional conflict (Santhanam)
