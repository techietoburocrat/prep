# Chapter 2 — Making of the Constitution

[← Chapter 1](01_Historical_Background.md) · [Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 3 →](03_Salient_Features.md)

**Read time ~12 min** · Prelims: 1–2 questions most years · Mains: "criticisms of the Constituent Assembly" is a recurring theme

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The Constituent Assembly was not elected by you and me.** It was *indirectly* elected by provincial legislators, who were themselves elected on a limited franchise. Yet it produced a Constitution beginning with "We, the people." Understanding that gap — and why the Assembly was still legitimate — is the heart of this chapter.

---

## 📖 The story

Imagine a housing society that has been managed for 200 years by an outside contractor. Finally the residents get to write their own rulebook. But there's a problem: not every resident gets a vote in choosing the rulebook committee. Only flat-owners vote, and they vote for their block representatives, who then pick the committee.

That's the Constituent Assembly. **Indirectly elected, limited franchise, and yet it wrote the most democratic document in Indian history.** Critics have attacked exactly this gap ever since — and defenders reply that the Assembly's *composition* mattered less than its *deliberation*: it debated for nearly three years, in public, and accommodated almost every viewpoint.

---

## PART A — Where did the idea come from?

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **1934** | **M.N. Roy** — a pioneer of the communist movement — is the **first person to propose** a Constituent Assembly for India |
| **1935** | The **Indian National Congress** officially demands a Constituent Assembly for the first time |
| **1938** | **Jawaharlal Nehru** declares that the Constitution of free India must be framed by a Constituent Assembly **elected on adult franchise, without outside interference** |
| **1940** | The **August Offer** — the British accept the demand *in principle* for the first time |
| **1942** | **Cripps Mission** — proposes a constitution-making body after the war; rejected by the Muslim League |
| **1946** | **Cabinet Mission Plan** — the Assembly is finally constituted under this plan |

> 🔑 **The chain to memorise:**
> **Roy proposed (1934) → Congress demanded (1935) → Nehru defined (1938) → British accepted (1940) → Cripps offered (1942) → Cabinet Mission delivered (1946).**

> 💡 **Note the irony worth quoting in an answer:** Nehru in 1938 demanded an Assembly elected on **adult franchise**. What we actually got in 1946 was **indirect election on a limited franchise**. The demand was diluted — and that dilution is the root of every criticism of the Assembly.

---

## PART B — How was it composed?

Under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**:

| | Seats |
|---|---|
| **British Indian provinces** | 296 |
| **Princely states** | 93 |
| **Total** | **389** |

**After Partition**, the Muslim League members withdrew to form Pakistan's Constituent Assembly, and the strength came down to **299**.

### How were members chosen?

Three layers, and Prelims tests all three:

1. **Seats allotted to each province** in proportion to its population — roughly **one seat per million people**
2. Seats within a province divided among three communities — **Muslims, Sikhs, and General** — in proportion to their population
3. Representatives of each community were **elected by the members of that community in the Provincial Legislative Assembly**, by the method of **proportional representation with single transferable vote**

> ⚠️ **The classic trap:** members were **NOT directly elected by the people.** They were elected by **Provincial Legislative Assemblies**.
> This was asked as [Prelims 2013 Q2](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2013.md) — answer: *elected by the Provincial Legislative Assemblies*.

Princely state representatives were **nominated** by the heads of the princely states.

---

## PART C — The Assembly at work

### First meeting: **9 December 1946**

- The Muslim League **boycotted** it and demanded a separate Pakistan
- **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** — the oldest member — was elected **temporary President**, following French practice
- On **11 December 1946**, **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was elected **permanent President**
- **H.C. Mukherjee** and **V.T. Krishnamachari** became Vice-Presidents
- **B.N. Rau** was appointed **constitutional advisor** — he prepared the original draft

> 💡 **Don't confuse these three people:**
> - **B.N. Rau** — constitutional *advisor*, prepared the **initial draft**
> - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** — chaired the **Drafting Committee**, which turned that draft into the final text
> - **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** — *President* of the Assembly, later India's first President

### The Objectives Resolution

- **Moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946**
- **Adopted on 22 January 1947**
- It laid down the philosophy and fundamental ideals of the Constitution

> 🔑 **This resolution, in modified form, became the PREAMBLE.** That single link is worth more marks than any date in this chapter. See [Chapter 4](04_Preamble.md).

## PART D — The committees

The Assembly worked through **committees** — 8 major ones and several minor.

| Committee | Chairman |
|---|---|
| Union Powers Committee | **Jawaharlal Nehru** |
| Union Constitution Committee | **Jawaharlal Nehru** |
| States Committee | **Jawaharlal Nehru** |
| Provincial Constitution Committee | **Sardar Patel** |
| Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal Areas | **Sardar Patel** |
| Rules of Procedure Committee | **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** |
| Steering Committee | **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** |
| **Drafting Committee** | **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** |

> 🔑 **Memory hook — "N-P-R-A":**
> **N**ehru took the **Union** (Union Powers, Union Constitution, States)
> **P**atel took the **Provinces and People** (Provincial Constitution, Fundamental Rights & Minorities)
> **R**ajendra Prasad took the **Procedure** (Rules, Steering)
> **A**mbedkar took the **Drafting**

### The Drafting Committee — set up **29 August 1947**

Seven members:
1. **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** *(Chairman)*
2. N. Gopalaswamy Ayyangar
3. Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar
4. Dr. K.M. Munshi
5. Syed Mohammad Saadulla
6. N. Madhava Rau *(replaced B.L. Mitter, who resigned)*
7. T.T. Krishnamachari *(replaced D.P. Khaitan, who died)*

> ⚠️ **A Prelims trap that has actually been asked:** the Drafting Committee was set up on **29 August 1947** — **not** on 26 November 1949.
> [Prelims 2023 Q15](../UPSC_Prelims_Polity_MCQs/2023.md) used exactly this: Statement-II claimed the Drafting Committee was set up on 26 November 1949. **False.** Answer: (c).

## PART E — Enactment and commencement

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **February 1948** | Draft Constitution published; people given 8 months to discuss and propose amendments |
| **4 November 1948** | Ambedkar introduces the final draft — **first reading** |
| **26 November 1949** | **Constitution ADOPTED**. Signed by 284 members. Contained **a Preamble, 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules** |
| **26 January 1950** | **Constitution COMMENCED** — India becomes a Republic |

**Some provisions came into force immediately on 26 November 1949** — citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament, and a few others. The rest came into force on 26 January 1950, called the **"date of commencement."**

### Why these two dates matter

| Date | Name | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **26 November** | **Constitution Day** *(Samvidhan Divas)* | The day it was **adopted** |
| **26 January** | **Republic Day** | The day it **commenced** |

> 🔑 **Why 26 January?** Because on **26 January 1930**, the Congress at its Lahore session (under Nehru) had declared **Purna Swaraj** — complete independence — and observed that date as Independence Day for years. The Assembly deliberately chose it to honour that pledge.
>
> 💡 This is a lovely detail for an answer: *the Republic began on a date the freedom movement had already made sacred.*

### The numbers

- **Time taken: 2 years, 11 months and 18 days**
- **11 sessions**, sitting for **165 days**
- The Draft Constitution was debated for **114 days**
- Total expenditure: about **₹64 lakh**

> 🔑 **"2-11-18"** — say it like a phone number. It comes up.

---

## PART F — Other functions the Assembly performed

The Assembly wasn't only a constitution-writing body. It also acted as the **legislature** for free India until 1950, and:

| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| 22 July 1947 | Adopted the **national flag** |
| May 1949 | Ratified India's membership of the **Commonwealth** |
| 24 January 1950 | Adopted the **national anthem and national song** |
| 24 January 1950 | Elected **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** as India's **first President** |

## PART G — Criticisms (this is where Mains marks live)

| Criticism | The counter-argument |
|---|---|
| **Not a representative body** — members were not directly elected by the people | The franchise was limited, but the Assembly included every shade of opinion; its debates, not its ballot, gave it legitimacy |
| **Not a sovereign body** — it was created by British proposals | After the **Indian Independence Act, 1947**, it became fully sovereign and could frame any constitution it wished |
| **Time-consuming** — nearly 3 years | The US Convention took 4 months, but for a far shorter document. India's Assembly wrote 395 Articles for a vastly more diverse society |
| **Dominated by the Congress** | True numerically — but Granville Austin noted the Congress itself was an umbrella containing wide ideological diversity |
| **Lawyer-politician domination** | This produced a long, precise, technical document — a strength as much as a flaw |
| **Hindu-dominated body** | Winston Churchill sneered that it represented *"only one major community in India."* The counter: the Muslim League chose to boycott; the Assembly did not exclude them |

> 💡 **How to use this in Mains:** don't list criticisms flatly. Structure your answer as *"the Assembly was procedurally imperfect but substantively legitimate"* — then show why. That's a Grade-A framing.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The date pairs that get confused
| | Date |
|---|---|
| First meeting | **9 Dec 1946** |
| Rajendra Prasad elected President | **11 Dec 1946** |
| Objectives Resolution moved | **13 Dec 1946** |
| Objectives Resolution adopted | **22 Jan 1947** |
| Drafting Committee constituted | **29 Aug 1947** |
| Draft published | **Feb 1948** |
| **Adopted** | **26 Nov 1949** |
| **Commenced** | **26 Jan 1950** |

> 🔑 **December 1946 rhythm: 9 → 11 → 13.** Meet, elect, propose. Two days apart each time.

### Adopted vs Commenced
> **"Adopted in 49, Applied in 50."**
> Constitution Day is the *adoption*; Republic Day is the *application*.

### The original size
> **Preamble + 395 Articles + 22 Parts + 8 Schedules**
> ⚠️ Today it has ~470 Articles, 25 Parts and 12 Schedules. **The exam usually asks about the ORIGINAL numbers** — know both, and know which is being asked.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The Constituent Assembly was not a representative body, yet it produced a representative Constitution." Critically examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* (i) The procedural charge — indirect election, limited franchise (~14% adult population), princely nominees. (ii) The substantive defence — every major ideology present; Advisory Committee on Minorities; 114 days of open debate; Objectives Resolution consensus. (iii) Granville Austin's point on Congress as an umbrella. (iv) Conclude: legitimacy came from deliberation and acceptance, not from the ballot alone.

**2. Discuss the significance of the Objectives Resolution in shaping the Indian Constitution. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Moved by Nehru 13 Dec 1946, adopted 22 Jan 1947. Content — sovereign independent republic, justice, equality, freedom, safeguards for minorities and depressed classes. Its transformation into the **Preamble**. Its use by courts (*Berubari*, *Kesavananda*) as a key to constitutional interpretation. Conclude: it fixed the Constitution's philosophy before a single article was drafted.

**3. "The framers of the Indian Constitution were more concerned with accommodation than with ideological purity." Comment. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Examples — federalism with a strong Centre; FR balanced with DPSP; secularism as accommodation rather than strict separation; special provisions for states (Art 370, 371 series); reservation. Argue accommodation was a deliberate design response to India's diversity, not indecision.

**4. Examine the role of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar in the making of the Indian Constitution. Was he, as is often claimed, its sole architect? (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* His role as Drafting Committee chairman, his defence of Article 32 as "the heart and soul", his framing of FR and safeguards for depressed classes. Balance: B.N. Rau's initial draft, S.N. Mukherjee's drafting skill, Nehru and Patel's committee work, the Assembly's collective deliberation. Conclude: chief architect of the *text*, not the sole author of the *document*.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- Idea: **M.N. Roy 1934** → Congress 1935 → Nehru 1938 → **August Offer 1940** → Cripps 1942 → **Cabinet Mission 1946**
- Composition: **389 → 299** after Partition. Provincial seats ~**1 per million**, divided among **Muslims, Sikhs, General**, elected by **Provincial Legislative Assemblies** via **proportional representation, single transferable vote**. Princely states **nominated**
- First meeting **9 Dec 1946**; **Sachchidananda Sinha** temporary President; **Rajendra Prasad** permanent (11 Dec); **B.N. Rau** constitutional advisor
- **Objectives Resolution** — Nehru, moved 13 Dec 1946, adopted 22 Jan 1947 → became the **Preamble**
- **Drafting Committee: 29 Aug 1947, 7 members, Ambedkar chair**
- **Adopted 26 Nov 1949** (Preamble + 395 Articles + 22 Parts + 8 Schedules, 284 signatures) · **Commenced 26 Jan 1950** (chosen for **Purna Swaraj Day, 1930**)
- **2 years 11 months 18 days**, 11 sessions, 165 days, ₹64 lakh
- Criticisms: not representative, not sovereign, time-consuming, Congress-dominated, lawyer-dominated, "Hindu-dominated" (Churchill)
