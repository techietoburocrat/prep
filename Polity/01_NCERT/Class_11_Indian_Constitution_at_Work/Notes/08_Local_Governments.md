# Chapter 8: Local Governments

## The Big Idea
⭐ **"In a democracy, it is NOT SUFFICIENT to have an elected government at the centre and at the State level. It is also necessary that EVEN AT THE LOCAL LEVEL there should be an elected government to look after local affairs."**

**Three things this chapter delivers:** the **importance** of local government bodies; the **provisions made by the 73rd and 74th Amendments**; and the **functions and responsibilities** of local government bodies.

---

## PART 1 — WHY LOCAL GOVERNMENTS?

### ⭐⭐ Real story 1: Geeta Rathore — participation
*(Jamonia Talab Gram Panchayat, **Sehore district, Madhya Pradesh** — Panchayati Raj Update, Vol. XI, No. 3, February 2004)*
- ⭐ **"She was elected Sarpanch in 1995 FROM A RESERVED SEAT; but IN 2000, the village people REWARDED HER FOR HER ADMIRABLE WORK BY ELECTING HER AGAIN — THIS TIME FROM A NON-RESERVED SEAT."**
- **"From a housewife, Geeta has grown into a leader displaying POLITICAL FARSIGHTEDNESS."**
- ⭐ **What she achieved by harnessing "the collective energy of her Panchayat" (learn this list):**
  1. **Renovated water tanks**
  2. **Built a school building**
  3. **Constructed village roads**
  4. **Fought against domestic violence and atrocities against women**
  5. **Created environmental awareness**
  6. **Encouraged afforestation and water management**

> **⭐ Why this story matters analytically: reservation worked as a *ladder*, not a *ceiling*.** She entered through a reserved seat and then won an **open** seat on merit. This is the single best answer to anyone who argues reservation in local bodies is merely symbolic.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Real story 2: Vengaivasal village, Tamil Nadu — accountability
*(Panchayati Raj Update, Vol. XII, June 2005)*
| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| **1997** | **"The Tamil Nadu government allotted TWO HECTARES OF LAND TO 71 GOVERNMENT EMPLOYEES."** The land **"fell within the vicinity of this Gram Panchayat."** |
| **The pressure** | ⭐ **"On the instructions of higher authorities, the DISTRICT COLLECTOR OF KANCHEEPURAM DIRECTED the President of the Gram Panchayat TO PASS A RESOLUTION ENDORSING the allotment of the said land for the purpose ALREADY DECIDED."** |
| **The refusal** | ⭐ **"The President and the Gram Panchayat REFUSED to pass such an order, and the COLLECTOR ISSUED AN ORDER TO ACQUIRE THE LAND."** |
| **Round 1 — lost** | **"The Gram Panchayat filed a WRIT PETITION in the MADRAS HIGH COURT against the Collector's action. The SINGLE JUDGE BENCH UPHELD THE COLLECTOR'S ORDER and ruled that THERE WAS NO NEED TO TAKE THE PANCHAYAT'S CONSENT."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Round 2 — won** | **"The Panchayat APPEALED TO THE DIVISION BENCH against the single judge's order. In its order, the DIVISION BENCH REVERSED the order of the single judge."** ⭐⭐⭐ **"The judges held that the government order amounted to NOT ONLY INFRINGEMENT OF THE POWERS OF THE PANCHAYATS BUT A GROSS VIOLATION OF THE CONSTITUTIONAL STATUS OF THE PANCHAYATS."** |

> **⭐⭐ Why this is the most important case in the chapter:** it establishes that after **1993**, panchayats have a **constitutional status** that even a **District Collector acting on higher instructions cannot override.** Note the sequence — a **panchayat sued the State and won.** *(Also note the Chapter 6 link: the panchayat used a **writ petition** under **Article 226**.)*

⭐ **"Both these stories are NOT ISOLATED INCIDENTS. They are REPRESENTATIVE OF A LARGER TRANSFORMATION that is taking place across India, ESPECIALLY AFTER CONSTITUTIONAL STATUS WAS ACCORDED TO LOCAL GOVERNMENT INSTITUTIONS IN 1993."**

### ⭐⭐ What local government is — the definitional cluster
1. **"Local government is government at the VILLAGE and DISTRICT level."**
2. **"Local government is about government CLOSEST TO THE COMMON PEOPLE."**
3. **"Local government is about government that involves the DAY-TO-DAY LIFE AND PROBLEMS OF ORDINARY CITIZENS."**
4. ⭐⭐ **"Local government believes that LOCAL KNOWLEDGE and LOCAL INTEREST are ESSENTIAL INGREDIENTS FOR DEMOCRATIC DECISION MAKING. They are also necessary for EFFICIENT AND PEOPLE-FRIENDLY ADMINISTRATION."**

### ⭐⭐ The case for local government (four arguments)
| # | Argument | The chapter's words |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐ **Proximity** | **"The advantage of local government is that it is SO NEAR THE PEOPLE. It is convenient for the people to approach the local government for solving their problems BOTH QUICKLY AND WITH MINIMUM COST."** |
| **2** | ⭐⭐ **Participation** | **"Democracy is about MEANINGFUL PARTICIPATION."** — **Geeta Rathore's story is one of committed participation.** |
| **3** | ⭐⭐ **Accountability** | **"It is also about ACCOUNTABILITY. STRONG AND VIBRANT LOCAL GOVERNMENTS ENSURE BOTH ACTIVE PARTICIPATION AND PURPOSEFUL ACCOUNTABILITY."** — **Vengaivasal's fight for its land was "a mission to ensure accountability."** |
| **4** | ⭐ **Familiarity and direct impact** | **"Common people are MORE FAMILIAR with their local government than with the government at the State or national level. They are also MORE CONCERNED with what local government does or has failed to do, as it has a DIRECT BEARING AND IMPACT ON THEIR DAY-TO-DAY LIFE."** |

⭐⭐ **"It is at the level of local government that COMMON CITIZENS CAN BE INVOLVED IN DECISION MAKING CONCERNING THEIR LIVES, THEIR NEEDS AND ABOVE ALL THEIR DEVELOPMENT."**

⭐⭐ **The principle of subsidiarity, in the chapter's words:** *"It is necessary that in a democracy, **TASKS WHICH CAN BE PERFORMED LOCALLY SHOULD BE LEFT IN THE HANDS OF THE LOCAL PEOPLE AND THEIR REPRESENTATIVES.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **"Thus, STRENGTHENING LOCAL GOVERNMENT IS LIKE STRENGTHENING DEMOCRATIC PROCESSES."**

### ⭐ Box: "Check your progress"
1. **"How does local government strengthen democracy?"** → Through **proximity** (quick, low-cost access), **meaningful participation**, **purposeful accountability**, and by involving citizens directly in decisions about **their lives, needs and development.**
2. ⭐ **"In the example given above, what do you think the Government of Tamil Nadu should have done?"** → **Consulted the Gram Panchayat and obtained its resolution genuinely**, rather than directing the President to endorse a decision **"already decided"** — because land is within the panchayat's area and the panchayat now enjoys **constitutional status.** *(Compare Exercise 1(a), the steel plant case, which is the same principle.)*

> *The chapter's own honest question in the margin, worth carrying into an answer:* *"Aren't there cases of male members of the village panchayat **harassing the woman Sarpanch** in some places? **Why are men not happy when women assume positions of responsibility?**"*

## ⭐⭐ PART 2 — GROWTH OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT IN INDIA

### The ancient roots
- ⭐ **"It is believed that SELF-GOVERNING VILLAGE COMMUNITIES existed in India FROM THE EARLIEST TIMES in the form of 'SABHAS' (village assemblies)."**
- ⭐ **"In the course of time, these village bodies took the shape of PANCHAYATS (an ASSEMBLY OF FIVE PERSONS), and these Panchayats RESOLVED ISSUES AT THE VILLAGE LEVEL. Their role and functions KEPT ON CHANGING at different points of time."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The colonial timeline (memorise every date)
| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1882** | **"In modern times, ELECTED LOCAL GOVERNMENT BODIES were created after 1882. LORD RIPPON, who was the VICEROY OF INDIA at that time, took the initiative in creating these bodies. THEY WERE CALLED THE LOCAL BOARDS."** |
| **(after 1882)** | ⭐ **"However, DUE TO SLOW PROGRESS in this regard, the INDIAN NATIONAL CONGRESS URGED THE GOVERNMENT to take necessary steps to make all local bodies more effective."** |
| ⭐⭐ **1919** | **"Following the GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT 1919, VILLAGE PANCHAYATS WERE ESTABLISHED IN A NUMBER OF PROVINCES."** |
| ⭐ **1935** | **"This trend continued after the GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT OF 1935."** |

*(**Lord Rippon is often called the "father of local self-government in India" — 1882 is the date to remember.**)*

### ⭐⭐ Gandhi on decentralisation
- ⭐ **"During India's freedom movement, MAHATMA GANDHI had STRONGLY PLEADED FOR DECENTRALISATION OF ECONOMIC AND POLITICAL POWER. He believed that STRENGTHENING VILLAGE PANCHAYATS WAS A MEANS OF EFFECTIVE DECENTRALISATION."**
- ⭐ **"ALL DEVELOPMENT INITIATIVES MUST HAVE LOCAL INVOLVEMENT IN ORDER TO BE SUCCESSFUL."**
- ⭐⭐ **"Panchayats therefore were looked upon as INSTRUMENTS OF DECENTRALISATION AND PARTICIPATORY DEMOCRACY."**
- ⭐⭐ **The anti-colonial root of the idea:** *"Our national movement was concerned about the **ENORMOUS CONCENTRATION OF POWERS IN THE HANDS OF THE GOVERNOR GENERAL SITTING AT DELHI.** Therefore, **for our leaders, INDEPENDENCE MEANT AN ASSURANCE THAT THERE WILL BE DECENTRALISATION OF DECISION MAKING, EXECUTIVE AND ADMINISTRATIVE POWERS.**"*

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ Gandhi's own words — the set-piece quotation of this chapter
> *"The independence of India should mean the independence of the whole of India... **INDEPENDENCE MUST BEGIN AT THE BOTTOM. Thus EVERY VILLAGE WILL BE A REPUBLIC**... It follows therefore that **every village has to be SELF-SUSTAINED and CAPABLE OF MANAGING ITS AFFAIRS.** In this structure composed of innumerable villages, there will be **EVER-WIDENING, EVER-ASCENDING CIRCLES. LIFE WILL BE A PYRAMID WITH THE APEX SUSTAINED BY THE BOTTOM.**"*
> — **Mahatma Gandhi**
>
> **⭐ Note the inversion: an ordinary pyramid rests its weight on the base and is *ruled* from the apex; Gandhi's pyramid has the apex *sustained by* the bottom. That is the whole philosophy of decentralisation in one image.**

### ⭐⭐ What the Constitution originally did — and why it was so little
- ⭐ **"When the Constitution was prepared, THE SUBJECT OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT WAS ASSIGNED TO THE STATES."**
- ⭐⭐ **"It was also mentioned in the DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES as one of the policy directives to all governments in the country."**
- ⭐⭐ **The consequence:** *"being a part of the Directive Principles of State Policy, **this provision of the Constitution was NON-JUSTICIABLE and PRIMARILY ADVISORY in its nature.**"* *(Recall Ch. 2: "promotion of village panchayats" is listed among the DPSP **policies**.)*

⭐ **"It is felt that the subject of local government including panchayats DID NOT RECEIVE ADEQUATE IMPORTANCE IN THE CONSTITUTION."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ WHY it received so little importance — the two reasons (a certain exam question)
| # | Reason | Whose objection |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐⭐ **"Firstly, the TURMOIL DUE TO THE PARTITION resulted in a STRONG UNITARY INCLINATION in the Constitution. NEHRU HIMSELF LOOKED UPON EXTREME LOCALISM AS A THREAT TO UNITY AND INTEGRATION OF THE NATION."** | **Nehru — a *national unity* objection** |
| **2** | ⭐⭐ **"Secondly, there was a POWERFUL VOICE IN THE CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY LED BY DR. B.R. AMBEDKAR which felt that the FACTION AND CASTE-RIDDEN NATURE OF RURAL SOCIETY WOULD DEFEAT THE NOBLE PURPOSE OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT AT THE RURAL LEVEL."** | **Ambedkar — a *social justice* objection** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the direct answer to the "Check your progress" question — "Did Nehru and Ambedkar have SIMILAR objections?" NO. Their objections were entirely different in kind:**
> - **Nehru feared that localism would fragment the NATION** (a unity/integration concern).
> - **Ambedkar feared that the village would oppress its own people** — that **faction and caste domination** would make local government a vehicle for the dominant castes rather than for democracy (a social-justice concern).
>
> **They arrived at the same caution for opposite reasons — and note that the 73rd Amendment's *reservations* are essentially the answer to Ambedkar's objection.**

⭐ **The nuance to keep:** *"**However, NOBODY DENIED THE IMPORTANCE OF PEOPLE'S PARTICIPATION IN DEVELOPMENT PLANNING.** Many members of the Constituent Assembly **WANTED Village Panchayats to be the BASIS OF DEMOCRACY in India, BUT they were CONCERNED ABOUT FACTIONALISM AND MANY OTHER ILLS present in the villages."*

> ### ⭐ Ananthasayanam Ayyangar in the Constituent Assembly
> *"... in the interests of democracy, **the villages may be TRAINED IN THE ART OF SELF-GOVERNMENT, even autonomy**... **We must be able to REFORM THE VILLAGES and INTRODUCE DEMOCRATIC PRINCIPLES OF GOVERNMENT THERE**..."*
> — **Ananthasayanam Ayyangar, CAD, Vol. VII, p. 428, 17 November 1948**
>
> *(Note the position: not rejection, but **"reform first, then self-government"** — a middle path between Gandhi's enthusiasm and Ambedkar's scepticism.)*

> *The chapter's own sharp margin comment, which vindicates Ambedkar:* *"I don't know about the past, but I suspect that **a NON-ELECTED village panchayat would naturally be dominated by the village elders, the rich and men from upper strata.**"*

## ⭐⭐ PART 3 — LOCAL GOVERNMENTS IN INDEPENDENT INDIA (before 1992)

### ⭐⭐ The pre-1992 record
| Year / period | Development |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1952** | **"FIRST IN THE LINE was the COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME IN 1952, which sought to PROMOTE PEOPLE'S PARTICIPATION IN LOCAL DEVELOPMENT in a range of activities."** |
| ⭐⭐ **(following that)** | **"In this background, a THREE-TIER PANCHAYATI RAJ SYSTEM of local government was RECOMMENDED FOR THE RURAL AREAS."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **around 1960** | **"SOME STATES (like GUJARAT, MAHARASHTRA) ADOPTED THE SYSTEM OF ELECTED LOCAL BODIES."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The five failures before 1992 (memorise this list — it is the "before" column of Exercise Q4)
*"But in many States those local bodies did not have enough powers and functions to look after local development."*
1. ⭐ **"They did NOT HAVE ENOUGH POWERS AND FUNCTIONS to look after the local development."**
2. ⭐⭐ **"They were VERY MUCH DEPENDENT ON THE STATE AND CENTRAL GOVERNMENTS FOR FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE."**
3. ⭐⭐ **"MANY STATES DID NOT THINK IT NECESSARY TO ESTABLISH ELECTED LOCAL BODIES."**
4. ⭐⭐ **"In many instances, LOCAL BODIES WERE DISSOLVED and the LOCAL GOVERNMENT WAS HANDED OVER TO GOVERNMENT OFFICERS."**
5. ⭐⭐ **"Many States had INDIRECT ELECTIONS to most local bodies."** and **"In many States, ELECTIONS TO THE LOCAL BODIES WERE POSTPONED FROM TIME TO TIME."**

### ⭐⭐ The review that led to reform
- ⭐ **"AFTER 1987, a thorough review of the functioning of local government institutions was initiated."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"IN 1989 THE P.K. THUNGON COMMITTEE RECOMMENDED CONSTITUTIONAL RECOGNITION FOR THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT BODIES."**
- ⭐⭐ **What it recommended, precisely (three things):** *"A constitutional amendment to provide for **(i) PERIODIC ELECTIONS to local government institutions, and (ii) ENLISTMENT OF APPROPRIATE FUNCTIONS to them, (iii) ALONG WITH FUNDS, was recommended."*

*(**Functions + Funds + regular Elections — the "3 Fs and an E" that the 73rd Amendment then delivered.**)*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 4 — THE 73RD AND 74TH AMENDMENTS

### ⭐⭐⭐ The timeline (a very frequently confused set of dates — learn all four)
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **1989** | **"The CENTRAL GOVERNMENT INTRODUCED two constitutional amendments."** *(Introduced — not passed.)* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1992** | **"Later in 1992, the 73RD AND 74TH CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS WERE PASSED BY THE PARLIAMENT."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1993** | **"The 73rd and 74th Amendments CAME INTO FORCE IN 1993."** |
| ⭐⭐ **one year** | **"They [the States] were given ONE YEAR'S TIME for making necessary changes in their respective State laws in the light of these amendments."** |

⭐ **Their stated aim:** *"These amendments aimed at **(i) STRENGTHENING LOCAL GOVERNMENTS and (ii) ensuring an ELEMENT OF UNIFORMITY IN THEIR STRUCTURE AND FUNCTIONING ACROSS THE COUNTRY.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Which amendment covers what (never mix these up)
| Amendment | Covers | Also known as |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **73rd** | **RURAL local governments** | **Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **74th** | **URBAN local governments** | **Nagarpalikas** |

⭐ **The federal point:** *"Local government is a **'STATE SUBJECT'. States are FREE to make their own laws on this subject. BUT once the Constitution was amended, THE STATES HAD TO CHANGE THEIR LAWS about local bodies IN ORDER TO BRING THESE IN CONFORMITY WITH THE AMENDED CONSTITUTION.**"*

> *The chapter's own delicious irony, worth quoting in an answer:* *"If I understand this correctly, **the centre FORCED local government reforms ON THE STATES. This is funny: you adopt DECENTRALISATION through a CENTRALISED PROCESS!**"*

### ⭐ Box: Local government in Brazil (the comparison for *protected* local power)
> **"The Constitution of Brazil has created STATES, FEDERAL DISTRICTS and MUNICIPAL COUNCILS. EACH of these is assigned INDEPENDENT POWERS AND JURISDICTION. ⭐⭐ JUST AS THE REPUBLIC CANNOT INTERFERE IN THE AFFAIRS OF THE STATES (except on grounds provided by the constitution), STATES ARE PROHIBITED FROM INTERFERING IN THE AFFAIRS OF THE MUNICIPAL COUNCILS. THIS PROVISION PROTECTS THE POWERS OF THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT."**
>
> **⭐ The contrast with India: in Brazil the third tier is *insulated from the second tier by the constitution itself.* In India, by contrast, the actual transfer of functions "depends upon the State legislation" — which is precisely why, as Part 6 shows, "many States have not transferred most of the subjects."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 5 — WHAT THE 73RD AMENDMENT DID (six provisions)

## ➊ Three Tier Structure
⭐⭐ **"ALL STATES NOW HAVE A UNIFORM THREE TIER PANCHAYATI RAJ STRUCTURE."**

| Tier | Body | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Base** | **GRAM PANCHAYAT** | **"Covers a VILLAGE OR GROUP OF VILLAGES"** |
| ⭐⭐ **Intermediary** | **MANDAL PANCHAYAT** — *"also referred to as **BLOCK** or **TALUKA**"* → **"Mandal or Taluka Panchayats"** | ⭐⭐ **"The INTERMEDIARY LEVEL BODY NEED NOT BE CONSTITUTED IN SMALLER STATES."** ← *the one exception to uniformity* |
| ⭐ **Apex** | **ZILLA PANCHAYAT** | **"Covering the ENTIRE RURAL AREA OF THE DISTRICT"** |

### ⭐⭐ The Gram Sabha
- ⭐⭐ **"The amendment also made a provision for the MANDATORY CREATION OF THE GRAM SABHA."**
- ⭐⭐ **"The Gram Sabha would comprise ALL THE ADULT MEMBERS REGISTERED AS VOTERS IN THE PANCHAYAT AREA."**
- ⭐⭐ **The catch:** *"**ITS ROLE AND FUNCTIONS ARE DECIDED BY STATE LEGISLATION.**"* ← *creation is mandatory; its powers are not*

> *The chapter's own question:* *"Does a Gram Sabha mean the democratic forum of the entire village? **Do Gram Sabhas actually meet regularly?**"* *(Class 10's federalism chapter answers this bluntly: **"Gram Sabhas are not held regularly."**)*

## ➋ Elections
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"ALL THE THREE LEVELS of Panchayati Raj institutions are ELECTED DIRECTLY BY THE PEOPLE."**
- ⭐⭐ **"The TERM of each Panchayat body is FIVE YEARS."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The anti-supersession safeguard:** *"**IF THE STATE GOVERNMENT DISSOLVES THE PANCHAYAT BEFORE THE END OF ITS FIVE YEAR TERM, FRESH ELECTIONS MUST BE HELD WITHIN SIX MONTHS OF SUCH DISSOLUTION.** This is an important provision that **ENSURES THE EXISTENCE OF ELECTED LOCAL BODIES.**"*
- ⭐⭐ **The contrast with before:** *"**BEFORE the 73rd amendment, in many States there used to be INDIRECT ELECTIONS to the district bodies, and there was NO PROVISION FOR IMMEDIATE ELECTIONS AFTER DISSOLUTION.**"*

## ➌ Reservations — the most examined provision
| Group | Reservation |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Women** | **"ONE THIRD of the positions in ALL panchayat institutions are reserved for women."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **SC and ST** | **"Reservations for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes are also provided for AT ALL THE THREE LEVELS, IN PROPORTION TO THEIR POPULATION."** |
| ⭐⭐ **OBCs** | **"IF THE STATES FIND IT NECESSARY, THEY CAN ALSO PROVIDE for reservations for the other backward classes (OBCs)."** ← *optional, State's choice* |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Two crucial extensions (this is where marks are won or lost)
1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Reservation covers the CHAIRPERSON's post, not just ordinary membership:** *"It is important to note that **these reservations apply NOT MERELY TO ORDINARY MEMBERS in Panchayats BUT ALSO TO THE POSITIONS OF CHAIRPERSONS or 'ADHYAKSHAS' AT ALL THE THREE LEVELS.**"*
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **The reservations INTERSECT:** *"Further, **reservation of one-third of the seats for women is NOT MERELY IN THE GENERAL CATEGORY OF SEATS BUT ALSO WITHIN THE SEATS RESERVED FOR SCHEDULED CASTES, SCHEDULED TRIBES AND BACKWARD CASTES.** This means that **a seat may be reserved SIMULTANEOUSLY for a woman candidate AND one belonging to the Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes.** ⭐⭐ **Thus, a Sarpanch would have to be a DALIT WOMAN or an ADIVASI WOMAN.**"*

> **⭐ This is "quota within quota."** It answers Saba's intersectionality objection from Chapter 3 — *"Muslim women are not going to get any share in any of these systems"* — by ensuring the **doubly disadvantaged** are not squeezed out by a single-axis quota. **And it is the direct institutional answer to Ambedkar's caste objection.**

> *The chapter's own pointed comparison with Chapter 3:* *"We read in the chapter on Elections that the bill for reservations for women in the Assemblies and in the Parliament could not be passed. **How come women's reservations in local bodies were accepted so easily?**"* *(Note: the Lok Sabha/Vidhan Sabha version finally arrived only with the **Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023** — three decades later.)*

## ➍ Transfer of Subjects — the Eleventh Schedule
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"TWENTY-NINE SUBJECTS, WHICH WERE EARLIER IN THE STATE LIST of subjects, are identified and listed in the ELEVENTH SCHEDULE of the Constitution. THESE SUBJECTS ARE TO BE TRANSFERRED TO THE PANCHAYATI RAJ INSTITUTIONS."**
- ⭐ **"These subjects were MOSTLY LINKED TO DEVELOPMENT AND WELFARE FUNCTIONS at the local level."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The fatal loophole:** *"**THE ACTUAL TRANSFER OF THESE FUNCTIONS DEPENDS UPON THE STATE LEGISLATION. EACH STATE DECIDES HOW MANY OF THESE TWENTY-NINE SUBJECTS WOULD BE TRANSFERRED TO THE LOCAL BODIES.**"*

> ### ⭐⭐ Bare text — **Article 243G**
> *"**Powers, authority and responsibilities of Panchayats** — ........., **the Legislature of a State MAY, by law, endow the Panchayats with such powers and authority....... ...with respect to--......the matters listed in the ELEVENTH SCHEDULE.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐ **Note the word "MAY" — not "shall."** That single word is the source of every implementation failure described in Part 6. **Compare Article 74(1) from Chapter 4, where "shall" made the advice binding.**

## ➎ ⭐⭐⭐ PESA — extension to Adivasi areas (1996)
- ⭐⭐ **"The provisions of the 73rd amendment were NOT MADE APPLICABLE TO THE AREAS INHABITED BY THE ADIVASI POPULATIONS in many States of India."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"IN 1996, A SEPARATE ACT WAS PASSED EXTENDING THE PROVISIONS OF THE PANCHAYAT SYSTEM TO THESE AREAS."** *(This is the **Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 — PESA**.)*
- ⭐⭐ **Why a separate act was needed:** *"**MANY ADIVASI COMMUNITIES HAVE THEIR TRADITIONAL CUSTOMS OF MANAGING COMMON RESOURCES SUCH AS FORESTS AND SMALL WATER RESERVOIRS**, etc. Therefore, **the new act PROTECTS THE RIGHTS OF THESE COMMUNITIES TO MANAGE THEIR RESOURCES IN WAYS ACCEPTABLE TO THEM.**"*
- ⭐⭐⭐ **How it does so:** *"For this purpose, **MORE POWERS ARE GIVEN TO THE GRAM SABHAS of these areas, and ELECTED VILLAGE PANCHAYATS HAVE TO GET THE CONSENT OF THE GRAM SABHA IN MANY RESPECTS.**"* ← *note the inversion: the Gram Sabha is made **stronger than** the elected panchayat*
- ⭐⭐ **The principle:** *"The idea behind this act is that **LOCAL TRADITIONS OF SELF GOVERNMENT SHOULD BE PROTECTED WHILE INTRODUCING MODERN ELECTED BODIES.** This is only **CONSISTENT WITH THE SPIRIT OF DIVERSITY AND DECENTRALISATION.**"*

## ➏ State Election Commissioner and State Finance Commission

### ⭐⭐⭐ State Election Commissioner
- **"The State government is required to appoint a STATE ELECTION COMMISSIONER who would be RESPONSIBLE FOR CONDUCTING ELECTIONS TO THE PANCHAYATI RAJ INSTITUTIONS."**
- ⭐⭐ **What changed:** *"**EARLIER, this task was performed by the STATE ADMINISTRATION WHICH WAS UNDER THE CONTROL OF THE STATE GOVERNMENT.** Now, the office of the State Election Commissioner is **AUTONOMOUS like the Election Commissioner of India.**"*
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The point everyone gets wrong:** *"**HOWEVER, the State Election Commissioner is an INDEPENDENT OFFICER and IS NOT LINKED TO, NOR IS THIS OFFICER UNDER THE CONTROL OF, THE ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA.**"* *(This is exactly what Chapter 3 said: *"The Election Commission is not responsible for the conduct of local body elections... each has its own sphere of operation."*)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ State Finance Commission
- ⭐⭐ **"The State government is also required to appoint a STATE FINANCE COMMISSION ONCE IN FIVE YEARS."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Its two tasks:**
  1. **"This Commission would EXAMINE THE FINANCIAL POSITION OF THE LOCAL GOVERNMENTS IN THE STATE."**
  2. **"It would also REVIEW THE DISTRIBUTION OF REVENUES (i) BETWEEN THE STATE AND LOCAL GOVERNMENTS on the one hand and (ii) BETWEEN RURAL AND URBAN LOCAL GOVERNMENTS on the other."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Its purpose — a superb line:** *"**THIS INNOVATION ENSURES THAT ALLOCATION OF FUNDS TO THE RURAL LOCAL GOVERNMENTS WILL NOT BE A POLITICAL MATTER.**"*

> *The chapter raises the obvious objection:* *"**State governments themselves are poor.** In the last chapter we read that they ask for money from the Central government. **How can they give money to the local government?**"* — this is the **"immense responsibilities but meagre revenue sources"** problem from Chapter 7, now cascading down a third level.

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 6 — THE 74TH AMENDMENT (urban local bodies / Nagarpalikas)

### ⭐⭐⭐ What counts as "urban"? — the Census definition (three criteria, all must be met)
*"It is very easy to identify a big city like Mumbai or Kolkata, but it is not so easy to say this about some very small urban areas that are somewhere between a village and a town."*

⭐⭐⭐ **"The CENSUS OF INDIA defines an urban area as having:"**
1. ⭐ **"a MINIMUM POPULATION OF 5,000"**
2. ⭐ **"AT LEAST 75 PER CENT OF MALE WORKING POPULATION ENGAGED IN NON-AGRICULTURAL OCCUPATIONS"**
3. ⭐ **"a DENSITY OF POPULATION OF AT LEAST 400 PERSONS PER SQ. KM."**

⭐⭐ **"As per the 2011 Census, ABOUT 31% OF INDIA'S POPULATION LIVES IN URBAN AREAS."**

### ⭐⭐ The 74th Amendment mirrors the 73rd
⭐ **"In many ways the 74th amendment is a REPETITION of the 73rd amendment, EXCEPT THAT IT APPLIES TO URBAN AREAS."**

⭐⭐ **"ALL the provisions of the 73rd amendment relating to (i) DIRECT ELECTIONS, (ii) RESERVATIONS, (iii) TRANSFER OF SUBJECTS, (iv) STATE ELECTION COMMISSION and (v) STATE FINANCE COMMISSION are INCORPORATED IN THE 74TH AMENDMENT ALSO, and thus apply to Nagarpalikas."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The one thing that differs — the Schedule:** *"The Constitution also mandated the transfer of a list of functions from the State government to the urban local bodies. **THESE FUNCTIONS HAVE BEEN LISTED IN THE TWELFTH SCHEDULE OF THE CONSTITUTION.**"*

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ The pairing you must never confuse
> | Amendment | Bodies | Schedule | Subjects |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **73rd** | **Panchayati Raj Institutions (rural)** | **ELEVENTH Schedule** | **29** |
> | **74th** | **Nagarpalikas (urban)** | **TWELFTH Schedule** | *(18, though the chapter does not state the number)* |
>
> **Memory hook: 73 → 11 → rural; 74 → 12 → urban. The smaller amendment number goes with the smaller schedule number.**

> *The chapter's own hope, worth remembering as a live policy question:* *"Can I hope that these urban local bodies will do something for **better housing for the slum dwellers? Or at least provide them toilets?**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 7 — IMPLEMENTATION: THE ACHIEVEMENTS AND THE FAILURES

### ⭐⭐ The record of implementation
- ⭐ **"ALL STATES HAVE NOW PASSED A LEGISLATION to implement the provisions of the 73rd and 74th amendments."**
- ⭐⭐ **"During the TEN YEARS since these amendments came into force (1994-2004), MOST STATES HAVE HAD AT LEAST TWO ROUNDS OF ELECTIONS to the local bodies."**
- ⭐⭐ **"States like MADHYA PRADESH, RAJASTHAN and a few others have in fact HELD THREE ELECTIONS so far."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The scale of Indian local democracy (a spectacular set of numbers — learn them)
| Body | Number |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Zilla Panchayats** | **more than 600** |
| ⭐⭐ **Block / intermediary Panchayats** | **about 6,000** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Gram Panchayats** | **2,40,000** |
| ⭐⭐ **City Corporations** | **over 100** |
| ⭐⭐ **Town Municipalities** | **1,400** |
| ⭐⭐ **Nagar Panchayats** | **over 2,000** |

⭐⭐⭐ **The headline comparison — the single most quotable statistic in the chapter:**
> **"MORE THAN 32 LAKH MEMBERS ARE ELECTED TO THESE BODIES EVERY FIVE YEARS. OF THESE, AT LEAST 13 LAKHS ARE WOMEN. In the State Assemblies and Parliament PUT TOGETHER we have LESS THAN 5,000 elected representatives. With local bodies, THE NUMBER OF ELECTED REPRESENTATIVES HAS INCREASED SIGNIFICANTLY."**

*(**32 lakh vs under 5,000** — that ratio is the measure of what the 73rd and 74th Amendments did to Indian democracy.)*

### ⭐⭐ Achievement 1 — Uniformity and a platform for participation
⭐ **"The 73rd and 74th amendments have created UNIFORMITY IN THE STRUCTURES of Panchayati Raj and Nagarpalika institutions ACROSS THE COUNTRY. THE PRESENCE OF THESE LOCAL INSTITUTIONS IS BY ITSELF A SIGNIFICANT ACHIEVEMENT and would CREATE AN ATMOSPHERE AND PLATFORM FOR PEOPLE'S PARTICIPATION IN GOVERNMENT."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Achievement 2 — Women in power (learn these figures)
*"As this reservation is also applicable for the positions of Sarpanch and Adhyaksha, **a large number of women elected representatives have come to occupy these positions.**"*

| Position | Number of women |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Adhyakshas in Zilla Panchayats** | **at least 200** |
| ⭐⭐ **Presidents of block / taluka panchayats** | **another 2,000** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Sarpanchas in Gram Panchayats** | **more than 80,000** |
| ⭐⭐ **Mayors in Corporations** | **more than 30** |
| ⭐⭐ **Adhyakshas of Town Municipalities** | **over 500** |
| ⭐⭐ **Nagar Panchayats headed by women** | **nearly 650** |

⭐⭐ **What this has achieved:**
1. **"Women have gained MORE POWER AND CONFIDENCE BY ASSERTING CONTROL OVER RESOURCES."**
2. **"Their presence in these institutions has given many women a GREATER UNDERSTANDING OF THE WORKING OF POLITICS."**
3. ⭐ **"In many cases, they have brought a NEW PERSPECTIVE AND A GREATER SENSITIVITY to discussions at local bodies."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The honest qualification — and the hopeful note:** *"**In many cases, WOMEN WERE UNABLE TO ASSERT THEIR PRESENCE OR WERE MERE PROXIES FOR THE MALE MEMBERS OF THEIR FAMILY WHO SPONSORED THEIR ELECTION. SUCH INSTANCES, HOWEVER, ARE BECOMING FEWER.**"*

*(The **"sarpanch pati"** phenomenon. Note how **Geeta Rathore's re-election from an unreserved seat** is the counter-example the chapter opens with.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Achievement 3 — A changed social profile
- ⭐⭐ **"While reservations for Scheduled Castes and Tribes are MANDATED by the constitutional amendment, MOST STATES HAVE ALSO MADE A PROVISION TO RESERVE SEATS FOR BACKWARD CASTES."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"As the Indian population has 16.2 PER CENT SCHEDULED CASTES and 8.2 PER CENT SCHEDULED TRIBES, ABOUT 6.6 LAKH ELECTED MEMBERS in the urban and local bodies HAIL FROM THESE TWO COMMUNITIES."**
- ⭐⭐ **"This has SIGNIFICANTLY ALTERED THE SOCIAL PROFILE OF LOCAL BODIES. These bodies have thus become MORE REPRESENTATIVE OF THE SOCIAL REALITY THEY OPERATE WITHIN."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The conflict this generates — and a wonderfully mature judgment on it:**
> **"Sometimes this leads to TENSIONS. THE DOMINANT SOCIAL GROUPS WHICH CONTROLLED THE VILLAGE EARLIER DO NOT WISH TO GIVE UP THEIR POWER. This leads to INTENSIFICATION OF STRUGGLE FOR POWER. ⭐⭐⭐ BUT TENSION AND STRUGGLE IS NOT ALWAYS BAD. WHENEVER THERE IS AN ATTEMPT TO MAKE DEMOCRACY MORE MEANINGFUL AND GIVE POWER TO THOSE WHO DID NOT ENJOY IT EARLIER, THERE IS BOUND TO BE SOME CONFLICT AND TENSION IN SOCIETY."**

*(Use this line in any answer on social justice: **conflict is evidence that redistribution of power is actually happening**, not evidence that it has failed. Note too that this is Ambedkar's objection being **worked through** rather than avoided.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ FAILURE 1 — Functions not transferred
- **"The Constitutional amendments assigned as many as 29 subjects to the local governments. All these subjects are related to functions linked to local welfare and development needs."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"The experience with the functioning of local government in the past decade has shown that LOCAL GOVERNMENTS IN INDIA ENJOY LIMITED AUTONOMY to perform the functions assigned to them. MANY STATES HAVE NOT TRANSFERRED MOST OF THE SUBJECTS TO THE LOCAL BODIES."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The consequence:** *"This means that **the local bodies CANNOT REALLY FUNCTION IN AN EFFECTIVE MANNER. Therefore, THE ENTIRE EXERCISE OF ELECTING SO MANY REPRESENTATIVES BECOMES SOMEWHAT SYMBOLIC.**"*
- ⭐⭐ **The sharper criticism:** *"**Some people criticise the formation of the local bodies because THIS HAS NOT CHANGED THE WAY IN WHICH DECISIONS ARE TAKEN AT THE CENTRAL AND THE STATE LEVEL. PEOPLE AT THE LOCAL LEVEL DO NOT ENJOY MUCH POWERS OF CHOOSING WELFARE PROGRAMMES OR ALLOCATION OF RESOURCES.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ FAILURE 2 — No money of their own (learn the two percentages)
- ⭐⭐ **"LOCAL BODIES HAVE VERY LITTLE FUNDS OF THEIR OWN. The DEPENDENCE of local bodies on the State and central governments for financial support HAS GREATLY ERODED THEIR CAPACITY TO OPERATE EFFECTIVELY."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The killer statistic:** *"**While rural local bodies RAISE 0.24% OF THE TOTAL REVENUES COLLECTED, they account for 4% OF THE TOTAL EXPENDITURE made by the government. SO THEY EARN MUCH LESS THAN THEY SPEND. THAT MAKES THEM DEPENDENT ON THOSE WHO GIVE THEM GRANTS.**"*

*(**0.24% of revenue, 4% of expenditure** — a roughly **17-fold gap**. This is the third-tier version of Chapter 7's *"immense responsibilities but very meagre revenue sources."*)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Box: Democratic decentralisation in Bolivia (the model of *fiscal* decentralisation)
> - ⭐ **"Bolivia is frequently cited as ONE OF THE MOST SUCCESSFUL CASES OF DEMOCRATIC DECENTRALISATION IN LATIN AMERICA."**
> - ⭐⭐ **"IN 1994, the POPULAR PARTICIPATION LAW decentralised power to the local level, (i) allowing for the POPULAR ELECTION OF MAYORS, (ii) DIVIDING THE COUNTRY INTO MUNICIPALITIES, and (iii) crafting a system of AUTOMATIC FISCAL TRANSFERS to the new municipalities."**
> - ⭐ **"Bolivia is divided into 314 MUNICIPAL GOVERNMENTS", headed by "POPULARLY-ELECTED MAYORS (*presidente municipal*) and a MUNICIPAL COUNCIL (*cabildo*). LOCAL ELECTIONS OCCUR NATIONWIDE EVERY FIVE YEARS."**
> - ⭐ **"Bolivian local governments have been entrusted with BUILDING LOCAL HEALTH AND EDUCATION FACILITIES, as well as MAINTENANCE OF THIS INFRASTRUCTURE."**
> - ⭐⭐⭐ **The crucial contrast with India:** *"**In Bolivia, 20% OF NATIONWIDE TAX COLLECTIONS ARE DISTRIBUTED AMONG MUNICIPALITIES ON A PER CAPITA BASIS.** While these municipalities may **LEVY TAXES ON MOTOR VEHICLES, URBAN PROPERTY, AND LARGE AGRICULTURAL PROPERTIES**, **FISCAL TRANSFERS PROVIDE THE BULK OF THE OPERATING BUDGET** for these units."*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **The lesson for India in one sentence: Bolivia made transfers *AUTOMATIC* and *FORMULA-BASED (per capita)*; India left them to State discretion — which is exactly why the State Finance Commission was created "to ensure that allocation of funds to the rural local governments will not be a political matter," and exactly why Indian panchayats still raise 0.24% and spend 4%.**

### ⭐⭐ Box: "Read an image" — two photographs worth remembering
1. **A flag bearing the slogan "WE ARE THE GOVERNMENT HERE IN THE VILLAGE!"** — ⭐ *"This flag is a symbol of the EXPECTATIONS of the people about local governments. **PEOPLE DON'T WANT ONLY FORMAL LAWS. THEY WANT GENUINE IMPLEMENTATION OF THOSE LAWS.**"*
2. **A photograph in which "the local *Sarkar* is sitting OUT IN THE SUN."** — *the implied point: the third tier often has **no building, no office, no infrastructure** of its own — the physical face of the 0.24% revenue problem.*

> *The chapter's own summing-up in the margin:* *"So, **the law is good but it is mostly on paper.** Is this what they call **the gap between theory and practice**?"*

## Conclusion
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"This experience suggests that LOCAL GOVERNMENTS CONTINUE TO BE AGENCIES IMPLEMENTING THE WELFARE AND DEVELOPMENT SCHEMES OF THE CENTRAL AND STATE GOVERNMENT."** ← *i.e. **agencies**, not **governments***
- ⭐⭐ **"GIVING MORE POWER TO LOCAL GOVERNMENT MEANS THAT WE SHOULD BE PREPARED FOR REAL DECENTRALISATION OF POWER."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"Ultimately, DEMOCRACY MEANS THAT POWER SHOULD BE SHARED BY THE PEOPLE; PEOPLE IN THE VILLAGES AND URBAN LOCALITIES MUST HAVE THE POWER TO DECIDE WHAT POLICIES AND PROGRAMMES THEY WANT TO ADOPT."**
- **"Democracy means decentralisation of power and giving more and more power to the people."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The closing line — memorise it:** **"The laws about local governments are AN IMPORTANT STEP IN THE DIRECTION OF DEMOCRATISATION. BUT THE TRUE TEST OF DEMOCRACY IS NOT MERELY IN THE LEGAL PROVISIONS BUT IN THE PRACTICE OF THOSE PROVISIONS."**

## Key Words (Glossary)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Local government** | Government at the **village and district level** — closest to the common people, dealing with their day-to-day life |
| ***Sabhas*** | Village assemblies believed to have existed **from the earliest times** in India |
| ***Panchayat*** | Literally an **assembly of five persons**, which resolved issues at the village level |
| **Local boards** | The **elected local bodies created after 1882** on **Lord Rippon's** initiative |
| **Community Development Programme (1952)** | The first post-independence effort to **promote people's participation in local development** |
| **P.K. Thungon Committee (1989)** | Recommended **constitutional recognition** for local bodies, with **periodic elections, appropriate functions and funds** |
| **73rd Amendment** | **Rural** local government — **Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)**; passed **1992**, in force **1993** |
| **74th Amendment** | **Urban** local government — **Nagarpalikas**; passed **1992**, in force **1993** |
| **Gram Panchayat** | The base tier, covering **a village or group of villages** |
| **Mandal / Block / Taluka Panchayat** | The intermediary tier — ⭐ **need not be constituted in smaller States** |
| **Zilla Panchayat** | The apex tier, covering the **entire rural area of the district** |
| **Gram Sabha** | **All adult members registered as voters in the panchayat area**; its **creation is mandatory** but its **role and functions are decided by State legislation** |
| ***Adhyaksha*** | The Chairperson of a panchayat body — ⭐ **reservations apply to this post too, at all three levels** |
| **Quota within quota** | One-third reservation for women applies **within** the SC, ST and backward-caste seats as well — so a seat may be reserved for a **Dalit woman** or an **Adivasi woman** |
| **Eleventh Schedule** | The **29 subjects** (formerly State List) to be transferred to **panchayats**, under **Article 243G** — which says the State legislature **"may"** endow them |
| **Twelfth Schedule** | The list of functions to be transferred to **urban local bodies** under the 74th Amendment |
| **PESA (1996)** | The **separate 1996 Act** extending the panchayat system to **Adivasi areas**, giving **more powers to the Gram Sabha**, whose **consent the elected panchayat must obtain** in many respects, so as to protect **traditional customs of managing forests and small water reservoirs** |
| **State Election Commissioner** | Appointed by the **State government** to conduct panchayat and municipal elections; **autonomous**, but ⭐ **NOT linked to or under the Election Commission of India** |
| **State Finance Commission** | Appointed **once in five years** to examine local bodies' finances and review revenue distribution **State↔local** and **rural↔urban** — so that allocation is **"not a political matter"** |
| **Urban area (Census definition)** | **Minimum population 5,000**; **at least 75% of male working population in non-agricultural occupations**; **density of at least 400 persons per sq. km** |
| **Popular Participation Law (Bolivia, 1994)** | Decentralised power via **elected mayors**, **314 municipalities**, and **automatic fiscal transfers — 20% of national tax collections distributed per capita** |

## Quick Recap (15 points)
1. ⭐ **Democracy needs an elected government at the local level too**, because local government is **closest to the people**, believes that **local knowledge and local interest are essential to democratic decision-making**, and can be approached **quickly and at minimum cost.** *"Strengthening local government is like strengthening democratic processes."*
2. ⭐⭐ **The two opening stories:** **Geeta Rathore** (Jamonia Talab, **Sehore, MP**) won a **reserved seat in 1995** and was re-elected from a **NON-reserved seat in 2000** for work on water tanks, a school building, roads, domestic violence, environment and afforestation — reservation as a **ladder, not a ceiling.** **Vengaivasal (Tamil Nadu)** refused to endorse a **1997** allotment of **two hectares to 71 government employees**; the **Kancheepuram Collector** ordered acquisition; the **single judge of the Madras High Court** upheld him, but the **Division Bench reversed it**, holding the order **"not only infringement of the powers of the Panchayats but a gross violation of the constitutional status of the Panchayats."** Both illustrate the transformation **since constitutional status in 1993.**
3. **Local government secures participation and accountability**, and is where *"common citizens can be involved in decision making concerning their lives, their needs and above all their development."*
4. ⭐⭐ **History:** self-governing ***sabhas*** from the earliest times → ***panchayats*** (assembly of five) → ⭐⭐ **elected local boards after 1882 under Viceroy Lord Rippon** → the **Congress pressed for effectiveness** after slow progress → **village panchayats in several provinces after the Government of India Act 1919**, continuing after **1935**.
5. ⭐⭐ **Gandhi pleaded for decentralisation of economic and political power**, seeing panchayats as **instruments of decentralisation and participatory democracy**, because the national movement resented **the concentration of power in the Governor General at Delhi**. ⭐ **"Independence must begin at the bottom. Thus every village will be a republic... Life will be a pyramid with the apex sustained by the bottom."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **Yet the Constitution said little:** local government was made a **State subject** and placed in the **non-justiciable, advisory Directive Principles.** **Two different reasons:** **Nehru** saw **extreme localism as a threat to national unity and integration** (Partition had produced a **strong unitary inclination**); **Ambedkar** and a powerful voice in the Assembly feared the **faction- and caste-ridden nature of rural society** would defeat the purpose. **Same caution, opposite grounds.** But **nobody denied the importance of people's participation**, and **Ananthasayanam Ayyangar** urged that villages be **"trained in the art of self-government"** and **reformed** first.
7. ⭐⭐ **Before 1992:** the **Community Development Programme (1952)** came first, a **three-tier Panchayati Raj was recommended for rural areas**, and **Gujarat and Maharashtra adopted elected local bodies around 1960.** But the **five failures** were: **too few powers and functions**, **financial dependence**, **many States not bothering to create elected bodies**, **bodies dissolved and handed to government officers**, and **indirect elections plus repeated postponement of elections.**
8. ⭐⭐⭐ **The reform path:** a review **after 1987** → the **P.K. Thungon Committee (1989)** recommended **constitutional recognition**, with **periodic elections, listed functions and funds** → amendments **introduced 1989** → ⭐ **passed 1992** → ⭐ **in force 1993**, with States given **one year** to align their laws. ⭐⭐ **73rd = rural (PRIs); 74th = urban (Nagarpalikas).** Note the irony: **decentralisation imposed on the States through a centralised process.**
9. ⭐⭐⭐ **73rd Amendment — six provisions.** ➊ A **uniform three-tier structure** — **Gram Panchayat** (village or group of villages), **Mandal/Block/Taluka Panchayat** (⭐ **need not exist in smaller States**), **Zilla Panchayat** (whole rural district) — plus the **mandatory Gram Sabha** of all registered adult voters, **whose role and functions are decided by State legislation.** ➋ **Direct election at all three levels**, a **five-year term**, and ⭐ **fresh elections within six months if a State dissolves a panchayat early** — where previously there were **indirect elections to district bodies and no such requirement.** ➌ **Reservations: one-third for women; SC and ST in proportion to population at all three levels; OBCs at the State's option** — ⭐⭐ applying **also to the Chairperson/*Adhyaksha* posts at all three levels**, and ⭐⭐⭐ **intersecting**, so that a Sarpanch's seat may require **a Dalit woman or an Adivasi woman.** ➍ **29 subjects from the State List listed in the Eleventh Schedule** for transfer — but **Article 243G says the State legislature "MAY" endow them**, and **each State decides how many.** ➎ ⭐ **A separate Act in 1996 (PESA) extended the system to Adivasi areas**, giving **more powers to the Gram Sabha** — whose **consent the elected panchayat must obtain** in many respects — to protect **traditional customs of managing forests and small water reservoirs**, on the principle that **local traditions of self-government be protected while introducing modern elected bodies.** ➏ A **State Election Commissioner**, **autonomous like the ECI but NOT linked to or controlled by it**, and a **State Finance Commission every five years** to examine local finances and review **State↔local** and **rural↔urban** revenue distribution, **"to ensure that allocation of funds... will not be a political matter."**
10. ⭐⭐⭐ **74th Amendment:** the **Census** defines urban as **population ≥ 5,000**, **≥ 75% of male working population in non-agricultural occupations**, and **density ≥ 400 per sq. km**; **about 31% of Indians were urban per the 2011 Census.** It **repeats the 73rd** on **direct elections, reservations, transfer of subjects, State Election Commission and State Finance Commission** — the difference being that urban functions are listed in the ⭐⭐ **TWELFTH Schedule.** **Remember: 73 → 11 → rural; 74 → 12 → urban.**
11. ⭐⭐ **Implementation:** all States legislated; **most held at least two rounds of elections in 1994–2004**, and **MP, Rajasthan and a few others held three.**
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **The scale:** **600+ Zilla Panchayats, ~6,000 block panchayats, 2,40,000 Gram Panchayats, 100+ city Corporations, 1,400 town Municipalities, 2,000+ Nagar Panchayats** — **more than 32 lakh members elected every five years, at least 13 lakh of them women**, against **fewer than 5,000** elected representatives in all the State Assemblies and Parliament combined.
13. ⭐⭐⭐ **Women's presence:** **200+ Zilla Panchayat *Adhyakshas*, 2,000 block/taluka Presidents, 80,000+ Gram Panchayat Sarpanchas, 30+ Mayors, 500+ Town Municipality *Adhyakshas*, ~650 Nagar Panchayats headed by women.** Women have gained **power and confidence by asserting control over resources**, a **greater understanding of politics**, and brought **a new perspective and greater sensitivity** — though **some were unable to assert themselves or were mere proxies for male family members, "such instances, however, are becoming fewer."**
14. ⭐⭐⭐ **Social profile:** SC/ST reservation is **mandated** and **most States also reserve for Backward Castes**; with **16.2% SC and 8.2% ST** in the population, **about 6.6 lakh elected members** come from these two communities, making local bodies **"more representative of the social reality they operate within."** ⭐ **This provokes tension because "the dominant social groups which controlled the village earlier do not wish to give up their power" — but "tension and struggle is not always bad. Whenever there is an attempt to make democracy more meaningful and give power to those who did not enjoy it earlier, there is bound to be some conflict."**
15. ⭐⭐⭐ **The two failures.** **(i) Functions:** local governments **"enjoy limited autonomy"** because **many States have not transferred most of the 29 subjects**, so **"the entire exercise of electing so many representatives becomes somewhat symbolic"**, and **people at the local level have little power over welfare programmes or resource allocation.** **(ii) Funds:** local bodies have **very little money of their own** — **rural local bodies raise 0.24% of total revenues but account for 4% of total expenditure**, so they **earn far less than they spend and depend on whoever gives them grants.** ⭐⭐ **Contrast Bolivia's Popular Participation Law (1994):** elected mayors, **314 municipalities**, and **automatic fiscal transfers of 20% of national tax collections distributed per capita**, on top of local taxes on **motor vehicles, urban property and large agricultural properties.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Hence the verdict: Indian local governments "continue to be AGENCIES implementing the welfare and development schemes of the central and State government" — and "the true test of democracy is not merely in the legal provisions but in the PRACTICE of those provisions."**

State Finance Commission every 5 years: State↔local + rural↔urban, so funding is "not a political matter."** **Urban = 5,000 population + 75% male non-agricultural + 400/sq km; 31% urban (2011).** **Numbers: 600+ Zilla, ~6,000 block, 2,40,000 Gram, 100+ Corporations, 1,400 Municipalities, 2,000+ Nagar Panchayats; 32 LAKH elected (13 lakh women) vs UNDER 5,000 in all Assemblies + Parliament.** **Women: 200 Zilla Adhyakshas, 2,000 block Presidents, 80,000 Sarpanchas, 30 Mayors, 500 Town Municipality heads, 650 Nagar Panchayats. Proxy problem shrinking.** **SC 16.2% + ST 8.2% → 6.6 lakh members. "Tension and struggle is not always bad."** **FAILURES: States haven't transferred subjects → "somewhat symbolic"; rural bodies RAISE 0.24% but SPEND 4%.** **BOLIVIA 1994 Popular Participation Law: elected mayors, 314 municipalities, AUTOMATIC transfer of 20% of national taxes per capita. BRAZIL: states constitutionally barred from interfering with municipal councils.** **Verdict: local bodies are still "AGENCIES" — "the true test of democracy is not merely in the legal provisions but in the practice of those provisions."**

## Try These Questions

### NCERT Exercises (with answers/pointers)
1. ⭐ **Do these situations strengthen or weaken panchayats as units of self-government?**
   | Situation | Verdict | Reasoning |
   |---|---|---|
   | **a.** *A State allows a big company to set up a huge steel plant affecting many villages; a Gram Sabha resolves that village people must be consulted and grievances redressed before any big industry is established* | ⭐⭐ **STRENGTHENS** | This is the **Vengaivasal principle** — the panchayat asserting its **constitutional status** and the right to be consulted on decisions affecting its area. It is the **Gram Sabha** functioning as a real deliberative forum, not a formality. |
   | **b.** *The government decides that 20% of all its expenditure would be done through the panchayats* | ⭐⭐⭐ **STRENGTHENS — the most powerful of all** | Money is the binding constraint: rural bodies **raise 0.24% and spend 4%.** Routing **20% of expenditure** through them attacks the root problem — exactly **Bolivia's model** (20% of national tax collections transferred automatically). *(Though note: **routing expenditure** still leaves them agents unless they also get **decision-making power over the schemes.**)* |
   | **c.** *A panchayat repeatedly demands funds for a village school building; officials refuse, saying funds are tied to certain other schemes and cannot be spent otherwise* | ⭐⭐⭐ **WEAKENS** | The textbook case of local bodies as **"agencies implementing the welfare and development schemes of the central and State government"** — with **no power of choosing welfare programmes or allocation of resources.** Tied funds mean the panchayat sets no priorities of its own. |
   | **d.** *The government divides village Dungarpur into two, merging parts into Jamuna and Sohana, so Dungarpur ceases to exist in government records* | ⭐⭐ **WEAKENS** | The State extinguishing a local unit **unilaterally** — the third-tier echo of Chapter 7's point that *"the very existence of a State... is in the hands of Parliament."* If a panchayat can simply be abolished from above, its **constitutional status** is hollow. Contrast **Brazil**, where **states are prohibited from interfering in municipal councils.** |
   | **e.** *A panchayat observes that water sources are depleting, and mobilises village youth to do voluntary work reviving old ponds and wells* | ⭐⭐ **STRENGTHENS** | **Local knowledge + local interest + participation** — precisely **Geeta Rathore's** water-tank renovation and water management. Note it needs **no transfer of funds**: this is self-government exercised through **collective energy.** *(Minor irrigation, water management and watershed development is **item 3 of the Eleventh Schedule**.)* |
2. **Five powers you would endow to village panchayats, with a two-line justification each.** *(Build from the Eleventh Schedule and the identified failures:)*
   - ⭐ **Untied funds — a fixed, formula-based share of State revenues.** *Because bodies that raise 0.24% but spend 4% cannot set priorities; tied grants reduce them to agencies. Bolivia's automatic per-capita transfer of 20% of tax collections is the model.*
   - ⭐ **Mandatory consent (not mere consultation) for land acquisition and industrial projects within the panchayat area.** *This is the Vengaivasal and steel-plant issue; without consent, the "constitutional status of Panchayats" can be overridden by a Collector's order.*
   - ⭐ **Control over primary schooling and primary health centres** *(Eleventh Schedule items 17 and 23). Local knowledge determines whether a school actually functions — timings, absent teachers, out-of-school girls.*
   - ⭐ **Authority over drinking water, minor irrigation and watershed management** *(items 3 and 11). Water is the most local of resources and the one where collective action works best — as Geeta Rathore's tanks and Exercise 1(e) show.*
   - ⭐ **Power to convene the Gram Sabha and a binding requirement that the annual budget and beneficiary lists be approved by it.** *Accountability needs a forum with teeth; today the Gram Sabha's "role and functions are decided by State legislation" and it often does not meet.*
3. ⭐⭐ **Reservations for socially disadvantaged groups under the 73rd Amendment, and how they changed village leadership.** → **Provisions:** **one-third of positions for women**; **SC and ST in proportion to population at all three levels**; **OBCs at the State's discretion** (and *"most States have also made a provision to reserve seats for Backward Castes"*); ⭐ applying **not only to ordinary members but to the *Adhyaksha*/Chairperson posts at all three levels**; and ⭐⭐ **operating within the SC/ST/backward-caste seats too**, so that a seat may be reserved for **a Dalit woman or an Adivasi woman.** **The change:** **13 lakh of 32 lakh** elected members are women — **80,000+ Sarpanchas, 2,000 block Presidents, 200+ Zilla *Adhyakshas*, 30+ Mayors**; and with **16.2% SC and 8.2% ST** in the population, **about 6.6 lakh** members come from these communities. **This "significantly altered the social profile of local bodies"**, made them *"more representative of the social reality they operate within"*, gave women **control over resources, understanding of politics, and a new perspective and greater sensitivity** — while producing **struggle with the dominant groups who "do not wish to give up their power."** ⭐ *"But tension and struggle is not always bad."*
4. ⭐⭐ **Main differences between local governments before and after the 73rd Amendment:**
   | Aspect | **Before** | **After** |
   |---|---|---|
   | Constitutional basis | Only a **non-justiciable Directive Principle**; a **State subject** | **Constitutional status** (1993); States compelled to align their laws within a year |
   | Structure | Varied; many States had **no elected local bodies at all** | **Uniform three-tier structure** in all States (intermediary tier optional in smaller States) |
   | Elections | Often **indirect**, especially to district bodies; **frequently postponed** | **Direct election at all three levels**, fixed **five-year term** |
   | Dissolution | **Bodies dissolved and handed over to government officers**, with no requirement of fresh polls | **Fresh elections within six months** of premature dissolution |
   | Conduct of elections | By the **State administration, under State government control** | By an **autonomous State Election Commissioner** |
   | Reservation | No mandate | **One-third women; SC/ST in proportion; chairperson posts included; quota within quota** |
   | Functions | **Not enough powers and functions** | **29 subjects listed in the Eleventh Schedule** for transfer *(though Art. 243G says "may")* |
   | Finances | **Wholly dependent, ad hoc** | **State Finance Commission every five years**, so allocation is *"not a political matter"* |
   | Gram Sabha | No requirement | **Mandatory creation** |
5. ⭐ **The conversation on women's reservation — write ~200 words.**
   - **Alok:** *reservations in local bodies ensure women's equal share in power.* → **Largely right**, and the numbers bear him out: **13 lakh women elected, 80,000+ Sarpanchas**, and reservation extending to **chairperson posts**. **Geeta Rathore** shows it can be a **ladder** — she won an open seat next time.
   - **Neha:** *presence is not enough; local budgets need separate provision for women.* → ⭐ **The strongest position.** The chapter concedes **some women "were mere proxies for the male members of their family."** Presence without **control over resources** is symbolic — and *"women have gained more power and confidence by ASSERTING CONTROL OVER RESOURCES."* **Gender budgeting** converts presence into outcomes. *(Note too the Eleventh Schedule item 25: **women and child development**.)*
   - **Jayesh:** *a local body takes care of all people, which automatically covers women.* → ⭐ **Refuted by the chapter's own history.** *"A non-elected village panchayat would naturally be dominated by the village elders, the rich and men from upper strata"* — **Ambedkar's exact objection.** "Automatic" inclusion is what failed for four decades; that is why reservation was needed. Note also that women Sarpanches are sometimes **harassed** precisely because power is being redistributed.
6. ⭐⭐ **Which concerns does the 73rd Amendment address?**
   | Concern | Addressed? |
   |---|---|
   | **a.** *Fear of replacement makes representatives accountable to the people* | ✅ **YES** — **direct elections at all three levels**, a **fixed five-year term**, and **compulsory fresh elections within six months** of dissolution, conducted by an **autonomous State Election Commissioner.** |
   | **b.** *The dominant castes and feudal landlords dominate the local bodies* | ✅ **YES** — the **reservation scheme** for SC/ST, women and (optionally) OBCs, extending to **chairperson posts** and operating as a **quota within quota.** This is the answer to **Ambedkar's objection.** |
   | **c.** *Rural illiteracy is very high; illiterate people cannot take decisions about the development of the village* | ❌ **NO — and the amendment rejects the premise.** The Constitution imposes **no educational qualification** on voters or candidates *(Ch. 3: "no restrictions of income, education or class or gender")*, and the whole rationale of local government is that **"local knowledge and local interest are essential ingredients for democratic decision making."** |
   | **d.** *To be effective the village panchayats need resources and powers to make plans for village development* | ⚠️ **PARTLY** — it lists **29 Eleventh Schedule subjects** and creates a **State Finance Commission**, but **Article 243G says the State "may" endow** them, so **"many States have not transferred most of the subjects"** and rural bodies still **raise 0.24% while spending 4%.** **Addressed on paper, not in practice.** |
7. ⭐⭐ **Rank the justifications for local government — and which did Vengaivasal rest on?**
   **Suggested ranking:** **(c) → (b) → (d) → (a).**
   - ⭐⭐⭐ **(c) "People know their area, needs, problems and priorities. By collective participation they should discuss and take decisions about their life."** — **First**, because it is a claim of **democratic right**, not of efficiency. The chapter's own formulation: *"democracy means that power should be shared by the people; people in the villages and urban localities must have the power to DECIDE what policies and programmes they want to adopt."* Every other justification is instrumental; this one is intrinsic.
   - **(b) Local plans have greater acceptability than officers' plans** — **second**: legitimacy follows from participation and makes implementation possible.
   - **(d) It is difficult for common people to contact State or national representatives** — **third**: real (*"it is convenient for the people to approach the local government... quickly and with minimum cost"*), but a matter of **access**, not of power.
   - **(a) Projects cost less with local involvement** — **last**: cost-efficiency is a welcome by-product, but if this were the main ground, an efficient **appointed officer** would do just as well — which is precisely the pre-1992 arrangement that failed.
   ⭐⭐ **Vengaivasal rested on (c)** — the Panchayat's claim was **not** that it would build cheaper or that the Collector was hard to reach, but that **it, and not the Collector, had the authority to decide about land within its area.** The Division Bench agreed, calling the government order a **"gross violation of the constitutional status of the Panchayats."** *(Secondarily (b): the resolution was demanded to manufacture acceptability the government had not earned.)*
8. ⭐⭐ **Which involve decentralisation? Why are the others insufficient?**
   | Option | Decentralisation? |
   |---|---|
   | **a.** *To hold election of the Gram Panchayat* | ⚠️ **Necessary but NOT sufficient.** Elections create **representation**; without transferred functions and funds *"the entire exercise of electing so many representatives becomes somewhat symbolic."* |
   | ⭐⭐⭐ **b.** *Decision by the villagers themselves about what policies and programmes are useful for the village* | ✅ **YES — this IS decentralisation.** It matches the Conclusion exactly: *"people in the villages and urban localities must have the POWER TO DECIDE what policies and programmes they want to adopt."* |
   | **c.** *Power to call a meeting of Gram Sabha* | ⚠️ **A procedural power only.** Convening a forum matters (and under **PESA** the Gram Sabha's **consent** is substantive), but by itself calling a meeting decides nothing. |
   | **d.** *A Gram Panchayat receiving the BDO's report on the progress of a State-government project* | ❌ **NO — the opposite.** The panchayat is a **recipient of information about somebody else's project** — the definition of an **"agency implementing the welfare and development schemes of the central and State government."** |
9. ⭐⭐ **Raghavendra Parpanna's field questions on decentralisation and primary education:**
   **a. Which day for the Gram Sabha meeting?** — *This is a question about **who gets excluded by the choice**.*
   | Option | Who is affected |
   |---|---|
   | **(i) A day specified by the BDO or Collector** | ❌ Convenient for **officials**, not villagers — and symbolically wrong: the agenda then belongs to the administration, not the Gram Sabha. |
   | **(ii) Day of the village *haat*** | ❌ Excludes **traders, vendors and most women**, who are occupied at the market — though it does mean people are already gathered. |
   | **(iii) Sunday** | ⚠️ Suits salaried people, **but the chapter's own case shows the cost: "No woman teacher attended the meeting as it was held on Sunday."** Also, agricultural labour does not observe a Sunday holiday. |
   | **(iv) *Naag Panchami* / *Sankranti*** | ❌ A **festival day** — people are busy with observances, and **choosing one community's festival can itself exclude others.** |
   ⭐ **Best answer: none of the four automatically.** Choose a day fixed **by the Gram Sabha itself**, avoiding the *haat*, festivals and peak agricultural operations, at an hour when **women, agricultural labourers and teachers** can all attend — and publicise it well in advance.
   **b. Which venue?**
   | Option | Assessment |
   |---|---|
   | **(i) Venue in the Collector's circular** | ❌ Again, the administration setting the terms. |
   | **(ii) A religious place** | ❌ **Excludes** those of other faiths, and in many villages, Dalits. |
   | **(iii) Dalit *mohalla*** / **(iv) Upper caste *tola*** | ❌ Either choice makes attendance a **caste statement**; the dominant groups may boycott the first, and Dalits may not feel safe in the second. |
   | ⭐⭐ **(v) The village school** | ✅ **Best** — a **neutral, public, secular space** that no group owns, physically accessible, and **thematically apt** for a meeting on schooling. |
   **c. What do you think of those proceedings as an instance of people's participation?** → ⭐⭐⭐ **It is participation in form and not in substance — the chapter's own "gap between theory and practice."** Note the four defects: **the agenda came from a district collector's circular**, not from the village; the **only business was an education RALLY and its ROUTE** — a display, not a decision; the meeting **never discussed the children who never attend, girls' education, the school building or the school timings** — i.e. **every question of substance was omitted**; and **the choice of Sunday excluded the women teachers**, the very people with the relevant knowledge. ⭐ This is exactly local bodies acting as **agencies implementing the schemes of higher governments** rather than as **self-government**. Contrast **Vengaivasal**, where the panchayat **refused** to pass the resolution an official demanded.
   **d. Treat the class as a Gram Sabha:** *set your own agenda — a village-wise survey of out-of-school children (especially girls); fixing school timings around children's work; the condition of the building, toilets and drinking water; teacher attendance; and mid-day meals — and pass a resolution assigning named responsibilities with a review date at the next meeting.*
