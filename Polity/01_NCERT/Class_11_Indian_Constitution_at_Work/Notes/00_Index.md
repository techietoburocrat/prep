## How the chapters connect (the architecture of the book)

1. **Chapter 1** sets up everything: a constitution **creates** government, **limits** it, **empowers** it, and gives a people its **identity**. Every later chapter is one of those functions in operation.
2. **Chapter 2** is the *limiting* function — Fundamental Rights, and the state's positive duties under the DPSPs.
3. **Chapters 3–6** are the four **institutions**: how representatives are chosen (3), who governs (4), who legislates and holds the executive to account (5), and who interprets and umpires (6).
4. **Chapters 7–8** are the **vertical** division of power — Centre vs States, then the third tier below the States.
5. **Chapters 9–10** step back to ask how the whole thing **survives** (amendment + interpretation = a living document) and what it is ultimately **for** (its philosophy).

## ⭐ The highest-value items in the book (if you revise nothing else)

- **Ch 1** — the **borrowed provisions table** (BIFUC), the Constituent Assembly dates and numbers, and **universal suffrage as the only undebated provision**
- **Ch 2** — the **five writs** (HM-PQC), **Article 21's judicial expansion**, and the **right to property's journey to Article 300A**
- **Ch 3** — the **1984 FPTP distortion** (48% votes → 415 seats) and the **five reasons India chose FPTP**
- **Ch 4** — **Article 74(1)**, the **pocket veto** (Zail Singh, 1986), and the **four effects of coalition politics**
- **Ch 5** — **Lok Sabha vs Rajya Sabha powers**, the **money-bill 14-day rule**, and **Question Hour vs Zero Hour**
- **Ch 6** — the **collegium's evolution (1982–98)**, the **Ramaswami case** as the definition of special majority, and **judicial review's two grounds**
- **Ch 7** — the **seven strong-centre provisions**, the **three phases of Centre–State relations**, and **Article 356's misuse**
- **Ch 8** — **Eleventh vs Twelfth Schedule**, **quota within quota**, and **"raise 0.24%, spend 4%"**
- **Ch 9** — the **three amendment methods**, the **special-majority arithmetic**, and the **three groups of amendments**
- **Ch 10** — **principled distance**, the **voice/opinion distinction**, and **"selective adaptation, not borrowing"**

## Suggested revision order

**Revision before a test / for Prelims:** **Ch 2 → Ch 5 → Ch 4 → Ch 6 → Ch 3 → Ch 7 → Ch 9 → Ch 8 → Ch 1 → Ch 10** — most fact-dense and most examined first.

**For Mains / PSIR answer-writing:** **Ch 10 → Ch 9 → Ch 7 → Ch 6** — these four carry the arguments and the quotable lines.
