# Chapter 7: Federalism

## 🎯 Chapter Outcome — what you should walk away with

> **The one thing this chapter exists to teach:** India built a deliberately **strong centre** because its framers had watched other federations break apart — and the word "federation" never once appears in the Constitution. Article 1 says "**Union of States**".

**After reading it, here is what you now understand:**

1. **Why federations failed elsewhere** — the USSR's over-centralisation and Russian domination, the West Indies Federation's weak centre (1958, dissolved 1962), Nigeria's regional, religious and oil-revenue splits. The chain the chapter draws is **domination → resentment → secession → civil war**.
2. **The four key ideas of federalism** — two levels of polity, two identities, a supreme written constitution, an independent judiciary to umpire — and the one place India departs: **single citizenship**, not dual.
3. **The three Lists, residuary powers to the Union** (which is why cyber law is central), and centralised finance — States carry "immense responsibilities" with "meagre revenue".
4. **The seven strong-centre provisions**, and why each counts: the very existence of States, emergency powers, financial dependence, the Governor's power to reserve bills, legislation on the State List via a Rajya Sabha resolution plus Article 257, the **All-India Services** (the Centre alone disciplines them), and Articles 33–34.
5. **The three phases of Centre–State relations** — Congress dominance through the 1950s–60s, the autonomy demands once opposition parties won States from the mid-1960s, and **mature federalism under coalitions from the 1990s**.
6. **The four kinds of autonomy demand** — over powers, finance, administration and culture/language — with the States and parties attached to each (Tamil Nadu, Punjab, West Bengal; DMK, Akali Dal, CPI-M; the 1977 Left Front document).
7. **Article 356 fully** — its exact trigger, Parliamentary ratification, the three-year limit, its sparing use until 1967 and misuse thereafter (Kerala 1959, dismissals without any test of majority), and that **the Supreme Court can examine the validity of its imposition**.
8. **Article 370's four residual differences** and the 2019 reorganisation into two Union Territories from 31 October 2019.
9. **The verdict**: "national unity cannot be built by streamlining differences" — federalism as a rainbow.

2. Why do the All-India Services count as a strong-centre provision, when States supervise them day to day?

## The Big Idea
**The chapter opens with two maps — India in 1947 and India in 2017 — and asks you to notice how dramatically they differ.** *"Boundaries of States have changed, names of States have changed, and the number of States has changed."*

- When India became independent, we had **provinces that the British government had organised only for ADMINISTRATIVE CONVENIENCE.**
- Then **more than 500 princely states merged** with the newly independent Indian union and **were joined to the existing provinces.**
- Since then **boundaries of States have been reorganised many times**, and **names changed according to the wishes of the people** — ⭐ **"Mysore changed to Karnataka and Madras became Tamil Nadu."**

⭐ **"In a way, these maps also tell us the story of functioning of federalism in India."**

**Four things this chapter delivers:** what **federalism** is; the **federal provisions** in the Indian Constitution; the **issues in Centre–State relations**; and the **special provisions** for certain States with a distinct composition and history.

## PART 1 — WHAT IS FEDERALISM?

### ⭐⭐ The puzzle: why did other federations break up while India held together?
*"USSR was one of the world's super powers, but **after 1989 it simply broke up into several independent countries.**"*

⭐⭐ **The reasons for the Soviet break-up (learn these — they are the diagnostic list):**
1. **"Excessive CENTRALISATION and CONCENTRATION OF POWER"**
2. **"The DOMINATION OF RUSSIA over other regions with independent languages and cultures of their own, e.g. Uzbekistan"**

**Other federations that failed or nearly failed:**
| Country | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Czechoslovakia** | **Division of the country** |
| **Yugoslavia** | **Division of the country** |
| **Pakistan** | **Division of the country** |
| ⭐ **Canada** | **"Came VERY CLOSE to a break up between the ENGLISH-SPEAKING and the FRENCH-SPEAKING regions"** |

⭐⭐ **The central question:** *"Isn't it a great achievement that **India, which emerged as an independent nation-state in 1947 after a painful partition, has REMAINED UNITED over seven decades**? What accounts for this achievement? Can we attribute it to the federal structure of governance?"*

⭐⭐ **The crucial answer:** *"**ALL the countries mentioned above WERE federations. Yet they could not remain united.** Therefore, **apart from adopting a federal CONSTITUTION, the NATURE of that federal system and the PRACTICE of federalism must also be important factors.**"*

> **This is the thesis of the entire chapter: a federal constitution is not enough — federal *practice* is what holds a country together.**

### ⭐⭐ Box: Federalism in the West Indies — "neither together nor apart"
*"You may have heard about the cricket team of West Indies. **But is there a country called West Indies?**"*
- **"Like India, West Indies was also colonised by the British."**
- ⭐ **"In 1958, the FEDERATION OF WEST INDIES came into being."**
- ⭐⭐ **Its two fatal features:** *"It had a **WEAK CENTRAL GOVERNMENT** and **the economy of each unit was INDEPENDENT.**"*
- ⭐ **"These features and POLITICAL COMPETITION AMONG THE UNITS led to the FORMAL DISSOLUTION of the federation in 1962."**
- ⭐⭐ **The second attempt:** *"Later, **in 1973, by the TREATY OF CHAGUARAMAS**, the independent islands established **joint authorities** in the form of:*
  - **a common legislature**
  - **a supreme court**
  - **a common currency**
  - **and, to a degree, a common market — known as the CARIBBEAN COMMUNITY**
- ⭐ **"The Caribbean Community has even a COMMON EXECUTIVE, and Heads of the governments of member countries are members of this executive."**
- ⭐⭐ **The punchline:** *"Thus, **the units could NEITHER LIVE TOGETHER as one country, NOR can they LIVE SEPARATELY!**"*

> **⭐ The lesson to draw:** the West Indies is the **mirror image of the Soviet failure.** The USSR broke up from **excessive centralisation**; the West Indies federation broke up from a **weak centre and independent economies.** **Federalism fails at both extremes** — which is exactly why India's framers chose a **strong centre within a federal frame.**

### ⭐⭐ Box: Federalism in Nigeria — when units do not trust each other
⭐ *"**If the regions and various communities do not trust each other, even a federal arrangement can fail to produce unity.** The example of Nigeria is instructive:"*
- **"Till 1914, Northern and Southern Nigeria were TWO SEPARATE BRITISH COLONIES."**
- ⭐ **"At the IBADAN CONSTITUTIONAL CONFERENCE OF 1950, Nigerian leaders decided to form a federal constitution."**
- ⭐⭐ **The three major ethnic groups and their regions:**
  | Ethnic group | Region controlled |
  |---|---|
  | **Yoruba** | **the West** |
  | **Ibo** | **the East** |
  | **Hausa-Fulani** | **the North** |
- ⭐ **"Their attempt to SPREAD THEIR INFLUENCE TO OTHER REGIONS led to FEARS AND CONFLICTS. These led to a MILITARY REGIME."**
- ⭐⭐ **The police question — a telling detail:** *"In the **1960 constitution**, **both federal and regional governments JOINTLY CONTROLLED the Nigerian police.** In the **military-supervised constitution of 1979, NO STATE was allowed to have ANY CIVIL POLICE.**"*
- ⭐ **"Though DEMOCRACY WAS RESTORED IN NIGERIA IN 1999, RELIGIOUS DIFFERENCES along with CONFLICTS OVER WHO WILL CONTROL REVENUES FROM THE OIL RESOURCES continue to present problems before the Nigerian federation. LOCAL ETHNIC COMMUNITIES RESIST CENTRALISED CONTROL OF THE OIL RESOURCES."**
- ⭐⭐ **"Thus, Nigeria is an example of OVERLAP OF RELIGIOUS, ETHNIC AND ECONOMIC DIFFERENCES among the units."**

> **The three-way comparison to carry into an answer:** **USSR** = over-centralisation + domination by one unit. **West Indies** = weak centre + independent economies + inter-unit competition. **Nigeria** = overlapping religious, ethnic and economic cleavages + absence of trust. **India** = a strong centre *plus* accommodation of diversity *plus* a political practice of cooperation.

### India's diversity — and its shared foundations
*"India is a land of **continental proportions and immense diversities.**"*
- ⭐ **"There are MORE THAN 20 MAJOR LANGUAGES and SEVERAL HUNDRED MINOR ones."**
- **"It is the home of several major religions."**
- **"There are SEVERAL MILLION INDIGENOUS PEOPLES living in different parts of the country."**

**But what we share:**
- **"In spite of all these diversities we share a COMMON LAND MASS."**
- ⭐ **"We have also participated in a COMMON HISTORY, especially when we FOUGHT FOR INDEPENDENCE."**

⭐⭐ **"This has led our national leaders to visualise India as a country where there is UNITY IN DIVERSITY. Sometimes it is described as UNITY WITH DIVERSITY."**

### ⭐⭐ Federalism has no fixed formula
⭐ **"Federalism does NOT consist of a set of FIXED PRINCIPLES which are applied to different historical situations. Rather, federalism as a principle of government has EVOLVED DIFFERENTLY IN DIFFERENT SITUATIONS."**

⭐ **"AMERICAN federalism — one of the FIRST MAJOR ATTEMPTS to build a federal polity — is DIFFERENT from GERMAN or INDIAN federalism."**

### ⭐⭐ The four key ideas of federalism (a core definition set)
| # | Idea | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐⭐ **Two sets of polities** | **"Essentially, federalism is an INSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM TO ACCOMMODATE TWO SETS OF POLITIES — one at the REGIONAL level and the other at the NATIONAL level. EACH GOVERNMENT IS AUTONOMOUS IN ITS OWN SPHERE."** ⭐ **"In some federal countries, there is even a system of DUAL CITIZENSHIP. INDIA HAS ONLY A SINGLE CITIZENSHIP."** |
| **2** | ⭐ **Two sets of identities and loyalties** | **"The people likewise have two sets of identities and loyalties — they belong to the REGION as well as the NATION. For example, we are Gujaratis or Jharkhandis AS WELL AS Indians."** **"Each level of the polity has DISTINCT POWERS AND RESPONSIBILITIES and has a SEPARATE SYSTEM OF GOVERNMENT."** |
| **3** | ⭐⭐ **A written constitution as the supreme source** | **"The details of this dual system of government are generally spelt out in a WRITTEN CONSTITUTION, which is considered to be SUPREME and which is also THE SOURCE OF THE POWER OF BOTH SETS OF GOVERNMENT."** ⭐ **"Certain subjects which concern the NATION AS A WHOLE — for example, DEFENCE or CURRENCY — are the responsibility of the union or central government. REGIONAL OR LOCAL MATTERS are the responsibility of the regional or State government."** |
| **4** | ⭐⭐ **An independent judiciary as umpire** | **"To PREVENT CONFLICTS between the centre and the State, there is an INDEPENDENT JUDICIARY to settle disputes. The judiciary has the powers to RESOLVE DISPUTES between the central government and the States on LEGAL MATTERS about the DIVISION OF POWER."** *(This is the Supreme Court's **Original Jurisdiction** from Ch. 6.)* |

### ⭐⭐ What actually makes a federation work — the practice, not the text
> **"REAL POLITICS, CULTURE, IDEOLOGY AND HISTORY determine the actual working of a federation. A CULTURE OF TRUST, COOPERATION, MUTUAL RESPECT AND RESTRAINT helps federations to function smoothly. POLITICAL PARTIES also determine the way a constitution would work."**

⭐⭐ **The warning about domination — the causal chain to memorise:**
> **"If ANY SINGLE UNIT or STATE or LINGUISTIC GROUP or IDEOLOGY comes to DOMINATE the entire federation, it could generate a DEEP RESENTMENT among people or units not sharing the dominant voice. These situations could lead to DEMANDS FOR SECESSION by the aggrieved units or could even result in CIVIL WARS. Many countries are embroiled in such conflict situations."**

**Domination → resentment → demands for secession → civil war.** *(Compare: Russia over Uzbekistan; the three ethnic groups in Nigeria; English vs French Canada; and — in the Indian case — the 1960s agitations against the imposition of Hindi.)*

### ⭐ Box: "Check your progress"
1. **"Who decides the powers of the central government in a federation?"** → ⭐ **The written CONSTITUTION**, which is *"considered to be supreme and which is also the source of the power of BOTH sets of government."* *(Recall Chapter 1: a constitution decides who should have how much power.)*
2. **"How are conflicts between the central government and the States resolved in a federation?"** → ⭐ **Legal disputes by the INDEPENDENT JUDICIARY** (the Supreme Court's Original Jurisdiction). ⭐ **But political demands for autonomy must be resolved through NEGOTIATION** — see Part 4.

## PART 2 — FEDERALISM IN THE INDIAN CONSTITUTION

### ⭐⭐ The pre-independence background
- **"Even before Independence, most leaders of our national movement were aware that to govern a large country like ours, it would be NECESSARY TO DIVIDE THE POWERS between provinces and the central government."**
- **"There was also awareness that Indian society had REGIONAL DIVERSITY and LINGUISTIC DIVERSITY. This diversity NEEDED RECOGNITION."**
- ⭐ **"People of different regions and languages had to SHARE POWER, and in each region, PEOPLE OF THAT REGION SHOULD GOVERN THEMSELVES. This was only logical if we wanted a democratic government."**

⭐⭐ **"The ONLY QUESTION was WHAT SHOULD BE THE EXTENT OF POWERS to be enjoyed by the regional governments."**

### ⭐⭐ How Partition changed the answer (an important causal link)
- **"In view of the AGITATION OF THE MUSLIM LEAGUE for greater representation to the Muslims, a COMPROMISE FORMULA TO GIVE VERY LARGE POWERS TO THE REGIONS was discussed during the negotiations BEFORE Partition."**
- ⭐⭐ **"ONCE THE DECISION TO PARTITION INDIA WAS TAKEN, the Constituent Assembly decided to frame a government that would be based on (i) the principles of UNITY and (ii) COOPERATION between the centre and the States and (iii) SEPARATE POWERS to the States."**

⭐⭐ **The single most important sentence in this section:** *"**The MOST IMPORTANT FEATURE of the federal system adopted by the Indian Constitution is the PRINCIPLE THAT RELATIONS BETWEEN THE STATES AND THE CENTRE WOULD BE BASED ON COOPERATION.** Thus, **while recognising DIVERSITY, the Constitution emphasised UNITY.**"*

### ⭐⭐ The word "federation" does not appear
⭐ **"Do you know for example, that the Constitution of India DOES NOT EVEN MENTION THE WORD FEDERATION?"**

> ### Bare text to memorise — **Article 1**
> *"**(1) India, that is Bharat, shall be a UNION OF STATES.**
> **(2) The States and the territories thereof shall be as specified in the FIRST SCHEDULE.**"*

*(Note the schedule distinction: **First Schedule** = the States and their territories, from this chapter. **Fourth Schedule** = the number of Rajya Sabha seats per State, from Chapter 5.)*

### Division of Powers
- **"There are TWO SETS of government created by the Indian Constitution: one for the entire nation called the UNION government (central government), and one for each unit or State called the STATE government."**
- ⭐ **"BOTH of these have a CONSTITUTIONAL STATUS and CLEARLY IDENTIFIED AREA OF ACTIVITY."**
- ⭐ **"If there is any dispute about which powers come under the control of the union and which under the States, this can be resolved by the JUDICIARY on the basis of the constitutional provisions."**
- **"The Constitution clearly demarcates subjects which are under the EXCLUSIVE DOMAIN of the Union and those under the States."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The three Lists (with the chapter's own examples — an absolutely certain exam table)
| **UNION LIST** | **STATE LIST** | **CONCURRENT LIST** |
|---|---|---|
| **Defence** | **Agriculture** | **Education** |
| **Atomic Energy** | **Police** | **Transfer of Property other than agricultural land** |
| **Foreign Affairs** | **Prison** | **Forests** |
| **War and Peace** | **Local Government** | **Trade Unions** |
| **Banking** | **Public Health** | **Adulteration** |
| **Railways** | **Land** | **Adoption and Succession** |
| **Post and Telegraph** | **Liquor** | |
| **Airways** | **Trade and Commerce** | |
| **Ports** | **Livestock and Animal Husbandry** | |
| **Foreign Trade** | **State Public Services** | |
| **Currency and Coinage** | | |
| ⭐ **"UNION LEGISLATURE ALONE can make laws on these matters"** | ⭐ **"NORMALLY ONLY the STATE LEGISLATURE can make laws on these matters"** | ⭐ **"BOTH Union and State Legislature can make laws on these matters"** |

### ⭐⭐ Residuary Powers
- ⭐ **"Include ALL OTHER MATTERS NOT MENTIONED IN ANY OF THE LISTS."**
- ⭐⭐ **The chapter's example: "CYBER LAWS."**
- ⭐⭐ **"UNION LEGISLATURE ALONE has the power to legislate on such matters."**

*(The "Check your progress" asks: **"Do you think that there is a need for mentioning Residuary powers separately? Why?"** → **Yes** — no constitution-maker can anticipate future subjects. **Cyber laws** did not exist in 1950; without a residuary clause, such subjects would fall into a legal vacuum, or require an amendment each time technology changes.)*

### ⭐⭐ The financial asymmetry — note this carefully
> **"One of the IMPORTANT ASPECTS of this division of powers is that ECONOMIC AND FINANCIAL POWERS ARE CENTRALISED IN THE HANDS OF THE CENTRAL GOVERNMENT by the Constitution. THE STATES HAVE IMMENSE RESPONSIBILITIES BUT VERY MEAGRE REVENUE SOURCES."**

*(The chapter's own analogy in the margin: *"It is like some families where the money is with the husband and the wife has to manage the household."* This is the direct answer to **"Why do the States feel dissatisfied about the division of powers?"** — look at the **State List**: agriculture, police, prisons, public health, land, local government — **enormous** responsibilities — against a Union List holding **banking, currency, foreign trade and railways** — the **revenue-generating** items.)*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 3 — FEDERALISM WITH A STRONG CENTRAL GOVERNMENT

### ⭐⭐ Why the framers wanted a strong centre (four reasons)
⭐ **"It is generally accepted that the Indian Constitution has created a STRONG CENTRAL GOVERNMENT."**

1. **Continental scale and diversity.** *"India is a country of **continental dimensions with immense diversities and social problems.**"* The framers *"believed that we required a **federal constitution that would ACCOMMODATE DIVERSITIES.**"*
2. ⭐⭐ **To prevent disintegration and drive change.** *"**But they also wanted to create a STRONG CENTRE to STEM DISINTEGRATION and BRING ABOUT SOCIAL AND POLITICAL CHANGE.**"*
3. ⭐⭐ **The integration task in 1947.** *"It was necessary for the centre to have such powers because **India at the time of independence was not only divided into provinces created by the British; but there were MORE THAN 500 PRINCELY STATES which had to be INTEGRATED into existing States, or NEW STATES had to be created.**"*
4. ⭐⭐ **The socio-economic agenda.** *"The makers of the Constitution also believed that the **SOCIO-ECONOMIC PROBLEMS of the country needed to be handled by a STRONG CENTRAL GOVERNMENT IN COOPERATION WITH THE STATES.** **POVERTY, ILLITERACY and INEQUALITIES OF WEALTH** were some of the problems that required **PLANNING AND COORDINATION.**"*

⭐⭐ **"Thus, the concerns for UNITY and DEVELOPMENT prompted the makers of the Constitution to create a strong central government."**

> ### ⭐⭐ T.T. Krishnamachari's explanation — the definitive quotation on centralisation
> *"Let me tell my honourable Friends in the House that **the DRIFT... in ALL constitutions has been TOWARDS THE CENTRE**... because of circumstances that have now come into being **that the States have become, ...federal or unitary, WELFARE STATES from being POLICE STATES**, and **the ultimate responsibility as for the ECONOMIC WELL-BEING of the country has become the PARAMOUNT RESPONSIBILITY OF THE CENTRE.**"*
> — **T.T. Krishnamachari, CAD, Vol. XI, pp. 955-956, 25 November 1949**
>
> ⭐ **The argument in one line: the shift from a "police state" to a "welfare state" is itself what drives power to the centre — in every constitution, not just India's.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The SEVEN provisions creating a strong centre (the core answer to Exercise Q7 — learn all seven)

#### ➊ Parliament controls the very existence of a State
- ⭐⭐ **"The VERY EXISTENCE of a State — INCLUDING ITS TERRITORIAL INTEGRITY — IS IN THE HANDS OF PARLIAMENT."**
- **"The Parliament is empowered to 'form a new State by SEPARATION OF TERRITORY from any State or by UNITING TWO OR MORE STATES...'."**
- ⭐ **"It can also ALTER THE BOUNDARY of any State or even ITS NAME."**
- ⭐ **The only safeguard:** *"The Constitution provides for some safeguards by way of **SECURING THE VIEW of the concerned State legislature.**"* *(Note: only its **view** — not its **consent**.)*

#### ➋ Emergency provisions
- ⭐⭐ **"The Constitution has certain VERY POWERFUL EMERGENCY PROVISIONS, which can TURN OUR FEDERAL POLITY INTO A HIGHLY CENTRALISED SYSTEM once emergency is declared."**
- ⭐ **"During an emergency, POWER BECOMES LAWFULLY CENTRALISED."**
- ⭐⭐ **"Parliament also assumes the power to MAKE LAWS ON SUBJECTS WITHIN THE JURISDICTION OF THE STATES."**

#### ➌ Financial powers — even in normal circumstances
- ⭐⭐ **"Items GENERATING REVENUE are under the control of the central government. Thus, the central government has MANY REVENUE SOURCES and THE STATES ARE MOSTLY DEPENDENT ON THE GRANTS AND FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE FROM THE CENTRE."**
- ⭐⭐ **Planning as a centralising force:** *"India adopted **PLANNING as the instrument of rapid economic progress** and development after independence. **PLANNING LED TO CONSIDERABLE CENTRALISATION OF ECONOMIC DECISION MAKING.** The **PLANNING COMMISSION appointed by the union government is the COORDINATING MACHINERY that CONTROLS AND SUPERVISES THE RESOURCES MANAGEMENT OF THE STATES.**"*
- ⭐⭐ **The discretion problem:** *"Besides, **the Union government uses its DISCRETION to give GRANTS AND LOANS to States. THIS DISTRIBUTION OF ECONOMIC RESOURCES IS CONSIDERED LOPSIDED and has led to CHARGES OF DISCRIMINATION AGAINST STATES RULED BY AN OPPOSITION PARTY.**"*

#### ➍ The Governor's powers
- ⭐ **"The Governor has certain powers to RECOMMEND DISMISSAL of the State government and the DISSOLUTION of the Assembly."**
- ⭐⭐ **The quieter but constant power:** *"Besides, **EVEN IN NORMAL CIRCUMSTANCES**, the Governor has the power to **RESERVE A BILL passed by the State legislature FOR THE ASSENT OF THE PRESIDENT.** **This gives the central government an opportunity to (i) DELAY the State legislation and also to (ii) EXAMINE such bills and (iii) VETO THEM COMPLETELY.**"*

#### ➎ Legislating on the State List, and the superiority of Union executive power
- ⭐ **"There may be occasions when the situation may demand that the central government needs to LEGISLATE ON MATTERS FROM THE STATE LIST. This is possible IF THE MOVE IS RATIFIED BY THE RAJYA SABHA."** *(Recall Chapter 5 — this is a **special power of the Rajya Sabha**.)*
- ⭐⭐ **"The Constitution CLEARLY STATES that EXECUTIVE POWERS OF THE CENTRE ARE SUPERIOR TO THE EXECUTIVE POWERS OF THE STATES."**
- ⭐ **"Furthermore, the central government MAY CHOOSE TO GIVE INSTRUCTIONS TO THE STATE GOVERNMENT."**

> ### ⭐⭐ Bare text to memorise — **Article 257(1)**
> *"**The executive power of every State shall be so exercised as NOT TO IMPEDE OR PREJUDICE the exercise of the executive power of the Union**, and **the executive power of the Union shall extend to the GIVING OF SUCH DIRECTIONS TO A STATE as may appear to the Government of India to be NECESSARY for that purpose.**"*

#### ➏ The integrated administrative system (the All-India Services)
- ⭐ **"We have an INTEGRATED ADMINISTRATIVE SYSTEM. The ALL-INDIA SERVICES are COMMON TO THE ENTIRE TERRITORY of India, and officers chosen for these services serve in the administration of the States."**
- ⭐⭐ **"Thus, an IAS officer who becomes the COLLECTOR, or an IPS officer who serves as the COMMISSIONER OF POLICE, are UNDER THE CONTROL OF THE CENTRAL GOVERNMENT. STATES CAN NEITHER TAKE DISCIPLINARY ACTION NOR CAN THEY REMOVE THESE OFFICERS FROM SERVICE."**
*(This is the point flagged forward from Chapter 4 — the bureaucracy chapter promised it would matter here, and it does.)*

#### ➐ Articles 33 and 34 — martial law protection, and AFSPA
- ⭐⭐ **"ARTICLES 33 AND 34 authorise the Parliament to PROTECT PERSONS IN THE SERVICE OF THE UNION OR A STATE in respect of any action taken by them DURING MARTIAL LAW to MAINTAIN OR RESTORE ORDER. This provision further strengthens the powers of the union government."**
- ⭐⭐ **"The ARMED FORCES SPECIAL POWERS ACT has been made on the basis of these provisions. THIS ACT HAS CREATED TENSIONS BETWEEN THE PEOPLE AND THE ARMED FORCES ON SOME OCCASIONS."**

**Mnemonic for the seven: "E-E-F-G-L-A-M"** → **E**xistence of States, **E**mergency, **F**inance/Planning, **G**overnor, **L**egislating on State List + superior executive power, **A**ll-India Services, **M**artial law (Arts. 33–34 / AFSPA).

### ⭐ Box: "Check your progress" — the unitary bias
1. **"Give two reasons for the claim that our Constitution has a UNITARY BIAS."** → Any two of the seven above; the strongest are **(i)** *"the very existence of a State including its territorial integrity is in the hands of Parliament"* and **(ii)** *"the executive powers of the centre are superior to the executive powers of the States"* (Art. 257). Add **residuary powers** to the Union, **single citizenship**, a **single integrated judiciary** (Ch. 6), **Governors appointed by the Centre**, and **All-India Services disciplinable only by the Centre.**
2. **"Do you think that: a strong centre makes the States weak? strong States will weaken the centre?"** → ⭐ **The chapter's own answer is that this is a false trade-off.** The federal principle it names is **COOPERATION**, not competition — *"while recognising diversity, the Constitution emphasised unity."* **The USSR (over-strong centre) and the West Indies (over-weak centre) both collapsed.** Strength at one level need not be subtracted from the other; what matters is **trust, cooperation, mutual respect and restraint.**

## ⭐⭐ PART 4 — CONFLICTS IN INDIA'S FEDERAL SYSTEM

### The structural source of conflict
*"The Constitution has vested **very strong powers in the centre.** Thus, **the Constitution RECOGNISES THE SEPARATE IDENTITY of the regions and YET GIVES MORE POWERS TO THE CENTRE.**"*

⭐⭐ **The logic of the resulting tension:** *"**Once the principle of identity of the State is accepted, it is quite natural that the States would expect a GREATER ROLE AND POWERS** in the governance of the State and the country as a whole. This leads to various demands from the States... **This leads to tensions and conflicts in the relations between the centre and the States.**"*

### ⭐⭐ The crucial distinction — legal disputes vs political demands
> **"While the LEGAL DISPUTES between the centre and the States (or between States) CAN BE RESOLVED BY THE JUDICIARY, DEMANDS FOR AUTONOMY ARE OF POLITICAL NATURE AND NEED TO BE RESOLVED THROUGH NEGOTIATIONS."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Centre–State Relations: the THREE PHASES (a guaranteed exam question)
⭐ **The framing insight:** *"**The Constitution is only a FRAMEWORK or a SKELETON; its FLESH AND BLOOD is provided by the ACTUAL PROCESSES OF POLITICS.** Hence federalism in India has to a large extent been **influenced by the CHANGING NATURE OF THE POLITICAL PROCESS.**"*

| Phase | Period | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Phase 1 — Foundation** | **1950s and early 1960s, under Jawaharlal Nehru** | **"A period of CONGRESS DOMINANCE over the centre AS WELL AS the States."** ⭐ **"EXCEPT ON THE ISSUE OF FORMATION OF NEW STATES, the relations between the centre and the States remained QUITE NORMAL."** **"The States were HOPEFUL that they would be making progress with the help of the GRANTS-IN-AID from the centre."** **"Besides, there was considerable OPTIMISM about the policies of SOCIO-ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT designed by the centre."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Phase 2 — Autonomy demands** | **Middle of the 1960s onwards** | **"Congress dominance DECLINED somewhat and in a LARGE NUMBER OF STATES OPPOSITION PARTIES CAME TO POWER. It resulted in DEMANDS FOR GREATER POWERS AND GREATER AUTONOMY to the States."** ⭐⭐ **"In fact, these demands were a DIRECT FALLOUT of the fact that DIFFERENT PARTIES WERE RULING AT THE CENTRE AND IN MANY STATES."** **"So, the State governments were PROTESTING AGAINST WHAT THEY SAW AS UNNECESSARY INTERFERENCE in their governments by the Congress government at the centre. The Congress too was NOT VERY COMFORTABLE with the idea of dealing with governments led by opposition parties."** ⭐ **"This peculiar political context GAVE BIRTH TO A DISCUSSION ABOUT THE CONCEPT OF AUTONOMY under a federal system."** ⭐ **"Thus, it is IN THE SECOND PHASE that the issue of autonomy became VERY POTENT POLITICALLY."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Phase 3 — Mature federalism** | **Since the 1990s** | **"Congress dominance has LARGELY ENDED and we have entered an ERA OF COALITION POLITICS especially at the centre. In the States too, DIFFERENT PARTIES, BOTH NATIONAL AND REGIONAL, have come to power."** ⭐⭐ **"This has resulted in (i) a GREATER SAY FOR THE STATES, (ii) a RESPECT FOR DIVERSITY, and (iii) the BEGINNING OF A MORE MATURE FEDERALISM."** |

> *The chapter's own realisation in the margin:* *"This is quite interesting. So, **laws and constitutions alone do not decide everything. After all, actual politics decides the nature of our government!**"* — **exactly the thesis flagged at the start of the chapter.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 5 — DEMANDS FOR AUTONOMY (the four kinds)

⭐ **"However, 'AUTONOMY' REFERS TO DIFFERENT THINGS FOR DIFFERENT STATES AND PARTIES."**

| # | Kind of autonomy | Content and examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐⭐ **Change in the division of powers** | *"These demands expect that **the DIVISION OF POWERS SHOULD BE CHANGED IN FAVOUR OF THE STATES** and more powers and important powers be assigned to the States."* ⭐⭐ **States: TAMIL NADU, PUNJAB, WEST BENGAL. Parties: DMK, AKALI DAL, CPI-M.** |
| **2** | ⭐⭐ **Financial autonomy** | *"States should have **INDEPENDENT SOURCES OF REVENUE and GREATER CONTROL OVER THE RESOURCES.** This is also known as **FINANCIAL AUTONOMY.**"* ⭐⭐ **"In 1977, the LEFT FRONT GOVERNMENT IN WEST BENGAL brought out a DOCUMENT DEMANDING A RESTRUCTURING OF CENTRE-STATE RELATIONS in India."** ⭐ **"In the autonomy demands of TAMIL NADU and PUNJAB also, there was an IMPLICIT SUPPORT to the idea of greater financial powers."** |
| **3** | ⭐ **Administrative autonomy** | *"The third aspect of the autonomy demands relates to **ADMINISTRATIVE POWERS of the States. STATES RESENT THE CONTROL OF THE CENTRE OVER THE ADMINISTRATIVE MACHINERY.**"* *(i.e. the All-India Services problem.)* |
| **4** | ⭐⭐ **Cultural and linguistic autonomy** | *"Autonomy demands may also be related to **CULTURAL AND LINGUISTIC ISSUES.**"* ⭐⭐ **"The OPPOSITION TO THE DOMINATION OF HINDI (in TAMIL NADU), or the DEMAND FOR ADVANCING THE PUNJABI LANGUAGE AND CULTURE, are instances of this."** ⭐ **"Some States also feel that there is a DOMINATION OF THE HINDI-SPEAKING AREAS OVER THE OTHERS. In fact, DURING THE DECADE OF 1960s, THERE WERE AGITATIONS IN SOME STATES AGAINST THE IMPOSITION OF THE HINDI LANGUAGE."** |

> *A Shankar cartoon (**18 September 1949**) records that **during the discussion on the national language in the Constituent Assembly, Nehru had to APPEAL TO THE HINDI-SPEAKING PROVINCES TO SHOW GREATER TOLERANCE TOWARDS OTHERS.***

**Mnemonic for the four: "P-F-A-C"** → **P**owers (division), **F**inancial, **A**dministrative, **C**ultural/linguistic.

### ⭐ Box: "Check your progress" — autonomy vs secession
1. **"Why do States want more autonomy?"** → The four reasons above, rooted in the structural fact that **"the States have immense responsibilities but very meagre revenue sources"** while the Centre holds superior executive power, controls their key officers, appoints their Governors, and distributes grants at its **discretion**.
2. ⭐⭐ **"What is the difference between AUTONOMY and SECESSION?"** → **Autonomy = a demand for MORE POWERS *WITHIN* the federal union** — a redistribution of powers, revenues, administrative control or cultural recognition, pursued through **negotiation** and legitimate politics. **Secession = a demand to LEAVE the union altogether** and become a separate sovereign state. ⭐ **The chapter's own warning shows how one turns into the other:** if a single unit, language or ideology **dominates**, the resulting **resentment** can escalate *"to demands for secession by the aggrieved units or could even result in civil wars."* **Accommodating autonomy is therefore how a federation prevents secession.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 6 — ROLE OF GOVERNORS AND PRESIDENT'S RULE

### ⭐⭐ Why the Governor's office is controversial (four reasons)
⭐ **"The role of Governors has ALWAYS been a controversial issue between the States and the central government."**
1. ⭐ **"The Governor is NOT AN ELECTED OFFICE-HOLDER."**
2. ⭐ **"Many Governors have been RETIRED MILITARY OFFICERS or CIVIL SERVANTS or POLITICIANS."**
3. ⭐⭐ **"Besides, the Governor is APPOINTED BY THE CENTRAL GOVERNMENT, and therefore ACTIONS OF THE GOVERNOR ARE OFTEN VIEWED AS INTERFERENCE BY THE CENTRAL GOVERNMENT in the functioning of the State government."**
4. ⭐⭐ **"WHEN TWO DIFFERENT PARTIES ARE IN POWER AT THE CENTRE AND THE STATE, THE ROLE OF THE GOVERNOR BECOMES EVEN MORE CONTROVERSIAL."**

*(A Shankar cartoon, **27 April 1952**, with the caption: **"When Nehru was appointing governors, some were reluctant to quit ministerial chairs."**)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The Sarkaria Commission (memorise both dates)
| Fact | Detail |

| **Appointed by** | **The central government** |
| ⭐⭐ **Appointed in** | **1983** |

| **Mandate** | **"To examine the issues relating to CENTRE-STATE RELATIONS"** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Article 356 — President's Rule
⭐⭐ **"One of the MOST CONTROVERSIAL ARTICLES in the Constitution is ARTICLE 356, which provides for PRESIDENT'S RULE in any State."**

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The trigger (learn the exact words)** | *"This provision is to be applied when **'a situation has arisen in which the Government of the State CANNOT BE CARRIED ON IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE PROVISIONS OF THIS CONSTITUTION.'**"* |
| ⭐ **Effect** | **"It results in the TAKEOVER OF THE STATE GOVERNMENT BY THE UNION GOVERNMENT."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ratification** | **"The President's proclamation HAS TO BE RATIFIED BY PARLIAMENT."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Maximum duration** | **"President's rule can be EXTENDED TILL THREE YEARS."** |
| ⭐⭐ **The Governor's role** | **"The Governor has the power to RECOMMEND the DISMISSAL of the State government and the SUSPENSION OR DISSOLUTION of the State assembly."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The abuse of Article 356 — the record (learn these specifics)
- ⭐ **"This has led to MANY CONFLICTS."**
- ⭐⭐ **Two patterns of misuse:**
  1. **"In some cases, State governments were DISMISSED EVEN WHEN THEY HAD A MAJORITY IN THE LEGISLATURE — as had happened in KERALA IN 1959."**
  2. **"...or WITHOUT TESTING THEIR MAJORITY, as happened in SEVERAL OTHER STATES AFTER 1967."**
- ⭐⭐ **The judicial check:** *"**Some cases went to the Supreme Court, and the Court has ruled that CONSTITUTIONAL VALIDITY OF THE DECISION TO IMPOSE PRESIDENT'S RULE CAN BE EXAMINED BY THE JUDICIARY.**"* *(This is the point Chapter 6 made — that even the powers of the President and Governor were brought under judicial review; and Class 10's chapter on federalism describes it as "a major Supreme Court judgment that made it difficult for the Central Government to dismiss state governments in an arbitrary manner" — the *S.R. Bommai* case.)*
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The timeline of misuse:**
  - **"Article 356 was VERY SPARINGLY USED TILL 1967."**
  - **"AFTER 1967 many States had NON-CONGRESS GOVERNMENTS and the CONGRESS WAS IN POWER AT THE CENTRE."**
  - **"The centre has often used this provision to (i) DISMISS STATE GOVERNMENTS, or (ii) has used the OFFICE OF THE GOVERNOR TO PREVENT THE MAJORITY PARTY OR COALITION FROM ASSUMING OFFICE."**
  - ⭐⭐ **"For instance, the central government REMOVED ELECTED GOVERNMENTS IN ANDHRA PRADESH AND JAMMU AND KASHMIR IN THE DECADE OF 1980s."**

*(An R.K. Laxman cartoon caption puts it bluntly: **"Toppling the State governments. Everyone loves to play this game!"**)*

*"The national movement not only created a **pan-Indian national unity**; it also generated **DISTINCT UNITY AROUND A COMMON LANGUAGE, REGION AND CULTURE.**"*

⭐⭐ **"Our national movement was ALSO A MOVEMENT FOR DEMOCRACY. Therefore, IN THE COURSE OF THE NATIONAL MOVEMENT ITSELF, IT WAS DECIDED THAT AS FAR AS POSSIBLE, STATES WOULD BE CREATED ON THE BASIS OF COMMON CULTURAL AND LINGUISTIC IDENTITY."**

## ⭐⭐ PART 8 — INTERSTATE CONFLICTS

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 9 — SPECIAL PROVISIONS (Asymmetric Federalism)

⭐⭐ **"The MOST EXTRA-ORDINARY FEATURE of the federal arrangement created in India is that MANY STATES GET A DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT."**

### ⭐ Two forms of asymmetry
1. **In representation** — *"the size and population of each State being different, an **ASYMMETRICAL REPRESENTATION is provided in the RAJYA SABHA.** While ensuring **minimum representation to each of the smaller States**, this arrangement also ensures that **larger States would get more representation.**"* *(Recall Ch. 5: **UP 31 seats, Sikkim 1**.)*
2. **In powers** — *"In the case of division of powers too, the Constitution provides a division of powers that is **COMMON to all the States. AND YET, the Constitution has some SPECIAL PROVISIONS for some States GIVEN THEIR PECULIAR SOCIAL AND HISTORICAL CIRCUMSTANCES.**"*

### ⭐⭐ Which States have special provisions
- ⭐⭐ **"MOST of the special provisions pertain to the NORTH EASTERN STATES (ASSAM, NAGALAND, ARUNACHAL PRADESH, MIZORAM, etc.), LARGELY DUE TO THEIR SIZEABLE INDIGENOUS TRIBAL POPULATION WITH A DISTINCT HISTORY AND CULTURE."**
- ⭐⭐ **The candid admission:** *"**HOWEVER, THESE PROVISIONS HAVE NOT BEEN ABLE TO STEM ALIENATION AND THE INSURGENCY IN PARTS OF THE REGION.**"*
- ⭐⭐ **"Special provisions ALSO exist for HILLY STATES like HIMACHAL PRADESH, and some other States like ANDHRA PRADESH, GOA, GUJARAT, MAHARASHTRA, SIKKIM and TELANGANA."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Jammu and Kashmir — Article 370 and its abrogation (learn this whole sequence)

#### How J&K came to India
- ⭐ **"Jammu and Kashmir was ONE OF THE LARGE PRINCELY STATES, which had the option of JOINING INDIA or PAKISTAN or REMAINING INDEPENDENT."**
- ⭐⭐ **"However, IMMEDIATELY AFTER INDEPENDENCE IN OCTOBER 1947, PAKISTAN SENT TRIBAL INFILTRATORS from its side TO CAPTURE KASHMIR. THIS FORCED THE MAHARAJA HARI SINGH TO ASK FOR INDIAN HELP AND ACCEDED TO THE INDIAN UNION."**
- ⭐ **"MANY OF THE MUSLIM MAJORITY AREAS in the Western and Eastern parts JOINED PAKISTAN, BUT J&K WAS AN EXCEPTION. Under these circumstances, IT WAS GIVEN MUCH GREATER AUTONOMY by the Constitution."**

#### ⭐⭐⭐ What Article 370 actually did
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The core rule:** *"According to **Article 370, THE CONCURRENCE OF THE STATE WAS REQUIRED FOR MAKING ANY LAWS IN MATTERS MENTIONED IN THE UNION AND CONCURRENT LISTS.**"*
- ⭐⭐ **The contrast:** *"This was DIFFERENT from the position of other states. **In the case of the OTHER States, the division of powers as listed through three lists AUTOMATICALLY APPLIES.** In the case of Jammu and Kashmir, **the central government had only LIMITED POWERS, and other powers listed in the Union list and Concurrent list COULD BE USED ONLY WITH THE CONSENT OF THE STATE GOVERNMENT.** This gave greater autonomy to the State of Jammu and Kashmir."*

#### ⭐⭐ How much of the Constitution actually applied
- **"Earlier, there was a constitutional provision that allowed **the PRESIDENT, WITH THE CONCURRENCE OF THE STATE GOVERNMENT, to SPECIFY WHICH PARTS OF THE UNION LIST SHOULD APPLY TO THE STATE.**"*
- ⭐⭐ **"The President had issued TWO CONSTITUTIONAL ORDERS in concurrence with the government of J&K, MAKING LARGE PARTS OF THE CONSTITUTION APPLICABLE TO THE STATE."**
- ⭐⭐ **The important nuance:** *"As a result, **THOUGH J&K HAD A SEPARATE CONSTITUTION AND A FLAG, THE PARLIAMENT'S POWER TO MAKE LAWS ON SUBJECTS IN THE UNION LIST WAS FULLY ACCEPTED.**"*

#### ⭐⭐⭐ The four remaining differences (a precise, high-yield list)
*"The REMAINING differences between the other States and the State of J&K were:"*
1. ⭐⭐ **"NO EMERGENCY DUE TO INTERNAL DISTURBANCES could be declared in J&K WITHOUT THE CONCURRENCE OF THE STATE."**
2. ⭐⭐ **"The Union government COULD NOT IMPOSE A FINANCIAL EMERGENCY in the State."**
3. ⭐⭐ **"The DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES DID NOT APPLY in J&K."**
4. ⭐⭐ **"AMENDMENTS to the Indian Constitution (under Art. 368) could apply ONLY IN CONCURRENCE WITH THE GOVERNMENT OF J&K."**

#### ⭐⭐⭐ The present position
> **"At present, THE SPECIAL STATUS GIVEN UNDER 370 NO LONGER EXISTS. By the JAMMU AND KASHMIR REORGANISATION ACT 2019, the State has been BIFURCATED INTO TWO UNION TERRITORIES, viz., (i) JAMMU AND KASHMIR and (ii) LADAKH. THE NEW ARRANGEMENT HAS COME INTO EFFECT FROM 31 OCTOBER 2019."**

*(Note the constitutional significance: a **State was converted into two Union Territories** — the starkest possible illustration of provision ➊ above, that *"the very existence of a State including its territorial integrity is in the hands of Parliament."*)*

## Conclusion
> ⭐⭐ **"Federalism is like a RAINBOW, where EACH COLOUR IS SEPARATE, YET TOGETHER THEY MAKE A HARMONIOUS PATTERN."**

- ⭐ **"Federalism has to CONTINUOUSLY MAINTAIN A DIFFICULT BALANCE between the centre and the States."**
- ⭐⭐ **"NO LEGAL OR INSTITUTIONAL FORMULA CAN GUARANTEE THE SMOOTH FUNCTIONING OF A FEDERAL POLITY. Ultimately, THE PEOPLE AND THE POLITICAL PROCESS MUST DEVELOP A CULTURE AND A SET OF VALUES AND VIRTUES LIKE MUTUAL TRUST, TOLERATION AND A SPIRIT OF COOPERATION."**
- ⭐ **"Federalism CELEBRATES BOTH UNITY AS WELL AS DIVERSITY."**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The most quotable line in the chapter:** **"NATIONAL UNITY CANNOT BE BUILT BY STREAMLINING DIFFERENCES. SUCH FORCED UNITY ONLY GENERATES GREATER SOCIAL STRIFE AND ALIENATION AND TENDS FINALLY TO DESTROY UNITY."**
- ⭐⭐ **"A RESPONSIVE POLITY SENSITIVE TO DIVERSITIES AND TO THE DEMANDS FOR AUTONOMY CAN ALONE BE THE BASIS OF A COOPERATIVE FEDERATION."**

## Key Words (Glossary)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Federalism** | An **institutional mechanism to accommodate two sets of polities** — regional and national — each **autonomous in its own sphere** |
| **Unity in diversity / unity with diversity** | The Indian national leaders' vision of a country sharing a common land mass and a common history despite **20+ major languages**, several religions and millions of indigenous people |
| **Dual citizenship** | Citizenship of both the region and the nation, found in some federations — ⭐ **India has only SINGLE citizenship** |
| **Article 1** | *"India, that is Bharat, shall be a **Union of States**"*; the States and territories are specified in the **First Schedule** |
| **First Schedule** | Lists the **States and their territories** |
| **Union List** | Subjects on which **only the Union legislature** can make laws — defence, atomic energy, foreign affairs, war and peace, banking, railways, post and telegraph, airways, ports, foreign trade, currency and coinage |
| **State List** | Subjects on which **normally only the State legislature** can make laws — agriculture, police, prison, local government, public health, land, liquor, trade and commerce, livestock and animal husbandry, State public services |
| **Concurrent List** | Subjects on which **both** may legislate — education, transfer of property other than agricultural land, forests, trade unions, adulteration, adoption and succession |
| **Residuary powers** | **All matters not mentioned in any list** — e.g. **cyber laws**; **only the Union legislature** may legislate |
| **Article 257(1)** | A State's executive power must **not impede or prejudice** the Union's, and the Union may **give directions to a State** |
| **Articles 33 and 34** | Authorise Parliament to **protect persons in the service of the Union or a State** for actions taken under **martial law**; the basis of the **Armed Forces Special Powers Act** |
| **Integrated administrative system** | The **All-India Services** — an IAS Collector or IPS Commissioner is **under central control**; States can **neither discipline nor remove** them |
| **Planning Commission** | The union-appointed **coordinating machinery that controlled and supervised the resources management of the States** — a major centralising force |
| **Autonomy** | A demand for **more powers within** the union — over the **division of powers, finances, administration, or culture and language** |
| **Secession** | A demand to **leave the union** and become separate — the endpoint of unaccommodated resentment |
| **Sarkaria Commission** | Appointed **1983**, reported **1988**, to examine **Centre–State relations**; recommended that **Governors' appointments be strictly non-partisan** |
| **Article 356** | **President's Rule**, applicable when *"a situation has arisen in which the Government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution"*; must be **ratified by Parliament**; **extendable to three years** |
| **States Reorganisation Commission** | Set up in **December 1953**; recommended **linguistic States** for at least the major linguistic groups; acted on in **1956** |
| **River water tribunal** | The mechanism for settling water disputes — though the **Cauvery dispute reached the Supreme Court** |
| **Article 370** | Gave J&K special status: **the State's concurrence was required for laws on Union and Concurrent List matters**; **abrogated**, with the **J&K Reorganisation Act 2019** bifurcating the State into the **Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh** with effect from **31 October 2019** |

## Quick Recap (16 points)
1. ⭐ **The 1947 vs 2017 maps tell the story of Indian federalism** — provinces created for British **administrative convenience**, **500+ princely states** merged, boundaries reorganised repeatedly, and names changed by popular wish (**Mysore → Karnataka, Madras → Tamil Nadu**).
2. ⭐⭐ **The thesis: a federal constitution is not enough.** The **USSR** broke up (**1989**) from **excessive centralisation** and **Russia's domination** over regions like **Uzbekistan**; **Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia and Pakistan** divided; **Canada** came close to splitting between **English- and French-speaking** regions. **All were federations.** So *"the NATURE of that federal system and the PRACTICE of federalism must also be important factors."*
3. ⭐⭐ **The two failure modes:** the **West Indies federation (1958)** collapsed in **1962** from a **weak central government, independent unit economies, and political competition** — then rebuilt loosely as the **Caribbean Community** under the **Treaty of Chaguaramas (1973)** with a common legislature, supreme court, currency, part-market and even a common executive, so that *"the units could neither live together as one country, nor can they live separately."* **Nigeria** shows the trust problem: two separate colonies till **1914**, a federal decision at the **Ibadan Constitutional Conference of 1950**, three ethnic groups holding three regions (**Yoruba–West, Ibo–East, Hausa-Fulani–North**), **jointly controlled police in 1960 but no state civil police under the 1979 military-supervised constitution**, and — even after **democracy returned in 1999** — continuing conflict over **religion and oil revenues**: *"an overlap of religious, ethnic and economic differences."*
4. **Federalism has no fixed formula** — **American federalism**, the first major attempt, **differs from German and Indian.** Its **four key ideas:** two autonomous sets of polities (⭐ **India has only single citizenship**); two sets of identities and loyalties; a **supreme written constitution** as the source of both governments' power (nation-wide subjects like **defence and currency** to the Union); and an **independent judiciary** as umpire.
5. ⭐⭐ **What actually makes federations work:** *"real politics, culture, ideology and history"*, plus **trust, cooperation, mutual respect and restraint** — and **political parties.** ⭐ **The danger chain: domination by one unit/language/ideology → deep resentment → demands for secession → civil war.**
6. ⭐⭐ **Partition shaped India's answer.** A formula for **very large regional powers** had been discussed to meet the **Muslim League's** demand; **once Partition was decided**, the Constituent Assembly chose **unity + cooperation + separate powers for States.** ⭐ **The defining feature: Centre–State relations based on COOPERATION** — *"while recognising diversity, the Constitution emphasised unity."*
7. ⭐⭐ **The Constitution never uses the word "federation."** **Article 1: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"**, with States and territories in the **First Schedule.**
8. ⭐⭐ **Three Lists** — Union (**defence, atomic energy, foreign affairs, war and peace, banking, railways, post and telegraph, airways, ports, foreign trade, currency and coinage**), State (**agriculture, police, prison, local government, public health, land, liquor, trade and commerce, livestock and animal husbandry, State public services**), Concurrent (**education, transfer of non-agricultural property, forests, trade unions, adulteration, adoption and succession**) — plus **residuary powers to the Union alone**, e.g. **cyber laws.** ⭐⭐ **The key asymmetry: "economic and financial powers are centralised... the States have immense responsibilities but very meagre revenue sources."**
9. ⭐⭐ **Why a strong centre:** continental scale and diversity; to **stem disintegration and drive social and political change**; the **500+ princely states** to integrate; and **poverty, illiteracy and inequalities of wealth** needing **planning and coordination.** ⭐ **T.T. Krishnamachari (25 Nov 1949): "the drift in ALL constitutions has been towards the centre"**, because states have become **welfare states from being police states.**
10. ⭐⭐⭐ **The seven strong-centre provisions ("E-E-F-G-L-A-M"):** ➊ **Parliament controls a State's very existence, boundary and name** — only the State legislature's **view** is secured, not its consent; ➋ **emergency provisions turn the polity into a highly centralised system**, with Parliament legislating on State subjects; ➌ **revenue-generating items are central**, States depend on **grants**, **planning centralised economic decision-making** through the **Planning Commission**, and the **Centre's discretion over grants and loans is "lopsided"**, drawing **charges of discrimination against opposition-ruled States**; ➍ **the Governor can recommend dismissal and dissolution**, and even normally can **reserve a State bill for the President's assent**, letting the Centre **delay, examine and completely veto** it; ➎ **Parliament may legislate on the State List if ratified by the Rajya Sabha**, and **Article 257(1)** makes **Union executive power superior**, allowing **directions to a State**; ➏ the **All-India Services** — a Collector or Police Commissioner is under **central control**, and **States can neither discipline nor remove** them; ➐ **Articles 33 and 34** on **martial law**, the basis of **AFSPA**, which *"has created tensions between the people and the armed forces."*
11. ⭐⭐⭐ **Three phases of Centre–State relations:** **1950s–early 1960s under Nehru** — Congress dominance at both levels, relations **normal except over the formation of new States**, States hopeful of **grants-in-aid** and optimistic about central development policy; **mid-1960s onwards** — Congress dominance declined, **opposition parties took many States**, producing **autonomy demands as a "direct fallout"** of different parties ruling at the two levels; **since the 1990s** — **coalition politics** and national *and* regional parties in the States, giving **a greater say for the States, respect for diversity, and the beginning of a more mature federalism.** ⭐ *"The Constitution is only a framework or a skeleton; its flesh and blood is provided by the actual processes of politics."*
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **Four kinds of autonomy demand ("P-F-A-C"):** a **changed division of powers** (**Tamil Nadu, Punjab, West Bengal**; **DMK, Akali Dal, CPI-M**); **financial autonomy** (the **West Bengal Left Front's 1977 document** demanding restructuring of Centre–State relations, with implicit support from Tamil Nadu and Punjab); **administrative autonomy** (resentment of central control over the machinery); and **cultural/linguistic autonomy** (**Tamil Nadu's opposition to Hindi domination**, the demand for **Punjabi language and culture**, and the **1960s anti-Hindi agitations** — with **Nehru himself appealing to Hindi-speaking provinces for greater tolerance** in the Constituent Assembly).
13. ⭐⭐⭐ **The Governor and Article 356.** The Governor is **not elected**, is often a **retired military officer, civil servant or politician**, is **appointed by the Centre**, and becomes most controversial **when different parties rule at the two levels.** The **Sarkaria Commission (appointed 1983, reported 1988)** recommended appointments be **strictly non-partisan.** **Article 356** applies when *"the Government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution"*, must be **ratified by Parliament**, and can run up to **three years.** ⭐⭐ **Abuse:** governments **dismissed despite a legislative majority (Kerala, 1959)** or **without testing their majority (several States after 1967)**; **very sparingly used till 1967**, then used to dismiss governments or to **prevent a majority party or coalition from assuming office** — with **elected governments removed in Andhra Pradesh and Jammu and Kashmir in the 1980s.** ⭐ **The check: the Supreme Court has ruled that the constitutional validity of imposing President's Rule can be examined by the judiciary.**
14. ⭐⭐⭐ **New States:** the national movement itself decided States would follow **common cultural and linguistic identity.** **SRC set up December 1953** → **reorganisation in 1956** → **Gujarat and Maharashtra 1960** → **Punjab and Haryana separated 1966** → **North East reorganised (Manipur, Tripura, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh)** → **2000: MP → Chhattisgarh, UP → Uttarakhand, Bihar → Jharkhand**, driven by identity **plus administrative efficiency** → **2014: Telangana from Andhra Pradesh.** **Vidarbha** still demands statehood.
15. ⭐⭐⭐ **Inter-State disputes are legal in form but political in substance**, so they *"can best be resolved only through negotiations and mutual understanding."* **Border disputes** cannot be settled by linguistic majority alone because **border populations are multilingual** — **Maharashtra vs Karnataka over Belgaum**, **Manipur vs Nagaland**, and **Punjab vs Haryana over Chandigarh** (shared capital; **Rajiv Gandhi's 1985 understanding to hand it to Punjab has still not been implemented**). ⭐ **Water disputes are "even more serious" because they concern drinking water and agriculture** — the **Cauvery (Tamil Nadu vs Karnataka**, which **reached the Supreme Court despite a river water tribunal**) and the **Narmada (Gujarat, MP, Maharashtra)**.
16. ⭐⭐⭐ **Special provisions — "the most extra-ordinary feature."** Asymmetry exists in **Rajya Sabha representation** and in **powers.** Most special provisions cover the **North East (Assam, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram)** because of their **indigenous tribal population with a distinct history and culture** — though they **"have not been able to stem alienation and the insurgency"**; others cover **Himachal Pradesh** and **Andhra Pradesh, Goa, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Sikkim, Telangana.** ⭐⭐ **J&K:** a large princely state with three options; **Pakistan sent tribal infiltrators in October 1947**, forcing **Maharaja Hari Singh** to seek Indian help and accede. Under **Article 370, the State's concurrence was required for laws on Union and Concurrent List subjects** — unlike every other State, where the three Lists apply automatically. Two **Presidential orders** made large parts of the Constitution applicable, so **despite a separate constitution and flag, Parliament's Union List power was fully accepted.** **Four residual differences:** no **internal-disturbance emergency** without State concurrence; no **financial emergency**; **DPSPs did not apply**; and **Article 368 amendments needed State concurrence.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The special status no longer exists: the J&K Reorganisation Act 2019 bifurcated the State into the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, effective 31 October 2019.**

## 60-Second Revision
**Failures: USSR (over-centralisation + Russian domination over Uzbekistan) | West Indies 1958→dissolved 1962 (weak centre + independent economies) → Treaty of Chaguaramas 1973 → Caribbean Community | Nigeria (two colonies till 1914, Ibadan Conference 1950, Yoruba-West/Ibo-East/Hausa-Fulani-North, joint police 1960 vs no state police 1979, democracy 1999, religion + oil revenue) | Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Pakistan divided; Canada nearly split.** **Four key ideas: two polities (India = SINGLE citizenship), two identities, supreme written constitution, independent judiciary.** **Domination → resentment → secession → civil war.** **Art 1: "Union of States"; First Schedule. The word "federation" never appears.** **Union / State / Concurrent + residuary (cyber laws) to the Union. Financial powers centralised — "immense responsibilities, meagre revenue."** **T.T. Krishnamachari: "the drift in all constitutions has been towards the centre" — police state → welfare state.** **Seven strong-centre provisions: existence of States, emergency, finance/Planning Commission + discretionary grants, Governor (reserve bills → delay/examine/veto), State List via Rajya Sabha + Art 257 superior executive power, All-India Services (Centre alone disciplines), Arts 33-34 → AFSPA.** **Three phases: Nehru 1950s-60s (Congress dominance, normal except new States) → mid-1960s (opposition States → autonomy) → 1990s (coalitions → mature federalism).** **Four autonomy types: powers (TN/Punjab/WB; DMK/Akali Dal/CPI-M), financial (WB Left Front document 1977), administrative, cultural-linguistic (anti-Hindi agitations 1960s).** **Governor: not elected, centrally appointed. SARKARIA COMMISSION 1983 → report 1988 → non-partisan appointments.** **Art 356: "cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution"; Parliament ratifies; up to 3 years; sparingly used till 1967; Kerala 1959 (had majority); after 1967 without testing majority; AP and J&K removed in the 1980s; SC can examine validity.** **New States: SRC Dec 1953 → 1956 → Gujarat/Maharashtra 1960 → Punjab/Haryana 1966 → North East → 2000 (MP→Chhattisgarh, UP→Uttarakhand, Bihar→Jharkhand) → Telangana 2014; Vidarbha pending.** **Borders (sentiment): Belgaum (Mah-Kar), Manipur-Nagaland, Chandigarh (Rajiv Gandhi 1985 understanding, not implemented). Water (more serious — drinking water + agriculture): Cauvery (TN-Kar, reached SC despite tribunal), Narmada (Guj-MP-Mah).** **Special provisions: North East (tribal, distinct history — but insurgency persists), HP, AP/Goa/Guj/Mah/Sikkim/Telangana.** **J&K: Oct 1947 tribal infiltrators → Hari Singh accedes. Art 370 = State's concurrence for Union/Concurrent List laws; separate constitution + flag but Union List power accepted; four residual differences (no internal-disturbance emergency, no financial emergency, no DPSPs, Art 368 needed concurrence). J&K Reorganisation Act 2019 → two UTs (J&K + Ladakh) from 31 Oct 2019.** **"National unity cannot be built by streamlining differences." Federalism = a rainbow.**

### NCERT Exercises (with answers/pointers)
1. ⭐ **Which of these events would you identify with the functioning of federalism, and why?**
   | Event | Federalism? | Why |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Sixth Schedule status to the GNLF-led Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council**, via a **tripartite Memorandum of Settlement between the Centre, the West Bengal government and the GNLF** | ✅ **YES — the clearest case** | **Autonomy negotiated politically**, not litigated; a **third tier** within a State; and note the **tripartite** form — Centre + State + a regional movement. This is the chapter's own prescription: political demands resolved *"through negotiations."* |
   | **Centre asks rain-ravaged States to submit reconstruction plans for extra relief** | ✅ **YES** | **Centre–State financial relations** — the States' dependence on **grants and financial assistance from the centre.** |
   | **New municipal commissioner for Delhi; an IAS officer serving as Chief Secretary in Arunachal Pradesh replaces the incumbent** | ✅ **YES** | The **integrated administrative system** — an **All-India Service** officer moving between a State and a UT, appointed and controlled by the **Centre.** |
   | **Rajya Sabha passes a Bill converting Manipur University into a Central University** | ✅ **YES** | **Education is on the Concurrent List**; the Union legislating for a State institution, with a promise to extend it to **Arunachal Pradesh, Tripura and Sikkim.** |
   | **Centre releases Rs. 553 lakh to Arunachal Pradesh under the rural water supply scheme** | ✅ **YES** | **Central grants** to a State — the financial dependence described in provision ➌. |
   | **"We'll teach the Biharis how to live in Mumbai" — 100 Shiv Sainiks storm J.J. Hospital, threaten action against non-Maharashtrian students** | ❌ **NOT federalism** | This concerns **citizenship and freedom of movement/residence** (Fundamental Rights, Ch. 2). ⭐ **India has SINGLE citizenship** — an Indian may **reside and settle in any part of India.** This is **regional chauvinism**, the very *"forced unity/exclusion"* the conclusion warns against. |
   | **Congress Legislature Party petitions the State Governor to dismiss the ruling DAN government in Nagaland for financial mismanagement** | ✅ **YES** | Directly the **Governor + Article 356** issue — and precisely the pattern the chapter criticises: seeking dismissal of a government **through the Governor** rather than on the floor of the House. |
   | **Bihar government appeals to naxalites to surrender arms, amid an opposition walkout** | ❌ **NOT federalism** | **Police and public order are State List subjects** — this is a **State government dealing with its own law-and-order and legislature**, not a Centre–State matter. |
2. **Which statements are correct?**
   - ✅ *"Federalism enhances the possibility of people from different regions to interact without the fear of one's culture being imposed upon them by others."* → **CORRECT.** This is the chapter's core claim: *"national unity cannot be built by streamlining differences"*; the anti-Hindi-imposition episodes and the Nigerian and Soviet failures show what happens when one culture dominates.
   - ❌ *"Federal system will hinder easier economic transaction between two different regions that have distinct types of resources."* → **INCORRECT.** **Trade and commerce** appear on both the Union (foreign trade) and State lists, and the Constitution provides for a common economic space; **India's problem has been over-centralised economic decision-making, not obstructed inter-regional trade.**
   - ❌ *"A federal system will ensure that the powers of those at the centre will remain limited."* → **INCORRECT for India.** *"It is generally accepted that the Indian Constitution has created a STRONG central government"*, and **T.T. Krishnamachari noted the drift towards the centre in *all* constitutions.**
3. ⭐ **The Belgian Constitution's first articles — how is federalism visualised there, and write a similar Article for India.**
   **Belgium's design:** *Art. 1 — "Belgium is a Federal State made up of **communities and regions**"; Art. 2 — **three communities** (French, Flemish, German); Art. 3 — **three regions** (Walloon, Flemish, Brussels); Art. 4 — **four linguistic regions** (French-speaking, Dutch-speaking, the **bilingual** region of Brussels Capital, German-speaking), each commune belonging to one; Art. 5 — the provinces composing each region.*
   ⭐⭐ **The contrast to draw:** Belgium builds federalism on a **dual axis — territorial *regions* AND non-territorial *communities* (language groups)** — and explicitly names a **bilingual** region. **India, by contrast, declares itself simply a "Union of States" (Art. 1) and does not name languages or communities in its federal architecture at all**; it lists **States and territories in the First Schedule**, and handles linguistic diversity through **reorganisation, the Eighth Schedule and special provisions** rather than through the definition of the federation. **Belgium's is explicitly a "coming together" arrangement; India's is "holding together."**
   *A similar Article for India might read:* **"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States and Union Territories, made up of peoples of diverse languages, religions and cultures, each State being constituted so far as possible on the basis of a common linguistic and cultural identity, as specified in the First Schedule."**
4. ⭐ **Essay (≤300 words): rewrite the provisions regarding federalism, covering — (a) division of powers, (b) distribution of financial resources, (c) methods of resolving inter-State disputes, (d) appointment of Governors.**
   *Skeleton to build on:* **(a)** Retain the three Lists but move some Concurrent List items (education) toward the States; require **Rajya Sabha ratification with a special majority** before Parliament legislates on the State List; consider assigning **some residuary subjects** to the Concurrent List. **(b)** The core grievance — *"immense responsibilities but very meagre revenue sources."* Replace **discretionary** grants with a **formula-based, statutory** transfer; give States **independent revenue heads**; end the "lopsided distribution" that produces **charges of discrimination against opposition-ruled States** (the West Bengal Left Front's 1977 demand). **(c)** Strengthen **inter-State councils and river water tribunals** with binding timelines, since these disputes *"can best be resolved only through negotiations"* — the Cauvery case reached the Supreme Court **despite** a tribunal, and Chandigarh remains unresolved **40 years after Rajiv Gandhi's 1985 understanding.** **(d)** Implement the **Sarkaria Commission (1988)**: appointments **strictly non-partisan**, made in **consultation with the State's Chief Minister**, a **fixed tenure** removable only for cause, and codified limits on **reserving bills for the President** and on **recommending dismissal under Article 356.**
5. **Which should be the basis for the formation of a State? Why?**
   → ⭐ **(a) Common language** is the historically established Indian answer: *"in the course of the national movement itself, it was decided that as far as possible, States would be created on the basis of common cultural and linguistic identity"*, implemented through the **SRC (1953)** and **1956** reorganisation. **(b) Common economic interests / (d) administrative convenience** became a **supplementary** basis in **2000** ("greater administrative efficiency"), and were the *British* basis pre-1947. ⭐⭐ **(c) Common religion must be rejected** — the Constituent Assembly refused religion as a basis of political representation (separate electorates, Ch. 3), and **Nigeria** shows what happens when **religious cleavages overlap** with regional ones. *A good answer: language/culture as the primary basis, tempered by administrative viability — never religion.*
6. ⭐ **If Rajasthan, MP, UP and Bihar were combined into one Hindi-speaking State, would that be in tune with federalism?** → **No.** *Two arguments: **(i)** Size and governability — the chapter's rationale for **dividing** large States in 2000 was precisely **administrative efficiency**; a single mega-State would hold a **crushing share of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha seats**, and *"if any single unit... comes to dominate the entire federation it could generate a deep resentment."* **(ii)** Common language is **not sufficient** — the same reasoning that created **Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand out of Hindi-speaking parent States** shows that **region, culture and administrative need** matter alongside language. It would also aggravate the existing grievance that *"there is a domination of the Hindi-speaking areas over the others."*
7. **List four features of the Indian Constitution that give greater power to the central government.** → Any four of the seven in Part 3. The strongest four: **(i)** Parliament controls a State's **existence, boundary and name**; **(ii)** **emergency provisions** let Parliament legislate on State subjects; **(iii)** **Article 257** makes Union executive power **superior**, with power to **direct** States; **(iv)** the **All-India Services** — the State's key officers are appointed and disciplined **only by the Centre.** *(Add: residuary powers to the Union, centrally appointed Governors who can reserve State bills, and central control of revenue heads.)*
8. **Why are many States unhappy about the role of the Governor?** → The four reasons in Part 6 — **not elected**, typically a **retired military officer, civil servant or politician**, **appointed by the Centre** so actions look like **central interference**, and most contentious **when different parties rule at the two levels.** Plus the substantive powers: **recommending dismissal and dissolution**, and **reserving State bills for the President's assent**, which lets the Centre **delay, examine and veto** State legislation. Hence the **Sarkaria Commission's** recommendation of **strictly non-partisan** appointments.
9. ⭐⭐ **Is each situation a fit case for President's Rule?** *(The test is always the Article 356 words: has a situation arisen in which the government of the State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of the Constitution**? Not: is there a serious problem?)*
   | Situation | Fit case? | Reason |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Two opposition MLAs killed by criminals; the opposition demands dismissal** | ❌ **No** | A grave **law-and-order failure**, but **police and public order are State subjects** and the remedy is political and legal — not the takeover of an elected government on the opposition's demand. |
   | **Rising kidnapping of children for ransom; increasing crimes against women** | ❌ **No** | Serious **administrative failure**, but the constitutional machinery **has not broken down**. If ordinary crime justified Art. 356, no State government would be safe. |
   | **No party secured a majority; MLAs may be lured with money** | ⚠️ **Not immediately — and this is the classic abuse** | The correct course is for the **Governor to invite the leader most likely to command a majority and require a floor test** — recall **President Narayanan's 1998 procedure** (Ch. 4). ⭐ The chapter expressly condemns dismissal *"without testing their majority, as happened in several other States after 1967"* and the use of the Governor's office *"to prevent the majority party or coalition from assuming office."* Only if **no** government can be formed after a floor test does Art. 356 become available. |
   | **Different parties rule the State and the Centre and are bitter opponents** | ❌ **Absolutely not** | ⭐ **This is the paradigm case of misuse** — precisely what happened after **1967**. Political opposition is not constitutional breakdown. |
   | **More than 2,000 people killed in communal riots** | ✅ **Arguably yes** | A **complete collapse of the State's duty to protect life** on this scale can amount to the government not being carried on in accordance with the Constitution. *(But note the Court's power to examine the validity of the proclamation — the finding must be genuine, not political.)* |
   | **A State government refuses to follow a Supreme Court decision in a water dispute** | ✅ **Yes** | ⭐ **Open defiance of a binding judicial order is, almost by definition, a government not functioning "in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution"** — recall **Article 144**: *"all authorities, civil and judicial, shall act in aid of the Supreme Court."* |
10. **What are the demands raised by States in their quest for greater autonomy?** → The four kinds in Part 5, with their examples: **redistribution of powers** (Tamil Nadu, Punjab, West Bengal; DMK, Akali Dal, CPI-M), **financial autonomy** (West Bengal Left Front document, **1977**), **administrative autonomy** (control over the machinery), and **cultural/linguistic autonomy** (opposition to Hindi domination in Tamil Nadu; advancement of Punjabi language and culture; the **1960s** anti-Hindi agitations).
11. ⭐ **Should some States be governed by special provisions? Does this create resentment among other States? Does it help forge greater unity?**
    *Structure: **Yes, and it is "the most extra-ordinary feature" of Indian federalism.** **The case for:** provisions for the **North East** exist because of a **sizeable indigenous tribal population with a distinct history and culture**; asymmetry is already accepted in **Rajya Sabha representation**; and *"a responsive polity sensitive to diversities and to the demands for autonomy can alone be the basis of a cooperative federation."* **The case against / the risks:** the chapter itself concedes these provisions **"have not been able to stem alienation and the insurgency in parts of the region"**, and differential treatment can breed resentment — the resentment that made **Article 370** politically contested until its **2019** abrogation. **The resolution:** asymmetry forged through **negotiation and consent** (the Darjeeling tripartite settlement model) builds unity; asymmetry perceived as **arbitrary privilege or as imposed** breeds grievance. And the closing principle applies to both sides: **"national unity cannot be built by streamlining differences."***
