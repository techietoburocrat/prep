# Chapter 4: Social Justice

## 🎯 Chapter Outcome — what you should walk away with

> **The one thing this chapter exists to teach:** justice means **giving each person their due** — and what counts as "due" has shifted from *the station you occupy in the social order* to *your humanity*, measured in the opportunity to develop your talents.

**After reading it, here is what you now understand:**

1. **Why justice is "a lot like love"** — intuitively understood, passionately advocated, nobody argues against it — but unlike love it concerns **life in society**, the ordering of public life, and the distribution of social goods and duties.
2. **Socrates' two answers** to the challenge that the unjust prosper: if everyone were unjust nobody would be secure, so justice is in our **long-term interest**; and justice is not good-to-friends-harm-to-enemies but **"involves the well-being of all people"** — the just ruler as a doctor.
3. **Kant as the hinge** — human beings possess **dignity**, so what is due to each is the opportunity to develop talents and pursue chosen goals.
4. **The three principles of justice**, and how each works:
   - **Equal treatment for equals** — judged on work and actions, not group; ₹100 to one caste and ₹75 to another is unjust; a male teacher paid more than a female one is "unjustifiable and wrong".
   - **Proportionate justice** — equal marks for everyone who sits an exam would itself be unjust; reward in proportion to effort, skill and danger, **provided everybody starts from the same base line of equal rights**. (Which is why the chapter says miners, skilled craftsmen and policemen are under-rewarded.)
   - **Special needs** — it "does not *contradict* equal treatment so much as **extend** it"; treating the deprived identically with the privileged produces "an unequal society, not an egalitarian and just one".
5. **That the three principles conflict** and governments must harmonise them — captured in the blindfolded-justice problem: she is impartial, "but how then does she see the special needs of people?"
6. **Rawls' veil of ignorance properly** — you decide without knowing your family, caste or wealth, and you still reason **selfishly**; but since no one knows who they will be, each envisages society **from the point of view of the worst-off**. Its merit is that it needs no heroism: "fairness would be the outcome of **rational action, not benevolence or generosity**."
7. **What makes a society unjust** — the rich and poor "living in different worlds altogether", and the deprived having "no chance at all to improve their condition however hard they may work". Hence the **basic minimum**: nourishment, housing, clean water, education, a minimum wage.
8. **The free market's strongest argument and its decisive weakness.** Strongest: **neutrality** — "the market does not care about the caste or religion… if you have the merit, nothing else matters." Weakness: business goes where it is most profitable, so "free markets eventually tend to work in the interest of the strong, the wealthy and the powerful" — few private schools in remote rural areas, and quality out of reach of the poor.
9. **Ambedkar's definition of a just society** — one in which the "ascending sense of reverence and descending sense of contempt is dissolved into the creation of a compassionate society" — and **Mill's**: justice is what "some individual person can claim from us as his **moral right**", not charity.

**Self-check — answer these three without looking:**
1. Why does the veil of ignorance **not** require anyone to be selfless?
2. Which principle of justice do reservations rest on, and why is it not a contradiction of equal treatment?
3. State the free market's best argument, then the chapter's answer to it.

## The Big Idea (Overview)
⭐⭐ **The chapter's opening analogy — justice is like love:**
> **"Just as we INTUITIVELY UNDERSTAND WHAT LOVE MEANS even if we cannot explain all its different shades of meaning, WE ALSO HAVE AN INTUITIVE UNDERSTANDING OF JUSTICE even though we may not be able to DEFINE IT PRECISELY. In that sense JUSTICE IS A LOT LIKE LOVE."**

⭐ **The similarities:** *"Both love and justice **EVOKE PASSIONATE RESPONSES FROM THEIR ADVOCATES.** And as with love, **NO ONE HATES JUSTICE — EVERYONE WANTS JUSTICE FOR ONESELF AND TO SOME EXTENT FOR OTHERS ALSO.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **THE DECISIVE DIFFERENCE (this is the definition of *social* justice):**
> **"BUT UNLIKE LOVE — WHICH IS AN ASPECT OF OUR RELATIONSHIPS WITH A FEW PEOPLE WHOM WE KNOW WELL — JUSTICE CONCERNS (i) OUR LIFE IN SOCIETY, (ii) THE WAY IN WHICH PUBLIC LIFE IS ORDERED, AND (iii) THE PRINCIPLES ACCORDING TO WHICH SOCIAL GOODS AND SOCIAL DUTIES ARE DISTRIBUTED AMONG DIFFERENT MEMBERS OF SOCIETY. ⭐⭐ AS SUCH, QUESTIONS OF JUSTICE ARE OF CENTRAL IMPORTANCE FOR POLITICS."**

**Three learning objectives:** identify **principles of justice** put forward in different societies and periods; explain **distributive justice**; and discuss **John Rawls' argument** that a fair and just society **would be in the interest of all members and could be defended on RATIONAL grounds.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 1 — WHAT IS JUSTICE?

### ⭐⭐⭐ Justice across cultures and traditions (a high-yield comparative set)
⭐ **"ALL CULTURES AND TRADITIONS HAVE GRAPPLED WITH QUESTIONS OF JUSTICE, ALTHOUGH THEY MAY HAVE INTERPRETED THE CONCEPT IN DIFFERENT WAYS."**

| Tradition | Conception of justice |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Ancient India** | **"Justice was associated with *DHARMA*, and MAINTAINING *DHARMA* — OR A JUST SOCIAL ORDER — WAS CONSIDERED TO BE A PRIMARY DUTY OF KINGS."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **China — Confucius** | **"CONFUCIUS, the famous philosopher, argued that KINGS SHOULD MAINTAIN JUSTICE BY PUNISHING WRONG DOERS AND REWARDING THE VIRTUOUS."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Greece — Plato** | **"In FOURTH CENTURY B.C. ATHENS, PLATO discussed issues of justice in his book *THE REPUBLIC*."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Plato's dialogue — the challenge to justice
⭐⭐ **"Through a LONG DIALOGUE BETWEEN SOCRATES AND HIS YOUNG FRIENDS, GLAUCON AND ADEIMANTUS, Plato examined WHY WE SHOULD BE CONCERNED ABOUT JUSTICE."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The young men's challenge (a superb piece of cynical realism — learn it):**
> **"They observe that PEOPLE WHO WERE UNJUST SEEMED TO BE MUCH BETTER OFF THAN THOSE WHO WERE JUST. ⭐⭐ THOSE WHO (i) TWISTED RULES TO SERVE THEIR INTERESTS, (ii) AVOIDED PAYING TAXES AND (iii) WERE WILLING TO LIE AND BE DECEITFUL, WERE OFTEN MORE SUCCESSFUL THAN THOSE WHO WERE TRUTHFUL AND JUST. ⭐⭐⭐ IF ONE WERE SMART ENOUGH TO AVOID BEING CAUGHT, THEN IT WOULD SEEM THAT BEING UNJUST IS BETTER THAN BEING JUST."**
>
> ⭐ **"You may have heard people expressing SIMILAR SENTIMENTS EVEN TODAY."**

### ⭐⭐ Glaucon's account of how laws arose (the quoted extract)
> **"They say that TO DO INJUSTICE IS, BY NATURE, GOOD; TO SUFFER INJUSTICE, EVIL; BUT THAT THE EVIL IS GREATER THAN THE GOOD. And so when men have BOTH DONE AND SUFFERED INJUSTICE and have had experience of both, NOT BEING ABLE TO AVOID THE ONE AND OBTAIN THE OTHER, THEY THINK THAT THEY HAD BETTER AGREE AMONG THEMSELVES TO HAVE NEITHER; HENCE THERE ARISE LAWS AND MUTUAL COVENANTS; and that which is ordained by law is termed by them LAWFUL AND JUST."**
> — **Glaucon to Socrates in *The Republic***

*(⭐ This is the classic **social-contract** account of justice: laws arise as a **bargain** — since we cannot always escape suffering injustice, we agree to give up doing it. Note that on this view justice is merely **conventional**, not natural — which is exactly what Socrates goes on to reject.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Socrates' TWO answers

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Answer 1 — the argument from long-term self-interest
> **"Socrates reminds these young people that IF EVERYONE WERE TO BE UNJUST, IF EVERYONE MANIPULATED RULES TO SUIT THEIR OWN INTERESTS, NO ONE COULD BE SURE OF BENEFITING FROM INJUSTICE. ⭐⭐ NOBODY WOULD BE SECURE, AND THIS WAS LIKELY TO HARM ALL OF THEM. ⭐⭐⭐ HENCE, IT IS IN OUR OWN LONG-TERM INTEREST TO OBEY THE LAWS AND BE JUST."**

**⭐ Note that this is a *prudential* argument, not a moral one — and it prefigures Rawls exactly: rational self-interest, properly understood, leads to justice.**

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Answer 2 — what justice actually *means*
⭐ **"Socrates clarified that WE NEED TO UNDERSTAND CLEARLY WHAT JUSTICE MEANS IN ORDER TO FIGURE OUT WHY IT IS IMPORTANT TO BE JUST."**

⭐⭐⭐ **What justice is NOT:** *"He explained that **justice DOES NOT ONLY MEAN (i) DOING GOOD TO OUR FRIENDS AND HARM TO OUR ENEMIES, OR (ii) PURSUING OUR OWN INTERESTS.**"* *(Precisely the definitions demolished in Chapter 1's Socrates box.)*

⭐⭐⭐ **What justice IS:**
> **"JUSTICE INVOLVES THE WELL-BEING OF ALL PEOPLE."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **The doctor analogy:** *"**JUST AS A DOCTOR IS CONCERNED WITH THE WELL-BEING OF HIS/HER PATIENTS, SIMILARLY THE JUST RULER OR THE JUST GOVERNMENT MUST BE CONCERNED WITH THE WELL-BEING OF THE PEOPLE.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"ENSURING THE WELL-BEING OF THE PEOPLE INCLUDES GIVING EACH PERSON HIS DUE."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ "GIVING EACH PERSON HIS DUE" — and how its meaning has CHANGED
⭐⭐ **"The idea that JUSTICE INVOLVES GIVING EACH PERSON HIS DUE continues to be an IMPORTANT PART OF OUR PRESENT DAY UNDERSTANDING of justice. ⭐⭐⭐ HOWEVER, OUR UNDERSTANDING OF WHAT IS DUE TO A PERSON HAS CHANGED FROM THE TIME OF PLATO."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The modern basis — Kant and human dignity:**
> **"Today, our understanding of what is just is CLOSELY LINKED TO OUR UNDERSTANDING OF WHAT IS DUE TO EACH PERSON AS A HUMAN BEING. ⭐⭐ According to the GERMAN PHILOSOPHER IMMANUEL KANT, HUMAN BEINGS POSSESS DIGNITY. ⭐⭐⭐ IF ALL PERSONS ARE GRANTED DIGNITY, THEN WHAT IS DUE TO EACH OF THEM IS THAT THEY HAVE THE OPPORTUNITY TO (i) DEVELOP THEIR TALENTS AND (ii) PURSUE THEIR CHOSEN GOALS."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"JUSTICE REQUIRES THAT WE GIVE DUE AND EQUAL CONSIDERATION TO ALL INDIVIDUALS."**

**⭐⭐ The shift to grasp: in Plato's world, "his due" was fixed by one's *station* in the social order. Today it is fixed by one's *humanity* — every person's dignity entitles them to develop their talents and pursue their goals. This is the crucial change, and it is the answer to Exercise Q1.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 2 — THE THREE PRINCIPLES OF JUSTICE

⭐ **The problem:** *"Although there might be broad agreement in modern society about **the equal importance of all people**, **IT IS NOT A SIMPLE MATTER TO DECIDE HOW TO GIVE EACH PERSON HIS/HER DUE. A NUMBER OF DIFFERENT PRINCIPLES HAVE BEEN PUT FORWARD** in this regard."*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PRINCIPLE 1 — EQUAL TREATMENT FOR EQUALS

⭐⭐ **The basis:** *"It is considered that **ALL INDIVIDUALS SHARE CERTAIN CHARACTERISTICS AS HUMAN BEINGS. THEREFORE THEY DESERVE EQUAL RIGHTS AND EQUAL TREATMENT.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The three kinds of rights granted in most liberal democracies (learn all three)
| Kind of right | Content |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **CIVIL rights** | **"The rights of LIFE, LIBERTY AND PROPERTY"** |
| ⭐⭐ **POLITICAL rights** | **"Like the RIGHT TO VOTE, which enable people to PARTICIPATE IN POLITICAL PROCESSES"** |
| ⭐⭐ **SOCIAL rights** | **"Which would include the right to ENJOY EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES with other members of the society"** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The non-discrimination requirement
⭐⭐ **"Apart from equal rights, the principle of treating equals equally would require that PEOPLE SHOULD NOT BE DISCRIMINATED AGAINST ON GROUNDS OF CLASS, CASTE, RACE OR GENDER. ⭐⭐⭐ THEY SHOULD BE JUDGED ON THE BASIS OF THEIR WORK AND ACTIONS, AND NOT ON THE BASIS OF THE GROUP TO WHICH THEY BELONG."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The two worked examples (both are excellent, concrete illustrations)
| Example | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **"If TWO PERSONS FROM DIFFERENT CASTES PERFORM THE SAME KIND OF WORK — whether it be BREAKING STONES OR DELIVERING PIZZAS — THEY SHOULD RECEIVE THE SAME KIND OF REWARD. ⭐ If a person gets ONE HUNDRED RUPEES for some work and another receives ONLY SEVENTY FIVE RUPEES for the SAME work BECAUSE THEY BELONG TO DIFFERENT CASTES..."** | ⭐ **"...then it would be UNFAIR OR UNJUST."** |
| ⭐⭐ **"Similarly, if a MALE TEACHER in a school gets a HIGHER SALARY than a FEMALE TEACHER..."** | ⭐ **"...then this difference would also be UNJUSTIFIABLE AND WRONG."** |

*(Note the pairing: one example on **caste**, one on **gender** — and note also that "breaking stones or delivering pizzas" deliberately covers both the traditional and the modern economy.)*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PRINCIPLE 2 — PROPORTIONATE JUSTICE

⭐⭐⭐ **"HOWEVER, EQUAL TREATMENT IS NOT THE ONLY PRINCIPLE OF JUSTICE. ⭐⭐ THERE COULD BE CIRCUMSTANCES IN WHICH WE MIGHT FEEL THAT TREATING EVERYBODY EQUALLY WOULD BE UNJUST."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The examination example — the argument that makes the point unanswerable
> **"How, for instance, would you react if it was decided in your school that ALL THOSE WHO DID AN EXAM SHOULD GET EQUAL MARKS BECAUSE THEY ARE ALL STUDENTS OF THE SAME SCHOOL AND DID THE SAME EXAM?**
> ⭐⭐ **Here you might think IT WOULD BE MORE FAIR IF STUDENTS WERE AWARDED MARKS ACCORDING TO (i) THE QUALITY OF THEIR ANSWER PAPERS AND ALSO, POSSIBLY, (ii) THE DEGREE OF EFFORT THEY HAD PUT IN."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The formulation of the principle — note the crucial precondition:**
> **"In other words, ⭐⭐⭐ PROVIDED EVERYBODY STARTS FROM THE SAME BASE LINE OF EQUAL RIGHTS, JUSTICE IN SUCH CASES WOULD MEAN REWARDING PEOPLE IN PROPORTION TO THE SCALE AND QUALITY OF THEIR EFFORT."**

**⭐⭐ Do not miss the clause "provided everybody starts from the same base line of equal rights." Proportionate justice is legitimate ONLY on a level starting line — otherwise it merely rewards inherited advantage. This is why Principle 1 must come first.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The four criteria for rewarding different work differently
⭐⭐ **"Most people would agree that although people should get the SAME REWARD FOR THE SAME WORK, ⭐ IT WOULD BE FAIR AND JUST TO REWARD DIFFERENT KINDS OF WORK DIFFERENTLY IF WE TAKE INTO ACCOUNT FACTORS SUCH AS:"**
1. ⭐ **"THE EFFORT REQUIRED"**
2. ⭐ **"THE SKILLS REQUIRED"**
3. ⭐ **"THE POSSIBLE DANGERS INVOLVED IN THAT WORK"**
4. **"...and so on"**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The application — who is UNDER-paid in our society
> ⭐⭐ **"If we use these criteria we may find that CERTAIN KINDS OF WORKERS IN OUR SOCIETY ARE NOT PAID A WAGE WHICH TAKES SUCH FACTORS SUFFICIENTLY INTO ACCOUNT. For instance:**
> - **MINERS** *(danger)*
> - **SKILLED CRAFTSMEN** *(skill)*
> - ⭐ **"or people in SOMETIMES DANGEROUS BUT SOCIALLY USEFUL PROFESSIONS LIKE POLICEMEN"**
>
> **"...MAY NOT ALWAYS GET A REWARD WHICH IS JUST IF WE COMPARE IT TO WHAT SOME OTHERS IN SOCIETY MAY BE EARNING."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The conclusion:** *"**FOR JUSTICE IN SOCIETY, THE PRINCIPLE OF EQUAL TREATMENT NEEDS TO BE BALANCED WITH THE PRINCIPLE OF PROPORTIONALITY.**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PRINCIPLE 3 — RECOGNITION OF SPECIAL NEEDS

⭐⭐ **"A THIRD principle of justice which we recognise is FOR A SOCIETY TO TAKE INTO ACCOUNT SPECIAL NEEDS OF PEOPLE WHILE DISTRIBUTING REWARDS OR DUTIES. ⭐ THIS WOULD BE CONSIDERED A WAY OF PROMOTING SOCIAL JUSTICE."**

⭐⭐⭐ **Why the first two principles are insufficient:**
> **"In terms of their BASIC STATUS AND RIGHTS as members of the society, justice may require that people be treated equally. ⭐⭐ BUT EVEN (i) NON-DISCRIMINATION BETWEEN PEOPLE AND (ii) REWARDING THEM PROPORTIONATELY TO THEIR EFFORTS MIGHT NOT BE ENOUGH TO ENSURE (a) THAT PEOPLE ENJOY EQUALITY IN OTHER ASPECTS OF THEIR LIVES IN SOCIETY, NOR (b) THAT THE SOCIETY AS A WHOLE IS JUST."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The key logical point — Principle 3 EXTENDS rather than contradicts Principle 1
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"THE PRINCIPLE OF TAKING ACCOUNT OF THE SPECIAL NEEDS OF PEOPLE DOES NOT NECESSARILY CONTRADICT THE PRINCIPLE OF EQUAL TREATMENT SO MUCH AS EXTEND IT — BECAUSE THE PRINCIPLE OF TREATING EQUALS EQUALLY COULD IMPLY THAT PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT EQUAL IN CERTAIN IMPORTANT RESPECTS COULD BE TREATED DIFFERENTLY."**
>
> **⭐⭐ This is the answer to Exercise Q3, and it is elegant: "treat equals equally" logically entails "treat UNequals UNequally." Special provision is not an exception to the equality principle — it is a *consequence* of it.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Which inequalities deserve special help?
⭐⭐ **"People with special needs or disabilities could be considered UNEQUAL IN SOME PARTICULAR RESPECT and DESERVING OF SPECIAL HELP. ⭐ BUT IT IS NOT ALWAYS EASY TO GET AGREEMENT REGARDING WHICH INEQUALITIES OF PEOPLE SHOULD BE RECOGNISED FOR PROVIDING THEM SPECIAL HELP."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The grounds recognised in many countries (learn this list):**
1. **"PHYSICAL DISABILITIES"**
2. **"AGE"**
3. ⭐ **"LACK OF ACCESS TO GOOD EDUCATION OR HEALTH CARE"**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The core justification (a crucial sentence)
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"IT IS BELIEVED THAT IF PEOPLE WHO ENJOY VERY DIFFERENT STANDARDS OF LIVING AND OPPORTUNITIES ARE TREATED EQUALLY IN ALL RESPECTS WITH THOSE WHO HAVE BEEN DEPRIVED OF EVEN THE BASIC MINIMUM NEEDS TO LIVE A HEALTHY AND PRODUCTIVE LIFE, THE RESULT IS LIKELY TO BE AN UNEQUAL SOCIETY — NOT AN EGALITARIAN AND JUST ONE."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The Indian application — why caste enters the picture
⭐⭐ **"In our country, LACK OF ACCESS TO GOOD EDUCATION OR HEALTH CARE AND OTHER SUCH FACILITIES IS OFTEN FOUND COMBINED WITH SOCIAL DISCRIMINATION ON GROUNDS OF CASTE. ⭐⭐⭐ THE CONSTITUTION THEREFORE ALLOWED FOR (i) RESERVATIONS OF GOVERNMENT JOBS AND (ii) QUOTAS FOR ADMISSIONS TO EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS FOR PEOPLE BELONGING TO THE SCHEDULED CASTES AND TRIBES."**

*(⭐ **Note the precise logic: it is the COMBINATION of economic deprivation with social discrimination that makes caste the operative category** — not caste alone, and not poverty alone.)*

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE BLINDFOLDED STATUE OF JUSTICE — the chapter's sharpest puzzle
> **"Why is the statue of justice BLINDFOLDED?"**
> — **"She is blindfolded BECAUSE SHE NEEDS TO BE IMPARTIAL."**
> ⭐⭐⭐ — **"Of course she needs to be impartial. BUT I WONDER HOW THEN DOES SHE SEE THE SPECIAL NEEDS OF PEOPLE?"**

**⭐⭐ This margin exchange states the entire tension of the chapter in three lines: IMPARTIALITY requires blindness to who a person is; RECOGNISING SPECIAL NEEDS requires seeing exactly that. ⭐⭐⭐ Rawls' "veil of ignorance" (Part 4) is precisely the device that resolves it — you are blindfolded about your OWN identity, which forces you to attend to the needs of the worst-off.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ HARMONISING THE THREE PRINCIPLES — the government's real task

⭐⭐ **"Our discussion of different principles of justice has indicated that GOVERNMENTS MIGHT SOMETIMES FIND IT DIFFICULT TO HARMONISE THE THREE PRINCIPLES OF JUSTICE:"**
1. ⭐ **"EQUAL TREATMENT FOR EQUALS"**
2. ⭐ **"RECOGNITION OF DIFFERENT EFFORTS AND SKILLS WHILE DETERMINING REWARDS AND BURDENS"**
3. ⭐ **"PROVISION OF MINIMUM STANDARD OF LIVING AND EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES TO THE NEEDY"**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The two tensions between them (learn both directions)
| Tension | The chapter's words |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Equality vs merit** | **"PURSUING EQUALITY OF TREATMENT BY ITSELF MIGHT SOMETIMES WORK AGAINST GIVING DUE REWARD TO MERIT."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Merit vs the marginalised** | **"EMPHASISING REWARDING MERIT AS THE MAIN PRINCIPLE OF JUSTICE MIGHT MEAN THAT MARGINALISED SECTIONS WOULD BE AT A DISADVANTAGE IN MANY AREAS, BECAUSE THEY HAVE NOT HAD ACCESS TO FACILITIES SUCH AS GOOD NOURISHMENT OR EDUCATION."** |

⭐⭐ **The political consequence:** *"**DIFFERENT GROUPS IN THE COUNTRY MIGHT FAVOUR DIFFERENT POLICIES DEPENDING UPON WHICH PRINCIPLE OF JUSTICE THEY EMPHASISE.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The conclusion:** *"**IT THEN BECOMES A FUNCTION OF GOVERNMENTS TO HARMONISE THE DIFFERENT PRINCIPLES TO PROMOTE A JUST SOCIETY.**"*

## ⭐⭐ BOX: "Let's Think" — Is each of these just? (an excellent applied exercise)
*(For each, name the **principle of justice** being invoked.)*

| Situation | Verdict and principle |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **"SURESH, a VISUALLY IMPAIRED student, gets THREE HOURS AND THIRTY MINUTES to finish his mathematics paper, while the rest of the class gets only three hours"** | ✅ **JUST — PRINCIPLE 3 (recognition of special needs).** He is *"unequal in some particular respect and deserving of special help"*; **physical disability** is expressly named as a ground. The extra time equalises the **conditions of the examination**, not the marks. |
| ⭐⭐ **"GEETA WALKS WITH A LIMP. The teacher decided to give her ALSO three hours and thirty minutes"** | ❌ **NOT JUST — the trap in the list.** ⭐ **A limp is a disability, but it is IRRELEVANT TO WRITING A MATHEMATICS PAPER.** Special provision must be **matched to the specific disadvantage it offsets** — Suresh's impairment affects **reading and writing**; Geeta's affects **walking**. Giving her extra time confers an **unearned advantage**, which offends **Principle 1**. *(What Geeta may justly need is a **ground-floor examination hall**, not extra time.)* |
| ⭐⭐ **"A teacher gives GRACE MARKS TO THE WEAKER STUDENTS in class, TO BOOST THEIR MORALE"** | ❌ **NOT JUST as stated.** This violates **Principle 2** — *"students should be awarded marks according to the quality of their answer papers and the degree of effort."* Marks certify **performance**; altering them to boost morale **falsifies the certification** and is unfair to those who scored them. ⭐ **The just response to weakness is extra teaching, coaching or facilities — not adjusted marks.** *(Compare the chapter's own preferred remedy for the deprived: "special facilities that can help to develop talents and skills from an early age.")* |
| ⭐⭐ **"A professor distributes DIFFERENT QUESTION PAPERS TO DIFFERENT STUDENTS based on her evaluation of their capabilities"** | ❌ **NOT JUST.** It destroys the **"same base line"** that **Principle 2** requires — results become **incomparable**, and the professor's private **evaluation of capability** replaces the students' **demonstrated performance**. It also risks **entrenching low expectations** for those judged less capable. |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **"There is a proposal to RESERVE 33 PER CENT OF THE SEATS IN THE PARLIAMENT FOR WOMEN"** | ✅ **DEFENSIBLE — PRINCIPLE 3.** Women's under-representation is not a matter of choice but of **entrenched social disadvantage**; formal equal rights have not produced equal presence. ⭐ It is precisely the case that **"even non-discrimination and rewarding people proportionately to their efforts might not be enough."** *(The chapter's own logic; and see *Indian Constitution at Work* Ch. 3 — this became the ***Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam*, 2023.**)* ⚠️ **The counter-argument to acknowledge: critics say it is "reverse discrimination"; the standard reply is that it is time-bound and corrective.** |

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 3 — JUST DISTRIBUTION

⭐⭐ **"To achieve social justice in society, GOVERNMENTS MIGHT HAVE TO DO MORE THAN JUST ENSURE THAT LAWS AND POLICIES TREAT INDIVIDUALS IN A FAIR MANNER."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The definition of distributive justice:**
> **"SOCIAL JUSTICE ALSO CONCERNS THE JUST DISTRIBUTION OF GOODS AND SERVICES — WHETHER IT IS (i) BETWEEN NATIONS OR (ii) BETWEEN DIFFERENT GROUPS AND INDIVIDUALS WITHIN A SOCIETY."**

⭐⭐ **When redistribution becomes necessary:** *"**IF THERE ARE SERIOUS ECONOMIC OR SOCIAL INEQUALITIES IN A SOCIETY, IT MIGHT BECOME NECESSARY TO TRY AND REDISTRIBUTE SOME OF THE IMPORTANT RESOURCES OF THE SOCIETY TO PROVIDE SOMETHING LIKE A LEVEL PLAYING FIELD FOR CITIZENS.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The two-part requirement of social justice within a country:**
> **"Social justice would require NOT ONLY (i) THAT PEOPLE BE TREATED EQUALLY IN TERMS OF THE LAWS AND POLICIES OF THE SOCIETY, BUT ALSO (ii) THAT THEY ENJOY SOME BASIC EQUALITY OF LIFE CONDITIONS AND OPPORTUNITIES. ⭐⭐ THIS IS SEEN AS NECESSARY FOR EACH PERSON TO BE ABLE TO PURSUE HIS/HER OBJECTIVES AND EXPRESS HIMSELF."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The two Indian examples of redistribution
| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Abolition of untouchability** | **"In our country the Constitution ABOLISHED THE PRACTICE OF UNTOUCHABILITY to (i) PROMOTE SOCIAL EQUALITY and (ii) ENSURE THAT PEOPLE BELONGING TO 'LOWER' CASTES HAVE ACCESS TO TEMPLES, JOBS AND BASIC NECESSITIES LIKE WATER."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Land reforms** | **"Different STATE GOVERNMENTS have also taken some measures TO REDISTRIBUTE IMPORTANT RESOURCES LIKE LAND IN A MORE FAIR MANNER BY INSTITUTING LAND REFORMS."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Why these questions arouse such passion
⭐⭐ **"Differences of opinion on matters such as WHETHER, AND HOW, TO DISTRIBUTE RESOURCES AND ENSURE EQUAL ACCESS TO EDUCATION AND JOBS AROUSE FIERCE PASSIONS IN SOCIETY AND EVEN SOMETIMES PROVOKE VIOLENCE. ⭐ PEOPLE BELIEVE THE FUTURE OF THEMSELVES AND THEIR FAMILIES MAY BE AT STAKE."**

⭐⭐ **"We have only to remind ourselves about THE ANGER AND EVEN VIOLENCE WHICH HAS SOMETIMES BEEN ROUSED BY PROPOSALS TO RESERVE SEATS IN EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS OR IN GOVERNMENT EMPLOYMENT in our country."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The discipline the chapter asks of you (a fine statement of the purpose of studying political theory):**
> **"AS STUDENTS OF POLITICAL THEORY, HOWEVER, WE SHOULD BE ABLE TO CALMLY EXAMINE THE ISSUES INVOLVED IN TERMS OF OUR UNDERSTANDING OF THE PRINCIPLES OF JUSTICE."**

⭐⭐ **The question that leads into Rawls:** *"**CAN SCHEMES TO HELP THE DISADVANTAGED BE JUSTIFIED IN TERMS OF A THEORY OF JUSTICE?**"* — *"Rawls has argued that **there could indeed be a RATIONAL JUSTIFICATION for acknowledging the need to provide help to the least privileged members of a society.**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 4 — JOHN RAWLS' THEORY OF JUSTICE (the centrepiece of the chapter)

### ⭐⭐⭐ The problem Rawls sets out to solve
⭐⭐ **"If people are asked to choose the kind of society in which they would like to live, THEY ARE LIKELY TO CHOOSE ONE IN WHICH THE RULES AND ORGANISATION OF SOCIETY ALLOT THEM A PRIVILEGED POSITION."**

⭐⭐⭐ **And you cannot simply ask them to be selfless:** *"**WE CANNOT EXPECT EVERYONE TO PUT ASIDE THEIR PERSONAL INTERESTS AND THINK OF THE GOOD OF SOCIETY** — especially if they believe that **their decision is going to have an impact on the kind of life and opportunities their children will have in the future.** ⭐ **Indeed, WE OFTEN EXPECT PARENTS TO THINK OF AND SUPPORT WHAT IS BEST FOR THEIR CHILDREN.**"*

⭐⭐ **"BUT SUCH PERSPECTIVES CANNOT FORM THE BASIS OF A THEORY OF JUSTICE FOR A SOCIETY. ⭐⭐⭐ SO HOW DO WE REACH A DECISION THAT WOULD BE BOTH FAIR AND JUST?"**

### ⭐⭐⭐ RAWLS' ANSWER — the device
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"He argues that THE ONLY WAY WE CAN ARRIVE AT A FAIR AND JUST RULE IS IF WE IMAGINE OURSELVES TO BE IN A SITUATION IN WHICH WE HAVE TO MAKE DECISIONS ABOUT HOW SOCIETY SHOULD BE ORGANISED — ALTHOUGH WE DO NOT KNOW WHICH POSITION WE WOULD OURSELVES OCCUPY IN THAT SOCIETY."**

⭐⭐⭐ **What we must not know:** *"That is, **WE DO NOT KNOW (i) WHAT KIND OF FAMILY WE WOULD BE BORN IN, (ii) WHETHER WE WOULD BE BORN INTO AN 'UPPER' CASTE OR 'LOWER' CASTE FAMILY, (iii) RICH OR POOR, (iv) PRIVILEGED OR DISADVANTAGED.**"*

⭐⭐ **"Rawls argues that IF WE DO NOT KNOW, IN THIS SENSE, WHO WE WILL BE AND WHAT OPTIONS WOULD BE AVAILABLE TO US IN THE FUTURE SOCIETY, WE WILL BE LIKELY TO SUPPORT A DECISION ABOUT THE RULES AND ORGANISATION OF THAT FUTURE SOCIETY WHICH WOULD BE FAIR FOR ALL THE MEMBERS."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE 'VEIL OF IGNORANCE'
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"RAWLS DESCRIBES THIS AS THINKING UNDER A 'VEIL OF IGNORANCE'."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The mechanism, step by step (this is the argument you must be able to reproduce):**
1. **"He expects that in such a situation of COMPLETE IGNORANCE about our possible position and status in society, ⭐ EACH PERSON WOULD DECIDE IN THE WAY THEY GENERALLY DO — THAT IS, IN TERMS OF THEIR OWN INTERESTS."**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **"BUT SINCE NO ONE KNOWS WHO HE WOULD BE, AND WHAT IS GOING TO BENEFIT HIM, EACH WILL ENVISAGE THE FUTURE SOCIETY FROM THE POINT OF VIEW OF THE WORST-OFF."**
3. **"It will be clear to a person who can reason and think for himself that THOSE WHO ARE BORN PRIVILEGED WILL ENJOY CERTAIN SPECIAL OPPORTUNITIES. ⭐ BUT WHAT IF THEY HAVE THE MISFORTUNE OF BEING BORN IN A DISADVANTAGED SECTION OF SOCIETY, WHERE FEW OPPORTUNITIES WOULD BE AVAILABLE TO THEM?"**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **"HENCE, IT WOULD MAKE SENSE FOR EACH PERSON, ACTING IN HIS OR HER OWN INTEREST, TO TRY TO THINK OF RULES OF ORGANISATION THAT WILL ENSURE REASONABLE OPPORTUNITIES TO THE WEAKER SECTIONS."**
5. ⭐⭐ **"THE ATTEMPT WILL BE TO SEE THAT IMPORTANT RESOURCES — LIKE EDUCATION, HEALTH, SHELTER, ETC. — ARE AVAILABLE TO ALL PERSONS, EVEN IF THEY ARE NOT PART OF THE UPPER CLASS."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ WHY the veil of ignorance is the right device — the argument from human failings
⭐⭐ **The honest admission:** *"**IT IS OF COURSE NOT EASY TO ERASE OUR IDENTITIES AND TO IMAGINE ONESELF UNDER A VEIL OF IGNORANCE.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **But the alternative is worse:** *"**BUT THEN IT IS EQUALLY DIFFICULT FOR MOST PEOPLE TO BE SELF-SACRIFICING AND SHARE THEIR GOOD FORTUNE WITH STRANGERS. ⭐⭐ THAT IS WHY WE HABITUALLY ASSOCIATE SELF-SACRIFICE WITH HEROISM.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The design principle:** *"**GIVEN THESE HUMAN FAILINGS AND LIMITATIONS, IT IS BETTER FOR US TO THINK OF A FRAMEWORK THAT DOES NOT REQUIRE EXTRAORDINARY ACTIONS.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE MERIT OF THE DEVICE (memorise this — it is the heart of Rawls' appeal):**
> **"THE MERIT OF THE 'VEIL OF IGNORANCE' POSITION IS THAT IT EXPECTS PEOPLE TO JUST BE THEIR USUAL RATIONAL SELVES: THEY ARE EXPECTED TO THINK FOR THEMSELVES AND CHOOSE WHAT THEY REGARD TO BE IN THEIR INTEREST. ⭐⭐⭐ THE PERTINENT THING HOWEVER IS THAT WHEN THEY CHOOSE UNDER THE 'VEIL OF IGNORANCE', THEY WILL FIND THAT IT IS IN THEIR INTEREST TO THINK FROM THE POSITION OF THE WORST-OFF."**

**⭐⭐ The genius of the argument: it demands NO moral heroism. It asks only that you be selfish — but selfish while not knowing who you are. Self-interest under ignorance produces the same result as altruism.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The SECOND requirement — benefiting society as a whole
⭐⭐ **"Wearing the imagined veil of ignorance is THE FIRST STEP in arriving at a system of fair laws and policies. ⭐⭐ It will be evident that RATIONAL PERSONS WILL NOT ONLY SEE THINGS FROM THE PERSPECTIVE OF THE WORST-OFF; THEY WILL ALSO TRY TO ENSURE THAT THE POLICIES THEY FRAME BENEFIT THE SOCIETY AS A WHOLE. ⭐ BOTH THINGS HAVE TO GO HAND-IN-HAND."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The reasoning (an important and often-missed point):**
> **"Since no one knows what position they will occupy in the future society, EACH WILL SEEK RULES THAT PROTECT THEM IN CASE THEY HAPPEN TO BE BORN AMONG THE WORST-OFF. ⭐⭐ BUT IT WOULD MAKE SENSE IF THEY ALSO TRY TO ENSURE THAT THEIR CHOSEN POLICY DOES NOT ALSO MAKE THOSE WHO ARE BETTER-OFF WEAKER — BECAUSE IT IS ALSO POSSIBLE THAT THEY COULD BE BORN INTO A PRIVILEGED POSITION IN THE FUTURE SOCIETY."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"THEREFORE, IT WOULD BE IN THE INTERESTS OF ALL THAT SOCIETY AS A WHOLE SHOULD BENEFIT FROM THE RULES AND POLICIES THAT ARE DECIDED — AND NOT JUST ANY PARTICULAR SECTION."**

**⭐⭐ So the veil of ignorance cuts BOTH ways: it protects the worst-off (you might be them) AND protects the better-off from ruinous levelling (you might be them too). This is why Rawls' theory is not simply a redistributive one.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE CONCLUSION — fairness as the outcome of RATIONALITY, not morality
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"SUCH FAIRNESS WOULD BE THE OUTCOME OF RATIONAL ACTION — NOT BENEVOLENCE OR GENEROSITY."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"Rawls therefore argues that RATIONAL THINKING, NOT MORALITY, COULD LEAD US TO BE FAIR AND JUDGE IMPARTIALLY REGARDING HOW TO DISTRIBUTE THE BENEFITS AND BURDENS OF A SOCIETY."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"In his example, THERE ARE NO GOALS OR NORMS OF MORALITY THAT ARE GIVEN TO US IN ADVANCE, AND WE REMAIN FREE TO DETERMINE WHAT IS BEST FOR OURSELVES. ⭐⭐⭐ IT IS THIS BELIEF WHICH MAKES RAWLS' THEORY AN IMPORTANT AND COMPELLING WAY TO APPROACH THE QUESTION OF FAIRNESS AND JUSTICE."**

**⭐⭐⭐ Why this matters so much: it means a scheme to help the disadvantaged need not be defended as CHARITY or as MORAL DUTY. It can be defended to a purely self-interested person as what they themselves would have chosen. That is the "rational justification" the chapter promised.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 5 — PURSUING SOCIAL JUSTICE

### ⭐⭐⭐ When is social justice lacking?
⭐⭐ **"If in a society DEEP AND PERSISTENT DIVISIONS EXIST BETWEEN (i) THOSE WHO ENJOY GREATER WEALTH AND PROPERTY — AND THE POWER WHICH GOES WITH SUCH OWNERSHIP — AND (ii) THOSE WHO ARE EXCLUDED AND DEPRIVED, WE WOULD SAY THAT SOCIAL JUSTICE IS LACKING THERE."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The crucial qualification — justice does NOT require sameness
> ⭐⭐ **"We are NOT talking here merely about the different standards of living which may be enjoyed by different individuals in a society. ⭐⭐⭐ JUSTICE DOES NOT REQUIRE ABSOLUTE EQUALITY AND SAMENESS IN THE WAY IN WHICH PEOPLE LIVE."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The two tests of an UNJUST society (learn both):**
> **"But a society would be considered unjust:**
> ⭐⭐ **(i) IF THE DIFFERENCES BETWEEN RICH AND POOR ARE SO GREAT THAT THEY SEEM TO BE LIVING IN DIFFERENT WORLDS ALTOGETHER, and**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) IF THE RELATIVELY DEPRIVED HAVE NO CHANCE AT ALL TO IMPROVE THEIR CONDITION HOWEVER HARD THEY MAY WORK."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The positive definition of a just society
> **"In other words, A JUST SOCIETY SHOULD PROVIDE PEOPLE WITH:**
> **(i) THE BASIC MINIMUM CONDITIONS TO ENABLE THEM TO LIVE HEALTHY AND SECURE LIVES,**
> **(ii) [THE CONDITIONS TO] DEVELOP THEIR TALENTS, AS WELL AS**
> **(iii) EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES TO PURSUE THEIR CHOSEN GOALS IN SOCIETY."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ What are the basic minimum conditions? (a certain exam list)
⭐ **"Various methods of calculating the basic needs of people have been devised by DIFFERENT GOVERNMENTS and by INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS LIKE THE WORLD HEALTH ORGANISATION."**

⭐⭐⭐ **"But in general it is agreed that AN IMPORTANT PART OF THESE BASIC CONDITIONS would be:"**
1. ⭐ **"THE BASIC AMOUNT OF NOURISHMENT NEEDED TO REMAIN HEALTHY"**
2. ⭐ **"HOUSING"**
3. ⭐ **"SUPPLY OF CLEAN DRINKING WATER"**
4. ⭐ **"EDUCATION"**
5. ⭐ **"A MINIMUM WAGE"**

⭐⭐ **"PROVIDING PEOPLE WITH THEIR BASIC NEEDS IS CONSIDERED TO BE ONE OF THE RESPONSIBILITIES OF A DEMOCRATIC GOVERNMENT."**

⭐⭐ **The honest difficulty:** *"**HOWEVER, PROVIDING SUCH BASIC CONDITIONS OF LIFE TO ALL CITIZENS MAY POSE A HEAVY BURDEN ON GOVERNMENTS — PARTICULARLY IN COUNTRIES LIKE INDIA WHICH HAVE A LARGE NUMBER OF POOR PEOPLE.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Where the disagreement really lies
⭐⭐ **"EVEN IF WE ALL AGREE THAT STATES SHOULD TRY AND HELP THE MOST DISADVANTAGED MEMBERS OF THE SOCIETY TO ENJOY SOME DEGREE OF EQUALITY WITH OTHERS, ⭐⭐⭐ DISAGREEMENTS COULD STILL ARISE REGARDING THE BEST METHODS OF ACHIEVING THIS GOAL."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The live debate, stated precisely:** *"A debate is currently going on in our society, as well as in other parts of the world, about whether:*
- **(a) PROMOTING OPEN COMPETITION THROUGH FREE MARKETS WOULD BE THE BEST WAY OF HELPING THE DISADVANTAGED WITHOUT HARMING THE BETTER-OFF MEMBERS OF A SOCIETY, or**
- **(b) THE GOVERNMENT SHOULD TAKE ON THE RESPONSIBILITY OF PROVIDING A BASIC MINIMUM TO THE POOR — IF NECESSARY EVEN THROUGH A REDISTRIBUTION OF RESOURCES."**

⭐ **"In our country these different approaches are being supported by different political groups who debate THE RELATIVE MERITS OF DIFFERENT SCHEMES FOR HELPING MARGINALISED SECTIONS OF THE POPULATION, SUCH AS THE RURAL OR URBAN POOR."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 6 — FREE MARKETS VERSUS STATE INTERVENTION

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE CASE FOR FREE MARKETS

### ⭐⭐ What a free market is
⭐ **"Supporters of free markets maintain that AS FAR AS POSSIBLE, INDIVIDUALS SHOULD BE FREE (i) TO OWN PROPERTY and (ii) TO ENTER INTO CONTRACTS AND AGREEMENTS WITH OTHERS REGARDING PRICES AND WAGES AND PROFITS. ⭐ THEY SHOULD BE FREE TO COMPETE WITH EACH OTHER TO GAIN THE GREATEST AMOUNT OF BENEFIT. This is a simple description of a free market."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The core claim
> ⭐⭐ **"Supporters of the free market believe that IF MARKETS ARE LEFT FREE OF STATE INTERFERENCE, THE SUM OF MARKET TRANSACTIONS WOULD ENSURE OVERALL A JUST DISTRIBUTION OF BENEFITS AND DUTIES IN SOCIETY. ⭐ THOSE WITH MERIT AND TALENT WOULD BE REWARDED ACCORDINGLY, WHILE THE INCOMPETENT WOULD GET A LESSER REWARD. ⭐⭐ THEY WOULD MAINTAIN THAT WHATEVER BE THE OUTCOME OF MARKET DISTRIBUTION, IT WOULD BE JUST."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The modern, moderated version (an important nuance)
⭐⭐ **"HOWEVER, NOT ALL FREE MARKET SUPPORTERS TODAY WOULD SUPPORT ABSOLUTELY UNREGULATED MARKETS. Many would now be willing to accept certain restrictions:"**
1. ⭐ **"States COULD STEP IN TO ENSURE A BASIC MINIMUM STANDARD OF LIVING TO ALL PEOPLE, SO THAT THEY ARE ABLE TO COMPETE ON EQUAL TERMS."**
2. ⭐⭐ **But even then: "the MOST EFFICIENT WAY of providing people with basic services might be TO ALLOW MARKETS IN HEALTH CARE, EDUCATION, AND SUCH SERVICES, TO DEVELOP. ⭐⭐ In other words, PRIVATE AGENCIES SHOULD BE ENCOURAGED TO PROVIDE SUCH SERVICES, WHILE STATE POLICIES SHOULD TRY TO EMPOWER PEOPLE TO BUY THOSE SERVICES."**
3. ⭐ **"It might also be necessary for the state TO GIVE SPECIAL HELP TO THE OLD AND THE SICK WHO CANNOT COMPETE."**
4. ⭐⭐ **"But apart from this, THE ROLE OF THE STATE SHOULD ONLY BE TO MAINTAIN A FRAMEWORK OF LAWS AND REGULATIONS TO ENSURE THAT COMPETITION BETWEEN INDIVIDUALS REMAINS FREE OF COERCION AND OTHER OBSTACLES."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE STRONGEST ARGUMENT FOR THE MARKET — its neutrality
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"They maintain that A FREE MARKET IS THE BASIS OF A FAIR AND JUST SOCIETY. THE MARKET, IT IS SAID, DOES NOT CARE ABOUT THE CASTE OR RELIGION OF THE PERSON; IT DOES NOT SEE WHETHER YOU ARE A MAN OR A WOMAN. ⭐⭐ IT IS NEUTRAL AND CONCERNED WITH THE TALENTS AND SKILLS THAT YOU HAVE. IF YOU HAVE THE MERIT, THEN NOTHING ELSE MATTERS."**
>
> **⭐⭐ This is a genuinely powerful argument and must be stated in full before it is criticised: the market is BLIND TO IDENTITY in a way that traditional society was not. For someone from a discriminated group, that blindness can be liberating.**

### ⭐⭐ The second argument — CHOICE
⭐ **"One of the arguments put forward in favour of market distribution is that IT GIVES US MORE CHOICES. There is no doubt that the market system gives us more choices AS CONSUMERS. We can choose the rice we eat and the school we go to..."**

⭐⭐⭐ **...and note the immediate qualification the chapter attaches:** *"**...PROVIDED THAT WE HAVE THE MEANS TO PAY FOR THEM.**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE CASE AGAINST — three criticisms

### ⭐⭐⭐ Criticism 1 — choice is meaningless without availability and affordability
⭐⭐ **"But regarding BASIC GOODS AND SERVICES, WHAT IS IMPORTANT IS THE AVAILABILITY OF GOOD QUALITY GOODS AND SERVICES AT A COST PEOPLE CAN AFFORD."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The market's failure where profit is absent:** *"**IF PRIVATE AGENCIES DO NOT FIND THIS PROFITABLE FOR THEM, THEY MAY PREFER (i) NOT TO ENTER THAT PARTICULAR MARKET, OR (ii) TO PROVIDE CHEAP AND SUBSTANDARD SERVICES.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The evidence:** *"**THAT IS WHY THERE MAY BE FEW PRIVATE SCHOOLS IN REMOTE RURAL AREAS, AND THE FEW WHICH HAVE BEEN SET UP MAY BE OF LOW QUALITY. THE SAME WOULD BE TRUE OF HEALTH CARE OR HOUSING. ⭐⭐ IN SUCH SITUATIONS THE GOVERNMENT MIGHT HAVE TO STEP IN.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Criticism 2 — superior quality, but out of the poor's reach
⭐⭐ **"Another argument often heard in defence of free markets and private enterprise is that THE QUALITY OF SERVICES THEY PROVIDE IS OFTEN SUPERIOR TO THAT PROVIDED IN GOVERNMENT INSTITUTIONS. ⭐⭐⭐ BUT THE COST OF SUCH SERVICES MAY PUT THEM OUT OF THE REACH OF THE POOR."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Criticism 3 — the market's structural tilt toward the strong
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"PRIVATE BUSINESS TENDS TO GO WHERE BUSINESS WOULD BE MOST PROFITABLE, AND HENCE FREE MARKETS EVENTUALLY TEND TO WORK IN THE INTEREST OF THE STRONG, THE WEALTHY AND THE POWERFUL. ⭐⭐ THE RESULT MAY BE TO DENY, RATHER THAN EXTEND, OPPORTUNITIES FOR THOSE WHO ARE RELATIVELY WEAK AND DISADVANTAGED."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The chapter's own verdict
> ⭐⭐ **"ARGUMENTS CAN BE PUT FORWARD ON BOTH SIDES OF THE DEBATE — BUT FREE MARKETS OFTEN EXHIBIT A TENDENCY TO WORK IN FAVOUR OF THE ALREADY PRIVILEGED. ⭐⭐⭐ THIS IS WHY MANY ARGUE THAT TO ENSURE SOCIAL JUSTICE THE STATE SHOULD STEP IN TO SEE THAT BASIC FACILITIES ARE MADE AVAILABLE TO ALL THE MEMBERS OF A SOCIETY."**

**⭐⭐ Note the balanced-but-not-neutral conclusion: the chapter concedes the market's neutrality on identity, but holds that neutrality between rich and poor in an already unequal society favours the rich. The market is blind to caste but not to money.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE CLOSING PASSAGE — disagreement as healthy
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"IN A DEMOCRATIC SOCIETY, DISAGREEMENTS ABOUT ISSUES OF DISTRIBUTION AND JUSTICE ARE INEVITABLE AND EVEN HEALTHY, BECAUSE THEY FORCE US TO EXAMINE DIFFERENT POINTS OF VIEW AND RATIONALLY DEFEND OUR OWN VIEWS."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"POLITICS IS ABOUT THE NEGOTIATION OF SUCH DISAGREEMENTS THROUGH DEBATE."** *(Exactly Chapter 1's definition of politics.)*
>
> ⭐⭐ **"In our own country MANY KINDS OF SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC INEQUALITIES EXIST, AND MUCH REMAINS TO BE DONE IF THEY ARE TO BE REDUCED. ⭐ STUDYING THE DIFFERENT PRINCIPLES OF JUSTICE SHOULD HELP US TO DISCUSS THE ISSUES INVOLVED AND COME TO AN AGREEMENT REGARDING THE BEST WAY OF PURSUING JUSTICE."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE TWO SET-PIECE QUOTATIONS (learn both verbatim)

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ B.R. AMBEDKAR on a just society
> **"A JUST SOCIETY IS THAT SOCIETY IN WHICH ASCENDING SENSE OF REVERENCE AND DESCENDING SENSE OF CONTEMPT IS DISSOLVED INTO THE CREATION OF A COMPASSIONATE SOCIETY."**
> — **B.R. Ambedkar**
>
> **⭐⭐ Unpack this carefully, because it is precisely aimed at CASTE: in a hierarchy you look UP with reverence and DOWN with contempt. Ambedkar's just society is one where that vertical structure of feeling is "dissolved" — replaced not merely by equality of rights but by COMPASSION. Note how much more radical this is than a purely distributive account of justice: it demands a transformation of sentiment, not just of resources.**

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ J.S. MILL on justice as a moral right
> **"JUSTICE IMPLIES SOMETHING WHICH IT IS NOT ONLY RIGHT TO DO AND WRONG NOT TO DO; BUT WHICH SOME INDIVIDUAL PERSON CAN CLAIM FROM US AS HIS MORAL RIGHT."**
> — **J.S. Mill**
>
> **⭐⭐ The distinction Mill is drawing: many things are right to do (generosity, kindness) but no one can DEMAND them of you. Justice is different — it is what someone can CLAIM as of right. ⭐⭐⭐ This is the exact answer to Exercise Q6(a): providing for the poor is NOT charity; it is a claim of right.**

## Key Words (Glossary)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Social justice** | Concerns **our life in society**, **the ordering of public life**, and **the principles by which social goods and social duties are distributed** among members of society |
| ⭐⭐ ***Dharma*** | In ancient India, justice was associated with *dharma*; **maintaining *dharma* — a just social order — was a primary duty of kings** |
| **Confucius** | Held that **kings should maintain justice by punishing wrong doers and rewarding the virtuous** |
| ***The Republic*** | **Plato's** book (**fourth century B.C. Athens**), in which **Socrates** debates justice with **Glaucon and Adeimantus** |
| ⭐⭐ **"Giving each person his due"** | The enduring core of justice — but **what is "due" has changed**: today it is fixed by **human dignity**, entitling everyone to **develop their talents and pursue their chosen goals** |
| ⭐⭐ **Kant on dignity** | The German philosopher held that **human beings possess dignity** — the modern basis of what is due to each person |
| ⭐⭐ **Equal treatment for equals** | People share characteristics as human beings, so deserve **equal rights** — **civil** (life, liberty, property), **political** (the vote), **social** (equal opportunities) — and must be **judged on their work and actions, not the group they belong to** |
| ⭐⭐ **Proportionate justice** | **Provided everybody starts from the same base line of equal rights**, rewarding people **in proportion to the scale and quality of their effort**; different work may be rewarded differently by **effort, skill and danger** |
| ⭐⭐ **Recognition of special needs** | Taking account of **special needs** in distributing rewards and duties; grounds include **physical disabilities, age, and lack of access to good education or health care.** ⭐ It **extends rather than contradicts** equal treatment |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Distributive justice** | The **just distribution of goods and services**, whether **between nations** or **between groups and individuals within a society** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Veil of ignorance** | **Rawls' device:** deciding how society should be organised **without knowing what position you will occupy in it** — not knowing your **family, caste, wealth or privilege** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The worst-off** | Because no one knows who they will be, **each rational person will envisage the future society from the point of view of the worst-off** — and will therefore seek rules ensuring **reasonable opportunities to the weaker sections** |
| ⭐⭐ **Free market** | Individuals free to **own property, contract over prices, wages and profits, and compete for the greatest benefit**; supporters hold that **whatever the outcome of market distribution, it would be just** |
| ⭐⭐ **Market neutrality** | The claim that the market **"does not care about the caste or religion... does not see whether you are a man or a woman"** — *"if you have the merit, then nothing else matters"* |
| ⭐⭐ **Basic minimum conditions** | **Nourishment to remain healthy, housing, clean drinking water, education and a minimum wage** — providing them is **a responsibility of a democratic government** |

## Quick Recap (14 points)
1. ⭐⭐ **Justice is like love** — intuitively understood, hard to define precisely, and passionately advocated; **no one hates justice.** ⭐ **But unlike love, which concerns a few people we know well, justice concerns OUR LIFE IN SOCIETY, THE ORDERING OF PUBLIC LIFE, and THE PRINCIPLES BY WHICH SOCIAL GOODS AND DUTIES ARE DISTRIBUTED** — hence *"questions of justice are of central importance for politics."*
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **All traditions grappled with justice:** in **ancient India** it was tied to ***dharma***, whose maintenance was **the primary duty of kings**; **Confucius** held that kings should **punish wrongdoers and reward the virtuous**; and **Plato** examined it in ***The Republic*** in **fourth-century B.C. Athens.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **The challenge from Glaucon and Adeimantus:** the unjust seem better off — those who **twist rules, avoid taxes, and lie** succeed more than the truthful, so **"if one were smart enough to avoid being caught, being unjust is better than being just."** Glaucon adds that **laws arose only as a bargain**: since we cannot escape *suffering* injustice, we agree to forgo *doing* it.
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **Socrates' two answers:** ⓵ **the prudential one** — *"if everyone were to be unjust... no one could be sure of benefiting from injustice. Nobody would be secure... hence it is in our own LONG-TERM INTEREST to obey the laws and be just."* ⓶ **the definitional one** — justice is **not** doing good to friends and harm to enemies, nor pursuing one's own interests: **"justice involves the WELL-BEING OF ALL PEOPLE"**, and *"just as a DOCTOR is concerned with the well-being of his patients, so the just ruler must be concerned with the well-being of the people"* — which **"includes giving each person his due."**
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **What is "due" has changed since Plato.** Today it rests on **Kant's** claim that **human beings possess dignity**: *"if all persons are granted dignity, then what is due to each of them is that they have the opportunity to DEVELOP THEIR TALENTS AND PURSUE THEIR CHOSEN GOALS."* **"Justice requires that we give due and equal consideration to all individuals."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **PRINCIPLE 1 — Equal treatment for equals.** All individuals **share certain characteristics as human beings**, so deserve **equal rights**: **civil** (life, liberty, property), **political** (the vote), **social** (equal opportunities). ⭐ And **no discrimination on grounds of class, caste, race or gender** — *"they should be judged on the basis of their work and actions, and not on the basis of the group to which they belong."* **Two illustrations: ₹100 to one and ₹75 to another for the same work because of caste is unjust; a male teacher paid more than a female teacher is "unjustifiable and wrong."**
7. ⭐⭐⭐ **PRINCIPLE 2 — Proportionate justice.** *"There could be circumstances in which we might feel that treating everybody equally would be UNJUST"* — as if **all exam candidates got equal marks.** ⭐⭐ **"PROVIDED EVERYBODY STARTS FROM THE SAME BASE LINE OF EQUAL RIGHTS, justice would mean rewarding people IN PROPORTION TO THE SCALE AND QUALITY OF THEIR EFFORT."** Different work may be rewarded differently by **the effort required, the skills required, and the possible dangers involved** — by which measure **miners, skilled craftsmen and policemen** may be **under-rewarded.** *"The principle of equal treatment needs to be BALANCED with the principle of proportionality."*
8. ⭐⭐⭐ **PRINCIPLE 3 — Recognition of special needs.** Even **non-discrimination plus proportionate reward** may not secure equality in other aspects of life, or a just society overall. ⭐⭐ **Crucially, this principle "does not necessarily CONTRADICT the principle of equal treatment so much as EXTEND it — because treating equals equally could imply that people who are NOT equal in certain important respects could be treated differently."** Grounds recognised: **physical disabilities, age, lack of access to good education or health care.** ⭐ **The justification: treating the deprived identically with the privileged produces "an unequal society, not an egalitarian and just one."** In India, deprivation is **"often found combined with social discrimination on grounds of caste"**, so the **Constitution allowed reservations in government jobs and quotas in educational institutions for SCs and STs.**
9. ⭐⭐ **The three principles can CONFLICT**, and harmonising them is **"a function of governments."** ⭐ **Two tensions: "pursuing equality of treatment by itself might sometimes work against giving due reward to MERIT"; and "emphasising rewarding MERIT... might mean that MARGINALISED SECTIONS would be at a disadvantage, because they have not had access to facilities such as good nourishment or education."** Different groups back different policies **depending on which principle they emphasise.** ⭐ **The blindfolded statue captures the dilemma: impartiality requires blindness, but recognising special needs requires sight.**
10. ⭐⭐⭐ **JUST DISTRIBUTION:** social justice concerns the **just distribution of goods and services, between nations and within a society**; where serious inequalities exist, it may be necessary to **redistribute important resources to provide "a level playing field."** It requires **not only equal treatment under laws and policies but also "some basic equality of LIFE CONDITIONS AND OPPORTUNITIES."** ⭐ **India's two examples: the Constitution abolished untouchability to secure access to temples, jobs and water; and State governments instituted land reforms.** These questions **"arouse fierce passions... and even sometimes provoke violence"** because **"people believe the future of themselves and their families may be at stake"** — yet students of political theory must **"calmly examine the issues."**
11. ⭐⭐⭐ **RAWLS' PROBLEM:** asked to choose a society, people **"choose one in which the rules allot them a privileged position"**, and we **cannot expect them to set aside personal interest** — especially where their children's prospects are involved. But such perspectives **cannot ground a theory of justice.**
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **RAWLS' SOLUTION — the VEIL OF IGNORANCE:** decide how society should be organised **without knowing which position you will occupy** — not knowing **your family, whether "upper" or "lower" caste, rich or poor, privileged or disadvantaged.** ⭐⭐ **Each person still decides SELFISHLY — but "since no one knows who he would be... EACH WILL ENVISAGE THE FUTURE SOCIETY FROM THE POINT OF VIEW OF THE WORST-OFF."** Hence it is **rational** to frame rules ensuring **reasonable opportunities to the weaker sections**, and that **education, health and shelter are available to all.**
13. ⭐⭐⭐ **WHY the device works:** *"It is not easy to erase our identities... BUT it is equally difficult for most people to be self-sacrificing and share their good fortune with strangers — THAT IS WHY WE HABITUALLY ASSOCIATE SELF-SACRIFICE WITH HEROISM."* So we should design **"a framework that does not require extraordinary actions."** ⭐⭐ **"The merit of the veil of ignorance position is that it expects people to JUST BE THEIR USUAL RATIONAL SELVES... yet they will find that IT IS IN THEIR INTEREST TO THINK FROM THE POSITION OF THE WORST-OFF."** ⭐ **And it cuts both ways: since one might also be born privileged, rational choosers will ensure their policy "does not make those who are better-off weaker" — so "society AS A WHOLE should benefit... not just any particular section."** ⭐⭐⭐ **Hence "such fairness would be the outcome of RATIONAL ACTION, NOT BENEVOLENCE OR GENEROSITY"** — *"rational thinking, not morality, could lead us to be fair"* — which is what makes the theory **"important and compelling."**
14. ⭐⭐⭐ **PURSUING SOCIAL JUSTICE.** Social justice is lacking where **deep and persistent divisions** separate those with **wealth, property and the power that goes with it** from those **excluded and deprived.** ⭐ But **"justice does not require absolute equality and sameness in the way in which people live."** ⭐⭐ **Two tests of injustice: differences so great that rich and poor "seem to be living in different worlds altogether", and the relatively deprived having "NO CHANCE AT ALL to improve their condition HOWEVER HARD THEY MAY WORK."** A just society provides **basic minimum conditions for healthy and secure lives, the means to develop talents, and equal opportunities** — the minimum being **nourishment, housing, clean drinking water, education and a minimum wage**, whose provision is **"one of the responsibilities of a democratic government"**, though a **heavy burden** in a country like India. ⭐⭐ **The debate is over METHOD: free markets versus state provision.** Free-market supporters claim market outcomes are **just by definition**, that even basic services are best delivered by **private agencies with the state empowering people to buy them**, and above all that **the market is NEUTRAL — "it does not care about the caste or religion... if you have the merit, nothing else matters"** — and that it **expands choice** (⭐ *"provided that we have the means to pay"*). ⭐⭐ **The criticisms: where provision is unprofitable, private agencies "prefer not to enter that particular market, or to provide cheap and substandard services" — hence "few private schools in remote rural areas", and the same for health care and housing; superior private quality "may put them out of the reach of the poor"; and "private business tends to go where business would be most profitable, hence FREE MARKETS EVENTUALLY TEND TO WORK IN THE INTEREST OF THE STRONG, THE WEALTHY AND THE POWERFUL", denying rather than extending opportunity.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The verdict: "free markets often exhibit a tendency to work in favour of the ALREADY PRIVILEGED. This is why many argue that to ensure social justice THE STATE SHOULD STEP IN."** And **disagreement itself is "inevitable and even healthy, because it forces us to examine different points of view and rationally defend our own."**

## 60-Second Revision
**Justice is like LOVE — intuitive, passionately held, nobody hates it. BUT unlike love it concerns LIFE IN SOCIETY, the ORDERING OF PUBLIC LIFE, and the DISTRIBUTION OF SOCIAL GOODS AND DUTIES.** **Ancient India = DHARMA, a king's primary duty. CONFUCIUS = punish wrongdoers, reward the virtuous. PLATO, THE REPUBLIC, 4th c. BC Athens, with GLAUCON and ADEIMANTUS.** **The challenge: the unjust prosper — twisting rules, avoiding taxes, lying — "if one were smart enough to avoid being caught, being unjust is better than being just."** **SOCRATES: (1) if everyone were unjust, "nobody would be secure" → it is in our LONG-TERM INTEREST to be just; (2) justice is NOT good-to-friends-harm-to-enemies nor self-interest — "JUSTICE INVOLVES THE WELL-BEING OF ALL PEOPLE"; the just ruler is like a DOCTOR; this "includes GIVING EACH PERSON HIS DUE."** **What is "due" has CHANGED — KANT: human beings possess DIGNITY ⇒ what is due is the OPPORTUNITY TO DEVELOP TALENTS AND PURSUE CHOSEN GOALS.** **THREE PRINCIPLES: (1) EQUAL TREATMENT FOR EQUALS — civil (life/liberty/property) + political (vote) + social (equal opportunity) rights; judged on WORK AND ACTIONS, not group; ₹100 vs ₹75 by caste = unjust; male teacher paid more than female = "unjustifiable and wrong." (2) PROPORTIONATE JUSTICE — equal marks for all exam-takers would be UNJUST; "PROVIDED EVERYBODY STARTS FROM THE SAME BASE LINE OF EQUAL RIGHTS", reward in proportion to SCALE AND QUALITY OF EFFORT; criteria = EFFORT + SKILL + DANGER ⇒ MINERS, SKILLED CRAFTSMEN, POLICEMEN are under-rewarded. (3) SPECIAL NEEDS — it "does not CONTRADICT equal treatment so much as EXTEND it"; grounds = physical disability, age, lack of access to education/health care; treating the deprived identically with the privileged yields "an unequal society, not an egalitarian and just one"; in India deprivation is COMBINED with caste discrimination ⇒ SC/ST reservations and quotas.** **The three CONFLICT: equality can work against MERIT; merit disadvantages the MARGINALISED "because they have not had access to good nourishment or education" ⇒ governments must HARMONISE. BLINDFOLDED JUSTICE: impartial, but "how then does she see the special needs of people?"** **JUST DISTRIBUTION = of goods and services, between NATIONS and WITHIN a society; needs not just equal laws but "basic equality of LIFE CONDITIONS AND OPPORTUNITIES." India: abolition of untouchability (temples, jobs, water) + LAND REFORMS.** **RAWLS: people choose societies that privilege them; we can't demand selflessness ⇒ the VEIL OF IGNORANCE — decide without knowing your family, caste, wealth or privilege. You still choose SELFISHLY, but "since no one knows who he would be, EACH WILL ENVISAGE THE FUTURE SOCIETY FROM THE POINT OF VIEW OF THE WORST-OFF" ⇒ rules ensuring EDUCATION, HEALTH, SHELTER for all. Merit of the device: it needs no heroism — "SELF-SACRIFICE [is associated] WITH HEROISM", so design a framework needing no extraordinary action; people are "JUST THEIR USUAL RATIONAL SELVES." It cuts BOTH ways — you might be born privileged too, so "society AS A WHOLE should benefit." ⇒ "FAIRNESS WOULD BE THE OUTCOME OF RATIONAL ACTION, NOT BENEVOLENCE OR GENEROSITY"; "RATIONAL THINKING, NOT MORALITY."** **A just society: NOT "absolute equality and sameness." UNJUST if (i) rich and poor "seem to be living in DIFFERENT WORLDS altogether" and (ii) the deprived have "NO CHANCE AT ALL to improve their condition HOWEVER HARD THEY MAY WORK." BASIC MINIMUM = nourishment + housing + clean drinking water + education + minimum wage (WHO and governments calculate it); a responsibility of DEMOCRATIC GOVERNMENT; a heavy burden in India.** **FREE MARKET: free to own property, contract on prices/wages/profits, compete; "whatever be the outcome of market distribution it would be just"; moderates accept a state-guaranteed basic minimum but prefer PRIVATE PROVISION with the state EMPOWERING PEOPLE TO BUY; state's role = a framework of laws keeping competition free of coercion. STRONGEST ARGUMENT — NEUTRALITY: "the market does not care about the caste or religion... if you have the merit, then nothing else matters." Plus CHOICE — "provided that we have the means to pay."** **CRITICISMS: unprofitable ⇒ "prefer not to enter that market, or provide CHEAP AND SUBSTANDARD services" — few private schools in remote rural areas, same for health care and housing; superior quality is "out of the reach of the poor"; "private business tends to go where business would be most profitable, hence FREE MARKETS EVENTUALLY TEND TO WORK IN THE INTEREST OF THE STRONG, THE WEALTHY AND THE POWERFUL." VERDICT: markets favour the ALREADY PRIVILEGED ⇒ THE STATE SHOULD STEP IN.** **AMBEDKAR: a just society is one in which "ASCENDING SENSE OF REVERENCE AND DESCENDING SENSE OF CONTEMPT IS DISSOLVED INTO THE CREATION OF A COMPASSIONATE SOCIETY."** **MILL: justice is not only right to do and wrong not to do, "BUT WHICH SOME INDIVIDUAL PERSON CAN CLAIM FROM US AS HIS MORAL RIGHT."**

## Try These Questions

### NCERT Exercises (with answers/pointers)
1. ⭐⭐⭐ **What does it mean to give each person his/her due? How has the meaning of "giving each his due" changed over time?**
   → **Socrates established that justice is not doing good to friends and harm to enemies, nor pursuing one's own interests, but concerns "the well-being of ALL people" — and that "ensuring the well-being of the people INCLUDES GIVING EACH PERSON HIS DUE."** *(The doctor analogy: as a doctor is concerned with his patients' well-being, so must the just ruler be with the people's.)*
   ⭐⭐ **The change:** *"The idea... continues to be an important part of our present day understanding of justice. **HOWEVER, OUR UNDERSTANDING OF WHAT IS DUE TO A PERSON HAS CHANGED FROM THE TIME OF PLATO.**"*
   - **Then:** what was "due" was largely fixed by one's **place in the social order** — the just society was one in which each performed the role allotted to their station, and **maintaining that order was itself justice** (compare *dharma* as **maintaining a just social order**, and Confucius's ruler **punishing and rewarding**).
   - ⭐⭐⭐ **Now:** *"our understanding of what is just is closely linked to our understanding of what is due to each person **AS A HUMAN BEING**."* Following **Kant**, **human beings possess dignity**; therefore what is due to each is **"the opportunity to DEVELOP THEIR TALENTS and PURSUE THEIR CHOSEN GOALS"**, and **"justice requires that we give DUE AND EQUAL CONSIDERATION to all individuals."**
   ⭐ **The shift in a sentence: what is due is no longer determined by your STATION but by your HUMANITY — and it is measured not in fixed allotments but in OPPORTUNITY.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **Briefly discuss the three principles of justice, with examples.** *(Reproduce Part 2. Structure your answer as: statement → example → limitation.)*
   - ⭐ **EQUAL TREATMENT FOR EQUALS** — all share characteristics as human beings, so deserve **equal civil, political and social rights**, and must be **judged by their work, not their group.** **Examples:** two people of different castes doing the same work — *"breaking stones or delivering pizzas"* — must get the same reward, so **₹100 to one and ₹75 to the other is unjust**; and a **male teacher paid more than a female teacher** is *"unjustifiable and wrong."*
   - ⭐ **PROPORTIONATE JUSTICE** — *"there could be circumstances in which treating everybody equally would be unjust."* **Example:** if **everyone taking an exam got equal marks**, we would object; marks should follow **"the quality of their answer papers and the degree of effort."** **Provided everyone starts from the same base line of equal rights**, reward should be **proportionate to effort**, and different work may be valued differently by **effort, skill and danger** — by which test **miners, skilled craftsmen and policemen** may be underpaid.
   - ⭐ **RECOGNITION OF SPECIAL NEEDS** — even non-discrimination plus proportionality **"might not be enough."** **Examples:** the **visually impaired student given extra examination time**; **physical disability, age, and lack of access to education or health care** as grounds; and in India, **SC/ST reservations**, because deprivation is *"often found combined with social discrimination on grounds of caste."*
   - ⭐⭐ **Close by noting the tension:** these three can pull against each other, and *"it becomes a function of governments to HARMONISE the different principles."*
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **Does the principle of considering the special needs of people conflict with the principle of equal treatment for all?**
   → ⭐⭐⭐ **No — the chapter's own answer is that it EXTENDS rather than contradicts it:** *"**The principle of taking account of the special needs of people DOES NOT NECESSARILY CONTRADICT THE PRINCIPLE OF EQUAL TREATMENT SO MUCH AS EXTEND IT — BECAUSE THE PRINCIPLE OF TREATING EQUALS EQUALLY COULD IMPLY THAT PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT EQUAL IN CERTAIN IMPORTANT RESPECTS COULD BE TREATED DIFFERENTLY.**"*
   ⭐⭐ **The logic:** "treat equals equally" is a **conditional** — it says *nothing* about how to treat people who are **unequal in a relevant respect.** Those with **physical disabilities, or without access to education or health care**, are **"unequal in some particular respect and deserving of special help."** ⭐ **And the consequence of ignoring this is decisive:** *"if people who enjoy very different standards of living and opportunities are treated equally in all respects with those who have been deprived of even the basic minimum needs, **the result is likely to be AN UNEQUAL SOCIETY, NOT AN EGALITARIAN AND JUST ONE.**"*
   ⚠️ **Two honest qualifications:** ⓵ *"It is not always easy to get agreement regarding WHICH inequalities... should be recognised"* — and the provision must **match the actual disadvantage** *(Suresh's extra time is just; giving Geeta extra time for a limp is not)*. ⓶ **There is a real tension with PROPORTIONATE justice** rather than with equal treatment: *"emphasising rewarding merit... might mean that marginalised sections would be at a disadvantage."* **So: no conflict with Principle 1, some tension with Principle 2 — which governments must harmonise.**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **How does Rawls use the idea of a veil of ignorance to argue that fair and just distribution can be defended on rational grounds?** *(Reproduce Part 4 in five steps.)*
   ⓵ **The problem:** people **"choose one in which the rules... allot them a privileged position"**, and we **cannot expect them to set personal interest aside** — indeed *"we often expect parents to think of and support what is best for their children."* But **such perspectives cannot ground a theory of justice.**
   ⓶ **The device:** imagine deciding how society should be organised **while not knowing which position you will occupy** — not knowing **your family, whether you will be "upper" or "lower" caste, rich or poor, privileged or disadvantaged.** This is **thinking under a "veil of ignorance."**
   ⓷ **The mechanism:** people still decide **"in terms of their own interests" — but "SINCE NO ONE KNOWS WHO HE WOULD BE... EACH WILL ENVISAGE THE FUTURE SOCIETY FROM THE POINT OF VIEW OF THE WORST-OFF."** The privileged enjoy special opportunities — **but what if one has "the misfortune of being born in a disadvantaged section"?** Hence *"it would make sense for each person, ACTING IN HIS OR HER OWN INTEREST, to think of rules... that will ensure reasonable opportunities to the weaker sections"*, making **education, health and shelter available to all.**
   ⓸ **Both directions:** rational persons **also ensure the policy "does not make those who are better-off weaker"**, since they might be born privileged — so **"society as a whole should benefit... not just any particular section."**
   ⓹ ⭐⭐⭐ **The conclusion:** *"**Such fairness would be the outcome of RATIONAL ACTION, NOT BENEVOLENCE OR GENEROSITY**"*; **"rational thinking, NOT MORALITY, could lead us to be fair."** ⭐ **And the practical merit: it "does not require extraordinary actions"** — unlike self-sacrifice, which we **"habitually associate with heroism"** — it asks people only to **"just be their usual rational selves."**
5. ⭐⭐ **What are generally considered the basic minimum requirements for living a healthy and productive life? What is the responsibility of governments in ensuring this minimum to all?**
   → ⭐ **The requirements** *(as calculated by governments and by international organisations like the **World Health Organisation**)*: **the basic amount of nourishment needed to remain healthy; housing; supply of clean drinking water; education; and a minimum wage.**
   ⭐⭐ **The government's responsibility:** *"**Providing people with their basic needs is considered to be ONE OF THE RESPONSIBILITIES OF A DEMOCRATIC GOVERNMENT.**"* Ground this in three ways: **(i)** the **positive definition of a just society** — one that provides *"the basic minimum conditions to enable them to live healthy and secure lives, develop their talents, as well as equal opportunities"*; **(ii)** **Rawls' argument** — rational choosers under the veil of ignorance would insist that **education, health and shelter be available to all**; **(iii)** **Mill's** point that justice is what **"some individual person can CLAIM from us as his MORAL RIGHT"** — so this is **not charity but obligation.**
   ⚠️ **Acknowledge the difficulty and the live debate:** it **"may pose a heavy burden on governments, particularly in countries like India which have a large number of poor people"**; and even those who agree on the goal disagree on **method** — **free markets with the state empowering people to buy services**, versus **direct state provision, if necessary through redistribution.** ⭐ **The chapter's own leaning: "free markets often exhibit a tendency to work in favour of the already privileged. This is why many argue that... the state should step in."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **Which arguments could justify state action to provide basic minimum conditions of life to all citizens?**
   | Argument | Verdict |
   |---|---|
   | **(a)** *"Providing free services to the poor and needy can be justified as an ACT OF CHARITY."* | ❌ **NOT a valid justification.** ⭐⭐ **Charity is precisely what justice is NOT.** **Mill:** justice is *"something which... some individual person CAN CLAIM FROM US AS HIS MORAL RIGHT"* — a **claim of right**, not a gift. And **Rawls** insists fairness is **"the outcome of rational action, NOT BENEVOLENCE OR GENEROSITY."** Charity is **discretionary, revocable and demeaning**; a right is none of these. |
   | ⭐⭐⭐ **(b)** *"Providing all citizens with a basic minimum standard of living is one way of ensuring EQUALITY OF OPPORTUNITY."* | ✅ **VALID.** Central to the chapter: without minimum conditions **"it is exceedingly difficult for all the members of the society to compete on equal terms"**, and social justice requires *"some basic equality of LIFE CONDITIONS AND OPPORTUNITIES."* It also answers the objection about merit — the marginalised are disadvantaged *"because they have not had access to facilities such as good nourishment or education."* |
   | **(c)** *"Some people are naturally LAZY and we should be KIND to them."* | ❌ **NOT valid — and doubly objectionable.** It **misdescribes** the deprived, attributing their condition to **character** rather than to **circumstance of birth** — whereas the chapter's whole concern is those who **"have NO CHANCE AT ALL to improve their condition HOWEVER HARD THEY MAY WORK."** And **"kindness"** repeats the **charity** error of (a). |
   | ⭐⭐⭐ **(d)** *"Ensuring basic facilities and a minimum standard of living to all is a recognition of our SHARED HUMANITY and a HUMAN RIGHT."* | ✅ **VALID — the strongest of the four.** It rests on exactly the chapter's foundation: **Kant's** claim that **human beings possess dignity**, so *"what is due to each of them is that they have the opportunity to develop their talents and pursue their chosen goals"*, and **"justice requires that we give due and equal consideration to all individuals."** The language of **right** rather than **charity** matches **Mill** precisely. |
   ⭐ **So: (b) and (d) justify state action; (a) and (c) do not — because both reduce a matter of RIGHT to a matter of KINDNESS.**
