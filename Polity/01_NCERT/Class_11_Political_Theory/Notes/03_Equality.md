# Chapter 3: Equality

## 🎯 Chapter Outcome — what you should walk away with

> **The one thing this chapter exists to teach:** **equal respect does not mean identical treatment.** Equality means that a person's treatment and opportunities **must not be pre-determined by birth or social circumstance**.

**After reading it, here is what you now understand:**

1. **The paradox that opens the chapter** — everybody accepts equality as an ideal, and everywhere we meet inequality: slums beside luxury housing, air-conditioned classrooms beside schools with no drinking water.
2. **What actually makes a society unjust** — not unequal status or wealth as such, but **unequal access to education, health care and safe housing**. A general's rank is acceptable "provided the privileges are not misused"; a slum child denied food and schooling "through no fault of his or her own" is not.
3. **Why the natural/social distinction is unreliable, and what replaces it.** "Weaker sex, lesser intelligence" justified denying women rights; black Africans being "better at manual work" justified slavery — both now seen as **differences of power, not inborn traits**. And technology keeps moving the line (computers for the blind, wheelchairs, Stephen Hawking). So the working distinction becomes **choice vs circumstance of birth**.
4. **The three dimensions** — political (equal citizenship and the vote, but these are only *legal* rights, hence the demand for a level playing field), social (minimum conditions; otherwise "a huge pool of potential talent tends to be wasted"; India's special obstacle is **custom**), and economic.
5. **Why entrenched inequality is the dangerous kind** — untouched across generations, it breeds resentment and violence, and the wealth it concentrates blocks reform.
6. **Feminism as a political doctrine of equal rights for women and men** (so men can be feminists), **patriarchy** as a system valuing men more, the **sex/gender** distinction — "only women can become pregnant… does not require that only women should look after children" — and the **double burden**.
7. **The four ideological answers** — Marx (private ownership of resources converts wealth into political power), liberals (fair competition; the problem is "not inequality as such but unjust and entrenched inequalities"), socialism (regulation and public provision of education and health), and **Lohia's five inequalities plus civil liberties and non-violence = the seven revolutions, *Sapta Kranti***.
8. **The three steps toward equality** — formal equality; **differential treatment** (ramps for the disabled, night-shift protection for call-centre women — "not infringements of equality but **enhancement** of equality"); and **affirmative action**, which corrects "the cumulative effect of past inequalities" and is **temporary and time-bound**.
9. **That the real dispute is about policies, not the goal** — quotas vs early skill development, economic criterion vs caste — and the test is simply which makes society more egalitarian.

**Self-check — answer these three without looking:**
1. Why is a ramp for a wheelchair user an *enhancement* of equality rather than a violation of it?
2. What replaced the natural/social inequality distinction, and what were the two reasons for abandoning it?
3. Name Lohia's five inequalities.

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 1 — WHY DOES EQUALITY MATTER?

### ⭐⭐ Equality as a moral and political ideal
⭐ **"Equality is a POWERFUL MORAL AND POLITICAL IDEAL that has INSPIRED AND GUIDED HUMAN SOCIETY FOR MANY CENTURIES. It is IMPLICIT IN ALL FAITHS AND RELIGIONS which proclaim ALL HUMAN BEINGS TO BE THE CREATION OF GOD."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The definition as a political ideal (memorise this):**
> **"As a political ideal, the concept of equality invokes the idea that ALL HUMAN BEINGS HAVE AN EQUAL WORTH REGARDLESS OF THEIR COLOUR, GENDER, RACE, OR NATIONALITY. It maintains that HUMAN BEINGS DESERVE EQUAL CONSIDERATION AND RESPECT BECAUSE OF THEIR COMMON HUMANITY."**

⭐⭐ **What follows from "shared humanity":** *"It is this notion of a **SHARED HUMANITY** that lies behind, for instance, the notions of **UNIVERSAL HUMAN RIGHTS** or **'CRIMES AGAINST HUMANITY'**."*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Equality as a rallying slogan in history (learn all four)
⭐ **"In the modern period, the equality of all human beings has been used as A RALLYING SLOGAN IN THE STRUGGLES AGAINST STATES AND SOCIAL INSTITUTIONS WHICH UPHOLD INEQUALITIES OF RANK, WEALTH, STATUS OR PRIVILEGE among people."**

| Struggle | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The French Revolution** | **"In the EIGHTEENTH CENTURY, the FRENCH REVOLUTIONARIES used the slogan 'LIBERTY, EQUALITY AND FRATERNITY' to revolt against THE LANDED FEUDAL ARISTOCRACY AND THE MONARCHY."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Anti-colonial struggles** | **"The demand for equality was also raised during ANTI-COLONIAL LIBERATION STRUGGLES IN ASIA AND AFRICA during the TWENTIETH CENTURY."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Contemporary struggles in India** | **"It continues to be raised by struggling groups such as WOMEN or DALITS who feel MARGINALISED in our society."** |
| **Today** | **"Equality is a WIDELY ACCEPTED IDEAL which is EMBODIED IN THE CONSTITUTIONS AND LAWS OF MANY COUNTRIES."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE PARADOX OF EQUALITY — the chapter's central puzzle
⭐⭐ **"YET, IT IS INEQUALITY RATHER THAN EQUALITY WHICH IS MOST VISIBLE AROUND US in the world as well as within our own society."**

⭐⭐ **The three Indian juxtapositions (a vivid, quotable set):**
1. **"SLUMS existing SIDE BY SIDE with LUXURY HOUSING"**
2. ⭐ **"SCHOOLS WITH WORLD CLASS FACILITIES AND AIRCONDITIONED CLASSROOMS along with SCHOOLS WHICH MAY LACK EVEN DRINKING WATER FACILITIES OR TOILETS"**
3. **"WASTE OF FOOD as well as STARVATION"**

⭐⭐ **"There are GLARING DIFFERENCES BETWEEN WHAT THE LAW PROMISES AND WHAT WE SEE AROUND US."**

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ THE PARADOX, stated exactly
> **"Thus we face a paradox: ALMOST EVERYONE ACCEPTS THE IDEAL OF EQUALITY, YET ALMOST EVERYWHERE WE ENCOUNTER INEQUALITY. We live in a complex world of UNEQUAL WEALTH, OPPORTUNITIES, WORK SITUATIONS, AND POWER."**

*(The chapter's own margin question puts it sharply: **"Everyone I know believes in a religion. Every religion I know preaches equality. Then why is there inequality in the world?"**)*

- ⭐ **"How should a society decide WHICH DIFFERENCES OF TREATMENT OR REWARD ARE ACCEPTABLE AND WHICH ARE NOT?"**
- **"What kind of policies should we pursue to try and make the society MORE EGALITARIAN?"**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 2 — WHAT IS EQUALITY?

### ⭐⭐ The images of segregation
*(The chapter opens this section with photographs of American segregation signage: **"COLOURED SERVED IN REAR", "DRINKING FOUNTAIN — WHITE / COLOURED", "REST ROOMS — WHITE ONLY".**)*

⭐⭐ **"All of them MAKE DISTINCTIONS BETWEEN HUMAN BEINGS ON GROUNDS OF RACE AND COLOUR, and these appear to most of us as UNACCEPTABLE. In fact, such distinctions VIOLATE OUR INTUITIVE UNDERSTANDING OF EQUALITY, which tells us that ALL HUMAN BEINGS SHOULD BE ENTITLED TO THE SAME RESPECT AND CONSIDERATION BECAUSE OF THEIR COMMON HUMANITY."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE CENTRAL DISTINCTION — equal respect ≠ identical treatment
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"HOWEVER, TREATING PEOPLE WITH EQUAL RESPECT NEED NOT MEAN ALWAYS TREATING THEM IN AN IDENTICAL WAY. NO SOCIETY TREATS ALL ITS MEMBERS IN EXACTLY THE SAME WAY UNDER ALL CONDITIONS."**

⭐⭐ **Why:** *"**THE SMOOTH FUNCTIONING OF SOCIETY REQUIRES DIVISION OF WORK AND FUNCTIONS, AND PEOPLE OFTEN ENJOY DIFFERENT STATUS AND REWARDS ON ACCOUNT OF IT. At times these differences of treatment may appear ACCEPTABLE OR EVEN NECESSARY.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The two contrasting examples (an excellent pair to reproduce)
| Example | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **"We usually do not feel that giving PRIME MINISTERS, or ARMY GENERALS, a SPECIAL OFFICIAL RANK AND STATUS goes against the notion of equality"** | ✅ **ACCEPTABLE** — ⭐ **with one crucial condition: "PROVIDED THEIR PRIVILEGES ARE NOT MISUSED."** |
| ⭐⭐ **"If a CHILD BORN IN A SLUM is DENIED NUTRITIOUS FOOD OR GOOD EDUCATION THROUGH NO FAULT OF HIS/HER OWN"** | ❌ **UNJUST — "it may appear UNFAIR to us."** ← *note the phrase **"through no fault of his/her own"** — it is doing the analytical work* |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Which differences are acceptable, and which are not — the test
⭐⭐ **"When people are treated differently JUST BECAUSE THEY ARE BORN IN A PARTICULAR RELIGION OR RACE OR CASTE OR GENDER, WE REGARD IT AS AN UNACCEPTABLE FORM OF INEQUALITY."**

⭐⭐ **"BUT human beings MAY PURSUE DIFFERENT AMBITIONS AND GOALS, AND NOT ALL MAY BE EQUALLY SUCCESSFUL. ⭐ SO LONG AS THEY ARE ABLE TO DEVELOP THE BEST IN THEMSELVES, WE WOULD NOT FEEL THAT EQUALITY HAS BEEN UNDERMINED."**

⭐ **The illustration:** *"Some may become **good musicians** while others may not be equally outstanding; some become **famous scientists** while others are more noted for their **hard work and conscientiousness.**"*

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ THE DEFINING FORMULA (learn this sentence — it answers half the exercises)
> **"THE COMMITMENT TO THE IDEAL OF EQUALITY DOES NOT IMPLY THE ELIMINATION OF ALL FORMS OF DIFFERENCES. IT MERELY SUGGESTS THAT THE TREATMENT WE RECEIVE AND THE OPPORTUNITIES WE ENJOY MUST NOT BE PRE-DETERMINED BY BIRTH OR SOCIAL CIRCUMSTANCE."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Equality of Opportunities
⭐⭐ **"The concept of equality implies that ALL PEOPLE, AS HUMAN BEINGS, ARE ENTITLED TO (i) THE SAME RIGHTS AND OPPORTUNITIES TO DEVELOP THEIR SKILLS AND TALENTS, AND (ii) TO PURSUE THEIR GOALS AND AMBITIONS."**

⭐ **"This means that in a society people MAY DIFFER WITH REGARD TO THEIR CHOICES AND PREFERENCES. They may also have DIFFERENT TALENTS AND SKILLS, which results in SOME BEING MORE SUCCESSFUL IN THEIR CHOSEN CAREERS THAN OTHERS. But just because only some become ACE CRICKETERS OR SUCCESSFUL LAWYERS, IT DOES NOT FOLLOW THAT THE SOCIETY SHOULD BE CONSIDERED UNEQUAL."**

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ The crucial reformulation — WHAT actually makes a society unjust
> **"In other words, IT IS NOT THE LACK OF EQUALITY OF STATUS OR WEALTH OR PRIVILEGE THAT IS SIGNIFICANT — BUT THE INEQUALITIES IN PEOPLE'S ACCESS TO SUCH BASIC GOODS AS EDUCATION, HEALTH CARE, SAFE HOUSING, THAT MAKE FOR AN UNEQUAL AND UNJUST SOCIETY."**
>
> **The three basic goods to name in any answer: EDUCATION, HEALTH CARE, SAFE HOUSING.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 3 — NATURAL AND SOCIAL INEQUALITIES

### ⭐⭐⭐ The distinction
| | ⭐⭐ **NATURAL INEQUALITIES** | ⭐⭐ **SOCIALLY-PRODUCED INEQUALITIES** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | **"Those that EMERGE BETWEEN PEOPLE AS A RESULT OF THEIR DIFFERENT CAPABILITIES AND TALENTS"** | **"Those which EMERGE AS A CONSEQUENCE OF (i) INEQUALITIES OF OPPORTUNITY or (ii) THE EXPLOITATION OF SOME GROUPS IN A SOCIETY BY OTHERS"** |
| **Origin** | **"The result of the different CHARACTERISTICS AND ABILITIES WITH WHICH PEOPLE ARE BORN"** | ⭐ **"Those CREATED BY SOCIETY"** |
| **Alterability** | **"It is GENERALLY ASSUMED that natural differences CANNOT BE ALTERED"** | Alterable — they **"reflect the VALUES of a society"** |
| **Examples** | Different talents and capabilities | ⭐ **"Certain societies may VALUE THOSE WHO PERFORM INTELLECTUAL WORK OVER THOSE WHO DO MANUAL WORK AND REWARD THEM DIFFERENTLY. They may treat differently people of different RACE, or COLOUR, or GENDER, or CASTE."** |
| **Verdict** | Not in themselves unjust | ⭐ **"Some of these may certainly appear to us to be UNJUST"** |

*(The chapter dramatises the distinction with a margin exchange: **"Men are superior to women. It is a natural inequality. You can't do anything about it."** — answered: **"I get more marks than you in every subject. And I also help my mother in housework. What makes you superior?"** ⭐ **The retort is the whole argument in miniature: the claim of "natural" superiority collapses on contact with evidence.**)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ WHY the distinction is NOT reliable — three problems

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Problem 1 — long-standing social inequalities MASQUERADE as natural
⭐⭐ **"When certain inequalities in the treatment of people HAVE EXISTED OVER A LONG PERIOD OF TIME, THEY MAY APPEAR TO US AS JUSTIFIABLE BECAUSE THEY ARE BASED ON NATURAL INEQUALITIES — that is, characteristics that people are born with and cannot easily change."**

| Case | The false "natural" claim | What it justified |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Women** | **"Women were for long described as 'THE WEAKER SEX', considered TIMID and OF LESSER INTELLIGENCE THAN MEN, NEEDING SPECIAL PROTECTION."** | ⭐ **"Therefore it was felt that DENYING WOMEN EQUAL RIGHTS COULD BE JUSTIFIED."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Black people in Africa** | **"Black people in Africa were considered by their COLONIAL MASTERS to be OF LESSER INTELLIGENCE, CHILD-LIKE, and BETTER AT MANUAL WORK, SPORTS AND MUSIC."** | ⭐⭐ **"This belief was used to JUSTIFY INSTITUTIONS LIKE SLAVERY."** |

⭐⭐⭐ **The verdict:** *"**ALL THESE ASSESSMENTS ARE NOW QUESTIONED. THEY ARE NOW SEEN AS DISTINCTIONS MADE BY SOCIETY AS A RESULT OF THE DIFFERENCES OF POWER BETWEEN PEOPLE AND NATIONS, RATHER THAN BASED ON THEIR INBORN CHARACTERISTICS.**"*

> **⭐⭐ This is the key insight of the section: what gets called "natural" is very often the ideology of the powerful. Note how the *same* move was used against women and against colonised peoples — attributing lesser intelligence and fitness only for manual work.**

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Problem 2 — some "natural" differences are no longer unalterable
⭐⭐ **"Another problem is that SOME DIFFERENCES WHICH COULD BE CONSIDERED NATURAL NEED NO LONGER BE SEEN AS UNALTERABLE. For instance, ADVANCES IN MEDICAL SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGIES HAVE HELPED MANY DISABLED PEOPLE TO FUNCTION EFFECTIVELY IN SOCIETY."**

⭐ **The examples:** *"**COMPUTERS can help BLIND people; WHEEL CHAIRS AND ARTIFICIAL LIMBS can help in cases of PHYSICAL DISABILITY; even a person's LOOKS can be changed with COSMETIC SURGERY.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The illustration:** *"**The famous physicist STEPHEN HAWKING CAN HARDLY MOVE OR SPEAK, BUT HE HAS MADE MAJOR CONTRIBUTIONS TO SCIENCE.**"*

⭐⭐ **The moral conclusion:** *"**IT WOULD SEEM UNJUST TO MOST PEOPLE TODAY IF DISABLED PEOPLE ARE (i) DENIED NECESSARY HELP TO OVERCOME THE EFFECTS OF THEIR DISABILITY, OR (ii) DENIED A FAIR REWARD FOR THEIR WORK, ON THE GROUNDS THAT THEY ARE NATURALLY LESS CAPABLE.**"*

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Problem 3 — so the distinction cannot serve as a policy standard, and is replaced
⭐⭐ **"Given all these complexities, IT WOULD BE DIFFICULT TO USE THE NATURAL/SOCIALLY-PRODUCED DISTINCTION AS A STANDARD BY WHICH THE LAWS AND POLICIES OF A SOCIETY CAN BE ASSESSED."**

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ THE REPLACEMENT DISTINCTION — the one modern theorists actually use
> **"For this reason, MANY THEORISTS TODAY DIFFERENTIATE BETWEEN:**
> **(i) INEQUALITY ARISING FROM OUR CHOICES, and**
> **(ii) INEQUALITIES OPERATING ON ACCOUNT OF THE FAMILY OR CIRCUMSTANCE IN WHICH A PERSON IS BORN.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **IT IS THE LATTER THAT IS A SOURCE OF CONCERN TO ADVOCATES OF EQUALITY, AND WHICH THEY WISH TO MINIMISE AND ELIMINATE."**

**The shift to remember: NOT "natural vs social", but "CHOICE vs CIRCUMSTANCE OF BIRTH."** *(Note how this matches the earlier formula — treatment must not be **"pre-determined by birth or social circumstance"** — and the slum child who suffers **"through no fault of his/her own."*)*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 4 — THE THREE DIMENSIONS OF EQUALITY

⭐⭐ **"While identifying different kinds of inequalities that exist in society, various thinkers and ideologies have highlighted THREE MAIN DIMENSIONS OF EQUALITY, namely POLITICAL, SOCIAL and ECONOMIC. ⭐⭐ IT IS ONLY BY ADDRESSING EACH OF THESE THREE DIFFERENT DIMENSIONS OF EQUALITY THAT WE CAN MOVE TOWARDS A MORE JUST AND EQUAL SOCIETY."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ Dimension 1 — POLITICAL EQUALITY
⭐⭐ **"In democratic societies, political equality would normally include GRANTING EQUAL CITIZENSHIP TO ALL THE MEMBERS OF THE STATE."**

⭐ **The rights it brings:** *"Equal citizenship brings with it certain **basic rights** such as **THE RIGHT TO VOTE, FREEDOM OF EXPRESSION, MOVEMENT AND ASSOCIATION, and FREEDOM OF BELIEF.** These are rights which are considered necessary to enable citizens **(i) TO DEVELOP THEMSELVES and (ii) TO PARTICIPATE IN THE AFFAIRS OF THE STATE.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The crucial limitation:** *"**BUT THEY ARE LEGAL RIGHTS, GUARANTEED BY THE CONSTITUTION AND LAWS. ⭐ WE KNOW THAT CONSIDERABLE INEQUALITY CAN EXIST EVEN IN COUNTRIES WHICH GRANT EQUAL RIGHTS TO ALL CITIZENS. These inequalities are often the result of DIFFERENCES IN THE RESOURCES AND OPPORTUNITIES WHICH ARE AVAILABLE TO CITIZENS IN THE SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC SPHERES.**"*

⭐⭐ **"For this reason A DEMAND IS OFTEN MADE FOR EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES, OR FOR 'A LEVEL PLAYING FIELD'."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The balanced verdict (note it carefully — it is a favourite exam trap):** *"**But we should remember that ALTHOUGH POLITICAL AND LEGAL EQUALITY BY ITSELF MAY NOT BE SUFFICIENT to build a just and egalitarian society, IT IS CERTAINLY AN IMPORTANT COMPONENT OF IT.**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ Dimension 2 — SOCIAL EQUALITY
⭐⭐ **"Political equality, or EQUALITY BEFORE THE LAW, is an IMPORTANT FIRST STEP in the pursuit of equality, BUT IT OFTEN NEEDS TO BE SUPPLEMENTED BY EQUALITY OF OPPORTUNITIES."**

⭐⭐ **The division of labour between the two:**
- **"The FORMER [political/legal equality] is necessary TO REMOVE ANY LEGAL HURDLES which might (i) EXCLUDE PEOPLE FROM A VOICE IN GOVERNMENT and (ii) DENY THEM ACCESS TO AVAILABLE SOCIAL GOODS."**
- ⭐ **"The pursuit of equality requires that PEOPLE BELONGING TO DIFFERENT GROUPS AND COMMUNITIES ALSO HAVE A FAIR AND EQUAL CHANCE TO COMPETE FOR THOSE GOODS AND OPPORTUNITIES."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The minimum conditions of life (a certain exam list)
⭐⭐ **"For this, it is necessary to MINIMISE THE EFFECTS OF SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC INEQUALITIES and GUARANTEE CERTAIN MINIMUM CONDITIONS OF LIFE TO ALL THE MEMBERS OF THE SOCIETY:"**
1. **"ADEQUATE HEALTH CARE"**
2. **"THE OPPORTUNITY FOR GOOD EDUCATION"**
3. **"ADEQUATE NOURISHMENT"**
4. **"A MINIMUM WAGE"**
*"...among other things."*

⭐⭐ **"IN THE ABSENCE OF SUCH FACILITIES IT IS EXCEEDINGLY DIFFICULT FOR ALL THE MEMBERS OF THE SOCIETY TO COMPETE ON EQUAL TERMS."**

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ The efficiency argument for equality — a powerful line
> **"WHERE EQUALITY OF OPPORTUNITY DOES NOT EXIST, A HUGE POOL OF POTENTIAL TALENT TENDS TO BE WASTED IN SOCIETY."**
>
> **Use this whenever you need a non-moral, purely practical argument for equality: inequality is not only unjust, it is WASTEFUL.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ India's special problem — CUSTOM, not just facilities
⭐⭐ **"In India, A SPECIAL PROBLEM regarding equal opportunities comes NOT JUST FROM LACK OF FACILITIES BUT FROM SOME OF THE CUSTOMS WHICH MAY PREVAIL IN DIFFERENT PARTS OF THE COUNTRY, OR AMONG DIFFERENT GROUPS."**

⭐⭐ **The three examples, all concerning women:**
1. **"Women may NOT ENJOY EQUAL RIGHTS OF INHERITANCE in some groups"**
2. **"There may be SOCIAL PROHIBITIONS regarding their TAKING PART IN CERTAIN KINDS OF ACTIVITIES"**
3. **"They may even be DISCOURAGED FROM OBTAINING HIGHER EDUCATION"**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The role of the state — and of society
⭐⭐ **"THE STATE HAS A SIGNIFICANT ROLE IN SUCH MATTERS. It should make policies:"**
1. **"TO PREVENT DISCRIMINATION OR HARASSMENT OF WOMEN IN PUBLIC PLACES OR EMPLOYMENT"**
2. **"TO PROVIDE INCENTIVES TO OPEN UP EDUCATION OR CERTAIN PROFESSIONS TO WOMEN"**
3. **"...and other such measures"**

⭐⭐ **But the state is not enough:** *"**BUT SOCIAL GROUPS AND INDIVIDUALS ALSO HAVE A ROLE TO PLAY IN (i) RAISING AWARENESS AND (ii) SUPPORTING THOSE WHO WANT TO EXERCISE THEIR RIGHTS.**"*

⭐⭐ **The chapter's own questions about this table (worth answering):**

- **"Could these differences have taken place JUST BY CHANCE?"** → **No — the gradient tracks the caste hierarchy too precisely to be random.**
- ⭐ **"Or do these differences POINT TO THE WORKING OF THE CASTE SYSTEM?"** → **Yes — SC, OBC and Muslim figures all fall far below the all-India average of 155, while Hindu-Upper Caste, Sikh and Christian figures sit far above it.**
- ⭐⭐ **"WHICH FACTOR OTHER THAN THE CASTE SYSTEM DO YOU SEE AT WORK HERE?"** → ⭐ **The Muslim figure (61) is the giveaway: Muslims are not part of the Hindu caste system, yet rank second-lowest.** So **RELIGIOUS-COMMUNITY disadvantage and, above all, ECONOMIC CLASS (poverty, first-generation-learner status) are operating alongside caste.** *(Note also that this is **urban** India only — so the **rural–urban gap** documented in the Census table is excluded from the picture entirely, and the real disparities are wider.)*

## ⭐⭐⭐ Dimension 3 — ECONOMIC EQUALITY
⭐⭐⭐ **The definition:** *"At the simplest level, we would say that **ECONOMIC INEQUALITY EXISTS IN A SOCIETY IF THERE ARE SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCES IN WEALTH, PROPERTY OR INCOME BETWEEN INDIVIDUALS OR CLASSES.**"*

⭐⭐ **The two ways of measuring it:**
1. **"MEASURE THE RELATIVE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE RICHEST AND POOREST GROUPS"**
2. **"ESTIMATE THE NUMBER OF PEOPLE WHO LIVE BELOW THE POVERTY LINE"**

⭐⭐ **The realistic starting point:** *"**Of course, ABSOLUTE EQUALITY OF WEALTH OR INCOME HAS PROBABLY NEVER EXISTED IN A SOCIETY.**"*

⭐⭐ **What democracies therefore do:** *"**MOST DEMOCRACIES TODAY TRY TO MAKE EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES AVAILABLE TO PEOPLE, IN THE BELIEF THAT THIS WOULD AT LEAST GIVE THOSE WHO HAVE TALENT AND DETERMINATION THE CHANCE TO IMPROVE THEIR CONDITION. ⭐ WITH EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES, INEQUALITIES MAY CONTINUE TO EXIST BETWEEN INDIVIDUALS, BUT THERE IS THE POSSIBILITY OF IMPROVING ONE'S POSITION IN SOCIETY WITH SUFFICIENT EFFORT.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ ENTRENCHED inequality — the real danger (a very important concept)
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"INEQUALITIES WHICH ARE ENTRENCHED — THAT IS, WHICH REMAIN RELATIVELY UNTOUCHED OVER GENERATIONS — ARE MORE DANGEROUS FOR A SOCIETY."**

⭐⭐ **The mechanism, spelt out:** *"**IF IN A SOCIETY CERTAIN CLASSES OF PEOPLE HAVE ENJOYED CONSIDERABLE WEALTH — AND THE POWER WHICH GOES WITH IT — OVER GENERATIONS, THE SOCIETY WOULD BECOME DIVIDED BETWEEN THOSE CLASSES AND OTHERS WHO HAVE REMAINED POOR OVER GENERATIONS.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The two consequences:**
1. **"OVER TIME SUCH CLASS DIFFERENCES CAN GIVE RISE TO RESENTMENT AND VIOLENCE."**
2. ⭐⭐ **"BECAUSE OF THE POWER OF THE WEALTHY CLASSES, IT MIGHT PROVE DIFFICULT TO REFORM SUCH A SOCIETY TO MAKE IT MORE OPEN AND EGALITARIAN."** ← *the vicious circle: entrenched inequality protects itself*

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE FOUR IDEOLOGIES (four boxes — all four are examinable)

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX 1: FEMINISM
⭐⭐⭐ **The definition:** *"**FEMINISM IS A POLITICAL DOCTRINE OF EQUAL RIGHTS FOR WOMEN AND MEN.**"*

⭐⭐ **Who feminists are:** *"**Feminists are those MEN AND WOMEN who believe that MANY OF THE INEQUALITIES WE SEE IN SOCIETY BETWEEN MEN AND WOMEN ARE NEITHER NATURAL NOR NECESSARY, AND CAN BE ALTERED so that both women and men can lead FREE AND EQUAL LIVES.**"* *(Note: **feminists can be men** — worth remembering.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ PATRIARCHY
⭐⭐⭐ **"According to feminists, INEQUALITY BETWEEN MEN AND WOMEN IN SOCIETY IS THE RESULT OF PATRIARCHY. This term refers to A SOCIAL, ECONOMIC AND CULTURAL SYSTEM THAT VALUES MEN MORE THAN WOMEN AND GIVES MEN POWER OVER WOMEN."**

⭐⭐ **Its justifying assumption:** *"**Patriarchy is based on the assumption that MEN AND WOMEN ARE DIFFERENT BY NATURE, AND THAT THIS DIFFERENCE JUSTIFIES THEIR UNEQUAL POSITIONS IN SOCIETY.**"* *(Exactly the "natural inequality" fallacy from Part 3.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ SEX vs GENDER — the decisive feminist distinction
> **"Feminists question this way of thinking by making a distinction between:**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **'SEX' — i.e. the BIOLOGICAL DIFFERENCE between men and women, and**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **'GENDER' — which determines THE DIFFERENT ROLES THAT MEN AND WOMEN PLAY IN SOCIETY."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The knock-down example:** *"**For instance, THE BIOLOGICAL FACT THAT ONLY WOMEN CAN BECOME PREGNANT AND BEAR CHILDREN DOES NOT REQUIRE THAT ONLY WOMEN SHOULD LOOK AFTER CHILDREN AFTER THEY ARE BORN.**"*

⭐⭐ **"Feminists show us that MUCH OF THE INEQUALITY BETWEEN MEN AND WOMEN IS PRODUCED BY SOCIETY AND NOT BY NATURE."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The public/private division and the "DOUBLE BURDEN"
⭐⭐ **"PATRIARCHY PRODUCES A DIVISION OF LABOUR by which WOMEN ARE SUPPOSED TO BE RESPONSIBLE FOR 'PRIVATE' AND 'DOMESTIC' MATTERS, WHILE MEN ARE RESPONSIBLE FOR WORK IN THE 'PUBLIC' DOMAIN."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The feminist refutation, in three moves:**
1. **"Feminists question this distinction by pointing out that IN FACT MOST WOMEN ARE ALSO ACTIVE IN THE 'PUBLIC' DOMAIN. ⭐ MOST WOMEN ALL OVER THE WORLD ARE EMPLOYED IN SOME FORM OF WORK OUTSIDE THE HOME..."**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **"...BUT WOMEN CONTINUE TO BE SOLELY RESPONSIBLE FOR HOUSEWORK AS WELL. ⭐⭐ HOWEVER, DESPITE THIS 'DOUBLE BURDEN' AS FEMINISTS TERM IT, WOMEN ARE GIVEN LITTLE OR NO SAY IN DECISIONS TAKEN IN THE PUBLIC DOMAIN."**
3. **"Feminists contend that THIS PUBLIC/PRIVATE DISTINCTION AND ALL FORMS OF GENDER INEQUALITIES CAN AND SHOULD BE ELIMINATED."**

**⭐⭐ The "double burden" is the key term: women do paid work outside AND all the unpaid work inside, yet get no corresponding voice in public decisions.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX 2: MARXISM
⭐⭐ **"MARX was an important NINETEENTH CENTURY thinker who argued that THE ROOT CAUSE OF ENTRENCHED INEQUALITY WAS PRIVATE OWNERSHIP OF IMPORTANT ECONOMIC RESOURCES — SUCH AS OIL, OR LAND, OR FORESTS — AS WELL AS OTHER FORMS OF PROPERTY."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The two-step argument (learn the chain — this is why Marx matters politically, not just economically):**
1. **"He pointed out that such private ownership DID NOT ONLY MAKE THE CLASS OF OWNERS WEALTHY..."**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **"...IT ALSO GAVE THEM POLITICAL POWER. ⭐⭐ SUCH POWER ENABLES THEM TO INFLUENCE STATE POLICIES AND LAWS, AND THIS COULD PROVE A THREAT TO DEMOCRATIC GOVERNMENT."**

⭐⭐ **The Marxist/socialist claim about the linkage of inequalities:** *"**MARXISTS AND SOCIALISTS FEEL THAT ECONOMIC INEQUALITY PROVIDES SUPPORT TO OTHER FORMS OF SOCIAL INEQUALITY, SUCH AS DIFFERENCES OF RANK OR PRIVILEGE.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The policy conclusion:** *"**Therefore, to tackle inequality in society WE NEED TO GO BEYOND PROVIDING EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES AND TRY AND ENSURE PUBLIC CONTROL OVER ESSENTIAL RESOURCES AND FORMS OF PROPERTY.**"*

⭐ **The chapter's balanced note:** *"Such views MAY BE DEBATABLE, but they have RAISED IMPORTANT ISSUES WHICH NEED TO BE ADDRESSED."*

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX 3: LIBERALISM (on equality)
⭐⭐ **"An OPPOSING point of view can be found in LIBERAL theories."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The core liberal position:** *"**LIBERALS UPHOLD THE PRINCIPLE OF COMPETITION AS THE MOST EFFICIENT AND FAIR WAY OF DISTRIBUTING RESOURCES AND REWARDS IN SOCIETY.**"*

⭐⭐ **What liberals concede — and where they stop:** *"**They believe that WHILE STATES MAY HAVE TO INTERVENE to try and ensure A MINIMUM STANDARD OF LIVING AND EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES FOR ALL, THIS CANNOT BY ITSELF BRING EQUALITY AND JUSTICE TO SOCIETY. ⭐ COMPETITION BETWEEN PEOPLE IN FREE AND FAIR CONDITIONS IS THE MOST JUST AND EFFICIENT WAY OF DISTRIBUTING REWARDS.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The liberal answer to the "entrenchment" worry:** *"**For them, AS LONG AS COMPETITION IS OPEN AND FREE, INEQUALITIES ARE UNLIKELY TO BECOME ENTRENCHED, AND PEOPLE WILL GET DUE REWARD FOR THEIR TALENTS AND EFFORTS.**"*

⭐⭐ **The Indian illustration the chapter gives:** *"**For liberals the principle of competition is the most just and efficient way of selecting candidates for jobs or admission to educational institutions.** For instance, in our country many students hope for admission to **professional courses** and entry is highly competitive. ⭐ **From time to time, THE GOVERNMENT AND THE COURTS HAVE STEPPED IN TO REGULATE EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS AND THE ENTRANCE TESTS TO ENSURE THAT EVERYBODY GETS A FAIR AND EQUAL CHANCE TO COMPETE. Some may still not get admission, but IT IS CONSIDERED TO BE A FAIR WAY OF DISTRIBUTING LIMITED SEATS.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Liberals vs Socialists — the crucial difference on the LINKAGE of inequalities
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"UNLIKE SOCIALISTS, LIBERALS DO NOT BELIEVE THAT POLITICAL, ECONOMIC AND SOCIAL INEQUALITIES ARE NECESSARILY LINKED. They maintain that INEQUALITIES IN EACH OF THESE SPHERES SHOULD BE TACKLED APPROPRIATELY."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"Thus, DEMOCRACY COULD HELP TO PROVIDE POLITICAL EQUALITY, BUT IT MIGHT BE NECESSARY TO ALSO DEVISE DIFFERENT STRATEGIES TO DEAL WITH SOCIAL DIFFERENCES AND ECONOMIC INEQUALITIES."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"THE PROBLEM FOR LIBERALS IS NOT INEQUALITY AS SUCH, BUT UNJUST AND ENTRENCHED INEQUALITIES WHICH PREVENT INDIVIDUALS FROM DEVELOPING THEIR CAPABILITIES."**

**⭐⭐ The sharpest way to state the Marxist–liberal split: MARXISTS see one inequality (economic) generating the rest, so they attack ownership. LIBERALS see three separate inequalities needing three separate remedies, so they defend fair competition.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX 4: SOCIALISM — and Lohia's SAPTA KRANTI
⭐⭐ **"SOCIALISM refers to a set of political ideas that emerged AS A RESPONSE TO THE INEQUALITIES PRESENT IN, AND REPRODUCED BY, THE INDUSTRIAL CAPITALIST ECONOMY."**

⭐⭐⭐ **"The MAIN CONCERN of Socialism is HOW TO MINIMISE EXISTING INEQUALITY AND DISTRIBUTE RESOURCES JUSTLY."**

⭐⭐ **Their stance on the market:** *"**Although advocates of socialism are NOT ENTIRELY OPPOSED TO THE MARKET, they favour SOME KIND OF GOVERNMENT REGULATION, PLANNING AND CONTROL OVER CERTAIN KEY AREAS SUCH AS EDUCATION AND HEALTH CARE.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ RAMMANOHAR LOHIA'S FIVE INEQUALITIES (a very frequently asked list)
⭐⭐⭐ **"In India the eminent socialist thinker RAMMANOHAR LOHIA identified FIVE KINDS OF INEQUALITIES THAT NEED TO BE FOUGHT AGAINST SIMULTANEOUSLY:"**
| # | Inequality |
|---|---|
| **1** | **"Inequality between MAN AND WOMAN"** |
| **2** | **"Inequality based on SKIN COLOUR"** |
| **3** | **"CASTE-BASED inequality"** |
| **4** | **"COLONIAL RULE of some countries over others"** |
| **5** | **"And, of course, ECONOMIC inequality"** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Why Lohia's argument was radical in its time
⭐⭐ **"This might appear a SELF-EVIDENT idea today. ⭐⭐⭐ BUT DURING LOHIA'S TIME IT WAS COMMON FOR THE SOCIALISTS TO ARGUE THAT CLASS INEQUALITY WAS THE ONLY FORM OF INEQUALITY WORTH STRUGGLING AGAINST. OTHER INEQUALITIES DID NOT MATTER, OR WOULD END AUTOMATICALLY IF ECONOMIC INEQUALITY COULD BE ENDED."**

⭐⭐⭐ **Lohia's counter-claim:** *"**LOHIA ARGUED THAT EACH OF THESE INEQUALITIES HAD INDEPENDENT ROOTS AND HAD TO BE FOUGHT SEPARATELY AND SIMULTANEOUSLY.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The SEVEN REVOLUTIONS — *SAPTA KRANTI*
⭐⭐ **"HE DID NOT SPEAK OF REVOLUTION IN THE SINGULAR. FOR HIM, STRUGGLE AGAINST THESE FIVE INEQUALITIES CONSTITUTED FIVE REVOLUTIONS. ⭐⭐⭐ HE ADDED TWO MORE REVOLUTIONS TO THIS LIST:"**
| # | The two additional revolutions |
|---|---|
| **6** | ⭐ **"Revolution for CIVIL LIBERTIES against UNJUST ENCROACHMENTS ON PRIVATE LIFE"** |
| **7** | ⭐ **"Revolution for NON-VIOLENCE — for RENUNCIATION OF WEAPONS IN FAVOUR OF *SATYAGRAHA*"** |

⭐⭐⭐ **"THESE WERE THE SEVEN REVOLUTIONS OR *SAPTA KRANTI*, WHICH FOR LOHIA WAS THE IDEAL OF SOCIALISM."**

> **⭐⭐ Memorise the seven: (1) man–woman, (2) skin colour, (3) caste, (4) colonial rule, (5) economic, (6) civil liberties, (7) non-violence/*satyagraha*.**
> **⭐ And note *why* Lohia matters: he is the anti-reductionist. Against orthodox socialism's claim that ending class inequality ends everything else, he insisted each inequality has INDEPENDENT ROOTS. This is the intellectual ancestor of what we now call intersectionality.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 5 — HOW CAN WE PROMOTE EQUALITY? (three steps)

⭐ **The framing:** *"While the relative merits and limitations of [the socialist and liberal] points of view are being debated the world over, we still need to consider **what principles and policies might be considered necessary for pursuing equality.** Specifically, we need to consider **IF THE USE OF AFFIRMATIVE ACTION IS JUSTIFIED for purposes of bringing about equality.** This issue **has raised a lot of controversy in recent years.**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ Step 1 — ESTABLISHING FORMAL EQUALITY
⭐⭐⭐ **"THE FIRST STEP towards bringing about equality is, of course, ENDING THE FORMAL SYSTEM OF INEQUALITY AND PRIVILEGES."**

⭐⭐ **"Social, economic and political inequalities all over the world HAVE BEEN PROTECTED BY CUSTOMS AND LEGAL SYSTEMS THAT PROHIBITED SOME SECTIONS OF SOCIETY FROM ENJOYING CERTAIN KINDS OF OPPORTUNITIES AND REWARDS."**

⭐⭐ **The four historical examples:**
1. **"POOR PEOPLE were NOT GRANTED THE RIGHT TO VOTE in a large number of countries"**
2. **"WOMEN were NOT ALLOWED TO TAKE UP MANY PROFESSIONS AND ACTIVITIES"**
3. ⭐ **"The CASTE SYSTEM in India PREVENTED PEOPLE FROM THE 'LOWER' CASTES FROM DOING ANYTHING EXCEPT MANUAL LABOUR"**
4. **"In many countries ONLY PEOPLE FROM SOME FAMILIES COULD OCCUPY HIGH POSITIONS"**

⭐⭐ **"Attainment of equality requires that ALL SUCH RESTRICTIONS OR PRIVILEGES SHOULD BE BROUGHT TO AN END. ⭐ SINCE MANY OF THESE SYSTEMS HAVE THE SANCTION OF LAW, EQUALITY REQUIRES THAT THE GOVERNMENT AND THE LAW OF THE LAND SHOULD STOP PROTECTING THESE SYSTEMS OF INEQUALITY."**

⭐⭐⭐ **What the Indian Constitution does:** *"**THIS IS WHAT OUR CONSTITUTION DOES. The Constitution PROHIBITS DISCRIMINATION ON GROUNDS OF RELIGION, RACE, CASTE, SEX OR PLACE OF BIRTH. Our Constitution also ABOLISHES THE PRACTICE OF UNTOUCHABILITY.**"*

⭐⭐ **The definition of formal equality:** *"**MOST MODERN CONSTITUTIONS AND DEMOCRATIC GOVERNMENTS HAVE FORMALLY ACCEPTED THE PRINCIPLE OF EQUALITY AND INCORPORATED IT AS IDENTICAL TREATMENT BY LAW TO ALL CITIZENS WITHOUT ANY REGARD TO THEIR CASTE, RACE, RELIGION OR GENDER.**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ Step 2 — EQUALITY THROUGH DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT
⭐⭐⭐ **"HOWEVER, FORMAL EQUALITY OR EQUALITY BEFORE LAW IS NECESSARY BUT NOT SUFFICIENT to realise the principle of equality. ⭐⭐ SOMETIMES IT IS NECESSARY TO TREAT PEOPLE DIFFERENTLY IN ORDER TO ENSURE THAT THEY CAN ENJOY EQUAL RIGHTS. Certain differences between people may have to be TAKEN INTO ACCOUNT for this purpose."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The two examples (learn both — they are the standard illustrations)
| Example | The reasoning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **"DISABLED people may justifiably demand SPECIAL RAMPS IN PUBLIC SPACES so that they get AN EQUAL CHANCE TO ENTER PUBLIC BUILDINGS"** | Identical treatment (steps for everyone) produces **unequal access**; differential treatment produces **equal access** |
| ⭐⭐ **"WOMEN WORKING IN CALL CENTRES AT NIGHT may need SPECIAL PROTECTION DURING THE JOURNEY TO AND FROM THE CENTRE so that THEIR EQUAL RIGHT TO WORK MAY BE PROTECTED"** | Without the special provision, the **right to work itself** becomes unusable for women |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE KEY SENTENCE:** **"THESE SHOULD NOT BE SEEN AS INFRINGEMENTS OF EQUALITY BUT AS ENHANCEMENT OF EQUALITY."**

⭐ **"What kinds of differences hinder access to equal opportunities, and what kinds of policies may be pursued to overcome those hindrances, are questions that are being discussed IN ALMOST ALL SOCIETIES TODAY. ⭐ SOME COUNTRIES HAVE USED POLICIES OF AFFIRMATIVE ACTION to enhance equality of opportunity. ⭐⭐ IN OUR COUNTRY WE HAVE RELIED ON THE POLICY OF RESERVATIONS."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ Step 3 — AFFIRMATIVE ACTION

### ⭐⭐⭐ The definition and rationale
⭐⭐⭐ **"AFFIRMATIVE ACTION IS BASED ON THE IDEA THAT IT IS NOT SUFFICIENT TO ESTABLISH FORMAL EQUALITY BY LAW. ⭐⭐ WHEN WE WISH TO ELIMINATE INEQUALITIES THAT ARE DEEPLY ROOTED, IT IS NECESSARY TO TAKE SOME MORE POSITIVE MEASURES TO MINIMISE AND ELIMINATE ENTRENCHED FORMS OF SOCIAL INEQUALITIES."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **"MOST POLICIES OF AFFIRMATIVE ACTION ARE THUS DESIGNED TO CORRECT THE CUMULATIVE EFFECT OF PAST INEQUALITIES."**
>
> **The phrase "CUMULATIVE EFFECT OF PAST INEQUALITIES" is the single best three-word justification of affirmative action. Learn it.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The forms affirmative action can take
⭐⭐ **"Affirmative action can however TAKE MANY FORMS:"**
1. ⭐ **"PREFERENTIAL SPENDING ON FACILITIES FOR DISADVANTAGED COMMUNITIES — such as SCHOLARSHIPS AND HOSTELS"**
2. **"SPECIAL CONSIDERATION FOR ADMISSIONS TO EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS AND JOBS"**
3. ⭐⭐ **"In our country we have adopted a policy of QUOTAS OR RESERVED SEATS in education and jobs to provide equality of opportunity to deprived groups — AND THIS HAS BEEN THE SUBJECT OF CONSIDERABLE DEBATE AND DISAGREEMENT."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE DEFENCE of reservation
⭐⭐ **"The policy has been defended on the ground that:"**
1. **"CERTAIN GROUPS HAVE BEEN VICTIMS OF SOCIAL PREJUDICE AND DISCRIMINATION IN THE FORM OF EXCLUSION AND SEGREGATION."**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **"THESE COMMUNITIES WHO HAVE SUFFERED IN THE PAST AND BEEN DENIED EQUAL OPPORTUNITIES CANNOT BE IMMEDIATELY EXPECTED TO COMPETE WITH OTHERS ON EQUAL TERMS."**
3. **"THEREFORE, IN THE INTEREST OF CREATING AN EGALITARIAN AND JUST SOCIETY, THEY NEED TO BE GIVEN SPECIAL PROTECTION AND HELP."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The TEMPORARY nature of affirmative action
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"SPECIAL ASSISTANCE IN THE FORM OF AFFIRMATIVE ACTION IS EXPECTED TO BE A TEMPORARY OR TIME-BOUND MEASURE. ⭐⭐ THE ASSUMPTION IS THAT SPECIAL CONSIDERATION WILL ENABLE THESE COMMUNITIES TO OVERCOME THE EXISTING DISADVANTAGES AND THEN COMPETE WITH OTHERS ON EQUAL TERMS."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE CASE AGAINST — the critics' argument (state it fairly)
⭐⭐ **"Although policies of affirmative action are supported for making society more equal, MANY THEORISTS ARGUE AGAINST THEM. THEY QUESTION WHETHER TREATING PEOPLE DIFFERENTLY CAN EVER LEAD TO GREATER EQUALITY."**

⭐⭐⭐ **"CRITICS OF POSITIVE DISCRIMINATION — PARTICULARLY POLICIES OF RESERVATIONS — THUS INVOKE THE PRINCIPLE OF EQUALITY TO ARGUE AGAINST SUCH POLICIES."** *(Note the irony: **both sides invoke equality.**)*

⭐⭐ **The critics' four claims:**
1. ⭐ **"Any provision of reservations or quotas for the deprived in admissions for higher education or jobs is UNFAIR, AS IT ARBITRARILY DENIES OTHER SECTIONS OF SOCIETY THEIR RIGHT TO EQUAL TREATMENT."**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **"They maintain that RESERVATIONS ARE A FORM OF REVERSE DISCRIMINATION, and they CONTINUE WITH THE PRACTICES THAT THE PRINCIPLE OF EQUALITY QUESTIONS AND REJECTS."**
3. ⭐⭐ **"EQUALITY REQUIRES THAT ALL PERSONS BE TREATED ALIKE — AND WHEN WE MAKE DISTINCTIONS BETWEEN INDIVIDUALS ON THE BASIS OF THEIR CASTE OR COLOUR, WE ARE LIKELY TO REINFORCE CASTE AND RACIAL PREJUDICES."**
4. ⭐ **"For these theorists, THE IMPORTANT THING IS TO DO AWAY WITH SOCIAL DISTINCTIONS THAT DIVIDE OUR SOCIETY."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE RESOLUTION — the distinction that settles the debate
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"In the context of this debate, IT IS RELEVANT TO DRAW A DISTINCTION BETWEEN (i) EQUALITY AS A GUIDING PRINCIPLE OF STATE POLICY AND (ii) EQUAL RIGHTS OF INDIVIDUALS."**

⭐⭐ **"INDIVIDUALS HAVE A RIGHT TO EQUAL CONSIDERATION for admission to educational institutions and public sector employment. ⭐⭐⭐ BUT COMPETITION SHOULD BE FAIR."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The argument from the starting line (the most persuasive passage in the chapter):**
> **"SOMETIMES, WHEN COMPETING FOR LIMITED SEATS OR JOBS, PEOPLE FROM DEPRIVED STRATA MAY BE AT A DISADVANTAGE. ⭐⭐⭐ THE NEEDS AND CIRCUMSTANCES OF A FIRST GENERATION LEARNER WHOSE PARENTS AND ANCESTORS WERE ILLITERATE ARE VERY DIFFERENT FROM THOSE WHO ARE BORN INTO EDUCATED FAMILIES."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"MEMBERS OF EXCLUDED GROUPS — WHETHER THEY ARE DALITS, WOMEN, OR ANY OTHER CATEGORY — DESERVE AND NEED SOME SPECIAL HELP. To provide this, THE STATE MUST DEVISE SOCIAL POLICIES WHICH WOULD HELP TO MAKE SUCH PEOPLE EQUAL AND GIVE THEM A FAIR CHANCE TO COMPETE WITH OTHERS."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ India's actual record — a blunt assessment
> ⭐⭐ **"THE FACT IS THAT IN THE SPHERES OF EDUCATION AND HEALTH CARE, INDIA HAS DONE FAR LESS FOR ITS DEPRIVED POPULATION THAN WHAT IS THEIR DUE."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The chain of cumulative disadvantage in schooling (trace each link — it is a superb answer structure):**
1. **"INEQUALITIES IN SCHOOL EDUCATION ARE GLARING."**
2. **"MANY POOR CHILDREN IN RURAL AREAS OR URBAN SLUMS HAVE LITTLE CHANCE OF ATTENDING SCHOOLS."**
3. ⭐ **"IF THEY DO GET THE CHANCE, THEIR SCHOOLS HAVE LITTLE TO OFFER THAT WOULD BE COMPARABLE TO THE FACILITIES AVAILABLE IN ELITE SCHOOLS."**
4. ⭐⭐ **"THE INEQUALITIES WITH WHICH CHILDREN ENTER SCHOOL TEND TO CONTINUE TO HAMPER THEIR CHANCES TO IMPROVE THEIR QUALIFICATIONS OR GET GOOD JOBS."**
5. ⭐⭐ **"These students FACE HURDLES IN GAINING ADMISSION TO ELITE PROFESSIONAL COURSES BECAUSE THEY LACK THE MEANS TO PAY FOR SPECIAL COACHING."**
6. ⭐ **"THE FEES FOR PROFESSIONAL COURSES ALSO MAY BE PROHIBITIVELY HIGH."**
7. **"CONSEQUENTLY, THEY CANNOT COMPETE ON EQUAL TERMS WITH THE MORE PRIVILEGED SECTIONS."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Where the real disagreement lies — a crucial clarification
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"Social and economic inequalities of this kind hinder the pursuit of equal opportunities. MOST THEORISTS TODAY RECOGNISE THIS. ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT THEY CONTEST IS NOT THE GOAL OF EQUAL OPPORTUNITY BUT THE POLICIES THAT THE STATE SHOULD PURSUE TO ACHIEVE THAT GOAL."**

⭐⭐ **The three live policy questions:**
1. ⭐ **"SHOULD THE STATE RESERVE SEATS FOR THE DEPRIVED COMMUNITIES, OR SHOULD THEY PROVIDE SPECIAL FACILITIES THAT CAN HELP TO DEVELOP TALENTS AND SKILLS FROM AN EARLY AGE?"**
2. ⭐⭐ **"HOW SHOULD WE DEFINE WHO IS DEPRIVED?"**
3. ⭐⭐ **"SHOULD WE USE AN ECONOMIC CRITERION TO IDENTIFY THE DEPRIVED, OR SHOULD WE USE SOCIAL INEQUALITIES ARISING FROM THE CASTE SYSTEM IN OUR COUNTRY AS THE BASIS OF IDENTIFYING THE DEPRIVED GROUPS?"**

⭐⭐⭐ **The ultimate test of any such policy:** *"**ULTIMATELY THE POLICIES THAT WE CHOOSE WOULD HAVE TO BE JUSTIFIED IN TERMS OF THEIR SUCCESS IN MAKING THE SOCIETY MORE EGALITARIAN AND FAIR TO ALL.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE FINAL DISTINCTION — identical treatment vs treating as equal
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"While reflecting on the issue of equality, A DISTINCTION MUST ALSO BE MADE BETWEEN (i) TREATING EVERYONE IN AN IDENTICAL MANNER AND (ii) TREATING EVERYONE AS EQUAL. ⭐⭐ THE LATTER MAY ON OCCASIONS NEED DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT, BUT IN ALL SUCH CASES THE PRIMARY CONSIDERATION IS TO PROMOTE EQUALITY."**

⭐⭐ **The necessary caution:** *"**DIFFERENTIAL OR SPECIAL TREATMENT MAY BE CONSIDERED TO REALISE THE GOAL OF EQUALITY, BUT IT REQUIRES JUSTIFICATION AND CAREFUL REFLECTION.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **Why liberals are wary — an honest and important point:** *"**SINCE DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT FOR DIFFERENT COMMUNITIES WAS PART AND PARCEL OF THE CASTE SYSTEM AND PRACTICES LIKE APARTHEID, LIBERALS ARE USUALLY VERY WARY OF DEVIATIONS FROM THE NORM OF IDENTICAL TREATMENT.**"*

**⭐⭐ This is the strongest form of the objection: the caste system and apartheid *also* treated people differently. So "differential treatment" cannot by itself be a good — everything depends on whether its PURPOSE is to promote equality.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 6 — THE WOMEN'S MOVEMENT: the historical proof that differential treatment can be necessary

⭐⭐ **"Many of these issues relating to the pursuit of equality HAVE BEEN RAISED BY THE WOMEN'S MOVEMENT."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Phase 1 — the demand for the SAME rights (nineteenth century)
⭐⭐ **"In the NINETEENTH CENTURY women struggled for EQUAL RIGHTS. They demanded, for instance:"**
1. **"THE RIGHT TO VOTE"**
2. **"THE RIGHT TO RECEIVE DEGREES IN COLLEGES AND UNIVERSITIES"**
3. **"THE RIGHT TO WORK"**

⭐ **"— that is, THE SAME RIGHTS AS THE MEN IN THEIR SOCIETY."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Phase 2 — the discovery that the same rights were not enough
⭐⭐⭐ **"HOWEVER, AS THEY ENTERED THE JOB MARKET, THEY REALISED THAT WOMEN REQUIRED SPECIAL FACILITIES IN ORDER TO EXERCISE THESE RIGHTS."**

⭐⭐ **The two facilities named:**
1. ⭐ **"SOME PROVISION FOR MATERNITY LEAVE"**
2. ⭐ **"CRÈCHES IN THE WORKPLACE"**

⭐⭐⭐ **The consequence:** *"**WITHOUT SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS OF THIS KIND, THEY COULD NOT SERIOUSLY COMPETE FOR JOBS OR ENJOY A SUCCESSFUL PROFESSIONAL AND PERSONAL LIFE.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE CONCLUDING FORMULA — the whole chapter in one sentence:**
> **"THEY NEEDED, IN OTHER WORDS, SOMETIMES TO BE TREATED DIFFERENTLY IF THEY ARE TO ENJOY THE SAME RIGHTS AS MEN."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The test to apply, and the danger to guard against
⭐⭐⭐ **"As we deliberate on issues of equality and examine whether different treatment is warranted in a particular case, WE NEED CONTINUOUSLY TO ASK OURSELVES WHETHER DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT IS ESSENTIAL TO ENSURE THAT A SET OF PEOPLE CAN ENJOY THE SAME RIGHTS AS THE REST OF SOCIETY."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The warning:** *"**CAUTION MUST, HOWEVER, BE EXERCISED TO SEE THAT DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT (i) DOES NOT YIELD NEW STRUCTURES OF DOMINANCE AND OPPRESSION, OR (ii) BECOME A MEANS FOR SOME DOMINANT GROUPS TO REASSERT SPECIAL PRIVILEGES AND POWER IN SOCIETY.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The final word:** *"**DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT IS INTENDED AND JUSTIFIED ONLY AS A MEANS TO PROMOTING A JUST AND EGALITARIAN SOCIETY.**"*

## ⭐⭐ BOX: "Let's Think" — Is special and differential treatment justified?
*(An excellent applied exercise. Apply the test: **is the differential treatment ESSENTIAL to let this group enjoy the SAME rights as everyone else?**)*

| Situation | Verdict and reasoning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **"WORKING WOMEN SHOULD RECEIVE MATERNITY LEAVE"** | ✅ **JUSTIFIED — the paradigm case.** This is precisely what the women's movement discovered: *"without special considerations of this kind, they could not seriously compete for jobs."* The differential treatment is **essential to the equal right to work.** |
| ⭐⭐ **"A SCHOOL SHOULD SPEND MONEY TO BUY SPECIAL EQUIPMENT FOR TWO VISUALLY CHALLENGED STUDENTS"** | ✅ **JUSTIFIED** — parallel to the **ramps** example. Recall that *"computers can help blind people"*, and **Stephen Hawking**: *"it would seem unjust... if disabled people are denied necessary help to overcome the effects of their disability."* This is **enhancement, not infringement, of equality.** |
| ⭐⭐ **"GEETA PLAYS BRILLIANT BASKETBALL, SO THE SCHOOL SHOULD BUILD A BASKETBALL COURT FOR HER so that she can develop her skills further"** | ❌ **NOT JUSTIFIED — this is the trap in the list.** Geeta is **not disadvantaged**; she is **exceptionally advantaged.** Differential treatment is justified *"only as a means to promoting a just and egalitarian society"* — **not to reward existing talent.** ⚠️ Building a court **for one student** is exactly the risk of *"some dominant groups reasserting special privileges."* *(A court for **all** students who wish to play is a different matter entirely.)* |
| ⭐⭐ **"JEET'S PARENTS WANT HIM TO WEAR A TURBAN IN SCHOOL, AND IRFAN'S PARENTS WANT HIM TO PRAY ON FRIDAY AFTERNOON — so the school should not insist that Jeet wear a HELMET while playing cricket, and Irfan's teacher should not ask him to STAY BACK FOR EXTRA CLASSES on Friday"** | ⚠️ **The two halves must be separated.** ⭐ **Irfan's Friday prayer: JUSTIFIED** — accommodating a religious obligation costs nothing to others and is required for equal participation; the extra class can be rescheduled. ⭐ **Jeet's helmet: the hard case** — here the accommodation collides with **his own safety**, not with anyone's convenience. *(Note this is the **dress-code** problem from Ch. 2: who has the authority, is the imposition excessive, and what follows from accepting it? A designed solution — a helmet worn over a *patka*, as in professional cricket — dissolves the conflict, which is usually the right answer where a right and a safety rule appear to clash.)* |

## Key Words (Glossary)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Equality (as a political ideal)** | That **all human beings have an equal worth regardless of colour, gender, race or nationality**, and **deserve equal consideration and respect because of their common humanity** |
| **Shared humanity** | The notion underlying **universal human rights** and **"crimes against humanity"** |
| ⭐⭐ **The paradox of equality** | **"Almost everyone accepts the ideal of equality, yet almost everywhere we encounter inequality"** |
| ⭐⭐ **Natural inequalities** | Those arising from **different capabilities and talents people are born with**; generally assumed to be **unalterable** |
| ⭐⭐ **Socially-produced inequalities** | Those **created by society** — arising from **inequality of opportunity** or the **exploitation of some groups by others**; they **reflect the values of a society** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Choice vs circumstance of birth** | The distinction **modern theorists use instead** of natural/social: it is **inequality arising from the family or circumstance in which a person is born** that advocates of equality wish to **minimise and eliminate** |
| ⭐⭐ **Political equality** | **Equal citizenship** — the right to vote, freedom of expression, movement, association and belief. These are **legal rights**, and **considerable inequality can exist even where they are granted** |
| **"A level playing field"** | The demand for **equal opportunities**, arising because legal equality alone leaves social and economic disparities in place |
| ⭐⭐ **Social equality** | Requires **minimum conditions of life for all** — **adequate health care, the opportunity for good education, adequate nourishment and a minimum wage** — without which people **cannot compete on equal terms** |
| ⭐⭐ **Economic equality** | Exists where there are no **significant differences in wealth, property or income** between individuals or classes; measured by the **richest–poorest gap** or the **number below the poverty line** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Entrenched inequality** | Inequality that **remains relatively untouched over generations** — **more dangerous**, because it produces **resentment and violence** and the **power of the wealthy makes reform difficult** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Feminism** | **"A political doctrine of equal rights for women and men"** — held by **men and women** who believe gender inequalities are **neither natural nor necessary** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Patriarchy** | **"A social, economic and cultural system that values men more than women and gives men power over women"**, resting on the assumption that natural difference **justifies** unequal positions |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Sex vs gender** | **Sex** = the **biological** difference; **gender** = the **socially determined roles** men and women play. *"The biological fact that only women can become pregnant... does not require that only women should look after children after they are born."* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Double burden** | Women do **paid work in the public domain AND remain solely responsible for housework** — yet are **given little or no say in public decisions** |
| ⭐⭐ **Marxism (on equality)** | The **root cause of entrenched inequality is private ownership of important economic resources**, which makes owners wealthy **and gives them political power to influence state policies and laws** — a **threat to democratic government**; hence the demand for **public control over essential resources** |
| ⭐⭐ **Liberalism (on equality)** | **Competition in free and fair conditions** is the most just and efficient way to distribute rewards; **political, economic and social inequalities are NOT necessarily linked** and must be **tackled separately**; the problem is **not inequality as such but unjust and entrenched inequalities** |
| ⭐⭐ **Socialism** | Emerged **in response to the inequalities of the industrial capitalist economy**; its main concern is **how to minimise inequality and distribute resources justly**; **not entirely opposed to the market**, but favours **regulation, planning and control over key areas such as education and health care** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ ***Sapta Kranti*** | **Rammanohar Lohia's seven revolutions:** against inequality of **(1) man and woman, (2) skin colour, (3) caste, (4) colonial rule, (5) economic inequality** — plus **(6) civil liberties against unjust encroachments on private life, and (7) non-violence, renouncing weapons in favour of *satyagraha*** |
| ⭐⭐ **Formal equality** | **Identical treatment by law to all citizens without regard to caste, race, religion or gender** — **necessary but not sufficient** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Affirmative action** | **Positive measures to minimise and eliminate entrenched social inequalities**, designed to **correct the cumulative effect of past inequalities**; expected to be **temporary or time-bound**; forms include **preferential spending (scholarships, hostels), special consideration in admissions and jobs**, and in India **quotas/reserved seats** |
| ⭐⭐ **Reverse discrimination** | The critics' charge that reservation **arbitrarily denies other sections their right to equal treatment** and **reinforces caste and racial prejudices** |

## Quick Recap (16 points)
1. ⭐⭐ **Equality as a political ideal means that all human beings have EQUAL WORTH regardless of colour, gender, race or nationality, and deserve EQUAL CONSIDERATION AND RESPECT because of their COMMON HUMANITY** — the notion of **shared humanity** behind **universal human rights** and **"crimes against humanity."**
2. ⭐⭐ **It has been a rallying slogan:** the **French revolutionaries' "Liberty, Equality and Fraternity"** against **feudal aristocracy and monarchy** in the **eighteenth century**; **anti-colonial struggles in Asia and Africa** in the **twentieth**; and today **women and Dalits** who feel marginalised.
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **THE PARADOX: "almost everyone accepts the ideal of equality, yet almost everywhere we encounter inequality"** — **slums beside luxury housing; air-conditioned classrooms beside schools without drinking water or toilets; waste of food alongside starvation.** *"There are glaring differences between what the law promises and what we see around us."* **The most arresting global figure: the risk of dying from pregnancy-related causes is 1 in 18 in Nigeria but 1 in 8,700 in Canada.**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **The central distinction: "treating people with EQUAL RESPECT need not mean always treating them in an IDENTICAL WAY."** Society needs a **division of work and functions**, so **special rank for Prime Ministers or army generals** is acceptable — ⭐ **"provided their privileges are not misused"** — whereas a **slum child denied nutritious food or good education "through no fault of his/her own"** is unjust.
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **The formula: "the commitment to the ideal of equality does not imply the elimination of all forms of differences. It merely suggests that THE TREATMENT WE RECEIVE AND THE OPPORTUNITIES WE ENJOY MUST NOT BE PRE-DETERMINED BY BIRTH OR SOCIAL CIRCUMSTANCE."** Differences of **ambition and success** — some become good musicians, some famous scientists — do not undermine equality, **so long as people can "develop the best in themselves."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **What makes a society unjust: "it is NOT the lack of equality of status or wealth or privilege that is significant, BUT THE INEQUALITIES IN PEOPLE'S ACCESS TO SUCH BASIC GOODS AS EDUCATION, HEALTH CARE, SAFE HOUSING."**
7. ⭐⭐ **Natural inequalities** arise from **capabilities and talents people are born with**; **socially-produced inequalities** arise from **inequality of opportunity or exploitation** and **reflect a society's values** — e.g. valuing **intellectual over manual work**, or treating people differently by **race, colour, gender or caste.**
8. ⭐⭐⭐ **But the natural/social distinction is unreliable, for three reasons.** ⓵ **Long-standing social inequalities masquerade as natural:** women were called **"the weaker sex", "timid and of lesser intelligence"**, which **justified denying them equal rights**; black Africans were held by colonial masters to be **"of lesser intelligence, child-like, and better at manual work, sports and music"**, which **justified slavery** — ⭐ **"all these assessments are now questioned... seen as distinctions made by society as a result of the DIFFERENCES OF POWER between people and nations."** ⓶ **Some "natural" differences are no longer unalterable** — **computers for the blind, wheelchairs and artificial limbs, cosmetic surgery**; and **Stephen Hawking "can hardly move or speak but he has made major contributions to science."** ⓷ Hence the distinction cannot serve as a policy standard, and ⭐⭐⭐ **theorists now distinguish INEQUALITY ARISING FROM OUR CHOICES from INEQUALITIES OPERATING ON ACCOUNT OF THE FAMILY OR CIRCUMSTANCE IN WHICH A PERSON IS BORN — it is the LATTER that advocates of equality wish to eliminate.**
9. ⭐⭐⭐ **Three dimensions, all of which must be addressed. POLITICAL equality** = **equal citizenship** with the right to vote and freedoms of expression, movement, association and belief — ⭐ but these are **legal rights**, and *"considerable inequality can exist even in countries which grant equal rights"*; still, **"although political and legal equality by itself may not be sufficient... it is certainly an important component."** **SOCIAL equality** requires **minimum conditions of life — adequate health care, opportunity for good education, adequate nourishment, a minimum wage** — without which people **cannot compete on equal terms**; ⭐ **"where equality of opportunity does not exist, a HUGE POOL OF POTENTIAL TALENT TENDS TO BE WASTED IN SOCIETY."** ⭐ **In India a special problem comes not just from lack of facilities but from CUSTOMS** — unequal **inheritance rights**, **social prohibitions on activities**, and **discouragement from higher education** for women — so the **state must legislate against discrimination and harassment and provide incentives**, while **social groups and individuals must raise awareness and support those exercising their rights.** **ECONOMIC equality** exists where there are no **significant differences in wealth, property or income**; measured by the **richest–poorest gap** or **numbers below the poverty line.**
10. ⭐⭐⭐ **ENTRENCHED inequalities — "which remain relatively untouched over generations — are MORE DANGEROUS for a society"**, because **class divisions harden over generations**, **"can give rise to resentment and violence"**, and **"because of the power of the wealthy classes it might prove difficult to reform such a society."**
11. ⭐⭐⭐ **FEMINISM** is **"a political doctrine of equal rights for women and men"**, held by **men and women** who think gender inequalities are **neither natural nor necessary.** It traces them to **PATRIARCHY — "a social, economic and cultural system that values men more than women and gives men power over women"** — which justifies itself by claiming natural difference. ⭐⭐ **The decisive move is the SEX/GENDER distinction: "the biological fact that only women can become pregnant and bear children DOES NOT REQUIRE that only women should look after children after they are born."** ⭐ **Patriarchy's public/private division of labour is refuted by the DOUBLE BURDEN: most women worldwide work outside the home yet remain solely responsible for housework — and are still "given little or no say in decisions taken in the public domain."**
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **MARXISM:** the **root cause of entrenched inequality is private ownership of important economic resources (oil, land, forests) and other property** — which **not only makes owners wealthy but gives them POLITICAL POWER to influence state policies and laws, "a threat to democratic government."** Marxists and socialists hold that **economic inequality supports other social inequalities**, so we must **"go beyond providing equal opportunities and ensure PUBLIC CONTROL over essential resources and forms of property."**
13. ⭐⭐⭐ **LIBERALISM:** **competition in free and fair conditions is the most just and efficient way of distributing rewards**; states may intervene for **a minimum standard of living and equal opportunities**, but that **cannot by itself bring equality and justice**; **"as long as competition is open and free, inequalities are unlikely to become entrenched."** ⭐⭐ **Unlike socialists, liberals deny that political, economic and social inequalities are NECESSARILY LINKED** — each must be **tackled appropriately** — and **"the problem for liberals is not inequality as such, but UNJUST AND ENTRENCHED INEQUALITIES WHICH PREVENT INDIVIDUALS FROM DEVELOPING THEIR CAPABILITIES."**
14. ⭐⭐⭐ **SOCIALISM** arose **in response to the inequalities of the industrial capitalist economy**; its main concern is **minimising inequality and distributing resources justly**; it is **not entirely opposed to the market** but favours **regulation, planning and control over key areas such as education and health care.** ⭐⭐ **RAMMANOHAR LOHIA identified FIVE inequalities to be fought SIMULTANEOUSLY — man/woman, skin colour, caste, colonial rule, economic** — against the then-orthodox socialist view that **class inequality was the only one worth fighting** and the rest would **end automatically.** ⭐ **Lohia insisted each had INDEPENDENT ROOTS**, and added **two more revolutions — civil liberties against unjust encroachments on private life, and non-violence/renunciation of weapons in favour of *satyagraha*** — making the **SEVEN REVOLUTIONS, *SAPTA KRANTI*.**
15. ⭐⭐⭐ **Three steps to promote equality.** ⓵ **FORMAL EQUALITY** — ending legally sanctioned privilege: **the poor denied the vote, women barred from professions, the caste system confining "lower" castes to manual labour, high positions reserved to certain families.** ⭐ **India's Constitution "prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth" and "abolishes the practice of untouchability."** ⓶ **DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT** — because formal equality is **"necessary but not sufficient"**: **ramps for the disabled**, and **protection for women working night shifts in call centres** so their **equal right to work** is protected. ⭐⭐ **"These should not be seen as INFRINGEMENTS of equality but as ENHANCEMENT of equality."** ⓷ **AFFIRMATIVE ACTION** — **positive measures to correct "the CUMULATIVE EFFECT OF PAST INEQUALITIES"**, in forms from **scholarships and hostels** to **special consideration in admissions and jobs**; India chose **quotas/reserved seats.** ⭐ **Expected to be TEMPORARY or TIME-BOUND**, on the assumption that it will enable communities to **overcome disadvantage and then compete on equal terms.**
16. ⭐⭐⭐ **The affirmative action debate.** **For:** certain groups were **victims of social prejudice, exclusion and segregation**, and **"cannot be immediately expected to compete with others on equal terms."** **Against:** ⭐ critics **invoke equality itself** — quotas **"arbitrarily deny other sections their right to equal treatment"**, are **"a form of REVERSE DISCRIMINATION"**, and **"when we make distinctions on the basis of caste or colour we are likely to REINFORCE caste and racial prejudices."** ⭐⭐ **The resolution: distinguish EQUALITY AS A GUIDING PRINCIPLE OF STATE POLICY from EQUAL RIGHTS OF INDIVIDUALS** — individuals have a right to equal consideration, **"BUT COMPETITION SHOULD BE FAIR"**, and ⭐⭐⭐ **"the needs and circumstances of a FIRST GENERATION LEARNER whose parents and ancestors were illiterate are very different from those who are born into educated families."** ⭐ **India "has done far less for its deprived population than what is their due"** — poor rural and slum children often **cannot attend school**, their schools **cannot match elite facilities**, entry inequalities **hamper qualifications and jobs**, and they **lack means for coaching** while **professional course fees are prohibitive.** ⭐⭐ **"What theorists contest is NOT the goal of equal opportunity but the POLICIES the state should pursue"** — quotas or early-age skill development? how to define the deprived? **economic criterion or caste?** — and **any policy "would have to be justified in terms of their success in making the society more egalitarian and fair to all."** ⭐⭐⭐ **Finally, distinguish TREATING EVERYONE IDENTICALLY from TREATING EVERYONE AS EQUAL**; differential treatment **"requires justification and careful reflection"**, and **liberals are wary because "differential treatment for different communities was part and parcel of the CASTE SYSTEM and practices like APARTHEID."** ⭐⭐ **The women's movement proves the point historically:** having won **the vote, degrees and the right to work** in the nineteenth century, women found they needed **maternity leave and crèches** — **"they needed, in other words, SOMETIMES TO BE TREATED DIFFERENTLY IF THEY ARE TO ENJOY THE SAME RIGHTS AS MEN."** ⭐ **The test: is differential treatment ESSENTIAL for a set of people to enjoy the same rights as the rest? And the caution: it must not "yield new structures of dominance and oppression" or "become a means for some dominant groups to reassert special privileges" — it is "justified ONLY as a means to promoting a just and egalitarian society."**

### NCERT Exercises (with answers/pointers)
1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Some argue inequality is natural; others that equality is natural and the inequalities we notice are created by society. Which view do you support?**
   → *Support the **second** view, but with the chapter's sophistication rather than a flat assertion.*
   - ⭐ **Start from the moral premise:** *"all human beings have an equal worth"* and *"deserve equal consideration and respect because of their common humanity"* — a premise **"implicit in all faiths and religions."**
   - ⭐⭐ **The historical evidence is decisive:** every inequality once defended as "natural" has collapsed under examination. Women were called **"the weaker sex... of lesser intelligence"**, which **justified denying them equal rights**; black Africans were held **"of lesser intelligence, child-like, and better at manual work"**, which **justified slavery.** ⭐⭐⭐ **"All these assessments are now questioned. They are now seen as distinctions made by society as a result of the DIFFERENCES OF POWER between people and nations, rather than based on their inborn characteristics."**
   - ⭐ **Even genuinely natural differences are increasingly alterable:** **computers for the blind, wheelchairs, artificial limbs** — and **Stephen Hawking** *"can hardly move or speak but he has made major contributions to science."*
   - ⭐⭐ **The concession to make (this is what earns marks):** natural differences of **talent** do exist, and *"not all may be equally successful"* — some become good musicians, others famous scientists. **That is not the point.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The right formulation is the chapter's replacement distinction: what matters is not natural vs social but INEQUALITY ARISING FROM OUR CHOICES versus INEQUALITY ARISING FROM THE FAMILY OR CIRCUMSTANCE IN WHICH A PERSON IS BORN.** The second is what equality opposes.
   - ⭐ **Clinch with the data:** rural households at **35% tap water against urban 71%**, and **47 graduates per thousand among Scheduled Castes against 315 among Hindu upper castes.** *"Could these differences have taken place just by chance?"* No — they track **birth**, not talent.
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **There is a view that absolute economic equality is neither possible nor desirable; the most a society can do is reduce the gaps between the richest and poorest. Do you agree?**
   → ***Largely agree — but with two important qualifications.***
   - ⭐ **On "not possible":** the chapter concedes it — *"**absolute equality of wealth or income has probably never existed in a society.**"*
   - ⭐ **On "not desirable":** the chapter's own logic supports this. Equality *"does not imply the elimination of all forms of differences"*; people **"pursue different ambitions and goals and not all may be equally successful"**, and **liberals** hold that **competition rewards "talents and efforts"** and is **"the most just and efficient way of distributing rewards."** Enforcing identical incomes would deny **choice** — and it is only **circumstance of birth**, not choice, that equality targets.
   - ⚠️ **First qualification — reducing the gap is NOT enough on its own.** ⭐⭐ **"It is NOT the lack of equality of status or wealth or privilege that is significant, but THE INEQUALITIES IN PEOPLE'S ACCESS TO SUCH BASIC GOODS AS EDUCATION, HEALTH CARE, SAFE HOUSING."** So the priority is **guaranteeing minimum conditions of life — adequate health care, good education, adequate nourishment, a minimum wage** — not merely narrowing a ratio.
   - ⚠️ **Second qualification — the gap itself becomes dangerous when it ENTRENCHES.** ⭐⭐ *"Inequalities which remain relatively untouched over generations are more dangerous... such class differences can give rise to resentment and violence"*, and **"because of the power of the wealthy classes it might prove difficult to reform such a society."* And **Marx's** warning is that **wealth converts into political power that can "influence state policies and laws"** — *"a threat to democratic government."* **So even a society that tolerates inequality must prevent it from hardening across generations.**
3. ⭐⭐ **Match the following:**
   | Concept | Instance |
   |---|---|
   | **(a) Affirmative action** | ⭐ **(ii) Banks offer higher rate of interest to senior citizens** *(differential treatment to help a disadvantaged group — the "separate counters for the old and disabled" logic)* |
   | **(b) Equality of opportunity** | ⭐ **(iii) Every child should get free education** *(providing the basic good that makes fair competition possible)* |
   | **(c) Equal rights** | ⭐ **(i) Every adult citizen has a right to vote** *(political equality / equal citizenship)* |
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **A report says small and marginal farmers cannot get good prices, and recommends government intervention to ensure a better price BUT ONLY for small and marginal farmers. Is this consistent with the principle of equality?**
   → ⭐⭐ **YES — it is consistent, and indeed an instance of "equality through differential treatment."**
   *The reasoning to give:* **formal equality is "necessary but not sufficient."** Small and marginal farmers face a **structural disadvantage in the market** — small volumes, no storage, no holding power, dependence on intermediaries — which is a **circumstance, not a choice.** Treating all farmers identically in such a market **produces unequal outcomes**, exactly as identical steps produce unequal access for the disabled. ⭐⭐ **So this is precisely the case of which the chapter says: "These should not be seen as INFRINGEMENTS of equality but as ENHANCEMENT of equality."** It parallels the **ramps** and **call-centre transport** examples, and satisfies the test — **is the differential treatment essential so that this group can enjoy the same right (to a fair price) as the rest?**
   ⚠️ **Two cautions to add for a complete answer:** the benefit must be **targeted by genuine deprivation, not captured by the powerful** — *"caution must be exercised to see that differential treatment does not... become a means for some dominant groups to reassert special privileges"*; and it should ideally be **transitional**, alongside measures that build lasting capacity (storage, credit, collective marketing), since affirmative measures are **"expected to be a temporary or time-bound measure."**
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **Which of the following violate the principles of equality, and why?**
   | Case | Verdict |
   |---|---|
   | **(a)** *Every child in class will read the text of the play by turn* | ✅ **NO VIOLATION** — **identical treatment where identical treatment is appropriate**; everyone gets an equal turn, and no one is advantaged by birth or circumstance. |
   | ⭐⭐⭐ **(b)** *The Government of Canada encouraged WHITE EUROPEANS to migrate to Canada from the end of the Second World War till 1960* | ❌ **CLEAR VIOLATION.** A distinction drawn on **race and colour** — precisely the ground the chapter calls **"unacceptable"**, violating **"our intuitive understanding of equality."** And note it fails the deeper test too: it **privileged an already advantaged group**, so it cannot be defended as affirmative action correcting **"the cumulative effect of past inequalities."** |
   | ⭐⭐ **(c)** *There is a separate railway reservation counter for senior citizens* | ✅ **NO VIOLATION — an ENHANCEMENT of equality.** Exactly the chapter's own example of **"separate counters for the old and disabled"** being **"justified"**; the ramp logic. It enables senior citizens to exercise the **same right** to book a ticket. |
   | ⭐⭐ **(d)** *Access to some forest areas is reserved for certain tribal communities* | ✅ **NO VIOLATION — justified differential treatment.** Forests are the **basis of Adivasi livelihood, culture and community life**; the reservation protects a group that has suffered **historic exclusion and displacement**, and is **essential for them to enjoy the same rights (to livelihood and cultural life) as others.** *(Compare *Indian Constitution at Work*: **Article 371A restricts immigration to protect local identity**, and **PESA protects traditional customs of managing forests.**)* |
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **Which arguments for women's right to vote are consistent with the idea of equality?**
   | Argument | Consistent? |
   |---|---|
   | **(a)** *"Women are our MOTHERS. We shall not disrespect our mothers by denying them the right to vote."* | ❌ **NOT consistent.** It grounds the right in a **relationship and a social role** ("our mothers"), not in the woman's **own equal worth as a human being.** ⭐ It is a **patriarchal** argument even while reaching a progressive conclusion — the right is granted *by* men out of **respect**, and what is granted as respect can be withdrawn. It also implies that women who are **not mothers** have a weaker claim. |
   | ⭐⭐⭐ **(b)** *"Decisions of the government affect women as well as men, therefore they also should have a say in choosing the rulers."* | ✅ **FULLY consistent — the best of the four.** It rests on **equal citizenship** and the principle that those affected by decisions must have **a say in the affairs of the state.** It treats women as **citizens**, not as anyone's relatives. |
   | **(c)** *"Not granting women the right to vote will cause DISHARMONY IN THE FAMILY."* | ❌ **NOT consistent.** A purely **prudential/instrumental** argument about **social peace**, not about **rights** at all. On this reasoning, if denial produced **no** disharmony, denial would be acceptable. It also locates women within the **"private" and "domestic"** sphere — the very **public/private division** feminists reject. |
   | ⭐⭐ **(d)** *"Women constitute HALF OF HUMANITY. You cannot subjugate them for long by denying them the right to vote."* | ⚠️ **Partly consistent — mixed.** The phrase **"half of humanity"** appeals to **common humanity**, which is sound. ⭐ But **"you cannot subjugate them FOR LONG"** is an argument from **inevitability and prudence**, not from **justice** — it says denial will *fail*, not that it is *wrong*. **Consistent in its premise, weak in its reasoning.** |
   ⭐ **Best answer: (b) is fully consistent; (d) is partly so; (a) and (c) are not — because they justify the right by reference to women's ROLES or to social CONVENIENCE, rather than to their equal worth as human beings and citizens.**
