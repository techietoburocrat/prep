# Chapter 7: Nationalism

## 🎯 Chapter Outcome — what you should walk away with

> **The one thing this chapter exists to teach:** a nation is an **imagined community** — **"a nation exists when its members believe that they belong together"** — which is precisely why it must be imagined in **political**, not cultural, terms.

**After reading it, here is what you now understand:**

1. **That nationalism cuts both ways** — it has "inspired intense loyalties as well as deep hatreds; united as well as divided; liberated as well as caused conflict, bitterness and wars."
2. **The three phases** — unification in 19th-century Europe (Germany, Italy); the break-up of the Austro-Hungarian, Russian and then the colonial empires; and separatism since 1960 (Quebecois, Basques, Kurds, Tamils in Sri Lanka). And the paradox: the same creed makes Arab nationalism seek **union** while Basques and Kurds seek **division**.
3. **Why a nation is neither a family nor a tribe** — we never come face to face with most fellow nationals, and need share no ties of descent — and why **no common characteristic** defines one (Canada has two languages, India many; no common religion, race or descent).
4. **The five assumptions** — shared beliefs, shared history (Nehru's "tremendous impress of oneness"), territory as homeland (with the warning that more than one people may claim the same land — **a major cause of conflict**), shared political ideals, and a common political identity.
5. **The two reasons against a cultural definition of the nation** — all major religions are **internally diverse** and have evolved through dialogue, so building identity on one creates "a highly authoritative and oppressive society"; and most societies are culturally diverse, so imposing one religious or linguistic identity **would necessarily exclude some groups**. Hence loyalty should be to values **enshrined in the constitution**.
6. **What self-determination cost** — "one culture, one state" applied at the Treaty of Versailles produced mass migration, millions displaced and expelled, and communal violence: "humanity paid a heavy price." And it **still failed**, because most states had more than one ethnic community.
7. **The paradox the chapter presses** — nation-states that themselves achieved independence through struggle now act against minorities inside their own territories making the very same claim.
8. **The solution** — "the solution does **not** lie in creating new states but in making **existing states more democratic and equal**", with co-existence as partners and equal citizens. New states would be too small to be viable and would merely **multiply the problems of minorities**.
9. **The reinterpretation** — the right to self-determination now means granting certain **democratic rights for a nationality within a state**; plus group rights, constitutional protection of minority language, culture and religion, and sometimes group representation.
10. **Multiple identities** — each person may be proud of many, and so long as each can be freely expressed, no single one needs to be pressed against the state. **"It would be dangerous if intolerant and homogenising forms of identity and nationalism are allowed to develop."**
11. **Tagore's position** — "patriotism cannot be our final spiritual shelter; my refuge is humanity" — and his key distinction between opposing western **imperialism** and rejecting western **civilisation**.

**Self-check — answer these three without looking:**
1. Complete the sentence: "A nation exists when…"
2. Why does the chapter reject a cultural definition of the nation? Give both reasons.
3. What does the right to self-determination now mean, and why was its original meaning abandoned?

**Where this leads:** this chapter ends by asking how a democratic state can ensure equality for different cultures — and **Ch 8 opens by answering it**. Never revise one without the other.

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 1 — INTRODUCING NATIONALISM

### ⭐⭐ What people commonly understand by it
⭐ **"If we were to take a quick poll... we are likely to get responses which talk about PATRIOTISM, NATIONAL FLAGS, SACRIFICING FOR THE COUNTRY, and the like. ⭐⭐ THE REPUBLIC DAY PARADE IN DELHI is a striking symbol of Indian nationalism, and it brings out the sense of POWER, STRENGTH, AS WELL AS DIVERSITY which many associate with the Indian nation."**

⭐⭐ **But the honest difficulty:** *"**If we try to go deeper, we will find that IT IS DIFFICULT TO ARRIVE AT A PRECISE AND WIDELY ACCEPTED DEFINITION of the term nationalism. This need not mean that we should abandon the effort. ⭐ NATIONALISM NEEDS TO BE STUDIED BECAUSE IT PLAYS SUCH AN IMPORTANT ROLE IN WORLD AFFAIRS.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE DOUBLE-EDGED CHARACTER OF NATIONALISM (the passage to memorise — it answers Exercise Q3)
> ⭐⭐ **"During the last TWO CENTURIES OR MORE, nationalism has emerged as ONE OF THE MOST COMPELLING OF POLITICAL CREEDS WHICH HAS HELPED TO SHAPE HISTORY."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"IT HAS INSPIRED INTENSE LOYALTIES AS WELL AS DEEP HATREDS.**
> **IT HAS UNITED PEOPLE AS WELL AS DIVIDED THEM.**
> **[IT HAS] HELPED TO LIBERATE THEM FROM OPPRESSIVE RULE AS WELL AS BEEN THE CAUSE OF CONFLICT AND BITTERNESS AND WARS.**
> **IT HAS BEEN A FACTOR IN THE BREAK UP OF EMPIRES AND STATES."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"NATIONALIST STRUGGLES HAVE CONTRIBUTED TO THE DRAWING AND REDRAWING OF THE BOUNDARIES OF STATES AND EMPIRES. At present a large part of the world is divided into different nation-states, ALTHOUGH THE PROCESS OF RE-ORDERING OF STATE BOUNDARIES HAS NOT COME TO AN END, AND SEPARATIST STRUGGLES WITHIN EXISTING STATES ARE COMMON."**

**⭐⭐ Learn the four antitheses as pairs: loyalty/hatred, unity/division, liberation/war, and the making/breaking of states. Every one of them is illustrated in the phases below.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 2 — THE THREE PHASES OF NATIONALISM (learn all three)

⭐ **"NATIONALISM HAS PASSED THROUGH MANY PHASES."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ Phase 1 — UNIFICATION (nineteenth-century Europe)
⭐⭐ **"For instance, in the NINETEENTH CENTURY EUROPE, IT LED TO THE UNIFICATION OF A NUMBER OF SMALL KINGDOMS INTO LARGER NATION-STATES."**

⭐⭐⭐ **"THE PRESENT DAY GERMAN AND ITALIAN STATES WERE FORMED THROUGH SUCH A PROCESS OF UNIFICATION AND CONSOLIDATION. ⭐ A LARGE NUMBER OF NEW STATES WERE ALSO FOUNDED IN LATIN AMERICA."**

⭐⭐ **What consolidation involved — a subtle and important point:** *"**Along with the consolidation of state boundaries, LOCAL DIALECTS AND LOCAL LOYALTIES WERE ALSO GRADUALLY CONSOLIDATED INTO STATE LOYALTIES AND COMMON LANGUAGES. ⭐⭐ THE PEOPLE OF THE NEW STATES ACQUIRED A NEW POLITICAL IDENTITY WHICH WAS BASED ON MEMBERSHIP OF THE NATION-STATE.**"*

⭐ **"WE HAVE SEEN A SIMILAR PROCESS OF CONSOLIDATION TAKING PLACE IN OUR OWN COUNTRY IN THE LAST CENTURY OR MORE."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ Phase 2 — BREAK-UP of empires
⭐⭐ **"BUT NATIONALISM ALSO ACCOMPANIED AND CONTRIBUTED TO THE BREAK UP OF LARGE EMPIRES:"**
| Empires | Where and when |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The AUSTRO-HUNGARIAN and RUSSIAN empires** | **"In the EARLY TWENTIETH CENTURY in EUROPE"** |
| ⭐⭐ **The BRITISH, FRENCH, DUTCH and PORTUGUESE empires** | **"In ASIA AND AFRICA"** |

⭐⭐⭐ **India's place in this phase:** *"**THE STRUGGLE FOR FREEDOM FROM COLONIAL RULE BY INDIA AND OTHER FORMER COLONIES WERE NATIONALIST STRUGGLES, INSPIRED BY THE DESIRE TO ESTABLISH NATION-STATES WHICH WOULD BE INDEPENDENT OF FOREIGN CONTROL.**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ Phase 3 — SEPARATIST movements within existing states (since 1960)
⭐⭐ **"The process of redrawing state boundaries CONTINUES TO TAKE PLACE. ⭐ SINCE 1960, EVEN APPARENTLY STABLE NATION-STATES HAVE BEEN CONFRONTED BY NATIONALIST DEMANDS PUT FORWARD BY GROUPS OR REGIONS — AND THESE MAY INCLUDE DEMANDS FOR SEPARATE STATEHOOD."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The four separatist movements named (memorise all four with their countries):**
| Movement | Country |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The QUEBECOIS** | **CANADA** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The BASQUES** | **NORTHERN SPAIN** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The KURDS** | **TURKEY AND IRAQ** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The TAMILS** | **SRI LANKA** |

⭐⭐ **"The language of nationalism IS ALSO USED BY SOME GROUPS IN INDIA."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The paradox of nationalism's two directions
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"ARAB NATIONALISM TODAY MAY HOPE TO UNITE ARAB COUNTRIES IN A PAN ARAB UNION, BUT SEPARATIST MOVEMENTS LIKE THE BASQUES OR KURDS STRUGGLE TO DIVIDE EXISTING STATES."**
>
> **⭐⭐ This single sentence captures the whole ambivalence: the SAME political creed can be used to MERGE states (pan-Arabism) and to SPLIT them (Basque, Kurdish). That is why nationalism cannot be judged good or bad in the abstract.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The questions the chapter will explore
⭐ **"We may all agree that nationalism is a POWERFUL FORCE in the world even today. But it is MORE DIFFICULT TO ARRIVE AT AGREEMENT REGARDING THE DEFINITION of terms like nation or nationalism:"**
1. **"WHAT IS A NATION?"**
2. **"WHY DO PEOPLE FORM NATIONS, AND TO WHAT DO NATIONS ASPIRE?"**
3. ⭐ **"WHY ARE PEOPLE READY TO SACRIFICE AND EVEN DIE FOR THEIR NATION?"**
4. ⭐ **"WHY, AND IN WHAT WAY, ARE CLAIMS TO NATIONHOOD LINKED TO CLAIMS TO STATEHOOD?"**
5. ⭐⭐ **"DO NATIONS HAVE A RIGHT TO STATEHOOD OR NATIONAL SELF-DETERMINATION? OR CAN THE CLAIMS OF NATIONALISM BE MET WITHOUT CONCEDING SEPARATE STATEHOOD?"**

> ⭐⭐ **The chapter's own margin exchange on globalisation, worth remembering:**
> — *"In this age of globalisation, the world is shrinking. We are living in a global village. **NATIONS ARE IRRELEVANT.**"*
> — ⭐ *"**That's not the case. NATIONALISM IS STILL RELEVANT.** You can see this when the **Indian team goes out to play cricket.** Or when you discover that **Indians living abroad still watch Bollywood films.**"*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 3 — WHAT IS A NATION? How it differs from other groups

### ⭐⭐⭐ The comparison with family and kinship groups
⭐⭐ **"A nation is NOT ANY CASUAL COLLECTION OF PEOPLE. At the same time it is also DIFFERENT FROM OTHER GROUPS OR COMMUNITIES found in human society:"**

| Group | Its distinguishing basis |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The FAMILY** | **"Based on FACE-TO-FACE RELATIONSHIPS, with each member having DIRECT PERSONAL KNOWLEDGE of the identity and character of others"** |
| ⭐ **TRIBES, CLANS and other KINSHIP groups** | **"Ties of MARRIAGE AND DESCENT link members to each other, so that EVEN IF WE DO NOT PERSONALLY KNOW ALL THE MEMBERS, WE CAN, IF NEED BE, TRACE THE LINKS THAT BIND THEM TO US"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The NATION** | **"BUT AS A MEMBER OF A NATION WE MAY NEVER COME FACE TO FACE WITH MOST OF OUR FELLOW NATIONALS, NOR NEED WE SHARE TIES OF DESCENT WITH THEM. ⭐⭐ YET NATIONS EXIST, ARE LIVED IN AND VALUED BY THEIR MEMBERS."** |

**⭐⭐ The point of the comparison: a nation cannot be explained by either PERSONAL ACQUAINTANCE (family) or TRACEABLE DESCENT (kinship). Something else must be doing the work — and that is what the next section identifies.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The refutation of the "common characteristics" view
⭐⭐ **"It is COMMONLY BELIEVED that nations are constituted by a group who share certain features such as DESCENT, OR LANGUAGE, OR RELIGION OR ETHNICITY. ⭐⭐⭐ BUT THERE IS IN FACT NO COMMON SET OF CHARACTERISTICS WHICH IS PRESENT IN ALL NATIONS."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The counter-examples (this is the answer to Exercise Q4):**
| Supposed common feature | Refutation |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Common LANGUAGE** | **"MANY NATIONS DO NOT HAVE A COMMON LANGUAGE. ⭐ CANADA IS AN EXAMPLE HERE. Canada includes ENGLISH SPEAKING AS WELL AS FRENCH SPEAKING PEOPLES. ⭐⭐ INDIA ALSO HAS A LARGE NUMBER OF LANGUAGES which are spoken in different regions and by different communities."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Common RELIGION** | **"NOR DO MANY NATIONS HAVE A COMMON RELIGION TO UNITE THEM."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Common RACE or DESCENT** | **"The same could be said of other characteristics such as RACE OR DESCENT."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE ANSWER — the nation as an "IMAGINED COMMUNITY"
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"What then constitutes a nation? A NATION IS TO A GREAT EXTENT AN 'IMAGINED' COMMUNITY, HELD TOGETHER BY THE COLLECTIVE BELIEFS, ASPIRATIONS AND IMAGINATIONS OF ITS MEMBERS. ⭐⭐ IT IS BASED ON CERTAIN ASSUMPTIONS WHICH PEOPLE MAKE ABOUT THE COLLECTIVE WHOLE WITH WHICH THEY IDENTIFY."**

**⭐⭐⭐ "Imagined" does NOT mean "imaginary" or false. It means the nation exists because and insofar as its members conceive of themselves as belonging together — which is precisely why it can include people you will never meet and to whom you are not related.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 4 — THE FIVE ASSUMPTIONS THAT CONSTITUTE A NATION

## ⭐⭐⭐ 1. SHARED BELIEFS
⭐⭐⭐ **"First, A NATION IS CONSTITUTED BY BELIEF. ⭐ NATIONS ARE NOT LIKE MOUNTAINS, RIVERS OR BUILDINGS WHICH WE CAN SEE AND FEEL. THEY ARE NOT THINGS WHICH EXIST INDEPENDENT OF THE BELIEFS THAT PEOPLE HAVE ABOUT THEM."**

⭐⭐ **"To speak of a people as a nation is NOT to make a comment about their PHYSICAL CHARACTERISTICS OR BEHAVIOUR. ⭐⭐ Rather, it is to refer to (i) THE COLLECTIVE IDENTITY and (ii) VISION FOR THE FUTURE of a group WHICH ASPIRES TO HAVE AN INDEPENDENT POLITICAL EXISTENCE."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The TEAM analogy (a very good one — learn it)
> **"To this extent, NATIONS CAN BE COMPARED WITH A TEAM. When we speak of a team, we mean a set of people who WORK OR PLAY TOGETHER and, ⭐⭐ MORE IMPORTANTLY, CONCEIVE OF THEMSELVES AS A COLLECTIVE GROUP. ⭐⭐⭐ IF THEY DID NOT THINK OF THEMSELVES IN THIS WAY, THEY WOULD CEASE TO BE A TEAM AND BE SIMPLY DIFFERENT INDIVIDUALS PLAYING A GAME OR UNDERTAKING A TASK."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE DEFINING SENTENCE: "A NATION EXISTS WHEN ITS MEMBERS BELIEVE THAT THEY BELONG TOGETHER."**

> ⭐⭐ **The chapter's margin exchange, which is worth using in an answer on what patriotism requires:**
> — *"**Why don't you cheer for our team? Don't you have any nationalist spirit?**"*
> — ⭐ *"**I am as much a nationalist as anyone else. I CAST MY VOTE; I PAY MY TAXES and I RESPECT THE LAWS of our country. I am also PROUD TO BELONG to this country.**"*
>
> **⭐⭐ The reply relocates nationalism from EMOTIONAL DISPLAY to CIVIC OBLIGATION — which is exactly the chapter's own position in the section on shared political ideals: "recognition of this framework of obligations is the strongest test of loyalty to the nation."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ 2. HISTORY
⭐⭐ **"Second, PEOPLE WHO SEE THEMSELVES AS A NATION ALSO EMBODY A SENSE OF CONTINUING HISTORICAL IDENTITY. That is, ⭐ NATIONS PERCEIVE THEMSELVES AS STRETCHING BACK INTO THE PAST AS WELL AS REACHING INTO THE FUTURE."**

⭐⭐⭐ **How they construct it:** *"**THEY ARTICULATE FOR THEMSELVES A SENSE OF THEIR OWN HISTORY BY DRAWING ON (i) COLLECTIVE MEMORIES, (ii) LEGENDS, (iii) HISTORICAL RECORDS, TO OUTLINE THE CONTINUING IDENTITY OF THE NATION.**"*

⭐⭐ **The Indian case:** *"**Thus NATIONALISTS IN INDIA INVOKED ITS ANCIENT CIVILISATION AND CULTURAL HERITAGE AND OTHER ACHIEVEMENTS to claim that INDIA HAS HAD A LONG AND CONTINUING HISTORY AS A CIVILISATION, AND THAT THIS CIVILISATIONAL CONTINUITY AND UNITY IS THE BASIS OF THE INDIAN NATION.**"*

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ Nehru, *The Discovery of India* — the set-piece quotation
> **"Though OUTWARDLY THERE WAS DIVERSITY AND INFINITE VARIETY AMONG THE PEOPLE, EVERYWHERE THERE WAS THAT TREMENDOUS IMPRESS OF ONENESS, WHICH HELD ALL OF US TOGETHER IN AGES PAST, WHATEVER POLITICAL FATE OR MISFORTUNE HAD BEFALLEN US."**
> — **Jawaharlal Nehru, *The Discovery of India***
>
> **⭐⭐ Note the structure of the claim: OUTWARD diversity, INWARD oneness — and note the phrase "whatever political fate or misfortune had befallen us", which asserts continuity *despite* conquest and division. This is exactly what the chapter means by "articulating a sense of one's own history."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ 3. TERRITORY
⭐⭐ **"Third, NATIONS IDENTIFY WITH A PARTICULAR TERRITORY. ⭐⭐ SHARING A COMMON PAST AND LIVING TOGETHER ON A PARTICULAR TERRITORY OVER A LONG PERIOD OF TIME GIVES PEOPLE A SENSE OF THEIR COLLECTIVE IDENTITY. IT HELPS THEM TO IMAGINE THEMSELVES AS ONE PEOPLE."**

⭐⭐ **"It is therefore not surprising that PEOPLE WHO SEE THEMSELVES AS A NATION SPEAK OF A HOMELAND. THE TERRITORY THEY OCCUPIED AND THE LAND ON WHICH THEY HAVE LIVED HAS A SPECIAL SIGNIFICANCE FOR THEM, AND THEY CLAIM IT AS THEIR OWN."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The three ways homelands are characterised:** *"Nations however characterise the homeland in different ways — for instance as **MOTHERLAND, or FATHERLAND, or HOLY LAND.**"*

⭐⭐ **Two examples:**
| Nation | Its territorial claim |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The JEWISH people** | **"In spite of being DISPERSED AND SCATTERED IN DIFFERENT PARTS OF THE WORLD, [they] always claimed that their ORIGINAL HOMELAND WAS IN PALESTINE — the 'PROMISED LAND'."** |
| ⭐⭐ **The INDIAN nation** | **"Identifies with the RIVERS, MOUNTAINS AND REGIONS OF THE INDIAN SUBCONTINENT."** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE DANGER IN THIS ASSUMPTION:** **"HOWEVER, SINCE MORE THAN ONE SET OF PEOPLE MAY LAY CLAIM TO THE SAME TERRITORY, THE ASPIRATION FOR A HOMELAND HAS BEEN A MAJOR CAUSE OF CONFLICT IN THE WORLD."**
>
> **⭐⭐ This is the one assumption that is inherently conflict-generating. Beliefs, history and ideals can be shared without limit; TERRITORY cannot. Note that the Jewish/Palestinian example is itself the standing illustration.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ 4. SHARED POLITICAL IDEALS (the most important of the five)
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"Fourth, WHILE TERRITORY AND SHARED HISTORICAL IDENTITY PLAY AN IMPORTANT ROLE IN CREATING A SENSE OF ONENESS, ⭐⭐⭐ IT IS A SHARED VISION OF THE FUTURE AND THE COLLECTIVE ASPIRATION TO HAVE AN INDEPENDENT POLITICAL EXISTENCE THAT DISTINGUISHES GROUPS FROM NATIONS."**

⭐⭐ **What members of a nation share:** *"**MEMBERS OF A NATION SHARE A VISION OF THE KIND OF STATE THEY WANT TO BUILD. ⭐⭐ THEY AFFIRM, AMONG OTHER THINGS, A SET OF VALUES AND PRINCIPLES SUCH AS DEMOCRACY, SECULARISM AND LIBERALISM.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **Why these ideals matter:** *"**THESE IDEALS REPRESENT THE TERMS UNDER WHICH THEY COME TOGETHER AND ARE WILLING TO LIVE TOGETHER. IT REPRESENTS, IN OTHER WORDS, THEIR POLITICAL IDENTITY AS A NATION.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The democratic claim — and the test of loyalty
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"IN A DEMOCRACY, IT IS SHARED COMMITMENT TO A SET OF POLITICAL VALUES AND IDEALS THAT IS THE MOST DESIRABLE BASIS OF A POLITICAL COMMUNITY OR A NATION-STATE."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"Within it, MEMBERS OF A POLITICAL COMMUNITY ARE BOUND BY A SET OF OBLIGATIONS. ⭐ THESE OBLIGATIONS ARISE FROM THE RECOGNITION OF THE RIGHTS OF EACH OTHER AS CITIZENS. ⭐⭐ A NATION IS STRENGTHENED WHEN ITS PEOPLE ACKNOWLEDGE AND ACCEPT THEIR OBLIGATIONS TO THEIR FELLOW MEMBERS."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"WE MIGHT EVEN SAY THAT RECOGNITION OF THIS FRAMEWORK OF OBLIGATIONS IS THE STRONGEST TEST OF LOYALTY TO THE NATION."**

**⭐⭐⭐ This is one of the most quotable propositions in the whole book: the test of patriotism is not flags or slogans but whether you recognise your fellow citizens' rights and your obligations to them.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ 5. COMMON POLITICAL IDENTITY (vs shared cultural identity)
⭐⭐ **The rival view stated fairly:** *"**MANY PEOPLE BELIEVE THAT A SHARED POLITICAL VISION about the state and society we wish to create IS NOT ENOUGH TO BIND INDIVIDUALS TOGETHER AS A NATION. THEY SEEK INSTEAD A SHARED CULTURAL IDENTITY — SUCH AS A COMMON LANGUAGE, OR COMMON DESCENT.**"*

⭐⭐ **The genuine attraction of the cultural view — conceded honestly:** *"**There is no doubt that (i) SPEAKING THE SAME LANGUAGE MAKES IT EASIER FOR US TO COMMUNICATE WITH EACH OTHER, and (ii) SHARING THE SAME RELIGION GIVES US A SET OF COMMON BELIEFS AND SOCIAL PRACTICES. (iii) OBSERVING THE SAME FESTIVALS, SEEKING THE SAME HOLIDAYS, AND HOLDING THE SAME SYMBOLS VALUABLE CAN BRING PEOPLE TOGETHER...**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **"...BUT IT CAN ALSO POSE A THREAT TO THE VALUES THAT WE CHERISH IN A DEMOCRACY."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE TWO REASONS why cultural identity is a dangerous basis for nationhood (crucial — learn both)

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Reason 1 — All major religions are INTERNALLY DIVERSE
> **"One, ALL MAJOR RELIGIONS IN THE WORLD ARE INTERNALLY DIVERSE. ⭐ THEY HAVE SURVIVED AND EVOLVED THROUGH A DIALOGUE WITHIN THE COMMUNITY. As a result, THERE EXISTS WITHIN EACH RELIGION A NUMBER OF SECTS WHO DIFFER SIGNIFICANTLY IN THEIR INTERPRETATION OF THE RELIGIOUS TEXTS AND NORMS."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"IF WE IGNORE THESE DIFFERENCES AND FORGE AN IDENTITY ON THE BASIS OF A COMMON RELIGION, WE ARE LIKELY TO CREATE A HIGHLY AUTHORITATIVE AND OPPRESSIVE SOCIETY."**
>
> **⭐⭐ The logic is subtle and worth grasping: to make a religion the basis of a nation, you must first decide WHICH version of it counts — and that means suppressing the internal diversity through which the religion has actually survived. The oppression begins INSIDE the majority community, not only outside it.**

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Reason 2 — Most societies are CULTURALLY DIVERSE, so imposition EXCLUDES
> **"Two, MOST SOCIETIES ARE CULTURALLY DIVERSE. THEY HAVE PEOPLE BELONGING TO DIFFERENT RELIGIONS AND LANGUAGES LIVING TOGETHER IN THE SAME TERRITORY."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"TO IMPOSE A SINGLE RELIGIOUS OR LINGUISTIC IDENTITY AS A CONDITION OF BELONGING TO A PARTICULAR STATE WOULD NECESSARILY EXCLUDE SOME GROUPS."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **The two specific harms:** *"**(i) IT MIGHT RESTRICT THE RELIGIOUS LIBERTY OF THE EXCLUDED GROUP, or (ii) DISADVANTAGE THOSE WHO DO NOT SPEAK THE NATIONAL LANGUAGE.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"EITHER WAY, THE IDEAL THAT WE CHERISH MOST IN DEMOCRACY — NAMELY, EQUAL TREATMENT AND LIBERTY FOR ALL — WOULD BE SEVERELY LIMITED."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE CONCLUSION — imagine the nation in POLITICAL, not CULTURAL, terms
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"FOR BOTH THESE REASONS IT IS DESIRABLE TO IMAGINE THE NATION IN POLITICAL RATHER THAN CULTURAL TERMS. THAT IS, DEMOCRACIES NEED TO EMPHASISE AND EXPECT LOYALTY TO A SET OF VALUES THAT MAY BE ENSHRINED IN THE CONSTITUTION OF THE COUNTRY, RATHER THAN ADHERENCE TO A PARTICULAR RELIGION, RACE OR LANGUAGE."**

**⭐⭐⭐ This is the central normative claim of the chapter, and it is the link between nationalism and democracy promised in the Overview. Loyalty is owed to the CONSTITUTION, not to a religion, race or language.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 5 — NATIONAL SELF-DETERMINATION

### ⭐⭐⭐ What the claim is
⭐⭐ **"NATIONS, UNLIKE OTHER SOCIAL GROUPS, SEEK (i) THE RIGHT TO GOVERN THEMSELVES AND (ii) DETERMINE THEIR FUTURE DEVELOPMENT. They seek, in other words, THE RIGHT TO SELF-DETERMINATION."**

⭐⭐ **"In making this claim, A NATION SEEKS RECOGNITION AND ACCEPTANCE BY THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY OF ITS STATUS AS A DISTINCT POLITICAL ENTITY OR STATE."**

⭐ **Who typically makes it:** *"**MOST OFTEN THESE CLAIMS COME FROM PEOPLE WHO HAVE LIVED TOGETHER ON A GIVEN LAND FOR A LONG PERIOD OF TIME AND WHO HAVE A SENSE OF COMMON IDENTITY.**"*

⭐⭐ **The cultural version of the claim:** *"**In some cases such claims to self-determination are linked also to THE DESIRE TO FORM A STATE IN WHICH THE CULTURE OF THE GROUP IS PROTECTED, IF NOT PRIVILEGED.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ "ONE CULTURE – ONE STATE" and the Treaty of Versailles
⭐⭐ **"Claims of the latter kind were frequently made IN THE NINETEENTH CENTURY IN EUROPE. ⭐⭐ THE NOTION OF ONE CULTURE – ONE STATE BEGAN TO GAIN ACCEPTABILITY AT THE TIME."**

⭐⭐⭐ **"Subsequently, THE IDEA OF ONE CULTURE – ONE STATE WAS EMPLOYED WHILE REORDERING STATE BOUNDARIES AFTER WORLD WAR I. ⭐ THE TREATY OF VERSAILLES ESTABLISHED A NUMBER OF SMALL, NEWLY INDEPENDENT STATES — BUT IT PROVED VIRTUALLY IMPOSSIBLE TO SATISFY ALL THE DEMANDS FOR SELF-DETERMINATION WHICH WERE MADE AT THE TIME."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE HUMAN COST — "humanity paid a heavy price"
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"Besides, RE-ORGANISATION OF STATE BOUNDARIES TO SATISFY THE DEMANDS OF ONE CULTURE – ONE STATE LED TO MASS MIGRATION OF POPULATION ACROSS STATE BOUNDARIES. ⭐⭐ MILLIONS OF PEOPLE AS A CONSEQUENCE WERE DISPLACED FROM THEIR HOMES AND EXPELLED FROM THE LAND WHICH HAD BEEN THEIR HOME FOR GENERATIONS. ⭐ MANY OTHERS BECAME VICTIMS OF COMMUNAL VIOLENCE."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"HUMANITY PAID A HEAVY PRICE FOR RE-ORGANISING BOUNDARIES IN A WAY THAT CULTURALLY DISTINCT COMMUNITIES COULD FORM SEPARATE NATION-STATES."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ And it did not even WORK — the minority problem simply reappears
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"Besides, EVEN IN THIS EFFORT IT WAS NOT POSSIBLE TO ENSURE THAT THE NEWLY CREATED STATES CONTAINED ONLY ONE ETHNIC COMMUNITY. ⭐⭐ INDEED, MOST STATES HAD MORE THAN ONE ETHNIC AND CULTURAL COMMUNITY LIVING WITHIN ITS BOUNDARIES."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"THESE COMMUNITIES, WHICH WERE OFTEN SMALL IN NUMBER AND CONSTITUTED A MINORITY WITHIN THE STATE, WERE OFTEN DISADVANTAGED. ⭐⭐⭐ HENCE, THE PROBLEM OF ACCOMMODATING MINORITIES AS EQUAL CITIZENS REMAINED."**

⭐⭐ **The one gain conceded:** *"**THE ONLY POSITIVE ASPECT OF THESE DEVELOPMENTS WAS THAT IT GRANTED POLITICAL RECOGNITION TO VARIOUS GROUPS WHO SAW THEMSELVES AS DISTINCT NATIONS AND WANTED THE OPPORTUNITY TO GOVERN THEMSELVES AND DETERMINE THEIR OWN FUTURE.**"*

**⭐⭐⭐ The decisive argument to grasp: partition on cultural lines does not SOLVE the minority problem — it RELOCATES it. Every new state has its own new minorities. This is the strongest theoretical objection to "one culture – one state."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Self-determination in the anti-colonial struggles
⭐⭐ **"THE RIGHT TO NATIONAL SELF-DETERMINATION HAS ALSO BEEN ASSERTED BY NATIONAL LIBERATION MOVEMENTS IN ASIA AND AFRICA WHEN THEY WERE STRUGGLING AGAINST COLONIAL DOMINATION."**

⭐⭐ **Their two arguments:** *"Nationalist movements maintained that political independence would **(i) PROVIDE DIGNITY AND RECOGNITION TO THE COLONISED PEOPLE, AND ALSO (ii) HELP THEM TO PROTECT THE COLLECTIVE INTERESTS OF THEIR PEOPLE.**"*

⭐ **"MOST NATIONAL LIBERATION MOVEMENTS WERE INSPIRED BY THE GOAL OF BRINGING JUSTICE AND RIGHTS AND PROSPERITY TO THE NATION."**

⭐⭐ **But the same difficulty recurred:** *"**HOWEVER, HERE ALSO, IT PROVED ALMOST IMPOSSIBLE TO ENSURE THAT EACH CULTURAL GROUP — SOME OF WHOM CLAIMED TO BE DISTINCT NATIONS — COULD ACHIEVE POLITICAL INDEPENDENCE AND STATEHOOD. ⭐ AS A RESULT, MIGRATION OF POPULATIONS, BORDER WARS, AND VIOLENCE HAVE CONTINUED TO PLAGUE MANY COUNTRIES IN THE REGION.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE PARADOX (a superb formulation — learn it)
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"THUS WE HAVE THE PARADOXICAL SITUATION OF NATION-STATES WHICH THEMSELVES HAD ACHIEVED INDEPENDENCE THROUGH STRUGGLE, NOW ACTING AGAINST MINORITIES WITHIN THEIR OWN TERRITORIES WHO CLAIM THE RIGHT TO NATIONAL SELF-DETERMINATION."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE REINTERPRETATION — the chapter's central solution
⭐⭐ **"VIRTUALLY EVERY STATE IN THE WORLD TODAY FACES THE DILEMMA OF HOW TO DEAL WITH MOVEMENTS FOR SELF-DETERMINATION, and this has raised questions about the right to national self-determination."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE KEY PASSAGE:** **"MORE AND MORE PEOPLE ARE BEGINNING TO REALISE THAT THE SOLUTION DOES NOT LIE IN CREATING NEW STATES, BUT IN MAKING EXISTING STATES MORE DEMOCRATIC AND EQUAL. ⭐⭐ THAT IS, IN ENSURING THAT PEOPLE WITH DIFFERENT CULTURAL AND ETHNIC IDENTITIES LIVE AND CO-EXIST AS PARTNERS AND EQUAL CITIZENS WITHIN THE COUNTRY."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **The double benefit:** *"**THIS MAY BE ESSENTIAL NOT ONLY FOR (i) RESOLVING PROBLEMS ARISING FROM NEW CLAIMS FOR SELF-DETERMINATION, BUT ALSO FOR (ii) BUILDING A STRONG AND UNITED STATE.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **The warning that clinches it:** **"AFTER ALL, A NATION-STATE WHICH DOES NOT RESPECT THE RIGHTS AND CULTURAL IDENTITY OF MINORITIES WITHIN THE STATE WOULD FIND IT DIFFICULT TO GAIN THE LOYALTY OF ITS MEMBERS."**

**⭐⭐ Note the pragmatic edge of that last sentence: respecting minorities is not merely a moral duty — it is the CONDITION of national unity. A state that disrespects minorities manufactures its own separatism.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX: DEMAND FOR NATIONAL SELF-DETERMINATION IN BASQUE (a detailed case study — learn its structure)

### ⭐⭐ The situation
- ⭐ **"BASQUE is a HILLY AND PROSPEROUS REGION IN SPAIN."**
- ⭐⭐ **"This region is RECOGNISED BY THE SPANISH GOVERNMENT AS AN 'AUTONOMOUS' REGION WITHIN THE SPANISH FEDERATION. ⭐⭐ BUT THE LEADERS OF THE BASQUE NATIONALIST MOVEMENT ARE NOT SATISFIED WITH THIS AUTONOMY. THEY WANT THIS REGION TO BECOME A SEPARATE COUNTRY."**
- ⭐ **"Supporters of this movement have used CONSTITUTIONAL AND, TILL RECENTLY, VIOLENT MEANS TO PRESS FOR THIS DEMAND."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The Basque nationalists' FOUR arguments
| # | Argument | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **1** | **Distinct culture and language** | **"Basque Nationalists say that THEIR CULTURE IS VERY DIFFERENT FROM THE SPANISH CULTURE. THEY HAVE THEIR OWN LANGUAGE THAT DOES NOT RESEMBLE SPANISH AT ALL."** ⚠️ **But note the crucial qualification the chapter inserts: "ONLY ONE-THIRD OF THE PEOPLE IN BASQUE UNDERSTAND THAT LANGUAGE TODAY."** |
| ⭐ **2** | **Geographical distinctness** | **"The HILLY TERRAIN makes the Basque region GEOGRAPHICALLY DISTINCT from the rest of Spain."** |
| ⭐⭐ **3** | **A history of never surrendering autonomy** | **"EVER SINCE THE ROMAN DAYS, THE BASQUE REGION NEVER SURRENDERED ITS AUTONOMY TO THE SPANISH RULERS. ⭐ ITS SYSTEMS OF JUSTICE, ADMINISTRATION AND FINANCE WERE GOVERNED BY ITS OWN UNIQUE ARRANGEMENTS."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **4** | **Repression triggered the modern movement** | **"The modern Basque Nationalist Movement started when, AROUND THE END OF THE NINETEENTH CENTURY, THE SPANISH RULERS TRIED TO ABOLISH THIS UNIQUE POLITICAL-ADMINISTRATIVE ARRANGEMENT. ⭐⭐ In the twentieth century, THE SPANISH DICTATOR FRANCO FURTHER CUT DOWN THIS AUTONOMY. HE WENT AS FAR AS TO BAN THE USE OF BASQUE LANGUAGE IN PUBLIC PLACES AND EVEN HOMES."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The present impasse — and the opposing view
⭐⭐ **"THESE REPRESSIVE MEASURES HAVE NOW BEEN WITHDRAWN. ⭐⭐ BUT THE LEADERS OF THE BASQUE MOVEMENT CONTINUE TO BE (i) SUSPICIOUS OF THE MOTIVES OF THE SPANISH GOVERNMENT AND (ii) FEARFUL OF THE ENTRY OF 'OUTSIDERS' IN THEIR REGION."**

⭐⭐ **The counter-view:** *"**THEIR OPPONENTS SAY THAT BASQUE SEPARATISTS ARE TRYING TO MAKE POLITICAL GAINS OUT OF AN ISSUE ALREADY RESOLVED.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The chapter's own questions on the case (be ready to argue both sides)
> **"Do you think Basque nationalists are JUSTIFIED in demanding a separate nation? IS BASQUE A NATION? WHAT MORE WOULD YOU LIKE TO KNOW before you can answer this question? Can you think of SIMILAR EXAMPLES from different parts of the world? Can you think of REGIONS AND GROUPS IN OUR COUNTRY where such demands have been made?"**

| **Test from Part 4** | **Does Basque satisfy it?** |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Shared beliefs** — do members *believe they belong together*? | ⚠️ **The decisive unknown.** The nationalist leaders clearly do — but **only one-third understand the language**, and the chapter asks what more we would need to know. **The obvious missing datum: how many Basques actually want independence?** |
| ⭐ **History** | ✅ **Strong** — autonomous **"ever since the Roman days"**, with its own systems of **justice, administration and finance** |
| ⭐ **Territory** | ✅ **Clear** — a geographically distinct hilly region |
| ⭐⭐ **Shared political ideals / aspiration to independent political existence** | ✅ **Asserted by the movement** — this is precisely the claim being pressed |
| ⚠️ **The chapter's own counter-considerations** | ⭐ Repression **has been withdrawn** and **autonomy already granted**; the demand rests partly on **suspicion and fear of "outsiders"**; and **violent means** were used until recently — against the principle that disputes be settled **without force.** ⭐⭐ **Above all, apply the chapter's own solution: "the solution does not lie in CREATING NEW STATES but in MAKING EXISTING STATES MORE DEMOCRATIC AND EQUAL" — and Spain has, since Franco, done a good deal of exactly that.** |
⭐ **Parallels the chapter invites:** the **Quebecois in Canada**, the **Kurds in Turkey and Iraq**, the **Tamils in Sri Lanka** — and, in India, movements using **"the language of nationalism"** *(compare the demands for new States and the autonomy movements in *Indian Constitution at Work* Ch. 7)*.

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 6 — NATIONALISM AND PLURALISM

### ⭐⭐⭐ What follows from abandoning "one culture – one state"
> ⭐⭐ **"ONCE WE ABANDON THE IDEA OF ONE-CULTURE-ONE-STATE, IT BECOMES NECESSARY TO CONSIDER WAYS BY WHICH DIFFERENT CULTURES AND COMMUNITIES CAN SURVIVE AND FLOURISH WITHIN A COUNTRY."**

⭐⭐ **"It is in pursuit of this goal that MANY DEMOCRATIC SOCIETIES TODAY HAVE INTRODUCED MEASURES FOR RECOGNISING AND PROTECTING THE IDENTITY OF CULTURAL MINORITY COMMUNITIES LIVING WITHIN THEIR TERRITORY. ⭐⭐ THE INDIAN CONSTITUTION HAS AN ELABORATE SET OF PROVISIONS FOR THE PROTECTION OF RELIGIOUS, LINGUISTIC AND CULTURAL MINORITIES."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The kinds of GROUP RIGHTS granted in different countries
| # | Group right |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **1** | **"CONSTITUTIONAL PROTECTION FOR THE LANGUAGE, CULTURES AND RELIGION OF MINORITY GROUPS AND THEIR MEMBERS"** |
| ⭐⭐ **2** | **"In some cases, identified communities also have THE RIGHT TO REPRESENTATION AS A GROUP IN LEGISLATIVE BODIES AND OTHER STATE INSTITUTIONS"** |

⭐⭐⭐ **The two-fold justification:** *"Such rights may be justified on the grounds that they provide **(i) EQUAL TREATMENT AND PROTECTION OF THE LAW FOR MEMBERS OF THESE GROUPS, AS WELL AS (ii) PROTECTION FOR THE CULTURAL IDENTITY OF THE GROUP.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The inclusive definition of national identity
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"DIFFERENT GROUPS NEED TO BE GRANTED RECOGNITION AS A PART OF THE NATIONAL COMMUNITY. ⭐⭐ THIS MEANS THAT THE NATIONAL IDENTITY HAS TO BE DEFINED IN AN INCLUSIVE MANNER WHICH CAN RECOGNISE THE IMPORTANCE AND UNIQUE CONTRIBUTION OF ALL THE CULTURAL COMMUNITIES WITHIN THE STATE."**

### ⭐⭐ The honest admission that recognition may not be enough
⭐⭐ **"ALTHOUGH IT IS HOPED THAT GRANTING GROUPS RECOGNITION AND PROTECTION WOULD SATISFY THEIR ASPIRATIONS, SOME GROUPS MAY CONTINUE TO DEMAND SEPARATE STATEHOOD. ⭐ THIS MAY SEEM PARADOXICAL WHEN GLOBALISATION IS ALSO SPREADING IN THE WORLD, BUT NATIONALIST ASPIRATIONS CONTINUE TO MOTIVATE MANY GROUPS AND COMMUNITIES."**

⭐ **"CONSIDERABLE GENEROSITY AND SKILL IS NEEDED FOR COUNTRIES TO BE ABLE TO DEAL WITH SUCH DEMANDS IN A DEMOCRATIC MANNER."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE SUMMING-UP — why independent statehood for every group is IMPOSSIBLE and UNDESIRABLE
> ⭐⭐ **"To sum up, THE RIGHT TO NATIONAL SELF-DETERMINATION WAS OFTEN UNDERSTOOD TO INCLUDE THE RIGHT TO INDEPENDENT STATEHOOD FOR NATIONALITIES."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"BUT NOT ONLY WOULD IT BE IMPOSSIBLE TO GRANT INDEPENDENT STATEHOOD TO EVERY GROUP THAT SEES ITSELF AS A DISTINCT CULTURAL GROUP, OR NATION — IT WOULD PROBABLY ALSO BE UNDESIRABLE."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **The two reasons why it would be UNDESIRABLE:**
> **(i) "IT MIGHT LEAD TO THE FORMATION OF A NUMBER OF STATES TOO SMALL TO BE ECONOMICALLY AND POLITICALLY VIABLE"**
> **(ii) "AND IT COULD MULTIPLY THE PROBLEMS OF MINORITIES"**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE REINTERPRETATION:** **"THE RIGHT HAS NOW BEEN REINTERPRETED TO MEAN GRANTING CERTAIN DEMOCRATIC RIGHTS FOR A NATIONALITY WITHIN A STATE."**

**⭐⭐⭐ Memorise this reinterpretation — it is the single most examinable proposition in the chapter: self-determination no longer means SEPARATE STATEHOOD but DEMOCRATIC RIGHTS WITHIN A STATE.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE FINAL ARGUMENT — MULTIPLE IDENTITIES
⭐⭐ **"The world we live in is one that is DEEPLY CONSCIOUS OF THE IMPORTANCE OF GIVING RECOGNITION TO IDENTITIES. Today we witness many struggles for the recognition of group identities, MANY OF WHICH EMPLOY THE LANGUAGE OF NATIONALISM."**

⭐⭐ **"WHILE WE NEED TO ACKNOWLEDGE THE CLAIMS OF IDENTITY, WE SHOULD BE CAREFUL NOT TO ALLOW IDENTITY CLAIMS TO LEAD TO DIVISIONS AND VIOLENCE IN THE SOCIETY."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE KEY INSIGHT — and it is a genuinely liberating one:**
> **"WE NEED TO REMEMBER THAT EACH PERSON HAS MANY IDENTITIES. For instance, A PERSON MAY HAVE IDENTITIES BASED ON GENDER, CASTE, RELIGION, LANGUAGE, OR REGION — AND MAY BE PROUD OF ALL OF THEM."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE CONDITIONAL THAT FOLLOWS:** **"SO LONG AS EACH PERSON FEELS THAT HE/SHE CAN FREELY EXPRESS THE DIFFERENT DIMENSIONS OF HIS/HER PERSONALITY, THEY MAY NOT FEEL THE NEED TO MAKE CLAIMS ON THE STATE FOR POLITICAL RECOGNITION AND CONCESSIONS FOR ANY ONE IDENTITY."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE DEMOCRATIC PRESCRIPTION:** **"IN A DEMOCRACY, THE POLITICAL IDENTITY OF CITIZEN SHOULD ENCOMPASS THE DIFFERENT IDENTITIES WHICH PEOPLE MAY HAVE."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE CLOSING WARNING — the last sentence of the chapter:** **"IT WOULD BE DANGEROUS IF INTOLERANT AND HOMOGENISING FORMS OF IDENTITY AND NATIONALISM ARE ALLOWED TO DEVELOP."**

**⭐⭐ The argument is elegant: separatism grows when people are forced to choose ONE identity and defend it politically. If all your identities can be freely expressed, none of them needs to become a demand for a separate state. The remedy for aggressive nationalism is therefore not suppression of identity but the FREE EXPRESSION of many identities.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX: TAGORE'S CRITIQUE OF NATIONALISM (very important — the chapter's counterweight)

### ⭐⭐⭐ The famous quotation (learn it verbatim)
> **"PATRIOTISM CANNOT BE OUR FINAL SPIRITUAL SHELTER; MY REFUGE IS HUMANITY. ⭐⭐ I WILL NOT BUY GLASS FOR THE PRICE OF DIAMONDS, AND I WILL NEVER ALLOW PATRIOTISM TO TRIUMPH OVER HUMANITY AS LONG AS I LIVE."**
> — **Rabindranath Tagore**

**⭐⭐ Unpack the metaphor: PATRIOTISM is the glass; HUMANITY is the diamond. Tagore refuses to pay the price of the greater good for the lesser one. This is the sharpest possible statement of nationalism's limitation.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ But Tagore was NOT anti-national — note the careful distinctions
⭐⭐ **"HE WAS AGAINST COLONIAL RULE AND ASSERTED INDIA'S RIGHT TO INDEPENDENCE."**

⭐⭐ **His objection to British rule:** *"**He felt that in the British administration of the colonies, THERE WAS NO PLACE FOR 'UPHOLDING OF DIGNITY OF HUMAN RELATIONSHIPS' — AN IDEA WHICH WAS OTHERWISE CHERISHED IN THE BRITISH CIVILISATION.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **TAGORE'S FIRST DISTINCTION — imperialism vs civilisation:**
> **"Tagore made a distinction between OPPOSING WESTERN IMPERIALISM and REJECTING WESTERN CIVILISATION. ⭐⭐ WHILE INDIANS SHOULD BE ROOTED IN THEIR OWN CULTURE AND HERITAGE, THEY SHOULD NOT RESIST LEARNING FREELY AND PROFITABLY FROM ABROAD."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ His critique of "patriotism"
⭐⭐ **"A critique of what he called 'PATRIOTISM' is A PERSISTENT THEME IN HIS WRITINGS. ⭐ HE WAS VERY CRITICAL OF THE NARROW EXPRESSIONS OF NATIONALISM THAT HE FOUND AT WORK IN PARTS OF OUR INDEPENDENCE MOVEMENT."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **His specific fear (a very acute prediction):**
> **"IN PARTICULAR, HE WAS AFRAID THAT A REJECTION OF THE WEST IN FAVOUR OF WHAT LOOKED LIKE INDIAN TRADITIONS WAS (i) NOT ONLY LIMITING IN ITSELF; (ii) IT COULD EASILY TURN INTO HOSTILITY TO OTHER INFLUENCES FROM ABROAD — INCLUDING CHRISTIANITY, JUDAISM, ZOROASTRIANISM AND ISLAM, WHICH HAVE BEEN PRESENT IN OUR COUNTRY."**

**⭐⭐⭐ The logic is important and subtle: Tagore saw that anti-Western nativism would not stop at the West. Once "authentic Indian tradition" becomes the test of belonging, every faith that arrived from outside — Christianity, Judaism, Zoroastrianism, Islam — becomes suspect, even though all four "have been present in our country" for centuries. This is precisely the chapter's own warning against "intolerant and homogenising forms of identity."**

**⭐⭐ Note how Tagore's box connects to the main text: the chapter argues the nation should be imagined in POLITICAL rather than CULTURAL terms; Tagore shows what happens when it is imagined culturally.**

## Key Words (Glossary)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Nationalism** | A political creed of the last **two centuries or more** that **"has inspired intense loyalties as well as deep hatreds"**, has **united and divided**, has **liberated and caused wars**, and has been **a factor in the break-up of empires and states** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Nation** | **"To a great extent an 'IMAGINED' COMMUNITY, held together by the COLLECTIVE BELIEFS, ASPIRATIONS AND IMAGINATIONS of its members"** — **"A nation exists when its members BELIEVE THAT THEY BELONG TOGETHER"** |
| ⭐⭐ **Imagined community** | Not *imaginary*: members **"may never come face to face with most of our fellow nationals, nor need we share ties of descent with them"** — yet **"nations exist, are lived in and valued by their members"** |
| ⭐⭐ **Homeland** | The territory a nation claims as its own, characterised as **motherland, fatherland or holy land** — and, because more than one people may claim the same land, **"a major cause of conflict in the world"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Shared political ideals** | **A shared vision of the future and the collective aspiration to independent political existence — "that distinguishes GROUPS from NATIONS"**; members affirm values such as **democracy, secularism and liberalism** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Framework of obligations** | Obligations arising **from recognition of each other's rights as citizens** — **"recognition of this framework of obligations is the STRONGEST TEST OF LOYALTY to the nation"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Political vs cultural nationhood** | Because **all major religions are internally diverse** and **most societies are culturally diverse**, it is **"desirable to imagine the nation in POLITICAL rather than CULTURAL terms"** — expecting loyalty **"to a set of values that may be enshrined in the CONSTITUTION... rather than adherence to a particular religion, race or language"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **National self-determination** | A nation's claim to **govern itself and determine its future development**, seeking **international recognition as a distinct political entity or state** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **One culture – one state** | The nineteenth-century European notion applied in **reordering boundaries after World War I** through the **Treaty of Versailles** — which produced **mass migration, displacement and communal violence**, and still **failed to create ethnically single states** |
| ⭐⭐ **Group rights** | **Constitutional protection for the language, culture and religion of minorities**, and in some cases **the right to representation as a group in legislative bodies and state institutions** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The reinterpreted right** | **"The right has now been reinterpreted to mean granting certain DEMOCRATIC RIGHTS FOR A NATIONALITY WITHIN A STATE"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Multiple identities** | **"Each person has many identities"** — gender, caste, religion, language, region — and where all can be **freely expressed**, people need not press **any one** identity as a claim on the state |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Tagore's critique** | **"Patriotism cannot be our final spiritual shelter; my refuge is HUMANITY... I will never allow patriotism to triumph over humanity"** — distinguishing **opposition to western IMPERIALISM** from **rejection of western CIVILISATION** |

## Quick Recap (14 points)
1. ⭐⭐ **Nationalism is hard to define precisely but must be studied "because it plays such an important role in world affairs."** Popularly it evokes **patriotism, flags, sacrifice**; the **Republic Day parade** symbolises Indian nationalism's **power, strength and diversity.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **Its double-edged character: over the last two centuries it "has INSPIRED INTENSE LOYALTIES AS WELL AS DEEP HATREDS; has UNITED people AS WELL AS DIVIDED them; has helped to LIBERATE them from oppressive rule AS WELL AS been the cause of CONFLICT AND BITTERNESS AND WARS"**, and has broken up empires and states, with **the re-ordering of boundaries still unfinished and separatist struggles common.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **Three phases.** ⓵ **UNIFICATION** — nineteenth-century Europe consolidated small kingdoms into larger nation-states, forming **present-day Germany and Italy**, with many new states in **Latin America**; and **"local dialects and local loyalties were gradually consolidated into state loyalties and common languages"**, giving people **a new political identity based on membership of the nation-state.** ⓶ **BREAK-UP** — of the **Austro-Hungarian and Russian empires in early twentieth-century Europe**, and of the **British, French, Dutch and Portuguese empires in Asia and Africa**; India's freedom struggle was such a nationalist struggle, **"inspired by the desire to establish nation-states independent of foreign control."** ⓷ **SEPARATISM since 1960** — **the Quebecois in Canada, the Basques in northern Spain, the Kurds in Turkey and Iraq, the Tamils in Sri Lanka**, and groups in India using **"the language of nationalism."** ⭐ **The paradox: "ARAB NATIONALISM may hope to UNITE Arab countries in a pan Arab union, but separatist movements like the Basques or Kurds struggle to DIVIDE existing states."**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **A nation differs from other collectivities:** unlike a **family** (face-to-face, direct personal knowledge) or **tribes and clans** (ties of marriage and descent that can be traced), **"as a member of a nation we may NEVER COME FACE TO FACE with most of our fellow nationals, NOR NEED WE SHARE TIES OF DESCENT with them — yet nations exist, are lived in and valued by their members."**
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **There is NO common set of characteristics present in all nations.** **Canada** has English and French speakers, **India** a large number of languages; **many nations lack a common religion**, and the same holds for **race or descent.** ⭐ **Hence a nation is "to a great extent an IMAGINED COMMUNITY, held together by the collective beliefs, aspirations and imaginations of its members."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **Assumption 1 — SHARED BELIEFS.** **"A nation is constituted by BELIEF."** Nations are **"not like mountains, rivers or buildings"**, nor a comment on physical characteristics, but a reference to **collective identity and vision for the future** in a group **aspiring to independent political existence.** ⭐ **Like a TEAM:** if members **"did not think of themselves in this way they would cease to be a team."** ⭐⭐⭐ **"A NATION EXISTS WHEN ITS MEMBERS BELIEVE THAT THEY BELONG TOGETHER."**
7. ⭐⭐ **Assumption 2 — HISTORY.** Nations **"perceive themselves as stretching back into the past as well as reaching into the future"**, articulating their history through **collective memories, legends and historical records.** Indian nationalists invoked **ancient civilisation and cultural heritage** to claim **civilisational continuity** as the basis of the nation — ⭐ **Nehru in *The Discovery of India*: "though outwardly there was diversity and infinite variety among the people, everywhere there was that TREMENDOUS IMPRESS OF ONENESS, which held all of us together in ages past, whatever political fate or misfortune had befallen us."**
8. ⭐⭐ **Assumption 3 — TERRITORY.** Living together on a territory over a long period **"helps them to imagine themselves as one people"**, so nations speak of a **homeland — motherland, fatherland or holy land.** The **Jewish people**, though dispersed, always claimed **Palestine, the "promised land"**; **India** identifies with the **rivers, mountains and regions of the subcontinent.** ⭐⭐ **But "since MORE THAN ONE SET OF PEOPLE MAY LAY CLAIM TO THE SAME TERRITORY, the aspiration for a homeland has been A MAJOR CAUSE OF CONFLICT IN THE WORLD."**
9. ⭐⭐⭐ **Assumption 4 — SHARED POLITICAL IDEALS, and this is what really matters:** **"it is a SHARED VISION OF THE FUTURE and the COLLECTIVE ASPIRATION TO HAVE AN INDEPENDENT POLITICAL EXISTENCE that DISTINGUISHES GROUPS FROM NATIONS."** Members share **a vision of the kind of state they want to build**, affirming **democracy, secularism and liberalism** — **"the terms under which they come together and are willing to live together."** ⭐⭐ **In a democracy, "it is SHARED COMMITMENT TO A SET OF POLITICAL VALUES AND IDEALS that is the MOST DESIRABLE BASIS of a political community"**, within which members are bound by **obligations arising from recognising each other's rights as citizens** — and ⭐⭐⭐ **"recognition of this FRAMEWORK OF OBLIGATIONS is the STRONGEST TEST OF LOYALTY to the nation."**
10. ⭐⭐⭐ **Assumption 5 — COMMON POLITICAL IDENTITY rather than shared cultural identity.** Many seek instead **a common language or common descent**, and the chapter concedes that **shared language eases communication**, shared religion gives **common beliefs and practices**, and **common festivals, holidays and symbols "can bring people together"** — ⭐ **"but it can also pose a threat to the values that we cherish in a democracy", for TWO reasons:** ⓵ **"ALL MAJOR RELIGIONS ARE INTERNALLY DIVERSE"**, having **survived and evolved through dialogue**, with **sects differing significantly in interpretation** — so **"if we ignore these differences and forge an identity on the basis of a common religion we are likely to create A HIGHLY AUTHORITATIVE AND OPPRESSIVE SOCIETY."** ⓶ **"MOST SOCIETIES ARE CULTURALLY DIVERSE"**, so imposing a single religious or linguistic identity **"WOULD NECESSARILY EXCLUDE SOME GROUPS"** — restricting **religious liberty** or **disadvantaging those who do not speak the national language** — and **"either way, the ideal that we cherish most in democracy, namely EQUAL TREATMENT AND LIBERTY FOR ALL, would be severely limited."** ⭐⭐⭐ **Hence: "it is desirable to IMAGINE THE NATION IN POLITICAL RATHER THAN CULTURAL TERMS... democracies need to expect loyalty to a set of values that may be ENSHRINED IN THE CONSTITUTION, rather than adherence to a particular religion, race or language."**
11. ⭐⭐⭐ **NATIONAL SELF-DETERMINATION:** nations seek **the right to govern themselves and determine their future development**, and **international recognition as a distinct political entity or state** — sometimes also **"to form a state in which the culture of the group is protected, if not privileged."** ⭐ **The notion of ONE CULTURE – ONE STATE gained acceptability in nineteenth-century Europe and was applied in reordering boundaries after WORLD WAR I; the TREATY OF VERSAILLES created many small new states but "it proved virtually impossible to satisfy all the demands."** ⭐⭐⭐ **The cost: it "led to MASS MIGRATION of population across state boundaries. MILLIONS were DISPLACED from their homes and EXPELLED from the land which had been their home for generations. Many others became victims of COMMUNAL VIOLENCE. HUMANITY PAID A HEAVY PRICE."** ⭐⭐ **And it failed on its own terms: "it was NOT POSSIBLE to ensure that the newly created states contained ONLY ONE ETHNIC COMMUNITY... hence THE PROBLEM OF ACCOMMODATING MINORITIES AS EQUAL CITIZENS REMAINED."** The one gain was **political recognition** for groups who wished to govern themselves.
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **In Asia and Africa**, national liberation movements asserted self-determination against colonial rule, arguing that independence would **"provide dignity and recognition to the colonised people"** and **"help them protect the collective interests of their people"**, inspired by **justice, rights and prosperity.** ⭐ But here too it **"proved almost impossible to ensure that each cultural group... could achieve political independence and statehood"**, leaving **migration, border wars and violence.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Hence the paradox: "NATION-STATES WHICH THEMSELVES HAD ACHIEVED INDEPENDENCE THROUGH STRUGGLE NOW ACT AGAINST MINORITIES WITHIN THEIR OWN TERRITORIES WHO CLAIM THE RIGHT TO NATIONAL SELF-DETERMINATION."**
13. ⭐⭐⭐ **THE SOLUTION: "the solution DOES NOT LIE IN CREATING NEW STATES, BUT IN MAKING EXISTING STATES MORE DEMOCRATIC AND EQUAL"** — ensuring that people of different cultural and ethnic identities **"live and co-exist as PARTNERS AND EQUAL CITIZENS."** This serves both **resolving self-determination claims** and **building a strong and united state** — because ⭐⭐ **"a nation-state which does not respect the rights and cultural identity of minorities WOULD FIND IT DIFFICULT TO GAIN THE LOYALTY OF ITS MEMBERS."** ⭐ **In practice this means PLURALISM: group rights — constitutional protection for minority language, culture and religion, and sometimes group representation in legislative bodies — justified as giving both equal protection of the law and protection of the group's cultural identity, with national identity "defined in an INCLUSIVE MANNER."** ⭐⭐⭐ **And the summing-up: granting independent statehood to every group claiming nationhood would be not only IMPOSSIBLE but "probably also UNDESIRABLE", since it "might lead to states TOO SMALL TO BE ECONOMICALLY AND POLITICALLY VIABLE" and "could MULTIPLY THE PROBLEMS OF MINORITIES." "The right has now been REINTERPRETED to mean granting certain DEMOCRATIC RIGHTS FOR A NATIONALITY WITHIN A STATE."**
14. ⭐⭐⭐ **The final arguments.** **MULTIPLE IDENTITIES:** *"each person has MANY identities — gender, caste, religion, language, or region — and may be PROUD OF ALL OF THEM"*; ⭐⭐ **"so long as each person feels he/she can FREELY EXPRESS the different dimensions of his/her personality, they may NOT FEEL THE NEED to make claims on the state for political recognition for ANY ONE identity."** ⭐ **"In a democracy, the POLITICAL IDENTITY OF CITIZEN SHOULD ENCOMPASS the different identities which people may have"**, and ⭐⭐⭐ **"IT WOULD BE DANGEROUS IF INTOLERANT AND HOMOGENISING FORMS OF IDENTITY AND NATIONALISM ARE ALLOWED TO DEVELOP."** ⭐⭐ **TAGORE'S CRITIQUE supplies the limit: "PATRIOTISM CANNOT BE OUR FINAL SPIRITUAL SHELTER; MY REFUGE IS HUMANITY. I WILL NOT BUY GLASS FOR THE PRICE OF DIAMONDS, AND I WILL NEVER ALLOW PATRIOTISM TO TRIUMPH OVER HUMANITY AS LONG AS I LIVE."** Yet he **opposed colonial rule and asserted India's right to independence**, distinguishing **opposition to western IMPERIALISM from rejection of western CIVILISATION** — Indians **"should be rooted in their own culture and heritage [but] should not resist learning freely and profitably from abroad"** — and fearing that **rejection of the west in favour of supposed Indian tradition** was **"not only limiting in itself"** but **"could easily turn into HOSTILITY TO OTHER INFLUENCES FROM ABROAD, including CHRISTIANITY, JUDAISM, ZOROASTRIANISM AND ISLAM, which have been present in our country."**

## 60-Second Revision
**Nationalism "has INSPIRED INTENSE LOYALTIES AS WELL AS DEEP HATREDS; UNITED as well as DIVIDED; LIBERATED as well as caused CONFLICT, BITTERNESS AND WARS."** **THREE PHASES: (1) UNIFICATION — 19th-c Europe → GERMANY and ITALY, plus new states in LATIN AMERICA; local dialects and loyalties consolidated into state loyalties and common languages. (2) BREAK-UP — AUSTRO-HUNGARIAN and RUSSIAN empires (early 20th c.); BRITISH, FRENCH, DUTCH, PORTUGUESE empires in Asia and Africa; India's freedom struggle. (3) SEPARATISM SINCE 1960 — QUEBECOIS (Canada), BASQUES (northern Spain), KURDS (Turkey and Iraq), TAMILS (Sri Lanka). PARADOX: ARAB nationalism seeks to UNITE in a pan-Arab union; Basques and Kurds seek to DIVIDE.** **A nation ≠ FAMILY (face-to-face) ≠ TRIBES/CLANS (traceable descent): "we may NEVER COME FACE TO FACE with most fellow nationals, nor need we share TIES OF DESCENT."** **NO common set of characteristics: CANADA (English + French), INDIA (many languages); no common religion, race or descent. ⇒ A nation is an "IMAGINED COMMUNITY held together by the collective beliefs, aspirations and imaginations of its members."** **FIVE ASSUMPTIONS: (1) SHARED BELIEFS — "not like mountains, rivers or buildings"; like a TEAM; ⭐ "A NATION EXISTS WHEN ITS MEMBERS BELIEVE THAT THEY BELONG TOGETHER." (2) HISTORY — collective memories, legends, historical records; NEHRU, THE DISCOVERY OF INDIA: "outwardly diversity and infinite variety... everywhere that TREMENDOUS IMPRESS OF ONENESS... whatever political fate or misfortune had befallen us." (3) TERRITORY — HOMELAND as MOTHERLAND / FATHERLAND / HOLY LAND; JEWS and PALESTINE, the "PROMISED LAND"; India and the rivers, mountains and regions of the subcontinent; ⚠️ "more than one set of people may lay claim to the SAME territory" → A MAJOR CAUSE OF CONFLICT. (4) SHARED POLITICAL IDEALS — "a shared VISION OF THE FUTURE and the collective aspiration to INDEPENDENT POLITICAL EXISTENCE DISTINGUISHES GROUPS FROM NATIONS"; democracy, secularism, liberalism; ⭐ "recognition of this FRAMEWORK OF OBLIGATIONS is the STRONGEST TEST OF LOYALTY." (5) COMMON POLITICAL IDENTITY — not cultural.** **TWO REASONS AGAINST CULTURAL NATIONHOOD: (i) "ALL MAJOR RELIGIONS ARE INTERNALLY DIVERSE", evolved through dialogue, with sects differing in interpretation ⇒ forging identity on one religion creates "a HIGHLY AUTHORITATIVE AND OPPRESSIVE SOCIETY"; (ii) "MOST SOCIETIES ARE CULTURALLY DIVERSE" ⇒ imposing one religious or linguistic identity "WOULD NECESSARILY EXCLUDE SOME GROUPS", restricting religious liberty or disadvantaging non-speakers of the national language, so "EQUAL TREATMENT AND LIBERTY FOR ALL would be severely limited." ⇒ ⭐ IMAGINE THE NATION IN POLITICAL, NOT CULTURAL, TERMS — loyalty to values ENSHRINED IN THE CONSTITUTION, not to a religion, race or language.** **SELF-DETERMINATION: ONE CULTURE – ONE STATE (19th-c Europe) applied at the TREATY OF VERSAILLES after WWI → "MASS MIGRATION", MILLIONS DISPLACED AND EXPELLED, COMMUNAL VIOLENCE — "HUMANITY PAID A HEAVY PRICE" — and it STILL failed: most states had more than one ethnic community, so "THE PROBLEM OF ACCOMMODATING MINORITIES AS EQUAL CITIZENS REMAINED."** **In Asia and Africa it promised DIGNITY, RECOGNITION and protection of collective interests — but also failed to give every cultural group a state ⇒ ⭐ THE PARADOX: "nation-states which themselves achieved independence through struggle NOW ACT AGAINST MINORITIES within their own territories who claim the right to national self-determination."** **⭐ THE SOLUTION: "the solution DOES NOT LIE IN CREATING NEW STATES BUT IN MAKING EXISTING STATES MORE DEMOCRATIC AND EQUAL" — co-existence as "PARTNERS AND EQUAL CITIZENS" — because "a nation-state which does not respect the rights and cultural identity of minorities WOULD FIND IT DIFFICULT TO GAIN THE LOYALTY OF ITS MEMBERS."** **PLURALISM: group rights = constitutional protection for minority LANGUAGE, CULTURE, RELIGION + sometimes GROUP REPRESENTATION in legislative bodies; justified as equal protection of law + protection of cultural identity; national identity "defined in an INCLUSIVE MANNER."** **⭐ REINTERPRETATION: independent statehood for every group is IMPOSSIBLE and "probably also UNDESIRABLE" — states "TOO SMALL TO BE ECONOMICALLY AND POLITICALLY VIABLE" + it would "MULTIPLY THE PROBLEMS OF MINORITIES." "The right has now been REINTERPRETED to mean granting certain DEMOCRATIC RIGHTS FOR A NATIONALITY WITHIN A STATE."** **BASQUE: hilly, prosperous, already an AUTONOMOUS region but leaders want a separate country; used constitutional and (till recently) VIOLENT means; own non-Spanish language BUT "ONLY ONE-THIRD understand it today"; autonomous "EVER SINCE THE ROMAN DAYS" with its own justice, administration and finance; movement began when Spanish rulers tried to abolish this at the END OF THE 19th CENTURY; FRANCO banned Basque "IN PUBLIC PLACES AND EVEN HOMES"; repression now withdrawn but leaders remain SUSPICIOUS and FEARFUL OF "OUTSIDERS"; opponents say they seek "POLITICAL GAINS OUT OF AN ISSUE ALREADY RESOLVED."** **MULTIPLE IDENTITIES: "each person has MANY identities — gender, caste, religion, language, region — and may be PROUD OF ALL OF THEM"; "SO LONG AS each person can FREELY EXPRESS the different dimensions of his/her personality, they may NOT FEEL THE NEED to make claims on the state for ANY ONE identity"; the political identity of citizen "should ENCOMPASS the different identities"; ⭐ "IT WOULD BE DANGEROUS IF INTOLERANT AND HOMOGENISING FORMS OF IDENTITY AND NATIONALISM ARE ALLOWED TO DEVELOP."** **TAGORE: "PATRIOTISM CANNOT BE OUR FINAL SPIRITUAL SHELTER; MY REFUGE IS HUMANITY. I WILL NOT BUY GLASS FOR THE PRICE OF DIAMONDS." He opposed COLONIAL RULE and asserted India's right to independence; distinguished opposing western IMPERIALISM from rejecting western CIVILISATION — be "ROOTED IN OUR OWN CULTURE" but "NOT RESIST LEARNING FREELY AND PROFITABLY FROM ABROAD"; feared nativism would turn into HOSTILITY TO CHRISTIANITY, JUDAISM, ZOROASTRIANISM AND ISLAM, "which have been PRESENT IN OUR COUNTRY."**

## Try These Questions

### NCERT Exercises (with answers/pointers)
1. ⭐⭐⭐ **How is a nation different from other forms of collective belonging?**
   → ⭐ **Start with what a nation is not:** *"a nation is NOT ANY CASUAL COLLECTION OF PEOPLE"*, and it differs from **the FAMILY**, which rests on **"face-to-face relationships with each member having DIRECT PERSONAL KNOWLEDGE of the identity and character of others"**, and from **TRIBES, CLANS and kinship groups**, where **"ties of MARRIAGE AND DESCENT link members, so that even if we do not personally know all the members we can TRACE THE LINKS that bind them to us."**
   ⭐⭐ **The distinctive feature:** *"**as a member of a nation we may NEVER COME FACE TO FACE with most of our fellow nationals, NOR NEED WE SHARE TIES OF DESCENT with them — YET NATIONS EXIST, ARE LIVED IN AND VALUED BY THEIR MEMBERS.**"* Nor is there **any common set of characteristics present in all nations** — **Canada** and **India** lack a common language, many nations lack a common religion, and the same holds for **race and descent.**
   ⭐⭐⭐ **So what makes it a nation:** it is **"to a great extent an IMAGINED COMMUNITY, held together by the collective beliefs, aspirations and imaginations of its members"**, resting on **shared beliefs** (**"a nation exists when its members BELIEVE THAT THEY BELONG TOGETHER"** — the **team** analogy), **a sense of continuing historical identity**, **identification with a territory or homeland**, and — decisively — **shared political ideals**: *"it is a SHARED VISION OF THE FUTURE and the collective aspiration to have an INDEPENDENT POLITICAL EXISTENCE that DISTINGUISHES GROUPS FROM NATIONS."* ⭐ **That last point is the crux: other collectivities do not aspire to statehood; nations do.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **What do you understand by the right to national self-determination? How has this idea resulted in both formation of and challenges to nation-states?**
   → ⭐ **The right:** nations, **"unlike other social groups, seek the right to GOVERN THEMSELVES and DETERMINE THEIR FUTURE DEVELOPMENT"**, seeking **"recognition and acceptance by the international community of its status as a distinct political entity or state"** — claims usually made by **people long resident on a given land with a sense of common identity**, and sometimes tied to **"the desire to form a state in which the culture of the group is protected, if not privileged."**
   ⭐⭐ **How it FORMED nation-states:** the notion of **"ONE CULTURE – ONE STATE"** gained ground in **nineteenth-century Europe** and was applied in **reordering boundaries after World War I**, when **the Treaty of Versailles established a number of small, newly independent states**; and it drove the **national liberation movements of Asia and Africa** against colonial rule, promising **dignity, recognition and protection of collective interests**, and inspired by **justice, rights and prosperity.**
   ⭐⭐⭐ **How it CHALLENGED nation-states:** **(i)** it **"proved virtually impossible to satisfy all the demands"** at Versailles; **(ii)** reorganisation produced **"MASS MIGRATION... MILLIONS displaced from their homes and EXPELLED from the land which had been their home for generations"**, with **many becoming victims of communal violence** — **"humanity paid a heavy price"**; **(iii)** it **failed on its own terms**, since **"it was not possible to ensure that the newly created states contained only one ethnic community"**, leaving disadvantaged minorities and **"the problem of accommodating minorities as equal citizens"**; **(iv)** in Asia and Africa it left **"migration of populations, border wars, and violence"**; **(v)** since **1960**, even stable states face separatist demands — **Quebecois, Basques, Kurds, Tamils.** ⭐⭐ **Hence the paradox: "nation-states which themselves had achieved independence through struggle now act against minorities within their own territories who claim the right to national self-determination."**
   ⭐⭐⭐ **The reinterpretation to conclude with:** granting statehood to every claimant would be **impossible and "probably also UNDESIRABLE"** — producing states **"too small to be economically and politically viable"** and **"multiplying the problems of minorities"** — so **"the right has now been REINTERPRETED to mean granting certain DEMOCRATIC RIGHTS FOR A NATIONALITY WITHIN A STATE."**
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **"Nationalism can unite people as well as divide them, liberate them as well as generate bitterness and conflict." Illustrate with examples.**
   | Face of nationalism | Illustrations |
   |---|---|
   | ⭐⭐ **UNITED** | **Nineteenth-century Europe: "the UNIFICATION of a number of small kingdoms into larger nation-states" — present-day GERMANY and ITALY**, plus new states in **Latin America**; and **"local dialects and local loyalties were gradually consolidated into state loyalties and common languages"**, giving people **a new political identity.** ⭐ Also **ARAB nationalism's hope "to unite Arab countries in a pan Arab union"**, and India's own **"similar process of consolidation... in the last century or more."** |
   | ⭐⭐ **LIBERATED** | **The break-up of the BRITISH, FRENCH, DUTCH and PORTUGUESE empires in Asia and Africa: "the struggle for freedom from colonial rule by India and other former colonies were NATIONALIST STRUGGLES, inspired by the desire to establish nation-states independent of foreign control"**, promising **dignity, recognition, justice, rights and prosperity.** |
   | ⭐⭐ **DIVIDED** | **The break-up of the AUSTRO-HUNGARIAN and RUSSIAN empires**; and since **1960**, separatist movements among the **Quebecois (Canada), Basques (northern Spain), Kurds (Turkey and Iraq), Tamils (Sri Lanka)** — while **"the language of nationalism is also used by some groups in India."** |
   | ⭐⭐⭐ **GENERATED BITTERNESS AND CONFLICT** | ⭐ Reorganising boundaries for **one culture – one state** caused **mass migration, the displacement and expulsion of millions, and communal violence — "HUMANITY PAID A HEAVY PRICE."** In Asia and Africa, **"migration of populations, border wars, and violence have continued to plague many countries."** ⭐ Because **more than one people may claim the same land, "the aspiration for a HOMELAND has been a major cause of conflict in the world."** And Basque separatists used **violent means "till recently."** ⚠️ **Tagore's fear**: nativist nationalism **"could easily turn into hostility to other influences from abroad, including Christianity, Judaism, Zoroastrianism and Islam."** |
   ⭐⭐ **Conclude with the chapter's own warning: "it would be DANGEROUS if INTOLERANT AND HOMOGENISING forms of identity and nationalism are allowed to develop"** — and the remedy: imagine the nation **politically, not culturally**, and make existing states **more democratic and equal.**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **"Neither descent, nor language, nor religion or ethnicity can claim to be a common factor in nationalisms all over the world." Comment.**
   → ⭐⭐ **Agree — and the chapter says so flatly: "THERE IS IN FACT NO COMMON SET OF CHARACTERISTICS WHICH IS PRESENT IN ALL NATIONS."**
   - ⭐ **LANGUAGE:** *"Many nations do not have a common language. **CANADA** is an example here — it includes **English speaking as well as French speaking peoples.** **INDIA** also has a large number of languages spoken in different regions and by different communities."*
   - ⭐ **RELIGION:** *"Nor do many nations have a common religion to unite them."*
   - ⭐ **RACE / DESCENT / ETHNICITY:** *"The same could be said of other characteristics such as race or descent"* — and note that **"as a member of a nation... nor need we SHARE TIES OF DESCENT"** with fellow nationals at all.
   ⭐⭐⭐ **What does the work instead:** the nation is **"an IMAGINED COMMUNITY held together by the collective beliefs, aspirations and imaginations of its members"** — **shared beliefs** ("a nation exists when its members believe that they belong together"), **a sense of continuing historical identity**, **a homeland**, and above all **shared political ideals.**
   ⭐⭐ **And the normative pay-off — this is what makes the question important:** because cultural markers are neither universal nor safe, **"it is DESIRABLE TO IMAGINE THE NATION IN POLITICAL RATHER THAN CULTURAL TERMS."** Basing nationhood on religion would create **"a highly authoritative and oppressive society"** (since **all major religions are internally diverse**), and imposing a single religious or linguistic identity **"would necessarily EXCLUDE some groups"** — limiting **"equal treatment and liberty for all."**
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **Illustrate with suitable examples the factors that lead to the emergence of nationalist feelings.**
   | Factor | Illustration |
   |---|---|
   | ⭐⭐ **Shared belief in belonging together** | The **team** analogy — **"a nation exists when its members believe that they belong together"** |
   | ⭐⭐ **A sense of continuing historical identity** | Indian nationalists invoking **ancient civilisation and cultural heritage**; **Nehru's "tremendous impress of oneness"**; the Basques' claim to autonomy **"ever since the Roman days"** |
   | ⭐⭐ **Attachment to a territory / homeland** | The **Jewish** claim to **Palestine, the "promised land"**, despite dispersal; India's identification with **the rivers, mountains and regions of the subcontinent**; the Basques' **geographically distinct hilly terrain** |
   | ⭐⭐⭐ **Shared political ideals and the aspiration to independent political existence** | **Democracy, secularism, liberalism** as **"the terms under which they come together and are willing to live together"** — and the anti-colonial demand for **nation-states "independent of foreign control"** |
   | ⭐⭐ **Cultural distinctness — language and culture** | The **Basques'** claim that **"their culture is very different from the Spanish culture"** and their language **"does not resemble Spanish at all"** |
   | ⭐⭐⭐ **REPRESSION — the most powerful trigger of all** | ⭐ **"The modern Basque Nationalist Movement STARTED WHEN... the Spanish rulers TRIED TO ABOLISH this unique political-administrative arrangement"**, and **Franco "went as far as to BAN the use of Basque language in public places and EVEN HOMES."** ⭐⭐ **Likewise colonial rule bred anti-colonial nationalism — and, generally, "a nation-state which does not respect the rights and cultural identity of minorities would find it difficult to gain the loyalty of its members."** |
   | ⭐ **Denial of dignity and recognition** | National liberation movements held that independence would **"provide dignity and recognition to the colonised people"** |
   | ⭐ **Fear of "outsiders"** | Basque leaders remain **"fearful of the entry of 'outsiders' in their region"** |
   ⭐⭐ **The pattern worth naming: nationalist feeling is strongest where a group with a distinct history, territory and culture experiences REPRESSION OR NON-RECOGNITION. That is precisely why the chapter's prescription is to make states "more democratic and equal."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **How is a democracy more effective than authoritarian governments in dealing with conflicting nationalist aspirations?**
   → ⭐⭐ **Five arguments, all from the chapter:**
   - ⭐⭐⭐ **A democracy can grant recognition instead of suppression.** *"Once we abandon the idea of one-culture-one-state, it becomes necessary to consider ways by which different cultures and communities can SURVIVE AND FLOURISH WITHIN A COUNTRY"* — hence **group rights**: constitutional protection for minority **language, culture and religion**, and sometimes **group representation in legislative bodies.** **India's Constitution "has an ELABORATE SET OF PROVISIONS for the protection of religious, linguistic and cultural minorities."**
   - ⭐⭐⭐ **Authoritarian suppression CREATES the nationalism it fears.** **Franco banned the Basque language "in public places and even homes"** — and the movement did not die; when repression was withdrawn, the leaders remained **"suspicious of the motives of the Spanish government."** ⭐ **Compare the general principle: "a nation-state which does not respect the rights and cultural identity of minorities would find it difficult to GAIN THE LOYALTY of its members."**
   - ⭐⭐ **A democracy can define national identity INCLUSIVELY** — **"in a manner which can recognise the importance and unique contribution of ALL the cultural communities within the state"** — and can rest nationhood on **loyalty to constitutional values rather than to a religion, race or language.**
   - ⭐⭐ **A democracy can accommodate MULTIPLE IDENTITIES.** *"So long as each person feels he/she can FREELY EXPRESS the different dimensions of his/her personality, they may NOT FEEL THE NEED to make claims on the state for political recognition for any ONE identity"* — so the **political identity of citizen "should ENCOMPASS the different identities."** An authoritarian state, by contrast, forces a single identity and thereby politicises it.
   - ⭐⭐ **A democracy offers legitimate channels of negotiation** rather than force — and the chapter's whole solution presupposes them: **"making existing states MORE DEMOCRATIC AND EQUAL"**, so that people **"live and co-exist as PARTNERS AND EQUAL CITIZENS."**
   ⚠️ **A mature answer adds the caveat the chapter itself supplies: recognition may not always satisfy — "SOME GROUPS MAY CONTINUE TO DEMAND SEPARATE STATEHOOD" — so "considerable GENEROSITY AND SKILL is needed for countries to deal with such demands in a democratic manner."**
7. ⭐⭐⭐ **What do you think are the limitations of nationalism?**
   → ⭐ **Six limitations, each grounded in the text:**
   - ⭐⭐⭐ **It divides as readily as it unites.** *"It has inspired intense loyalties AS WELL AS DEEP HATREDS... helped to liberate them from oppressive rule AS WELL AS been the cause of CONFLICT AND BITTERNESS AND WARS."* The same creed builds **pan-Arab unity** and **Basque and Kurdish separatism.**
   - ⭐⭐⭐ **The territorial claim is inherently conflictual.** *"Since MORE THAN ONE SET OF PEOPLE MAY LAY CLAIM TO THE SAME TERRITORY, the aspiration for a homeland has been A MAJOR CAUSE OF CONFLICT IN THE WORLD."*
   - ⭐⭐⭐ **"One culture – one state" is impossible and inhumane.** It produced **mass migration, millions displaced and expelled, communal violence — "humanity paid a heavy price"** — and still **failed to create ethnically single states**, so **"the problem of accommodating minorities as equal citizens REMAINED."**
   - ⭐⭐⭐ **It turns on its own minorities.** **The paradox: "nation-states which themselves had achieved independence through struggle NOW ACT AGAINST MINORITIES within their own territories."**
   - ⭐⭐ **Cultural nationalism threatens democracy from within.** Since **all major religions are internally diverse**, forging identity on one religion produces **"a highly authoritative and oppressive society"**; and imposing one religion or language **"would necessarily EXCLUDE some groups"**, severely limiting **"equal treatment and liberty for all."** ⭐ **"It would be DANGEROUS if INTOLERANT AND HOMOGENISING forms of identity and nationalism are allowed to develop."**
   - ⭐⭐ **Fragmentation would be self-defeating.** Statehood for every claimant would create states **"too small to be economically and politically viable"** and would **"multiply the problems of minorities."**
   ⭐⭐⭐ **Close with TAGORE — the deepest limitation of all:** **"PATRIOTISM CANNOT BE OUR FINAL SPIRITUAL SHELTER; MY REFUGE IS HUMANITY. I WILL NOT BUY GLASS FOR THE PRICE OF DIAMONDS, AND I WILL NEVER ALLOW PATRIOTISM TO TRIUMPH OVER HUMANITY AS LONG AS I LIVE."** And note that Tagore was **no opponent of independence** — he distinguished **opposing western IMPERIALISM from rejecting western CIVILISATION**, and feared that nativism **"could easily turn into hostility to other influences from abroad, including Christianity, Judaism, Zoroastrianism and Islam, which have been present in our country."**
