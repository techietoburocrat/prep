## How the chapters connect (the architecture of the book)

1. **Chapter 1** is the method chapter: what political theory *does*, and why political concepts admit **many definitions** while mathematical ones do not.
2. **Chapters 2–5** are the **four core values**, and they build on one another in a deliberate sequence:
   - **Freedom** (Ch 2) → but freedom for whom, on what terms? →
   - **Equality** (Ch 3) → but which inequalities are unjust? →
   - **Social Justice** (Ch 4) → and how are just claims secured? →
   - **Rights** (Ch 5).
3. **Chapters 6–8** are the **three membership questions**: who belongs (**Citizenship**), what binds them together (**Nationalism**), and how a diverse people can live together as equals (**Secularism**).
4. ⭐ **Chapters 7 and 8 are a single argument in two halves.** Nationalism ends by asking how a democratic state can ensure equality for different cultures; Secularism opens by answering it. **Never revise one without the other.**

## ⭐ The highest-value items in the book (if you revise nothing else)

- **Ch 1** — the definition of **politics**; why **"unlike in mathematics... we encounter many definitions"**; the **three layers of equality**
- **Ch 2** — ***Swaraj* = rule OF the self AND rule OVER self**; the **harm principle**; the **negative/positive liberty table**; **Mill's four arguments**
- **Ch 3** — **"pre-determined by birth or social circumstance"**; **choice vs circumstance**; the **four ideologies**; **affirmative action corrects "the cumulative effect of past inequalities"**
- **Ch 4** — the **three principles of justice**; the **veil of ignorance**; **Ambedkar** and **Mill** on justice as a **right, not charity**
- **Ch 5** — **natural rights vs human rights**; **Kant's price/dignity**; the state's **double obligation**; **UDHR, 10 December 1948**
- **Ch 6** — **full and equal membership**; the **railway-compartment** analogy; **King's three arguments**; **Marshall's quality/quantity pair**; ***Olga Tellis* (1985)**
- **Ch 7** — the **imagined community**; **"a nation exists when its members believe that they belong together"**; **political vs cultural nationhood**; the **reinterpreted right to self-determination**
- **Ch 8** — **inter- vs intra-religious domination**; **mutual exclusion vs principled distance**; **"equal disrespect"**; the **train** and **cinema** analogies

**For Mains / Essay / PSIR:** **Ch 4 → Ch 8 → Ch 3 → Ch 7 → Ch 2** — these five carry the arguments, analogies and quotable lines that lift an answer.
