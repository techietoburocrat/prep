# Chapter 6: Citizenship

## The Big Idea (Overview)
⭐⭐⭐ **THE DEFINITION that governs the whole chapter:**
> **"CITIZENSHIP IMPLIES FULL AND EQUAL MEMBERSHIP OF A POLITICAL COMMUNITY."**

**The chapter's structure — note how each section tests that definition:**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 1 — INTRODUCTION: what citizenship gives

### ⭐⭐ What states provide their members
⭐⭐ **"In the contemporary world, STATES PROVIDE (i) A COLLECTIVE POLITICAL IDENTITY TO THEIR MEMBERS AS WELL AS (ii) CERTAIN RIGHTS. Therefore WE THINK OF OURSELVES AS INDIANS, OR JAPANESE, OR GERMANS, DEPENDING ON THE STATE TO WHICH WE BELONG."**

⭐ **"Citizens expect CERTAIN RIGHTS from their state, as well as HELP AND PROTECTION WHEREVER THEY MAY TRAVEL."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Why full membership matters — the argument from its ABSENCE
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"The importance of full membership of a state can be appreciated if we think of the condition of THE THOUSANDS OF PEOPLE IN THE WORLD WHO HAVE THE BAD FORTUNE TO BE FORCED TO LIVE AS REFUGEES OR ILLEGAL MIGRANTS BECAUSE NO STATE IS WILLING TO GRANT THEM MEMBERSHIP."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"Such people are NOT GUARANTEED RIGHTS BY ANY STATE and generally live in PRECARIOUS CONDITIONS. ⭐ For them, FULL MEMBERSHIP OF A STATE OF THEIR CHOICE IS A GOAL FOR WHICH THEY ARE WILLING TO STRUGGLE — as we see today with PALESTINIAN REFUGEES IN THE MIDDLE EAST."**

**⭐⭐ Note the method: the value of citizenship is demonstrated not by describing what citizens have, but by describing what the stateless LACK. This argument returns in Section 6.5.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The rights of citizenship (three kinds)
⭐⭐ **"The PRECISE NATURE of the rights granted to citizens MAY VARY FROM STATE TO STATE, but in most democratic countries today they would include:"**
| Kind | Examples given |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Political rights** | **"The RIGHT TO VOTE"** |
| ⭐ **Civil rights** | **"The FREEDOM OF SPEECH OR BELIEF"** |
| ⭐ **Socio-economic rights** | **"The RIGHT TO A MINIMUM WAGE, or the RIGHT TO EDUCATION"** |

⭐⭐⭐ **"EQUALITY OF RIGHTS AND STATUS IS ONE OF THE BASIC RIGHTS OF CITIZENSHIP."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Every right was WON — the history of struggle
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"EACH OF THE RIGHTS NOW ENJOYED BY CITIZENS HAS BEEN WON AFTER STRUGGLE."**

| Struggle | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Against monarchies** | **"Some of the earliest struggles were fought by people TO ASSERT THEIR INDEPENDENCE AND RIGHTS AGAINST POWERFUL MONARCHIES. Many European countries experienced such struggles, SOME OF THEM VIOLENT, LIKE THE FRENCH REVOLUTION IN 1789."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Anti-colonial** | **"In the COLONIES OF ASIA AND AFRICA, DEMANDS FOR EQUAL CITIZENSHIP FORMED PART OF THEIR STRUGGLE FOR INDEPENDENCE FROM COLONIAL RULERS."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **South Africa** | **"In South Africa, THE BLACK AFRICAN POPULATION HAD TO UNDERTAKE A LONG STRUGGLE AGAINST THE RULING WHITE MINORITY FOR EQUAL CITIZENSHIP. ⭐ THIS CONTINUED UNTIL THE EARLY 1990s."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ongoing, in India** | **"Struggles to achieve full membership and equal rights CONTINUE EVEN NOW in many parts of the world. You may have read about THE WOMEN'S MOVEMENT AND THE DALIT MOVEMENT in our country."** ⭐ **Their twofold purpose: "(i) TO CHANGE PUBLIC OPINION BY DRAWING ATTENTION TO THEIR NEEDS, as well as (ii) TO INFLUENCE GOVERNMENT POLICY TO ENSURE THEM EQUAL RIGHTS AND OPPORTUNITIES."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Citizenship is MORE than the citizen–state relationship
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"However, citizenship is about MORE THAN THE RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN STATES AND THEIR MEMBERS. IT IS ALSO ABOUT CITIZEN-CITIZEN RELATIONS, and involves CERTAIN OBLIGATIONS OF CITIZENS TO EACH OTHER AND TO THE SOCIETY."**

⭐⭐ **The two kinds of obligation:**
1. **"NOT JUST THE LEGAL OBLIGATIONS IMPOSED BY STATES..."**
2. ⭐⭐ **"...BUT ALSO A MORAL OBLIGATION TO PARTICIPATE IN, AND CONTRIBUTE TO, THE SHARED LIFE OF THE COMMUNITY."**

⭐⭐ **A third dimension — the trusteeship idea:** *"**CITIZENS ARE ALSO CONSIDERED TO BE THE INHERITORS AND TRUSTEES OF THE CULTURE AND NATURAL RESOURCES OF THE COUNTRY.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The chapter's METHOD (worth noting — it explains why the chapter is built around struggles)
> **"A GOOD WAY TO UNDERSTAND A POLITICAL CONCEPT IS TO LOOK FOR INSTANCES WHERE ITS ACCEPTED MEANING IS BEING QUESTIONED BY GROUPS WHO FEEL THAT IT DOES NOT TAKE ACCOUNT OF THEIR NEEDS AND ASPIRATIONS."**

### ⭐⭐ Box: "Let's Think" — apartheid South Africa until 1994
*The chapter describes the policy: "**The whites had the right to VOTE, CONTEST ELECTIONS AND ELECT GOVERNMENT; they were free to PURCHASE PROPERTY AND GO TO ANY PLACE in the country. BLACKS DID NOT HAVE SUCH RIGHTS. SEPARATE COLONIES for whites and blacks were established. The blacks had to take 'PASSES' TO WORK IN WHITE NEIGHBOURHOODS. THEY WERE NOT ALLOWED TO KEEP THEIR FAMILIES IN THE WHITE AREAS. THE SCHOOLS WERE ALSO SEPARATE for the people of different colour.**"*

⭐⭐ **"Do you think the Blacks had full and equal membership in South Africa? Give reasons."** → ⭐ **No, on every count of the definition.** They lacked **political rights** (no vote, no contesting elections), **civil rights** (no freedom of movement — the **pass** system — and no freedom to purchase property), **socio-economic rights** (segregated, inferior schools), and even **family life** (families barred from white areas). ⭐⭐ **Crucially, this was not incidental exclusion but a legally constructed system** — so they were **residents without membership**, exactly the condition the Introduction describes as **"precarious."**

⭐⭐ **"What does the above description tell us about the RELATIONSHIP OF DIFFERENT GROUPS in South Africa?"** → ⭐ **A relationship of DOMINATION maintained by law, in which a numerical minority held all rights.** Note the **spatial** dimension — separate colonies, passes, separate schools — showing that inequality was enforced through **physical separation**, which is precisely **Martin Luther King's** target in the next box.

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 2 — FULL AND EQUAL MEMBERSHIP: the migration question

### ⭐⭐⭐ The railway-compartment analogy — a brilliant opening
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"If you have ever travelled in a crowded railway compartment or bus, you will be familiar with the way in which THOSE WHO MAY HAVE EARLIER FOUGHT EACH OTHER TO ENTER, ONCE INSIDE DISCOVER A SHARED INTEREST IN KEEPING OTHERS OUT! ⭐⭐ A DIVISION SOON DEVELOPS BETWEEN 'INSIDERS' AND 'OUTSIDERS', WITH 'OUTSIDERS' BEING SEEN AS A THREAT."**

**⭐⭐ The analogy is doing real analytical work: the insider/outsider division is not based on any prior identity — it is created by the *scarcity of space itself*, and by the accident of who got in first. Exactly the same logic scales up to cities and nations.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ How it scales up
⭐⭐ **"Similar processes take place from time to time in CITIES, REGIONS, OR EVEN THE NATION AS A WHOLE. ⭐⭐⭐ IF (i) JOBS, (ii) FACILITIES LIKE MEDICAL CARE OR EDUCATION, AND (iii) NATURAL RESOURCES LIKE LAND OR WATER ARE LIMITED, DEMANDS MAY BE MADE TO RESTRICT ENTRY TO 'OUTSIDERS' — EVEN THOUGH THEY MAY BE FELLOW CITIZENS."**

⭐⭐ **The Indian example:** *"You may remember the slogan **'MUMBAI FOR MUMBAIKARS'**, which expressed such feelings. **Many similar struggles have taken place in different parts of India and the world.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The two questions this raises
> **"This raises questions about what 'FULL AND EQUAL MEMBERSHIP' really means:**
> ⭐ **(i) Does it mean that CITIZENS SHOULD ENJOY EQUAL RIGHTS AND OPPORTUNITIES WHEREVER IN THE COUNTRY THEY MAY DECIDE TO LIVE, STUDY, OR WORK?**
> ⭐ **(ii) Does it mean that ALL CITIZENS, RICH OR POOR, SHOULD ENJOY CERTAIN BASIC RIGHTS AND FACILITIES?"**

*(Question (i) is Section 6.2; question (ii) is Section 6.3.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Freedom of movement — and why it matters most to workers
⭐⭐ **"One of the rights granted to citizens in our country, and in many others, is FREEDOM OF MOVEMENT. ⭐⭐ THIS RIGHT IS OF PARTICULAR IMPORTANCE FOR WORKERS. LABOUR TENDS TO MIGRATE IN SEARCH OF JOBS WHEN OPPORTUNITIES ARE NOT AVAILABLE NEAR THEIR HOMES. Some people may even TRAVEL OUTSIDE THE COUNTRY in search of jobs."**

⭐⭐ **The chapter's four concrete examples of internal migration:**
1. ⭐ **"MARKETS FOR SKILLED AND UNSKILLED WORKERS have developed in different parts of our country."**
2. ⭐ **"For instance, I.T. WORKERS MAY FLOCK TO TOWNS LIKE BANGALORE."**
3. ⭐ **"NURSES FROM KERALA may be found all over the country."**
4. ⭐ **"The BOOMING BUILDING INDUSTRY in town attracts workers from different parts of the country. SO DO INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECTS LIKE ROAD MAKING."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The resistance — and how it escalates
⭐⭐ **"However, OFTEN RESISTANCE BUILDS UP AMONG THE LOCAL PEOPLE AGAINST SO MANY JOBS GOING TO PEOPLE FROM OUTSIDE THE AREA — SOMETIMES AT LOWER WAGES."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The escalation, stage by stage:**
1. **"A DEMAND MAY DEVELOP TO RESTRICT CERTAIN JOBS TO (i) THOSE WHO BELONG TO THE STATE, OR (ii) THOSE WHO KNOW THE LOCAL LANGUAGE."**
2. **"POLITICAL PARTIES MAY TAKE UP THE ISSUE."**
3. ⭐⭐ **"RESISTANCE COULD EVEN TAKE THE FORM OF ORGANISED VIOLENCE AGAINST 'OUTSIDERS'."**
4. ⭐ **"ALMOST EVERY REGION OF INDIA HAS EXPERIENCED SUCH MOVEMENTS."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The chapter's own question:** *"**ARE SUCH MOVEMENTS EVER JUSTIFIED?**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE INCONSISTENCY the chapter exposes — two uncomfortable double standards
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Double standard 1 — us abroad vs them here:**
> **"WE ALL BECOME INDIGNANT IF INDIAN WORKERS IN OTHER COUNTRIES ARE ILL-TREATED BY THE LOCAL POPULATION. Some of us may also feel that SKILLED AND EDUCATED WORKERS HAVE THE RIGHT TO MIGRATE FOR WORK. STATES MAY EVEN BE PROUD OF THEIR ABILITY TO ATTRACT SUCH WORKERS. ⭐⭐ BUT IF JOBS ARE SCARCE IN A REGION, LOCAL RESIDENTS MAY RESENT COMPETITION FROM 'OUTSIDERS'."**
> ⭐ **The question: "DOES THE RIGHT TO FREEDOM OF MOVEMENT INCLUDE THE RIGHT TO LIVE OR WORK IN ANY PART OF THE COUNTRY?"**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Double standard 2 — skilled vs poor migrants:**
> **"Another factor that we need to consider is that THERE MAY SOMETIMES BE A DIFFERENCE BETWEEN OUR RESPONSE TO POOR MIGRANTS AND TO SKILLED MIGRANTS. ⭐⭐ WE MAY NOT ALWAYS BE AS WELCOMING TO POOR MIGRANTS WHO MOVE INTO OUR AREAS AS WE MAY BE TO SKILLED AND AFFLUENT WORKERS."**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **The question: "THIS RAISES THE QUESTION OF WHETHER POOR AND UNSKILLED WORKERS SHOULD HAVE THE SAME RIGHT TO LIVE AND WORK ANYWHERE IN THE COUNTRY AS DO SKILLED WORKERS?"**

**⭐⭐ These two paragraphs are the analytical heart of Section 6.2 — and both are *tu quoque* arguments: they do not settle the question, they show that most people's position is INCONSISTENT. Use them in any answer on regional chauvinism.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ How such disputes should be resolved — the democratic method
⭐⭐ **"However, DISPUTES MAY SOMETIMES ARISE EVEN IN DEMOCRATIC SOCIETIES. How can such disputes be resolved?"**

⭐⭐⭐ **The right to protest, with its limit:** *"**THE RIGHT TO PROTEST IS AN ASPECT OF THE FREEDOM OF EXPRESSION GUARANTEED TO CITIZENS IN OUR CONSTITUTION — ⭐ PROVIDED PROTEST DOES NOT HARM THE LIFE OR PROPERTY OF OTHER PEOPLE OR THE STATE.**"*

⭐⭐ **The five legitimate channels:** *"Citizens are free to try and INFLUENCE PUBLIC OPINION AND GOVERNMENT POLICY by:*
1. **FORMING GROUPS**
2. **HOLDING DEMONSTRATIONS**
3. **USING THE MEDIA**
4. **APPEALING TO POLITICAL PARTIES**
5. ⭐ **BY APPROACHING THE COURTS"** — *"The courts may GIVE A DECISION on the matter, or they may URGE THE GOVERNMENT TO ADDRESS THE ISSUE."*

⭐⭐ **The realistic note:** *"**IT MAY BE A SLOW PROCESS, BUT VARYING DEGREES OF SUCCESS ARE SOMETIMES POSSIBLE.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE GUIDING PRINCIPLE AND THE OBLIGATION:**
> **"IF THE GUIDING PRINCIPLE OF PROVIDING FULL AND EQUAL MEMBERSHIP TO ALL CITIZENS IS KEPT IN MIND, IT SHOULD BE POSSIBLE TO ARRIVE AT AN ACCEPTABLE SOLUTION."**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"A BASIC PRINCIPLE OF DEMOCRACY IS THAT SUCH DISPUTES SHOULD BE SETTLED BY NEGOTIATION AND DISCUSSION RATHER THAN FORCE. THIS IS ONE OF THE OBLIGATIONS OF CITIZENSHIP."**

### ⭐⭐ Box: "Let's Think" — freedom of movement and occupation
*"Examine the arguments FOR and AGAINST freedom of movement and occupation throughout the country for citizens:*
- ⭐ **"Should the LONG-TERM INHABITANTS of a region enjoy PREFERENCE FOR JOBS AND FACILITIES?"**
- ⭐ **"Or should states be allowed to FIX QUOTAS FOR ADMISSIONS TO PROFESSIONAL COLLEGES FOR STUDENTS WHO DO NOT BELONG TO THAT STATE?"**

| **FOR freedom of movement** | **FOR local preference** |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **It is a guaranteed right of citizenship**, and **"of particular importance for WORKERS"** who must migrate when opportunities are absent at home | ⭐ Where **jobs, medical care, education, land and water are limited**, residents' access to basic facilities is genuinely threatened |
| ⭐⭐ **Consistency:** we are **"indignant if Indian workers in other countries are ill-treated"** — we cannot demand mobility abroad and deny it at home | Local people may face **competition at lower wages**, depressing their own livelihoods |
| ⭐⭐ **The class test:** restrictions bite hardest on **poor and unskilled** migrants, while **skilled and affluent** workers are welcomed — an indefensible asymmetry | ⭐ Some preference may be justified for **language-dependent public employment** (teaching, local administration) |
| ⭐ Migrants make **real contributions** (Section 6.3's list) | Long-term inhabitants may claim a stake built up over generations |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The decisive objection to restriction:** it converts fellow **citizens** into **"outsiders"**, which contradicts *"full and equal membership"* itself | |
⭐ **A balanced conclusion: some limited, transparent preference in local public employment may be defensible; blanket bars on residence, work or education for fellow citizens are not — and violence never is, since "such disputes should be settled by negotiation and discussion rather than force."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX: MARTIN LUTHER KING JR. (a very important box)

### ⭐⭐ The context
⭐⭐ **"The 1950s witnessed the emergence of CIVIL RIGHTS MOVEMENTS against inequalities that existed between BLACK AND WHITE POPULATIONS IN MANY OF THE SOUTHERN STATES OF THE USA."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The SEGREGATION LAWS:** *"Such inequalities were maintained in these states by a set of laws called **SEGREGATION LAWS**, through which **THE BLACK PEOPLE WERE DENIED MANY CIVIL AND POLITICAL RIGHTS.** ⭐ **These laws CREATED SEPARATE AREAS FOR COLOURED AND WHITE PEOPLE in various civic amenities like RAILWAYS, BUSES, THEATRES, HOUSING, HOTELS, RESTAURANTS**, etc."*

⭐ **"MARTIN LUTHER KING JR. WAS A BLACK LEADER OF THE MOVEMENT AGAINST THESE LAWS."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ King's THREE arguments against segregation (learn all three — this is the examinable core)
| # | Argument | The chapter's words |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1** | **The argument from EQUAL WORTH AND DIGNITY** | **"First, in terms of SELF-WORTH AND DIGNITY, EVERY HUMAN PERSON IN THE WORLD IS EQUAL, REGARDLESS OF ONE'S RACE OR COLOUR."** *(The Kantian ground — see Ch. 5.)* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **2** | **The argument from PSYCHOLOGICAL HARM — "social leprosy"** | **"Second, King argued that SEGREGATION IS LIKE 'SOCIAL LEPROSY' ON THE BODY POLITIC, BECAUSE IT INFLICTS DEEP PSYCHOLOGICAL WOUNDS ON THE PEOPLE WHO SUFFER as a result of such laws."** ⭐⭐ **And, remarkably, it harms the oppressor too:** *"King argued that **THE PRACTICE OF SEGREGATION DIMINISHES THE QUALITY OF LIFE FOR THE WHITE COMMUNITY ALSO.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **3** | **The argument from LOST COOPERATION** | **"Thirdly, THE SEGREGATION LAWS CREATE ARTIFICIAL BOUNDARIES BETWEEN PEOPLE AND PREVENT THEM FROM COOPERATING WITH EACH OTHER FOR THE OVERALL BENEFIT OF THE COUNTRY."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ King's two illustrations of how segregation harmed the whites too (memorable and often asked)
> ⭐⭐ **"He illustrates this point by examples:**
> **(i) THE WHITE COMMUNITY, INSTEAD OF ALLOWING THE BLACK PEOPLE TO ENTER SOME COMMUNITY PARKS AS WAS DIRECTED BY THE COURT, DECIDED TO CLOSE THEM.**
> **(ii) Similarly, SOME BASEBALL TEAMS HAD TO BE DISBANDED, AS THE AUTHORITIES DID NOT WANT TO ACCEPT BLACK PLAYERS."**

**⭐⭐ The logic is powerful and worth grasping: rather than share, the segregationists DESTROYED the good itself — closing the parks, disbanding the teams. So prejudice cost the privileged their own parks and their own sport. This is the strongest possible answer to anyone who thinks discrimination is costless to the discriminator.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ King's method — non-violence
⭐⭐ **"For these reasons, King argued that THESE LAWS SHOULD BE ABOLISHED. ⭐ HE GAVE A CALL FOR PEACEFUL AND NON-VIOLENT RESISTANCE AGAINST THE SEGREGATION LAWS."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **His own words:** **"WE MUST NOT ALLOW OUR CREATIVE PROTEST TO DEGENERATE INTO PHYSICAL VIOLENCE."**

*(Note the resonance with the chapter's own principle that *"disputes should be settled by negotiation and discussion rather than force"* — and with **Gandhi's** influence on King.)*

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 3 — EQUAL RIGHTS: the urban poor

⭐⭐ **The question of this section:** *"whether full and equal membership means that **ALL CITIZENS, RICH OR POOR, SHOULD BE GUARANTEED CERTAIN BASIC RIGHTS AND A MINIMUM STANDARD OF LIVING BY THE STATE.**"*

⭐ **"To discuss this issue, we will look at one set of people — THE URBAN POOR. ⭐ DEALING WITH THE PROBLEM OF THE POOR IN TOWNS IS ONE OF THE URGENT PROBLEMS FACING THE GOVERNMENT TODAY."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ How slum-dwellers are perceived vs what they actually do
| **How they are seen** | **What they actually contribute** |
|---|---|
⭐⭐ **"There is a LARGE POPULATION OF SLUM-DWELLERS AND SQUATTERS IN EVERY CITY IN INDIA. ⭐ ALTHOUGH THEY MAY DO NECESSARY AND USEFUL WORK, OFTEN AT LOW WAGES, THEY ARE OFTEN VIEWED AS UNWELCOME VISITORS BY THE REST OF THE TOWN POPULATION. ⭐⭐ THEY MAY BE BLAMED (i) FOR STRAINING THE RESOURCES OF THE CITY OR (ii) FOR SPREADING CRIME AND DISEASE."** | ⭐⭐⭐ **"SLUM DWELLERS MAKE A SIGNIFICANT CONTRIBUTION TO THE ECONOMY THROUGH THEIR LABOUR. They may be HAWKERS, PETTY TRADERS, SCAVENGERS, OR DOMESTIC WORKERS, PLUMBERS, OR MECHANICS, among other professions. ⭐ SMALL BUSINESSES SUCH AS CANE WEAVING, OR TEXTILE PRINTING, OR TAILORING, MAY ALSO DEVELOP IN SLUMS."** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The conditions in slums
> ⭐⭐ **"THE CONDITIONS IN SLUMS ARE OFTEN SHOCKING. MANY PEOPLE MAY BE CRAMMED INTO SMALL ROOMS WITH (i) NO PRIVATE TOILETS, (ii) RUNNING WATER, OR (iii) SANITATION. ⭐ LIFE AND PROPERTY ARE INSECURE IN A SLUM."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The injustice sharpened:** *"**THE CITY PROBABLY SPENDS RELATIVELY LITTLE ON PROVIDING SLUM-DWELLERS WITH SERVICES SUCH AS SANITATION OR WATER SUPPLY.**"*

**⭐⭐ The argument to draw out: slum-dwellers contribute labour the city depends on, yet receive the least public spending — and are blamed for "straining the resources of the city." That inversion is the citizenship failure.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Growing awareness — and the street vendors policy
⭐⭐ **"AWARENESS ABOUT THE CONDITION OF THE URBAN POOR IS GROWING among (i) GOVERNMENTS, (ii) N.G.O's AND OTHER AGENCIES, AND (iii) AMONG THE SLUM-DWELLERS THEMSELVES."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The policy example (learn the date):**
> **"For instance, A NATIONAL POLICY ON URBAN STREET VENDORS WAS FRAMED IN JANUARY 2004."**
> ⭐ **The problem it addressed: "THERE ARE LAKHS OF STREET VENDORS IN BIG CITIES, AND THEY OFTEN FACE HARASSMENT FROM THE POLICE AND TOWN AUTHORITIES."**
> ⭐⭐ **What it aimed to do: "The policy was intended to provide RECOGNITION AND REGULATION FOR VENDORS, TO ENABLE THEM TO CARRY ON THEIR PROFESSION WITHOUT HARASSMENT — SO LONG AS THEY OBEYED GOVERNMENT REGULATIONS."**

*(⭐ The chapter's "Let's Do It" asks you to **"find out about the STREET VENDORS (PROTECTION OF LIVELIHOOD AND REGULATION OF STREET VENDING) ACT, 2014"** — the 2004 policy eventually became **law in 2014**, giving vendors statutory protection, town vending committees and a survey-and-certificate system.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The obstacle even to the most basic right — the VOTE
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"Slum-dwellers also are becoming aware of their rights and are beginning to organise to demand them. They have sometimes even APPROACHED THE COURTS. ⭐⭐ EVEN A BASIC POLITICAL RIGHT LIKE THE RIGHT TO VOTE MAY BE DIFFICULT FOR THEM TO EXERCISE, BECAUSE TO BE INCLUDED IN THE LIST OF VOTERS A FIXED ADDRESS IS REQUIRED — AND SQUATTERS AND PAVEMENT DWELLERS MAY FIND IT DIFFICULT TO PROVIDE THIS."**

**⭐⭐⭐ This is the single best illustration in the chapter of Exercise Q2 — "all citizens may be granted equal rights but all may not be able to equally exercise them." The right to vote is formally universal, yet an administrative requirement (a fixed address) silently excludes the homeless.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The other marginalised group — tribal people and forest dwellers
⭐⭐ **"Among other groups of people who are becoming MARGINALISED in our society are THE TRIBAL PEOPLE AND FOREST DWELLERS. ⭐ THESE PEOPLE ARE DEPENDENT ON ACCESS TO FORESTS AND OTHER NATURAL RESOURCES TO MAINTAIN THEIR WAY OF LIFE."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The three threats they face:**
1. ⭐ **"Many of them face threats to their way of life and livelihood BECAUSE OF THE PRESSURE OF INCREASING POPULATIONS AND THE SEARCH FOR LAND AND RESOURCES TO MAINTAIN THEM."**
2. ⭐⭐ **"PRESSURES FROM COMMERCIAL INTERESTS WANTING TO MINE THE RESOURCES WHICH MAY EXIST IN FORESTS OR COASTS."**
3. ⭐ **"...AS DOES THE TOURIST INDUSTRY."**

⭐⭐ **The government's dilemma:** *"**GOVERNMENTS ARE STRUGGLING WITH THE PROBLEM OF HOW TO PROTECT THESE PEOPLE AND THEIR HABITAT WITHOUT AT THE SAME TIME ENDANGERING DEVELOPMENT OF THE COUNTRY.** ⭐ **THIS IS AN ISSUE THAT AFFECTS ALL CITIZENS, NOT JUST TRIBAL PEOPLE.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE KEY PRINCIPLE — equal rights ≠ uniform policies
> ⭐⭐ **"To try and ensure equal rights and opportunities for all citizens CANNOT BE A SIMPLE MATTER FOR ANY GOVERNMENT. ⭐ DIFFERENT GROUPS OF PEOPLE MAY HAVE DIFFERENT NEEDS AND PROBLEMS, AND THE RIGHTS OF ONE GROUP MAY CONFLICT WITH THE RIGHTS OF ANOTHER."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"EQUAL RIGHTS FOR CITIZENS NEED NOT MEAN THAT UNIFORM POLICIES HAVE TO BE APPLIED TO ALL PEOPLE, SINCE DIFFERENT GROUPS OF PEOPLE MAY HAVE DIFFERENT NEEDS."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"IF THE PURPOSE IS NOT JUST TO MAKE POLICIES WHICH WOULD APPLY IN THE SAME WAY TO ALL PEOPLE, BUT TO MAKE PEOPLE MORE EQUAL, THE DIFFERENT NEEDS AND CLAIMS OF PEOPLE WOULD HAVE TO BE TAKEN INTO ACCOUNT WHEN FRAMING POLICIES."**

*(This is Chapter 3's **"identical treatment vs treating as equal"** and Chapter 4's **third principle of justice**, restated in the language of citizenship.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The evolving character of citizenship
> ⭐⭐ **"What should become clear from this discussion is that CHANGES IN THE WORLD SITUATION, THE ECONOMY, AND SOCIETY DEMAND NEW INTERPRETATIONS OF THE MEANING AND RIGHTS OF CITIZENSHIP."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"THE FORMAL LAWS REGARDING CITIZENSHIP ONLY FORM THE STARTING POINT, AND THE INTERPRETATION OF LAWS IS CONSTANTLY EVOLVING."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"While answers to some of the problems which may arise may not be easy to find, THE CONCEPT OF EQUAL CITIZENSHIP WOULD MEAN THAT PROVIDING EQUAL RIGHTS AND PROTECTION TO ALL CITIZENS SHOULD BE ONE OF THE GUIDING PRINCIPLES OF GOVERNMENT POLICIES."**

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX: T.H. MARSHALL — CITIZENSHIP, EQUALITY AND RIGHTS (a crucial box)

### ⭐⭐⭐ Marshall's definition
⭐⭐ **"Citizenship is NOT MERELY A LEGAL CONCEPT. It is also CLOSELY RELATED TO LARGER NOTIONS OF EQUALITY AND RIGHTS. A widely accepted formulation of this relationship was provided by the BRITISH SOCIOLOGIST T.H. MARSHALL (1893-1981) in his book *CITIZENSHIP AND SOCIAL CLASS* (1950)."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Marshall's definition, verbatim:** **"Citizenship is 'A STATUS BESTOWED ON THOSE WHO ARE FULL MEMBERS OF A COMMUNITY. ALL WHO POSSESS THE STATUS ARE EQUAL WITH RESPECT TO THE RIGHTS AND DUTIES WITH WHICH THE STATUS IS ENDOWED.'"**

### ⭐⭐⭐ EQUALITY as the key concept — and its two implications
> ⭐⭐ **"THE KEY CONCEPT IN MARSHALL'S IDEA OF CITIZENSHIP IS THAT OF 'EQUALITY'. THIS IMPLIES TWO THINGS:**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **FIRST, THAT THE QUALITY OF THE GIVEN RIGHTS AND DUTIES IMPROVES.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **SECOND, THAT THE QUANTITY OF PEOPLE UPON WHOM THEY ARE BESTOWED GROWS."**

**⭐⭐ Learn this as the QUALITY–QUANTITY pair: citizenship expands in two directions at once — rights get DEEPER (quality) and reach MORE PEOPLE (quantity). It is a compact statement of the entire history of citizenship struggles in this chapter.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Marshall's THREE kinds of rights
| Kind | What it does |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **CIVIL rights** | **"Protect the individual's LIFE, LIBERTY AND PROPERTY"** |
| ⭐⭐ **POLITICAL rights** | **"Enable the individual to PARTICIPATE IN THE PROCESS OF GOVERNANCE"** |
| ⭐⭐ **SOCIAL rights** | **"Give the individual ACCESS TO EDUCATION AND EMPLOYMENT"** |

⭐⭐⭐ **"TOGETHER THEY MAKE IT POSSIBLE FOR THE CITIZEN TO LEAD A LIFE OF DIGNITY."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Citizenship as the counter to CLASS
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"MARSHALL SAW SOCIAL CLASS AS A 'SYSTEM OF INEQUALITY'. ⭐⭐ CITIZENSHIP ENSURES EQUALITY BY COUNTERING THE DIVISIVE EFFECTS OF CLASS HIERARCHY. IT THUS FACILITATES THE CREATION OF A BETTER-INTEGRATED AND HARMONIOUS COMMUNITY."**

**⭐⭐ This is Marshall's central thesis and it is highly examinable: CLASS divides, CITIZENSHIP integrates. The equal status of citizen cuts across, and partly compensates for, the inequalities of the market.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ BOX: *OLGA TELLIS v. BOMBAY MUNICIPAL CORPORATION* (1985) — a landmark case

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **What it was** | **"The Supreme Court gave an important decision regarding THE RIGHTS OF SLUM-DWELLERS IN BOMBAY, in response to a PUBLIC INTEREST LITIGATION filed by a SOCIAL ACTIVIST, OLGA TELLIS, against BOMBAY MUNICIPAL CORPORATION IN 1985."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The petition's claim** | **"The petition claimed THE RIGHT TO LIVE ON PAVEMENTS OR IN SLUMS, BECAUSE THERE WAS NO ALTERNATIVE ACCOMMODATION AVAILABLE CLOSE TO THEIR PLACE OF WORK. ⭐ IF THEY WERE FORCED TO MOVE, THEY WOULD LOSE THEIR LIVELIHOOD AS WELL."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The judgment** | **"The Supreme Court said: 'ARTICLE 21 OF THE CONSTITUTION, WHICH GUARANTEED THE RIGHT TO LIFE, INCLUDED THE RIGHT TO LIVELIHOOD. THEREFORE, IF PAVEMENT DWELLERS WERE TO BE EVICTED, THEY SHOULD FIRST BE PROVIDED ALTERNATIVE ACCOMMODATION UNDER THE RIGHT TO SHELTER.'"** |

> **⭐⭐ Why this case matters so much: it is the exact point at which the *Indian Constitution at Work* material (Ch. 6 — PIL and the judicial expansion of Article 21 to include dignity, shelter and livelihood) meets the citizenship material here. The pavement dweller's claim was not charity but a RIGHT, established through a PIL brought by someone else on their behalf.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 4 — CITIZEN AND NATION

### ⭐⭐ The nation state
⭐⭐ **"The concept of NATION STATE evolved in THE MODERN PERIOD. ⭐ One of the earliest assertions regarding (i) THE SOVEREIGNTY OF THE NATION STATE and (ii) DEMOCRATIC RIGHTS OF CITIZENS was made by THE REVOLUTIONARIES IN FRANCE IN 1789."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The nation state's claim:** *"**Nation states claim that THEIR BOUNDARIES DEFINE NOT JUST A TERRITORY BUT ALSO A UNIQUE CULTURE AND SHARED HISTORY.**"*

⭐ **How national identity is expressed:** *"through symbols like **A FLAG, NATIONAL ANTHEM, NATIONAL LANGUAGE, OR CERTAIN CEREMONIAL PRACTICES**, among other things."*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The gap between the inclusive ideal and actual practice
⭐⭐ **"MOST MODERN STATES INCLUDE PEOPLE OF DIFFERENT RELIGIONS, LANGUAGES, AND CULTURAL TRADITIONS. ⭐ BUT THE NATIONAL IDENTITY OF A DEMOCRATIC STATE IS SUPPOSED TO PROVIDE CITIZENS WITH A POLITICAL IDENTITY THAT CAN BE SHARED BY ALL THE MEMBERS OF THE STATE."**

⭐⭐ **"Democratic states usually try to define their identity SO THAT IT IS AS INCLUSIVE AS POSSIBLE — that is, WHICH ALLOWS ALL CITIZENS TO IDENTIFY THEMSELVES AS PART OF THE NATION."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **BUT the reality:** **"IN PRACTICE, MOST COUNTRIES TEND TO DEFINE THEIR IDENTITY IN A WAY WHICH MAKES IT EASIER FOR SOME CITIZENS TO IDENTIFY WITH THE STATE THAN OTHERS. ⭐⭐ IT MAY ALSO MAKE IT EASIER FOR THE STATE TO EXTEND CITIZENSHIP TO SOME PEOPLE AND NOT OTHERS. ⭐ THIS WOULD BE AS TRUE OF THE UNITED STATES, WHICH PRIDES ITSELF ON BEING A COUNTRY OF IMMIGRANTS, AS ANY OTHER COUNTRY."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ CASE STUDY 1 — FRANCE: the public/private problem
⭐⭐ **"FRANCE is a country which claims to be BOTH SECULAR AND INCLUSIVE. It includes not only people of EUROPEAN ORIGIN but also citizens who originally came from other areas such as NORTH AFRICA."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The French model of assimilation:** *"**CULTURE AND LANGUAGE ARE IMPORTANT FEATURES OF ITS NATIONAL IDENTITY, AND ALL CITIZENS ARE EXPECTED TO ASSIMILATE INTO IT IN THE PUBLIC ASPECTS OF THEIR LIVES. ⭐ THEY MAY, HOWEVER, RETAIN THEIR PERSONAL BELIEFS AND PRACTICES IN THEIR PRIVATE LIVES.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The difficulty:** *"**This may seem like a reasonable policy, BUT IT IS NOT ALWAYS SIMPLE TO DEFINE WHAT IS PUBLIC AND WHAT IS PRIVATE, AND THIS HAS GIVEN RISE TO SOME CONTROVERSIES. ⭐ RELIGIOUS BELIEF IS SUPPOSED TO BELONG TO THE PRIVATE SPHERE OF CITIZENS, BUT SOMETIMES RELIGIOUS SYMBOLS AND PRACTICES MAY ENTER INTO THEIR PUBLIC LIVES.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **The two examples (learn both):**
1. **"The demand of SIKH SCHOOL BOYS IN FRANCE TO WEAR THE TURBAN TO SCHOOL"**
2. **"...and of MUSLIM GIRLS TO WEAR THE HEAD SCARF WITH THEIR SCHOOL UNIFORMS"**

⭐⭐ **"THIS WAS DISALLOWED BY SOME SCHOOLS ON THE GROUND THAT IT INVOLVED BRINGING RELIGIOUS SYMBOLS INTO THE PUBLIC SPHERE OF STATE EDUCATION."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE DECISIVE CRITICISM — the unequal burden of "neutrality":**
> **"THOSE WHOSE RELIGIONS DID NOT DEMAND SUCH PRACTICES NATURALLY DID NOT FACE THE SAME PROBLEM. ⭐⭐⭐ CLEARLY, ASSIMILATION INTO THE NATIONAL CULTURE WOULD BE EASIER FOR SOME GROUPS THAN FOR OTHERS."**
>
> **⭐⭐ This is the crucial analytical point: a rule that looks perfectly neutral on its face — "no religious symbols in state schools" — falls entirely on those whose faith requires visible observance, and not at all on others. Formal neutrality can produce substantive inequality.**

*(⭐ The chapter's related **"Let's Debate"** prompt: *"**It is not appropriate for schools, or any other public agencies like the army, to insist on a common uniform and to ban the display of religious symbols such as the turban.**"* — argue it with the French case, and with Ch. 2's dress-code framework: **who** imposes it, is it **excessive**, does it **diminish the ways people express themselves**?)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ CASE STUDY 2 — ISRAEL and GERMANY: criteria for granting citizenship
⭐⭐⭐ **"THE CRITERIA FOR GRANTING CITIZENSHIP TO NEW APPLICANTS VARIES FROM COUNTRY TO COUNTRY. ⭐ In countries such as ISRAEL, OR GERMANY, FACTORS LIKE RELIGION, OR ETHNIC ORIGIN, MAY BE GIVEN PRIORITY WHEN GRANTING CITIZENSHIP."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The German case — Turkish workers' children:**
> **"In GERMANY there has been A PERSISTENT DEMAND FROM TURKISH WORKERS — WHO WERE AT ONE TIME ENCOURAGED TO COME AND WORK IN GERMANY — THAT THEIR CHILDREN, WHO HAVE BEEN BORN AND BROUGHT UP IN GERMANY, SHOULD AUTOMATICALLY BE GRANTED CITIZENSHIP. ⭐ THIS IS STILL BEING DEBATED."**

⭐⭐ **The chapter's summing-up:** *"These are only a few examples of **THE KINDS OF RESTRICTIONS WHICH MAY BE PLACED ON CITIZENSHIP EVEN IN DEMOCRATIC COUNTRIES WHICH PRIDE THEMSELVES ON BEING INCLUSIVE.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ CASE STUDY 3 — INDIA: the unique experiment
⭐⭐ **"INDIA DEFINES ITSELF AS A SECULAR, DEMOCRATIC, NATION STATE."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The nationalist inheritance:** *"**THE MOVEMENT FOR INDEPENDENCE WAS A BROAD BASED ONE, AND DELIBERATE ATTEMPTS WERE MADE TO BIND TOGETHER PEOPLE OF DIFFERENT RELIGIONS, REGIONS AND CULTURES.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **On Partition — a very important formulation:** *"**True, PARTITION OF THE COUNTRY DID TAKE PLACE IN 1947, WHEN DIFFERENCES WITH THE MUSLIM LEAGUE COULD NOT BE RESOLVED — ⭐⭐ BUT THIS ONLY STRENGTHENED THE RESOLVE OF INDIAN NATIONAL LEADERS TO MAINTAIN THE SECULAR AND INCLUSIVE CHARACTER OF THE INDIAN NATION STATE THEY WERE COMMITTED TO BUILD. THIS RESOLVE WAS EMBODIED IN THE CONSTITUTION.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The diversities the Constitution had to accommodate (learn all four examples)
⭐⭐ **"The Indian Constitution ATTEMPTED TO ACCOMMODATE A VERY DIVERSE SOCIETY. To mention just a few of these diversities, it attempted to provide FULL AND EQUAL CITIZENSHIP to groups as different as:"**
1. ⭐ **"The SCHEDULED CASTES AND SCHEDULED TRIBES"**
2. ⭐ **"MANY WOMEN WHO HAD NOT PREVIOUSLY ENJOYED EQUAL RIGHTS"**
3. ⭐⭐ **"SOME REMOTE COMMUNITIES IN THE ANDAMAN AND NICOBAR ISLANDS WHO HAD HAD LITTLE CONTACT WITH MODERN CIVILIZATION"**
4. **"...and many others"**

⭐⭐ **"It also attempted to FIND A PLACE FOR THE DIFFERENT LANGUAGES, RELIGIONS AND PRACTICES FOUND IN DIFFERENT PARTS OF THE COUNTRY."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE UNIQUE BALANCE INDIA ATTEMPTED:**
> **"IT HAD TO PROVIDE EQUAL RIGHTS TO ALL WITHOUT AT THE SAME TIME FORCING PEOPLE TO GIVE UP THEIR PERSONAL BELIEFS, LANGUAGES OR CULTURAL PRACTICES. ⭐⭐ IT WAS THEREFORE A UNIQUE EXPERIMENT WHICH WAS UNDERTAKEN THROUGH THE CONSTITUTION."**
>
> **⭐⭐ Note the deliberate contrast with FRANCE: France requires ASSIMILATION in the public sphere; India provides equal rights WITHOUT requiring people to surrender their beliefs, languages or practices. That contrast is the single most examinable comparison in this chapter.**

⭐ **The symbol:** *"**THE REPUBLIC DAY PARADE IN DELHI SYMBOLISES THE ATTEMPT OF THE STATE TO INCLUDE PEOPLE OF DIFFERENT REGIONS, CULTURES AND RELIGIONS.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ India's constitutional provisions on citizenship
| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Where they are** | **"The provisions about citizenship in the Constitution can be found in PART TWO, and in SUBSEQUENT LAWS PASSED BY PARLIAMENT."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Its character** | **"The Constitution adopted an ESSENTIALLY DEMOCRATIC AND INCLUSIVE NOTION OF CITIZENSHIP."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The FIVE modes of acquiring it** | **"In India, citizenship can be acquired by (i) BIRTH, (ii) DESCENT, (iii) REGISTRATION, (iv) NATURALISATION, or (v) INCLUSION OF TERRITORY."** |
| ⭐ **Rights and obligations** | **"The RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF CITIZENS ARE LISTED IN THE CONSTITUTION."** |
| ⭐⭐ **Non-discrimination** | **"There is also a provision that THE STATE SHOULD NOT DISCRIMINATE AGAINST CITIZENS ON GROUNDS ONLY OF RELIGION, RACE, CASTE, SEX, PLACE OF BIRTH, OR ANY OF THEM."** |
| ⭐ **Minorities** | **"THE RIGHTS OF RELIGIOUS AND LINGUISTIC MINORITIES ARE ALSO PROTECTED."** |

**Mnemonic for the five modes: "B-D-R-N-T" → Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Territory.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ CITIZENSHIP AS A PROJECT — the chapter's most important conclusion
⭐⭐ **"However, EVEN SUCH INCLUSIVE PROVISIONS HAVE GIVEN RISE TO STRUGGLES AND CONTROVERSIES."**

⭐⭐ **The three struggles named:** *"**(i) THE WOMEN'S MOVEMENT, (ii) THE DALIT MOVEMENT, or (iii) STRUGGLES OF PEOPLE DISPLACED BY DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS** represent only a few of the struggles being waged by **people who feel that they are being DENIED FULL RIGHTS OF CITIZENSHIP.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **"THE EXPERIENCE OF INDIA INDICATES THAT DEMOCRATIC CITIZENSHIP IN ANY COUNTRY IS A PROJECT, AN IDEAL TO WORK TOWARDS."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"NEW ISSUES ARE CONSTANTLY BEING RAISED AS SOCIETIES CHANGE AND NEW DEMANDS ARE MADE BY GROUPS WHO FEEL THEY ARE BEING MARGINALISED. ⭐ IN A DEMOCRATIC STATE THESE DEMANDS HAVE TO BE NEGOTIATED."**

*(This is the answer to Exercise Q6: **citizenship is a PROJECT, not an accomplished fact.**)*

### ⭐⭐ Box: "Let's Think" — the Zimbabwe land question
*The chapter presents official figures: "**Some 4,400 WHITE FAMILIES owned 32 PER CENT of agricultural land — about 10m hectares. About ONE MILLION BLACK PEASANT FAMILIES own just 16m hectares, that is 38 PER CENT of the land. ⭐ While the land that is with the white families is FERTILE AND IRRIGATED, the land in the hands of the black population is LESS FERTILE AND UNIRRIGATED. ⭐⭐ While tracing the history of land ownership, it is very obvious that A CENTURY AGO THE WHITES HAD TAKEN THE FERTILE LAND FROM THE NATIVE PEOPLE. ⭐ Whites have now been in Zimbabwe FOR GENERATIONS and CONSIDER THEMSELVES AS ZIMBABWEANS. The total population of whites in Zimbabwe is JUST 0.06 PER CENT of the population. In the year 1997, the President of Zimbabwe, MUGABE, announced the plans to TAKE OVER AROUND 1500 FARMS.**"*

⭐⭐⭐ **"What ideas from citizenship would you use to SUPPORT OR OPPOSE the claims of Black and White Citizens of Zimbabwe?"**
| **Ideas supporting the BLACK citizens' claim** | **Ideas supporting the WHITE citizens' claim** |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Full and equal membership** is empty if **0.06% of the population holds 32% of the land — and the fertile, irrigated portion at that** | ⭐⭐ **They are CITIZENS too** — *"whites have now been in Zimbabwe for generations and consider themselves as Zimbabweans"*; **equality of rights and status is a basic right of citizenship** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The historical injustice is undisputed:** *"a century ago the whites HAD TAKEN the fertile land from the native people"* — this is **entrenched inequality** originating in dispossession | ⭐⭐ **Civil rights protect "life, liberty and PROPERTY"** (Marshall); arbitrary confiscation violates them |
| ⭐ **Social rights** require **access to the means of livelihood**; unirrigated land for a million families is not equal opportunity | ⭐ Citizenship cannot be **conditional on ancestry or race** — that is the very principle used against apartheid |
| ⭐ **Equal rights need not mean uniform policies**; redistribution may be needed **"to make people more equal"** | ⭐⭐ **"Disputes should be settled by NEGOTIATION AND DISCUSSION RATHER THAN FORCE"** — a **take-over of 1,500 farms** bypasses that |
⭐⭐ **The balanced position: the historical claim for redistribution is strong, but the METHOD matters — compensated, legally regulated land reform respects both the black majority's social rights and the white minority's status as citizens. Note the parallel with India's own land reforms (Ch. 4).**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 5 — UNIVERSAL CITIZENSHIP AND STATELESS PEOPLE

### ⭐⭐ The images of displacement
⭐ **"When we think of refugees, or illegal migrants, many images may come to mind:"**
1. ⭐ **"People from ASIA OR AFRICA who have PAID AGENTS TO SMUGGLE THEM INTO EUROPE OR AMERICA. ⭐ THE RISKS ARE HIGH BUT THEY SEEM WILLING TO MAKE THE EFFORT."**
2. ⭐ **"People DISPLACED BY WAR OR FAMINE."**

⭐⭐ **The examples named:** *"**Refugees in the DARFUR region of SUDAN, PALESTINIANS, BURMESE or BANGLADESHIS** — the examples are many. ⭐ **All these are people who have been FORCED TO BECOME REFUGEES IN THEIR OWN, OR NEIGHBOURING COUNTRIES.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The tension at the heart of "universal" citizenship
> ⭐⭐ **"We often assume that FULL MEMBERSHIP OF A STATE SHOULD BE AVAILABLE (i) TO ALL THOSE WHO ORDINARILY LIVE AND WORK IN THE COUNTRY, AS WELL AS (ii) TO THOSE WHO APPLY FOR CITIZENSHIP."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"BUT ALTHOUGH MANY STATES MAY SUPPORT THE IDEA OF UNIVERSAL AND INCLUSIVE CITIZENSHIP, EACH OF THEM ALSO FIXES CRITERIA FOR THE GRANT OF CITIZENSHIP. These would generally be WRITTEN INTO THE CONSTITUTION AND LAWS OF THE COUNTRY. ⭐⭐ STATES USE THEIR POWER TO KEEP UNWANTED VISITORS OUT."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ How people become STATELESS
⭐⭐ **"However, IN SPITE OF RESTRICTIONS — EVEN THE BUILDING OF WALLS OR FENCES — CONSIDERABLE MIGRATION OF PEOPLES STILL TAKES PLACE IN THE WORLD. People may be DISPLACED BY WARS, OR PERSECUTION, FAMINE, OR OTHER REASONS."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE DEFINITION:** **"IF NO STATE IS WILLING TO ACCEPT THEM AND THEY CANNOT RETURN HOME, THEY BECOME STATELESS PEOPLES OR REFUGEES."**

⭐⭐⭐ **What statelessness means in practice — the four deprivations:**
1. **"They may be forced to LIVE IN CAMPS, or AS ILLEGAL MIGRANTS"**
2. ⭐ **"Often they CANNOT LEGALLY WORK"**
3. ⭐ **"...or EDUCATE THEIR CHILDREN"**
4. ⭐ **"...or ACQUIRE PROPERTY"**

⭐⭐ **"THE PROBLEM IS SO GREAT THAT THE U.N. HAS APPOINTED A HIGH COMMISSIONER FOR REFUGEES TO TRY TO HELP THEM."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The dilemma for states — humanitarian vs security
⭐⭐ **"Decisions regarding HOW MANY PEOPLE CAN BE ABSORBED AS CITIZENS in a country poses A DIFFICULT HUMANITARIAN AND POLITICAL PROBLEM for many states."**

⭐⭐⭐ **Both sides stated:** *"**MANY COUNTRIES HAVE A POLICY OF ACCEPTING THOSE FLEEING FROM PERSECUTION OR WAR. ⭐ BUT THEY MAY NOT WANT TO ACCEPT AN UNMANAGEABLE NUMBER OF PEOPLE, OR EXPOSE THE COUNTRY TO SECURITY RISKS.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ India's record
⭐⭐⭐ **"INDIA PRIDES ITSELF ON PROVIDING REFUGE TO PERSECUTED PEOPLES, AS IT DID WITH THE DALAI LAMA AND HIS FOLLOWERS IN 1959."**

⭐⭐ **"ENTRY OF PEOPLE FROM NEIGHBOURING COUNTRIES HAS TAKEN PLACE ALONG ALL THE BORDERS OF THE INDIAN STATE, AND THE PROCESS CONTINUES."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The uncomfortable reality:** *"**MANY OF THESE PEOPLE REMAIN AS STATELESS PEOPLES FOR MANY YEARS OR GENERATIONS — LIVING IN CAMPS, OR AS ILLEGAL MIGRANTS. ⭐⭐ ONLY A RELATIVELY FEW OF THEM ARE EVENTUALLY GRANTED CITIZENSHIP.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The challenge to democratic citizenship
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"Such problems POSE A CHALLENGE TO THE PROMISE OF DEMOCRATIC CITIZENSHIP — WHICH IS THAT THE RIGHTS AND IDENTITY OF CITIZEN WOULD BE AVAILABLE TO ALL PEOPLE IN THE CONTEMPORARY WORLD."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE MOST HAUNTING SENTENCE IN THE CHAPTER:** **"ALTHOUGH MANY PEOPLE CANNOT ACHIEVE CITIZENSHIP OF A STATE OF THEIR CHOICE, NO ALTERNATIVE IDENTITY EXISTS FOR THEM."**

⭐⭐ **"The problem of stateless people is AN IMPORTANT ONE CONFRONTING THE WORLD TODAY. ⭐ BORDERS OF STATES ARE STILL BEING REDEFINED BY WAR OR POLITICAL DISPUTES, and for the people caught up in such disputes THE CONSEQUENCES MAY BE SEVERE. ⭐⭐ THEY MAY LOSE (i) THEIR HOMES, (ii) POLITICAL IDENTITIES, AND (iii) SECURITY — AND BE FORCED TO MIGRATE."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The three questions that lead into the final section:**
1. **"CAN CITIZENSHIP PROVIDE A SOLUTION TO THE PROBLEMS OF SUCH PEOPLE?"**
2. **"IF NOT, WHAT KIND OF ALTERNATIVE IDENTITY CAN BE PROVIDED TODAY?"**
3. ⭐ **"DO WE NEED TO TRY AND EVOLVE A MORE GENUINELY UNIVERSAL IDENTITY THAN NATIONAL CITIZENSHIP?"**

## ⭐⭐⭐ PART 6 — GLOBAL CITIZENSHIP

### ⭐⭐ The three statements the chapter opens with
1. ⭐ **"There was an OUTPOURING OF SYMPATHY AND HELP FOR THE VICTIMS OF THE TSUNAMI which affected a number of countries in SOUTH ASIA IN 2004."**
2. ⭐ **"INTERNATIONAL NETWORKS LINK TERRORISTS TODAY."**
3. ⭐ **"The UNITED NATIONS IS WORKING WITH DIFFERENT STATES TO TRY AND PREVENT THE SPREAD OF BIRD FLU AND THE POSSIBLE EMERGENCE OF A HUMAN VIRAL PANDEMIC."**

⭐⭐ **"What is common to the statements given above? What do they tell us about the world in which we live today?"** → ⭐⭐ **All three describe problems and sympathies that CROSS NATIONAL BOUNDARIES — a disaster, a threat, and a disease — none of which any single state can handle alone. Note the balance: one is compassionate, one is malign, one is technical. Interconnection is not automatically benign.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The interconnected world
⭐⭐ **"WE LIVE TODAY IN AN INTERCONNECTED WORLD. New means of communication such as THE INTERNET, AND TELEVISION, AND CELL PHONES have brought A MAJOR CHANGE IN THE WAY IN WHICH WE UNDERSTAND OUR WORLD."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The transformation in speed:** *"**IN THE PAST IT MIGHT HAVE TAKEN MONTHS FOR NEWS ABOUT DEVELOPMENTS IN ONE PART OF THE WORLD TO BECOME KNOWN IN OTHER PARTS. BUT NEW MODES OF COMMUNICATION HAVE PUT US INTO IMMEDIATE CONTACT WITH DEVELOPMENTS IN DIFFERENT PARTS OF THE GLOBE. ⭐ WE CAN WATCH DISASTERS AND WARS ON OUR TELEVISION SCREENS AS THEY ARE TAKING PLACE.**"*

⭐⭐ **The consequence:** *"**THIS HAS HELPED TO DEVELOP SYMPATHIES AND SHARED CONCERNS AMONG PEOPLE IN DIFFERENT COUNTRIES OF THE WORLD.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The argument FOR global citizenship
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"Supporters of global citizenship argue that ALTHOUGH A WORLD COMMUNITY AND GLOBAL SOCIETY DOES NOT YET EXIST, PEOPLE ALREADY FEEL LINKED TO EACH OTHER ACROSS NATIONAL BOUNDARIES."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **"They would say that THE OUTPOURING OF HELP FROM ALL PARTS OF THE WORLD FOR VICTIMS OF THE ASIAN TSUNAMI AND OTHER MAJOR CALAMITIES IS A SIGN OF THE EMERGENCE OF A GLOBAL SOCIETY. ⭐ THEY FEEL THAT WE SHOULD TRY TO STRENGTHEN THIS FEELING AND WORK TOWARDS A CONCEPT OF GLOBAL CITIZENSHIP."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Why national citizenship may not be enough
⭐⭐ **"The concept of national citizenship ASSUMES THAT OUR STATE CAN PROVIDE US WITH THE PROTECTION AND RIGHTS WHICH WE NEED TO LIVE WITH DIGNITY in the world today. ⭐⭐ BUT STATES TODAY ARE FACED WITH MANY PROBLEMS WHICH THEY CANNOT TACKLE BY THEMSELVES."**

⭐⭐⭐ **The two questions:** *"**In this situation, ARE INDIVIDUAL RIGHTS, GUARANTEED BY THE STATE, SUFFICIENT TO PROTECT THE FREEDOM OF PEOPLE TODAY? OR HAS THE TIME COME TO MOVE TO A CONCEPT OF HUMAN RIGHTS AND GLOBAL CITIZENSHIP?**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Its main attraction — cross-border problems
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"ONE OF THE ATTRACTIONS OF THE NOTION OF GLOBAL CITIZENSHIP IS THAT IT MIGHT MAKE IT EASIER TO DEAL WITH PROBLEMS WHICH EXTEND ACROSS NATIONAL BOUNDARIES AND WHICH THEREFORE NEED COOPERATIVE ACTION BY THE PEOPLE AND GOVERNMENTS OF MANY STATES."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **The specific promise:** *"For instance, **IT MIGHT MAKE IT EASIER TO FIND AN ACCEPTABLE SOLUTION TO THE ISSUE OF MIGRANTS AND STATELESS PEOPLES — OR AT LEAST TO ENSURE THEM BASIC RIGHTS AND PROTECTION REGARDLESS OF THE COUNTRY IN WHICH THEY MAY BE LIVING.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE CHAPTER'S VERDICT — a supplement, NOT a replacement
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"In the previous section we saw that EQUAL CITIZENSHIP WITHIN A COUNTRY CAN BE THREATENED BY THE SOCIO-ECONOMIC INEQUALITIES OR OTHER PROBLEMS WHICH MIGHT EXIST. ⭐⭐ SUCH PROBLEMS CAN ULTIMATELY ONLY BE SOLVED BY THE GOVERNMENTS AND PEOPLE OF THAT PARTICULAR SOCIETY. ⭐⭐⭐ THEREFORE, FULL AND EQUAL MEMBERSHIP OF A STATE REMAINS IMPORTANT FOR PEOPLE TODAY."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"BUT THE CONCEPT OF GLOBAL CITIZENSHIP REMINDS US THAT NATIONAL CITIZENSHIP MIGHT NEED TO BE SUPPLEMENTED BY AN AWARENESS (i) THAT WE LIVE IN AN INTERCONNECTED WORLD, AND (ii) THAT THERE IS ALSO A NEED FOR US TO STRENGTHEN OUR LINKS WITH PEOPLE IN DIFFERENT PARTS OF THE WORLD AND BE READY TO WORK WITH PEOPLE AND GOVERNMENTS ACROSS NATIONAL BOUNDARIES."**

**⭐⭐⭐ This is the answer to the Overview's question — "could global citizenship REPLACE national citizenship?" NO. Global citizenship SUPPLEMENTS national citizenship, because only your own state and society can fix your society's socio-economic inequalities.**

*(⭐ The chapter's "Let's Do It" points to **GLOBAL CITIZENSHIP EDUCATION (GCED)** — a UNESCO programme.)*

## Key Words (Glossary)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Citizenship** | **"FULL AND EQUAL MEMBERSHIP OF A POLITICAL COMMUNITY"** — states give members a **collective political identity** and **certain rights**, plus **help and protection wherever they travel** |

## Quick Recap (15 points)
1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Citizenship is "FULL AND EQUAL MEMBERSHIP OF A POLITICAL COMMUNITY."** States give members a **collective political identity** — *"we think of ourselves as Indians, or Japanese, or Germans"* — plus **rights** and **help and protection wherever they travel.**
2. ⭐⭐ **The value of citizenship is shown by its absence:** those **"forced to live as refugees or illegal migrants because no state is willing to grant them membership"** are **"not guaranteed rights by any state and generally live in precarious conditions"** — as with **Palestinian refugees in the Middle East**, for whom full membership is **"a goal for which they are willing to struggle."**
3. ⭐⭐ **The rights of citizenship vary by state but generally include political (the vote), civil (freedom of speech or belief) and socio-economic (a minimum wage, education) rights** — and **"equality of rights and status is one of the basic rights of citizenship."**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **"Each of the rights now enjoyed by citizens has been WON AFTER STRUGGLE":** against **powerful monarchies** (the **French Revolution, 1789**); in the **colonies of Asia and Africa** as part of independence struggles; by **South Africa's black population against the ruling white minority, continuing until the early 1990s**; and today by the **women's movement and the dalit movement** in India, whose purpose is **to change public opinion and to influence government policy.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **Citizenship is not only about citizen–state relations but about CITIZEN–CITIZEN relations** — involving **not just legal obligations but "a MORAL OBLIGATION to participate in, and contribute to, the shared life of the community"**, with citizens as **"the inheritors and TRUSTEES of the culture and natural resources of the country."** ⭐ **The chapter's method: understand a concept by finding where its accepted meaning "is being questioned by groups who feel that it does not take account of their needs."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **The insider/outsider problem:** as in a crowded compartment, **"those who may have earlier fought each other to enter, once inside discover a shared interest in keeping others out."** Where **jobs, medical care, education, land or water are limited**, demands arise to exclude **"outsiders" even though they may be fellow citizens** — hence **"Mumbai for Mumbaikars."** **Freedom of movement matters most to workers** — **IT workers to Bangalore, nurses from Kerala, construction and road-building labour** — yet resistance builds against **jobs going to outsiders, sometimes at lower wages**, producing demands to **reserve jobs for state residents or local-language speakers**, and sometimes **organised violence.**
7. ⭐⭐⭐ **The chapter exposes two double standards:** we are **"indignant if Indian workers in other countries are ill-treated"** and proud when states attract skilled migrants, yet **resent competition from outsiders at home**; and **"we may not always be as welcoming to POOR migrants as to SKILLED AND AFFLUENT workers"** — raising **whether poor and unskilled workers should have the same right to live and work anywhere as skilled workers.** ⭐ **The democratic answer to such disputes: the right to protest (an aspect of freedom of expression, provided it "does not harm the life or property of other people or the State"), exercised by forming groups, demonstrating, using the media, appealing to parties, or approaching the courts** — because **"such disputes should be settled by NEGOTIATION AND DISCUSSION RATHER THAN FORCE. This is one of the OBLIGATIONS of citizenship."**
8. ⭐⭐⭐ **MARTIN LUTHER KING JR. against the US Segregation Laws** — laws that **denied black people civil and political rights** and created **separate areas in railways, buses, theatres, housing, hotels and restaurants.** ⭐ **His THREE arguments:** **(i)** in **self-worth and dignity every human person is equal regardless of race or colour**; **(ii)** segregation is **"SOCIAL LEPROSY on the body politic"**, inflicting **deep psychological wounds** — and it **"diminishes the quality of life for the WHITE community also"**, as when the whites **closed community parks** rather than admit black people, and **baseball teams were disbanded** rather than accept black players; **(iii)** the laws **"create artificial boundaries between people and PREVENT THEM FROM COOPERATING for the overall benefit of the country."** ⭐ **His method was peaceful, non-violent resistance: "We must not allow our creative protest to degenerate into physical violence."**
9. ⭐⭐⭐ **The urban poor:** every Indian city has **slum-dwellers and squatters** who, **"although they may do necessary and useful work, often at low wages, are often viewed as unwelcome visitors"** and **blamed for straining the city's resources or spreading crime and disease.** ⭐ **In fact they "make a significant contribution to the economy through their labour"** — as **hawkers, petty traders, scavengers, domestic workers, plumbers, mechanics** — and slums host **cane weaving, textile printing and tailoring.** ⭐ Conditions are **"shocking"** — crammed rooms with **no private toilets, running water or sanitation**, and **life and property are insecure** — while **"the city probably spends relatively little on providing slum-dwellers with services such as sanitation or water supply."**
10. ⭐⭐⭐ **Progress and obstacles:** a **national policy on urban street vendors was framed in JANUARY 2004**, to give **recognition and regulation** so that the **lakhs of vendors facing police and municipal harassment** could work unmolested if they obeyed regulations *(becoming the **Street Vendors Act, 2014**)*. ⭐⭐ **But even the most basic right can be unusable: "even a basic political right like THE RIGHT TO VOTE may be difficult for them to exercise, because to be included in the list of voters A FIXED ADDRESS IS REQUIRED — and squatters and pavement dwellers may find it difficult to provide this."** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation* (1985)**, a **PIL**, established that **"Article 21... which guaranteed the right to life INCLUDED THE RIGHT TO LIVELIHOOD"**, so evicted pavement dwellers must **first be given alternative accommodation under the right to shelter.**
11. ⭐⭐ **Tribal people and forest dwellers** depend on **access to forests and natural resources**, and are threatened by **population pressure and the search for land**, by **commercial interests wanting to mine forests and coasts**, and by **the tourist industry** — leaving governments **"struggling with... how to protect these people and their habitat without endangering development"**, an issue **"that affects ALL citizens, not just tribal people."**
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **The governing principle of Section 6.3: "EQUAL RIGHTS FOR CITIZENS NEED NOT MEAN THAT UNIFORM POLICIES HAVE TO BE APPLIED TO ALL PEOPLE, since different groups may have different needs. If the purpose is not just to make policies which would apply in the same way to all people, BUT TO MAKE PEOPLE MORE EQUAL, the different needs and claims of people would have to be taken into account."** ⭐ **And "the formal laws regarding citizenship only form THE STARTING POINT; the interpretation of laws is CONSTANTLY EVOLVING."**
13. ⭐⭐⭐ **T.H. MARSHALL (*Citizenship and Social Class*, 1950):** citizenship is **"a STATUS bestowed on those who are full members of a community"**, and **all who possess it are EQUAL in the rights and duties it endows.** ⭐⭐ **Equality implies TWO things — the QUALITY of rights and duties improves, and the QUANTITY of people on whom they are bestowed grows.** ⭐ **Three kinds of rights: CIVIL (life, liberty, property), POLITICAL (participation in governance), SOCIAL (access to education and employment)** — together making **a life of dignity** possible. ⭐⭐ **And his central thesis: social class is "a SYSTEM OF INEQUALITY", while "CITIZENSHIP ENSURES EQUALITY BY COUNTERING THE DIVISIVE EFFECTS OF CLASS HIERARCHY", facilitating "a better-integrated and harmonious community."**
14. ⭐⭐⭐ **CITIZEN AND NATION.** The **nation state** emerged in the modern period, with the **French revolutionaries of 1789** among the first to assert **its sovereignty and citizens' democratic rights**; such states claim their boundaries define **"not just a territory but also a unique culture and shared history"**, expressed through **flag, anthem, national language and ceremonial practices.** ⭐⭐ **But though democratic states try to be inclusive, "in practice most countries define their identity in a way which makes it easier for SOME citizens to identify with the state than others"** — even the **USA, "which prides itself on being a country of immigrants."** ⭐⭐ **FRANCE** claims to be **secular and inclusive** but requires **assimilation in the public sphere**, and since **"it is not always simple to define what is public and what is private"**, the demands of **Sikh boys to wear the turban** and **Muslim girls the head scarf** were **disallowed by some schools** — ⭐ **and "those whose religions did not demand such practices naturally did not face the same problem. Clearly, assimilation would be EASIER FOR SOME GROUPS THAN FOR OTHERS."** ⭐ **ISRAEL and GERMANY may give priority to religion or ethnic origin**, and Germany still debates whether the **German-born children of Turkish workers** should automatically be citizens. ⭐⭐⭐ **INDIA defines itself as a secular, democratic nation state**; the broad-based independence movement bound together different religions, regions and cultures, and though **Partition occurred in 1947 when differences with the Muslim League could not be resolved, "this only STRENGTHENED THE RESOLVE... to maintain the secular and inclusive character"**, embodied in the Constitution. It accommodated groups as different as **SCs and STs, women who had not previously enjoyed equal rights, and remote communities in the Andaman and Nicobar islands** — ⭐⭐ **"it had to provide equal rights to all WITHOUT AT THE SAME TIME FORCING PEOPLE TO GIVE UP THEIR PERSONAL BELIEFS, LANGUAGES OR CULTURAL PRACTICES. It was therefore A UNIQUE EXPERIMENT."** ⭐ **Citizenship provisions lie in PART TWO and later laws; it may be acquired by BIRTH, DESCENT, REGISTRATION, NATURALISATION or INCLUSION OF TERRITORY;** the state may not discriminate on grounds only of **religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth**, and **religious and linguistic minorities are protected.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Yet struggles persist — the women's movement, the dalit movement, people displaced by development projects — so "DEMOCRATIC CITIZENSHIP IN ANY COUNTRY IS A PROJECT, AN IDEAL TO WORK TOWARDS", and new demands "have to be NEGOTIATED."**
15. ⭐⭐⭐ **UNIVERSAL CITIZENSHIP AND STATELESSNESS.** Though states support universal and inclusive citizenship in principle, **"each of them also FIXES CRITERIA"** and **"uses their power to keep unwanted visitors out."** ⭐ Despite restrictions and **even walls or fences**, migration continues through **war, persecution, famine** — and **"if no state is willing to accept them and they cannot return home, they become STATELESS PEOPLES or REFUGEES"**, often unable to **legally work, educate their children or acquire property**, which is why the **UN has a High Commissioner for Refugees.** ⭐⭐ **India "prides itself on providing refuge to persecuted peoples, as it did with the DALAI LAMA and his followers in 1959"**, yet **many remain stateless "for many years or generations... and only a relatively few are eventually granted citizenship."** ⭐⭐⭐ **The haunting conclusion: "although many people cannot achieve citizenship of a state of their choice, NO ALTERNATIVE IDENTITY EXISTS FOR THEM."** ⭐⭐ **GLOBAL CITIZENSHIP** is proposed in response: in an interconnected world of **internet, television and cell phones**, where we **"watch disasters and wars on our television screens as they are taking place"**, supporters argue that **"although a world community and global society does not yet exist, people ALREADY FEEL LINKED to each other across national boundaries"** — instancing the **help for victims of the 2004 Asian tsunami.** ⭐ Its **attraction** is that it could ease **problems extending across national boundaries** and secure **"basic rights and protection [for migrants and stateless peoples] REGARDLESS OF THE COUNTRY in which they may be living."** ⭐⭐⭐ **But the verdict is clear: since socio-economic inequalities "can ultimately ONLY be solved by the governments and people of that particular society", "FULL AND EQUAL MEMBERSHIP OF A STATE REMAINS IMPORTANT" — and global citizenship is a SUPPLEMENT, reminding us to "strengthen our links with people in different parts of the world and be ready to work with people and governments across national boundaries."**

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Citizenship as full and equal membership involves both rights and obligations. Which rights could citizens expect to enjoy in most democratic states today? What kind of obligations will they have to their state and fellow citizens?**
   → ⭐⭐ **RIGHTS** *(note that the chapter offers two overlapping lists — give both)*: **political** (the **right to vote**, and per Marshall, to **participate in the process of governance**); **civil** (**freedom of speech or belief**, and per Marshall, protection of **life, liberty and property**); **socio-economic/social** (the **right to a minimum wage**, the **right to education**, and per Marshall, **access to education and employment**). ⭐ Add **freedom of movement** — *"of particular importance for workers"* — and **the right to protest** as an aspect of freedom of expression. ⭐⭐ **Above all: "EQUALITY OF RIGHTS AND STATUS is one of the basic rights of citizenship"**, and together these rights **"make it possible for the citizen to lead a life of dignity."**

2. ⭐⭐⭐ **"All citizens may be granted equal rights but all may not be able to equally exercise them." Explain.**
   → ⭐⭐⭐ **The chapter's own best illustration:** *"**even a basic political right like THE RIGHT TO VOTE may be difficult for [slum-dwellers] to exercise, because to be included in the list of voters A FIXED ADDRESS IS REQUIRED — and squatters and pavement dwellers may find it difficult to provide this.**"* ⭐ **The right is formally universal; an administrative requirement silently excludes the homeless.**

   - ⭐⭐ **In France**, a uniform ban on religious symbols in schools is formally equal, yet **"those whose religions did not demand such practices naturally did not face the same problem"** — so **"assimilation into the national culture would be easier for some groups than for others."**
   - ⭐ **Tribal people** hold equal rights but lose the **forests their way of life depends on** to mining, tourism and population pressure.
   - ⭐ **Migrants** hold freedom of movement, yet face **demands to reserve jobs for locals** and even **organised violence.**
   ⭐⭐⭐ **The principle that follows: "EQUAL RIGHTS FOR CITIZENS NEED NOT MEAN THAT UNIFORM POLICIES HAVE TO BE APPLIED TO ALL PEOPLE... if the purpose is not just to make policies which would apply in the same way to all people, BUT TO MAKE PEOPLE MORE EQUAL, THE DIFFERENT NEEDS AND CLAIMS OF PEOPLE WOULD HAVE TO BE TAKEN INTO ACCOUNT."** *(This is Ch. 3's identical-treatment-vs-treating-as-equal and Ch. 4's third principle of justice.)*
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **Write a short note on any TWO struggles for full enjoyment of citizen rights in India in recent years. Which rights were being claimed in each case?**

   |---|---|

4. ⭐⭐⭐ **What are some of the problems faced by refugees? In what ways could the concept of global citizenship benefit them?**
   → ⭐⭐ **The problems:** they are people **"forced to become refugees in their own, or neighbouring countries"** by **war, persecution or famine** — as in **Darfur (Sudan)**, and among **Palestinians, Burmese and Bangladeshis.** Since **"no state is willing to accept them and they cannot return home"**, they become **stateless.** Consequently: **(i)** they are **"not guaranteed rights by any state and generally live in PRECARIOUS conditions"**; **(ii)** they live **in camps or as illegal migrants**; **(iii)** they often **"cannot legally work, or educate their children, or acquire property"**; **(iv)** where borders are **"redefined by war or political disputes"**, they **"may lose their HOMES, POLITICAL IDENTITIES, and SECURITY, and be forced to migrate"**; **(v)** even in a hospitable country like India, many **"remain as stateless peoples for many years or generations"**, with **"only a relatively few eventually granted citizenship."** ⭐⭐⭐ **And most fundamentally: "although many people cannot achieve citizenship of a state of their choice, NO ALTERNATIVE IDENTITY EXISTS FOR THEM."**
   ⭐⭐ **How global citizenship could help:** it would address problems that **"extend across national boundaries and therefore need cooperative action by the people and governments of many states"** — and specifically **"might make it easier to find an acceptable solution to the issue of migrants and stateless peoples, OR AT LEAST TO ENSURE THEM BASIC RIGHTS AND PROTECTION REGARDLESS OF THE COUNTRY IN WHICH THEY MAY BE LIVING."** It would supply the **alternative identity** that presently does not exist, resting on **human rights** rather than on the accident of which state will admit you. ⚠️ **But note the limit: since a "world community and global society DOES NOT YET EXIST", and socio-economic problems "can ultimately only be solved by the governments and people of that particular society", global citizenship SUPPLEMENTS rather than replaces national citizenship.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **Migration of people to different regions within the country is often resisted by local inhabitants. What are some of the contributions that migrants could make to the local economy?**
   → ⭐⭐ **Use the chapter's own lists.** **Skilled migration:** **I.T. workers** flocking to **Bangalore** built that city's industry; **nurses from Kerala** staff hospitals **"all over the country"** — and note that **"states may even be PROUD of their ability to attract such workers."** **Unskilled and semi-skilled migration:** the **"booming building industry"** and **"infrastructure projects like road making"** depend on **workers from different parts of the country** — the physical construction of the city is migrant work.
   ⭐⭐⭐ **And the urban poor specifically:** *"**slum dwellers make a SIGNIFICANT CONTRIBUTION TO THE ECONOMY THROUGH THEIR LABOUR**"* — as **hawkers, petty traders, scavengers, domestic workers, plumbers and mechanics** — while slums themselves generate **small businesses such as cane weaving, textile printing and tailoring.** ⭐⭐ **The sharp point to make: they "do NECESSARY AND USEFUL work, OFTEN AT LOW WAGES", yet "are often viewed as unwelcome visitors" and blamed for "straining the resources of the city" — even though the city "spends relatively little on providing slum-dwellers with services such as sanitation or water supply."** ⭐ **Close with the consistency argument: we are "indignant if Indian workers in other countries are ill-treated", and we welcome skilled migrants while resisting poor ones — so the resistance rests on prejudice about class rather than on any accounting of contribution.**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **"Democratic citizenship is a project rather than an accomplished fact even in countries like India which grant equal citizenship." Discuss some of the issues regarding citizenship being raised in India today.**
   → ⭐⭐⭐ **Begin with the chapter's own formulation:** *"**The experience of India indicates that DEMOCRATIC CITIZENSHIP IN ANY COUNTRY IS A PROJECT, AN IDEAL TO WORK TOWARDS. New issues are constantly being raised as societies change and new demands are made by groups who feel they are being marginalised. In a democratic state these demands have to be NEGOTIATED.**"* ⭐ **And: "the formal laws regarding citizenship only form THE STARTING POINT, and the interpretation of laws is CONSTANTLY EVOLVING."**
   ⭐⭐ **What India granted:** an **essentially democratic and inclusive** notion of citizenship in **Part Two**, acquirable by **birth, descent, registration, naturalisation or inclusion of territory**; **non-discrimination** on grounds only of **religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth**; **protection of religious and linguistic minorities**; and a **unique experiment** in providing equal rights **"without forcing people to give up their personal beliefs, languages or cultural practices."**
   ⭐⭐ **The live issues:**
   | Issue | The citizenship question it raises |
   |---|---|
   | ⭐ **The insider/outsider conflict — "Mumbai for Mumbaikars"** | Does **full and equal membership** include the right to **live, study and work anywhere in the country**? Should jobs be reserved for **state residents or local-language speakers**? |
   | ⭐ **The double standard on poor vs skilled migrants** | **"Should poor and unskilled workers have the SAME right to live and work anywhere in the country as do skilled workers?"** |
   | ⭐⭐ **The urban poor — slum-dwellers, squatters, street vendors** | Whether **"all citizens, rich or poor, should be guaranteed certain basic rights and a minimum standard of living"** — and the **fixed-address barrier to voting** shows rights can exist without being exercisable |
   | ⭐⭐ **Tribal people and forest dwellers** | How to **protect their habitat and way of life "without endangering development of the country"** — against **mining, tourism and land pressure** |
   | ⭐ **The women's movement and the dalit movement** | Formal equality against **continuing social subordination**, requiring **differential provision** to secure the **same** rights |
   | ⭐ **People displaced by development projects** | Livelihood, shelter and rehabilitation as **citizenship claims**, not welfare |
   | ⭐⭐ **Refugees and stateless people on India's borders** | Whether the **promise that "the rights and identity of citizen would be available to all people"** can be honoured, when many remain stateless **"for many years or generations"** |
   ⭐⭐⭐ **Conclude with the standard:** the concept of equal citizenship means that **"providing equal rights and protection to all citizens should be ONE OF THE GUIDING PRINCIPLES OF GOVERNMENT POLICIES"** — and that these demands must be met through **negotiation and discussion rather than force.**
