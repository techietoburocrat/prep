# Chapter 2 · Practice Set 02 — The Assembly at Work: First Sitting & the Objectives Resolution

[← Set 01](Set_01.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 03 →](Set_03.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟢 Foundation**
**Covers:** First meeting, 9 Dec 1946 · officers of the Assembly · B.N. Rau · the Objectives Resolution · its link to the Preamble

> **The idea behind this set.** Three dates fall inside nine days of December 1946 — 9th, 11th, 13th — and every year candidates swap them. Learn the *rhythm* (meet → elect → propose) rather than three isolated dates, and this set becomes almost free marks.

🔥 = high probability · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

### 1. 🔥
The Constituent Assembly of India held its first meeting on

- (a) 11 December 1946
- (b) 9 December 1946
- (c) 13 December 1946
- (d) 26 January 1946

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **9 December 1946.** The Muslim League boycotted this and every subsequent sitting, having already committed itself to a separate constitution-making process.

> 🔑 **Anchor date for the whole December rhythm — the other two dates in this set are built from it: +2 days, +4 days.**

</details>

### 2. 🔥 ⚠️
Who was elected the **temporary** President of the Constituent Assembly at its first sitting?

- (a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
- (b) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha
- (c) H.C. Mukherjee
- (d) B.N. Rau

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha**, the **oldest member present**, was elected temporary (provisional) chairman — following the French constitutional convention of letting the most senior member open a new assembly.

**Why not (a):** Rajendra Prasad became **permanent** President two days later, on 11 December — a different office, a different date.

> ⚠️ **The trap:** "President of the Constituent Assembly" without a qualifier defaults, in most people's memory, to Rajendra Prasad — and UPSC exploits exactly that reflex by asking about the temporary chair.

</details>

---

### 3. 🔥
Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected the **permanent** President of the Constituent Assembly on

- (a) 9 December 1946
- (b) 11 December 1946
- (c) 13 December 1946
- (d) 22 January 1947

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **11 December 1946** — two days after the first sitting.

> 🔑 **The December rhythm, worth reciting until automatic:** **9th** meet, elect temporary chair → **11th** elect permanent President → **13th** Objectives Resolution moved. Three events, two-day intervals, one month.

</details>

### 4. 🔥
Who were elected Vice-Presidents of the Constituent Assembly?

- (a) H.C. Mukherjee and V.T. Krishnamachari
- (b) B.N. Rau and B.R. Ambedkar
- (c) K.M. Munshi and Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar
- (d) J.B. Kripalani and Gopinath Bardoloi

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **H.C. Mukherjee** and **V.T. Krishnamachari** were the two Vice-Presidents. Note that H.C. Mukherjee held a second role too — chair of the Minorities Sub-Committee (Set 3).

**Why not (b):** Rau and Ambedkar are correct people in this chapter but wrong roles here — Rau was constitutional advisor, Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee, neither was Vice-President.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Several names in this chapter wear two hats. Track *role plus person* as a pair, not the person alone — a name in the wrong slot is the commonest trap in this entire chapter.

</details>

### 5. 🔥
Who was appointed **constitutional advisor** to the Constituent Assembly, and prepared the original draft of the Constitution?

- (a) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
- (b) B.N. Rau
- (c) K.M. Munshi
- (d) N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **B.N. Rau**, a distinguished civil servant and jurist, was appointed constitutional advisor and prepared the Assembly's **initial draft**, drawing on the constitutions of several other countries.

**Why not (a):** Ambedkar's Drafting Committee took Rau's initial draft and turned it into the **final** text — a later, distinct stage.

> 🔑 **Don't confuse these three roles, ever:**
> **B.N. Rau** — advisor, prepared the **initial** draft.
> **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** — chaired the **Drafting Committee**, produced the **final** text.
> **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** — President of the Assembly, later India's first President.
> This exact triad is one of the highest-yield facts in the chapter.

</details>

### 6. 🔥
The Objectives Resolution was moved in the Constituent Assembly by

- (a) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
- (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
- (c) Jawaharlal Nehru
- (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Jawaharlal Nehru** moved the Objectives Resolution, laying down the philosophy and fundamental ideals the future Constitution would embody.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is Nehru's second major appearance in the chapter — he defined what the Assembly should look like in 1938 (Set 1), and now shapes what the Constitution should *say* in 1946. The same person, two different kinds of contribution nine years apart.

</details>

### 7. 🔥
The Objectives Resolution was moved on

- (a) 9 December 1946
- (b) 11 December 1946
- (c) 13 December 1946
- (d) 22 January 1947

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **13 December 1946** — completing the December rhythm from Q3: 9th, 11th, 13th.

**Why not (d):** 22 January 1947 is when the Resolution was **adopted**, after five weeks of debate — moving and adopting are different events, tested separately (see Q8).

</details>

### 8. 🔥 ⚠️
The Objectives Resolution was formally **adopted** by the Constituent Assembly on

- (a) 13 December 1946
- (b) 26 January 1947
- (c) 22 January 1947
- (d) 26 November 1947

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **22 January 1947** — over five weeks after it was moved, following extensive debate.

**Why not (b) and (d):** Both are constructed from real dates elsewhere in the chapter — 26 January is Republic Day (a year later, 1950) and 26 November is Constitution Day (1949) — recombined with the wrong year to build a plausible-looking wrong answer.

> ⚠️ **Moved (13 Dec 1946) vs adopted (22 Jan 1947) is asked as two separate facts almost every year this topic appears.** Keep the verb attached to the date: *moved* on the 13th, *adopted* on the 22nd.

</details>

### 9. 🔥
The Objectives Resolution, in a modified form, eventually became the

- (a) Ninth Schedule
- (b) Preamble to the Constitution
- (c) Directive Principles of State Policy
- (d) Fundamental Duties

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Objectives Resolution's language and philosophy carried forward, in modified form, into the **Preamble**.

> 🔑 **The single most valuable link in this chapter.** The Objectives Resolution is not a fact to memorise in isolation — it *becomes* the Preamble. That one arrow connects this chapter directly to Chapter 4, and courts have since read the Resolution as a key to interpreting the Preamble itself (Set 10 develops this further).

</details>

### 10. 🔥
Which of the following ideals did the Objectives Resolution lay down for the future Constitution?

1. India to be declared an independent sovereign republic
2. Justice, equality and freedom for all citizens
3. Safeguards for minorities and the depressed and backward classes

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** All three were part of the Objectives Resolution — an independent sovereign republic, guarantees of justice, equality and freedom, and explicit safeguards for minorities and backward classes.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Read this list against the Preamble you already know by heart — sovereignty, justice, liberty, equality — and the minority/backward-class safeguards against Part III and Part XVI. The Resolution is not a vague sentiment; it is a checklist the Constitution later fulfilled clause by clause.

</details>

### 11.
Which one of the following statements about B.N. Rau is correct?

- (a) He chaired the Drafting Committee
- (b) He was the Constituent Assembly's constitutional advisor and prepared its initial draft
- (c) He was elected the first Vice-President of the Assembly
- (d) He moved the Objectives Resolution

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Rau's role was **constitutional advisor**, and his output was the Assembly's **initial draft** — the raw material the Drafting Committee later worked from.

**Why not (a):** The Drafting Committee was chaired by **Ambedkar**, not Rau — a distinction hammered repeatedly in this set because it is hammered repeatedly by the exam.

> 🔑 **Concept — B.N. Rau's forgotten wider career.** Beyond India, Rau went on to serve as a judge of the International Court of Justice and played a role in shaping the UN Security Council's early procedural rules — a reminder that the people who built India's Constitution were often deeply embedded in the *international* constitutional and legal thinking of their era, not working from Indian precedent alone.

</details>

### 12.
Consider the following statements about the officers of the Constituent Assembly:

1. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was elected temporary chairman as the oldest member present.
2. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President two days after the first sitting.
3. B.N. Rau was elected a Vice-President of the Assembly.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 1 and 2 are correct.

**Why not 3:** Rau was **constitutional advisor**, an appointed technical role, not an elected Vice-President — that office belonged to H.C. Mukherjee and V.T. Krishnamachari.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** Notice the pattern — a true fact about a real person, paired with a role that belongs to someone else. This construction (right person, wrong role) is this chapter's single most common trap, appearing in at least one question in nearly every set.

</details>

### 13. 🔥
Arrange the following events of December 1946 – January 1947 in correct chronological order:

1. Objectives Resolution adopted
2. First sitting of the Constituent Assembly
3. Objectives Resolution moved
4. Rajendra Prasad elected permanent President

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 2 – 4 – 3 – 1
- (b) 2 – 3 – 4 – 1
- (c) 4 – 2 – 3 – 1
- (d) 2 – 4 – 1 – 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** First sitting **9 Dec** → Rajendra Prasad elected **11 Dec** → Objectives Resolution moved **13 Dec** → adopted **22 Jan**. That is **2 – 4 – 3 – 1**.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** This exact four-item chronology is one of the most reusable patterns in Prelims polity — meet, elect, propose, adopt. Once you have internalised the December rhythm from earlier questions, this kind of ordering question stops requiring any new recall at all.

</details>

### 14. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Objectives Resolution laid down the philosophy of the Constitution before a single Article had been drafted.

**Statement-II:** The Drafting Committee was constituted before the Objectives Resolution was adopted.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Statement-I is correct — the Resolution (adopted 22 January 1947) preceded any Article-drafting by a wide margin. Statement-II is false: the **Drafting Committee was constituted only on 29 August 1947**, more than seven months **after** the Resolution was adopted, not before it.

> ⚠️ **A subtle trap: the sequence feels intuitively plausible** — philosophy first, then a committee to execute it — but the *specific* claim that the Committee predates the Resolution is factually reversed. Always verify a plausible-sounding sequence against the actual dates rather than the story-logic alone.

</details>

### 15.
The Muslim League's absence from the Assembly's proceedings from the very first sitting meant that

- (a) the Assembly could not legally conduct any business
- (b) the Objectives Resolution was adopted without the League's participation
- (c) the first sitting had to be postponed indefinitely
- (d) Congress agreed to suspend all proceedings until the League returned

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Assembly proceeded regardless — electing its officers and moving and adopting the Objectives Resolution entirely **without** League participation, which continued throughout the Assembly's life.

**Why not (a) and (c):** Nothing in the Cabinet Mission Plan or the Assembly's own rules required the League's presence for business to be valid; the boycott was a political choice by one party, not a legal impediment.

> 🔑 **Concept:** The Assembly's willingness to proceed without the League is itself part of the "not fully representative" criticism developed in Set 10 — but it is also evidence *for* the Assembly's legitimacy, since it never used the League's absence as an excuse to stall or to exclude other minority voices.

</details>

### 17. 📰
The Objectives Resolution has since been cited by courts as an aid to interpreting the

- (a) Seventh Schedule
- (b) Preamble
- (c) Table of Precedence
- (d) Anti-Defection Law

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Because the Resolution's language carried forward into the **Preamble**, courts have repeatedly gone back to the Resolution's debates to understand what the Preamble's framers actually intended.

> 📰 **Two landmark examples worth knowing by name.** In ***Berubari Union* (1960)**, the Supreme Court examined whether the Preamble was part of the Constitution and drew on the Objectives Resolution debates to understand its status. In ***Kesavananda Bharati* (1973)**, the Court went further and held the Preamble **is** part of the Constitution — and can itself be amended, though not so as to damage the basic structure. The Resolution you read about in this chapter is not a dead historical fact; it has been active legal material in some of India's most-cited judgments.

</details>

### 18.
Which one of the following best explains why the Assembly chose to adopt an Objectives Resolution before beginning detailed drafting?

- (a) The Cabinet Mission Plan required a resolution before any committee could be formed
- (b) It let the Assembly agree on shared philosophy and goals first, so that detailed drafting could proceed within an already-settled framework
- (c) The British government insisted on a resolution as a precondition for transferring power
- (d) It was a purely ceremonial requirement with no bearing on later drafting

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Settling the *philosophy* first — sovereignty, republicanism, justice, equality, minority safeguards — gave every later committee a shared frame to draft within, reducing the chance of foundational disagreement resurfacing article by article.

**Why not (d):** Far from ceremonial, the Resolution's content demonstrably shaped the Preamble and, through it, the interpretive approach later courts took to the whole Constitution (Q17) — the opposite of having no bearing.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Sequencing philosophy before detail is a recognisable drafting strategy, not unique to India — settle first principles, then let detail follow within agreed boundaries. It is why the Resolution's five weeks of debate (13 Dec to 22 Jan) were time well spent rather than a delay.

</details>

### 20. 🔥
Which one of the following is the correct sequence of roles held, in order of when each person's contribution began?

- (a) B.N. Rau (initial draft) → Nehru (Objectives Resolution) → Ambedkar (final draft)
- (b) Nehru (Objectives Resolution) → B.N. Rau (initial draft) → Ambedkar (final draft)
- (c) Ambedkar (final draft) → B.N. Rau (initial draft) → Nehru (Objectives Resolution)
- (d) B.N. Rau (initial draft) → Ambedkar (final draft) → Nehru (Objectives Resolution)

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution in **December 1946**; Rau's initial draft followed through 1947 as the Assembly's committees did their work; Ambedkar's Drafting Committee, constituted **29 August 1947**, then produced the final text from Rau's draft. Philosophy first, initial draft second, final text last.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This ordering is really the *logical* pipeline of constitution-writing — goals, then a working draft, then a polished final text — which happens to also be the *chronological* order. When a process has an inherent logical sequence, chronology and logic usually agree, and you can use either to check the other.

</details>

### 21.
Which one of the following statements about the Objectives Resolution's content is **not** correct?

- (a) It declared India would be an independent sovereign republic
- (b) It promised justice, social, economic and political, to all citizens
- (c) It explicitly named the words "Socialist" and "Secular"
- (d) It promised safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal areas

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c) is the answer:** Neither the Objectives Resolution nor the original 1949 Preamble used the words **"Socialist"** or **"Secular"** — both were inserted only by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, decades later.

**Why the others are genuine content:** sovereignty, republicanism, comprehensive justice, and minority/backward/tribal safeguards were all present in the Resolution's actual text, and all carried forward into the Preamble as originally adopted.

> ⚠️ **A trap worth remembering permanently.** People routinely assume "Socialist Secular" was always part of India's founding vocabulary. It was not — not in the Objectives Resolution, and not in the Preamble until 1976. This exact confusion is tested across multiple chapters, not just this one.

</details>

### 22.
Which one of the following correctly states the relationship between the Objectives Resolution and the Preamble?

- (a) They are identical documents adopted on the same date
- (b) The Preamble is a modified, condensed version of the philosophy first laid out in the Objectives Resolution
- (c) The Objectives Resolution was adopted after the Preamble was finalised
- (d) They have no textual or philosophical connection

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Preamble is not a separate invention — it is the Objectives Resolution's philosophy, carried forward and refined into the Constitution's opening statement.

**Why not (a):** They are related but not identical — the Preamble was finalised only as part of the completed Constitution in November 1949, nearly three years after the Resolution's adoption in January 1947, and its wording differs.

> 🔑 **This question is Q9 restated as a "relationship" question rather than a "what did it become" question — recognising the same underlying fact in different phrasings is a core exam skill this whole chapter is designed to drill.**

</details>

### 24.
Which one of the following best explains why the Objectives Resolution took over five weeks to move from proposal to adoption?

- (a) The Muslim League demanded changes before agreeing to vote
- (b) It required extensive debate to secure the widest possible agreement on the Constitution's foundational philosophy
- (c) The British government had to approve its wording first
- (d) It was delayed by a procedural dispute over the Assembly's quorum

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** A resolution meant to state the *shared* philosophy of a document not yet written needed genuine debate to earn that status — rushing it would have undermined the very consensus it was meant to represent.

**Why not (a):** The League was **boycotting** the Assembly throughout this period (Set 1, Q22; this set, Q15) and was not a party to negotiations over the Resolution's wording.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Five weeks of debate on a single resolution, before a single Article was drafted, tells you how seriously the Assembly treated foundational agreement. This pace is itself evidence for the "substantively legitimate" side of the legitimacy debate developed across this chapter.

</details>

### 25. 🔥
Which one of the following best summarises the achievement of the Assembly's first six weeks, from 9 December 1946 to 22 January 1947?

- (a) It elected its principal officers and settled the Constitution's foundational philosophy, without yet drafting a single Article
- (b) It completed the entire Draft Constitution
- (c) It formally adopted the Constitution
- (d) It dissolved the Drafting Committee after disagreements

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** In six weeks the Assembly elected its temporary and permanent presiding officers, its Vice-Presidents, appointed its constitutional advisor, and debated and adopted the philosophy that would eventually become the Preamble — all **before** the Drafting Committee (Set 3) or the Draft Constitution itself (Set 4) even existed.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** The Assembly did not begin by writing Articles — it began by building its own institutional structure and agreeing on shared philosophy. Structure and philosophy first, text second: that ordering is the thread connecting this set to the next, where the committees that would actually do the drafting work are built on exactly this foundation.

</details>
