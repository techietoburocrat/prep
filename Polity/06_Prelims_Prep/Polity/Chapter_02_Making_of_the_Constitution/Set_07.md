### 1. 🔥
The Constituent Assembly adopted the national flag of India on

- (a) 15 August 1947
- (b) 22 July 1947
- (c) 26 January 1950
- (d) 26 November 1949

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **22 July 1947** — just over three weeks **before** independence itself. The Assembly, already functioning as the country's provisional legislature even before formal independence, settled the flag ahead of the transfer of power.

**Why not (a):** Independence Day is when the flag was first hoisted as a **free** nation's flag, not the date the Assembly formally **adopted** its design — adoption and first ceremonial use are two different events.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is an early sign that the Assembly's identity was never purely "constitution-drafting body" — it was already acting as the nation's legislature and decision-making authority on symbolic national questions weeks before independence was even formally declared.

</details>

### 2. 🔥
India's membership of the Commonwealth was ratified by the Constituent Assembly in

- (a) August 1947
- (b) May 1949
- (c) January 1950
- (d) November 1949

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **May 1949** — a significant decision, since India was simultaneously preparing to become a **republic**, a status that might have seemed incompatible with Commonwealth membership at the time.

> 🔑 **Concept — why this mattered.** Until 1949, Commonwealth membership was understood to require accepting the British monarch as head of state — precisely what a republic does not do. India's continued membership as a republic required a new arrangement, worked out through the **London Declaration of April 1949**, under which the monarch was accepted only as symbolic "Head of the Commonwealth" rather than India's head of state. The Assembly's ratification the following month confirmed India's place in a Commonwealth that had just redefined itself to make room for republics.

</details>

### 3. 🔥
The Constituent Assembly adopted the national anthem and the national song on

- (a) 26 November 1949
- (b) 22 July 1947
- (c) 24 January 1950
- (d) 26 January 1950

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **24 January 1950** — two days **before** the Constitution came into force, in the same short final burst of activity that also saw the first President elected (Q4).

**Why not (d):** 26 January is commencement day itself — a date already carrying enormous symbolic weight (Set 5), which is exactly why the Assembly deliberately settled the anthem, song and presidency **before** it, leaving the 26th free for the Republic's actual birth.

> 🔑 **The final countdown, worth memorising as a unit:** **24 Jan 1950** — anthem, national song, first President elected → **26 Jan 1950** — Constitution commences, Republic begins. Two days, two very different kinds of event.

</details>

### 4. 🔥
Who was elected India's first President by the Constituent Assembly, on 24 January 1950?

- (a) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha
- (b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
- (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
- (d) Jawaharlal Nehru

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**, who had been the Assembly's own permanent President since 11 December 1946 (Set 2), was elected India's first **President of the Republic** on 24 January 1950 — a continuity of leadership from the Assembly's own presiding office into the new nation's highest constitutional office.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Notice the neat symmetry — the man who presided over the writing of the Constitution became the first person to preside over the country it created. This is a satisfying, memorable link precisely because it is also a factually precise one: same person, two related but formally distinct offices, four years apart.

</details>

### 5. 🔥
Which one of the following statements correctly distinguishes the Constituent Assembly's role as a constitution-drafting body from its role as India's legislature?

- (a) These were two entirely separate bodies that happened to share the same building
- (b) The same body performed both functions — it drafted the Constitution while also acting as the provisional Parliament of independent India
- (c) The Assembly's legislative role began only after 26 January 1950
- (d) The Assembly never exercised any legislative function

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** One body, two roles simultaneously — the Constituent Assembly drafted the Constitution **and**, from independence in 1947, doubled as India's **provisional Parliament**, passing ordinary laws and conducting the government's business alongside its drafting work.

**Why not (c):** This dual role began well **before** 26 January 1950 — the flag decision in July 1947 (Q1) is itself an example of the Assembly exercising authority as the nation's decision-making body, over two years before the Constitution came into force.

> 🔑 **Concept:** "Constituent Assembly" and "provisional Parliament" are not two sequential phases — they are two labels for the **same sittings**, worn simultaneously. This dual identity is precisely why the Assembly's 165 sitting days (Set 6) never went entirely to Constitution-writing alone.

</details>

### 6. 🔥
Until when did the Constituent Assembly continue functioning as India's provisional Parliament?

- (a) It stopped immediately once the Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949
- (b) It continued until the first general elections were held and a new Parliament was constituted, in 1951–52
- (c) It continued indefinitely, and technically still exists today
- (d) It stopped the moment the Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Assembly continued as the **provisional Parliament** even after the Constitution came into force, right up until India's **first general elections (1951–52)** produced a newly and directly elected Parliament to take over.

**Why not (d):** 26 January 1950 marks the Constitution's commencement, not the end of the provisional arrangement — the country could not simply have no Parliament at all while the first general election, then still an enormous and unprecedented logistical undertaking, was being organised.

> 🔑 **Concept — the Assembly's full working life, end to end:** first sitting **Dec 1946** → drafting body **and** provisional legislature **1947–50** → Constitution in force but Assembly still sitting as provisional Parliament **1950–52** → replaced by the first directly elected Parliament, **1952**. Over five years of continuous institutional life, only the middle chunk of which is "writing the Constitution" in the narrow sense.

</details>

### 7.
Which one of the following best explains why India could not simply hold elections to a permanent Parliament immediately upon the Constitution coming into force on 26 January 1950?

- (a) The Constitution prohibited elections for five years after commencement
- (b) Organising India's first general election under universal adult franchise — an unprecedented logistical undertaking for the world's largest-ever electorate at the time — took time to prepare
- (c) The Election Commission had not yet been established
- (d) The Constituent Assembly refused to dissolve under any circumstances

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Preparing electoral rolls, constituencies and polling infrastructure for a **universal adult franchise election** — an entirely new undertaking on this scale, anywhere in the world at the time — genuinely required real preparation time, which is exactly why the country needed a working provisional Parliament to bridge that gap.

**Why not (c):** The Election Commission (Article 324) was among the provisions given **early effect** on 26 November 1949 (Set 5) — it already existed well before commencement, precisely so it could begin this preparatory work in advance.

> 🔑 **Cross-reference:** This directly explains *why* Article 324 needed to come into force early (Set 5, Q10) — an Election Commission with no time to prepare could not have delivered India's first general election on any reasonable timeline.

</details>

### 8. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the Constituent Assembly's activities in 1947–1950:

1. It adopted the national flag before independence was formally declared.
2. It ratified India's Commonwealth membership after the Constitution had already come into force.
3. It elected the first President before the Constitution came into force.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 1 and 3 are correct — the flag (22 July 1947) preceded independence (15 August 1947), and the first President (24 January 1950) was elected two days **before** commencement (26 January 1950).

**Why not 2:** Commonwealth ratification was in **May 1949** — well **before**, not after, the Constitution came into force in January 1950.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** This question sequences three genuine events against three different reference points (independence, commencement) rather than against each other directly — a useful reminder to always check *which* anchor date a statement is measuring "before" or "after" against.

</details>

### 11. 📰
Which one of the following best explains why India's continued Commonwealth membership as a republic, ratified in May 1949, is considered historically significant beyond India's own constitutional story?

- (a) It had no wider significance beyond India's own internal politics
- (b) It set the precedent that allowed the Commonwealth itself to evolve into an association that could include republics, not only monarchies acknowledging the British Crown as head of state
- (c) It ended the Commonwealth's existence entirely
- (d) It required India to abandon its plan to become a republic

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** India's insistence on **both** becoming a republic **and** remaining in the Commonwealth forced the association itself to redefine what membership required — accepting the monarch only as a symbolic Head of the Commonwealth rather than as every member's head of state. Many other Commonwealth republics since have followed the precedent India's 1949 position established.

> 📰 **Why this belongs in current affairs, not just history.** The Commonwealth today includes dozens of republics precisely because of the framework worked out around India's 1949 position — a genuinely consequential Indian contribution to how an international association is structured, still relevant whenever the modern Commonwealth's membership or purpose is discussed.

</details>

### 12.
Which one of the following statements about the timing of the first President's election is correct?

- (a) Rajendra Prasad was elected first President on 26 January 1950, the same day the Constitution came into force
- (b) Rajendra Prasad was elected first President on 24 January 1950, two days before the Constitution came into force
- (c) Rajendra Prasad was elected first President only after the first general elections of 1951–52
- (d) India had no President between 1947 and 1950

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **24 January 1950** — deliberately two days ahead of commencement, so that the Republic would already have its first President in place the moment the Constitution actually took effect on the 26th, rather than beginning its existence with the office vacant.

**Why not (d):** Between 1947 and 1950, India was a **dominion**, with the King as head of state represented by a Governor-General (a fact from Chapter 1) — there was no President yet, precisely because the office of President is a creation of the Constitution that had not yet come into force.

</details>

### 13. 🔥
Which one of the following correctly describes the sequence of India's heads of state, from independence to the Republic?

- (a) Governor-General (as the King's representative) until 26 January 1950, then President
- (b) President from 15 August 1947, then Governor-General from 1950
- (c) No head of state existed until 1952
- (d) The Constituent Assembly's President automatically became head of state on 15 August 1947

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** From independence until the Republic began, India was a **dominion** with a **Governor-General** as the King's representative (Chapter 1); only from **26 January 1950** did India have a **President** as its own, republican head of state.

**Why not (d):** The Assembly's own President (Rajendra Prasad, an internal Assembly office) and India's head of state (initially the Governor-General, later the President of India) were, until 1950, two entirely different offices — one the Assembly's presiding officer, the other the dominion's constitutional head.

> 🔑 **Cross-reference:** This directly recalls Chapter 1's account of the Governor-General's transformed, nominal role after the Indian Independence Act, 1947 — the dominion period explains the gap between independence in 1947 and India actually having its own President only in 1950.

</details>

### 14.
Which one of the following statements about the Assembly's dual role is correct?

- (b) As provisional Parliament, the Assembly passed ordinary laws and conducted government business, separately from its work as a constitution-drafting body
- (c) The Assembly's legislative role was purely symbolic and had no legal effect
- (d) The Assembly's dual role was explicitly prohibited by the Cabinet Mission Plan

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why not (d):** Nothing in the Cabinet Mission Plan (Set 1) prohibited this dual role — indeed, some form of continuing legislative authority was a practical necessity for any newly independent country still writing its permanent constitution.

</details>

### 17.

Understanding the Assembly only as "the committee that wrote 395 Articles" understates what it actually was.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** Everything here — flag, Commonwealth, anthem, first President, provisional Parliament — is evidence for a bigger claim: the Constituent Assembly was not a drafting committee that happened to also exist; it was, for over five years, functionally the sovereign authority of a new nation, and Constitution-writing was the largest, but not the only, exercise of that authority.

</details>
