# Chapter 2 · Practice Set 05 — Adoption vs Commencement

[← Set 04](Set_04.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 06 →](Set_06.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟡 Prelims Standard**
**Covers:** 26 November 1949 in full detail · 26 January 1950 and why that date · the handful of Articles that came into force early · why Fundamental Rights did NOT

> **The idea behind this set.** "Adopted in '49, applied in '50" is the one-line version everyone knows. This set is about the fine print behind that line — because UPSC has repeatedly asked exactly the fine print, not the headline.

🔥 = high probability · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

### 1. 🔥
The Constitution of India was **adopted** by the Constituent Assembly on

- (a) 15 August 1947
- (b) 26 November 1949
- (c) 26 January 1950
- (d) 24 January 1950

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **26 November 1949** — the date the third reading concluded (Set 4) and the finished text was formally accepted by the Assembly. This is now commemorated as **Samvidhan Divas**, Constitution Day.

**Why not (c):** That is the date the Constitution **came into force** — a distinct legal moment, two months later.

</details>

---

### 2. 🔥
The Constitution of India **came into force** — India became a republic — on

- (a) 26 November 1949
- (b) 15 August 1950
- (c) 26 January 1950
- (d) 22 January 1950

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **26 January 1950** — the "date of commencement," now observed as **Republic Day**.

> 🔑 **The one-line version: "Adopted in '49, applied in '50."** Adoption is a legal act by the Assembly; commencement is when the document actually takes legal effect for the country.

</details>

### 3. 🔥
How many members of the Constituent Assembly signed the Constitution on 26 November 1949?

- (a) 299
- (b) 389
- (c) 284
- (d) 395

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **284 members** signed on adoption day. Recall the shrinking sequence from Set 1: **389** (original strength) → **299** (post-Partition strength) → **284** (actual signatories present on 26 November 1949 — smaller still, since not every sitting member was present or chose to sign).

**Why not (d):** **395** is the number of **Articles**, not people — a unit-confusion trap using a number that genuinely belongs elsewhere in this exact set (Q4).

</details>

### 4. 🔥
As originally adopted, the Constitution of India contained

- (a) a Preamble, 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules
- (b) a Preamble, 470 Articles, 25 Parts and 12 Schedules
- (c) a Preamble, 395 Articles, 25 Parts and 12 Schedules
- (d) no Preamble, 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Preamble + 395 Articles + 22 Parts + 8 Schedules** — the original 1949 structure.

**Why not (b):** That is the **current** (post-amendment) structure — roughly 470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules — a live, growing document, not the one adopted in 1949.

> ⚠️ **The exam usually asks about the ORIGINAL numbers, but tests whether you know both sets and can tell which is being asked.** If a question does not say "originally" or "as amended," read carefully for other clues — a question about 1949 itself always means the original figures.

</details>

### 5. 🔥 ⚠️
Which one of the following statements about 26 January 1950 is correct?

- (a) It is the date the Constitution was adopted
- (b) It is the date India became a Sovereign Democratic Republic
- (c) It is the date the words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added to the Preamble
- (d) It is the date the Drafting Committee was dissolved

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** On 26 January 1950, the Preamble as it then stood declared India a **"Sovereign Democratic Republic"** — this exact phrase was tested directly in **Prelims 2021**.

**Why not (c):** "Socialist" and "Secular" were inserted only by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, twenty-six years later — not part of the original 1950 wording.

> ⚠️ **Prelims 2021 asked exactly this: "What was the exact constitutional status of India on 26th January, 1950?"** The correct option was **"A Sovereign Democratic Republic"** — not the fuller modern phrase most people say from memory, which already includes words that did not exist yet in 1950.

</details>

### 6. 🔥
Which one of the following best explains why the Constituent Assembly chose 26 January as the date of commencement, rather than any other date?

- (a) It was the earliest date the Draft Constitution's printing could be completed
- (b) It honoured 26 January 1930, when the Congress at its Lahore session declared Purna Swaraj and observed it as Independence Day
- (c) It was chosen at random by the Drafting Committee
- (d) It was the date the British Parliament formally approved the Constitution

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The date deliberately honours **26 January 1930**, when Congress under Nehru declared **Purna Swaraj** (complete independence) at its Lahore session and thereafter observed that date annually as Independence Day.

**Why not (d):** By 1950, the Assembly was **fully sovereign** (a fact from Chapter 1) — nothing required British parliamentary approval of anything the Assembly did.

> 🔑 **The republic began on a date the freedom movement had already made sacred.** This detail alone can carry a full sentence in a Mains answer, and it explains a fact — why *this* date — that a bare timeline never would.

</details>

### 7. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the two dates 26 November 1949 and 26 January 1950:

1. 26 November is observed as Constitution Day, marking adoption.
2. 26 January is observed as Republic Day, marking commencement.
3. Both dates mark the same single legal event, described in two different ways.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 1 and 2 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 1 and 2 are correct.

**Why not 3:** Adoption and commencement are **two distinct legal events**, two months apart — a document can be adopted (finalised and accepted by its framers) well before it takes legal effect, exactly as happened here.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Think of adoption as *signing* a contract and commencement as the date the contract's terms actually start binding the parties — the two rarely happen simultaneously in law, and the Constitution's own history is a clean illustration of that general legal principle.

</details>

### 8. 🔥
Which of the following provisions of the Constitution came into force **immediately** on 26 November 1949, rather than waiting until 26 January 1950?

1. Provisions relating to citizenship
2. Provisions relating to elections
3. Provisions relating to the provisional Parliament

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** All three genuinely came into force on 26 November 1949 itself — **citizenship**, **elections**, and **provisional Parliament** provisions, along with a handful of temporary and transitional clauses. These had to operate *before* 26 January 1950 so that a functioning citizenry, an Election Commission, and a sitting provisional legislature already existed the moment the rest of the Constitution took effect.

> 🔑 **Concept — the practical logic behind the exception.** You cannot hold the Republic's first elections, define who its citizens even are, or have a working Parliament ready on day one, if those provisions only start existing on the same day everything else does. A handful of "infrastructure" Articles had to switch on early precisely so the main event on 26 January would not begin from zero.

</details>

### 9. 🔥 ⚠️
Which one of the following parts of the Constitution did **NOT** come into force on 26 November 1949, despite the Constitution as a whole being adopted that day?

- (a) Provisions on citizenship
- (b) Provisions on the Election Commission
- (c) Part III, Fundamental Rights
- (d) Temporary and transitional provisions

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Fundamental Rights (Part III)** — along with the vast majority of the Constitution — came into force only on **26 January 1950**. This is the trap most candidates fall into: since the Constitution was "adopted" on 26 November, many wrongly assume rights were legally available from that date.

**Why the others came in early:** citizenship (Articles 5–9), the Election Commission (Article 324) and temporary/transitional provisions (Articles 379, 380, 388, 391–393) were the deliberately chosen early-start exceptions covered in Q8.

> ⚠️ **This is one of the highest-value single facts in the entire chapter.** "Adopted" does not mean "in force," and even among the provisions the Assembly *could* have started early, it chose only a narrow, functional set — rights were not among them.

</details>

### 10. 🔥
Which one of the following Articles is correctly matched with the subject that came into force on 26 November 1949?

- (a) Article 21 — Right to Life
- (b) Article 324 — Election Commission
- (c) Article 32 — Right to Constitutional Remedies
- (d) Article 14 — Equality before Law

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Article 324**, establishing the Election Commission, was one of the provisions given immediate effect on 26 November 1949 — precisely so election machinery would exist ahead of the Republic's actual commencement.

**Why not the others:** Articles 21, 32 and 14 all sit within **Part III, Fundamental Rights** — and as Q9 establishes, the whole of Part III waited until **26 January 1950**.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** Any option naming a famous Fundamental Right (Articles 14, 19, 21, 32 are the most quoted) as something that came into force on 26 November 1949 is almost certainly a trap — none of Part III got early treatment.

</details>

### 11.
Which one of the following statements about Articles 5 to 9 of the Constitution is correct?

- (a) They deal with citizenship, and came into force on 26 November 1949
- (b) They deal with Fundamental Rights, and came into force on 26 January 1950
- (c) They deal with the amendment procedure, and have never come into force
- (d) They deal with emergency provisions, and came into force on 26 November 1949

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Articles 5–9** are the Constitution's original citizenship provisions — determining who became a citizen of India at commencement, and how citizenship could later be lost or acquired. They were among the handful of provisions in force from **26 November 1949**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Citizenship had to be settled before the Republic's other machinery (voting, holding office, claiming rights as a citizen) could sensibly operate — which is exactly why these Articles sit in the "early" group with elections and the provisional Parliament, rather than waiting for 26 January like almost everything else.

</details>

### 12.
Which one of the following statements about Article 60 is correct in the context of this set?

- (a) It concerns the President's oath, and was among the provisions in force from 26 November 1949
- (b) It concerns Fundamental Duties, and came into force only in 1976
- (c) It concerns the Finance Commission, and has never been amended
- (d) It concerns Panchayati Raj, introduced by the 73rd Amendment

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Article 60**, prescribing the President's oath, was among the early-effect Articles — a practical necessity, since a functioning provisional Head of State needed a valid oath provision available before the main commencement date.

**Why not (b) and (d):** Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) were inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**; Panchayati Raj's constitutional status came from the **73rd Amendment, 1992** — both are much later additions, unrelated to this 1949 early-effect list.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When an option attaches a real Article number to a subject from a completely different era of constitutional history, check the *era* first — Fundamental Duties and Panchayati Raj are Emergency-era and post-1990s topics respectively, both far too late to have anything to do with 26 November 1949.

</details>

### 13.
Consider the following statements:

1. Articles 366 and 367, dealing with definitions and interpretation, came into force on 26 November 1949.
2. This was necessary because later-commencing provisions needed settled definitions to be interpreted correctly from day one.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Both are correct, and II is exactly why I makes sense. Definitional Articles are the Constitution's own dictionary — if the provisions that took effect early (citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament) were to be interpreted correctly, the definitions underpinning their key terms needed to already be in force too.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is a good example of *why* a seemingly technical early-effect list (Articles 366, 367) makes complete sense once you think about what those provisions actually do — definitional and interpretive machinery has to switch on alongside, not after, the substantive provisions that depend on it.

</details>

### 14. 🔥
Which one of the following best explains the logic connecting all the Articles that came into force on 26 November 1949?

- (a) They were the shortest Articles in the Constitution
- (b) They were the provisions the Assembly's members personally found most important
- (c) They were the "infrastructure" provisions — citizenship, elections, provisional legislature, definitions, and transitional machinery — needed to make an orderly commencement on 26 January possible
- (d) They were chosen alphabetically from the Constitution's text

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Every early-effect Article fits one description — **infrastructure** needed *before* the main event, not the main event itself. Citizens had to be defined, an Election Commission had to exist, a provisional Parliament had to be ready, and definitions had to be settled, all ahead of 26 January.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** "Adopted" and "in force" split into two dates for a reason more specific than mere ceremony — the Assembly needed a two-month runway to get the Republic's basic machinery operating before the rest of the Constitution, including every citizen's rights, actually switched on.

</details>

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

### 16.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. India becomes a Sovereign Democratic Republic
2. The Constitution is adopted, with 284 signatures
3. The words "Socialist" and "Secular" are added to the Preamble
4. Draft Constitution's third reading concludes

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 4 – 2 – 1 – 3
- (b) 2 – 4 – 1 – 3
- (c) 4 – 1 – 2 – 3
- (d) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Third reading concludes and adoption happen together on **26 Nov 1949** (Set 4, Q6), so item 4 and item 2 both sit at that same moment, with the third reading's conclusion being the *procedural* act and adoption/signing being its *outcome* — placed in that order. India becomes a republic on **26 Jan 1950** (item 1). The Preamble gains "Socialist" and "Secular" only in **1976** (item 3), decades later.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When two events share essentially the same date, order them by which is the cause and which is the immediate consequence — the third reading concluding is the procedural act; signing and adoption is its direct result.

</details>

### 17.
Which one of the following statements about the 284 signatories is correct?

- (a) It represents every member who was ever part of the Constituent Assembly
- (b) It represents the members present and willing to sign on the specific date of adoption, a smaller number than the Assembly's full post-Partition strength
- (c) It represents only the members of the Drafting Committee
- (d) It represents the number of women members of the Assembly

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** 284 is smaller than the post-Partition strength of 299 (Set 1) — not every sitting member was present, or chose to sign, on the specific day of adoption. It is a snapshot of one day's attendance and willingness to sign, not a count of everyone who ever contributed to the Assembly's work.

**Why not (d):** The number of women members was **15** (Set 6 develops this) — an entirely different, much smaller figure that happens to share no numerical relationship with 284 at all.

</details>

### 18.
Which one of the following correctly distinguishes "adoption" from "commencement" in the context of this chapter?

- (a) Adoption is when the Assembly formally accepts the finished text; commencement is when that text becomes legally operative for the country
- (b) They are two names for the identical legal act, used interchangeably by convention
- (c) Adoption applies only to the Preamble; commencement applies only to the Articles
- (d) Adoption happened in 1950; commencement happened in 1949

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Adoption is the Assembly's own act of finalising and accepting the text (26 Nov 1949); commencement is the separate moment the text actually takes legal effect and begins governing the country (26 Jan 1950, with the narrow early-effect exceptions of Q8–Q14).

**Why not (d):** This is the dates reversed — adoption is **1949**, commencement is **1950**, not the other way round.

</details>

### 19. 📰
Constitution Day, 26 November, was formally notified as an annual observance by the Government of India in

- (a) 1950
- (b) 1976
- (c) 2015
- (d) 2000

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** The Government of India formally notified **26 November** as **Samvidhan Divas (Constitution Day)** in **2015**, decades after the date itself. The date's *historical* significance (1949) and its *formal annual observance* (from 2015) are two different facts — the event long predates the modern commemoration.

> 📰 **Why this matters now.** **26 November 2024** marked **75 years since adoption**, and was widely covered with special sessions in Parliament and renewed public discussion of the Assembly's debates — including, in some coverage, direct references back to the Objectives Resolution (Set 2) as the philosophical starting point being celebrated. Knowing that the *observance* is comparatively recent (2015) while the *event* is much older (1949) helps you correctly place a current-affairs mention of "Constitution Day" in this chapter's older timeline.

</details>

### 20.
Which one of the following best explains why UPSC has repeatedly tested the *exact* constitutional status phrase for 26 January 1950, rather than simply asking "what happened on 26 January 1950"?

- (a) Because the exact wording is uniquely difficult to find in any source
- (b) Because it tests whether a candidate has absorbed the specific original wording rather than substituting today's familiar, longer phrase from memory
- (c) Because no reliable answer exists
- (d) Because the phrase has never been quoted in any official source

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The trap is not obscurity — it is **familiarity working against you**. Almost everyone can recite "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic" from memory, because that is today's Preamble. The exam rewards knowing that in 1950 the phrase was shorter, and penalises candidates who unconsciously substitute the modern version.

> 🔑 **Concept — a pattern worth generalising.** Whenever a fact you know well *today* has an earlier, different version, expect the exam to test the **earlier** version specifically, precisely because it is the version most people get wrong by assuming continuity where there was actually change. You met this exact pattern already with "395 Articles" versus "~470 Articles" (Q4).

</details>

### 25. 🔥
Which one of the following best summarises why this set treats "26 November 1949" and "26 January 1950" as two dates worth an entire set of their own, rather than one line of the chapter?

- (a) Because they are the only two dates in the whole chapter
- (b) Because the gap between them is filled with real legal substance — a narrow, deliberately chosen set of early-effect provisions — and UPSC tests that substance in fine detail, not just the two headline dates
- (c) Because Mains examiners have never asked about either date
- (d) Because the two dates are actually the same date under two different calendars

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The headline — "adopted in '49, applied in '50" — is only the entry point. The real examinable content is the **specific list** of what moved early (citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament, definitions, transitional provisions) and the **specific fact** of what did not (Fundamental Rights, and nearly everything else) — exactly the material Q8 through Q14 and Q24 drilled directly.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** Two dates, a two-month gap, and one carefully chosen list of exceptions — that is the entire technical content here, and it rewards precision over general knowledge. A candidate who knows only "adopted in '49, applied in '50" will get roughly half of this set's questions wrong; a candidate who also knows the early-effect list gets nearly all of them right.

</details>
