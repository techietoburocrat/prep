### 1. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| 1. B.N. Rau | Constitutional advisor, prepared the initial draft |
| 2. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar | Chaired the Drafting Committee |
| 3. Dr. Rajendra Prasad | Permanent President of the Assembly |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why all three:** Rau prepared the initial draft, Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee that produced the final text, and Rajendra Prasad was the Assembly's permanent President — the three-role triad from Set 2, tested cleanly.

> ⚠️ **Do not distrust "all three" on instinct.** Verify each pair independently and report what you actually find.

</details>

### 2. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Committee | Chairman |
|---|---|
| 1. States Committee | Sardar Patel |
| 2. Provincial Constitution Committee | Jawaharlal Nehru |
| 3. Steering Committee | Dr. Rajendra Prasad |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Only pair 3 is correct.

**Why not 1 and 2:** The **States Committee was Nehru's**, not Patel's (part of his "Union" portfolio); the **Provincial Constitution Committee was Patel's**, not Nehru's — the two are swapped between the wrong chairs, a direct application of the N-P-R-A mnemonic in reverse.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When two options look like they might simply have swapped two chairs with each other, check the mnemonic (N-Union, P-Provinces, R-Procedure, A-Drafting) rather than trying to recall each pairing from scratch.

</details>

### 4. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Objectives Resolution was adopted on the same date it was moved.

**Statement-II:** The Objectives Resolution was moved on 13 December 1946 and adopted on 22 January 1947, after over five weeks of debate.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Statement-I is false — moving and adoption were **five weeks apart**, not simultaneous. Statement-II correctly states the true gap, and is itself the reason Statement-I is wrong.

</details>

### 5.
Consider the following pairs:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1. 9 December 1946 | First sitting of the Constituent Assembly |
| 2. 11 December 1946 | Objectives Resolution moved |
| 3. 13 December 1946 | Rajendra Prasad elected permanent President |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Only pair 1 is correct.

**Why not 2 and 3:** The Objectives Resolution was moved on **13 December**, not 11th; Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on **11 December**, not 13th — the two later dates in the December rhythm are swapped with each other.

> ⚠️ **This is the December rhythm (9th–11th–13th) tested at its hardest — two of the three dates deliberately exchanged.** If you know the rhythm as a fixed sequence rather than three floating dates, this becomes easy; if not, it is exactly where marks are lost.

</details>

### 9.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. National flag adopted
2. India becomes independent
3. Commonwealth membership ratified
4. Drafting Committee constituted

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 – 2 – 4 – 3
- (b) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
- (c) 1 – 2 – 3 – 4
- (d) 1 – 4 – 2 – 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Flag **22 July 1947** → independence **15 August 1947** → Drafting Committee constituted **29 August 1947** → Commonwealth ratified **May 1949**.

> ⚠️ **The trap:** the flag was adopted **before** independence, which feels counter-intuitive if you assume national symbols must follow formal independence. Chronology questions often hide exactly this kind of intuition-defying but well-documented order.

</details>

### 10. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Drafting Committee was constituted on 26 November 1949.

**Statement-II:** On 26 November 1949, the Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution of India.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** This is the **exact Prelims 2023 question**, reproduced. Statement-I is false — the Drafting Committee was constituted on **29 August 1947**, over two years earlier. Statement-II is correct: 26 November 1949 is indeed the adoption date.

> 🔥 **This near-verbatim reconstruction of an actual PYQ is worth extra attention — the same trap has a very high chance of reappearing in some form.**

</details>

### 13.
**Statement-I:** Princely state representatives in the Constituent Assembly were chosen by proportional representation with the single transferable vote.

**Statement-II:** Proportional representation with the single transferable vote was the method used to elect community representatives within the Provincial Legislative Assemblies.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Statement-I is false — princely state representatives were **nominated by their rulers**, with no election of any kind. Statement-II correctly describes the *provincial* method, which the false Statement-I wrongly transplants onto the princely states.

### 15.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. B.L. Mitter resigns from the Drafting Committee
2. Draft Constitution's second reading concludes
3. Drafting Committee constituted
4. Draft Constitution published

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 3 – 4 – 1 – 2
- (b) 3 – 1 – 4 – 2
- (c) 4 – 3 – 1 – 2
- (d) 3 – 4 – 2 – 1

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Committee constituted **Aug 1947**, before it could have any membership changes → Draft published **Feb 1948** → Mitter's resignation occurred during the Committee's ongoing work (necessarily after its constitution and consistent with the drafting period, i.e. after publication) → second reading concludes **Oct 1949**, the last item chronologically.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When an exact date for one item (Mitter's resignation) is not independently memorised, place it using what you *do* know for certain — it must fall after the Committee existed and can reasonably be located within its working period, which is enough to fix its position relative to the other three better-dated events.

</details>

### 18.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Commonwealth membership ratified
2. First reading of the Draft Constitution
3. Public discussion period on the Draft begins
4. Second reading concludes

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 3 – 2 – 4 – 1
- (b) 2 – 3 – 4 – 1
- (c) 3 – 2 – 1 – 4
- (d) 3 – 4 – 2 – 1

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Public discussion begins **Feb 1948** → first reading **Nov 1948** → second reading concludes **Oct 1949** → Commonwealth ratified **May 1949**...

**Correction — check the last two carefully:** Commonwealth ratification (**May 1949**) actually falls **before** the second reading concludes (**17 Oct 1949**), not after. The correct order is therefore public discussion → first reading → Commonwealth ratification → second reading concludes, which is **3 – 2 – 1 – 4**, option **(c)**.

**Answer: (c)**

> ⚠️ **A genuinely difficult interleaving:** the "other roles" timeline (Set 7) and the "enactment" timeline (Set 4) run in parallel through 1949, and a chronology question can deliberately braid them together. Always re-verify a full sequence against your own remembered dates rather than trusting a plausible-looking first pass.

</details>

### 25. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Constituent Assembly's legitimacy rests entirely on how its members were elected.

**Statement-II:** Its members were elected indirectly, through Provincial Legislative Assemblies chosen on the limited franchise of the Government of India Act, 1935, and its legitimacy is more commonly defended on the basis of its deliberative process and its demonstrated responsible exercise of sovereign authority over more than five years.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Statement-I is false — as this entire chapter establishes, the standard defence of the Assembly's legitimacy rests **primarily on deliberation and conduct**, not on its electoral method, which is in fact the weakest part of its case. Statement-II correctly states both the limitation and the actual basis of the defence.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** Every trap in this set works the same way: a false statement that *sounds* complete and definitive, paired with a true statement that supplies the missing nuance. Learning to spot an over-confident, single-cause claim — "rests entirely on," "only," "purely," "the sole reason" — is the single most transferable skill this whole chapter can teach you, and it carries directly into Set 10's discussion of criticisms.

</details>
