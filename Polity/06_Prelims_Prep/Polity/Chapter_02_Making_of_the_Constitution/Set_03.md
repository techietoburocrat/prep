# Chapter 2 · Practice Set 03 — The Committees, and the Drafting Committee

[← Set 02](Set_02.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 04 →](Set_04.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟢 Foundation**
**Covers:** the eight major committees and their chairs · the "N-P-R-A" grouping · sub-committees of the Advisory Committee · the Drafting Committee's formation, membership and mid-course changes

> **The idea behind this set.** The Assembly did not write a Constitution as one body of 299 people talking at once — it delegated to committees, each handling one slice of the problem. Learn the **grouping** (who reported to whom) rather than eight isolated names, and the sub-committees in Sets beyond this one stop feeling like new facts.

🔥 = high probability · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

---

### 1. 🔥
Which one of the following correctly pairs a major committee of the Constituent Assembly with its chairman?

- (a) Union Powers Committee — Sardar Patel
- (b) Provincial Constitution Committee — Jawaharlal Nehru
- (c) Union Constitution Committee — Jawaharlal Nehru
- (d) Drafting Committee — Dr. Rajendra Prasad

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Nehru chaired the **Union Constitution Committee** — along with the Union Powers Committee and the States Committee, his complete portfolio of "the Union."

**Why not the others:** the Union Powers Committee was also **Nehru's**, not Patel's (a); the Provincial Constitution Committee was **Patel's**, not Nehru's (b) — the exact reverse pairing of the true fact; and the Drafting Committee was **Ambedkar's**, not Rajendra Prasad's (d).

> 🔑 **Concept:** Three of the four options here are built by taking a genuine chair-committee pair and swapping the chair with someone else's. This "correct fact, wrong owner" construction is this chapter's dominant trap — expect it in nearly every committee question you meet.

</details>

### 2. 🔥
Which THREE committees did Jawaharlal Nehru chair?

- (a) Union Powers Committee, Union Constitution Committee, States Committee
- (b) Union Powers Committee, Provincial Constitution Committee, Drafting Committee
- (c) States Committee, Rules of Procedure Committee, Steering Committee
- (d) Union Constitution Committee, Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Drafting Committee

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Nehru's complete portfolio — **Union Powers, Union Constitution, and States** — everything to do with the shape and powers of the central government and its relationship with the princely states.

> 🔑 **Memory hook — "N-P-R-A":**
> **N**ehru → the **Union** (Union Powers, Union Constitution, States)
> **P**atel → **Provinces and People** (Provincial Constitution, Advisory Committee on FR/Minorities/Tribal Areas)
> **R**ajendra Prasad → **Procedure** (Rules of Procedure, Steering)
> **A**mbedkar → **Drafting**
> Four names, four portfolios, eight committees. Learn the four portfolios and the individual committee names follow almost automatically.

</details>

- (b) Provincial Constitution Committee and the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal Areas
- (c) Steering Committee and Rules of Procedure Committee
- (d) Drafting Committee and the Order of Business Committee

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Patel's portfolio — **Provincial Constitution Committee** and the **Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas**. Applying "N-P-R-A": Patel's letter is P for Provinces and People, and both his committees fit exactly that description.

### 4.
Which TWO committees did Dr. Rajendra Prasad chair, in his capacity as President of the Assembly rather than as a committee specialist?

- (a) Union Constitution Committee and States Committee
- (b) Provincial Constitution Committee and Drafting Committee
- (c) Rules of Procedure Committee and Steering Committee
- (d) Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights and Order of Business Committee

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Rules of Procedure Committee** and the **Steering Committee** — both about *how* the Assembly conducted its business, fitting his role as its presiding officer.

> 🔑 **Concept:** It makes structural sense that the Assembly's own President handled procedure rather than substantive drafting — he needed to remain a neutral facilitator across all the other committees' work, not an advocate for one particular slice of content.

</details>

### 5. 🔥
The Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly was chaired by

- (a) Jawaharlal Nehru
- (b) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
- (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
- (d) N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** chaired the Drafting Committee — the single most consequential committee of the Assembly, since it turned B.N. Rau's initial draft into the actual text that was debated, amended and finally adopted.

</details>

### 6. 🔥 ⚠️
The Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly was constituted on

- (a) 26 January 1947
- (b) 29 August 1947
- (c) 26 November 1949
- (d) 4 November 1948

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **29 August 1947** — just two weeks after independence.

**Why not (c):** This is the exact trap used in **Prelims 2023**, where a Statement-II falsely claimed the Drafting Committee was set up on 26 November 1949. It was not — that date is when the **finished Constitution was adopted**, over two years after the Committee began its work.

> ⚠️ **This trap has actually been asked and is near-certain to be tried again.** Fix the two dates apart permanently: Committee **constituted** 29 August 1947; Constitution **adopted** 26 November 1949. Twenty-seven months separate them.

</details>

### 8. 🔥
Which one of the following was **not** an original member of the Drafting Committee?

- (a) N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar
- (b) Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar
- (c) Dr. K.M. Munshi
- (d) J.B. Kripalani

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **J.B. Kripalani** was not on the Drafting Committee — he chaired the separate **Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee** (Q13), a different body entirely.

**Why the others belong:** N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar and Dr. K.M. Munshi were all original members of the seven-person Drafting Committee, alongside Ambedkar, Saadulla, and the two members who later changed (Q9–Q10).

> ⚠️ **The trap:** Kripalani is a genuine, important committee chairman in this chapter — just not on *this* committee. Being real is not the same as being relevant to the specific question asked.

</details>

### 9. 🔥
Which member of the Drafting Committee resigned owing to ill health, and was replaced?

- (a) D.P. Khaitan, replaced by T.T. Krishnamachari
- (b) B.L. Mitter, replaced by N. Madhava Rau
- (c) Syed Mohammad Saadulla, replaced by N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar
- (d) Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar, replaced by K.M. Munshi

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **B.L. Mitter resigned owing to ill health** and was replaced by **N. Madhava Rau**.

**Why not (a):** D.P. Khaitan is the *other* mid-course change on this same committee — but he **died in office**, not resigned, and was replaced by T.T. Krishnamachari (Q10). Two similar-looking events, two different causes — a natural trap when they sit right next to each other.

</details>

### 10. 🔥
Which member of the Drafting Committee died during its work, and was replaced?

- (a) B.L. Mitter, replaced by N. Madhava Rau
- (b) D.P. Khaitan, replaced by T.T. Krishnamachari
- (c) N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, replaced by Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar
- (d) Dr. K.M. Munshi, replaced by Syed Mohammad Saadulla

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **D.P. Khaitan died**, and was replaced by **T.T. Krishnamachari**, who went on to play a significant role in the Assembly's later financial and constitutional debates.

> 🔑 **The two changes, side by side, cause and replacement:**
> **Resignation (ill health):** B.L. Mitter → N. Madhava Rau
> **Death:** D.P. Khaitan → T.T. Krishnamachari
> Learn them as a pair — one resigned, one died — and the "which reason goes with which name" trap disappears.

</details>

### 11.
After the two mid-course replacements, the effective seven members of the Drafting Committee were

- (a) Ambedkar, Ayyangar, Alladi Ayyar, Munshi, Saadulla, N. Madhava Rau, T.T. Krishnamachari
- (b) Ambedkar, Ayyangar, Alladi Ayyar, Munshi, Saadulla, B.L. Mitter, D.P. Khaitan
- (c) Ambedkar, Kripalani, Munshi, Saadulla, N. Madhava Rau, T.T. Krishnamachari, B.N. Rau
- (d) Ambedkar, Ayyangar, Alladi Ayyar, Munshi, H.C. Mukherjee, N. Madhava Rau, T.T. Krishnamachari

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The final, effective membership swaps out both original members who left: N. Madhava Rau **in place of** B.L. Mitter, and T.T. Krishnamachari **in place of** D.P. Khaitan — everyone else stays.

**Why not (b):** This lists the two members who **left**, not their **replacements** — the exact roster before the changes, not after.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When a committee has documented membership changes, expect questions on the *original* roster, the *changes*, and the *final effective* roster as three separate facts. This question tests the third.

</details>

### 14.
Which of the following sub-committees dealt with tribal and excluded areas, under the umbrella Advisory Committee chaired by Patel?

1. North-East Frontier (Assam) Tribal and Excluded Areas Sub-Committee, chaired by Gopinath Bardoloi
2. Excluded and Partially Excluded Areas (Other than Assam) Sub-Committee, chaired by A.V. Thakkar
3. North-West Frontier Tribal Areas Sub-Committee

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** All three genuinely existed under the same Advisory Committee — the Assembly gave the country's tribal and frontier areas separate, dedicated attention through **three** distinct sub-committees rather than treating "tribal areas" as one undifferentiated category.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This granularity — separate sub-committees for the North-East, for excluded areas elsewhere, and for the North-West Frontier — reflects how differently these regions' problems were understood at the time, and is a direct institutional ancestor of the Fifth and Sixth Schedules you will meet later in the Constitution proper.

</details>

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

### 16. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Drafting Committee is regarded as the most important committee of the Constituent Assembly.

**Statement-II:** It was the Drafting Committee that converted B.N. Rau's initial draft into the text that was finally debated, amended and adopted as the Constitution.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Both are correct, and II is precisely why I is true — a committee that produces the actual working text of a constitution is naturally more consequential than committees that produce recommendations or reports that still had to be turned into legal language.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Every other committee in this set fed *into* the Drafting Committee's work in some way — Nehru's committees defined the Union, Patel's defined the provinces and rights, Rajendra Prasad's set procedure — but only the Drafting Committee turned all of that into the actual Articles that were voted on.

</details>

### 17.
Consider the following statements about the Drafting Committee:

1. It was constituted before the Objectives Resolution was adopted.
2. It had seven members at the time of its constitution.
3. Two of its original members left during its work, one by resignation and one by death.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 2 and 3 are correct.

**Why not 1:** The Drafting Committee was constituted on **29 August 1947** — over seven months **after** the Objectives Resolution was adopted on 22 January 1947 (Set 2, Q8), not before it. This is the reverse-sequence version of the same trap tested in Set 2, Q14.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** The same underlying date relationship — Resolution before Drafting Committee — can be tested as a true/false pair (Set 2) or folded into a counting question (here). Recognise the *fact*, not just the *format* it arrives in.

</details>

### 18.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Drafting Committee constituted
2. Advisory Committee's sub-committees (Fundamental Rights, Minorities) begin their work
3. Objectives Resolution adopted
4. Union Powers and Union Constitution Committees begin their work under Nehru

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 3 – 4 – 2 – 1
- (b) 3 – 2 – 4 – 1
- (c) 4 – 3 – 2 – 1
- (d) 3 – 4 – 1 – 2

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The Objectives Resolution's adoption (**22 Jan 1947**) set the philosophical frame first; Nehru's Union-focused committees and Patel's Advisory Committee sub-committees then did substantive work through 1947 (Nehru's committees are generally treated as beginning first, closest on the Resolution's heels, given the urgency of settling the Union's shape ahead of independence); the Drafting Committee, needing input from all of them, was necessarily constituted last, on **29 August 1947**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Even where you cannot recall an exact sub-date for every committee's start, the *logical* dependency — philosophy, then substantive committee work across parallel tracks, then a drafting body that needs their output — lets you place the Drafting Committee reliably last and the Objectives Resolution reliably first, which is usually enough to answer a chronology question correctly.

</details>

### 19. 🔥
Which one of the following is the correct "portfolio" grouping of committees, using the N-P-R-A mnemonic?

- (a) Nehru — Procedure; Patel — Union; Prasad — Provinces; Ambedkar — Drafting
- (b) Nehru — Union; Patel — Provinces and People; Prasad — Procedure; Ambedkar — Drafting
- (c) Nehru — Drafting; Patel — Union; Prasad — Procedure; Ambedkar — Provinces
- (d) Nehru — Union; Patel — Drafting; Prasad — Provinces; Ambedkar — Procedure

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **N**ehru–Union, **P**atel–Provinces and People, (Rajendra) **P**rasad–Procedure, **A**mbedkar–Drafting. Every other option scrambles at least one pairing.

> 🔑 **This question exists purely to force you to state the mnemonic correctly once, out loud, in full.** If you can answer it without hesitation, every individual committee-chair question in this set becomes a lookup rather than a guess.

</details>

### 20.
Which one of the following statements about the relationship between the Advisory Committee and its sub-committees is correct?

- (a) The Advisory Committee and the Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee were the same body under two names
- (b) The Fundamental Rights and Minorities Sub-Committees reported to the Advisory Committee, which Patel chaired
- (c) The sub-committees reported directly to the Drafting Committee, bypassing the Advisory Committee
- (d) The Advisory Committee had no sub-committees at all

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee (Kripalani), the Minorities Sub-Committee (Mukherjee), and the tribal-area sub-committees (Bardoloi, Thakkar, and the North-West Frontier body) all sat **under** the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas — the parent body Patel himself chaired.

**Why not (c):** The Drafting Committee received input from across the Assembly's committee structure once it began work in August 1947, but it did not sit directly above these sub-committees in the reporting chain — the Advisory Committee did.

> 🔑 **Concept — draw the hierarchy once and it never needs re-deriving:** Patel chairs the **Advisory Committee** → which oversees **five sub-committees** (Fundamental Rights, Minorities, and three tribal/frontier bodies) → whose recommendations eventually feed into the **Drafting Committee's** text. Three levels, not two.

</details>

### 21.
Which one of the following was **not** among the eight major committees of the Constituent Assembly listed in this chapter?

- (a) Union Powers Committee
- (b) States Committee
- (c) Finance Commission
- (d) Steering Committee

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** The **Finance Commission** is a constitutional body created by **Article 280** of the finished Constitution — it did not exist as an Assembly committee, and could not have, since it is itself a creature of the document the Assembly was still writing.

**Why the others belong:** Union Powers, States, and Steering are all genuine major committees covered earlier in this set (Q2, Q4).

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** Watch for options that borrow a name from *later* in the syllabus — the Finance Commission belongs to the Union–State financial relations chapters, not to the Assembly's own internal structure. A familiar-sounding name from elsewhere in Polity does not make it correct here.

</details>

### 22.
Which one of the following statements about T.T. Krishnamachari is correct?

- (a) He was an original member of the Drafting Committee from its constitution in August 1947
- (b) He joined the Drafting Committee as a replacement for D.P. Khaitan, who died during its work
- (c) He chaired the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights
- (d) He was the Assembly's constitutional advisor

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** He entered the Drafting Committee specifically as **D.P. Khaitan's replacement** after Khaitan's death — not as an original member.

**Why not (a):** This is the trap: assuming that anyone who is a well-known Drafting Committee figure must have been there from day one, when the documented record shows a mid-course entry.

> 🔑 **Concept:** T.T. Krishnamachari went on to become independent India's Finance Minister — a reminder that several Assembly figures had substantial careers in government both before and after 1950, and their Assembly role is often just one line in a much longer public life.

### 23.
Which one of the following correctly distinguishes the Provincial Constitution Committee from the Union Constitution Committee?

- (a) The former dealt with the constitutional structure of the provinces, chaired by Patel; the latter dealt with the structure of the Union government, chaired by Nehru
- (b) Both dealt with identical subject matter and were chaired by the same person
- (c) The former was chaired by Ambedkar, the latter by Rajendra Prasad
- (d) The former dealt with fundamental rights, the latter with the drafting of the Preamble

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The names describe the split precisely — **Provincial** Constitution Committee (Patel) for the provinces, **Union** Constitution Committee (Nehru) for the central government. This is the N-P-R-A grouping applied to one specific pair of committees.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When two committee names differ by a single word — "Provincial" versus "Union" — that word is the entire answer. Read committee names literally before reaching for outside knowledge; the Assembly's naming convention was almost always descriptive rather than decorative.

</details>

### 24.
Which one of the following best explains why the Assembly created **separate** sub-committees for Fundamental Rights and for Minorities, rather than handling both issues in a single body?

- (a) The two issues were considered entirely unrelated and were assigned to different parent committees
- (b) Fundamental Rights required drafting universal guarantees for all citizens, while Minorities required specific safeguards for particular communities — distinct enough tasks to justify dedicated bodies
- (c) The Assembly's rules required every subject to have exactly one dedicated sub-committee, regardless of scope
- (d) The Muslim League insisted on the separation as a condition of its limited participation

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Universal rights (applying identically to every citizen) and group-specific safeguards (applying to particular communities) are different drafting problems requiring different expertise and different consultation — which is exactly why both sub-committees sat under the **same** parent Advisory Committee (Q20) rather than being unrelated.

**Why not (a):** They were related enough to share a parent body — the split was about *task specialisation*, not about the issues being unconnected.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This same universal-versus-group-specific distinction reappears throughout the finished Constitution — Part III's general rights sit alongside specific provisions for SCs, STs and minorities elsewhere in the text. The Assembly's committee structure previews a design choice the Constitution itself would make permanent.

</details>

### 25. 🔥
Which one of the following best captures how the committee structure as a whole functioned?

- (a) A single committee wrote the entire Constitution independently, with no other body involved
- (b) Specialised committees under four principal figures worked in parallel on distinct problems, feeding their output into the Drafting Committee, which alone produced the final text
- (c) Every member of the Assembly sat on every committee simultaneously
- (d) Committees operated with no chairman and decided all questions by simple majority vote of the whole Assembly

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Four principal figures — Nehru, Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Ambedkar — each led a cluster of committees handling a distinct slice of the problem (Union, Provinces and Rights, Procedure, Drafting respectively), and only the last of these actually produced the constitutional text, drawing on everything the others had settled.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** Do not memorise eight committee names as an unordered list. Memorise **four portfolios**, each with its logical committees attached, and one **funnel** — Advisory Committee sub-committees feeding the Advisory Committee, all committees' work eventually feeding the Drafting Committee, which alone turns recommendations into Articles. That structure is what Set 4 assumes you already know when it moves on to how the resulting draft was actually enacted.

</details>
