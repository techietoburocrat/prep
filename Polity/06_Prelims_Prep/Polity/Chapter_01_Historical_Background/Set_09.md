# Chapter 1 · Practice Set 09 — Pairs, Chronology & Assertion-Reason

[← Set 08](Set_08.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 10 →](Set_10.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🔴 Exam Simulation**
**Covers:** the entire chapter, drilled through the three hardest Prelims formats

> **The idea behind this set.** Since **2023**, UPSC has leaned heavily on three formats that defeat elimination shortcuts:
> **1.** *"How many of the above statements/pairs are correct?"* — you cannot get there by knowing two of three.
> **2.** *Statement-I / Statement-II* — you must judge each statement **and** the link between them.
> **3.** *Chronology* — you must date items, not merely recognise them.
>
> This set is **entirely** those three formats. It is the most useful set in the chapter for exam technique, and the least forgiving.

🔥 = high probability · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

### 1. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Commission / Committee | Subject |
|---|---|
| 1. Hunter Committee, 1919 | Jallianwala Bagh massacre |
| 2. Muddiman Committee, 1924 | Working of dyarchy |
| 3. Lee Commission, 1923–24 | Public services |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why all three:** Hunter (1919) inquired into **Jallianwala Bagh**; Muddiman (1924) reviewed **dyarchy**; Lee (1923–24) examined the **public services** and recommended the **Public Service Commission**, set up in 1926.

> ⚠️ **Beware the "all three are correct" reflex.** Many candidates instinctively distrust option (c) and downgrade to (b), reasoning that UPSC "must" have hidden an error. That instinct costs marks. **Verify each pair on its own merits and report what you find** — sometimes everything is right.
>
> 🔑 **The 1919–1927 committee cluster, by function:**
> **Hunter (1919)** massacre · **Lee (1923–24)** services · **Muddiman (1924)** dyarchy · **Butler (1927)** princely states · **Simon (1927)** the whole Act.

</details>

### 2. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Act | First introduced |
|---|---|
| 1. Charter Act, 1853 | Open competition for the civil services |
| 2. Indian Councils Act, 1892 | Direct election to the legislative councils |
| 3. Government of India Act, 1919 | Provincial autonomy |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Only pair 1 is correct — **open competition** for the civil services was introduced by the **Charter Act of 1853**.

**Why not 2:** The 1892 Act introduced **indirect** election, and did so without using the word "election" at all. **Direct** election came with the **1909** Act and was expanded in **1919**.

**Why not 3:** **Provincial autonomy is 1935.** The 1919 Act gave the provinces **dyarchy**, not autonomy. What 1919 *did* introduce for the first time was **bicameralism at the Centre** — exactly the true pairing the question withheld.

> 🔑 **Indirect vs direct is the distinction being tested, and it recurs.** 1892 = indirect and unnamed; 1909 = direct but restricted and communal; 1919 = direct and wider. Three Acts, three different things, all called "election" in casual summaries.

</details>

### 3. 🔥
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Lahore Resolution of the Muslim League
2. Congress ministries resign in the provinces
3. Quit India Movement
4. Cripps Mission

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
- (b) 1 – 2 – 4 – 3
- (c) 2 – 1 – 3 – 4
- (d) 1 – 2 – 3 – 4

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Congress ministries resign **October–November 1939** → **Lahore Resolution, March 1940** → **Cripps Mission, March 1942** → **Quit India, August 1942**.

**Why not (b):** It puts the Lahore Resolution before the resignations. The League passed the Lahore ("Pakistan") Resolution **after** Congress had left office and after it had observed a **"Day of Deliverance"** in December 1939 — the sequence is causally connected.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Congress's resignation created a political vacuum in the provinces which the League filled, and its bargaining position strengthened sharply thereafter. That is why the resignation of 1939 is treated as one of the turning points on the road to Partition, not merely a protest against the war.
>
> ⚠️ Note also that **Cripps (March 1942) precedes Quit India (August 1942)** — the failure of the mission is what triggered the movement. Cause before effect, five months apart.

</details>

### 4. 🔥
**Statement-I:** Dyarchy was abolished in the provinces by the Government of India Act, 1935.

**Statement-II:** The Government of India Act, 1935 introduced provincial autonomy, under which the Governor was to act on the advice of ministers responsible to the provincial legislature.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Both correct, and II explains I. Dyarchy existed because some provincial subjects were withheld from ministerial control. Provincial autonomy meant **all** provincial subjects came under responsible ministers — which necessarily **abolishes** the transferred/reserved split. The abolition is not merely accompanied by autonomy; it is **entailed** by it.

> 🔑 **Exam skill — a reliable test for option (a).** Ask: *"If Statement-II is true, must Statement-I follow?"* If yes, choose (a). If both are merely true and coexist without one compelling the other, choose (b). Here, autonomy logically requires the end of dyarchy, so (a).

</details>

### 5. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Round Table Conference | Feature |
|---|---|
| 1. First (1930–31) | Congress did not participate |
| 2. Second (1931) | Gandhi attended as the sole Congress representative |
| 3. Third (1932) | Congress did not participate |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why all three:** Congress boycotted the **First** (it was engaged in Civil Disobedience); **Gandhi attended the Second** as Congress's sole representative, following the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 1931)**; and Congress stayed away from the **Third**, which was also poorly attended and led to the **White Paper of 1933**.

>
> This single chain covers a large slice of both Polity Chapter 1 and Modern History. Learn it once, use it twice.

</details>

### 6.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Establishment of the Supreme Court at Calcutta
2. Establishment of the Federal Court
3. Establishment of the Supreme Court of India
4. Abolition of the Privy Council's jurisdiction over India

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 – 2 – 3 – 4
- (b) 1 – 2 – 4 – 3
- (c) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
- (d) 1 – 4 – 2 – 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Supreme Court at Calcutta **1774** → Federal Court **1937** → Privy Council jurisdiction abolished **1949** → Supreme Court of India **28 January 1950**.

**Why not (a):** It reverses the last two. The **Privy Council's jurisdiction was abolished in 1949**, *before* the Supreme Court came into existence — the old apex had to be removed to make room for the new one.

> 🔑 **Concept — the judiciary's route to independence:**
> **1774** a colonial court in Calcutta, jurisdiction limited to Presidency towns → **1861** High Courts established (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras) → **1937** Federal Court, but appeals still go to London → **1949** Privy Council jurisdiction abolished → **1950** Supreme Court of India, final and Indian.
>
> 📰 The **Supreme Court completed 75 years in January 2025**, which put this lineage back in circulation. Note the first Chief Justices: **Sir Maurice Gwyer** (Federal Court) and **H.J. Kania** (Supreme Court).

</details>

### 7. 🔥 ⚠️
**Statement-I:** The Indian Councils Act, 1909 permitted the provincial legislative councils to have a non-official majority.

**Statement-II:** The Indian Councils Act, 1909 thereby introduced responsible government in the provinces.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Statement-I is correct. **Statement-II is false** — a non-official majority does **not** amount to responsible government, and none existed in 1909. Responsible government reached the provinces partially in **1919** and fully in **1935**.

> ⚠️ **The trap is the word "thereby."** It invites you to accept a false conclusion because it is presented as flowing from a true premise. A non-official majority means the executive can be **outvoted**; responsible government means the executive can be **removed**. Being outvoted and losing office are entirely different things — the executive in 1909 was answerable to London regardless of any vote in India.
>
> **Discipline:** in Statement I/II questions, a **false Statement-II never rescues a true Statement-I**. Judge II on its own facts first, always.

</details>

Consider the following pairs:

| Feature of the Indian Constitution | Borrowed from |
|---|---|
| 1. Directive Principles of State Policy | Ireland |
| 2. Concurrent List | Australia |
| 3. Emergency provisions | Government of India Act, 1935 |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why all three:** **DPSP from Ireland** (which took the idea from the Spanish Constitution); **Concurrent List from Australia**, along with freedom of trade and commerce and the joint sitting device; **emergency provisions from the 1935 Act** (with the suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency influenced by the **Weimar Constitution of Germany**).

> - **Amendment procedure** — South Africa · **Liberty, Equality, Fraternity** — France

### 10. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Government of India Act, 1919 is regarded as having introduced responsible government in the provinces only partially.

**Statement-II:** Under the Act, ministers were responsible to the legislature for transferred subjects, while reserved subjects — including finance — remained outside their control.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Both correct, and II is precisely why I is said. Responsibility extended to **some** subjects and not others — that partition **is** the partiality.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Note that the word "partially" in Statement-I is doing real work. Had it said "introduced responsible government in the provinces" without qualification, the statement would be **false**. Single qualifiers — *partially, largely, only, first* — decide truth value in this format.
>
> **Read qualifiers as carefully as facts.** In counting-format questions especially, a statement is often made false by one adverb.

</details>

| 2. Transfer of the capital from Calcutta to Delhi announced | 1911 |

### 13. 🔥 ⚠️
**Statement-I:** The Government of India Act, 1935 abolished the Council of India.

**Statement-II:** The Government of India Act, 1935 abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Statement-I is correct — the **Council of India** (1858–1935) was abolished, the Secretary of State being given a team of **advisers** instead.

**Statement-II is false:** the **Secretary of State survived until the Indian Independence Act, 1947**.

> ⚠️ **This pairing is engineered to exploit a natural assumption** — that abolishing the assisting body must mean abolishing the office it assisted. It does not. The Council died in **1935**; the office it served died in **1947**.

> 🔑 **Institution lifespans — a compact, high-yield list:**
> Board of Control **1784–1858** · Court of Directors **–1858** · Council of India **1858–1935** · Secretary of State **1858–1947** · Viceroy **1858–1947** · Federal Court **1937–1950** · Governor-General **1833–1950**.

### 16. 🔥
**Statement-I:** Under the Indian Independence Act, 1947, India became independent but did not immediately become a republic.

**Statement-II:** The Indian Independence Act, 1947 abolished the office of the Secretary of State for India.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Both statements are true, but II does not explain I.**

The reason India was not yet a republic is that until **26 January 1950** the **British monarch remained head of state**, represented by a Governor-General. A **republic** is a state whose head of state is not a hereditary monarch.

Statement-II is true — the **Secretary of State for India was abolished** in 1947, his functions passing to the Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs. But that abolition concerns **who administered India from London**, not **who was head of state**. It is evidence of *sovereignty*, not of *republican status*.

> 🔑 **Concept — sovereignty and republicanism are different questions.** India was **fully sovereign from 15 August 1947** (the British government retained no responsibility for its governance) yet **not a republic until 26 January 1950**. Canada and Australia are sovereign to this day and are still not republics.
>
> ⚠️ This precision matters because Prelims asks about "sovereign", "republic" and "dominion" as **technical terms**, especially in Preamble questions in **Chapter 4**.

</details>

### 18. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the Charter Acts:

1. The Charter Act of 1813 ended the Company's monopoly of trade with China.
2. The Charter Act of 1833 introduced open competition for recruitment to the civil services.
3. The Charter Act of 1853 renewed the Company's charter for a further twenty years.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d) — all three are wrong, each by exactly one step:**
- **1** The 1813 Act ended the monopoly of trade with **India**. The **China and tea** monopolies survived until **1833**.
- **2** The 1833 Act only **attempted** open competition and the provision was **negated**. It was **introduced in 1853**.
- **3** The 1853 Act extended the Company's rule **without specifying any period**, unlike every earlier Charter Act. That signalled Parliament could end Company rule whenever it chose — and it did so five years later.

> ⚠️ **This is the "off by one Act" trap at full strength.** Every statement is a true fact attached to the neighbouring Act. Only the **sequence** protects you — which is why the ladders in Sets 01 and 02 matter more than any single date.

> ⚠️ **Statement 3 is false because of a pattern, not a fact.** All previous Charter Acts used twenty years, so "twenty years" *feels* right. UPSC builds false statements by extending a genuine pattern one step too far. **The exception is always the examinable part.**

</details>

### 20. 🔥 ⚠️
**Statement-I:** The Government of India Act, 1935 is described as the blueprint of the Indian Constitution.

**Statement-II:** The Government of India Act, 1935 contained a preamble declaring the sovereignty of the Indian people and a chapter on fundamental rights.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Statement-I is correct — roughly two-thirds of the Constitution derives from the 1935 Act. **Statement-II is emphatically false**: the Act had **no fundamental rights** and **no declaration of popular sovereignty**. Those absences are exactly what "blueprint but **not** its spirit" means.

> ⚠️ **A very well-built trap.** Because Statement-I is true and famous, Statement-II inherits credibility from it. But Statement-II names the two things the 1935 Act **conspicuously lacked**.
>
> 🔑 **Rule:** if a statement attributes **rights** or **popular sovereignty** to any colonial statute, it is false. The British granted **powers and procedures**, never **rights against the State**. That is the deepest single distinction between the colonial Acts and the Constitution.

</details>

### 22. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the Indian Independence Act, 1947:

1. It empowered the Constituent Assembly to repeal any Act of the British Parliament.
2. It made the Governor-General and the provincial Governors constitutional heads.
3. It provided that the princely states would automatically accede to the Dominion of India.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 1 and 2 are correct.

**Why not 3:** **Paramountcy lapsed**, leaving the states **free** to accede to either dominion **or remain independent**. Nothing was automatic. Accession was achieved by **political negotiation** — Patel and V.P. Menon, through the **Instrument of Accession** — not by operation of the statute.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is the difference between what the **law** provided and what **politics** delivered. The Act created a legal vacuum; the vacuum was filled by persuasion, and in three cases (**Junagadh, Hyderabad, Jammu & Kashmir**) by other means.
>
> 📰 That vacuum is why **Article 370** existed at all, and why the **December 2023 Supreme Court judgment** on its abrogation turned on what J&K's accession did and did not preserve.

</details>

### 25. 🔥 📰
**Statement-I:** The Constitution of India retained a large part of the administrative structure created by the Government of India Act, 1935.

**Statement-II:** The Constituent Assembly considered that a country facing partition, communal violence and the integration of princely states required a strong and continuous administrative machinery.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Both correct, and II explains I. The Assembly's choice to retain the colonial machinery — the all-India services, the emergency provisions, a strong Centre, the Governor's discretion — was **deliberate and defended**, not merely inertia.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set, and the most useful thing you can carry into the Mains paper.**
>
> Retention was a **decision made under specific conditions**: Partition, mass migration, communal violence, the integration of 560 princely states, and the fear of further fragmentation. **Ambedkar** defended a strong Centre on exactly this ground; **Patel** defended the all-India services as the **"steel frame"** holding India together.
>
> This lets you answer the standard critical question — *"is the Indian Constitution too colonial?"* — with balance rather than assertion:
> **The critique:** we kept emergency provisions, preventive detention, the Governor's discretion, and a Centre that can dismiss elected state governments.
> **The defence:** these were retained knowingly, for a state facing dissolution — and they were placed under **judicial review**, a **sovereign electorate** and **Fundamental Rights**, none of which existed before.
>
> 📰 This is a genuinely live argument, not a settled one. It surfaces every time **Article 356**, a **Governor's delay in granting assent**, or **preventive detention** is in the news — and Prelims now regularly draws its conceptual questions from exactly these debates.

</details>
