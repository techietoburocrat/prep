# Chapter 1 · Practice Set 05 — Government of India Act, 1919 & Dyarchy

[← Set 04](Set_04.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 06 →](Set_06.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟡 Prelims Standard**
**Covers:** Montagu Declaration 1917 · GoI Act 1919 · dyarchy · Muddiman Committee · Simon Commission

> **The idea behind this set.** This is the **most-asked Act** in the chapter. It is also the one candidates get wrong most often, because dyarchy has a mirror-image in 1935 and the two get swapped.
>
> Fix this sentence in your head before you begin: **1919 introduced dyarchy in the provinces. 1935 abolished it there and provided it at the Centre, where it never operated.** Half the questions in this set and the next turn on that one line.

🔥 = high probability · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

### 1. 🔥
The Government of India Act, 1919 is popularly known as the

- (a) Morley-Minto Reforms
- (b) Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
- (c) Minto-Chelmsford Reforms
- (d) Montagu-Curzon Reforms

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Edwin Montagu**, Secretary of State for India, and **Lord Chelmsford**, Viceroy. Secretary of State first, Viceroy second — the same convention as Morley-Minto.

> 🔑 **Note the date it took effect.** The Act was passed in **1919** but **came into force in 1921**. UPSC has asked precisely this gap. Passed 1919, operative 1921.

</details>

### 2. 🔥
The declaration of 20 August 1917, which promised the "progressive realisation of responsible government in India as an integral part of the British Empire", was made by

- (a) Lord Chelmsford
- (b) Ramsay MacDonald
- (c) Lord Minto
- (d) Edwin Montagu

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **Montagu's August Declaration, 1917**, promised "the increasing association of Indians in every branch of the administration and the **gradual development of self-governing institutions**, with a view to the **progressive realisation of responsible government** in India as an integral part of the British Empire."

> 🔑 **Concept — this is a genuine turning point, and worth knowing as a Mains point too.** For the first time Britain stated **responsible government** as the *declared goal* of British policy in India. Everything before 1917 offered consultation; from 1917 the argument shifted from *whether* to *when*.
>
> But read the words carefully: **"gradual"**, **"progressive"**, and **"as an integral part of the British Empire."** Three qualifiers, each a brake. That gap between the promise and the qualifiers is why the 1919 Act disappointed almost everyone.

</details>

### 3. 🔥 ⚠️
Under the Government of India Act, 1919, dyarchy was introduced in the

- (a) Centre only
- (b) provinces only
- (c) both Centre and provinces
- (d) princely states only

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Dyarchy operated **in the provinces**, where provincial subjects were split into **transferred** and **reserved**.

**Why not (c):** There was **no dyarchy at the Centre in 1919**. Dyarchy at the Centre was *provided for* by the **1935** Act — and **never came into being**.

> ⚠️ **The single most-swapped fact in Chapter 1. Memorise this table:**
> | | **Provinces** | **Centre** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **1919 Act** | Dyarchy **introduced** | No dyarchy |
> | **1935 Act** | Dyarchy **abolished** (provincial autonomy) | Dyarchy **provided** — never operated |
>
> One line to carry it: ***"Dyarchy moved upstairs and then died."***

</details>

### 4. 🔥
Under the dyarchical scheme of 1919, which of the following were **transferred** subjects?

1. Education
2. Local government
3. Police
4. Land revenue

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 3 and 4 only
- (c) 1, 2 and 3 only
- (d) 2 and 4 only

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Transferred** subjects — education, local government, public health, agriculture, industries. Administered by the Governor **with Indian ministers responsible to the legislative council**.

**Why not 3 and 4:** **Police, jails, land revenue and — decisively — finance** were **reserved**, administered by the Governor with his executive council and answerable to nobody in India.

> 🔑 **The rule that answers every transferred/reserved question:** the British kept **power and money**; they gave away **welfare**.
> **Reserved = the sinews of control** (police, jails, land revenue, **finance**).
> **Transferred = the services** (education, health, local government, agriculture).
> If an option puts anything to do with **money or coercion** in the transferred list, it is wrong.

</details>

### 5. 🔥
The principal reason why dyarchy failed in practice was that

- (a) Indian ministers refused to take office
- (b) finance remained a reserved subject, so ministers had no control over funds for their own departments
- (c) the transferred subjects were abolished within two years
- (d) the provincial legislatures were never constituted

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Finance was reserved.** A minister for education was answerable to the legislature for schools, but could not command the money to build them. Responsibility without resources is not responsibility at all.

> 🔑 **Concept — the analogy that makes this stick.** Parents let the teenager control the TV and the fridge, and keep the house keys, the bank account and the front door. It *looks* like power-sharing. It is not.
>
> This is also why the **Muddiman Committee's minority report** called dyarchy unworkable in principle rather than merely badly implemented — and why the **1935 Act abolished it in the provinces**. The failure was structural, not administrative. That distinction is exactly what "Examine why dyarchy failed" wants in Mains.

</details>

### 6. 🔥
The Government of India Act, 1919 introduced bicameralism at the Centre. The two Houses were

- (a) the Council of State and the Legislative Assembly
- (b) the Council of India and the Central Legislative Council
- (c) the Chamber of Princes and the Legislative Assembly
- (d) the Federal Assembly and the Council of State

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Upper House — **Council of State**; Lower House — **Legislative Assembly**. The old Indian Legislative Council was replaced by these two.

**Why not the others:**
- (b) The **Council of India** was the advisory body in **London** (1858–1935).
- (c) The **Chamber of Princes** was set up in **1921** as a consultative body for the princely rulers — real, but not a House of the legislature.
- (d) **Federal Assembly** belongs to the **1935** Act's never-operated federal scheme.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Both Houses had a **majority chosen by direct election** — the first direct elections in India. Note the tenures: **Council of State, 5 years; Legislative Assembly, 3 years.** The Council of State was also **male-only**.

</details>

### 7. 🔥
Under the Act of 1919, how many of the six members of the Viceroy's Executive Council (other than the Commander-in-Chief) were required to be Indian?

- (a) One
- (b) Two
- (c) Three
- (d) Four

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Three of six.** Note the progression — **1909 permitted** Indian association without prescribing a number (result: one, S.P. Sinha); **1919 prescribed three of six.**

> ⚠️ **The trap is the word.** 1909 = **may**. 1919 = **shall, three of six**. UPSC attaches the 1919 number to the 1909 Act and waits. Permission versus requirement.

</details>

### 8. 🔥
The Government of India Act, 1919 relaxed central control over the provinces by

- (a) granting the provinces full autonomy in all matters
- (b) demarcating and separating central and provincial subjects
- (c) abolishing the office of Provincial Governor
- (d) transferring residuary powers to the provinces

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** For the **first time**, central and provincial subjects were **demarcated and separated**, and each legislature could make laws on its own list.

**Why not (a):** **Provincial autonomy** is the **1935** Act. In 1919 the structure remained **unitary and centralised** — Laxmikanth's own phrasing, and a favourite quotation for statement-based questions.

> 🔑 **Concept — the ancestry of the Seventh Schedule:**
> **1919** central and provincial subjects separated (**two** categories) → **1935** **three** lists: Federal, Provincial, Concurrent → **1950** **Seventh Schedule**: Union, State, Concurrent.
> Two lists, then three, then three again. When you reach **Chapter 14, Centre-State Relations**, this is where it starts.

</details>

### 9. 🔥 📰
The Government of India Act, 1919 provided for the establishment of a Public Service Commission. It was actually set up in

- (a) 1919
- (b) 1921
- (c) 1926
- (d) 1935

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Provided for by the 1919 Act, the **Public Service Commission was established in 1926**.

> 📰 **The lineage of the body conducting your exam — know all four steps:**
> **1926** Public Service Commission established (on the **Lee Commission's** recommendation) → **1935 Act** created a **Federal Public Service Commission** and provincial PSCs → **1947** it became the **Union Public Service Commission** → **1950** UPSC became a **constitutional body** under **Articles 315–323**.
>
> And the deeper root: open competitive recruitment itself begins with the **Charter Act of 1853** and the **Macaulay Committee, 1854**. From 1853 to 2027 is one unbroken institutional line, and you are standing at the end of it.

</details>

### 10. 🔥
The Act of 1919 extended separate electorates to which of the following?

1. Sikhs
2. Indian Christians
3. Anglo-Indians
4. Europeans

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 1, 2 and 3 only
- (c) 2, 3 and 4 only
- (d) 1, 2, 3 and 4

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why all four:** The 1919 Act extended communal representation from Muslims (1909) to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans**.

> 🔑 **The expansion chain — worth reciting:**
> **1909** Muslims → **1919** Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans → **1932** Depressed Classes (Communal Award) → **1935** women and labour given special representation.
> Every reform Act widened the fracture it claimed to be managing.

</details>

### 11. 🔥
Which one of the following was created for the first time by the Government of India Act, 1919?

- (a) The office of the Secretary of State for India
- (b) The office of the Governor-General of India
- (c) The office of the Viceroy of India
- (d) The office of the High Commissioner for India in London

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Act created the **High Commissioner for India in London** and transferred to him some functions previously discharged by the Secretary of State.

**Why not the others:** (a) and (c) are **1858**; (b) is **1833**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Note why this matters — some functions moved **out of the Secretary of State's hands** for the first time since 1858. It is a small crack in the London monopoly. The title survives today: India's envoys to **Commonwealth countries** are still called **High Commissioners**, not Ambassadors — a colonial usage still in daily service.

</details>

### 12. 🔥
The Government of India Act, 1919 separated provincial budgets from the central budget for the first time. The corresponding provision in the Constitution today is

- (a) Article 112
- (b) Article 266
- (c) Article 202
- (d) Article 280

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Article 202** — the **Annual Financial Statement of a State**, laid before the State legislature. The 1919 separation of provincial budgets is its direct ancestor.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) **Article 112** is the Union's Annual Financial Statement.
- (b) **Article 266** — Consolidated Fund and Public Account.
- (d) **Article 280** — the **Finance Commission**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** A separate provincial budget is what makes a province a real unit of government rather than a branch office. Fiscal separation is the substance of federalism; legislative separation is only its form. Watch the sequence — **1919** separate budgets → **1935** provincial autonomy → **1950** Articles 202 and 280 → **1992** the 73rd/74th Amendments and **State Finance Commissions (Article 243-I)**.

</details>

### 13. 🔥 ⚠️
The Government of India Act, 1919 provided for the appointment of a statutory commission to review its working after ten years. That commission

- (a) was appointed in 1929 and was chaired by Lord Simon
- (b) was never appointed
- (c) was appointed in 1927, two years ahead of schedule, and was chaired by Sir John Simon
- (d) was appointed in 1930 and included three Indian members

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** The **Simon Commission** was appointed in **1927** — **two years early**, because the Conservative government in Britain feared losing the next election to Labour.

**Why not (d):** The Commission had **seven members, all British** — its defining feature and the reason for the boycott. It submitted its report in **1930**.

> 🔑 **Concept — why it was boycotted.** Not because Indians objected to a review, but because a commission to determine India's constitutional future contained **no Indian**. Hence *"Simon Go Back"*, the black-flag demonstrations, and the **lathi charge at Lahore in which Lala Lajpat Rai was injured** and died soon after.
>
> **Consequence chain:** Simon boycott → **Nehru Report (1928)**, India's own constitutional draft → **Jinnah's Fourteen Points** → **Round Table Conferences (1930–32)** → **Communal Award (1932)** → **GoI Act 1935**. The 1935 Act's entire lineage runs through this boycott.

</details>

### 14. 🔥
The Muddiman Committee, appointed in 1924, was set up to

- (a) examine the working of dyarchy under the 1919 Act
- (b) recommend the reorganisation of provinces on linguistic lines
- (c) inquire into the Jallianwala Bagh massacre
- (d) recommend reforms in the recruitment of the civil services

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The **Muddiman Committee (Reforms Enquiry Committee), 1924** examined the working of the 1919 Act, and dyarchy in particular. Its **majority report** proposed changes within the existing framework; its **minority report** — including **Jinnah** and **Tej Bahadur Sapru** — held dyarchy to be a **structural failure** requiring full provincial autonomy.

**Why not the others:**
- (c) That was the **Hunter Committee (1919)**.
- (d) That was the **Lee Commission (1923–24)**, on the public services.

> ⚠️ **Committee names cluster in this decade and get confused.** Fix them by **function**: Hunter = massacre · Lee = services · Muddiman = dyarchy · Simon = review of the whole Act. Four committees, four jobs, one decade.

</details>

### 15. 🔥
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Montagu's declaration on responsible government
2. Government of India Act comes into force
3. Appointment of the Simon Commission
4. Establishment of the Public Service Commission

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 – 2 – 4 – 3
- (b) 1 – 2 – 3 – 4
- (c) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
- (d) 1 – 3 – 2 – 4

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Montagu Declaration **1917** → Act in force **1921** → PSC established **1926** → Simon Commission **1927**.

**Why not (b):** It swaps the last two. The **PSC (1926) precedes Simon (1927)** by one year — another consecutive-year trap.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** The Act is **passed in 1919** but **comes into force in 1921**. A chronology question can use either date, so read the item's wording exactly: "comes into force" means **1921**, "passed"/"enacted" means **1919**. This kind of precision is what separates 95 from 110 in Prelims.

</details>

### 16. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Government of India Act, 1919 established responsible government at the Centre.

**Statement-II:** Under the Act of 1919, the Governor-General and his executive council were not answerable to the central legislature.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **Statement-I is false; Statement-II is true** — and II is precisely what makes I false.

Responsible government means an executive **answerable to, and removable by, the legislature**. The Governor-General's executive council was answerable only to **London**. There was therefore **no responsible government at the Centre** under the 1919 Act — only **partial** responsibility in the provinces, for transferred subjects.

> 🔑 **Concept — the two-question test from Set 03, applied:**
> **Who chooses?** Members of the Assembly — mostly **directly elected**. ✅ Partial pass.
> **Can they remove the executive?** **No.** ❌ Fail.
> So 1919 gives **representation without responsibility** at the Centre, and **partial responsibility** in the provinces (transferred subjects only). That one sentence is the whole Act.

</details>

### 17.
Under the Act of 1919, the reserved subjects in the provinces were administered by

- (a) the Governor acting with ministers responsible to the legislature
- (b) the Governor acting with his executive council, without responsibility to the legislature
- (c) the provincial legislature directly
- (d) the Governor-General in Council

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Reserved subjects — **Governor plus executive council**, with **no responsibility** to the legislature. Transferred subjects — **Governor plus Indian ministers**, who **were** responsible to the legislative council.

> 🔑 **Concept:** The Governor sat at the top of **both** halves. That is what made it dyarchy rather than a partition of power — one head, two bodies, and only one of them answerable to Indians. And when the two halves disagreed, the Governor decided.

</details>

### 18. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Provision | Act |
|---|---|
| 1. First direct elections in India | Government of India Act, 1919 |
| 2. First separation of central and provincial subjects | Government of India Act, 1919 |
| 3. First bicameral central legislature | Government of India Act, 1919 |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why all three:** Direct elections, the separation of central and provincial subjects, and bicameralism at the Centre are **all firsts of the 1919 Act**.

> ⚠️ **Resist the reflex to downgrade.** When every pair points to the same Act, candidates suspect a trick and pick "Only two". Here the common answer *is* the point — 1919 is unusually rich in firsts.

> 🔑 **The "firsts" of 1919 — three of them, all high-yield:**
> **1.** First **direct elections**
> **2.** First **bicameralism**
> **3.** First **separation of central and provincial subjects**
> Plus first separate **provincial budgets**, and the first statutory provision for a **Public Service Commission**. That is five firsts in one Act — which is exactly why it is asked more than any other in this chapter.

</details>

### 19.
The franchise under the Government of India Act, 1919 was extended to approximately

- (a) 3% of the population
- (b) 10% of the population
- (c) 25% of the population
- (d) all adult males

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Roughly **3%**, on the basis of **property, tax payment or education**. The **1935** Act raised it to about **10%**.

> 🔑 **The franchise ladder, complete:**
> **1892** no election → **1909** restricted direct election → **1919** ~**3%** → **1935** ~**10%** → **1950** **all adults**, Article 326 (voting age lowered from 21 to **18** by the **61st Amendment, 1988**).
>
> Note the shape of the final step: from one in ten to everybody, at a stroke. That is not an extension of the colonial trajectory — it is a **repudiation** of it.

</details>

### 20. 🔥 ⚠️
Which one of the following statements about the Government of India Act, 1919 is **not** correct?

- (a) It introduced direct elections for the first time in India
- (b) It made the central legislature bicameral
- (c) It made the structure of government in India federal
- (d) It separated provincial budgets from the central budget

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c) is wrong (and therefore the answer):** Despite separating central and provincial subjects, the structure under the 1919 Act **remained unitary and centralised**. A **federal** structure was proposed only by the **1935** Act — and even that federation **never came into being**.

**Why the others are correct:** (a), (b) and (d) are all genuine firsts of 1919.

> ⚠️ **The distinction being tested:** a **division of subjects** is not the same as a **federation**. A federation requires that the units derive their authority **from the constitution**, not from a delegation the Centre can revoke. In 1919 the Centre could override the provinces at will — so it was **devolution**, not federalism.
>
> Carry that distinction forward: it is the heart of **Chapter 13**, and it is why India is called **"quasi-federal"** rather than federal.

</details>

### 21.
The Council of State under the Act of 1919 had a tenure of ___ years and the Legislative Assembly a tenure of ___ years.

- (a) 5 and 3
- (b) 3 and 5
- (c) 6 and 5
- (d) 5 and 4

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Council of State — 5 years; Legislative Assembly — 3 years.** The Council of State admitted **only male members**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Upper House longer, Lower House shorter — the same logic as today's **Rajya Sabha (6-year terms, staggered)** and **Lok Sabha (5 years)**. The upper chamber is designed for continuity, the lower for responsiveness. The principle predates the Constitution by three decades.

</details>

### 22. 🔥
Which one of the following best explains why the Government of India Act, 1919 satisfied neither the British nor the Indian nationalists?

- (a) It gave Indians control over finance while denying them control over education
- (b) It abolished separate electorates against Muslim wishes
- (c) It conceded partial responsibility in the provinces while retaining an unaccountable executive at the Centre
- (d) It transferred all provincial subjects to Indian ministers at once

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Nationalists were offered **responsibility without power** in the provinces (no control over finance) and **no responsibility at all** at the Centre. British officials, meanwhile, resented sharing even that much. A halfway house satisfies neither side of the argument.

**Why not (b):** The 1919 Act **extended** separate electorates to four more groups — the opposite of abolishing them.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Congress's response tells the story. The Act was denounced as **"disappointing and unsatisfactory"**, and the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)** followed within a year of it coming into force. Constitutional concession and mass agitation are not alternatives in this period — the inadequacy of the first repeatedly produces the second.

</details>

### 23. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the transferred subjects under the Act of 1919:

1. They were administered by the Governor with his executive council, without responsibility to the legislative council.
2. The ministers in charge of them were appointed from among the nominated members of the legislative council.
3. The Governor could not overrule his ministers on transferred subjects in any circumstances.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d) — all three are wrong:**
- **1** describes the **reserved** subjects, not the transferred ones. Transferred subjects were administered by the Governor **with ministers responsible to the legislative council**.
- **2** inverts the position: ministers were drawn from the **elected** members, not the nominated ones.
- **3** is false because the Governor retained **overriding powers** even over transferred subjects.

> ⚠️ **A "None" answer is rare but real, and this is how it is built** — one statement swapped with its opposite category, one inverted, one absolute. If every statement feels slightly off, trust that reading rather than forcing two of them to be true.

> ⚠️ **Absolutes are almost always false in colonial constitutional law.** Words like "in any circumstances", "never", "wholly", "entirely" are red flags. The British **never** gave up a final override — not in 1919, not in 1935, not until 1947.
>
> **Use this as a working rule in the exam:** in a statement about colonial reforms, an absolute denial of British power is false roughly nine times out of ten. It will not always be right, but it is the best prior you have when genuinely unsure.

</details>

### 24. 📰
The Chamber of Princes, established in 1921, was

- (a) the upper house of the central legislature
- (b) the executive council of the Viceroy
- (c) a consultative body of the rulers of princely states, with no legislative power
- (d) a federal court for disputes between princely states

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** A purely **consultative** forum for the rulers of the princely states, inaugurated in 1921. It had **no legislative authority** and could not bind anyone.

> 🔑 **Concept — and it matters more than it looks.** The princely states covered roughly **two-fifths of India's territory** and a quarter of its population, yet they were outside British India's constitutional structure. They were bound to the Crown by **paramountcy**, which **lapsed** under the Indian Independence Act, 1947 — leaving 500-odd states legally free to choose.
>
> That lapse is what made **integration** the great problem of 1947–49, solved by Sardar Patel and V.P. Menon through the **Instrument of Accession**. The princes' refusal to join the 1935 federation is also **the reason it never came into being** — the subject of the next set.

</details>

### 25. 🔥
Which one of the following best describes the constitutional significance of the Government of India Act, 1919?

- (a) It established a federal constitution for India
- (b) It made responsible government the declared goal, and took the first structural steps towards it, without delivering it
- (c) It transferred sovereignty from the British Parliament to the Indian legislature
- (d) It ended the system of communal representation

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Following Montagu's 1917 declaration, the Act built the **machinery** of responsible government — direct elections, bicameralism, ministers answerable to a legislature, separated subjects and budgets — while withholding the **substance**: finance stayed reserved, the Centre stayed unaccountable, and the Governor kept an override.

**Why not the others:** (a) is 1935 (and it never operated); (c) never happened before 1947; (d) is inverted — separate electorates were **extended**.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** 1919 is the **hinge** of the chapter. Before it, Indians were consulted; after it, Indians governed — partially, in some subjects, in some provinces, under supervision. It is the first Act where the question stops being *"may Indians speak?"* and becomes *"how much may Indians decide?"*
>
> **Sort the Act into two columns and you have understood it:**
> **Machinery given:** direct elections · bicameralism · responsible ministers · separated subjects · separate budgets · PSC.
> **Substance withheld:** finance reserved · Governor's override · unaccountable Centre · 3% franchise · separate electorates widened.
>
> Every question in this set is a sample from one of those two columns.

</details>
