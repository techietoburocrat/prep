# Chapter 1 · Practice Set 07 — Missions, Transfer of Power & the Indian Independence Act, 1947

[← Set 06](Set_06.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 08 →](Set_08.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟡 Prelims Standard**
**Covers:** August Offer 1940 · Cripps Mission 1942 · Wavell Plan 1945 · Cabinet Mission 1946 · Mountbatten Plan · Indian Independence Act 1947

> **The idea behind this set.** Between 1940 and 1947 the British made **four offers**, each larger than the last, and each rejected. Then in 1947 they conceded everything at once. Understanding *why each offer failed* is what makes this period memorable — and it is exactly what the analytical Prelims question tests.
>
> ⚠️ This set overlaps with **Chapter 2 (Making of the Constitution)** by design. The missions produced the Constituent Assembly, so you will meet them twice. Learning them properly now means Chapter 2 costs you half the effort.

🔥 = high probability · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

### 1. 🔥
The August Offer of 1940 was made by

- (a) Lord Wavell
- (b) Lord Linlithgow
- (c) Lord Mountbatten
- (d) Sir Stafford Cripps

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Lord Linlithgow**, Viceroy, announced the August Offer in **August 1940**, after the outbreak of the Second World War.

> 🔑 **Fix the Viceroy sequence for this period, because every offer is identified by its author:**
> **Linlithgow** (1936–43) → August Offer 1940, Cripps Mission 1942, Quit India 1942
> **Wavell** (1943–47) → Wavell Plan / Simla Conference 1945, Cabinet Mission 1946
> **Mountbatten** (1947) → 3 June Plan, transfer of power
> Three Viceroys, eight years, the end of an empire.

</details>

### 2. 🔥
Which of the following was offered for the first time in the August Offer of 1940?

- (a) Immediate independence
- (b) Partition of India
- (c) Dominion status as the objective for India
- (d) Universal adult franchise

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** For the **first time**, **dominion status** was declared as the **objective** of British policy in India, along with an expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council and a constitution-making body after the war.

> 🔑 **Concept — the escalating ladder of British goals, one of the cleanest timelines in the syllabus:**
> **1917** Montagu: *responsible government* as the goal
> **1940** August Offer: *dominion status* as the goal
> **1942** Cripps: dominion status **after the war**, with the right of provinces **not to accede**
> **1946** Cabinet Mission: a **constitution-making body** now
> **1947** Independence Act: **sovereign dominions immediately**
> Each step concedes what the previous one withheld — always about five years too late to be accepted.

</details>

### 3. 🔥 ⚠️
The provision of the August Offer, 1940 that Congress found most objectionable was

- (a) the promise of dominion status
- (b) the assurance that no future constitution would be adopted without the agreement of minorities
- (c) the expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council
- (d) the proposal to set up a war advisory council

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Offer assured minorities that **no future constitution would be adopted without their agreement** — in effect a **minority veto** over India's constitutional future. Congress saw this as handing the Muslim League a permanent block on any settlement. The League, unsurprisingly, welcomed it.

> 🔑 **Concept — this is the pivot of the whole 1940–47 period.** From here on, every British proposal contains some form of communal safeguard, and every safeguard strengthens the League's claim to be the sole voice of Muslim India.
>
> **The chain:** August Offer 1940 (minority veto) → Cripps 1942 (provinces may **not accede**) → Cabinet Mission 1946 (**grouping** of provinces) → 3 June Plan 1947 (**partition**). Each concession makes the next one harder to refuse. Trace that line and Partition stops being an event and becomes a **process**.

</details>

### 4. 🔥
The Cripps Mission of 1942 proposed that after the war

- (a) India would immediately become a sovereign republic
- (b) India would be granted dominion status, with provinces free not to accede to the Indian Union
- (c) India would be partitioned into two dominions at once
- (d) the Government of India Act, 1935 would be permanently retained

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Dominion status** after the war, a **constitution-making body**, and — critically — the right of any province **not to accede** to the Indian Union, plus the right of Indian states to sign a new treaty.

> 🔑 **Concept:** The non-accession clause conceded the **principle of partition** in everything but name, three years before it happened. Congress opposed it precisely because it accepted that India might not remain one country.
>
> **Gandhi's verdict is the line to remember:** the offer was ***"a post-dated cheque on a crashing bank."*** Everything promised lay after the war; nothing was given during it.

</details>

### 5. 🔥
The Wavell Plan of 1945 and the Simla Conference broke down primarily over

- (a) the demand for immediate independence
- (b) the boundaries of Bengal and Punjab
- (c) the future of the princely states
- (d) the question of who would nominate the Muslim members of the Executive Council

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Plan proposed reconstituting the Viceroy's Executive Council with **equal representation for Caste Hindus and Muslims**. It collapsed when **Jinnah insisted that the Muslim League alone** could nominate Muslim members — a claim Congress rejected, since it had Muslim members of its own.

> 🔑 **Concept — this is the real fight of the 1940s, and it is not about independence.** By 1945 nobody seriously disputed that the British were leaving. The question was **who speaks for whom**. Jinnah's demand was for recognition of the League as the **sole representative of Indian Muslims**; Congress's claim was to represent **all Indians**. Both claims could not be true.
>
> That, and not any disagreement with Britain, is what the last two years of negotiation are about.

</details>

### 6. 🔥
The Cabinet Mission of 1946 consisted of which of the following?

1. Pethick-Lawrence
2. Stafford Cripps
3. A.V. Alexander
4. Lord Wavell

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1, 2 and 3 only
- (b) 1, 2 and 4 only
- (c) 2, 3 and 4 only
- (d) 1, 2, 3 and 4

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Three members — **Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary of State for India, chairman), **Sir Stafford Cripps** (President of the Board of Trade) and **A.V. Alexander** (First Lord of the Admiralty).

**Why not 4:** **Lord Wavell was the Viceroy**, not a member of the Mission. He participated in the negotiations but was not one of the three.

> 🔑 **Note the repeat appearance.** **Cripps came twice** — leading his own mission in **1942** and as a member of the **Cabinet Mission in 1946**. UPSC has used this overlap to build distractors. Cripps 1942 = solo mission; Cripps 1946 = one of three.

</details>

### 7. 🔥
The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946

- (a) accepted the demand for a separate state of Pakistan
- (b) proposed the continuation of British rule for ten more years
- (c) proposed immediate partition along religious lines
- (d) rejected the demand for Pakistan and proposed a Union of India with a limited centre

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Mission **expressly rejected** the demand for a sovereign Pakistan and proposed instead a **Union of India** whose centre would handle only **foreign affairs, defence and communications**, with all residuary powers in the provinces, and provinces free to form **groups**.

> ⚠️ **A very common misconception.** The Cabinet Mission did **not** propose Pakistan; it proposed a device intended to make Pakistan **unnecessary** — a weak centre plus provincial grouping. Partition came a year later, through the **Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947**.
>
> 🔑 Note the structural inversion: the Cabinet Mission wanted a **weak centre with residuary powers in the provinces** — exactly the opposite of what the Constituent Assembly eventually built. Ambedkar and Nehru drew a direct lesson from Partition: a weak centre invites fragmentation. **Our strong-centre federalism is, in part, a reaction to this plan.**

</details>

### 8. 🔥
Under the Cabinet Mission Plan, the provinces were to be divided into how many groups?

- (a) Two
- (b) Three
- (c) Four
- (d) Five

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Three groups** — **Group A** (Hindu-majority provinces of the centre), **Group B** (Punjab, NWFP, Sindh, Baluchistan) and **Group C** (Bengal and Assam).

> 🔑 **Concept:** The grouping formula was the Mission's attempt to give the League a Muslim-majority bloc **without partition**. The ambiguity over whether grouping was **compulsory or optional** is precisely what wrecked the plan — Congress said optional, the League said compulsory, and each accepted the plan on its own reading.
>
> **The lesson worth remembering:** the plan failed not because it was rejected but because it was **accepted in two incompatible senses**. A constitutional text that means different things to different parties is not a settlement.

</details>

### 9. 🔥
The Constituent Assembly of India was constituted under the

- (a) Government of India Act, 1935
- (b) Mountbatten Plan, 1947
- (c) Indian Independence Act, 1947
- (d) Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Constituent Assembly was constituted in **November 1946** under the scheme of the **Cabinet Mission Plan**. It first met on **9 December 1946**.

**Why not (c):** The 1947 Act did not create the Assembly — it **transformed** it, making it a **fully sovereign body** free to frame any constitution and to repeal any British statute, including the Independence Act itself. Before that, it worked under British-derived authority.

> 🔑 **Concept — the change of status is the point:**
> **Under the Cabinet Mission (1946):** the Assembly exists, but with limited authority derived from a British plan.
> **Under the Independence Act (1947):** the Assembly becomes **sovereign**, and doubles as the **legislature** of the dominion.
> This is the constitutional hinge of Chapter 2. You are meeting it early, and it will pay for itself there.

</details>

### 10. 🔥 ⚠️
The Constituent Assembly first met on

- (a) 26 November 1946
- (b) 22 January 1947
- (c) 9 December 1946
- (d) 15 August 1947

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **9 December 1946**. **Dr Sachchidananda Sinha** was elected temporary (provisional) chairman as the oldest member; **Dr Rajendra Prasad** was elected permanent President on **11 December 1946**.

**Why the others matter too — learn all four dates:**
- **13 December 1946** — Nehru moves the **Objectives Resolution**; adopted **22 January 1947**.
- **26 November 1949** — Constitution **adopted**. *(Note: 1949, not 1946 — the option above is the trap.)*
- **26 January 1950** — Constitution **came into force**.

> ⚠️ **26 November 1946 vs 26 November 1949.** Same day and month, three years apart. This is the single most effective date trap in Indian polity, and it is asked repeatedly.
> 📰 **26 November** is observed as **Samvidhan Divas (Constitution Day)**, and **26 November 2024** marked **75 years of adoption** — heavily covered, and a reason this cluster of dates is even more likely than usual.

</details>

### 11. 🔥
The Mountbatten Plan, which formed the basis of the Indian Independence Act, 1947, was announced on

- (a) 20 February 1947
- (b) 18 July 1947
- (c) 3 June 1947
- (d) 15 August 1947

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** The **3 June Plan, 1947**.

**The other dates are all real and all examinable:**
- **20 February 1947** — Attlee's declaration that Britain would quit India by **June 1948**.
- **18 July 1947** — the **Indian Independence Act receives royal assent**.
- **15 August 1947** — transfer of power.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Note how the deadline **moved forward**, from June 1948 to August 1947 — Mountbatten advanced it by ten months. The compression is a standard explanation for the scale of the violence that accompanied Partition, since the **Radcliffe boundary award** was published only **after** independence.

</details>

### 12. 🔥
The Indian Independence Act, 1947 abolished which of the following offices?

1. Secretary of State for India
2. Viceroy
3. Governor-General

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 1 and 2 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Act abolished the **Secretary of State for India** (his functions passing to the Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs) and the office of **Viceroy**.

**Why not 3:** The **Governor-General survived** — but transformed. Each dominion got a Governor-General, appointed by the King **on the advice of the dominion cabinet**, acting as a **constitutional (nominal) head**.

> ⚠️ **"Viceroy" and "Governor-General" were held by the same person, which is why this is confusing.** From 1858 the Governor-General of India was **also** styled Viceroy — the Viceroy title expressing his role as the Crown's representative to the princely states. In 1947 the **Viceroy title dies** (there are no more paramountcy relations to represent) while the **Governor-General title survives** in a wholly new, constitutional form.
>
> **Mountbatten** became the first Governor-General of free India; **C. Rajagopalachari** was the last (1948–50); **Jinnah** was Governor-General of Pakistan.

</details>

### 13. 🔥 📰
The Indian Independence Act, 1947 provided that the paramountcy of the Crown over the princely states would

- (a) be transferred to the Dominion of India
- (b) be exercised jointly by India and Pakistan
- (c) continue for a further ten years
- (d) lapse, leaving the states free to join either dominion or remain independent

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **Paramountcy lapsed.** Roughly **560 princely states** became legally free to accede to India, accede to Pakistan, or remain independent.

> 🔑 **Concept — this is the biggest constitutional problem of 1947, and it was solved politically, not legally.** **Sardar Patel** and **V.P. Menon**, through the States Department, secured accession of almost every state before 15 August using the **Instrument of Accession**, under which a state ceded only **defence, external affairs and communications**. The hard cases — **Junagadh, Hyderabad and Jammu & Kashmir** — were resolved separately.

> 📰 **The most live current-affairs link in this chapter.** **Jammu & Kashmir** acceded by an Instrument of Accession in **October 1947**, which is the historical basis of **Article 370**. Article 370 was abrogated in **August 2019**, and in **December 2023** a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court **upheld the abrogation**, holding that J&K retained **no element of internal sovereignty** after accession. The state was reorganised into the Union Territories of **Jammu & Kashmir** and **Ladakh**.
>
> A Prelims question here can enter from **1947** (lapse of paramountcy, Instrument of Accession) or from **2023** (the judgment). They are the same fact, seventy-six years apart. **Know both ends.**

</details>

Until new constitutions were framed, the two dominions and their provinces were to be governed under the

- (c) Government of India Act, 1919
- (d) Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

### 15. 🔥
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Cabinet Mission arrives in India
2. Cripps Mission
3. Attlee's declaration on quitting India
4. Mountbatten Plan announced

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 – 2 – 3 – 4
- (b) 2 – 3 – 1 – 4
- (c) 2 – 1 – 3 – 4
- (d) 2 – 3 – 4 – 1

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Cripps Mission **March 1942** → Cabinet Mission arrives **March 1946** → Attlee's declaration **20 February 1947** → Mountbatten Plan **3 June 1947**. That is **2 – 1 – 3 – 4**.

**Why not (b):** It puts Attlee's declaration before the Cabinet Mission. The famous Attlee statement fixing a withdrawal date is **February 1947** — *after* the Cabinet Mission of 1946, not before it.

> ⚠️ **Why this one is genuinely hard.** Attlee made **two** relevant announcements — one in **early 1946** announcing that a Cabinet Mission would be sent, and the **20 February 1947** declaration fixing June 1948 as the deadline for withdrawal. Only the second is "Attlee's declaration" in exam usage.

> 🔑 **Exam skill — anchor the ends.** Cripps is unambiguously first (1942) and Mountbatten unambiguously last (June 1947). That alone leaves only (b) and (c), and the Cabinet Mission's arrival in **March 1946** settles it. You never had to date all four items precisely.

</details>

### 16. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Indian Independence Act, 1947 established the Constituent Assembly of India.

**Statement-II:** The Act empowered the Constituent Assemblies of the two dominions to frame any constitution and to repeal any Act of the British Parliament, including the Indian Independence Act itself.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **Statement-I is false; Statement-II is true.**

The Constituent Assembly was **established in November 1946 under the Cabinet Mission Plan** and first met on **9 December 1946** — before the 1947 Act existed. What the Independence Act did was **transform** it: it made the Assembly **fully sovereign** and gave it legislative functions as well.

**Statement-II is correct**, and it describes that transformation. **Sovereignty means being subject to no higher legislative authority**, and the power to repeal any British statute — including the Act granting the power — is precisely that.

> ⚠️ **Distinguish creating an institution from changing its status.** Created 1946, made sovereign 1947. UPSC uses this distinction across the chapter: the Governor-General (created 1833, made nominal 1947) and the Federal Court (created 1937, replaced 1950) work the same way.

> 🔑 **Concept — the elegance of this provision.** Britain passed a law saying its own law could be repealed by an Indian body. That is the legal mechanism by which sovereignty actually transferred. Constitutionally, our Constitution's authority derives **not** from the 1947 Act but from **"We, the People of India"** in the Preamble — which is why the Preamble says the people **"adopt, enact and give to ourselves"** this Constitution.
>
> Compare with **Australia and Canada**, whose constitutions remained British statutes for decades (patriated in 1982 and 1986). India cut the cord immediately. That comparison is strong Mains material and a possible analytical Prelims question.

</details>

### 18. 🔥
Which one of the following statements about the position of the Governor-General after the Indian Independence Act, 1947 is correct?

- (a) He was appointed by the British Parliament and was answerable to it
- (b) He was appointed by the British King on the advice of the dominion cabinet and acted as a constitutional head
- (c) He retained all the discretionary powers he had under the 1935 Act
- (d) He was elected by the Constituent Assembly

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Appointed by the **King on the advice of the dominion cabinet**, and made a **constitutional (nominal) head** bound to act on ministerial advice. Britain's government retained **no responsibility** for the government of India or Pakistan.

**Why not (c):** The **overriding and discretionary powers ended**. This is the decisive change of 1947 — the same office, emptied of independent authority.

> 🔑 **Concept — the two-question test, final application.**
> **Who chooses?** Ministers chosen by an elected legislature. ✅
> **Can the legislature remove the executive?** **Yes.** ✅
> For the first time since 1773, **both** answers are yes. That, and nothing else, is what independence meant constitutionally.
>
> And note the continuity: the Governor-General becomes a nominal head bound by advice — exactly the position of the **President under Article 74** today. The office survived; the power moved.

</details>

### 19.
The Indian Independence Act, 1947 discontinued

- (a) the Federal Court
- (b) the Government of India Act, 1935
- (c) the provincial legislatures
- (d) appointments to the civil services and reservation of posts by the Secretary of State

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Act **discontinued appointments to the civil services and the reservation of posts by the Secretary of State for India**. Existing members of the services retained their guarantees.

**Why not (a) and (b):** The **Federal Court continued** until 1950, and the **1935 Act continued** as the working constitution.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is the moment the **Indian Civil Service stops being an imperial service**. It becomes the **Indian Administrative Service** under the Constitution — an **All-India Service** under **Article 312**, recruited by the UPSC, serving both Union and States.
>
> Sardar Patel defended the retention of an all-India service in the Constituent Assembly with the phrase that gave it its nickname: the **"steel frame"** of India. Note the irony deliberately — a colonial instrument of control, retained on purpose as an instrument of national unity. That is the whole chapter in one institution.

</details>

The Interim Government formed in September 1946 was headed by

### 22. 🔥
Which one of the following best explains why the Cripps Mission failed?

- (a) It offered independence immediately, which Congress considered premature
- (b) It refused to discuss the future of the princely states
- (c) It proposed the partition of Bengal and Punjab
- (d) It offered only post-war dominion status while conceding the right of provinces not to accede

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Two objections, both fatal. Everything offered lay **after the war**, giving Congress nothing in the present — hence Gandhi's "post-dated cheque". And the right of provinces **not to accede** conceded the principle of partition, which Congress would not accept.

**Why not (c):** The partition of Bengal and Punjab came with the **Mountbatten Plan of 1947**.

> 🔑 **Concept — the shape of every failed offer between 1940 and 1946 is the same:**
> **Too little** (only post-war, only dominion status, only advisory), **too late** (each offer concedes what was demanded five years earlier), and **too divisive** (each contains a communal safeguard that hardens the League's position).
>
> This one framework answers *"why did X mission fail"* for **all four missions**. Learn the pattern rather than four separate lists of reasons.

</details>

### 23. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the Indian Independence Act, 1947:

1. It declared India an independent and sovereign state with effect from 15 August 1947.
2. It provided that the two dominions had the right to secede from the British Commonwealth.
3. It abolished the Federal Court established under the 1935 Act.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The dominions were expressly given the **right to secede from the Commonwealth**.

**Why not 3:** The **Federal Court continued** until the Supreme Court replaced it on **28 January 1950**.

> 🔑 **Concept — the Commonwealth question has a neat sequel.** India became a **republic** in 1950 and yet **remained in the Commonwealth**, under the **London Declaration of 1949**, which accepted the King as "**Head of the Commonwealth**" rather than as head of state. India's insistence made membership compatible with republican status — the reason the modern Commonwealth exists in its present form.
>
> Both **secession from** and **remaining in** the Commonwealth are examinable; do not assume independence meant leaving.

</details>

### 25. 🔥 📰
Which one of the following best describes the constitutional character of India between 15 August 1947 and 26 January 1950?

- (a) A sovereign republic governed by its own Constitution
- (b) A federation of princely states under a common Governor-General
- (c) A British colony with limited self-government
- (d) A dominion governed under the Government of India Act, 1935, with a sovereign Constituent Assembly framing a new Constitution

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** For two years and five months India was a **dominion** — the King was still head of state, represented by a Governor-General — governed under the **1935 Act as adapted**, while a **sovereign Constituent Assembly** wrote the Constitution and simultaneously acted as the **legislature**.

**Why not (a):** India became a **republic** only on **26 January 1950**, when the Constitution came into force and the President replaced the King as head of state.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** Independence and republic are **two different events, two and a half years apart**:
> **15 August 1947** — sovereignty transferred; India independent, but a **dominion** with the King as head of state.
> **26 November 1949** — Constitution **adopted** by the Constituent Assembly.
> **26 January 1950** — Constitution **in force**; India becomes a **sovereign democratic republic**.
>
> 📰 Note why **26 January** was chosen rather than any other date: it commemorates **26 January 1930**, when the Congress at its **Lahore session (Dec 1929)** had declared **Purna Swaraj** and called for it to be observed as Independence Day. The Constituent Assembly deliberately made the promise of 1930 the reality of 1950.
>
> Between **Independence Day** and **Republic Day** sits the entire drafting of the Constitution — which is **Chapter 2**, and where you go next in your reading.
