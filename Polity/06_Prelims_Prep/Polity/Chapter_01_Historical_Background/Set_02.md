# Chapter 1 · Practice Set 02 — Company Rule II: The Charter Acts (1813–1853)

[← Set 01](Set_01.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 03 →](Set_03.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟢 Foundation**
**Covers:** Charter Act 1813 · Charter Act 1833 · Charter Act 1853

> **The idea behind this set.** Three Acts, twenty years apart, each renewing the Company's charter — and each taking something away. Trade dies in two steps (1813, 1833), then the exam you are writing is born (1853). The Company walks in as a merchant and walks out as a civil service.

🔥 = high probability · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

---

### 1. 🔥
The Charter Act of 1813 is best known for

- (a) ending the Company's monopoly of trade with India
- (b) ending the Company's monopoly of trade with China
- (c) making the Company a purely administrative body
- (d) separating legislative and executive functions

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The 1813 Act threw the **India trade open to all British subjects**, ending the Company's exclusive privilege there. This is its headline provision.

**Why not the others:**
- (b) The **China trade and the tea monopoly survived 1813** and were abolished only in **1833**. ⚠️ This is the single most-tested distinction in the Charter Acts.
- (c) The Company became purely administrative in **1833**.
- (d) That is **1853**.

> 🔑 **Concept — trade dies in two steps:**
> **1813** = India trade opened (tea + China retained).
> **1833** = tea + China gone; commerce ends completely.
> Say it as one sentence and you will never mix them.

</details>

### 2. 🔥
Which of the following was/were provided for by the Charter Act of 1813?

1. Permission for Christian missionaries to come to India and preach.
2. An annual grant for the revival of literature and the encouragement of learning among Indians.
3. Authorisation to provincial governments to impose taxes.

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why all three:** The 1813 Act opened India to missionaries (the "pious clause"), set aside **₹1 lakh a year** for reviving literature and encouraging the learned natives of India and promoting the sciences, and empowered provincial governments to levy taxes and punish non-payment.

> 🔑 **Concept:** That ₹1 lakh clause is the **first instance of the state accepting responsibility for education in India**. Small sum, enormous precedent — it leads straight to Macaulay's Minute (1835), then to English education, then to the class of English-educated Indians who eventually demanded self-rule. The British funded their own opposition.

</details>

### 3. ⚠️
Under the Charter Act of 1813, the East India Company retained its monopoly over trade in

- (a) cotton and indigo
- (b) spices and silk
- (c) salt and opium
- (d) tea, and trade with China

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Exactly two exclusions survived — **tea**, and **trade with China**. Both went in 1833.

**Why not the others:** Salt and opium were revenue **monopolies of the government**, a different thing from a chartered trading monopoly — a distinction the question is testing.

> 🔑 **Memory hook:** *"1813 — everything opened except the teacup and China."*

</details>

### 4. 🔥
The Charter Act of 1833 designated the Governor-General of Bengal as the Governor-General of India. Who was the first to hold this office?

- (a) Warren Hastings
- (b) Lord Cornwallis
- (c) Lord William Bentinck
- (d) Lord Canning

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Lord William Bentinck** became the first Governor-General of India in 1833.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Warren Hastings — first Governor-General of **Bengal** (1773).
- (d) Lord Canning — first **Viceroy** (1858).

> 🔑 **The three "firsts" that get swapped every year:**
> | Title | Act | Person |
> |---|---|---|
> | Governor-General of **Bengal** | 1773 | **Warren Hastings** |
> | Governor-General of **India** | 1833 | **William Bentinck** |
> | **Viceroy** | 1858 | **Lord Canning** |
> Learn this table cold. It is worth 2 marks somewhere in your career.

</details>

### 5. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the Charter Act of 1833:

1. It vested the Governor-General of India with exclusive legislative power for the whole of British India.
2. It deprived the Governors of Bombay and Madras of their legislative powers.
3. Laws made under this Act were called Regulations, as under earlier Acts.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 1 and 2 are correct — legislative power was centralised in the Governor-General of India, and Bombay and Madras lost theirs.

**Why not 3:** It is exactly inverted. Laws made under **previous** Acts were called **Regulations**; laws made under the **1833** Act were called **Acts**. That change in nomenclature is itself a favourite question.

> 🔑 **Concept:** 1833 is called the **final step towards centralisation** of British India. Note the shape — the same shape that produces the **Union List's** dominance in the Seventh Schedule a century later.

</details>

### 6. 🔥
The Charter Act of 1833 ended the East India Company's commercial activities and declared that it would henceforth hold its Indian territories

- (a) as a sovereign power in its own right
- (b) jointly with the Board of Control
- (c) in trust for His Majesty, his heirs and successors
- (d) on lease from the Mughal Emperor

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** The Act provided that the Company's Indian possessions were held **"in trust for His Majesty, his heirs and successors."** The Company was reduced from owner to trustee, and from merchant to administrator.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Follow the legal fiction across three Acts — *1784* "British possessions in India" → *1813* Crown sovereignty asserted → *1833* held **in trust** for the Crown. Each step brings the Crown closer to formal ownership, which finally arrives in **1858**. This is a 75-year slow-motion nationalisation.

</details>

### 7.
Who was appointed the first Law Member of the Governor-General's Council under the Charter Act of 1833?

- (a) Lord Macaulay
- (b) Sir Elijah Impey
- (c) Sir Henry Maine
- (d) Sir Charles Wood

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Thomas Babington Macaulay** was the first Law Member. Crucially, the Law Member had **no right to vote** in the Council under the 1833 Act — he became a **full member with voting rights only under the Charter Act of 1853**.

**Why not (d):** Sir Charles Wood is associated with the **Wood's Despatch of 1854**, the "Magna Carta of English education in India" — a common and deliberate confusion in this area.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Macaulay appears three times in your syllabus — first **Law Member** (1833), author of the **Minute on Education** (1835), and head of the **first Law Commission** (1834) that drafted the **Indian Penal Code**. One man, three questions.

</details>

### 8. 🔥 📰
The first Law Commission, set up under the Charter Act of 1833, is most significant because its work led to the drafting of the

- (a) Indian Penal Code
- (b) Indian Contract Act
- (c) Government of India Act
- (d) Indian Succession Act

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The **first Law Commission (1834)**, chaired by Macaulay, produced the draft of the **Indian Penal Code**, which was enacted in **1860** and came into force in **1862**.

> 📰 **Current-affairs hook — very high yield.** The IPC was **replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023**, which came into force on **1 July 2024**, along with the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (replacing CrPC, 1973) and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (replacing the Indian Evidence Act, 1872). A 164-year-old colonial code, drafted under an Act of 1833, was retired in your lifetime.
>
> Prelims can approach this from either end — the colonial origin **or** the 2024 replacement. Know both. Note especially the trap: the three new laws are **not** "amendments"; they **repeal and replace**.

</details>

### 9. ⚠️
The Charter Act of 1833 attempted to introduce open competition for the selection of civil servants. What happened to this provision?

- (a) It was implemented immediately across British India
- (b) It was restricted to Europeans by a subsequent royal proclamation
- (c) It was struck down by the Supreme Court at Calcutta
- (d) It was negated after opposition from the Court of Directors

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Act declared that **no Indian should be debarred from holding any office** on grounds of religion, place of birth, descent or colour — a remarkable statement for 1833. But the provision on **open competition was negated** after the Court of Directors objected, since patronage in appointments was their most valuable privilege.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Note the pattern — a **liberal declaration on paper, defeated in practice**. Open competition was actually introduced only in **1853**, and even then Indians faced practical barriers (the exam was held in **London** only, until 1922 when it began to be held in India as well).

> 💡 The 1833 anti-discrimination clause is the ancestor of **Article 16(2)** — no discrimination in public employment on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth or residence. Read the two side by side; the resemblance is not accidental.

</details>

### 10. 🔥
Which one of the following was introduced for the first time by the Charter Act of 1853?

- (a) Separation of the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's Council
- (b) The system of double government
- (c) Bicameralism in the central legislature
- (d) Separate electorates for religious minorities

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** For the first time, the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's Council were **separated**, and a distinct legislative wing — the **Indian (Central) Legislative Council** — was created.

**Why not the others:** (b) is 1784, (c) is 1919, (d) is 1909.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is where **legislation is first treated as a special function requiring special machinery**. The council adopted British parliamentary procedures and functioned as a **mini-Parliament** — the earliest institutional ancestor of the Indian Parliament.

</details>

### 11. 🔥
The Charter Act of 1853 differed from all earlier Charter Acts in one important respect. It

- (a) renewed the Company's charter for thirty years instead of twenty
- (b) did not specify any period for the continuance of Company rule
- (c) permanently vested Indian territories in the Crown
- (d) transferred the Company's army to the British Crown

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Every earlier Charter Act renewed the Company's rule for a fixed **twenty years**. The 1853 Act extended it **without specifying any period**, meaning Parliament could terminate it **at any time**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is a signal, not an accident. Parliament was already contemplating the end of Company rule five years before it happened. When the Revolt of 1857 came, the legal groundwork for taking over — an open-ended tenure revocable at will — was already in place. **1853 makes 1858 easy.**

</details>

### 12.
Under the Charter Act of 1853, the number of Directors of the Company was

- (a) increased from 18 to 24, with six nominated by the Crown
- (b) left unchanged, but their term was reduced to two years
- (c) reduced from 18 to 12, with four nominated by the Crown
- (d) reduced from 24 to 18, with six nominated by the Crown

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Directors were cut from **24 to 18**, and **six of the 18 were to be nominated by the Crown** — putting government appointees inside the Company's own governing body.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Read what this actually does. It is not an administrative tidy-up; it is the Crown **placing its own people inside the boardroom**. Five years before abolition, the Company was already partly government-run from within.

</details>

### 13. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the Indian (Central) Legislative Council created by the Charter Act of 1853:

1. It introduced local representation, with four of the six new legislative members appointed by the local governments.
2. It functioned as a mini-Parliament, adopting the procedures of the British Parliament.
3. Its members included elected representatives of Indian subjects.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Four of the six new legislative members were appointed by the local governments of **Madras, Bombay, Bengal and Agra** — the first recognition of the local element in central law-making. And the body deliberately adopted British parliamentary procedure.

**Why not 3:** There was **no election and no Indian representation** whatsoever. Indians were associated with law-making only from **1861**, and then only by **nomination**.

> 🔑 **Concept — "local representation" ≠ "Indian representation".** The 1853 Act gave provinces a voice, not Indians. Members appointed by local *governments* were still British officials. UPSC exploits exactly this ambiguity.

</details>

### 14. 🔥 📰
The competitive examination for the civil services introduced by the Charter Act of 1853 was given effect on the recommendation of

- (a) the Macaulay Committee
- (b) the Muddiman Committee
- (c) the Hunter Commission
- (d) the Aitchison Commission

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The **Macaulay Committee (Committee on the Indian Civil Service), 1854** recommended the machinery for open competitive recruitment.

**Why not the others:**
- (b) **Muddiman Committee (1924)** reviewed **dyarchy** under the 1919 Act.
- (c) **Hunter Commission (1882)** dealt with education; a different Hunter Committee (1919) inquired into Jallianwala Bagh.
- (d) **Aitchison Commission (1886)** on public services recommended classification into Imperial, Provincial and Subordinate services.

> 📰 **Note where you are sitting.** The exam you are preparing for descends directly from the Charter Act of **1853** and the Macaulay Committee of **1854**. The **UPSC** itself traces to the Public Service Commission set up in **1926** under the 1919 Act, and became a **constitutional body** under **Articles 315–323**.

</details>

### 15.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Company's trade monopoly with China abolished
2. Company's trade monopoly with India abolished
3. Open competition for the civil services introduced
4. Governor-General of India created

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 2 – 4 – 1 – 3
- (b) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
- (c) 1 – 2 – 4 – 3
- (d) 4 – 2 – 1 – 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** India trade monopoly ended **1813** → China/tea monopoly ended **1833** → Governor-General of India created **1833** → open competition **1853**.

**Note on 1 and 4:** Both are 1833, so either order between them could be argued; the code resolves it because only one option puts 1813 first and 1853 last. **In a chronology question, anchor the first and last item first** — it usually eliminates three options without your having to sequence the middle.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** Never sequence all four items. Find the earliest, eliminate; find the latest, eliminate. You will normally be down to one option in fifteen seconds.

</details>

### 16. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Charter Act of 1833 is regarded as the first step towards the decentralisation of administration in British India.

**Statement-II:** The Charter Act of 1833 concentrated all civil and military powers, and exclusive legislative authority for all British India, in the Governor-General of India.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **Statement-I is false and Statement-II is true** — and notice that II actually *contradicts* I.

The 1833 Act is the **final step towards centralisation**, not the first step towards decentralisation. It concentrated all civil, military and exclusive legislative authority in the Governor-General of India and **stripped Bombay and Madras of their legislative powers**.

**Decentralisation begins in 1861**, when the Indian Councils Act **restored** legislative powers to Bombay and Madras — followed by **Mayo's financial decentralisation (1870)** and **Ripon's local self-government resolution (1882)**.

> ⚠️ **A powerful trap worth studying.** Statement-II is true and is the **evidence against** Statement-I. When II is true but points the opposite way, the answer is (c) or (d) — never (a). **Read II as evidence, not as endorsement.**

</details>

### 17.
The Charter Act of 1813 asserted the sovereignty of the British Crown over

- (a) the princely states of India
- (b) the Company's territorial possessions in India
- (c) the Indian Ocean trade routes
- (d) the Mughal court at Delhi

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Act expressly asserted the **Crown's sovereignty over the Company's territorial possessions in India** — a formal claim that these lands belonged to Britain, not to a corporation.

**Why not (a):** The princely states were bound by **subsidiary alliances and later by paramountcy**, a relationship of suzerainty rather than sovereignty — and paramountcy **lapsed** in 1947 under the Indian Independence Act.

> 🔑 **Concept — three different legal relationships, frequently confused:**
> **Sovereignty** (Crown over British India) · **Paramountcy** (Crown over princely states) · **Trusteeship** (Company holding for the Crown, from 1833).

</details>

### 18. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Charter Act | Provision |
|---|---|
| 1. 1813 | Grant of ₹1 lakh annually for education |
| 2. 1833 | Company's territories held in trust for His Majesty |
| 3. 1853 | Law Member became a full member of the Governor-General's Council |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why all three:** The ₹1 lakh education grant is **1813**; the Company was reduced to holding its territories **"in trust for His Majesty, his heirs and successors"** in **1833**; and the Law Member — added in 1833 **without a vote** — became a **full voting member** in **1853**.

> ⚠️ **The pairing this question deliberately avoided.** "1833 — Indian Civil Service established by open competition" would have been **false**: the 1833 Act only **attempted** open competition and the provision was **negated** by the Court of Directors. It was actually introduced in **1853**.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** Watch for the difference between **"provided for"**, **"attempted"** and **"introduced"**. UPSC uses this gap constantly — the 1833 Act *attempted*; the 1853 Act *introduced*. Same subject, different verb, different answer.

</details>

### 19.
Which one of the following statements about the Charter Act of 1833 is **not** correct?

- (a) It ended the Company's commercial activities entirely
- (b) It provided for a Law Commission for the codification of Indian laws
- (c) It abolished the Board of Control established in 1784
- (d) It required measures to be taken for mitigating the state of slavery

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c) is wrong (and therefore the answer):** The Board of Control survived until the **Government of India Act, 1858**, which ended double government. Nothing in 1833 abolished it.

**Why the others are correct:** The Company became purely administrative; the **first Law Commission (1834)** was provided for; and the Act directed the Governor-General-in-Council to take measures for **mitigating slavery** — slavery was finally abolished in India by **Act V of 1843**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** The slavery clause is easy to overlook and therefore a good question. Sequence it: **1833** mitigate → **1843** abolish → and much later, **Article 23** of the Constitution prohibits traffic in human beings and forced labour.

</details>

### 20. 🔥 📰
Which one of the following best explains why the Charter Act of 1813 is considered a turning point in the history of Indian education?

- (a) It made English the medium of instruction in all schools
- (b) It committed public funds to education for the first time
- (c) It established the first three universities in India
- (d) It made primary education compulsory in the Presidency towns

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The ₹1 lakh annual grant was the **first time the colonial state accepted financial responsibility for education**. Modest in amount, decisive in principle.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) English as the medium followed **Macaulay's Minute, 1835** and Bentinck's resolution — not the 1813 Act itself. ⚠️ Classic trap.
- (c) The universities of **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** were founded in **1857**, following **Wood's Despatch, 1854**.
- (d) Compulsory primary education was never achieved in colonial India; it came only with the **86th Amendment (2002)** inserting **Article 21A**, and the **RTE Act, 2009**.

> 📰 **Current-affairs bridge:** trace the whole arc — 1813 (first funding) → 1835 (English medium) → 1854 (Wood's Despatch) → 1857 (first universities) → 1937 (Wardha Scheme) → **Article 45 (DPSP)** → **Article 21A (2002)** → **RTE 2009** → **NEP 2020**, whose mother-tongue-medium push is in a real sense an argument *with* Macaulay, 190 years later. Questions on education policy love this arc.

</details>

### 21.
Under the Charter Act of 1853, the Indian (Central) Legislative Council consisted of six new legislative members. Of these, four were appointed by the local governments of

- (a) Bengal, Bombay, Madras and Punjab
- (b) Bengal, Bombay, Madras and Burma
- (c) Bengal, Bombay, Madras and Awadh
- (d) Bengal, Bombay, Madras and Agra

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The four were **Bengal, Bombay, Madras and Agra**.

**Why not the others:** Each distractor is a territory not yet in a position to send a member in 1853 — **Punjab** was annexed in 1849 but not yet organised as such a province, **Awadh** was annexed only in **1856**, and **Burma** was annexed progressively through the Anglo-Burmese wars.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** In a list-based option, test the **odd one against the date**. If a territory was not under British administration in the relevant year, the option falls. Chronology beats memorisation.

</details>

### 22. 🔥
The provision of the Charter Act of 1833 declaring that no Indian subject of the Company should be debarred from holding any office by reason of religion, place of birth, descent or colour is most directly reflected today in

- (a) Article 14
- (b) Article 15(1)
- (c) Article 16(2)
- (d) Article 29(2)

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Article 16(2)** — no citizen shall be ineligible for or discriminated against in respect of any **employment or office under the State** on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth or residence. The 1833 clause is about **holding office**, which makes 16(2) the exact match.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Article 14 is general equality before law — true but not the *most direct* match.
- (b) Article 15(1) bars State discrimination generally, not employment specifically.
- (d) Article 29(2) concerns admission to **educational institutions**.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When several Articles are "sort of" right, pick the one whose **subject matter** matches the phrase in the question. Here the phrase is "holding any office" → employment → **16(2)**. UPSC rewards precision, not proximity.

</details>

### 23.
Which one of the following correctly states the effect of the Charter Act of 1833 on the legislative powers of the Presidencies?

- (a) Bombay and Madras retained concurrent legislative powers with Bengal
- (b) All Presidencies were given equal and independent legislative powers
- (c) Bombay and Madras gained legislative powers for the first time
- (d) Bombay and Madras lost their legislative powers to the Governor-General of India

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Legislative power was **stripped from Bombay and Madras** and vested exclusively in the Governor-General of India.

> 🔑 **Concept — and here is the useful part.** This is the **low point** of provincial power. The pendulum then swings back: **1861** restores legislative powers to Bombay and Madras (the beginning of decentralisation) → **1919** separates central and provincial subjects → **1935** grants provincial autonomy → **1950** creates a State List.
>
> **1833 down, 1861 up.** If you can state that reversal in one line, you understand this chapter's spine better than most candidates.

</details>

### 24. ⚠️
Consider the following statements:

1. The Charter Act of 1853 was the last of the series of Charter Acts.
2. The Charter Act of 1853 provided for the appointment of a Secretary of State for India.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) Both 1 and 2
- (d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** 1853 was indeed the **last** Charter Act.

**Why not 2:** The **Secretary of State for India** was created by the **Government of India Act, 1858**, and was a member of the **British Cabinet**, assisted by a **15-member Council of India**. The office was abolished by the **Indian Independence Act, 1947**.

> 🔑 **Life of the Secretary of State: 1858 → 1947.** Learn the birth and death dates of each colonial office. Half the questions in this chapter are answered by knowing when an institution **started** and when it **stopped**.

</details>

### 25. 🔥
Which one of the following best captures the constitutional significance of the Charter Acts taken together (1793–1853)?

- (a) They progressively transferred sovereignty from the Crown to the Company
- (b) They progressively decentralised administration in favour of the provinces
- (c) They progressively introduced representative institutions in India
- (d) They progressively converted a trading corporation into an organ of government

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Across sixty years the Company lost its trade (1813, 1833), lost its commercial character entirely (1833), was made a trustee for the Crown (1833), had Crown nominees placed on its board and an open-ended, revocable tenure (1853). By 1853 it was a government department wearing a merchant's coat.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Sovereignty moved **towards** the Crown, not away from it — a straight inversion.
- (b) The direction was **centralisation**, not decentralisation, until 1861.
- (c) No representative institutions existed until **1861** (nomination) and **1892** (indirect election).

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** UPSC increasingly asks the "**which one best describes**" question, where no fact will save you — only understanding will. There is no date to recall here, and yet it is the hardest question in the set.
>
> **Your one-line summary of Sets 01–02:** *A private company was slowly nationalised by statute, and the machinery it left behind became the Indian state.*

</details>
