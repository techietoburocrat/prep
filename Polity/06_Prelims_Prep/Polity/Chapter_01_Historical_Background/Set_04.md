# Chapter 1 · Practice Set 04 — Morley-Minto 1909 & Communal Representation

[← Set 03](Set_03.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 05 →](Set_05.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟡 Prelims Standard**
**Covers:** Indian Councils Act 1909 · separate electorates · Communal Award · Poona Pact · the constitutional answer

> **The idea behind this set.** One Act in 1909 introduced separate electorates, and the consequences run all the way to Partition — and then to Articles 325 and 330, which are India's deliberate answer to it. This is the most **consequential** Act in the chapter, and the one where Prelims most often asks *why* rather than *what*.
>
> Difficulty rises from here. Expect counting formats, pair-matching and questions whose wrong options are all true statements about **other** years.

### 1. 🔥
The Indian Councils Act of 1909 is most significant in Indian constitutional history because it

- (a) introduced dyarchy in the provinces
- (b) introduced universal adult franchise for the first time
- (c) abolished the official majority in the Central Legislative Council
- (d) introduced the system of communal representation through separate electorates

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Act accepted the principle of the **separate electorate** — Muslim members were to be elected **only by Muslim voters**. Religion became, for the first time, a legal category of political representation.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Dyarchy is **1919**.
- (b) Universal adult franchise came only with the **Constitution (Article 326)** in 1950.
- (c) The official majority at the **Centre was retained** in 1909; only the **provincial** councils were allowed a non-official majority.

> 🔑 **Concept:** The Act is said to have **"legalised communalism"**, and **Lord Minto** is called the **"Father of the Communal Electorate."** If a question asks which Act made religion a basis of representation, the answer is always **1909**.

</details>

### 2. 🔥 ⚠️
Consider the following statements about the Indian Councils Act, 1909:

1. It abolished the official majority in the Central Legislative Council.
2. It allowed the provincial legislative councils to have a non-official majority.
3. It introduced the principle of responsible government at the provincial level.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Only statement 2 is correct.

**Why not 1:** The **official majority at the Centre was retained**, not abolished. Only the **provincial** councils were allowed a non-official majority — and that asymmetry is the whole design of the Act.

**Why not 3:** There was **no responsible government** anywhere. Morley said so explicitly: *"If it could be said that this chapter of reforms led directly or indirectly to the establishment of a parliamentary system in India, I for one would have nothing at all to do with it."*

> ⚠️ **Note the trap in statement 2.** A **non-official majority** is not the same as an **elected majority**, and neither amounts to **responsible government**. Three different things:
> **Non-official majority** = most members are not government servants.
> **Elected majority** = most members are chosen by voters.
> **Responsible government** = the executive can be removed by the legislature.
> The 1909 Act gives the first, partially. It gives neither of the other two.

</details>

### 4. 🔥
Who was the first Indian to be appointed to the Viceroy's Executive Council, and in what capacity?

- (a) Satyendra Prasad Sinha, as Law Member
- (b) Raja of Benaras, as Revenue Member
- (c) Dinkar Rao, as Finance Member
- (d) Aga Khan, as Education Member

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Satyendra Prasad Sinha (S.P. Sinha)** joined the Viceroy's **Executive** Council as **Law Member** in 1909. He later became the first Indian to be made a **Governor** (of Bihar and Orissa) and the only Indian raised to the **peerage** as Lord Sinha.

**Why not (b) and (c):** The Raja of Benaras and Sir Dinkar Rao were among the three Indians nominated by **Canning in 1862** — and to the **legislative** council, not the executive one.

> ⚠️ **The distinction that decides this question:**
> **Legislative council** — Indians associated from **1861** (Canning's three nominees, 1862).
> **Executive council** — Indians associated from **1909** (S.P. Sinha).
> Forty-seven years apart. Every year, some candidates lose two marks because they read "council" and stop reading.

</details>

### 5. 🔥
The demand that led directly to the introduction of separate electorates in 1909 was made by

- (a) the Indian National Congress at its Calcutta session
- (b) the Simla Deputation led by the Aga Khan
- (c) the Home Rule League
- (d) the Servants of India Society

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The **Simla Deputation of October 1906**, a group of Muslim leaders led by the **Aga Khan**, met Lord Minto and demanded separate representation. The **All India Muslim League** was founded two months later, in **December 1906 at Dacca**.

> 🔑 **Sequence — learn it as one chain:** **Simla Deputation (Oct 1906)** → **Muslim League founded (Dec 1906)** → **separate electorates enacted (1909)**. Demand, organisation, statute — in three years.
>
> This chain sits on the boundary of Polity and Modern History, which makes it high-yield: it can be asked from either paper's angle.

</details>

### 6. 🔥
Which of the following deliberative powers were granted to members of the legislative councils by the Act of 1909?

1. Asking supplementary questions
2. Moving resolutions on the budget
3. Voting on demands for grants

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Members could now ask **supplementary questions** and **move resolutions on the budget** — one rung above the 1892 power merely to *discuss* it.

### 7. 🔥 ⚠️
**Statement-I:** The Indian Councils Act of 1909 is described as having legalised communalism in India.

**Statement-II:** The Indian Councils Act of 1909 provided that Muslim members of the legislative councils were to be elected exclusively by Muslim voters.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Both are correct, and II is exactly **why** I is said. Confining a community's vote to its own candidates is what turns religion into a legal-electoral category — which is what "legalising communalism" means.

> 🔑 **Concept — why separate electorates were so corrosive.** In a **joint electorate**, a candidate must win votes across communities, so moderation pays. In a **separate electorate**, a candidate needs only his own community's votes — so the incentive flips, and outbidding your rival in communal appeal becomes the winning strategy. The system did not merely *reflect* division; it **rewarded** it.
>
> This is the reasoning the Constituent Assembly accepted when it abolished separate electorates. Understand the mechanism and you can answer any question on this, in Prelims or Mains.

</details>

### 8. 🔥
Besides Muslims, the Act of 1909 also provided separate representation for

- (a) Sikhs and Indian Christians
- (b) presidency corporations, chambers of commerce, universities and zamindars
- (c) the depressed classes and women
- (d) Anglo-Indians and Europeans

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The 1909 Act carved out seats for **presidency corporations, chambers of commerce, universities and zamindars** — representation by **interest**, alongside representation by **religion**.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) and (d) **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans** got separate electorates under the **1919** Act.
- (c) The **depressed classes** got them under the **Communal Award of 1932**; **women** and labour under the **1935** Act.

> 🔑 **The expansion of separate electorates — a near-perfect counting question:**
> **1909** Muslims → **1919** Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans → **1932** Depressed Classes (Communal Award) → **1935** women and labour also given special representation.
> Each Act widens the fracture. Learn which group belongs to which year; UPSC asks it in "how many of the above" form.

</details>

### 9. 🔥 ⚠️
The Communal Award of 1932, which extended separate electorates to the Depressed Classes, was announced by

- (a) Lord Irwin
- (b) Stafford Cripps
- (c) Lord Willingdon
- (d) Ramsay MacDonald

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **Ramsay MacDonald**, the British **Prime Minister**, announced the Communal Award in **August 1932** after the Second Round Table Conference deadlocked on minority representation.

**Why not (a) and (c):** Irwin and Willingdon were **Viceroys** — a deliberate distractor, since the Award came from the **Prime Minister in London**, not from the Viceroy.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Gandhi began a **fast unto death** in Yerwada jail against the extension of separate electorates to the Depressed Classes, arguing it would permanently split Hindu society. The result was the **Poona Pact**.

</details>

### 10. 🔥 📰
The Poona Pact of September 1932, concluded between Gandhi and Dr B.R. Ambedkar, replaced separate electorates for the Depressed Classes with

- (a) nominated seats in the provincial legislatures
- (b) reserved seats within a joint electorate
- (c) a separate electorate with a larger number of seats
- (d) proportional representation by single transferable vote

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The Pact abandoned separate electorates and substituted **reserved seats in a joint electorate** — and, importantly, the **number of reserved seats was substantially increased** (from 71 under the Award to 147 in the provincial legislatures).

> 🔑 **Concept — this is the single most important idea in the set.** Understand the difference:
> | | **Separate electorate** | **Reserved seat (joint electorate)** |
> |---|---|---|
> | Who votes | Only that community | **Everyone** in the constituency |
> | Who may contest | Only that community | Only that community |
> | Incentive | Appeal to your own group alone | Must appeal **across** groups |
>
> **India rejected the first and adopted the second.** That is precisely what **Articles 330 and 332** do — reserve seats for SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, elected by **all** voters of the constituency.

> 📰 **Current-affairs bridge — high yield.** The **106th Amendment (2023)**, the *Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam*, reserves **one-third of seats** for women in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies — again as **reserved seats in a joint electorate**, never a separate electorate. It takes effect only **after a census and the delimitation exercise** that follows. Also note the **104th Amendment (2019)**, which extended SC/ST reservation to **2030** and **discontinued the nomination of Anglo-Indians**.
>
> A 1932 principle, still the operating rule of Indian representation in 2026.

</details>

### 12. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Reform | Year |
|---|---|
| 1. Simla Deputation | 1906 |
| 2. Founding of the All India Muslim League | 1916 |
| 3. Separate electorates enacted | 1911 |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Only pair 1 is correct — the **Simla Deputation** met Lord Minto in **October 1906**.

**Why not 2:** The **All India Muslim League was founded in December 1906** at Dacca, not 1916. **1916** is the year of the **Lucknow Pact** between the Congress and the League, and of the **Home Rule Leagues**.

**Why not 3:** Separate electorates were enacted in **1909**. **1911** is the year of the **annulment of the Partition of Bengal** and the **transfer of the capital from Calcutta to Delhi** at the Delhi Durbar.

> ⚠️ **A "near-miss year" is the hardest distractor there is.** 1909 and 1911 are two years apart and both are famous. The defence is to attach a **distinct event** to every nearby year, so a wrong year immediately feels occupied by something else. 1911 is *already taken* by Bengal and Delhi — so it cannot be separate electorates.

</details>

### 13. 🔥
Which one of the following statements about the elective principle under the Act of 1909 is correct?

- (a) It introduced direct election for the first time to the legislative councils, though on a restricted franchise
- (b) It introduced universal adult franchise for all male subjects
- (c) It retained nomination as the only method of filling non-official seats
- (d) It provided for election by the provincial legislatures alone

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The 1909 Act moved from the disguised, indirect arrangement of 1892 to a **genuine but restricted election**, with the franchise limited by **property, income and educational qualifications** — and, for Muslim seats, by community.

**Why not (b):** Universal adult franchise arrived only in **1950**. Even the **1935** Act extended the vote to roughly **10%** of the population.

> 🔑 **The franchise ladder:**
> **1892** none (recommendation only) → **1909** restricted direct election → **1919** ~**3%** of the population → **1935** ~**10%** → **1950** **100%** of adults, Article 326.
> Note the last jump. The Constituent Assembly went from 10% to universal suffrage in a single step, in a country that was overwhelmingly poor and illiterate — a decision no comparable nation had dared. It is a favourite Mains point and an increasingly common Prelims one.

</details>

### 14.
Lord Minto is remembered as the "Father of the Communal Electorate" because

- (a) he founded the All India Muslim League
- (b) he partitioned Bengal on communal lines
- (c) he accepted the demand for separate electorates and gave it statutory form
- (d) he announced the Communal Award

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Minto received the Simla Deputation, accepted its demand, and gave it effect through the 1909 Act.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) The League was founded by Indian Muslim leaders at Dacca, not by Minto.
- (b) The **Partition of Bengal (1905)** was **Lord Curzon's**, and was annulled in **1911**.
- (d) The Communal Award (1932) was **Ramsay MacDonald's**.

> ⚠️ **All three wrong options are real events tied to real people.** This is the standard construction: surround the right answer with true facts belonging to *other* actors. The defence is to attach each event to its author as a **pair**, never as a floating fact.

</details>

### 15. 🔥
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Separate electorates extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans
2. Separate electorates introduced for Muslims
3. Poona Pact
4. Communal Award

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 2 – 4 – 1 – 3
- (b) 2 – 1 – 3 – 4
- (c) 1 – 2 – 4 – 3
- (d) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Muslims **1909** → Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans **1919** → Communal Award **August 1932** → Poona Pact **September 1932**.

**Why not (b):** It reverses the Award and the Pact. The **Award came first**; the **Pact was the response to it**, one month later. Cause precedes cure.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When two items fall in the **same year**, decide the order by **causation**, not by memory of months. The Poona Pact exists *because* the Communal Award happened. A response cannot precede what it responds to.

</details>

### 16. 🔥 📰
The constitutional provision that most directly repudiates the principle of separate electorates introduced in 1909 is

- (a) Article 14
- (b) Article 15(4)
- (c) Article 330
- (d) Article 325

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **Article 325** provides that there shall be **one general electoral roll** for every constituency, and that no person shall be ineligible for inclusion in it on grounds only of **religion, race, caste or sex**. That is the precise, deliberate opposite of a separate electorate.

**Why not (c):** **Article 330** provides **reserved seats** for SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha — which is the **Poona Pact model**, not the answer to separate electorates. It is a strong distractor because both concern representation of groups.

> 🔑 **Concept — hold these two apart, permanently:**
> **Article 325** kills the **separate electorate** (one common roll for all).
> **Articles 330/332** deliver **reserved seats** within that common roll.
> India rejected separate electorates and kept reservation. One roll, some reserved seats. That sentence answers a whole family of questions.

> 📰 The Constituent Assembly's **Advisory Committee on Minorities**, chaired by **Sardar Patel**, recommended abolishing separate electorates; and in **1949** the Assembly went further and dropped **reservation of seats for religious minorities** altogether — retaining it only for SCs and STs. That decision is why India reserves on grounds of **social backwardness**, never **religion**.

</details>

### 17.
Which one of the following was **not** a feature of the Indian Councils Act, 1909?

- (a) Association of Indians with the executive councils of the Viceroy and Governors
- (b) Enlargement of the size of the legislative councils
- (c) Introduction of a non-official majority in the Central Legislative Council
- (d) Provision for separate representation of chambers of commerce

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c) is the answer:** The **official majority at the Centre was expressly retained**. Only **provincial** councils were permitted a non-official majority.

**Why the others are genuine features:** S.P. Sinha's appointment (a); enlargement from 16 to 60 (b); representation for chambers of commerce, universities, zamindars and presidency corporations (d).

> 🔑 **Concept — the constant of colonial reform.** Whatever is conceded in the **provinces** is withheld at the **Centre**. It happens in 1909 (non-official majority), in 1919 (dyarchy in provinces only) and in 1935 (provincial autonomy operates, central federation never does). If you must guess about the Centre, guess that the British kept control.

</details>

### 19.
The franchise under the Indian Councils Act, 1909 was based on

- (a) universal adult suffrage
- (b) literacy in English alone
- (c) property, income and educational qualifications
- (d) nomination by district collectors

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

### 20. 🔥
Which one of the following best explains why the Indian National Congress was dissatisfied with the Morley-Minto Reforms?

- (a) They gave no representation to Indians in the legislative councils
- (b) They conceded consultation without any share in real power, and divided the electorate on religious lines
- (c) They abolished the provincial legislative councils
- (d) They imposed direct taxation without representation

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Two objections, both fundamental. Indians could now be **elected and could speak**, but the executive remained wholly beyond their control — consultation, not power. And the **separate electorate** split the nation along the very line the Congress claimed did not politically exist.

**Why not (a):** Indians *were* represented — that is precisely what makes the criticism subtle. The complaint was never about presence; it was about **powerlessness**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Notice the shape of the nationalist argument across this whole chapter. It is never "let us in." It is always **"let us decide."** Presence without power is the recurring colonial offer, and the recurring nationalist refusal — from 1909 to the rejection of the Simon Commission to Quit India.

</details>

### 21. ⚠️
Consider the following statements about representation of the Depressed Classes:

1. The Communal Award granted them separate electorates.
2. The Poona Pact increased the number of seats reserved for them compared with the Communal Award.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) Both 1 and 2
- (d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Both are correct, and the second point is the one most candidates miss. The Poona Pact **surrendered the separate electorate but won more seats** — reserved seats in the provincial legislatures rose from **71 under the Award to 147**, with reservation in the central legislature as well.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is why the Poona Pact is genuinely contested history rather than a simple settlement. Ambedkar gave up the **mechanism** he considered essential — a electorate that only Dalits controlled — in exchange for **more seats** filled by a general electorate. He later wrote that he had been compelled by Gandhi's fast.
>
> For Prelims, hold the two facts together: **separate electorate lost, number of seats increased.** A statement asserting only the first half is incomplete — and UPSC has asked exactly that kind of half-truth.

</details>

### 22.
Which one of the following correctly describes the relationship between the Acts of 1892 and 1909 on the elective principle?

- (a) 1892 introduced direct election; 1909 replaced it with nomination
- (b) Both Acts provided for direct election on a common electoral roll
- (c) Both Acts relied entirely on nomination
- (d) 1892 introduced election indirectly and without naming it; 1909 gave it open statutory form

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** 1892 used **recommendation** and avoided the word "election"; 1909 provided for **election openly**, albeit restricted and, for some seats, communal.

**Why not (b):** A **common electoral roll** is exactly what 1909 destroyed by creating separate electorates — and exactly what **Article 325** restored in 1950.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Track the elective principle as a single storyline across the chapter — *denied (1861) → disguised (1892) → granted but divided (1909) → widened but still divided (1919, 1935) → universal and undivided (1950)*. Every Act is one move in that story. Prelims questions are just snapshots of it.

</details>

### 23. 🔥 📰
**Statement-I:** Indian nationalists opposed separate electorates but accepted reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in the Constitution.

**Statement-II:** Reservation of seats operates within a joint electorate in which all voters of the constituency vote, whereas a separate electorate confines voting to members of one community.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Both correct, and II is the whole reason for I. Nationalists objected to separate electorates because they **fragmented the political community**; reserved seats **preserve** a common electorate while guaranteeing representation. The objection was to the fragmentation, not to the representation — so the second device was acceptable where the first was not.

> 📰 **Why this is worth real attention.** This exact distinction underlies live debates you will meet in Current Affairs: the **women's reservation** rollout under the 106th Amendment, **delimitation** after the next census, and periodic demands for reservation on religious lines — which run straight into Article 325.
>
> Prelims 2025 and 2026 both leaned heavily on questions that reward understanding a **mechanism** rather than recalling a **fact**. This is that kind of question, and its answer is a principle you can reuse across three chapters.

</details>

### 24.
Which one of the following statements about the Indian Councils Act, 1909 is correct?

- (a) It provided that the Viceroy's executive council must have a majority of Indian members
- (b) It excluded Indians from provincial executive councils
- (c) It required three of six members of the Viceroy's executive council to be Indian
- (d) It permitted the association of Indians with the executive councils, without prescribing any minimum number

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The 1909 Act **permitted** Indian association with the executive councils of the Viceroy and the Governors. It fixed **no quota**; in practice it meant **one** Indian, S.P. Sinha.

**Why not (c):** The requirement that **three of the six** members of the Viceroy's executive council be Indian belongs to the **Government of India Act, 1919**. ⚠️ That number is a favourite, and it is placed here deliberately.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Permission is not a quota. **1909 permits** (result: one Indian). **1919 prescribes** (result: three of six). The difference between "may" and "shall" is a legal distinction UPSC tests directly — and one you will meet again in Article 74 ("shall act in accordance with such advice", after the 42nd Amendment).

</details>

### 25. 🔥
Which one of the following best captures the long-term constitutional legacy of the Indian Councils Act, 1909?

- (a) It established the framework of responsible government later adopted in the Constitution
- (b) It introduced the parliamentary form of government in the provinces
- (c) It created the federal division of powers between the Centre and the provinces
- (d) It made religious identity a legal basis of political representation, a principle India later expressly rejected

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Act's lasting significance is not administrative but **conceptual** — it made religion a legal category of representation. India's Constitution then **expressly rejected** that principle through **Article 325**, while retaining group representation on the different ground of **social backwardness** (Articles 330, 332).

**Why not the others:** (c) belongs to **1919** (separation of central and provincial subjects) and **1935** (the three lists); (a) and (b) never happened under this Act.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** Most of this chapter is about institutions India **inherited**. 1909 is the great exception — it is about a principle India **refused**.
>
> That contrast is exactly the kind of thing the modern Prelims paper likes, because it cannot be answered by memory alone:
> **Inherited:** parliamentary system, Governor's office, the three lists, emergency provisions, the civil services, ordinance-making.
> **Refused:** separate electorates, official majorities, an irremovable executive, a restricted franchise.
>
> If you can sort any colonial feature into the right column, you have understood Chapter 1 — and you are ready for Chapter 3, *Salient Features*, where the same sorting is the whole syllabus.
