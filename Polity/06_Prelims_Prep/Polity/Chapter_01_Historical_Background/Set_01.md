# Chapter 1 · Practice Set 01 — Company Rule I (1773–1793)

[← Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 02 →](Set_02.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟢 Foundation**
**Covers:** Regulating Act 1773 · Amending Act 1781 · Pitt's India Act 1784 · Act of 1786 · Charter Act 1793

> **The idea behind this set.** These five laws are one continuous argument between London and a company it could not control. Don't memorise them as five items — read each explanation and watch the *leash tighten*. If you can explain **why** each Act was passed, you will never need to memorise **what** it said.

**Tags used below**
🔥 = high probability of appearing in Prelims (repeat-prone area) · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

### 1. 🔥
The Regulating Act of 1773 is historically significant primarily because it was the first instance where

- (a) the British Crown assumed direct responsibility for governing India
- (b) the East India Company's commercial monopoly was formally ended
- (c) Indians were associated with the law-making process in India
- (d) the British Parliament asserted control over the East India Company's affairs

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Until 1773 the Company was a private trading body answerable only to its shareholders. This Act was the first time Parliament in London said, in law, *your affairs are our business*. That assertion of parliamentary supervision is its defining feature.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Crown rule began in **1858**, 85 years later, after the Revolt of 1857.
- (b) The India trade monopoly ended in **1813**; the residual tea/China monopoly ended in **1833**.
- (c) Indians entered law-making with the **Indian Councils Act, 1861** (Canning nominated three Indians).

> 🔑 **Concept:** 1773 = supervision, not takeover. Parliament put a leash on the animal; it did not yet hold it.

</details>

---

### 2. 🔥 ⚠️
Under the Regulating Act of 1773, the Governor-General of Bengal was assisted by an Executive Council consisting of

- (a) three members
- (b) six members
- (c) five members
- (d) four members

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** A council of **four** members was created, and the Governor-General had **no casting vote or veto**. He could therefore be — and Warren Hastings repeatedly was — outvoted 3–1 by his own councillors.

**Why not the others:** Pitt's India Act, 1784 *reduced* the council to **three**, precisely to make it manageable. Option (a) is the post-1784 number — that is the whole trap.

> 🔑 **Concept:** "Four-member council, no veto" is the single biggest **defect** of the 1773 Act, and 1784, 1786 and 1793 are all attempts to fix it. Learn the defect and the sequence explains itself.

</details>

### 3. 🔥 ⚠️
Consider the following statements regarding the Regulating Act of 1773:

1. It designated the Governor of Bengal as the Governor-General of India.
2. It made the Governors of Bombay and Madras subordinate to the Governor of Bengal.
3. It provided for the establishment of a Supreme Court at Calcutta.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 2 and 3 are exact. Bombay and Madras were subordinated to Bengal — the first step towards centralised administration. The Supreme Court at Calcutta was provided for, and set up in **1774**.

**Why not 1:** The 1773 Act created the Governor-General of **Bengal**, not of India. "Governor-General of **India**" came only with the **Charter Act of 1833** (Lord William Bentinck).

> 🔑 **The trap that repeats:** *Bengal in 1773, India in 1833.* Sixty years apart. UPSC swaps these two words more often than any other pair in this chapter.

</details>

### 4.
Who among the following was the first Chief Justice of the Supreme Court established at Calcutta?

- (a) Warren Hastings
- (b) Sir William Jones
- (c) Lord Cornwallis
- (d) Sir Elijah Impey

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** **Sir Elijah Impey** headed the Supreme Court at Calcutta, which had one Chief Justice and three other judges. Impey's conflict with the Governor-General's Council over jurisdiction is exactly what forced the Amending Act of 1781.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Warren Hastings was the first **Governor-General of Bengal** — the executive, not the judiciary.
- (b) Sir William Jones was a judge of the same court and founder of the Asiatic Society, but never its Chief Justice.
- (c) Cornwallis became Governor-General in 1786.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Impey (court) vs Hastings (council) is the personality clash behind the 1781 Act — an early Indian separation-of-powers dispute, settled in the executive's favour.

</details>

### 5.
The Regulating Act of 1773 prohibited the servants of the East India Company from

- (a) owning landed property in Bengal
- (b) serving in the Company's military establishment
- (c) holding any judicial office under the Company
- (d) engaging in private trade and accepting presents or bribes

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Bengal famine of 1770 killed millions while Company servants grew visibly rich through private trade and "presents" from Indian rulers. Parliament's answer was to ban both outright.

**Why not the others:** None of (a), (b) or (c) was prohibited; Company servants routinely held judicial and revenue offices, which is exactly why the executive–judiciary boundary stayed blurred.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Read the **cause**. The Company came to London asking for a loan after the famine; London replied, in effect, *"our money, our conditions."* Every provision of 1773 is a condition attached to a bailout.

</details>

### 6.
The Amending Act of 1781 is also known as the

- (a) Act of Settlement
- (b) Declaratory Act
- (c) Act of Supremacy
- (d) Act of Union

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** It is called the **Act of Settlement** because it *settled* the bitter jurisdictional quarrel between the Supreme Court at Calcutta and the Governor-General-in-Council.

**Why not the others:** These names belong to other British statutes and appear here purely as distractors — the Declaratory Act of 1788, for instance, dealt with the Board of Control's powers.

> 🔑 **Concept:** The nickname tells you the function. "Settlement" = ending a dispute. Whenever an Act's nickname is an option, ask what problem it *settled*.

</details>

### 7. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the Amending Act of 1781:

1. It exempted the Governor-General and Council from the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court for acts done in their official capacity.
2. It provided that the Supreme Court was to administer the personal law of the defendants.
3. It made appeals from the Provincial Courts lie to the Supreme Court.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Officials were placed beyond the Court's reach for official acts, and the Court was directed to apply **Hindu law to Hindus and Muslim law to Muslims** — an early statutory recognition of personal law.

**Why not 3:** Appeals from the Provincial Courts lay to the **Governor-General-in-Council**, *not* to the Supreme Court. The Act's whole purpose was to shrink the Court's reach, not extend it.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Every provision of 1781 pushes one way — **cut the Supreme Court down to size**. If an option expands the Court's power, suspect it immediately.

> 📰 **Why this still matters:** the personal-law recognition begun here is the direct ancestor of today's **Uniform Civil Code** debate (Article 44), and of Uttarakhand's UCC — the first state UCC, in force since **27 January 2025**. Colonial India chose legal pluralism by statute; independent India is still arguing about it.

</details>

### 8. 🔥
Pitt's India Act of 1784 is best remembered for introducing

- (a) provincial autonomy
- (b) dyarchy in the provinces
- (c) the portfolio system
- (d) the system of double government

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Act split the Company's functions in two — the **Court of Directors** kept commercial affairs, a newly created **Board of Control** in London took political affairs. Two authorities, one territory: "double government."

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Provincial autonomy came with the **1935 Act**.
- (b) Dyarchy in provinces came with the **1919 Act**.
- (c) The portfolio system was Canning's, recognised by the **1861 Act**.

> 🔑 **Don't confuse the two "doubles":**
> **Double government (1784)** = trade vs politics, split in *London*.
> **Dyarchy (1919)** = transferred vs reserved subjects, split in *Indian provinces*.

</details>

### 9.
Under Pitt's India Act of 1784, the Board of Control consisted of

- (a) four commissioners nominated by the Court of Directors
- (b) twelve members drawn equally from Parliament and the Company
- (c) eight members appointed by the Crown for five years
- (d) six commissioners including two Cabinet Ministers

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Board comprised **six commissioners**, including the Chancellor of the Exchequer and a Secretary of State — both Cabinet Ministers — plus four Privy Councillors.

**Why not the others:** The essential point is that the Board was a **government** body, not a Company body; option (a) inverts this by making the Directors its appointing authority.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Note *who* sits on it. Cabinet Ministers on the Board means the British **government**, not Parliament in the abstract, was now directing Indian policy. That is why 1784 marks the real beginning of political control.

</details>

### 10. 🔥
Which one of the following Acts, for the first time, referred to the Company's territories in India as the "British possessions in India"?

- (a) Regulating Act, 1773
- (b) Amending Act, 1781
- (c) Pitt's India Act, 1784
- (d) Charter Act, 1793

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Pitt's India Act was the first statute to describe the Company's Indian territories as **"the British possessions in India"** — a legal admission that this was no longer private commerce but imperial territory.

**Why not the others:** The 1773 Act still spoke the language of regulating a trading company; 1781 dealt only with courts; 1793 renewed the charter.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Watch the **vocabulary of empire**. "Company's factories" → "British possessions" → "British India". A change in wording is a change in claim — and in law, wording *is* the claim.

</details>

### 11.
The Act of 1786 was passed principally to

- (a) permit the Governor-General to override the decision of his Council
- (b) abolish the Board of Control created two years earlier
- (c) extend the Company's charter for a further twenty years
- (d) transfer the Company's revenue functions to the Crown

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Lord Cornwallis** agreed to become Governor-General only on two conditions: that he could **override his Council** in special cases, and that he would simultaneously be **Commander-in-Chief**. Parliament passed the 1786 Act to grant exactly that.

**Why not the others:** The Board of Control survived until 1858; the charter renewal came in 1793; revenue functions passed to the Crown only in 1858.

> 🔑 **Concept:** A whole Act of Parliament was passed to meet one man's terms of appointment — and it permanently cured the 1773 defect of an outvotable Governor-General. Personal bargain, structural consequence.

</details>

### 12.
The Charter Act of 1793 extended the power of overriding the Council to

- (a) the Governor-General alone, for a term of twenty years
- (b) the President of the Board of Control in London
- (c) the Commander-in-Chief in matters of military security
- (d) all future Governors-General and Governors

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** What 1786 gave Cornwallis personally, the 1793 Act made **permanent and general** — available to every future Governor-General and to the Governors of the subordinate presidencies.

**Why not (c):** It inverts a real 1793 provision — the Commander-in-Chief was **not** to be a member of the Governor-General's Council unless separately appointed to it.

> 🔑 **The three-step move:** *1773* creates a weak Governor-General → *1786* strengthens one man → *1793* strengthens the office forever. A personal exception hardens into a permanent rule. That is how institutions actually form.

</details>

### 13. 🔥
Consider the following statements about the Charter Act of 1793:

1. It renewed the Company's commercial privileges for a further twenty years.
2. It provided that the members and staff of the Board of Control were to be paid out of Indian revenues.
3. It made the Commander-in-Chief an ex officio member of the Governor-General's Council.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 1 and 2 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The charter was renewed for twenty years, and — notoriously — the salaries of the Board of Control sitting in **London** were charged to **Indian** revenues, a practice that continued until **1919**.

**Why not 3:** The Act provided the **opposite**: the Commander-in-Chief was **not** to sit on the Council unless separately appointed.

> 🔑 **Cross-subject link:** Statement 2 is the seed of the **"drain of wealth"** argument later made by **Dadabhai Naoroji** in *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*. A Polity fact that is simultaneously an Economy and Modern History fact — mark it, it can be asked from any of the three.

</details>

### 14. ⚠️
Which one of the following was **not** a provision of the Regulating Act of 1773?

- (a) The Governor-General was given a casting vote in the Executive Council
- (b) The Court of Directors was required to report on revenue, civil and military affairs
- (c) The Governors of Madras and Bombay were placed under Bengal's supervision
- (d) A Supreme Court was to be established at Calcutta

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** The **absence** of a casting vote was the Act's fatal flaw, not one of its provisions. The casting vote arrived only with **Pitt's India Act, 1784**.

**Why the others are genuine provisions:** (b) obliged the Directors to disclose Indian affairs to the British Government; (c) began centralisation; (d) produced the Calcutta Supreme Court of 1774.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** In a "**not** a provision" question, the trap is usually the *fix* that a **later** Act supplied. Always ask: was this the problem, or the solution?

</details>

### 15.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Establishment of the Board of Control
2. Establishment of the Supreme Court at Calcutta
3. Grant of overriding power to the Governor-General over his Council
4. Exemption of the Governor-General-in-Council from the Supreme Court's jurisdiction

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 2 – 4 – 1 – 3
- (b) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
- (c) 4 – 2 – 3 – 1
- (d) 1 – 2 – 4 – 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Supreme Court **1774** → exemption from its jurisdiction **1781** → Board of Control **1784** → overriding power **1786**.

**Why not the others:**
- **(b)** puts the Board of Control (1784) before the exemption (1781).
- **(c)** puts the exemption (1781) before the Court that was being curbed (1774) — the Court had to exist and cause trouble before Parliament could restrict it.
- **(d)** puts the Board of Control (1784) first of all, before the Supreme Court (1774).

> 🔑 **Concept:** Chronology questions are really **causation** questions. Court created → Court overreaches → Court curbed → Company's politics taken over → Governor-General empowered. Learn the story and the order comes free.

</details>

### 16. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Regulating Act of 1773 failed to establish effective control over the East India Company's administration in India.

**Statement-II:** The Regulating Act of 1773 provided for the establishment of a Supreme Court at Calcutta.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Both statements are true, but II does not explain I.**

The Act did fail to establish effective control — but the reason was that the Governor-General had **no casting vote** and could be outvoted 3–1 by his four-member Council, which is why Parliament had to legislate again in **1784, 1786 and 1793**.

The **Supreme Court at Calcutta** is a genuine provision of the same Act, but it is unrelated to the failure of executive control. If anything, the Court created a **different** problem — a jurisdictional war with the Council that had to be settled by the **Amending Act of 1781**.

> ⚠️ **This is the commonest way (b) is built:** Statement-II is a **true fact from the very same Act**, placed where the real explanation should be. Proximity is not causation. Ask specifically: *does II cause I?* — not merely *are they both about 1773?*

> 🔑 **Exam skill — how to attack Statement I/II:** first mark each statement true or false **independently**. Only then ask whether II is the *reason* for I. Most candidates lose these by testing the link first and letting it colour their reading of the facts.

> 📰 This format entered Prelims at scale in **2023** and has appeared every year since. Expect 2–4 of them in 2027. Drill it deliberately.

</details>

### 17.
The immediate circumstance that prompted the British Parliament to pass the Regulating Act of 1773 was

- (a) the Battle of Buxar and acquisition of the Diwani of Bengal
- (b) the East India Company's application to the British Government for a loan
- (c) the outbreak of the First Anglo-Maratha War
- (d) widespread protests in England against the slave trade

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Despite ruling Bengal, the Company was near bankruptcy after the famine of 1770 and approached the British Government for a **loan**. That request handed Parliament its leverage.

**Why not (a):** The Diwani (1765) created the *conditions* — a trading company suddenly collecting revenue — but the **immediate** trigger eight years later was the financial crisis.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** UPSC distinguishes **immediate cause** from **background cause**. Diwani = background. Loan request = immediate. When a question says "immediate", pick the nearest event in time, not the deepest reason.

</details>

### 18. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Act | Provision |
|---|---|
| 1. Regulating Act, 1773 | First Governor-General of Bengal appointed |
| 2. Pitt's India Act, 1784 | Council of the Governor-General reduced to three members |
| 3. Charter Act, 1793 | Overriding power extended to all future Governors-General |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why all three:** Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal (1773); Pitt's Act cut the Council from four to three (1784); and the 1793 Act made the override power, granted personally to Cornwallis in 1786, available to **all future Governors-General and Governors**.

> ⚠️ **Do not distrust "All three" on instinct.** Many candidates downgrade to "Only two" on the assumption that UPSC must have hidden an error somewhere. Verify each pair on its own and report what you find.

> 🔑 **The trap this question could have used:** the **Board of Control** was **created** in 1784 and abolished only by the **Government of India Act, 1858**. Born **1784**, died **1858** — seventy-four years. Any pairing that kills it earlier is wrong.

</details>

### 19.
The Charter Act of 1793 is regarded as significant in constitutional history because it

- (a) introduced elections to the legislative councils
- (b) laid the foundation of government by written laws and regulations
- (c) separated the legislative and executive functions of government
- (d) permitted Indians to enter the covenanted civil service

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The 1793 Act required regulations to be **written, codified and published**, and made them justiciable in the courts — replacing rule by personal discretion with rule by declared law.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Indirect elections arrived in **1892**.
- (c) Legislative–executive separation came in **1853**.
- (d) The service was opened by the **1833** Act and made competitive in **1853**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** This is the ancestor of the **rule of law** in India. The idea that government must act through published rules a court can test is the seed of **Article 14**. Trace forward: 1793 → Dicey's rule of law → Article 14 → *E.P. Royappa* (arbitrariness). One line, two centuries.

</details>

### 20. 🔥
Which one of the following statements about the "double government" established in 1784 is correct?

- (a) One government functioned in London and another in Calcutta
- (b) One authority controlled commerce and another controlled political affairs
- (c) One set of subjects was administered by Indian ministers and another by the Governor
- (d) One legislature sat at the Centre and another in each province

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Double government split the Company's work by **function**, not by geography: Directors kept trade, the Board took politics. Both sat in **London**.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Both bodies were in London — the commonest misconception about the term.
- (c) That is **dyarchy** (1919).
- (d) That is the federal legislative scheme of **1935**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** All three wrong options describe **real** arrangements from other Acts. UPSC builds distractors from adjacent true facts, not from fiction. That is why learning facts in isolation fails and learning them in sequence works.

</details>

### 21.
Under the Regulating Act of 1773, the term of office of the Directors of the East India Company was fixed at

- (a) two years
- (b) four years
- (c) six years
- (d) ten years

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Directors were elected for **four years**, with **one-fourth retiring every year** — a staggered rotation giving continuity plus regular renewal.

> 🔑 **Concept — recognise the design:** a **staggered term with partial retirement each year** is the same device used today for the **Rajya Sabha** (one-third retiring every two years) and the **State Legislative Councils**. The mechanism outlived the empire. When you meet Chapter 22, you will already know where it came from.

</details>

### 22.
Consider the following statements:

1. The Supreme Court at Calcutta was established under the Regulating Act of 1773 and began functioning in 1774.
2. Its jurisdiction was confined to the Presidency towns and did not extend over the Indian population of the interior.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) Both 1 and 2
- (d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Both are correct. The Court was provided for in 1773 and constituted in **1774**. The Amending Act of 1781 then confirmed that its reach did not extend over the general population of the interior.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Note the **two-tier judiciary** this created — a Crown court for Europeans and Presidency towns, the Company's own courts for everyone else. That colonial duality is exactly what a **single integrated judiciary** and **Article 14** later abolished. Every colonial exclusion has a constitutional answer; train yourself to find the pair.

</details>

### 23.
Which one of the following correctly describes the position of the Governor-General of Bengal in relation to the Presidencies of Bombay and Madras after 1773?

- (a) He exercised exclusive legislative authority over both
- (b) They remained wholly independent of him
- (c) They were merged into the Presidency of Bengal
- (d) He exercised supervisory control, but they retained separate governments

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Bombay and Madras were made **subordinate** to Bengal, but not abolished or absorbed — they kept their own Governors and administrations under Bengal's supervision.

**Why not (a):** It overstates the position — legislative authority over all British India came only in **1833**.

> 🔑 **Centralisation in three steps:** *1773* subordination → *1833* Governor-General of India with legislative power over all British India → *1858* a single Secretary of State in London. Power flows steadily upward for 85 years — and the Constitution of 1950 kept that centralising instinct, which is why Indian federalism is called "quasi-federal". This question is really about **Chapter 13**.

</details>

### 24.
The system introduced by Pitt's India Act, 1784 was criticised chiefly on the ground that it

- (a) gave excessive power to Indian nobles in the administration
- (b) divided responsibility, allowing each authority to blame the other
- (c) abolished the office of the Governor-General entirely
- (d) transferred the Company's assets to the British Crown

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Double government meant **divided responsibility**. When something went wrong in India, the Board of Control and the Court of Directors could each point at the other. Accountability dissolved in the gap between them.

**Why not (d):** Assets passed to the Crown only in **1858**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** The Constitution absorbed this lesson. **Collective responsibility** under **Article 75(3)** exists precisely so that no one can say "not my department." The colonial failure taught the constitutional rule.

</details>

### 25. 🔥 📰
Which one of the following best explains why the period 1773–1793 is described as the beginning of India's constitutional development, even though no Indian participated in it?

- (a) It saw the first written codes of Hindu and Muslim personal law
- (b) It secured judicial independence from executive interference
- (c) It created the first representative legislature in India
- (d) It established written rules defining and limiting the powers of authorities

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** A constitution, at its core, is a set of **written rules that define and limit power**. These Acts did exactly that — created offices, fixed their composition, prescribed what each could and could not do, and made those rules enforceable. That the rules were written in London by Britons does not change their constitutional character.

**Why not the others:**
- (b) The 1781 Act *reduced* judicial independence by exempting officials from the Court's reach.
- (c) No representative legislature existed; even nominated Indians appear only in **1861**.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** Prelims **2023** asked what best reflects the chief purpose of a Constitution — the answer was *"it defines and limits the powers of government."* Same idea, tested here. Constitutional development is not about democracy arriving; it is about **power becoming rule-bound**. Democracy comes later, and separately.

> 📰 **Current-affairs hook:** this framing has been everywhere since **Constitution Day, 26 November 2024** marked **75 years of the adoption** of the Constitution, and the special Parliament debate that followed. Expect "purpose/nature of a constitution" conceptual questions to keep appearing — they need no factual recall, only clarity, which makes them free marks if you have it.

</details>
