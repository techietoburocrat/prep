# Chapter 1 · Practice Set 03 — Crown Rule Begins (1858 · 1861 · 1892)

[← Set 02](Set_02.md) · [Chapter Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Set 04 →](Set_04.md)

**25 questions · 30 minutes · +2 / −0.66 · Level 🟢 Foundation**
**Covers:** Government of India Act 1858 · Queen's Proclamation · Indian Councils Act 1861 · Indian Councils Act 1892

> **The idea behind this set.** 1857 changes everything. Before it, London leashes a company; after it, London governs directly — and then, slowly and grudgingly, starts letting Indians into the room. 1858 = takeover. 1861 = Indians nominated. 1892 = Indians may now ask questions. Three tiny concessions, twenty years apart.

🔥 = high probability · 📰 = current-affairs linked · ⚠️ = classic trap

---

### 1. 🔥
The Government of India Act, 1858 is popularly known as the

- (a) Act for the Good Government of India
- (b) Act of Settlement
- (c) Morley-Minto Act
- (d) Act of Accession

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Its popular title was the **"Act for the Good Government of India"** — an unmistakable verdict on the Company's *bad* government, delivered in the aftermath of the Revolt of 1857.

**Why not the others:** (b) is the Amending Act of **1781**; (c) is the Indian Councils Act of **1909**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** The title is the argument. Parliament was not merely reorganising; it was declaring that a private company could no longer be trusted with an empire. **1857 → 1858**: revolt in one year, Company abolished the next.

</details>

### 2. 🔥
Which of the following were abolished by the Government of India Act, 1858?

1. The East India Company
2. The Board of Control
3. The Court of Directors

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 1 and 3 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why all three:** The Act abolished the **Company** itself, and with it both halves of the **double government** — the **Board of Control** (created 1784) and the **Court of Directors**. Powers, territories and revenues passed to the **British Crown**.

> 🔑 **Concept — the clean sweep.** Every institution of the Company era dies in one Act. If a question asks whether some Company-era body survived past 1858, the default answer is **no**. Only the machinery of administration survived; the corporate structure did not.

</details>

### 3. 🔥
The office created by the Government of India Act, 1858 to exercise complete authority and control over Indian administration was

- (a) the Viceroy of India
- (b) the Secretary of State for India
- (c) the President of the Board of Control
- (d) the Governor-General in Council

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** The **Secretary of State for India** was a **member of the British Cabinet**, answerable ultimately to the **British Parliament**, and held complete authority over Indian administration.

**Why not (a):** The Viceroy was not a *new* office — the existing Governor-General of India was simply **also given the title of Viceroy**, as the Crown's direct representative.

> 🔑 **Concept — where power actually sat.** The Viceroy ruled *in* India; the Secretary of State ruled *over* India, from London. Real authority was 6,000 miles away. That is what "direct Crown rule" meant in practice, and it is why Indian nationalists later demanded that the seat of decision move to India.

</details>

### 4.
The Council of India, established under the Government of India Act, 1858 to assist the Secretary of State, consisted of

- (a) 10 members
- (b) 12 members
- (c) 15 members
- (d) 20 members

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** A **15-member** Council of India, which was an **advisory body**. The Secretary of State was its **chairman**.

> 🔑 **Concept — and the trap ahead.** The **Council of India was abolished by the Government of India Act, 1935**. Its lifespan is therefore **1858–1935**, while the **Secretary of State** himself survived until **1947**. UPSC loves the fact that the assistant died before the master. Keep the two dates separate.

</details>

### 5.
The Government of India Act, 1858 constituted the Secretary of State-in-Council as a body corporate, meaning that it

- (a) could legislate for British India without parliamentary approval
- (b) could sue and be sued in India and in England
- (c) had exclusive control over the Indian army
- (d) was immune from all legal proceedings

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** As a **body corporate**, the Secretary of State-in-Council could **sue and be sued**, both in India and in England.

**Why not (d):** This is the exact inversion, and the tempting wrong answer — corporate personality creates **liability**, it does not remove it.

> 🔑 **Concept — this has a real constitutional afterlife.** The liability of the Secretary of State-in-Council is the ancestor of **Article 300**, under which the Union and the States may sue and be sued. The whole modern law of **State liability in tort** (*P&O Steam Navigation*, *Kasturilal*, *Nilabati Behera*) grows out of this 1858 provision. Remember it when you reach the Union and its Territory chapter.

</details>

### 6. 🔥
Queen Victoria's Proclamation of 1858, read out by Lord Canning at Allahabad, is often described as the "Magna Carta of the Indian people" because it promised

1. an end to the policy of territorial annexation
2. non-interference in the religious beliefs of Indians
3. admission of Indians to public office without discrimination of race or creed

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why all three:** The Proclamation renounced further **annexation** (ending the Doctrine of Lapse), promised **religious non-interference**, and pledged that Indians would be freely admitted to offices "without distinction of race or creed."

> 🔑 **Concept — a promise repeatedly broken.** Indian nationalists spent the next seventy years quoting the 1858 Proclamation back at the British to expose the gap between promise and practice, particularly on entry to the civil services. A pledge you can cite against its author is a political weapon; that is exactly how the Congress used it.

</details>

### 7. 🔥
The Indian Councils Act of 1861 is regarded as a landmark because it

- (a) introduced elections to the legislative councils
- (b) established the office of Secretary of State for India
- (c) associated Indians with the law-making process for the first time
- (d) granted provincial autonomy to Bombay and Madras

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** The Act required the Viceroy to nominate some Indians as **non-official members** of his expanded council. In **1862**, **Lord Canning** nominated three — the **Raja of Benaras**, the **Maharaja of Patiala** and **Sir Dinkar Rao**.

**Why not the others:**
- (a) Even indirect election appears only in **1892**.
- (b) That is 1858.
- (d) Provincial autonomy is **1935**; 1861 merely **restored legislative powers** to Bombay and Madras.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Note who was chosen — a Raja, a Maharaja and a senior administrator. The British associated **loyal aristocrats**, not popular representatives. "Representation" here means presence, not accountability. The distinction runs through this entire chapter.

</details>

### 8. 🔥 ⚠️
The Indian Councils Act of 1861 initiated the process of decentralisation by

- (a) creating separate provincial budgets
- (b) establishing elected municipal bodies in the Presidency towns
- (c) transferring police and land revenue to provincial control
- (d) restoring legislative powers to the Bombay and Madras Presidencies

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Bombay and Madras had lost their legislative powers under the **Charter Act of 1833**. The 1861 Act **gave them back** — the first reversal of seventy years of relentless centralisation.

**Why not the others:** Financial decentralisation began with **Lord Mayo's resolution of 1870**; local self-government was expanded by **Lord Ripon's resolution of 1882**, which earned him the title **"Father of Local Self-Government in India."**

> 🔑 **The pendulum — worth writing on your wall:**
> **1833** provinces stripped → **1861** legislative powers restored → **1870** Mayo, financial decentralisation → **1882** Ripon, local self-government → **1919** central/provincial subjects separated → **1935** provincial autonomy → **1950** State List → **1992** the 73rd/74th Amendments reach the village.
> That single line is 160 years of Indian federalism.

</details>

### 9. 🔥 📰
The Indian Councils Act of 1861 empowered the Viceroy to issue ordinances, without the concurrence of the legislative council, during an emergency. The life of such an ordinance was

- (a) six weeks
- (b) three months
- (c) six months
- (d) one year

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** **Six months.**

> 📰 **Very high yield — connect it forward.** This is the direct ancestor of the President's ordinance-making power under **Article 123** and the Governor's under **Article 213**. But note what changed at independence:
> - **Colonial ordinance (1861):** valid **6 months**, issued on the Viceroy's own authority.
> - **Article 123 ordinance:** must be laid before Parliament and **ceases six weeks** after reassembly (or earlier if disapproved), and is issued on the **advice of the Council of Ministers**, not personally.
>
> **Ordinance-making was a featured topic in Prelims 2025.** Expect it again — and expect the **6 months / 6 weeks** confusion to be the trap. Colonial ordinance = **6 months**; constitutional ordinance = **6 weeks after reassembly**.
>
> Also recall *D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar* (1987) on re-promulgation, and *Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar* (2017), which held re-promulgation a **fraud on the Constitution**.

</details>

### 10. 🔥
The "portfolio system", under which each member of the Viceroy's council was placed in charge of a department, was introduced by Lord Canning in 1859 and given statutory recognition by the

- (a) Government of India Act, 1858
- (b) Indian Councils Act, 1909
- (c) Indian Councils Act, 1892
- (d) Indian Councils Act, 1861

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The 1861 Act empowered the Viceroy to make rules and orders for the more convenient transaction of business in council, which **recognised the portfolio system** Canning had begun in **1859**.

> 🔑 **Concept — you use this word every day.** "Finance portfolio", "Home portfolio", "portfolio reshuffle" — all of it starts here. Today the same function is performed by the **Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961**, made under **Article 77(3)**. Institution in 1859, statute in 1861, Article in 1950. The machine never stopped running; only its owner changed.

</details>

### 11.
Under the Indian Councils Act of 1861, new legislative councils were provided for. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?

- (a) Bengal — 1862
- (b) Punjab — 1866
- (c) North-Western Frontier Province — 1897
- (d) Burma — 1862

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** **Bengal — 1862**, **NWFP — 1866**, **Punjab — 1897**. Only the Bengal pairing is correct as given; options (b) and (c) have the Punjab and NWFP years **swapped**.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** When three items share a common structure and two options swap two of them, the untouched item is usually the answer. Verify it, then move — do not spend forty seconds reconstructing all three.

### 12. 🔥
The Indian Councils Act of 1892 enlarged the functions of the legislative councils by giving them the power to

- (a) discuss the budget and address questions to the executive
- (b) vote on the budget and move resolutions
- (c) move a motion of no confidence against the executive
- (d) legislate on subjects reserved to the Governor-General

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Councils could now **discuss the budget** and **ask questions of the executive** — but nothing more.

**Why not (b):** The **right to move resolutions on the budget** came with the **1909** Act. The power to **vote** on demands for grants arrived only in **1919**.

> 🔑 **Concept — the ladder of financial control, a guaranteed question somewhere:**
> **1892** discuss the budget → **1909** move resolutions on it → **1919** vote on it → **1935** wider provincial financial powers → **1950** Article 113, Parliament votes demands for grants.
> Each rung is one Act. *Discuss → resolve → vote.* Three verbs, three Acts.

</details>

### 13. 🔥 ⚠️
Which one of the following statements about the Indian Councils Act of 1892 is correct?

- (a) It introduced direct elections and used the word "election" in the statute
- (b) It introduced separate electorates for Muslims
- (c) It abolished the official majority in the legislative councils
- (d) It made a limited and indirect provision for election, without using the word "election"

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** The Act allowed some non-official members to be **nominated on the recommendation** of provincial councils, district boards, municipalities, universities, trade associations and chambers of commerce. In substance this was election; in form the word **"election" was deliberately avoided** and the process was called nomination.

**Why not (c):** The **official majority was retained**, and it was retained again in 1909. It was breached at the provincial level only in **1919**.

> 🔑 **Concept — read the evasion.** The British granted the substance while withholding the name, because naming it would have conceded the principle. Watch for this everywhere in colonial constitutional history: **the form is always more conservative than the fact**.

</details>

### 14. 🔥
**Statement-I:** The Government of India Act, 1858 made no substantial change in the system of government that prevailed in India.

**Statement-II:** The Government of India Act, 1858 conferred on the Governor-General the additional title of Viceroy.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for Statement-I
- (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
- (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** **Both statements are true, but II does not explain I.**

Statement-I is correct: the 1858 Act changed **who supervised India from London** — Crown instead of Company — while leaving the structure of government **in India** essentially untouched.

The **real** explanation is that the Act was concerned almost entirely with the **London end** of the machinery: it abolished the Company, the Board of Control and the Court of Directors, and created the Secretary of State and the Council of India.

Statement-II is true — the Governor-General was additionally styled **Viceroy** — but a **change of title** is exactly the kind of thing that makes *no* substantial difference. It illustrates Statement-I rather than explaining it.

> 🔑 **Concept — the single most under-appreciated point in this chapter.** 1858 feels like a revolution and was constitutionally almost none. For an ordinary Indian, nothing changed: same officials, same courts, same taxes, different letterhead. Real change in India begins in **1861** and only becomes meaningful in **1919**.

</details>

### 15.
Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

1. Nomination of the first Indians to the Viceroy's legislative council
2. Queen Victoria's Proclamation
3. Power to discuss the budget granted to the legislative councils
4. Introduction of the portfolio system by Lord Canning

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
- (b) 4 – 2 – 1 – 3
- (c) 2 – 4 – 1 – 3
- (d) 4 – 1 – 2 – 3

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Queen's Proclamation **1 November 1858** → portfolio system introduced by Canning **1859** → first three Indians nominated to the legislative council **1862** → power to discuss the budget **1892**. That is **2 – 4 – 1 – 3**.

**Why not the others:** Option (b) and (d) put the portfolio system (1859) before the Proclamation (1858) — only one year apart, which is exactly why it is the trap. Option (a) places the nominations (1862) before the portfolio system (1859).

> ⚠️ **The one-year trap.** When two events sit in **consecutive years**, UPSC will build the wrong options around swapping them. Here it is **1858 vs 1859**. Both are "Canning events", both feel simultaneous — and they are not.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** Write the four years in the margin **first** (1858, 1859, 1862, 1892), *then* read the options. Never reason from the options towards the facts — that is how you get talked out of a correct sequence.

</details>

### 16.
Lord Canning holds a unique position in Indian constitutional history because he was

- (a) the last Governor-General of Bengal and the first Governor-General of India
- (b) the first Governor-General to preside over a legislative council
- (c) the last Governor-General of India under the Company and the first Viceroy
- (d) the first Viceroy to nominate an Indian to his executive council

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** Canning was the last Governor-General appointed under Company rule and became the **first Viceroy** in 1858 when the Crown took over.

**Why not (d):** The first Indian in the Viceroy's **Executive** Council was **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** in **1909**. Canning's three nominees in 1862 were on the **legislative** council. ⚠️ Executive council and legislative council are different bodies — a distinction UPSC tests directly.

> 🔑 **Canning's four claims to fame:** first **Viceroy** (1858) · **portfolio system** (1859) · nominated the **first three Indians** to the legislative council (1862) · read the **Queen's Proclamation** at Allahabad (1858). One man, four possible questions.

</details>

### 17. 🔥
Consider the following pairs:

| Body | Created by |
|---|---|
| 1. Secretary of State for India | Indian Councils Act, 1861 |
| 2. Council of India | Indian Councils Act, 1861 |
| 3. Indian (Central) Legislative Council | Charter Act, 1853 |

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** Only pair 3 is correct — the **Indian (Central) Legislative Council** was created by the **Charter Act of 1853**.

**Why not 1 and 2:** **Both** the Secretary of State for India **and** the 15-member Council of India that advised him were created by the **Government of India Act, 1858** — not by the 1861 Act.

> ⚠️ **Do not confuse three similarly named bodies:**
> **Council of India** — London, 1858–1935, advises the Secretary of State.
> **Indian Councils Acts** — 1861, 1892, 1909, legislation in India.
> **Indian (Central) Legislative Council** — created 1853, the legislative body in India.
> Similar names, different places, different decades. Read the wording of the option, not its shape.

</details>

### 18.
Under the Indian Councils Act of 1892, non-official members of the Central Legislative Council were nominated by the Viceroy on the recommendation of

- (a) the provincial legislative councils and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce
- (b) the district boards and municipalities alone
- (c) the Indian National Congress and the Muslim League
- (d) the universities and zamindars of Bengal

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** For the **Central** council — provincial legislative councils and the **Bengal Chamber of Commerce**. For the **provincial** councils, Governors acted on the recommendation of district boards, municipalities, universities, trade associations, zamindars and chambers.

**Why not (c):** The **Muslim League was founded only in 1906**, fourteen years later — a date check that kills the option instantly.

> 🔑 **Exam skill:** **Date-check every proper noun in the options.** An organisation that did not yet exist cannot recommend anybody. This one trick disposes of a surprising number of questions across History and Polity alike.

</details>

### 19.
Which one of the following was **not** an effect of the Government of India Act, 1858?

- (a) The Governor-General was given the additional title of Viceroy
- (b) The system of double government came to an end
- (c) Indians were associated with the legislative process
- (d) The Company's territories and revenues passed to the Crown

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c) is the odd one out:** Indians entered law-making under the **Indian Councils Act, 1861**, not the 1858 Act.

**Why the others did happen:** The Viceroy title, the end of double government and the transfer of territories and revenues are all 1858.

> 🔑 **Concept:** 1858 was about **who rules from London**. 1861 was the first time it mattered **who sits in the room in India**. Keep those two questions separate and this chapter's Crown-rule half becomes simple.

</details>

### 20. 🔥
The retention of an "official majority" in the legislative councils under the Acts of 1861, 1892 and 1909 meant that

- (a) the councils could never discuss government policy
- (b) only officials of the Indian Civil Service could be nominated
- (c) government nominees always outnumbered non-official members, so the government could never be defeated
- (d) legislation required the assent of a majority of Indian members

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** An official majority guaranteed that the government's own members outnumbered the rest, so **no vote could ever go against it**. Discussion was permitted precisely because it was harmless.

> 🔑 **Concept — this is the heart of colonial constitutionalism.** Concede **voice**, retain **votes**. Indians could speak, question, criticise — and never win. The official majority survived until **1919**, when it was abandoned in the **provinces** but effectively retained at the **Centre**.
>
> Compare it with today: in Parliament the government survives only while it commands a majority that the **electorate** creates. That single difference — where the majority comes from — is the difference between colonial rule and responsible government.

</details>

### 21.
Which one of the following statements about the Secretary of State for India is correct?

- (a) He was appointed by the Viceroy and was answerable to him
- (b) He was elected by the Council of India from among its members
- (c) He was a member of the British Cabinet and was ultimately responsible to the British Parliament
- (d) He was nominated by the Court of Directors of the East India Company

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Why (c):** A **British Cabinet Minister**, ultimately answerable to the **British Parliament** — and paid, until **1919**, out of **Indian revenues**.

**Why not (d):** The Court of Directors was **abolished** by the very Act that created this office.

> 🔑 **Concept — the accountability gap that defined the freedom struggle.** India was governed by a minister answerable to an electorate that was not Indian. No Indian voted for him; no Indian could remove him. That is the precise constitutional grievance that **responsible government** was demanded to cure — and which **Article 75(3)** finally cures, by making ministers answerable to a House elected by Indians.

</details>

### 22. ⚠️
Consider the following statements:

1. The Government of India Act, 1858 transferred the Company's armed forces to the Crown.
2. The Indian Councils Act, 1861 gave the legislative councils the power to vote on the budget.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) Both 1 and 2
- (d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Why (a):** All the Company's powers, territories, revenues **and forces** passed to the Crown in 1858.

**Why not 2:** In **1861** the councils had **no financial powers at all**. They could not even *discuss* the budget — that came in **1892**. Voting on the budget arrived only in **1919**.

> 🔑 **Concept:** Note how far ahead statement 2 jumps. Sliding a power **backwards in time** by one or two Acts is UPSC's favourite way to make a false statement look true. Whenever you see a power attributed to an Act, ask: *was the previous, smaller version of this power already granted?* If not, the statement is too early.

</details>

### 23. 🔥 📰
The transformation brought about between 1858 and 1892 is best described as

- (a) the replacement of company rule by responsible government
- (b) the replacement of company rule by direct Crown rule, with the beginnings of Indian association in law-making
- (c) the establishment of a federal structure with autonomous provinces
- (d) the grant of dominion status to British India

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Why (b):** Two changes, exactly: **Crown replaces Company** (1858), and Indians are **associated** with law-making by nomination (1861) and quasi-election (1892).

**Why not the others:**
- (a) **Responsible government** — an executive answerable to a legislature — never existed at the Centre in colonial India. It reached the **provinces** partially in 1919 and more fully in 1935.
- (d) Dominion status was demanded from the **Nehru Report (1928)**, offered in the **August Offer (1940)** and **Cripps Mission (1942)**, and actually granted only in **1947**.

> 📰 **Why this matters for 2027.** Prelims 2025 and 2026 both moved sharply towards **analytical** questions of exactly this shape — no date to recall, only a demand that you know what a term *means*. "Responsible government", "provincial autonomy", "dominion status", "federation" are technical terms with precise content. Learn them as **definitions**, not as labels, and this entire question type becomes free marks.

</details>

### 24.
Which one of the following correctly describes the position of the non-official members nominated under the Indian Councils Act, 1892?

- (a) They were elected by a limited franchise of property owners
- (b) They held office at the pleasure of the provincial legislative councils
- (c) They were appointed by the Secretary of State from London
- (d) They were nominated, though on the recommendation of certain bodies

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Formally **nominated by the Viceroy or Governor**, but on the **recommendation** of specified bodies — an indirect election in everything but name.

**Why not (a):** A **franchise** based on property qualification appears only with the **1909** Act, and is substantially widened in **1919** and **1935**.

> 🔑 **Concept — the three-stage entry of the elective principle:**
> **1892** recommendation (election in substance, denied in name) → **1909** limited direct election with separate electorates → **1919** direct election on a property franchise → **1935** franchise extended to roughly **10%** of the population → **1950** **universal adult suffrage** at one stroke, Article 326.
> That last jump — from 10% to everybody, overnight, in a poor and largely illiterate country — is the boldest thing the Constituent Assembly did. Remember it when you reach Chapter 3.

</details>

### 25. 🔥
Which one of the following best explains why the Acts of 1861 and 1892, despite associating Indians with the councils, did not amount to representative government?

- (a) The councils had no power to make laws
- (b) Indians were barred from speaking in the councils
- (c) The councils met only once a year
- (d) The members were not chosen by the people and the executive was not answerable to the councils

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: (d)**

**Why (d):** Two defects, and both are fatal. The members were **nominated**, not chosen by the people — so they represented nobody. And the executive was **not answerable** to the councils — so even a hostile council could change nothing.

**Why not the others:** The councils did make laws, met regularly, and Indians could and did speak in them. Speech was never the constraint.

> 🔑 **The big idea of this set.** Test any body against two questions:
> **1. Who chooses the members?** *(representation)*
> **2. Can the members remove the executive?** *(responsibility)*
>
> Colonial councils fail both until 1919, and fail the second one at the Centre right up to 1947. Our own Lok Sabha passes both — elected under Article 326, and able to remove the government under Article 75(3).
>
> **Carry this two-question test into every remaining set of this chapter.** It converts a mass of dates into a single, usable idea — and that idea is what Prelims is now actually testing.

</details>
