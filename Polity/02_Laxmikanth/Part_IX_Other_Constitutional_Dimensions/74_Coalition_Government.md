# Chapter 74 — Coalition Government

[← Chapter 73](73_Voting_Behaviour.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 75 →](75_Anti_Defection_Law.md)

**Read time ~7 min** · **No article — constitutional practice** · 🟡 Prelims: through parliamentary-system questions · Mains: **2002**

> 📌 ⭐ **A short chapter that carries two things you will use constantly:** ⭐ **the SARKARIA ORDER OF PREFERENCE for a Governor facing a hung House**, and ⭐ **the *S.R. Bommai* floor-test rule.** ⚠️ **Those two together answer every "hung assembly / Governor's discretion" question — and those come up far more often than "coalition government" as a topic.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **The Constitution assumes a single party will command a majority. It says nothing about what happens when none does.** ⭐ **Every rule that governs coalition formation in India — the order of preference, the floor test, the anti-defection merger clause — was built by COMMISSIONS AND COURTS filling that silence.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ The phases

| Period | Character |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1952–1967** | Single-party majority at the Centre and in most states |
| ⭐ **1967 onward, in the STATES** | ⭐ **The 1967 elections produced the first coalitions — the *Samyukta Vidhayak Dals*** — and with them the era of ⭐ **"Aya Ram Gaya Ram"** defections and frequent **President's Rule** |
| ⭐ **1977–79** | The ⭐ **Janata Party** government — a **merger** rather than a coalition in form, and it collapsed on the same fault lines |
| ⭐⭐ **1989–2014** | ⭐ **The coalition era. NO single party won a Lok Sabha majority for twenty-five years.** National Front, United Front, NDA, UPA |
| ⭐ **2014 onward** | A return to **single-party majorities at the Centre**, with coalitions continuing to matter in many states |

⭐ **Two definitions to keep separate:** ⭐ **a PRE-POLL alliance** *(parties contest together on a shared platform and seat-sharing arrangement — this is what a voter can be said to have chosen)*; and ⭐ **a POST-POLL coalition** *(assembled after the result — legitimate, but with a weaker democratic mandate)*. ⭐ **The distinction drives the Sarkaria order of preference below.**

---

## PART B — ⭐⭐ The two rules that decide questions

### ⭐⭐ 1. The Sarkaria order of preference — whom the Governor should invite

⭐ **Where no party has a majority, the ⭐ SARKARIA COMMISSION (1988) laid down an order of preference for the Governor, endorsed by the ⭐ PUNCHHI COMMISSION and repeatedly relied on by the courts:**

| Order | Whom to invite |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1** | ⭐ **An alliance of parties formed BEFORE the elections *(a pre-poll alliance)*** |
| ⭐ **2** | ⭐ **The SINGLE LARGEST PARTY, staking a claim with the support of others, including independents** |
| ⭐ **3** | ⭐ **A POST-ELECTORAL coalition, with ALL the partners joining the government** |
| ⭐ **4** | ⭐ **A post-electoral alliance in which SOME parties join the government and others support it FROM OUTSIDE** |

> ⭐⭐ **The Governor's task is to identify who is most likely to command a majority — NOT to choose who should govern.** ⚠️ ⭐ **And where he cannot be certain, the answer is not discretion but a FLOOR TEST**, which is rule 2.

### ⭐⭐ 2. *S.R. Bommai v Union of India* (1994) — the floor test

> ⭐⭐ **The strength of a ministry is to be tested ON THE FLOOR OF THE HOUSE, and NOT in the Governor's subjective assessment.**

⭐ **Its corollaries, all examinable:**
- ⭐ **Article 356 is subject to JUDICIAL REVIEW**; the President's satisfaction must rest on **relevant material**;
- ⭐ **A dissolved Assembly can be REVIVED if the proclamation is struck down** — so dissolution should await parliamentary approval;
- ⭐ **The Assembly cannot be dissolved before both Houses of Parliament approve the proclamation**;
- ⭐ **SECULARISM is part of the basic structure**, and a state government acting against it may be dismissed.

⭐ **The later application worth naming:** ⭐ ***Rameshwar Prasad v Union of India* (2006)** — the Supreme Court held the **dissolution of the Bihar Assembly** on the Governor's report of anticipated horse-trading to be **unconstitutional**, since ⭐ **the apprehension of defections cannot substitute for a floor test.** ⭐ **In subsequent hung-assembly disputes the Court has ordered floor tests within days, by open ballot and with a specified time.**

## PART C — ⭐ The balance sheet

### ⭐ How coalitions work in practice

> ⭐ **A COMMON MINIMUM PROGRAMME** — the negotiated policy floor of the alliance;
> ⭐ **A COORDINATION COMMITTEE** — the standing mechanism for resolving disputes between partners;
> ⭐ **Portfolio allocation** proportionate to strength;
> ⭐ **Consensus decision-making**, with contentious items deferred rather than forced.

### ⭐ Merits

1. ⭐⭐ **They made Indian federalism operate.** ⭐ **A Union government dependent on regional parties cannot dismiss state governments casually, cannot ignore state demands, and must negotiate — which is why the coalition era coincided with the sharpest decline in the misuse of Article 356** *(alongside ***Bommai***)*;
2. ⭐ **Wider representation** — more social groups and regions inside the government;
3. ⭐ **A check on concentrated power** — a partner can withdraw, which no backbencher can;
4. ⭐ **Consensus policymaking** — major economic and social legislation of the period passed with broad support;
5. ⭐ **Accommodation of regional aspirations** without recourse to agitation.

### ⚠️ Demerits

1. ⚠️ ⭐ **Instability** — governments falling mid-term, and the recurring cost of mid-term polls;
2. ⚠️ ⭐ **Blackmail by small partners** disproportionate to their strength;
3. ⚠️ ⭐ **Portfolio bargaining over policy** — ministries sought for their resources rather than their agenda;
4. ⚠️ ⭐ **Diluted COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY under Articles 75(3) and 164(2)** — ministers from an allied party may publicly dissent from Cabinet decisions and remain in office, which the doctrine does not contemplate;
5. ⚠️ **Diffused accountability** — the voter cannot easily identify whom to punish;
6. ⚠️ **Policy paralysis** on contested reforms.

> ⭐⭐ **The judgement to offer:** ⚠️ **coalitions cost efficiency and bought federalism.** ⭐ **Given a Constitution as centralised as India's, that was a favourable exchange — and the institutional lesson is that the mechanisms which made coalitions workable *(the CMP, the coordination committee, the floor test)* are worth preserving even in a single-party era, because they are the habits of consultative government.**

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The two rules
> ⭐⭐ **SARKARIA ORDER: pre-poll alliance → single largest party → post-poll coalition all joining → post-poll coalition with outside support.**
> ⭐⭐ ***S.R. BOMMAI* (1994): the FLOOR OF THE HOUSE, not the Governor's opinion.**

### The dates
> ⭐ **1967 — coalitions begin in the states.** ⭐ **1989–2014 — no single-party Lok Sabha majority for 25 years.**

### The machinery
> ⭐ **COMMON MINIMUM PROGRAMME + COORDINATION COMMITTEE.**

### The constitutional casualty
> ⚠️ ⭐ **COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY — Articles 75(3) and 164(2) — is what coalition politics strains hardest.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The issue of a hung Parliament adversely affects the stability of Indian Government. Discuss, and consider how far changing to a Presidential form of government would be a solution." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2002 Q6(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2002.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **The instability is real** — mid-term polls, blackmail by small partners, diluted collective responsibility, and policy paralysis. (ii) ⚠️ ⭐ **But the diagnosis is incomplete** — the coalition era also **deepened federalism, curbed Article 356, and widened representation**; and ⭐ ***S.R. Bommai* (1994)** plus the **Tenth Schedule** removed the two mechanisms *(arbitrary dismissal and defection)* that had made coalitions unstable in the first place. (iii) ⭐ **Would a presidential system solve it?** ⚠️ **A fixed-term executive gains stability but loses DAILY ACCOUNTABILITY to the legislature**; ⚠️ ⭐ **India's diversity makes a single winner-takes-all national office risky — a parliamentary system distributes power among many groups, which is why the Constituent Assembly chose it** *(see [Ch 12](../Part_II_System_of_Government/12_Parliamentary_System.md))*; ⚠️ **and a hung LEGISLATURE would still occur, producing deadlock between a fixed president and a hostile House** — the classic failure mode of presidential systems. (iv) ⭐ **The workable alternatives** — a ⭐ **CONSTRUCTIVE VOTE OF NO CONFIDENCE** *(a government may be removed only by simultaneously electing a successor, as in Germany)*; ⭐ **codified conventions for the Governor's discretion on the Sarkaria model**; ⭐ **time-bound floor tests**; and a **stronger Tenth Schedule**. ⭐ **Conclude:** the answer to instability is **better rules within parliamentary government**, not a change of system.

**2. "Coalition governments strengthened Indian federalism and weakened collective responsibility." Examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The federal gains** — regional parties as coalition partners with real leverage; the near-disappearance of casual **Article 356**; consultative bodies given weight; state demands entering the national agenda. ⚠️ **The collective-responsibility loss** — **Articles 75(3) and 164(2)** presume a Cabinet that speaks with one voice; in a coalition, allied ministers publicly dissent, resign selectively, and treat their party rather than the Cabinet as their principal; **the Prime Minister's power to seek a minister's dismissal is constrained by the alliance**. ⭐ **The institutional responses** — the **Common Minimum Programme** as a written substitute for shared ideology, and the **coordination committee** as a substitute for Cabinet discipline. ⭐ **Conclude:** coalitions moved collective responsibility from a **constitutional convention** to a **negotiated arrangement** — and whether that was a loss depends on whether you value **executive coherence** or **consultative federalism** more.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Coalitions began in the STATES after 1967 *(Samyukta Vidhayak Dals)*; the CENTRE's coalition era ran 1989–2014, when no single party won a Lok Sabha majority.**
- ⭐ **Pre-poll alliance vs POST-poll coalition — the distinction that drives the Governor's choice.**
- ⭐⭐ **SARKARIA order of preference: (1) a pre-poll alliance · (2) the single largest party with others' support · (3) a post-poll coalition with all partners in government · (4) a post-poll alliance with some supporting from outside.** ⭐ **Endorsed by the PUNCHHI Commission.**
- ⭐⭐ ***S.R. Bommai* (1994)** — ⭐ **the FLOOR TEST, not the Governor's satisfaction**; Article 356 is **justiciable**; a dissolved Assembly may be **revived**; the Assembly should not be dissolved before **parliamentary approval**; **secularism is basic structure**. ⭐ ***Rameshwar Prasad* (2006)** — dissolution on an apprehension of horse-trading is unconstitutional.
- ⭐ **Machinery: a COMMON MINIMUM PROGRAMME and a COORDINATION COMMITTEE.**
- ⭐ **Merits: federalism · wider representation · a check on concentrated power · consensus · accommodation of regional demands.**
- ⚠️ **Demerits: instability · blackmail by small partners · portfolio bargaining · ⭐ diluted COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY (Articles 75(3) and 164(2)) · diffused accountability · policy paralysis.**
- ⭐ **The reform worth naming: a CONSTRUCTIVE VOTE OF NO CONFIDENCE.**
