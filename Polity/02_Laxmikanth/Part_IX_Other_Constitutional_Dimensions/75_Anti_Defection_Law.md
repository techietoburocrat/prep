# Chapter 75 — Anti-Defection Law

[← Chapter 74](74_Coalition_Government.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 76 →](76_Pressure_Groups.md)

**Read time ~11 min** · **TENTH SCHEDULE · 52nd Amendment Act, 1985 · 91st Amendment Act, 2003** · 🔴 ⚠️ **Prelims: 2014, 2022, 2025** · **Mains: 2013**

> 📌 ⭐ **One of the shortest topics in the book and one of the most reliably examined.** ⚠️ **The Tenth Schedule is about four paragraphs long, and questions from it are almost always about the same three things: the GROUNDS, the MERGER exception, and WHO DECIDES.** ⭐ **Learn those three precisely and the chapter is finished.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **The Tenth Schedule was enacted to stop legislators selling their votes — and it succeeded.** ⚠️ **Its cost is that it also stopped legislators from thinking for themselves.** ⭐ **A whip now binds an MP on EVERY vote, not merely on the survival of the government — so Parliament's members vote as party delegates rather than as representatives, and that is the point [Mains 2013 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md) asked about.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ Origin

⭐ **The disease had a name before it had a cure:** ⭐ **"AYA RAM GAYA RAM"** — from a Haryana legislator who changed party three times in a fortnight in **1967**. ⚠️ **In the four years after 1967, roughly half of all state legislators in India defected at least once, and governments fell in a dozen states.**

⭐ **The cure:** ⭐⭐ **the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985**, which inserted the ⭐ **TENTH SCHEDULE** and amended ⭐ **Articles 101, 102, 190 and 191** *(vacation of seats and disqualifications, for Parliament and the state legislatures respectively)*.

> ⭐ **A fact worth using:** ⭐ **the 52nd Amendment is the FIRST time the Constitution acknowledged the existence of POLITICAL PARTIES** *(see [Ch 68](68_Political_Parties.md))*. ⭐ **India's parliamentary system ran for thirty-five years on a document that did not name the institution that actually forms its governments.**

---

## PART B — ⭐⭐ The grounds of disqualification

| Who | ⭐ **Disqualified if** |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **A member of a POLITICAL PARTY** | **(a)** ⭐ **He VOLUNTARILY GIVES UP his membership of that party; or** **(b)** ⭐ **He VOTES OR ABSTAINS FROM VOTING contrary to the DIRECTION (whip) of his party** — ⚠️ **unless he has obtained PRIOR PERMISSION, or the act is CONDONED by the party within FIFTEEN DAYS** |
| ⭐ **An INDEPENDENT member** | ⭐ **He JOINS any political party AFTER the election** |
| ⭐ **A NOMINATED member** | ⭐ **He joins a political party AFTER SIX MONTHS from the date he takes his seat** — ⚠️ ⭐ **within those six months he MAY join a party freely** |

> ⭐⭐ **"Voluntarily giving up membership" is far wider than resignation.** ⭐ **In *Ravi S. Naik v Union of India* (1994) the Supreme Court held that it can be INFERRED FROM CONDUCT** — publicly criticising the party, attending a rival's rallies, or asking the Governor to install another government. ⚠️ **You do not have to resign to be treated as having left.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The nominated-member rule is a favourite Prelims item: SIX MONTHS, and the clock runs from taking the seat.**

---

## PART C — ⭐⭐ The exceptions — and the one that was deleted

| Exception | Status |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **MERGER *(Paragraph 4)*** | ⭐ **ALIVE.** A member is not disqualified if his original party MERGES with another and ⭐ **NOT LESS THAN TWO-THIRDS of the members of the LEGISLATURE PARTY have agreed to the merger.** ⭐ **He may then either join the merged party or function as a separate group** |
| ⚠️⚠️ ⭐ **SPLIT *(the original Paragraph 3)*** | ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **DELETED by the 91st AMENDMENT ACT, 2003.** ⭐ **It used to exempt a breakaway faction of ONE-THIRD of the legislature party — and it became the standard route for engineered defections.** ⚠️ **"Split" is NO LONGER a defence** |
| ⭐ **The PRESIDING OFFICER *(Paragraph 5)*** | ⭐ **ALIVE.** A person elected **Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Chairman or Deputy Chairman** is not disqualified if he gives up his party membership on election to that office, or rejoins his party after ceasing to hold it |

> ⭐⭐ **The one-third → two-thirds change is the single most examinable fact in the chapter.** ⭐ **Before 2003: one-third could "split" and survive. After 2003: only a two-thirds MERGER survives.** ⚠️ **And note the precise wording — the two-thirds requirement applies to the LEGISLATURE PARTY *(the members in that House)*, not to the political party's general membership.**

### ⭐ The 91st Amendment Act, 2003 — all three limbs

| # | What it did |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1** | ⭐ **Deleted the SPLIT exception** |
| ⭐ **2** | ⭐ **Capped the COUNCIL OF MINISTERS at 15% of the total strength of the House** — ⭐ **Articles 75(1A)** *(Union)* and ⭐ **164(1A)** *(state, with a **minimum of 12** ministers)* |
| ⭐ **3** | ⭐ **A member disqualified for DEFECTION cannot be appointed a MINISTER, or hold any REMUNERATIVE POLITICAL POST, until his term expires or he is re-elected** — ⭐ **Articles 75(1B), 164(1B) and 361B** |

> ⭐ **Present the three limbs as one design.** ⚠️ **The framers of 2003 understood that defection was purchased with MINISTERIAL BERTHS — so they closed the escape hatch, shrank the supply of berths, and disqualified the defector from holding one.**

---

## PART D — ⭐⭐ Who decides, and can a court review it?

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The deciding authority** | ⭐ **The CHAIRMAN or the SPEAKER of the House concerned.** ⭐ **Where the question concerns the Speaker or Chairman himself, it is decided by a member of the House elected by it** |
| ⚠️ **The original bar** | ⭐ **Paragraph 7 of the Tenth Schedule barred the jurisdiction of ALL courts** in respect of any disqualification under the Schedule |
| ⭐⭐ ***Kihoto Hollohan v Zachillhu* (1992)** | ⭐ **The Constitution Bench UPHELD the Tenth Schedule as a whole**, but ⭐⭐ **STRUCK DOWN PARAGRAPH 7** — because, by ousting the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and the High Courts under **Articles 136, 226 and 227**, it changed a provision protected by the ⭐ **proviso to Article 368(2)** and therefore needed ⭐ **RATIFICATION BY HALF THE STATES**, which had not been obtained |
| ⭐⭐ **The consequence** | ⭐ **The Speaker acts as a TRIBUNAL, so his decision IS subject to JUDICIAL REVIEW** — ⚠️ **but only on limited grounds: MALA FIDES, PERVERSITY, VIOLATION OF NATURAL JUSTICE, or want of jurisdiction.** ⚠️ ⭐ **And there can be NO judicial intervention at an INTERLOCUTORY stage — the court cannot be moved before the Speaker decides** |

> ⭐⭐ **Note the parallel with [Ch 63](63_Cooperative_Societies.md).** ⚠️ **Twice now the proviso to Article 368(2) — ratification by half the states — has been the reason a constitutional amendment failed in part: Paragraph 7 in 1992, and Part IXB in 2021.** ⭐ **Pointing that out ties two chapters together and shows you understand Article 368 rather than merely reciting it.**

### ⭐ The four judgments after *Kihoto*

| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| ⭐ ***Ravi S. Naik* (1994)** | ⭐ **"Voluntarily giving up membership" may be INFERRED FROM CONDUCT** |
| ⭐ ***Rajendra Singh Rana* (2007)** | ⭐ **A Speaker's FAILURE TO DECIDE is itself justiciable** — inaction cannot defeat review |
| ⭐ ***Shrimanth Balasaheb Patil* (2019)** | ⭐ **A Speaker CANNOT fix the PERIOD of disqualification, and disqualification does NOT bar the member from contesting a bye-election** |
| ⭐⭐ ***Keisham Meghachandra Singh v Speaker, Manipur* (2020)** | ⭐ **A Speaker should decide a disqualification petition within a REASONABLE PERIOD — ORDINARILY THREE MONTHS.** ⭐⭐ **And the Court RECOMMENDED that Parliament consider amending the Constitution to transfer the decision to a PERMANENT TRIBUNAL headed by a RETIRED SUPREME COURT JUDGE or a RETIRED HIGH COURT CHIEF JUSTICE** |

⭐ **The most recent application:** ⭐ ***Subhash Desai v Principal Secretary, Governor of Maharashtra* (2023)** — arising from the Shiv Sena split. ⭐ **The Court held that the WHIP is appointed by the POLITICAL PARTY and not by the legislature party; that the Speaker must independently determine which faction is the real political party by reference to the party constitution and its leadership structure, rather than by counting legislators; and that the Governor had no objective material to call a floor test.**

## PART E — ⚠️⚠️ The critique, and the reform

### ⚠️ What is wrong with it

1. ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The whip applies to EVERY vote, not only to confidence motions and money bills.** ⭐ **So an MP cannot vote according to conscience, constituency interest or the evidence before a committee** — ⭐ **which is precisely why debate has hollowed out, and precisely the causation [Mains 2013 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md) asked you to assess;**
2. ⚠️ ⭐ **The Speaker is a partisan officer deciding a partisan question.** ⭐ **He belongs to a party, and in India — unlike the UK — he does not resign from it on election** *(see [Ch 22](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md))*;
3. ⚠️ ⭐ **There is NO time-limit in the Schedule itself.** ⭐ **Speakers have sat on petitions until the House's term expired**, which is why *Keisham Meghachandra* had to read in three months;
4. ⚠️ ⭐ **The MERGER exception rewards WHOLESALE defection while punishing individual conscience** — ⭐ **defect alone and you lose your seat; defect with two-thirds of your colleagues and you keep it, and may become a minister;**
5. ⚠️ **The RESIGN-AND-RE-CONTEST route** — a legislator resigns *(so there is no "defection")*, the government helps him win the bye-election, and he returns as a minister of the party he left;
6. ⚠️ **"Voluntarily giving up" is undefined**, so its scope depends on the Speaker;
7. ⚠️ **It strengthens the party LEADERSHIP, not the party** — the whip is issued by whoever controls the party machinery, so internal dissent is suppressed as effectively as external defection.

### ⭐⭐ The reform proposals — attach a name to each

| Reform | Proposed by |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Confine the whip to CONFIDENCE and NO-CONFIDENCE motions, MONEY BILLS, and votes on which the government's survival depends** | ⭐ **Law Commission's 170th Report (1999) · the NCRWC (2002) · the Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990)** |
| ⭐⭐ **Transfer the decision from the Speaker** — to the **President/Governor acting on the ECI's binding advice**, or to a **permanent tribunal** | ⭐ **Goswami Committee · the ECI · the NCRWC · the 2nd ARC** — and ⭐ **the Supreme Court in *Keisham Meghachandra* (2020)** |
| ⭐ **A statutory TIME-LIMIT for deciding petitions** | ⭐ **Read in as "ordinarily three months" by the Supreme Court (2020); still not in the Schedule** |
| ⭐ **Remove or tighten the MERGER exception** | Widely proposed |
| ⭐ **Bar a defector from office for the remainder of the term** | ⭐ **Partially done by the 91st Amendment, 2003** |

> ⭐⭐ **The framing for a top answer:** ⚠️ ⭐ **the Tenth Schedule solved a problem of INTEGRITY by creating a problem of REPRESENTATION.** ⭐ **It made governments stable and legislators interchangeable.** ⭐ **The fix that commands the widest agreement is the narrowest: LIMIT THE WHIP, and MOVE THE ADJUDICATION OUT OF THE SPEAKER'S HANDS.** ⭐ **Both would restore the individual member without reopening the market in defections.**

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The two amendments
> ⭐⭐ **52nd (1985) — the Tenth Schedule, amending Articles 101, 102, 190, 191.** ⭐⭐ **91st (2003) — SPLIT deleted · ministers capped at 15% *(states: minimum 12)* · a defector barred from office.**

### The three grounds
> ⭐ **Party member: VOLUNTARILY GIVES UP membership, or VOTES/ABSTAINS against the whip *(unless permitted or condoned within 15 DAYS)*.** ⭐ **INDEPENDENT: joins any party after the election.** ⭐ **NOMINATED: joins a party AFTER SIX MONTHS.**

### The exceptions
> ⭐⭐ **MERGER — TWO-THIRDS of the LEGISLATURE PARTY. ⚠️ SPLIT (one-third) — DELETED in 2003.** ⭐ **PRESIDING OFFICER — exempt.**

### The case
> ⭐⭐ ***Kihoto Hollohan* (1992)** — the Schedule upheld; ⭐ **PARAGRAPH 7 struck down for want of state ratification under the proviso to Article 368(2)**; ⭐ **the Speaker acts as a TRIBUNAL, so review lies — on mala fides, perversity, natural justice and jurisdiction only, and NOT before he decides.**

### The clock
> ⭐ ***Keisham Meghachandra* (2020)** — decide within a reasonable period, ⭐ **ORDINARILY THREE MONTHS**; and ⭐ **consider a PERMANENT TRIBUNAL under a retired Supreme Court judge.**

### The one-line critique
> ⚠️ ⭐ **"Defect alone and lose your seat; defect with two-thirds and become a minister."**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The role of individual MPs has diminished over the years and as a result healthy constructive debates on policy issues are not usually witnessed. How far can this be attributed to the anti-defection law which was legislated but with a different intention?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2013 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md). ⭐ **Note what the question demands: "HOW FAR" — so you must attribute PART of the decline to the Tenth Schedule and part to other causes. An answer that blames only the anti-defection law misses the question.***
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **The original intention** — to end **"Aya Ram Gaya Ram"** defections after 1967 and give governments stability; the **52nd Amendment, 1985**. (ii) ⭐⭐ **How it diminished the individual MP** — ⭐ **the whip binds EVERY vote, not only confidence motions**, so an MP who votes on the merits loses his seat; ⭐ **the Speaker, a party officer, adjudicates**; ⭐ **the party leadership, not the party, controls the whip, so internal dissent is suppressed too**; and ⭐ **committee work loses its point if the floor vote is predetermined**. (iii) ⭐ **But the other causes matter as much** — ⚠️ **declining sitting days**; **legislation passed with little or no debate, and Bills not referred to committees**; **frequent disruptions consuming House time**; **the practice of passing Bills as Money Bills to bypass the Rajya Sabha**; **the guillotine on Demands for Grants**; **the decline of private members' business**; and **candidate selection controlled centrally, so an MP's career depends on the leadership rather than the constituency**. (iv) ⭐ **The verdict** — the Tenth Schedule is a **substantial but not sufficient** explanation; it removed the *ability* to dissent, while institutional decay removed the *occasion* for it. (v) ⭐ **The reform** — ⭐ **confine the whip to confidence motions, money bills and votes on the government's survival *(170th Law Commission Report, NCRWC, Goswami)*; move adjudication to an independent tribunal *(Keisham Meghachandra, 2020)*; guarantee minimum sitting days; and refer every Bill to a committee.** ⭐ **Conclude:** stability was bought with representation, and the price can be reduced without giving up the purchase.

**2. "The anti-defection law punishes conscience and rewards arithmetic." Critically examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The paradox** — ⭐ **an individual voting on conviction is disqualified; two-thirds moving together are protected by the MERGER exception and may hold office.** ⭐ **Set out the grounds, the merger exception, and the deleted split exception.** ⚠️ **Show how the design produces its own evasion** — engineered mass resignations, the resign-and-re-contest route, and Speakers delaying decisions until the term ends. ⭐ **Cite ***Kihoto Hollohan*** on the Speaker as tribunal, ***Ravi S. Naik*** on inferred defection, ***Keisham Meghachandra*** on the three-month rule and the tribunal proposal, and ***Subhash Desai* (2023)** on the whip belonging to the political party. ⭐ **Balance:** the law did achieve its object — ⭐ **wholesale individual defection has all but disappeared, and governments now complete their terms.** ⭐ **Conclude with the two-part reform** — **limit the whip; move the adjudication.**

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐⭐ **52nd Amendment Act, 1985 → the TENTH SCHEDULE**, amending **Articles 101, 102, 190, 191**. ⭐ **The FIRST constitutional recognition of political parties.**
- ⭐⭐ **Grounds — a party member: VOLUNTARILY GIVING UP membership *(inferable from conduct — *Ravi S. Naik*, 1994)*, or VOTING/ABSTAINING against the whip *(unless permitted in advance or condoned within 15 DAYS)*.** ⭐ **An INDEPENDENT: joining any party after the election.** ⭐ **A NOMINATED member: joining a party AFTER SIX MONTHS.**
- ⭐⭐ **Exceptions: MERGER with the agreement of TWO-THIRDS of the LEGISLATURE PARTY · the PRESIDING OFFICER.** ⚠️ ⭐ **The SPLIT exception (one-third) was DELETED by the 91st Amendment, 2003.**
- ⭐ **91st Amendment, 2003 also: capped ministers at 15% of the House *(Articles 75(1A), 164(1A); states minimum 12)*, and barred a defector from ministerial or remunerative political office *(75(1B), 164(1B), 361B)*.**
- ⭐⭐ **Decided by the SPEAKER or CHAIRMAN.** ⭐ ***Kihoto Hollohan* (1992)** — the Schedule upheld, ⭐ **Paragraph 7 struck down for want of state ratification under the proviso to Article 368(2)**; ⭐ **the Speaker acts as a TRIBUNAL, so judicial review lies on mala fides, perversity, natural justice and jurisdiction — but NOT at an interlocutory stage.**
- ⭐ ***Rajendra Singh Rana* (2007)** — inaction is reviewable. ⭐ ***Shrimanth Balasaheb Patil* (2019)** — no fixed period of disqualification; a defector may contest a bye-election. ⭐⭐ ***Keisham Meghachandra* (2020)** — decide in ⭐ **three months**; consider a ⭐ **permanent tribunal**. ⭐ ***Subhash Desai* (2023)** — the **whip belongs to the POLITICAL party**.
- ⚠️ **Critique: the whip binds EVERY vote · a partisan Speaker adjudicates · no statutory time-limit · the merger exception rewards mass defection · the resign-and-re-contest route · "voluntarily giving up" is undefined · it empowers the leadership over the party.**
- ⭐ **Reforms: confine the whip to confidence motions, money bills and survival votes *(170th Law Commission Report, NCRWC, Goswami)*; move adjudication to the ECI-advised President/Governor or a permanent tribunal; a statutory time-limit.**
