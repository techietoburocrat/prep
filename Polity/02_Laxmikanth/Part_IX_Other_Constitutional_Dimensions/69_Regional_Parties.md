# Chapter 69 — Role of Regional Parties

**Read time ~7 min** · **No article — a political-science chapter** · 🟠 ⚠️ **Mains: 2007, 2022 — both answerable almost entirely from this chapter**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **India's Constitution is federal in form and centralised in design.** ⭐ **The institution that made it behave federally in practice was not a court or a commission — it was the REGIONAL PARTY**, which for twenty-five years held the balance of power at the Centre and used it to extract autonomy for the states.

---

## PART A — ⭐ What a regional party is, and why they rose

⭐ **A regional party** is one whose influence is confined to a **particular state or region**, whose agenda is built around **regional interests, identity or grievances**, and which is recognised by the ECI as a ⭐ **"State party"** *(the criteria are in [Ch 68](68_Political_Parties.md))*.

### ⭐⭐ The six causes of their rise

| # | Cause | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐⭐ **Linguistic and cultural identity** | ⭐ **The linguistic reorganisation of states (1956)** created units with a self-conscious identity; the ⭐ **anti-Hindi agitation of 1965** *(see [Ch 64](64_Official_Language.md))* converted that identity into an electoral force |
| 2 | ⭐ **Regional economic imbalance** | Backward regions demanding a larger share of central resources, and better-off states resenting redistribution — ⭐ **the same fault line that runs through the 15th Finance Commission's population criterion** *(see [Ch 44](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/44_Finance_Commission.md))* |
| 3 | ⭐ **Reaction against centralisation** | ⭐ **Article 356's misuse**, Governor's discretion, and central control over resources — the grievances the **Sarkaria Commission** was set up to examine *(see [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md))* |
| 4 | ⭐ **The organisational decline of the dominant party** | The **1967** elections cost the Congress power in **eight states**, and the space it vacated was filled regionally |
| 5 | ⭐ **Charismatic leadership and splinter groups** | Many state parties began as **splits from national parties** or around a single leader |
| 6 | ⭐ **Ethnic and tribal assertion** | Particularly in the **North-East**, where parties articulate demands rooted in the **Sixth Schedule and Article 371-series** arrangements *(see [Ch 40](../Part_VI_Union_Territories_and_Special_Areas/40_Scheduled_and_Tribal_Areas.md) and [Ch 36](../Part_IV_State_Government/36_Special_Provisions.md))* |

### ⭐ The types, with the classification worth reproducing

> ⭐ **(i) Parties rooted in LINGUISTIC or CULTURAL identity** · ⭐ **(ii) parties rooted in RELIGIOUS identity** · ⭐ **(iii) parties rooted in TRIBAL or ETHNIC identity** · ⭐ **(iv) SPLINTER groups from national parties** · ⭐ **(v) parties built around a CHARISMATIC LEADER** · ⭐ **(vi) parties representing a CASTE-BASED social coalition.**

---

## PART B — ⭐⭐ The role: the balance sheet

### ⭐ What they contributed

1. ⭐⭐ **They made federalism real.** In the **coalition era (1989–2014)** no party won a Lok Sabha majority, so ⭐ **the Union government depended on regional parties for survival** — and they used that leverage for **resources, portfolios, and restraint on Article 356**;
2. ⭐ **They restrained the misuse of Article 356.** ⚠️ **The *S.R. Bommai* judgment (1994) is usually credited with ending casual President's Rule** — ⭐ **but coalition arithmetic did as much work as the judgment**, because a Union government dependent on state parties cannot dismiss state governments freely;
3. ⭐ **They gave political voice to identities the national parties could not accommodate** — linguistic, tribal, caste-based and regional;
4. ⭐ **They deepened democratic participation**, mobilising groups previously outside politics and raising turnout;
5. ⭐ **They forced consultative policymaking** — the era of the **Common Minimum Programme**, and of genuine bargaining in the **National Development Council** and later the **GST Council** *(see [Ch 45](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/45_GST_Council.md))*;
6. ⭐ **They produced administrative innovation** — several of India's most-copied welfare schemes *(midday meals, subsidised food grain, social pensions)* began as state-level programmes of regional parties before being nationalised.

### ⚠️ What they cost

1. ⚠️ ⭐ **Parochialism and "sons of the soil"** — demands for local preference in employment and education that sit uneasily with ⭐ **Articles 15 and 16, and with Article 19(1)(e)'s right to reside and settle anywhere in India**;
2. ⚠️ **Instability** — coalition governments dependent on parties whose priorities are state-specific;
3. ⚠️ ⭐ **Bargaining for portfolios rather than policy** — the **Railways or a resource ministry** in exchange for support, which distorts national investment;
4. ⚠️ **Personality cults and dynastic control** — regional parties are, on the whole, even more leader-centric than national ones;
5. ⚠️ **Inter-state conflict** — river-water disputes, boundary disputes and language disputes are sharpened when each side has a party whose electoral base demands maximalism *(see [Ch 15](../Part_II_System_of_Government/15_Inter_State_Relations.md))*;
6. ⚠️ **Weak national perspective** on foreign policy, defence and macroeconomic questions.

> ⭐⭐ **The judgment to offer:** ⚠️ **the costs are real but the counterfactual is worse.** ⭐ **A Constitution this centralised, governing a country this diverse, needed a political mechanism through which regions could bargain rather than secede** — ⭐ **and the regional party has been that mechanism.** ⭐ **Note the historical fact that closes the argument: the states with the strongest regionalist parties are the ones where secessionist movements weakened, because regional grievance found an electoral outlet.**

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The six causes
> ⭐ **LANGUAGE · ECONOMIC IMBALANCE · CENTRALISATION · the decline of the dominant party · CHARISMA and SPLITS · ETHNIC and TRIBAL assertion.**

### The two dates
> ⭐ **1967 — Congress loses eight states, and regional parties take office.** ⭐ **1989–2014 — the coalition era, when regional parties held the balance at the Centre.**

### The thesis
> ⭐⭐ **"Regional parties converted a centralised Constitution into a federal practice."**

### The cost, in one phrase
> ⚠️ ⭐ **"Sons of the soil"** — regional preference against **Articles 15, 16 and 19(1)(e)**.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "While the national political parties in India favour centralisation, the regional parties are in favour of State autonomy." Comment. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2022 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2022.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **Establish why the proposition is broadly TRUE — and structurally, not morally so.** ⭐ **A national party's electoral logic runs across states, so it gains from uniform national schemes, a strong Centre and a single narrative; a regional party's entire base is one state, so its gains come from resources and autonomy for that state.** ⭐ **The incentive, not the ideology, drives the position.** (ii) ⭐ **Evidence for centralisation by national parties** — **Article 356's** historical use; **centrally sponsored schemes** under **Article 282** with matching shares; **cesses and surcharges outside the divisible pool**; the **Disaster Management Act, 2005** and **GST**; a preference for **"one nation, one X"** policy framing. (iii) ⭐ **Evidence for autonomy demands by regional parties** — the ⭐ **Rajamannar Committee (1969)** and the **Anandpur Sahib Resolution (1973)**; the **West Bengal memorandum (1977)**; the demand for the ⭐ **Sarkaria Commission (1983)** and later the **Punchhi Commission (2007)**; opposition to the **15th Finance Commission's 2011 census criterion**; and the **withdrawal of general consent to the CBI** since 2018 *(see [Ch 59](../Part_VIII_Non_Constitutional_Bodies/59_CBI.md))*. (iv) ⚠️ ⭐ **Now qualify the proposition, which is what earns the higher marks:** ⭐ **a national party in opposition at the Centre argues for state autonomy, and a regional party in office at the Centre defends central power** — the **Punchhi Commission** was appointed by a coalition; **GST was supported by state governments of every party**; and ⭐ **regional parties centralise ruthlessly WITHIN their own states, resisting devolution to panchayats and municipalities** *(see [Ch 37](../Part_V_Local_Government/37_Panchayati_Raj.md))*. ⭐ **So the real variable is PROXIMITY TO POWER, not party type.** (v) ⭐ **Conclude:** the statement describes a reliable tendency produced by electoral incentives, not a principled divide — and the institutional answer is a **functioning Inter-State Council under Article 263**, which removes the need for either side to bargain through crisis.

**2. "What is regionalism? In which ways has regionalism affected the Indian polity?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2007 Q7(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2007.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **Define** — a sense of loyalty to a region, expressed as a demand for greater recognition, resources or autonomy; distinguish ⭐ **supra-state, inter-state and intra-state** regionalism *(the standard three-fold classification)*. ⭐ **Its constitutional origins** — the **linguistic reorganisation of states (1956)**, and **Article 371-series** special provisions. ⭐ **Positive effects** — deeper federalism; regional parties as a channel for grievance; recognition of cultural diversity; state-level policy innovation; ⭐ **it defused secessionism by giving it an electoral outlet**. ⚠️ **Negative effects** — **sons-of-the-soil** movements against internal migration; **river-water and boundary disputes**; **demands for new states**; **insurgency** where the outlet failed; and **national policy paralysis in coalition periods**. ⭐ **Conclude:** regionalism is not the opposite of nationalism in the Indian experience — ⭐ **it has been the form in which regions consented to the nation**, and the constitutional devices that manage it *(reorganisation, the 371-series, the Sixth Schedule, the Finance Commission and the Inter-State Council)* are what keep it constructive.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **A "regional party" = the ECI's STATE PARTY** *(criteria in [Ch 68](68_Political_Parties.md))*, confined to a state or region and organised around regional interests.
- ⭐ **Six causes: LANGUAGE and culture · regional economic imbalance · reaction to CENTRALISATION · the decline of the dominant party · charisma and splinter groups · ETHNIC and TRIBAL assertion.**
- ⭐ **Six types: linguistic/cultural · religious · tribal/ethnic · splinter · leader-centred · caste-coalition.**
- ⭐⭐ **Contribution: made federalism real in the COALITION ERA (1989–2014) · restrained Article 356 alongside *S.R. Bommai* (1994) · voiced excluded identities · deepened participation · forced consultative policymaking (Common Minimum Programme, NDC, GST Council) · pioneered welfare innovations later nationalised.**
- ⚠️ **Costs: parochialism and "sons of the soil" *(against Articles 15, 16, 19(1)(e))* · instability · portfolio bargaining · dynastic and leader-centric control · sharper inter-state disputes · weak national perspective.**
- ⭐ **The autonomy documents to name: RAJAMANNAR Committee (1969) · Anandpur Sahib Resolution (1973) · West Bengal memorandum (1977) · SARKARIA Commission (1983) · PUNCHHI Commission (2007).**
- ⭐⭐ **The qualifier that lifts an answer: the position on centralisation tracks PROXIMITY TO POWER, not party type — and regional parties centralise within their own states.**
