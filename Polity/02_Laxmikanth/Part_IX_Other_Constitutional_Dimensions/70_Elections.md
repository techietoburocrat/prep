# Chapter 70 — Elections

**Read time ~14 min** · **Part XV, Articles 324–329** · 🔴 ⚠️ **Prelims: 2017, 2021, 2024, 2025** · **Mains: 2017, 2024**

> 📌 ⭐ **Chapters 70, 71 and 72 are one subject split into three: the SYSTEM, the LAW, and the REFORM.** ⚠️ **Read all three together, once.** ⭐ **Between them they have produced more than a dozen Mains questions since 2000 — the largest single cluster left in the book.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **Article 326 gave every adult Indian the vote in 1950, when most democracies had reached universal suffrage only in stages over a century.** ⭐ **The rest of this chapter is the machinery built to make that promise operable at a scale no other country has attempted — and Article 329, which keeps the courts out of it until polling is over.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ The constitutional provisions

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **324** | ⭐ **The ELECTION COMMISSION — superintendence, direction and control** *(see [Ch 41](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/41_Election_Commission.md))* |
| ⭐⭐ **325** | ⭐ **ONE GENERAL ELECTORAL ROLL for every territorial constituency**, and ⭐ **no person may be excluded from it, or claim inclusion in a special roll, on grounds ONLY of RELIGION, RACE, CASTE or SEX** |
| ⭐⭐ **326** | ⭐ **Elections to the LOK SABHA and the state LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLIES shall be on the basis of ADULT SUFFRAGE** — every citizen ⭐ **not less than 18 years** of age *(reduced from 21 by the ⭐ **61st Amendment Act, 1988**)*, unless disqualified for ⭐ **non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, or corrupt or illegal practice** |
| ⭐ **327** | ⭐ **PARLIAMENT may make provision with respect to all matters relating to elections** to Parliament and to state legislatures |
| ⭐ **328** | ⭐ **A STATE LEGISLATURE may do so for its own elections — subject to any law made by Parliament** |
| ⭐⭐ **329** | ⭐ **The BAR ON COURTS** — **(a)** the validity of any law relating to ⭐ **DELIMITATION of constituencies or the allotment of seats CANNOT be questioned in any court**; **(b)** ⭐ **no election to Parliament or a state legislature may be called in question EXCEPT by an ELECTION PETITION** presented to the authority and in the manner provided by law |

> ⭐⭐ **Article 325 does two separate things and both are examinable.** ⭐ **A SINGLE roll** — a rejection of the ⭐ **separate communal electorates** created by the Morley-Minto reforms of 1909 and entrenched by 1919 and 1935 *(see [Ch 1](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/01_Historical_Background.md))*. ⭐ **And NO exclusion** on religion, race, caste or sex.
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **Note the word "ONLY".** ⭐ **Exclusion on those grounds ALONE is barred; exclusion on other grounds — age, non-residence, unsoundness of mind — is permitted by Article 326 itself.**

### ⚠️⭐ Article 329A — the provision that was struck down

⭐ **The 39th Amendment, 1975 inserted Article 329A**, placing the election of the ⭐ **Prime Minister and the Speaker beyond the reach of any court**, retrospectively. ⭐ **In *Indira Nehru Gandhi v Raj Narain* (1975) the Supreme Court struck it down as violating the BASIC STRUCTURE** — ⭐ **free and fair elections and judicial review**. ⭐ **The 44th Amendment, 1978 formally deleted it.**

> ⭐ **Cite this case in any answer on the basic structure, on emergency-era amendments, or on free and fair elections.** ⚠️ **It is the first time the basic-structure doctrine was used to strike down a CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT after *Kesavananda*.**

## PART B — ⭐⭐ The two electoral systems India uses

| | ⭐ **FIRST PAST THE POST *(simple plurality)*** | ⭐ **PROPORTIONAL REPRESENTATION by SINGLE TRANSFERABLE VOTE** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Used for** | ⭐ **LOK SABHA · state LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLIES** | ⭐ **RAJYA SABHA · state LEGISLATIVE COUNCILS · the PRESIDENT · the VICE-PRESIDENT** |
| **How it works** | The candidate with the **most votes wins**, even without a majority | Voters rank candidates; a **quota** is computed; surpluses transfer |
| ⭐ **Ballot** | Secret | ⚠️ ⭐ **RAJYA SABHA: OPEN BALLOT since 2003** *(to curb cross-voting — the party's authorised agent may see the ballot)*. ⚠️ ⭐ **The PRESIDENTIAL election is by SECRET BALLOT** |
| ⭐ **Advantages** | **Simple**; produces **stable majorities**; a clear **constituency–representative link** | **Accurate reflection of preference**; protects minority representation |
| ⚠️ **Disadvantages** | ⭐ **A party can win a majority of seats on a minority of votes**; wasted votes; disadvantages dispersed minorities | Complex; weakens the constituency link; can produce fragmentation |

> ⭐⭐ **The open-ballot/secret-ballot contrast is a classic Prelims item.** ⚠️ **Rajya Sabha: OPEN. President: SECRET.** ⭐ **The reason is that MLAs and MPs voting for the Rajya Sabha are voting as party delegates, whereas the presidential electoral college votes as individuals.**

## PART C — ⭐ Delimitation

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **What it is** | ⭐ **Fixing the number and boundaries of constituencies**, and the seats reserved for SCs and STs |
| ⭐ **Who does it** | ⭐ **A DELIMITATION COMMISSION**, constituted by the **Central Government** under a **Delimitation Act** passed by Parliament |
| ⭐⭐ **Composition** | ⭐ **A retired JUDGE OF THE SUPREME COURT *(chairperson)* · the CHIEF ELECTION COMMISSIONER · the State Election Commissioner of the state concerned** |
| ⭐⭐ **Its orders** | ⭐ **Have the FORCE OF LAW and ⚠️ CANNOT BE CALLED IN QUESTION IN ANY COURT** *(Article 329(a))* |
| ⭐ **How many so far** | ⭐ **FOUR — 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002** |

### ⚠️⭐⭐ The freeze — the most important political fact in the chapter

| Amendment | What it did |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **42nd Amendment, 1976** | ⭐ **FROZE the allocation of Lok Sabha seats among the states on the basis of the 1971 CENSUS, until after the year 2000** |
| ⭐ **84th Amendment, 2001** | ⭐ **EXTENDED the freeze to 2026** |
| ⭐ **87th Amendment, 2003** | ⭐ **Permitted the readjustment of constituency BOUNDARIES within a state on the 2001 census — ⚠️ WITHOUT changing the number of seats allotted to any state** |

> ⭐⭐ **Why the freeze exists, and why it is about to become the biggest federal question in Indian politics:** ⚠️ ⭐ **states that succeeded in reducing population growth would have LOST Lok Sabha seats to states that did not.** ⭐ **Freezing seat allocation removed the penalty.** ⚠️ ⭐ **The freeze expires after 2026 — so the first delimitation thereafter would redistribute seats towards the more populous northern states, and the resistance to it will be exactly the same coalition of southern and western states that objected to the 15th Finance Commission's 2011 census criterion** *(see [Ch 44](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/44_Finance_Commission.md))*.
>
> ⭐ **And note the knock-on effect: the 106th Amendment's WOMEN'S RESERVATION is expressly triggered by "a census and the delimitation thereafter"** *(see [Ch 67](67_Special_Provisions_Certain_Classes.md))* — ⚠️ **so these two questions are now locked together.** ⭐ *[Prelims 2024 Q6](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2024.md) asked about Delimitation Commissions directly.*

## PART D — ⭐ The machinery and the process

### ⭐ The election machinery

> ⭐ **Election Commission of India → CHIEF ELECTORAL OFFICER *(one per state)* → DISTRICT ELECTION OFFICER → ELECTORAL REGISTRATION OFFICER *(rolls)* and RETURNING OFFICER *(the conduct of the election in a constituency)* → PRESIDING and POLLING OFFICERS *(the booth)*.** ⭐ **Plus OBSERVERS — general, expenditure and police — appointed directly by the ECI.**

### ⭐ The sequence

⭐ **Notification → nominations → scrutiny → withdrawal → campaign *(minimum 14 days between the last date of withdrawal and the poll)* → the ⭐ 48-HOUR SILENCE PERIOD → poll → counting → declaration of result → the ECI's report of the returned candidates to the President/Governor.**

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Security deposit — Lok Sabha** | ⭐ **₹25,000** *(₹12,500 for an SC/ST candidate)* |
| ⭐ **Security deposit — Assembly** | ⭐ **₹10,000** *(₹5,000 for an SC/ST candidate)* |
| ⭐⭐ **When it is forfeited** | ⭐ **If the candidate fails to poll MORE THAN ONE-SIXTH of the valid votes** |
| ⭐ **Proposers required** | ⭐ **1** for a recognised party's candidate; ⭐ **10** for an independent or an unrecognised party's candidate |
| ⭐ **Maximum constituencies** | ⭐ **TWO** *(Section 33(7), RPA 1951)* — ⚠️ **and a winner in both must vacate one** *(exactly what [Prelims 2021 Q18](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2021.md) asked)* |
| ⭐ **Expenditure ceiling** | ⚠️ ⭐ **On the CANDIDATE, not on the PARTY** — a Lok Sabha ceiling of about **₹95 lakh** in larger states and **₹75 lakh** in smaller ones, and about **₹40 lakh / ₹28 lakh** for Assembly seats after the 2022 revision. ⚠️ **Verify the current figures before quoting them** |

> ⭐⭐ **The candidate-only ceiling is the single biggest hole in Indian election finance and the reason "expenditure limits" achieve so little.** ⚠️ **A political party's own expenditure on general propaganda is not chargeable to any candidate**, and a **recognised party's 40 star campaigners' travel costs** are excluded as well *(see [Ch 68](68_Political_Parties.md))*. ⭐ **Say this explicitly in any funding answer.**

### ⭐ The technology

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **EVMs** | ⭐ **Standalone, non-networked machines** with one-time programmable chips; used in phases from the 1980s and ⭐ **universally from 2004** |
| ⭐⭐ **VVPAT** | ⭐ **Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail** — introduced from **2013** and used at ⭐ **every polling station from 2019**. ⭐ **Slips are counted and matched in FIVE randomly selected polling stations per Assembly segment**, a requirement laid down by the Supreme Court in 2019 and reaffirmed in **2024** |
| ⭐⭐ **NOTA** | ⭐ **Introduced after *PUCL v Union of India* (2013)**, which held that the ⭐ **right to reject is part of the freedom of expression under Article 19(1)(a)** and that **secrecy must be preserved for those who reject**. ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **NOTA has NO electoral consequence — even if NOTA polls the most votes, the leading candidate among the rest is declared elected** |
| ⭐ **Postal and absentee voting** | ⭐ **ETPBS** for service voters; ⭐ **the postal-ballot facility for ABSENTEE VOTERS — senior citizens above 85, persons with disabilities, and those in notified essential services** |

### ⭐⭐ The three rights — a distinction examiners love

| Right | Status |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The right to VOTE** | ⚠️ ⭐ **A STATUTORY right** *(conferred by Section 62 of the RPA, 1951)* — ⚠️ **NOT a Fundamental Right**, though it flows from a constitutional provision *(Article 326)*. ⭐ *[Prelims 2017 Q15](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2017.md) tested this* |
| ⭐⭐ **The right to KNOW about a candidate** | ⭐ **A FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT under Article 19(1)(a)** — ***Union of India v Association for Democratic Reforms* (2002)** and ***PUCL* (2003)**, which produced the ⭐ **mandatory affidavit on assets, liabilities, education and criminal cases** |
| ⭐ **The right to CONTEST** | ⚠️ **A statutory right only** |

> ⭐⭐ **That middle row is one of the most useful facts in the entire book.** ⭐ **The same Article 19(1)(a) reasoning produced the candidate affidavit in 2002, underpins the RTI Act, and struck down electoral bonds in 2024** *(see [Ch 68](68_Political_Parties.md))*. ⭐ **Three landmark outcomes, one doctrine.**

## PART E — ⭐ Simultaneous elections *("one nation, one election")*

| ⭐ **The case FOR** | ⚠️ **The case AGAINST** |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Cost** — one exercise instead of many | ⚠️ ⭐ **It requires curtailing or extending the term of some legislatures, which offends the five-year term in Articles 83 and 172** |
| ⭐ **The MCC would not be in force for much of the year**, so governance and project approvals would not stall | ⚠️ ⭐ **What happens on a mid-term no-confidence motion?** Either the House waits, which weakens **collective responsibility**, or elections are held anyway, which defeats the purpose |
| ⭐ **Reduced burden on security forces and administrative staff** | ⚠️ ⭐ **A serious FEDERAL objection — evidence suggests national issues dominate when polls coincide, disadvantaging regional parties and state-level accountability** |
| ⭐ **It was the norm from 1952 to 1967**, so it is not an untried idea | ⚠️ **Accountability is exercised more often when elections are more frequent** |
| ⭐ Recommended by the **Law Commission's 170th Report (1999)** and a **Parliamentary Standing Committee (2015)** | ⚠️ **Requires amendments and, on some views, ratification by the states** |

⭐ **The current position:** ⭐ **a High-Level Committee chaired by former President Ram Nath Kovind**, constituted in **September 2023**, submitted its report in **March 2024**, recommending simultaneous Lok Sabha and Assembly elections as a first step and **local body elections within 100 days** thereafter, with a **single electoral roll** and the constitutional amendments needed to synchronise terms. ⭐ **Constitution Amendment Bills to that effect were introduced in the Lok Sabha in December 2024 and referred to a Joint Committee of Parliament.** ⚠️ **Verify the current status before writing about it as settled law.**

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The five articles
> ⭐ **324 ECI · 325 ONE ROLL, no exclusion on religion, race, caste or sex ONLY · 326 ADULT SUFFRAGE at 18 (61st Amendment, 1988) · 327 PARLIAMENT legislates · 328 the STATE legislature may, subject to Parliament · 329 COURTS BARRED, election petitions only.**

### The struck-down article
> ⭐ **329A (39th Amendment, 1975) → struck down in *Indira Nehru Gandhi v Raj Narain* (1975) → deleted by the 44th Amendment, 1978.**

### The two systems
> ⭐ **FPTP — Lok Sabha and Assemblies.** ⭐ **PR by STV — Rajya Sabha, Legislative Councils, President, Vice-President.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Rajya Sabha OPEN ballot (2003); Presidential election SECRET ballot.**

### Delimitation
> ⭐ **Retired SUPREME COURT judge + CEC + State Election Commissioner.** ⭐ **Orders have the force of law and are BEYOND COURTS.** ⭐ **Four Commissions: 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002.** ⭐ **42nd froze it on the 1971 census to 2000 · 84th extended to 2026 · 87th allowed boundary readjustment on the 2001 census without changing state totals.**

### The money numbers
> ⭐ **Deposit: ₹25,000 Lok Sabha / ₹10,000 Assembly *(half for SC/ST)*; forfeited below ONE-SIXTH of valid votes.** ⭐ **1 proposer for a recognised party, 10 otherwise.** ⭐ **Maximum TWO constituencies.**

### The three rights
> ⚠️ ⭐ **VOTE = statutory. CONTEST = statutory. ⭐⭐ KNOW = FUNDAMENTAL, under Article 19(1)(a) *(ADR 2002, PUCL 2003)*.**

### NOTA
> ⭐ ***PUCL* (2013)** — the **right to reject** under Article 19(1)(a). ⚠️ ⭐ **No electoral consequence whatsoever.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Examine the need for electoral reforms as suggested by various committees, with particular reference to the 'one nation – one election' principle." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2024 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **The need** — criminalisation, money power, opaque funding, frequent polls stalling governance, and the MCC's near-permanent operation. Name the committees: ⭐ **Goswami (1990) · Vohra (1993) · Indrajit Gupta (1998) on state funding · the Law Commission's 170th (1999) and 255th (2015) Reports · the 2nd ARC's *Ethics in Governance* · the ECI's own 2016 proposals.** (ii) ⭐ **"One nation, one election"** — the practice from **1952 to 1967**; recommended by the **170th Law Commission Report** and a **2015 Standing Committee**; the ⭐ **Kovind High-Level Committee (2023–24)** recommending synchronised Lok Sabha and Assembly polls, **local body polls within 100 days**, and a **single electoral roll**. (iii) ⭐ **The arguments both ways** — the Part E table. (iv) ⚠️ ⭐ **The constitutional obstacles** — the fixed five-year terms in **Articles 83 and 172**; the effect on **Article 356** and on a mid-term loss of confidence; possible **state ratification**. (v) ⭐ **Conclude:** simultaneous elections address **cost and continuity**, which are real but secondary problems; ⭐ **the primary problems — criminalisation, funding opacity and inner-party democracy — are untouched by it.** ⭐ **Sequence the reform accordingly: disqualification on framing of charges, transparent funding after the 2024 electoral-bonds judgment, and a Registration of Political Parties Act first.**

**2. "Simultaneous election to the Lok Sabha and the State Assemblies will limit the amount of time and money spent in electioneering but it will reduce the government's accountability to the people." Discuss. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2017 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2017.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **Concede the first half fully** — cost, security deployment, teacher and staff diversion, and the MCC freeze on new projects. ⭐ **Then take the accountability half seriously.** ⭐ **Arguments that accountability falls:** a government faces the electorate once in five years instead of repeatedly; ⚠️ ⭐ **evidence that national issues crowd out state issues when polls coincide, so state governments escape scrutiny**; and the structural problem that a **mid-term collapse** must either be papered over *(weakening Article 75(3)/164(2) collective responsibility)* or trigger the very mid-term poll the reform was meant to abolish. ⚠️ **Arguments that accountability need not fall** — continuous campaigning produces **populist announcements rather than scrutiny**; accountability comes from **Parliament, the CAG, the RTI regime and the courts**, not only from elections. ⭐ **Conclude:** the trade-off is genuine and asymmetric — ⭐ **the savings accrue to the state, the accountability loss falls on the voter and disproportionately on state-level politics.** ⭐ **Any workable scheme must therefore protect the no-confidence route** *(for instance a constructive vote of no confidence on the German model, which the Kovind Committee's approach approaches)*.

**3. "The right to vote is statutory, the right to know is fundamental. Discuss the significance of this distinction for Indian democracy." (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The distinction** — the right to vote is conferred by **Section 62 of the RPA, 1951** under Article 326, and the Supreme Court has treated it as **statutory**; ⭐ **the right to know a candidate's antecedents is a FUNDAMENTAL right under Article 19(1)(a)** *(**ADR** 2002, **PUCL** 2003)*. ⭐ **Why it matters** — a statutory right can be modified by Parliament; ⚠️ **a fundamental one cannot**, and this is precisely why ⭐ **Parliament's attempt to undo the affidavit requirement was struck down**, why ⭐ ***PUCL* (2013)** could create **NOTA**, and why ⭐ **electoral bonds fell in 2024**. ⭐ **Conclude:** the courts have protected the **INFORMATIONAL** dimension of democracy far more strongly than the **participatory** one — and every major electoral-transparency advance since 2002 rests on that single article.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Part XV, Articles 324–329.** ⭐ **325 — ONE general roll; no exclusion on grounds ONLY of religion, race, caste or sex.** ⭐ **326 — adult suffrage at 18 *(61st Amendment, 1988)*; disqualification for non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, or corrupt or illegal practice.** ⭐ **327 Parliament · 328 state legislature, subject to Parliament.**
- ⭐⭐ **329 — (a) delimitation laws beyond the courts; (b) an election may be challenged ONLY by an ELECTION PETITION.** ⭐ **329A (39th Amendment, 1975) struck down in *Indira Nehru Gandhi v Raj Narain* (1975); deleted by the 44th Amendment.**
- ⭐ **FPTP for the Lok Sabha and Assemblies; PR by STV for the Rajya Sabha, Legislative Councils, President and Vice-President.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Rajya Sabha OPEN ballot (2003); Presidential election SECRET.**
- ⭐ **Delimitation Commission: a retired SUPREME COURT judge + the CEC + the State Election Commissioner; orders have the force of law and are NOT justiciable.** ⭐ **Four Commissions — 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002.** ⭐ **42nd (1976) froze seat allocation on the 1971 census · 84th (2001) extended to 2026 · 87th (2003) allowed boundary readjustment on the 2001 census.**
- ⭐ **Machinery: CEO (state) → DEO → ERO and Returning Officer → Presiding Officer**, plus **ECI observers**.
- ⭐ **Deposit ₹25,000 (LS) / ₹10,000 (Assembly), half for SC/ST; forfeited below ONE-SIXTH of valid votes.** ⭐ **1 proposer for a recognised party's candidate, 10 otherwise.** ⭐ **Maximum TWO constituencies *(s.33(7))*.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Expenditure ceilings bind the CANDIDATE, not the PARTY.**
- ⭐ **EVMs universal from 2004; VVPAT from 2013 and universal from 2019; slips counted in FIVE random booths per Assembly segment.** ⭐ **NOTA from *PUCL* (2013)** — ⚠️ **no electoral consequence.**
- ⭐⭐ **Right to VOTE = statutory · right to CONTEST = statutory · right to KNOW = FUNDAMENTAL under Article 19(1)(a) *(ADR 2002, PUCL 2003 → the candidate affidavit)*.**
- ⭐ **Simultaneous elections — the norm 1952–67; Law Commission 170th Report (1999); ⭐ Kovind High-Level Committee (2023–24) → Constitution Amendment Bills introduced December 2024, referred to a Joint Committee.** ⚠️ **Verify current status.**
