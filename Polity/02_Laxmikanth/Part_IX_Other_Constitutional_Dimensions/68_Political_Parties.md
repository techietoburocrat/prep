# Chapter 68 — Political Parties

[← Chapter 67](67_Special_Provisions_Certain_Classes.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 69 →](69_Regional_Parties.md)

**Read time ~10 min** · **No Part of the Constitution · RPA 1951 s.29A + the Symbols Order, 1968** · 🟠 Prelims: **2024** · ⚠️ **Mains: 2016, 2022**

> 📌 ⭐ **A fact worth opening any answer with: the Constitution as enacted in 1950 did not mention political parties at all.** ⚠️ **The first constitutional recognition of a political party came in 1985, with the TENTH SCHEDULE.** ⭐ **Thirty-five years of parliamentary democracy ran on a document that pretended parties did not exist.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **Parties are the machinery through which parliamentary democracy actually works — they select candidates, form governments, enforce discipline and structure choice — and they are regulated almost entirely by an ELECTION LAW and an ECI ORDER, not by the Constitution.** ⚠️ **That regulatory thinness is the source of every problem in Part C.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ Registration and recognition

### ⭐ Registration — Section 29A of the RPA, 1951

⭐ **Every association wanting to be treated as a political party must register with the ELECTION COMMISSION**, filing a memorandum affirming ⭐ **true faith and allegiance to the Constitution, to SOCIALISM, SECULARISM and DEMOCRACY, and to the SOVEREIGNTY, UNITY AND INTEGRITY of India.**

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The ECI can register a party but CANNOT DE-REGISTER one** *(***INC v Institute of Social Welfare***, 2002 — see [Ch 41](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/41_Election_Commission.md))*, except in narrow cases such as registration obtained by fraud. ⭐ **The ECI has repeatedly asked Parliament for the power. It has not been given.**

### ⭐⭐ Recognition — the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968

⚠️ **Registration and recognition are different things.** ⭐ **Recognition brings a RESERVED SYMBOL and a set of privileges, and it depends on electoral performance.**

| ⭐ **NATIONAL party — satisfy ANY ONE** | ⭐ **STATE party — satisfy ANY ONE** |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **6% of valid votes** in a Lok Sabha or Assembly election in ⭐ **FOUR or more states, AND at least 4 LOK SABHA seats** | ⭐ **6% of valid votes** in an Assembly election **AND 2 Assembly seats** |
| ⭐ **2% of Lok Sabha seats *(i.e. 11)* won from at least THREE different states** | ⭐ **6% of valid votes** in a Lok Sabha election from that state **AND 1 Lok Sabha seat** |
| ⭐ **Recognised as a STATE party in at least FOUR states** | ⭐ **3% of Assembly seats, or 3 seats, whichever is MORE** |
| | ⭐ **1 Lok Sabha seat for every 25 seats allotted to the state** |
| | ⭐ **8% of the total valid votes in the state** in a Lok Sabha or Assembly election — ⚠️ **even without winning a single seat** *(added in 2011)* |

### ⭐ What recognition actually gets you

| Privilege | Recognised | Unrecognised |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **A RESERVED SYMBOL** across the country or state | ⭐ **Yes** | ⚠️ **No — a free symbol allotted each time** |
| ⭐ **Free broadcast time** on Doordarshan and All India Radio | ⭐ **Yes** | No |
| ⭐ **"STAR CAMPAIGNERS"** whose travel expenditure is **NOT counted** in a candidate's election expenses | ⭐ **40** | ⚠️ **20** |
| ⭐ **Proposers required for a nomination** | ⭐ **1** | ⚠️ **10** |
| **Free copies of the electoral rolls** | ⭐ **Yes** | No |

> ⭐ **The star-campaigner rule is the most exam-relevant of these** — ⚠️ **it is a legal route by which a large party's national campaign spending stays outside every candidate's expenditure ceiling**, and it is a standing item on every electoral-reform list.

⭐ **Splits and mergers** — when a recognised party splits, the **ECI decides which faction gets the symbol** under **Paragraph 15 of the Symbols Order**, applying the test laid down in ***Sadiq Ali v ECI* (1971)**: the ⭐ **aims and objects of the party, its constitution, and above all the MAJORITY IN ITS ORGANISATIONAL AND LEGISLATIVE WINGS.**

## PART B — ⭐ The evolution of the party system

| Phase | Character |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1952–1967 — the "CONGRESS SYSTEM"** | ⭐ **Rajni Kothari's term.** One-party DOMINANCE within a competitive framework: the Congress was itself a coalition of interests, and opposition parties functioned as ⭐ **"parties of pressure"** that influenced it from outside without displacing it |
| ⭐ **1967–1989 — erosion and defection** | The **1967** elections cost Congress power in **eight states**; the era of ⭐ **"Aya Ram Gaya Ram"** defections, unstable coalitions and President's Rule |
| ⭐ **1989–2014 — the COALITION era** | ⭐ **No single party won a majority for 25 years.** Regional parties became **kingmakers**; policy was negotiated through **common minimum programmes**; ⭐ **federalism deepened in practice more than in law** |
| ⭐ **2014 onward** | A return to **single-party majorities** at the Centre, with regional parties remaining **dominant within their states** — producing the pattern ⭐ **[Mains 2022 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2022.md)** described: **national parties favouring centralisation, regional parties favouring state autonomy** |

⭐ **The classification worth reproducing:** ⭐ **national parties · state (regional) parties · registered-unrecognised parties**; and by ideology, ⭐ **the Congress-type centrist, the left, the right, and identity-based parties** *(caste, religion, language, tribe)*.

## PART C — ⚠️⚠️ The five problems, and the funding case

### ⚠️ The five

1. ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **No internal democracy** — organisational elections are often nominal; ⭐ **candidate selection, the most consequential decision in Indian politics, is made by a leadership with no obligation to consult members**; and ⭐ **dynastic succession follows naturally from that**;
2. ⚠️ ⭐ **Opaque funding** *(below)*;
3. ⚠️ ⭐ **Criminalisation** — parties field candidates with pending serious criminal cases because winnability outweighs propriety *(see [Ch 72](72_Electoral_Reforms.md))*;
4. ⚠️ **Personality over programme** — manifestos converge, and parties are identified with leaders rather than positions;
5. ⚠️ **Factionalism, splits and defections** — the reason the **Tenth Schedule** exists *(see [Ch 75](75_Anti_Defection_Law.md))*.

### ⭐⭐ Funding — and the 2024 judgment

| Stage | What it did |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **RPA s.29B and 29C** | Parties may receive contributions from any person or company **other than a foreign source**, and must ⭐ **report every contribution above ₹20,000 to the ECI** |
| ⭐ **Finance Act, 2017** | ⭐ **Reduced the permissible ANONYMOUS CASH donation to ₹2,000**, and introduced ⭐⭐ **ELECTORAL BONDS** — bearer instruments bought from a designated bank and donated to a party, with the donor's identity known to the bank but ⚠️ **disclosed to nobody else**. It also ⚠️ ⭐ **removed the cap on CORPORATE donations** *(previously 7.5% of the average net profit of the last three financial years)* and ⚠️ **removed the requirement that a company disclose its political contributions in its profit and loss account** |
| ⭐⭐ **The ADR judgment, February 2024** | ⭐ **A five-judge Constitution Bench STRUCK DOWN the Electoral Bonds Scheme and the accompanying amendments as UNCONSTITUTIONAL.** ⭐ **Reasoning: the scheme violated the VOTER'S RIGHT TO INFORMATION under ARTICLE 19(1)(a)** — information about political funding is essential to an informed vote; the "donor privacy" justification did not satisfy the **proportionality** test; and ⚠️ **unlimited corporate donations enable quid pro quo and distort political equality.** ⭐ **The Court directed the State Bank of India to disclose bond details to the ECI for publication** |

> ⭐⭐ **The ADR judgment is the single most citable case in the elections cluster and belongs in answers on funding, transparency AND Article 19(1)(a).** ⚠️ **Note the parallel with [Ch 56](../Part_VIII_Non_Constitutional_Bodies/56_Central_Information_Commission.md): the RIGHT TO INFORMATION as a facet of free speech is the same doctrine that underpins the RTI Act.**

⭐ **Two further transparency points worth a line each:**
- ⚠️ ⭐ **Political parties are not treated as "public authorities" under the RTI Act.** ⭐ **The Central Information Commission held in 2013 that six national parties were, and they have not complied** — the question remains unresolved, and it is the sharpest available criticism of party opacity;
- ⚠️ **Amendments to the FCRA in 2016 and 2018 retrospectively permitted donations from the Indian subsidiaries of foreign companies**, reversing a High Court finding of illegality.

⭐ **The reform menu:** ⭐ **state funding of elections** *(Indrajit Gupta Committee, 1998 — partial, in kind)*; ⭐ **a National Election Fund**; ⭐ **compulsory internal party elections and a statutory Registration of Political Parties Act** *(Law Commission's 255th Report)*; ⭐ **the power to DE-REGISTER**; ⭐ **bringing parties under the RTI Act**; and ⭐ **caps on and disclosure of corporate donations.**

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The absent word
> ⭐⭐ **The Constitution did not mention political parties until the ⭐ 52nd Amendment, 1985 (Tenth Schedule).**

### National party — the three routes
> ⭐ **6% in FOUR states + 4 Lok Sabha seats · 2% of Lok Sabha seats (11) from THREE states · a State party in FOUR states.**

### State party — the five routes
> ⭐ **6% + 2 MLAs · 6% + 1 MP · 3% of Assembly seats or 3 seats (whichever more) · 1 MP per 25 seats allotted · 8% of votes with NO seat (2011).**

### The privileges
> ⭐ **Reserved symbol · free broadcast time · 40 star campaigners (vs 20) · 1 proposer (vs 10) · free electoral rolls.**

### The funding case
> ⭐⭐ ***Association for Democratic Reforms* (Feb 2024, five judges)** — ⭐ **ELECTORAL BONDS STRUCK DOWN**; ⭐ **the voter's RIGHT TO INFORMATION under Article 19(1)(a)**; ⭐ **the removal of the corporate-donation cap also struck down**; SBI directed to disclose to the ECI.

### The split test
> ⭐ ***Sadiq Ali* (1971)** — aims and objects, the party constitution, and ⭐ **the MAJORITY in the organisational and legislative wings.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The Indian party system is passing through a phase of transition which looks to be full of contradictions and paradoxes." Discuss. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2016 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2016.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **Name the transitions** — from **one-party dominance** *(the Congress system)* to **coalition politics (1989–2014)** to a **return of single-party majorities with regional dominance in the states**. ⭐ **The paradoxes to develop:** (i) ⚠️ **parties are the most powerful institutions in Indian democracy and the least internally democratic**; (ii) ⭐ **the system is more competitive than ever — record turnout, high anti-incumbency, genuine alternation — yet candidate selection is more centralised than ever**; (iii) ⭐ **the anti-defection law strengthened parties against individual legislators, and thereby weakened Parliament as a deliberative body** *(the point of [Mains 2013 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md))*; (iv) ⚠️ **the electorate is more informed** *(RTI, affidavits under ***ADR***, media)* **while party finance became less transparent** — until the **2024 judgment**; (v) ⭐ **regional parties deepened federalism while nationalising their own ambitions**; (vi) ⚠️ **social coalitions widened representation while dynastic control narrowed leadership.** ⭐ **Conclude:** the transition is from **parties as movements to parties as electoral machines** — the reform agenda that follows is **internal democracy, funding transparency, de-registration powers and RTI coverage.**

**2. "While the national political parties in India favour centralisation, the regional parties are in favour of State autonomy." Comment. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2022 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2022.md). ⭐ **Full skeleton in [Ch 69](69_Regional_Parties.md).***

**3. "Should political parties be brought within the ambit of the Right to Information Act?" (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The case for** — parties are ⭐ **substantially financed indirectly by the state** *(free broadcast time, free electoral rolls, tax exemption under Section 13A of the Income Tax Act, subsidised accommodation)*; they ⭐ **perform a public function** in selecting the people who make law; ⭐ **the CIC so held in 2013**; and the ⭐ **2024 ADR judgment establishes that the voter's right to information about parties is protected by Article 19(1)(a)**. ⚠️ **The case against** — internal strategy and candidate assessments would become public; the risk of vexatious applications; parties argue they are voluntary associations already answerable through the ECI and the Income Tax Act. ⭐ **Conclude with the workable middle** — ⭐ **mandatory disclosure of accounts, donations, candidate selection criteria and internal election records, through a statutory Registration of Political Parties Act**, rather than blanket RTI coverage which would collapse under exemption litigation.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⚠️ ⭐ **The Constitution did not mention political parties until the ⭐ 52nd Amendment, 1985 (Tenth Schedule).**
- ⭐ **Registration: Section 29A, RPA 1951**, with the **ECI**, on a memorandum affirming allegiance to the Constitution, **socialism, secularism, democracy** and the **sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.** ⚠️ ⭐ **The ECI cannot DE-REGISTER** *(INC v Institute of Social Welfare, 2002)*.
- ⭐ **Recognition: the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.** ⭐ **NATIONAL — 6% in 4 states + 4 LS seats · 2% of LS seats (11) from 3 states · a State party in 4 states.** ⭐ **STATE — 6% + 2 MLAs · 6% + 1 MP · 3% of Assembly seats or 3 seats · 1 MP per 25 seats · 8% of votes with no seat (2011).**
- ⭐ **Privileges: a reserved symbol · free broadcast time · 40 star campaigners (vs 20) · 1 proposer (vs 10) · free electoral rolls.** ⭐ **Splits decided under Para 15 of the Symbols Order, on the *Sadiq Ali* (1971) test.**
- ⭐ **Phases: the CONGRESS SYSTEM (Rajni Kothari, 1952–67) → defection and instability (1967–89) → COALITIONS (1989–2014) → single-party majority with regional dominance (2014–).**
- ⭐ **Funding: RPA s.29C — report contributions above ₹20,000** · **Finance Act 2017 — ₹2,000 anonymous cash cap + ELECTORAL BONDS + removal of the corporate-donation cap** · ⭐⭐ ***ADR v Union of India* (Feb 2024, five judges) — bonds and the accompanying amendments STRUCK DOWN, on the voter's RIGHT TO INFORMATION under Article 19(1)(a).**
- ⚠️ **Five problems: no internal democracy · opaque funding · criminalisation · personality over programme · factionalism and defection.** ⭐ **Reforms: Indrajit Gupta Committee (1998) on state funding · a Registration of Political Parties Act (255th Law Commission Report) · de-registration powers · RTI coverage · corporate donation caps.**
