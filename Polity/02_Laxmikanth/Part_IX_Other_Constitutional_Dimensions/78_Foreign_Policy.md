# Chapter 78 — Foreign Policy

[← Chapter 77](77_National_Integration.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 79 →](../Part_X_Working_of_the_Constitution/79_NCRWC.md)

**Read time ~5 min** · **Article 51 (DPSP) · Article 253 · Union List entries 10–21** · ⚪ Prelims: **2013, 2014** · Mains: this belongs to GS-II *International Relations*, not to polity

> 📌 ⚠️ ⭐ **Read this chapter for its CONSTITUTIONAL content only — Articles 51 and 253 — and get the substance of foreign policy from your International Relations preparation.** ⭐ **Laxmikanth's own treatment is brief, and the polity examiner asks only about the constitutional machinery.** ⭐ **Two articles, and one genuinely important federal point.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **Foreign affairs is entirely a UNION subject, and treaty-making is entirely an EXECUTIVE act.** ⚠️ **Parliament's role is confined to IMPLEMENTING treaties under Article 253 — and when it does, it may legislate on a STATE LIST subject.** ⭐ **So an international agreement can move a subject out of the states' hands without any constitutional amendment.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ Article 51 — the directive

⭐ **Article 51, a DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLE, requires the State to endeavour to:**
> ⭐ **(a) promote INTERNATIONAL PEACE AND SECURITY;**
> ⭐ **(b) maintain JUST AND HONOURABLE RELATIONS between nations;**
> ⭐ **(c) foster respect for INTERNATIONAL LAW and TREATY OBLIGATIONS in the dealings of organised peoples with one another; and**
> ⭐ **(d) encourage settlement of international disputes by ARBITRATION.**

> ⭐ **Two things to note.** ⭐ **First, this is the ONLY DPSP that looks outward** — every other one concerns domestic policy *(see [Ch 8](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/08_DPSP.md))*. ⭐ **Second, note the word "ARBITRATION" in (d)** — ⚠️ **that precise word has been tested; it is arbitration, not negotiation, mediation or adjudication.** ⭐ *[Prelims 2014 Q3](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2014.md) asked on this.*
>
> ⭐ **Article 51 is also the constitutional hook the Supreme Court uses to read INTERNATIONAL CONVENTIONS into Indian law where domestic law is silent** — most famously in the sexual-harassment guidelines drawn from CEDAW, and in environmental cases importing the **precautionary** and **polluter pays** principles.

---

## PART B — ⭐⭐ Article 253 — the treaty-implementation power, and the federal problem

> ⭐⭐ **"Notwithstanding anything in the foregoing provisions of this Chapter, Parliament has power to make any law for the whole or any part of the territory of India for implementing any TREATY, AGREEMENT or CONVENTION with any other country or countries, or any DECISION made at any international conference, association or other body."**

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The federal consequence** | ⭐ **"Notwithstanding anything in the foregoing provisions" means the SEVENTH SCHEDULE is overridden.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Parliament may legislate on a STATE LIST subject to implement an international obligation** |
| ⭐ **Illustrations** | ⭐ **Environmental legislation** *(the Air Act, 1981 and the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, enacted to implement the Stockholm Conference decisions)* — ⚠️ **although "public health and sanitation" is a State List subject** |
| ⭐ **The check** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Almost none.** ⭐ **No state consent is required, no ratification threshold applies, and no Article 368 amendment is needed** |
| ⭐ **The comparison** | ⭐ **Contrast Article 249** *(a Rajya Sabha resolution by two-thirds present and voting, valid for one year)* **and Article 252** *(two or more state legislatures request it)* — ⭐ **both have safeguards. Article 253 has none** *(see [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md))* |

⭐ **The treaty-making power itself:**
- ⭐ **Union List entries 10–21** cover **foreign affairs, diplomatic and consular representation, the United Nations, participation in international conferences, entering into and implementing treaties, war and peace, foreign jurisdiction, citizenship, extradition, passports and visas, and piracies and crimes on the high seas**;
- ⭐ **Article 73** — the **executive power of the Union** extends to all matters on which Parliament may legislate, and to the exercise of rights and jurisdiction under any **treaty or agreement**;
- ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Consequence: the UNION EXECUTIVE alone concludes a treaty. NO PARLIAMENTARY RATIFICATION is constitutionally required**, unlike the United States Senate's two-thirds requirement.

> ⭐⭐ **The reform point worth making in any answer:** ⚠️ ⭐ **India's Parliament has no role in the making of a treaty and no choice about implementing one, since Article 253 lets the executive create a binding obligation and then have it legislated into law.** ⭐ **The standing proposal — from the NCRWC and from parliamentary committees — is a TREATY-SCRUTINY MECHANISM: mandatory tabling of treaties in Parliament, and reference of significant agreements to a standing committee before signature.** ⭐ **A parallel demand exists for consultation with the states where a treaty affects a State List subject** — trade agreements affecting agriculture being the obvious case.

## PART C — ⭐ The principles, in brief

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **PANCHSHEEL** *(1954)* | ⭐ **Mutual respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty · mutual non-aggression · mutual non-interference in internal affairs · equality and mutual benefit · peaceful coexistence** |
| ⭐ **NON-ALIGNMENT** | ⭐ **Bandung Conference, 1955; the Non-Aligned Movement founded at Belgrade, 1961** — independence of judgement rather than neutrality |
| ⭐ **Anti-colonialism and anti-racialism** | Support for decolonisation and the campaign against apartheid |
| ⭐ **Support for the UN, and nuclear disarmament** | Peaceful settlement of disputes, consistent with Article 51 |
| ⭐ **The modern vocabulary** | ⭐ **Strategic autonomy · multi-alignment · Neighbourhood First · Act East · SAGAR · the Indo-Pacific** |

⭐ **The determinants** — geography, historical tradition, economic needs, domestic politics, military capability, leadership, and the international environment.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The two articles
> ⭐⭐ **Article 51 — the only OUTWARD-LOOKING DPSP: peace and security · just and honourable relations · respect for international law and treaty obligations · settlement by ⭐ ARBITRATION.**
> ⭐⭐ **Article 253 — Parliament may legislate to implement a treaty, ⚠️ EVEN ON A STATE LIST SUBJECT, with no state consent required.**

### The gap
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The executive MAKES treaties *(Article 73 + Union List 10–21)*; Parliament only IMPLEMENTS them *(Article 253)*.** ⭐ **No ratification requirement exists.**

### The five
> ⭐ **PANCHSHEEL — territorial integrity and sovereignty · non-aggression · non-interference · equality and mutual benefit · peaceful coexistence.**

## ❓ Expected Mains question

**1. "Article 253 makes international obligation a route around Indian federalism." Examine. (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The provision** — Parliament may make any law to implement a **treaty, agreement, convention or a decision of an international body**, ⭐ **notwithstanding the distribution of legislative powers**. ⭐ **The consequence** — a **State List** subject may be legislated upon by Parliament without a state's consent, without a Rajya Sabha resolution *(contrast **Article 249**)*, without a state request *(contrast **Article 252**)*, and without an amendment. ⭐ **Illustrate** with **environmental legislation implementing the Stockholm decisions**, and with trade and investment agreements touching **agriculture, land and health**. ⚠️ **The safeguard problem** — ⭐ **the executive alone concludes the treaty, so the obligation that triggers Article 253 is itself created without parliamentary or state involvement.** ⭐ **Reform:** ⭐ **mandatory tabling of treaties in Parliament, pre-signature scrutiny by a standing committee, and consultation with the states — plausibly through the Inter-State Council under Article 263 — where a State List subject is affected.** ⭐ **Conclude:** Article 253 is necessary *(India must be able to honour its commitments)* and unbalanced *(nothing constrains how those commitments are made)*.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐⭐ **Article 51 *(DPSP)* — promote international peace and security · maintain just and honourable relations · foster respect for international law and treaty obligations · encourage settlement of disputes by ⭐ ARBITRATION.** ⭐ **The only outward-looking DPSP; also the hook for reading international conventions into Indian law.**
- ⭐⭐ **Article 253 — Parliament may make any law to implement a treaty, agreement, convention or decision of an international body, ⚠️ NOTWITHSTANDING the Seventh Schedule — so a STATE LIST subject may be legislated on, with no state consent.**
- ⭐ **Union List entries 10–21** — foreign affairs, diplomatic representation, the UN, treaties, war and peace, extradition, passports, piracies. ⭐ **Article 73 — the executive power extends to treaty rights and obligations.** ⚠️ ⭐ **The executive alone makes treaties; no parliamentary ratification is required.**
- ⭐ **PANCHSHEEL (1954): territorial integrity and sovereignty · non-aggression · non-interference · equality and mutual benefit · peaceful coexistence.** ⭐ **NON-ALIGNMENT — Bandung 1955, NAM at Belgrade 1961.** ⭐ **Today: strategic autonomy, multi-alignment, Neighbourhood First, Act East, SAGAR, Indo-Pacific.**
- ⭐ **Reform: a treaty-scrutiny mechanism — tabling in Parliament, standing-committee scrutiny before signature, and consultation with the states.**
