# Chapter 73 — Voting Behaviour

[← Chapter 72](72_Electoral_Reforms.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 74 →](74_Coalition_Government.md)

**Read time ~6 min** · **No article — political sociology** · 🟡 Prelims: rare · Mains: as supporting material in elections and society questions

> 📌 ⭐ **This chapter supplies EVIDENCE, not facts.** ⚠️ **It will rarely be a question on its own — but a paragraph of it turns a generic answer on elections, criminalisation, women's representation or federalism into an argued one.** ⭐ **Learn the four trends in Part B; they are the ones worth citing.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐ **"Psephology" — from the Greek *psephos*, the pebble dropped in an urn to vote — is the study of why people vote as they do.** ⭐⭐ **The single most important finding about the Indian voter is that the standard Western pattern is inverted: in India, the POOR, the RURAL and the historically MARGINALISED vote MORE than the rich and the urban.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ The determinants

| Determinant | How it works in India |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **CASTE** | ⚠️ **The most cited and the most misunderstood.** ⭐ **Caste rarely operates as a single bloc voting for one party; it operates as a SOCIAL COALITION that parties assemble** — and the coalitions shift election to election. ⭐ **Parties select candidates by the caste arithmetic of the constituency, which is where caste bites hardest** |
| ⭐ **RELIGION** | Both as identity and as a **security concern** for minorities; ⚠️ ⭐ **an appeal on the ground of religion is a CORRUPT PRACTICE** under Section 123(3) — see ***Abhiram Singh* (2017)** in [Ch 71](71_Election_Laws.md) |
| ⭐ **LANGUAGE and REGION** | The base of regional parties *(see [Ch 69](69_Regional_Parties.md))*; strongest where a linguistic identity was politicised early |
| ⭐ **PERSONALITY and CHARISMA** | ⭐ **Rising** — the "presidentialisation" of parliamentary campaigns, where a national leader's appeal transfers to local candidates |
| ⭐ **PERFORMANCE and INCUMBENCY** | ⭐ **Anti-incumbency was the dominant pattern from the 1980s to the 2000s; since the 2010s, PRO-incumbency has been common where welfare delivery is visible** |
| ⭐ **WELFARE DELIVERY** | ⭐ **Direct benefit transfer, housing, gas connections, toilets and food entitlements have created identifiable "beneficiary" constituencies that cut across caste** — the most significant new determinant of the last decade |
| ⚠️ **MONEY and "freebies"** | Cash, liquor and goods distribution; ⚠️ **the "freebies" debate has reached the Supreme Court, which has asked whether pre-election promises of free goods distort the level playing field** |
| ⭐ **MEDIA and SOCIAL MEDIA** | Agenda-setting, targeted messaging, ⚠️ **paid news and misinformation** — the reason the ECI extended the **48-hour silence period** to social media in 2019 |
| **Kinship, local issues and candidate accessibility** | Decisive in Assembly and local elections, far less so nationally |

---

## PART B — ⭐⭐ The four trends worth citing

### ⭐ 1. The participatory inversion

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **In most democracies turnout rises with income and education. In India it falls.** ⭐ **Rural turnout exceeds urban; SC, ST and OBC turnout exceeds that of upper castes; poorer states often out-vote richer ones.**
>
> ⭐ **Yogendra Yadav's framing is the standard one:** the ⭐ **FIRST democratic upsurge** in the **1960s–70s** brought the rural poor into the electorate in numbers; the ⭐ **SECOND upsurge** in the **1990s**, around the **Mandal moment**, brought OBC and Dalit assertion into party politics. ⭐ **Together they made India's electorate the most participatory among large democracies.**

### ⭐⭐ 2. Women voters have overtaken men

⭐ **Female turnout, historically far below male turnout, closed the gap through the 1990s and has EXCEEDED male turnout in recent general elections and in many state elections.**

> ⭐ **Why this matters for your answers:** ⭐ **it is the strongest available evidence that reservation of seats is not needed to bring women to the polls — the gap is in CANDIDATURE and OFFICE, not in participation.** ⚠️ **Use it in any answer on the 106th Amendment** *(see [Ch 67](67_Special_Provisions_Certain_Classes.md))*: ⭐ **women vote in higher proportions than men and hold a small fraction of the seats — which is precisely the mismatch the reservation addresses.**

### ⭐ 3. Split-ticket voting

⭐ **The same voter increasingly chooses a different party for the Lok Sabha and for the Assembly, often within months.**

> ⭐ **This is the single best evidence that the Indian voter is DISCERNING rather than tribal** — ⚠️ **and it is a serious argument AGAINST simultaneous elections**, since holding both polls together suppresses precisely this differentiation *(see [Ch 70](70_Elections.md))*.

### ⚠️ 4. Criminality does not deter voters

⚠️ ⭐ **Candidates with declared criminal cases have a HIGHER win rate than those without.** ⭐ **This is the finding that defeats the "disclosure will solve it" argument in [Ch 72](72_Electoral_Reforms.md)** — voters weigh **winnability, local service and identity** above a pending case, particularly where the case is believed to be politically motivated.

---

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The inversion
> ⭐⭐ **In India, turnout FALLS with income and education — the opposite of the Western pattern.**

### The two upsurges
> ⭐ **FIRST — 1960s–70s, the rural poor enter the electorate.** ⭐ **SECOND — 1990s, the Mandal moment, OBC and Dalit assertion.**

### The reversal
> ⭐⭐ **WOMEN'S turnout now EXCEEDS men's** — while women hold a small fraction of the seats.

### The two findings that decide arguments
> ⭐ **SPLIT-TICKET voting → the voter distinguishes national from state → an argument against simultaneous elections.**
> ⚠️ ⭐ **Candidates with criminal cases WIN MORE OFTEN → disclosure alone will not decriminalise politics.**

**A standalone question, if it comes:**

**1. "What determines how Indians vote, and has it changed?" (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The classical account** — caste, religion, region, language, kinship and candidate accessibility. ⭐ **What has changed** — ⭐ **welfare delivery has created cross-caste beneficiary constituencies**; ⭐ **leader-centred national campaigns transfer appeal to local candidates**; ⭐ **anti-incumbency has weakened where delivery is visible**; ⭐ **women vote in higher proportions than men**; ⭐ **split-ticket voting shows differentiated judgment**; and **social media has changed how information reaches the voter**. ⚠️ **What has not changed** — **caste still governs candidate SELECTION even where it no longer governs voting**, and **money remains decisive at the constituency level**. ⭐ **Conclude:** the Indian voter is better described as **transactional and discerning** than as **captive to identity** — the identity story survives mainly at the point where parties choose whom to put on the ballot.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Psephology** — from the Greek ***psephos*** *(the voting pebble)*.
- ⭐ **Determinants: caste · religion · language and region · personality · performance and incumbency · ⭐ WELFARE DELIVERY · money and freebies · media and social media · kinship and local issues.**
- ⭐⭐ **The inversion — turnout in India falls with income and education**; rural above urban, SC/ST/OBC above upper castes.
- ⭐ **The FIRST democratic upsurge (1960s–70s); the SECOND (1990s, the Mandal moment).**
- ⭐⭐ **Women's turnout now EXCEEDS men's** — so the representation gap is in **candidature**, not participation.
- ⭐ **SPLIT-TICKET voting** — different choices for the Lok Sabha and the Assembly — evidence of a discerning electorate, and an argument against **simultaneous elections**.
- ⚠️ ⭐ **Candidates with declared criminal cases have a HIGHER win rate** — which is why disclosure alone has not decriminalised politics.
- ⭐ **Caste's real bite today is at CANDIDATE SELECTION rather than at the ballot.**
