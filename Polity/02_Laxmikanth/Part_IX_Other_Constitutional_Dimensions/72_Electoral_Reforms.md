# Chapter 72 — Electoral Reforms

[← Chapter 71](71_Election_Laws.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 73 →](73_Voting_Behaviour.md)

**Read time ~14 min** · **No article — committee reports, judgments and pending Bills** · 🔴 ⚠️ **Mains: 2000, 2017, 2018, 2020, 2024, 2025 — the most frequently asked topic in Part IX**

> 📌 ⭐ **This chapter is a TOOLKIT, not a narrative.** ⚠️ **Almost every Mains question in the elections cluster can be answered by pulling the right items out of Parts B and C below.** ⭐ **Learn it as four problems — CRIMINALISATION, MONEY, PARTIES, INSTITUTIONS — each with a committee, a judgment and a pending proposal attached.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **India solved the hardest problem in electoral administration — conducting a free, credible poll for a billion people — and has not solved the easier ones.** ⚠️ **Who may stand, who pays for the campaign, and how parties choose candidates remain almost as unregulated as in 1951.** ⭐ **Every reform proposal in this chapter attacks one of those three.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ The committees, in one table

| Committee / Report | Year | ⭐ **What it is remembered for** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Tarkunde Committee** | 1975 | The first comprehensive reform blueprint; ⭐ **proposed an ECI appointed on the advice of the PM, the Leader of Opposition and the CJI** — the formula the Supreme Court adopted in **2023** |
| ⭐⭐ **Dinesh Goswami Committee** | 1990 | ⭐ **The most influential report.** Multi-member ECI; state funding in kind; curbing booth capturing; ⭐ **equal removal protection for all Election Commissioners** |
| ⭐⭐ **Vohra Committee** | 1993 | ⭐ **Documented the CRIMINAL–POLITICIAN–BUREAUCRAT NEXUS.** The founding document of the criminalisation debate |
| ⭐ **Indrajit Gupta Committee** | 1998 | ⭐ **STATE FUNDING of elections — but PARTIAL and IN KIND, and only to recognised parties** and their candidates |
| ⭐ **Law Commission, 170th Report** | 1999 | ⭐ **Proposed a mixed system — 25% additional seats by LIST PROPORTIONAL REPRESENTATION**; ⭐ **"negative voting"** *(later implemented as **NOTA**)*; internal party democracy; a bar on contesting from more than one seat |
| ⭐ **NCRWC** | 2002 | Consolidated the agenda; recommended disqualification on the framing of charges *(see [Ch 79](../Part_X_Working_of_the_Constitution/79_NCRWC.md))* |
| ⭐ **2nd ARC, 4th Report — *Ethics in Governance*** | 2007 | ⭐ **Disqualification on the FRAMING OF CHARGES; partial state funding; a bar on government advertising before polls** |
| ⭐⭐ **Law Commission, 244th Report** | 2014 | ⭐ **Electoral disqualifications — the definitive statement of the "framing of charges" proposal**, with its safeguards |
| ⭐⭐ **Law Commission, 255th Report** | 2015 | ⭐ **The most cited modern report.** ⭐ **Statutory backing for the MCC · a cap on party expenditure · disclosure of donations · regulation of opinion polls · a Registration of Political Parties Act with COMPULSORY INTERNAL DEMOCRACY** · ⚠️ **it OPPOSED full state funding until those come first** |
| ⭐ **ECI's own proposals** | 1998, 2004, **2016** | ⭐ **The set [Mains 2017 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2017.md) asked about** *(Part C)* |

## PART B — ⭐ Reforms already made *(you must be able to list these)*

| Reform | Year |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Voting age lowered from 21 to 18** | ⭐ **61st Amendment Act, 1988** |
| ⭐ **Electronic Voting Machines** universally | ⭐ **2004** |
| ⭐ **Contesting from more than TWO constituencies barred** | ⭐ **1996** *(Section 33(7))* |
| ⭐⭐ **Mandatory affidavit on CRIMINAL CASES, ASSETS, LIABILITIES and EDUCATION** | ⭐ **2003, following *Union of India v ADR* (2002)** — ⚠️ **Parliament first tried to undo it by amending Section 33B, and that was struck down in *PUCL* (2003)** |
| ⭐ **OPEN BALLOT in Rajya Sabha elections** | ⭐ **2003** — to curb cross-voting |
| ⭐ **Domicile requirement for Rajya Sabha candidates removed** | ⭐ **2003** — upheld in ***Kuldip Nayar v Union of India* (2006)** |
| ⭐ **Prohibition of EXIT POLLS** during the polling period | ⭐ **2009** *(Section 126A)* |
| ⭐ **Ban on the sale of liquor for 48 hours before the close of poll**; the 48-hour silence period | Section 126, 135C |
| ⭐⭐ **NOTA** | ⭐ **2013, after *PUCL v Union of India*** |
| ⭐⭐ **Immediate disqualification on conviction** | ⭐ **2013, *Lily Thomas*** — Section 8(4) struck down |
| ⭐ **VVPAT with mandatory verification in 5 booths per Assembly segment** | ⭐ **2013 → universal 2019 → reaffirmed 2024** |
| ⭐ **Parties must PUBLISH the criminal antecedents of their candidates and the REASONS for selecting them**, in newspapers and on social media | ⭐ ***Rambabu Singh Thakur v Sunil Arora* (2020)** |
| ⭐ **Special courts for cases against MPs and MLAs** | ⭐ **Directed by the Supreme Court from 2017, monitored since** |
| ⭐ **Aadhaar–voter ID linking *(voluntary)* · FOUR qualifying dates for enrolment · gender-neutral "spouse" for service voters** | ⭐ **Election Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021** |
| ⭐⭐ **Electoral bonds STRUCK DOWN** | ⭐ ***ADR v Union of India* (February 2024)**, five judges — Article 19(1)(a) |

> ⭐⭐ **Notice the pattern in that table and state it in an answer:** ⚠️ ⭐ **almost every major reform since 2000 came from the SUPREME COURT or the ELECTION COMMISSION, not from Parliament.** ⭐ **Affidavits, NOTA, immediate disqualification, VVPAT verification, publication of criminal antecedents and the end of electoral bonds are all judicial or ECI achievements.** ⭐ **The reforms Parliament has itself enacted are, with the honourable exception of the voting age, largely administrative.**

## PART C — ⭐⭐ The four unsolved problems

### ⭐⭐ Problem 1 — CRIMINALISATION

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The scale** | ⚠️ ⭐ **The proportion of Lok Sabha members with declared criminal cases has risen at EVERY general election since 2004 — roughly a quarter then, and now approaching half**, with the share facing **serious** charges rising too. ⚠️ **Verify the latest figures from the ADR analysis before quoting a number** |
| ⭐ **The present rule** | ⭐ **Disqualification only on CONVICTION** *(Section 8, and *Lily Thomas* making it immediate)*. ⚠️ **Trials take a decade; a member can serve several full terms while under charge** |
| ⭐⭐ **The proposal** | ⭐ **Disqualification on the FRAMING OF CHARGES** for offences punishable with **five years or more**, ⭐ **provided the charges were framed at least ONE YEAR before the election** and ⭐ **the case is not one filed within a year of the poll** — ⭐ **the Law Commission's 244th Report design, endorsed by the 2nd ARC and the ECI** |
| ⚠️ **The objection** | ⭐ **The presumption of innocence**; ⚠️ **the risk that politically motivated FIRs become a tool to disqualify opponents**; and the fact that **framing of charges is a low judicial threshold** |
| ⭐ **The middle path** | ⭐ **Time-bound special courts** *(already directed)*; ⭐ **the *Rambabu Singh Thakur* publication requirement** working through the voter rather than the law; ⭐ **and a narrow framing-of-charges rule with the safeguards above** |

> ⭐ **The sharpest point available:** ⚠️ ⭐ **the *Rambabu Singh Thakur* remedy assumes voters will punish criminality once informed. The evidence is that candidates with criminal cases have a HIGHER win rate than those without.** ⭐ **So disclosure alone has not worked — which is the strongest argument for a legal disqualification rule, and worth stating plainly.**

### ⭐⭐ Problem 2 — MONEY

| The defect | The proposal |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ ⭐ **The expenditure ceiling binds the CANDIDATE only; a party's general-propaganda spending is unlimited** | ⭐ **A statutory ceiling on PARTY expenditure** *(255th Law Commission Report)* |
| ⚠️ ⭐ **A recognised party's 40 STAR CAMPAIGNERS' travel costs are excluded from every candidate's account** | ⭐ Tighten or cap the exclusion |
| ⚠️ ⭐ **After the 2024 judgment struck down electoral bonds, there is no transparent replacement** | ⭐ **A National Election Fund · restored caps on corporate donations · full disclosure above a low threshold · digital-only donations** |
| ⚠️ **Anonymous cash donations up to ₹2,000, with no limit on the number of such donations** | ⭐ **Aggregate cap per donor** |
| ⚠️ **"Paid news" and surrogate advertising** | ⭐ **Make paid news an ELECTORAL OFFENCE** *(the ECI's standing demand)* |
| ⚠️ **The incumbency advantage of government advertising** | ⭐ **A bar on government advertisements featuring political leaders before polls** — ***Common Cause* (2015)** |
| ⭐ **State funding** | ⭐ **Indrajit Gupta (1998): PARTIAL and IN KIND, to recognised parties only.** ⚠️ ⭐ **The 255th Report opposed it until inner-party democracy and expenditure transparency exist — otherwise public money subsidises opaque organisations** |

### ⭐⭐ Problem 3 — PARTIES

> ⭐ **A statutory REGISTRATION OF POLITICAL PARTIES ACT** requiring ⭐ **compulsory internal elections, published accounts, membership registers and transparent candidate-selection criteria** *(255th Report)*;
> ⭐ **The power to DE-REGISTER a party** — ⚠️ the ECI has asked for it since 1998 and does not have it *(see [Ch 41](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/41_Election_Commission.md))*;
> ⭐ **Bringing parties within the RTI Act** — ⚠️ the CIC so ruled in 2013 and parties have not complied;
> ⭐ **Reform of the Tenth Schedule** so that anti-defection discipline does not extinguish the individual legislator's judgment *(see [Ch 75](75_Anti_Defection_Law.md))*.

### ⭐⭐ Problem 4 — INSTITUTIONS AND THE SYSTEM

| Proposal | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **ECI appointments** | ⭐ **A broad-based collegium including the CJI**, as ***Anoop Baranwal* (2023)** ordered and the **2023 Act reversed** |
| ⭐ **ECI autonomy** | ⭐ **Expenditure CHARGED on the Consolidated Fund · an independent secretariat · equal removal protection for all three Commissioners** *(Goswami, 2nd ARC, 255th Report)* |
| ⭐ **Statutory backing for the MCC** | ⭐ **255th Report** — ⚠️ **the ECI OPPOSES it, because litigation would destroy the code's only advantage, its speed** |
| ⭐ **TOTALISER machines** | ⭐ **Mixing votes from several booths before counting**, so that booth-level voting patterns cannot be used to identify and punish communities. ⚠️ **The ECI has proposed it repeatedly; not accepted** |
| ⭐ **Electoral system change** | ⭐ **The 170th Report's mixed system — 25% additional seats by LIST PR**; and the proposal that a winner must poll ⭐ **50% + 1** *(a run-off or preferential system)*, since FPTP regularly elects members on a third of the vote |
| ⭐ **Simultaneous elections** | ⭐ **The Kovind Committee (2024)** and the pending Constitution Amendment Bills *(see [Ch 70](70_Elections.md))* |
| ⚠️ **RIGHT TO RECALL** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Rejected by the ECI and the Law Commission as unworkable** — it would produce perpetual instability, be captured by money power, and is impractical in constituencies of over a million voters |
| ⭐ **Opinion polls** | ⭐ **The ECI has proposed a bar from a specified date before polling.** ⚠️ **Not accepted — the free-speech objection is strong** |

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The committee ladder
> ⭐ **TARKUNDE 1975 · GOSWAMI 1990 · VOHRA 1993 *(the criminal nexus)* · INDRAJIT GUPTA 1998 *(state funding, partial and in kind)* · Law Commission 170th 1999 · NCRWC 2002 · 2nd ARC 2007 · Law Commission 244th 2014 *(framing of charges)* · Law Commission 255th 2015 *(the modern master list)*.**

### The four judgments
> ⭐ ***ADR* (2002) + *PUCL* (2003)** — the affidavit, and Article 19(1)(a). ⭐ ***PUCL* (2013)** — NOTA. ⭐ ***Lily Thomas* (2013)** — immediate disqualification. ⭐ ***ADR* (2024)** — electoral bonds struck down.

### The framing-of-charges formula
> ⭐ **Offences punishable with 5 YEARS or more · charges framed at least ONE YEAR before the election · excluding cases filed within a year of the poll.**

### The money loophole
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The ceiling binds the CANDIDATE, not the PARTY. And 40 star campaigners' travel is outside it.**

### The state-funding position
> ⭐ **Indrajit Gupta: YES, but partial and in kind.** ⚠️ ⭐ **255th Law Commission: NOT until internal party democracy and expenditure transparency exist first.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "To enhance the quality of democracy in India the Election Commission of India has proposed electoral reforms in 2016. What are the suggested reforms and how far are they significant to make democracy successful?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2017 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2017.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The ECI's proposals** — ⭐ **statutory backing for the MCC** · ⭐ **the power to DE-REGISTER parties** · ⭐ **disqualification on the FRAMING OF CHARGES** for serious offences · ⭐ **making PAID NEWS an electoral offence** · ⭐ **a ban on anonymous donations above a low threshold and full disclosure** · ⭐ **a bar on government advertising with political content before polls** · ⭐ **TOTALISER machines** · ⭐ **restricting a candidate to ONE constituency** · ⭐ **a cap on party expenditure** · ⭐ **charged expenditure and an independent secretariat for the ECI**. ⭐ **Significance, grouped by problem:** criminalisation *(framing of charges, special courts)*; money *(paid news, disclosure, party ceiling)*; party opacity *(de-registration, disclosure)*; institutional autonomy *(charged expenditure, secretariat, appointments)*; and voter protection *(totaliser)*. ⚠️ ⭐ **The critical finding to end on: almost every one of these requires LEGISLATION Parliament has not passed** — and the reforms that HAVE happened since 2000 came from the **Supreme Court and the ECI's own powers under Article 324**, not from Parliament. ⭐ **That asymmetry is itself the answer to "how far are they significant."**

**2. "Examine the need for electoral reforms as suggested by various committees, with particular reference to 'one nation – one election'." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2024 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md). ⭐ **Skeleton in [Ch 70](70_Elections.md)** — build the "need" half from Part C here and the "one nation, one election" half from Chapter 70's Part E.*

**3. "Criminalisation of politics cannot be cured by disclosure alone." Critically examine, with reference to the proposal to disqualify candidates on the framing of charges. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **The scale** — the rising proportion of MPs with declared criminal cases at every election since 2004, and the higher win rate of candidates facing charges. (ii) ⭐ **What disclosure achieved** — ***ADR* (2002)** and ***PUCL* (2003)** made the **affidavit** mandatory and grounded it in **Article 19(1)(a)**; ***Rambabu Singh Thakur* (2020)** required parties to **publish antecedents and reasons for selection**. ⚠️ ⭐ **Why it has not sufficed** — voters weigh winnability, caste and local service above criminal record; parties select on winnability; and information without an alternative candidate changes nothing. (iii) ⭐ **The proposal** — the **244th Law Commission Report** formula, with its three safeguards. (iv) ⚠️ **The objections** — the **presumption of innocence**; the risk of **politically motivated prosecutions**; a **low judicial threshold** at the charge-framing stage; and the danger of **executive agencies deciding who may contest** *(a concern sharpened by the debates in [Ch 59](../Part_VIII_Non_Constitutional_Bodies/59_CBI.md))*. (v) ⭐ **The balanced conclusion:** ⭐ **the safeguards in the 244th Report — five-year offences, charges framed a year in advance, exclusion of freshly filed cases — meet most of the objections**; and the complementary reform is ⭐ **genuinely time-bound special courts**, because the real defect is that a trial outlasts a career. ⭐ **Disqualification without speedy trials would be unjust; speedy trials without disqualification have proved insufficient. Both are needed.**

**4. "Identify the major electoral reforms which are necessary in the Indian political system." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2000 Q8(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2000.md); answer it as a structured version of Part C.* ⭐ **Organise under the four heads — criminalisation, money, parties, institutions — attaching one committee and one judgment to each, and close with the observation that reform has come predominantly from the judiciary and the ECI rather than the legislature.**

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Committees: TARKUNDE (1975) · GOSWAMI (1990) · VOHRA (1993, the criminal–politician–bureaucrat nexus) · INDRAJIT GUPTA (1998, partial state funding in kind) · Law Commission 170th (1999, 25% list PR + negative voting) · NCRWC (2002) · 2nd ARC 4th Report (2007) · Law Commission 244th (2014, framing of charges) · Law Commission 255th (2015, the master list).**
- ⭐ **Done:** voting age 18 *(61st Amendment, 1988)* · EVMs *(2004)* · max **two** constituencies *(1996)* · ⭐ **affidavits on criminal cases, assets and education *(2003, after ADR 2002 and PUCL 2003)*** · **open ballot for the Rajya Sabha and removal of the domicile requirement *(2003)*** · **exit polls barred *(2009)*** · ⭐ **NOTA *(2013)*** · ⭐ ***Lily Thomas* (2013)*** · **VVPAT with 5-booth verification** · ⭐ ***Rambabu Singh Thakur* (2020)*** publication of antecedents · **Election Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021** · ⭐ **electoral bonds struck down *(ADR, 2024)*.**
- ⭐⭐ **Pending — CRIMINALISATION:** disqualification on the **framing of charges** *(5-year offences, charges framed 1 year before the poll, excluding freshly filed cases)*; **time-bound special courts**.
- ⭐⭐ **Pending — MONEY:** a ceiling on **PARTY** expenditure *(the candidate-only ceiling is the loophole)*; a replacement funding regime after 2024; caps on corporate and anonymous donations; **paid news** as an electoral offence; a bar on **government advertising** *(Common Cause, 2015)*; ⭐ **state funding only partially and in kind *(Indrajit Gupta)*, and the 255th Report's precondition of inner-party democracy first.**
- ⭐⭐ **Pending — PARTIES:** a **Registration of Political Parties Act** with compulsory internal democracy; **de-registration** powers; **RTI coverage**; Tenth Schedule reform.
- ⭐⭐ **Pending — INSTITUTIONS:** ECI appointments by a broad collegium; **charged expenditure and an independent secretariat**; equal removal protection for all three Commissioners; **statutory MCC** *(ECI opposes)*; **TOTALISERS**; **list PR / 50%+1**; simultaneous elections. ⚠️ ⭐ **RIGHT TO RECALL — rejected as unworkable.**
- ⭐⭐ **The finding to close any answer with: since 2000, reform has come from the SUPREME COURT and the ECI, not from Parliament.**
