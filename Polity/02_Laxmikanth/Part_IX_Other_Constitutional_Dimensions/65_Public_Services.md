# Chapter 65 — Public Services

[← Chapter 64](64_Official_Language.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 66 →](66_Rights_and_Liabilities_of_the_Government.md)

**Read time ~12 min** · **Part XIV, Articles 308–314** · 🟠 ⚠️ **Mains: 2013, 2014, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2021, 2024 — SEVEN questions since 2013**

> 📌 ⚠️ ⭐ **This chapter is rated 🟠 and is the most under-rated in the book after [Ch 37](../Part_V_Local_Government/37_Panchayati_Raj.md).** ⭐ **Seven Mains questions in twelve years — more than the President, the Supreme Court or the Finance Commission.** ⭐ **The reason: the "governance" half of GS-II is largely about the civil service, and this is the only chapter that covers it.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **Article 310 says every civil servant serves at the PLEASURE of the President or Governor. Article 311 then takes most of that pleasure away.** ⚠️ **The tension between those two articles — dismissal at will versus protection through inquiry — is the constitutional core of the chapter.** ⭐ **Everything else is reform debate, and the reform debate is where the marks are.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ The three services

| Type | ⭐ Recruited by | ⭐ Serves | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **ALL-INDIA SERVICES** | ⭐ **The UNION** | ⭐⭐ **BOTH the Union AND the states** | ⭐ **IAS · IPS · Indian Forest Service** *(created 1966)* |
| **Central Services** | The Union | The Union only | IFS, IRS, IA&AS, Indian Railway services |
| **State Services** | The state | The state only | State civil service, state police service |

### ⭐⭐ Article 312 — how an All-India Service is created

> ⭐ **If the RAJYA SABHA declares by resolution, supported by ⭐ NOT LESS THAN TWO-THIRDS OF THE MEMBERS PRESENT AND VOTING, that it is NECESSARY OR EXPEDIENT IN THE NATIONAL INTEREST to do so, PARLIAMENT may by law create one or more All-India Services common to the Union and the states.**

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Why the Rajya Sabha?** | ⭐ **Because an All-India Service intrudes into state administration — so the House OF THE STATES must consent.** ⚠️ **This is one of only two special powers of the Rajya Sabha** *(the other being **Article 249** — see [Ch 22](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md))* |
| ⭐ **The two originals** | ⭐ **The IAS and the IPS are DEEMED to have been created under Article 312** — they pre-date it |
| ⭐ **Article 312(2)** | ⭐ **An ALL-INDIA JUDICIAL SERVICE may be created the same way** — ⚠️ **but not including any post inferior to that of a district judge.** ⚠️ ⭐ **It has never been created** *(see [Ch 35](../Part_IV_State_Government/35_Subordinate_Courts.md))* |

### ⭐⭐ The dual-control feature — the strongest federal argument in this chapter

> ⭐ **An All-India Service officer is recruited and trained by the Union, allotted to a STATE CADRE, works under the STATE government — and can be ⭐ DISMISSED ONLY BY THE UNION.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **A Chief Minister may suspend or transfer an IAS officer; only the CENTRAL GOVERNMENT can remove or dismiss one.** ⭐ **Cadre-controlling authorities: IAS → the DoPT · IPS → the Home Ministry · Indian Forest Service → the Environment Ministry.**

⭐ **Sardar Patel's defence of the AIS in the Constituent Assembly is worth quoting:** he called it the ⭐ **"steel frame"** whose loyalty to the Union would hold the country together, and warned that a service that could be victimised by a state government would cease to speak its mind. ⚠️ **The critique is the mirror image:** the arrangement dilutes state control over its own administration, and ⭐ **serving two masters is precisely why AIS officers are exposed in Centre–state confrontations** — a live issue whenever a state and the Union disagree over an officer's deputation.

## PART B — ⭐⭐ Articles 309, 310, 311

### ⭐ Article 309 — who makes the rules

⭐ **Parliament (for the Union) and the state legislature (for a state) may regulate the RECRUITMENT and CONDITIONS OF SERVICE of persons appointed to public services.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Until such a law is made, the PRESIDENT or the GOVERNOR may make RULES** — and in practice most service conditions still rest on rules, not statute.

### ⭐⭐ Article 310 — the doctrine of pleasure

> ⭐ **A member of the DEFENCE SERVICES, or of a CIVIL SERVICE OF THE UNION, or of an all-India service, or a holder of any post connected with defence or of a civil post under the Union, holds office DURING THE PLEASURE OF THE PRESIDENT.** ⭐ **A member of a state civil service, or holder of a civil post under a state, holds office during the pleasure of the GOVERNOR.**

⚠️ **Two clarifications examiners test:**
1. ⭐ **The Indian doctrine is NOT the English one.** ⭐ **It is expressly "except as expressly provided by this Constitution" — and Article 311 is that express provision.**
2. ⭐ **The pleasure doctrine does not apply to** the ⭐ **Supreme Court and High Court judges, the CAG, the CEC and the Chairman and members of the UPSC** — all of whom have their own removal procedures.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Article 311 — the two safeguards, and the three exceptions

| Safeguard | Text |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **311(1)** | ⭐ **No person shall be DISMISSED OR REMOVED by an authority SUBORDINATE to that by which he was APPOINTED** |
| ⭐⭐ **311(2)** | ⭐ **No person shall be DISMISSED, REMOVED or REDUCED IN RANK except after an INQUIRY in which he has been INFORMED OF THE CHARGES and given a REASONABLE OPPORTUNITY OF BEING HEARD** in respect of those charges |

⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The THREE exceptions to 311(2) — memorise these; they are the most examinable set in the chapter:**

| # | Exception |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **(a)** | ⭐ **Where the person is dismissed, removed or reduced in rank on the ground of CONDUCT WHICH HAS LED TO HIS CONVICTION ON A CRIMINAL CHARGE** |
| ⭐ **(b)** | ⭐ **Where the authority is satisfied that, for some reason TO BE RECORDED BY THAT AUTHORITY IN WRITING, it is NOT REASONABLY PRACTICABLE to hold the inquiry** |
| ⭐ **(c)** | ⭐ **Where the PRESIDENT or the GOVERNOR is satisfied that in the INTEREST OF THE SECURITY OF THE STATE it is not expedient to hold the inquiry** |

> ⚠️ ⭐ **The 42nd Amendment removed the "second opportunity"** — before it, a civil servant had to be heard **twice**: once on the charges and again on the **proposed penalty**. ⭐ **Since 1976 only ONE opportunity survives, at the inquiry stage.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **Who is NOT protected by Article 311:** ⭐ **members of the DEFENCE SERVICES** *(Article 310's pleasure applies to them without 311's protection)*, and employees of ⭐ **local bodies and statutory corporations**, who are governed by their own service rules and contract.

⭐ **Article 314** — the special protection for pre-Constitution **Indian Civil Service** officers — was ⭐ **REPEALED by the 28th Amendment, 1972.**

## PART C — ⭐⭐ Reform — where the seven Mains questions live

### ⭐ The diagnosis, in five lines

1. ⚠️ ⭐ **Generalist dominance in an era of specialised policy** — finance, climate, health, cyber and data governance;
2. ⚠️ ⭐ **Politicisation of transfers and postings** — the single most-cited cause of the loss of neutrality;
3. ⚠️ **Seniority-driven promotion** with weak performance differentiation;
4. ⚠️ **Rule-bound, process-oriented culture** that punishes initiative and rewards caution *(the "why did you decide?" problem, where inaction is always the safer choice)*;
5. ⚠️ **Accountability to the hierarchy, not to the citizen** — the reason **citizens' charters** were invented, and the reason they underperform.

### ⭐⭐ The remedies to name — this is what separates a good answer

| Reform | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ ***T.S.R. Subramanian v Union of India* (2013)** | ⭐ **The Supreme Court directed a MINIMUM FIXED TENURE for civil servants, the creation of CIVIL SERVICES BOARDS to advise on transfers and postings, and held that civil servants should ACT ON WRITTEN INSTRUCTIONS, recording oral directions given by ministers** |
| ⭐⭐ ***Prakash Singh v Union of India* (2006)** | ⭐ **The police-reform directions: a STATE SECURITY COMMISSION; a minimum TWO-YEAR tenure for the DGP, SPs and SHOs; ⭐ SEPARATION OF INVESTIGATION FROM LAW AND ORDER; a POLICE ESTABLISHMENT BOARD for transfers; and a POLICE COMPLAINTS AUTHORITY** |
| ⭐ **2nd ARC's 10th Report** | *"Refurbishing of Personnel Administration"* — competency frameworks, mid-career review, an end to the **guarantee of employment till 60**, and a **Civil Services Authority** |
| ⭐ **Baswan Committee (2016)** | Review of the **civil services examination** — age, attempts, syllabus |
| ⭐ **Mission Karmayogi (2020)** | ⭐ **The National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building** — a shift from **rule-based to ROLE-BASED** human-resource management, with a competency framework and continuous online learning |
| ⭐ **Lateral entry** | ⭐ **Direct recruitment of domain specialists at Joint Secretary and Director level, through the UPSC** — ⚠️ **contested on reservation grounds** |
| ⭐ **Citizens' charters** | ⭐ **The stated service standards of an organisation, with a grievance-redress mechanism** — ⚠️ **their weakness is that they are NOT justiciable**; the **Sevottam** model and a **statutory right to service** in several states are the proposed fix |
| ⭐ **e-governance and DBT** | ⭐ **Reduce discretion at the point of delivery, which is where petty corruption lives** |

> ⭐⭐ **The framing sentence to reuse across all seven questions:** ⚠️ **"The Indian civil service was designed for a REGULATORY state in a scarcity economy and is being asked to run a DEVELOPMENTAL state in a data economy."** ⭐ **Neutrality, permanence and anonymity — the three classical virtues — were built to protect the service from politics; the modern demand is for specialisation, measurable performance and responsiveness, and those pull in a different direction.** ⭐ **The reform question is how to add the second set without destroying the first.**

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The three articles
> ⭐ **309 — RULES *(legislature, or President/Governor until then)*.** ⭐ **310 — PLEASURE.** ⭐ **311 — PROTECTION.**

### The two safeguards
> ⭐⭐ **311(1) — not by a SUBORDINATE authority. 311(2) — not without an INQUIRY and a REASONABLE OPPORTUNITY.**

### The three exceptions
> ⭐⭐ **CONVICTION on a criminal charge · NOT REASONABLY PRACTICABLE *(reasons in writing)* · SECURITY OF THE STATE *(President's or Governor's satisfaction)*.**

### Article 312
> ⭐ **RAJYA SABHA resolution, TWO-THIRDS of members PRESENT AND VOTING, in the NATIONAL INTEREST.** ⭐ **IAS and IPS are deemed created under it.** ⚠️ **The All-India JUDICIAL Service has never been created.**

### The two cases
> ⭐ ***T.S.R. Subramanian* (2013)** — fixed tenure · **Civil Services Boards** · **written instructions**.
> ⭐ ***Prakash Singh* (2006)** — State Security Commission · **2-year tenures** · **separation of investigation from law and order** · Police Establishment Board · Police Complaints Authority.

### The removed protection
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The 42nd Amendment removed the SECOND opportunity to be heard on the proposed penalty.** ⭐ **The 28th Amendment (1972) repealed Article 314's ICS protections.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Initially Civil Services in India were designed to achieve the goals of neutrality and effectiveness, which seems to be lacking in the present context. Do you agree that drastic reforms are required in Civil Services?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2017 Q18](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2017.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **The original design** — Article 311's inquiry protection and Article 312's dual control were meant to make the officer **secure enough to say no**; permanence, anonymity and neutrality as the Weberian package; Patel's **"steel frame."** (ii) ⚠️ **Why neutrality has eroded** — ⭐ **politicisation of transfers and postings**; the absence of fixed tenure; **oral instructions**; **post-retirement appointments**; and the incentive to align with the political executive for empanelment. (iii) ⚠️ **Why effectiveness has eroded** — generalist leadership over specialised subjects; seniority-based promotion; a process culture that penalises decision-making; ⭐ **Article 311's inquiry protections, in practice, making dismissal for non-performance almost impossible**. (iv) ⭐ **The reforms** — ***T.S.R. Subramanian* (2013)** fixed tenure, **Civil Services Boards**, written instructions; ***Prakash Singh* (2006)** for the police; **2nd ARC's** competency framework and mid-career review; ⭐ **Mission Karmayogi**; **lateral entry** through the UPSC; **360-degree appraisal**; and ⭐ **a Public Services Bill / Code of Ethics**. (v) ⭐ **"Drastic" — qualify it.** ⚠️ **Dismantling Article 311's protections would produce a compliant service, not a competent one** — the record of politicisation is the reason the protection exists. ⭐ **The right reform sequence is: secure tenure FIRST, then demand performance.** **Conclude** on that ordering.

**2. "Has the cadre-based Civil Services Organisation been the cause of slow change in India?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2014 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2014.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **Explain the cadre system** — recruitment by the Union, allotment to a **state cadre**, dual control, cadre-controlling ministries, deputation and empanelment. ⭐ **How it slows change** — ⚠️ **generalists rotating every two to three years accumulate breadth and not depth**; **institutional memory sits in files, not people**; **seniority governs promotion**; cadre-strength rigidities create **chronic vacancies in some states and surpluses in others**; and **cadre loyalty can shade into resistance to specialists and to lateral entrants**. ⭐ **How it accelerates change** — a **national administrative spine** in a deeply diverse country; officers who have run a district understand implementation; **dual control gives protection against local capture**; and cadre-based recruitment supplies **uniform standards and reservation-compliant diversity**. ⭐ **The evidence** — the same cadre delivered the **Green Revolution, the elections machinery and Aadhaar**, which suggests the constraint is **incentive design, not the cadre form itself**. ⭐ **Conclude:** the cadre is not the cause; **the absence of tenure security, specialisation and performance differentiation within it is.**

**3. "The Citizens' Charter has been a landmark initiative in ensuring citizen-centric administration. But it is yet to reach its full potential. Identify the factors hindering the realisation of its promise and suggest measures." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2024 Q16](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md); a closely similar question was [Mains 2013 Q16](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **What a citizens' charter is** — a public declaration of an organisation's **service standards, timelines, entitlements and grievance-redress route**, introduced in India from **1997** under the Conference of Chief Ministers' Action Plan. ⚠️ **The hindering factors** — ⭐ **not legally enforceable**, so a breach carries no consequence; **drafted top-down without consulting the citizens or the frontline staff**; **vague, unquantified standards**; ⚠️ **no periodic revision**; **staff unaware of their own charter**; **no grievance-redress capacity behind it**; and **no penalty or compensation** for failure. ⭐ **Measures** — ⭐ **make service delivery a statutory RIGHT** *(as several states have done through Right to Public Services Acts, with time-limits and penalties on the defaulting officer)*; adopt the ⭐ **SEVOTTAM** framework; **consult users while drafting**; **quantify and publish performance against standards**; **integrate with e-governance so breaches are auto-detected**; **independent grievance appeal**; and ⭐ **link charter compliance to the organisation's and the officer's appraisal.** ⭐ **Conclude:** a charter without a remedy is a promise; the reform that works is converting the promise into an **enforceable entitlement.**

**4. "Institutional quality is a crucial driver of economic performance. In this context suggest reforms in Civil Service for strengthening democracy." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2020 Q7](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2020.md).* ⭐ **Use the Part C table**, and organise the reforms under three heads: ⭐ **(i) INDEPENDENCE** *(fixed tenure, Civil Services Boards, written instructions, post-retirement cooling-off)*; ⭐ **(ii) CAPACITY** *(Mission Karmayogi, lateral entry, mid-career specialisation, domain cadres)*; ⭐ **(iii) ACCOUNTABILITY** *(performance appraisal with consequences, citizens' charters made statutory, RTI Section 4 disclosure, social audit)*. ⭐ **Tie each back to economic performance** — contract enforcement, regulatory predictability, and the cost of discretion.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Part XIV, Articles 308–314.** Three services: ⭐ **ALL-INDIA (IAS, IPS, Indian Forest Service — recruited by the Union, serving BOTH levels)**, Central, and State.
- ⭐⭐ **Article 312 — a RAJYA SABHA resolution supported by TWO-THIRDS of members PRESENT AND VOTING, "in the national interest," then Parliament may create an All-India Service.** ⭐ **IAS and IPS are DEEMED created under it.** ⚠️ **The All-India JUDICIAL Service (312(2)) has never been created.**
- ⭐ **Dual control — the STATE deploys, only the UNION dismisses.** Cadre-controlling authorities: **IAS → DoPT · IPS → MHA · IFoS → MoEFCC.**
- ⭐ **Article 309 — recruitment and service conditions by law; the President/Governor may make RULES until then.**
- ⭐⭐ **Article 310 — the PLEASURE doctrine**, subject to what the Constitution expressly provides. ⚠️ **Does not apply to SC/HC judges, the CAG, the CEC or UPSC members.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Article 311 — (1) no dismissal by a SUBORDINATE authority; (2) no dismissal, removal or reduction in rank without an INQUIRY and a REASONABLE OPPORTUNITY.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Three exceptions: CONVICTION on a criminal charge · NOT REASONABLY PRACTICABLE (reasons in writing) · SECURITY OF THE STATE (President's/Governor's satisfaction).** ⚠️ **The 42nd Amendment removed the SECOND opportunity.** ⚠️ **Defence personnel and local-body/corporation employees are outside Article 311.**
- ⭐ **Article 314 (ICS protections) repealed by the 28th Amendment, 1972.**
- ⭐⭐ **Reforms: *T.S.R. Subramanian* (2013)** — fixed tenure, **Civil Services Boards**, **written instructions**; ⭐ ***Prakash Singh* (2006)** — State Security Commission, **2-year tenures**, **separation of investigation from law and order**, Police Establishment Board, Police Complaints Authority; ⭐ **2nd ARC 10th Report**; ⭐ **Baswan Committee (2016)**; ⭐ **Mission Karmayogi (2020)**; ⭐ **lateral entry via the UPSC**; ⭐ **citizens' charters + Sevottam + state Right to Public Services Acts.**
