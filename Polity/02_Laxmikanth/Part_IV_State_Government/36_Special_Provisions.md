# Chapter 36 — Special Provisions for Some States

[← Chapter 35](35_Subordinate_Courts.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md)

**Read time ~10 min** · **Articles 371 to 371-J** · **The last chapter of Part IV** · 🟠 Prelims: recurring · Mains: **2016, 2025**

> 📌 **This chapter is the constitutional answer to a question [Ch 13](../Part_II_System_of_Government/13_Federal_System.md) raised and did not resolve:** if all states are formally equal, how does a single Constitution accommodate Nagaland, Mizoram, Sikkim and Hyderabad-Karnataka? ⭐ **The answer is asymmetric federalism — twelve states with tailored provisions.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **Indian federalism is not symmetric.** Part XXI carries **special provisions for twelve states**, each written for a specific historical settlement — a peace accord, a merger agreement, a regional grievance. ⭐ **They are not exceptions to the constitutional scheme; they are what made a single scheme possible** across territories that would not otherwise have joined it on the same terms.

---

## 📖 The story

A constitution can treat all its units identically only if they arrived on identical terms. **India's did not.**

Some states were **British provinces**. Some were **princely states that acceded** — with agreements attached. **Sikkim** was an independent protectorate that joined in 1975. **Nagaland** came out of an armed insurgency and a **16-point agreement**. **Mizoram** came out of the **1986 Mizo Accord**. Some regions inside states — **Vidarbha, Marathwada, Saurashtra, Kutch, Hyderabad-Karnataka** — had grievances about development that were the price of their merger into larger units.

> 💡 **The analogy:** a **merger of several companies** where some units insisted on written guarantees — that their local practices would survive, that their staff would be protected, that a specific division would get guaranteed investment. **You can call those guarantees exceptions to the merged company's uniform policy. But without them the merger would not have happened**, so they are better understood as **the terms of the merger itself**.
>
> That is precisely how to frame asymmetric federalism in a Mains answer: **not a departure from the constitutional scheme, but a condition of it.**

**And the story is live.** ⭐ **Article 370 was made largely inoperative in August 2019**, and Jammu & Kashmir was **reorganised into two Union Territories** — the single largest change to India's federal map since 1956, and the subject of both Mains questions on this chapter.

## PART A — ⭐ The map: which state gets which article

| Article | State(s) | The core provision |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **371** | **Maharashtra and Gujarat** | The **Governor** has special responsibility to establish **separate development boards** for ⭐ **Vidarbha, Marathwada and the rest of Maharashtra**, and ⭐ **Saurashtra, Kutch and the rest of Gujarat**; equitable allocation of funds and equitable arrangements for technical education, vocational training and employment |
| ⭐⭐ **371A** | **Nagaland** | ⭐ **No Act of Parliament shall apply** to Nagaland in respect of **religious or social practices of the Nagas**, **Naga customary law and procedure**, **administration of civil and criminal justice involving decisions according to Naga customary law**, and **ownership and transfer of land and its resources** — ⚠️ **unless the Legislative Assembly of Nagaland so decides by a resolution**. The Governor has special responsibility for **law and order** while internal disturbance continues, and a **Regional Council for the Tuensang district** |
| **371B** | **Assam** | The President may provide for a **committee of the Legislative Assembly** consisting of members elected from the **tribal areas** |
| **371C** | **Manipur** | The President may provide for a **committee of the Legislative Assembly** consisting of members elected from the **Hill Areas**; the Governor reports annually to the President on their administration |
| ⭐ **371D** | ⭐ **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana** | ⭐ **Equitable opportunities in public employment and education** — the President may require the state to organise **local cadres** and provide for **direct recruitment**; may establish an ⭐ **Administrative Tribunal** outside the High Court's jurisdiction for service disputes |
| **371E** | **Andhra Pradesh** | ⭐ Parliament may provide for the establishment of a **Central University** in the state |
| ⭐⭐ **371F** | **Sikkim** | ⭐ Members of the Legislative Assembly elect the state's **Lok Sabha representative**; ⭐ the Assembly's strength is at least **30**; the Governor has special responsibility for **peace and equitable arrangement for the social and economic advancement of different sections**; ⭐ **all pre-merger laws continue** and **no court has jurisdiction over disputes arising out of any treaty or agreement relating to Sikkim before its merger** |
| **371G** | ⭐ **Mizoram** | ⭐ **Identical in structure to 371A** — no Act of Parliament applies in respect of **religious or social practices of the Mizos**, **Mizo customary law and procedure**, **administration of civil and criminal justice involving Mizo customary law**, and **ownership and transfer of land**, unless the Assembly so resolves. ⭐ **Assembly strength at least 40** |
| **371H** | **Arunachal Pradesh** | ⭐ The **Governor** has special responsibility for **law and order**, exercising his **individual judgement** after consulting the Council of Ministers; ⭐ Assembly strength at least **30** |
| **371I** | **Goa** | ⭐ The Legislative Assembly shall consist of **not less than 30 members** |
| ⭐ **371J** | ⭐ **Karnataka** | ⭐ Inserted by the **98th Amendment Act, 2012** — a separate **development board for the Hyderabad-Karnataka region** *(now Kalyana-Karnataka)*, with **equitable allocation of funds** and **reservation in education and state government jobs** for that region |

> ⚠️ **Note there is no 371 provision for Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Rajasthan, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand or Telangana separately** *(Telangana shares 371D with Andhra Pradesh)*.

### 🧠 How to remember the map

> **Group them by what the provision actually does:**
>
> ⭐ **① Protection of customary law and land** — ⭐ **371A Nagaland** and **371G Mizoram** *(structurally identical — learn them as a pair)*
> ⭐ **② Development boards for backward regions** — **371 Maharashtra/Gujarat** and ⭐ **371J Karnataka** *(also a pair)*
> ⭐ **③ Committees of the Assembly for tribal/hill areas** — **371B Assam**, **371C Manipur**
> ⭐ **④ Employment and education equity** — **371D/E Andhra Pradesh and Telangana**
> ⭐ **⑤ Minimum Assembly size** — **371F Sikkim (30)**, **371G Mizoram (40)**, **371H Arunachal (30)**, **371I Goa (30)**
> ⭐ **⑥ Governor's special responsibility for LAW AND ORDER** — **371A Nagaland**, **371H Arunachal Pradesh**

## PART B — ⭐⭐ Article 370 and Jammu & Kashmir

*This is where both Mains questions on the chapter come from.*

### What Article 370 was

- ⭐ Placed in **Part XXI**, under the marginal note ⭐ **"Temporary provisions with respect to the State of Jammu and Kashmir."**
- Its effects: **Parliament's legislative power** over the state was limited to the **Union and Concurrent List** matters corresponding to the **Instrument of Accession**, and other provisions could be extended only **with the concurrence of the state government**; ⭐ **most of the Constitution applied to J&K only as modified by Presidential orders** under Article 370(1)(d).
- ⭐ **Article 35A**, inserted by a **Presidential Order of 1954**, empowered the state legislature to define **"permanent residents"** and confer special rights on them in **employment, property acquisition, settlement and scholarships**.
- ⭐ **Article 370(3)** permitted the **President to declare it inoperative**, ⚠️ **on the recommendation of the Constituent Assembly of the state** — which had **ceased to exist in 1957**, and which was the central legal difficulty.

> ⭐ ***Mains 2016 Q2 asked exactly this:*** *"To what extent is Article 370, bearing the marginal note 'Temporary provision with respect to the State of Jammu and Kashmir', temporary?"* **The tension in the question is the whole answer** — it is *headed* temporary and sat in the **"Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions"** Part, but the body that could recommend its removal had dissolved, and it had operated for close to seven decades.

### ⭐ What happened in 2019

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **August 2019** | **Presidential Orders** applied the **whole Constitution** to J&K and rendered **Article 370 largely inoperative**; **Article 35A** ceased to have effect |
| ⭐ **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** | ⭐ The state was **reorganised into TWO UNION TERRITORIES**: ⭐ **Jammu & Kashmir — a UT WITH a legislature**, and ⭐ **Ladakh — a UT WITHOUT a legislature** |
| ⚠️ **The Legislative Council** | ⭐ **Abolished** *(see [Ch 33](33_State_Legislature.md))* |
| ⭐ **The Supreme Court** | In ***In re: Article 370* (December 2023)** the Court **upheld** the abrogation, holding that J&K **retained no element of internal sovereignty** after accession and that Article 370 was a **temporary provision**; ⚠️ but it **directed that statehood be restored** as soon as possible and that **elections be held** |

### ⭐ The nature of the J&K Assembly now

*This is the substance of [Mains 2025 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2025.md).*

- ⭐ J&K is a ⭐ **Union Territory WITH a Legislative Assembly** — the same category as **Delhi and Puducherry**, governed by **Article 239A** and the Reorganisation Act.
- ⚠️ ⭐ **Its legislative competence is narrower than a state's:** the Assembly may make laws for the **State List and Concurrent List** ⭐ **EXCEPT "public order" and "police"**, which remain with the **Lieutenant Governor / Centre**.
- ⭐ The **Lieutenant Governor** has substantial powers, including over **All-India Services** and the **Anti-Corruption Bureau**.
- ⭐ The **Chief Minister and Council of Ministers** aid and advise the LG on matters within the Assembly's competence.
- ⚠️ **Restoration of statehood** remains **pending** — the Supreme Court directed it in 2023 without fixing a deadline for statehood *(it did fix one for elections)*.

> ⭐ **The framing that lifts this answer:** J&K's Assembly is **constitutionally weaker than any state Assembly** — it cannot legislate on **police and public order**, the two subjects at the heart of the region's politics. **The reorganisation converted an asymmetry of *privilege* (Article 370) into an asymmetry of *subordination* (UT status)** — and both Mains questions are really asking you to notice that inversion.

## PART C — Related asymmetries worth knowing

⭐ **These are NOT part of Articles 371–371J**, but examiners group them with it:

| Provision | Covers |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Fifth Schedule** *(Article 244(1))* | **Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes** in states **other than** Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram — Governor's report to the President, **Tribes Advisory Council**, power to modify the application of laws ([Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md)) |
| ⭐ **Sixth Schedule** *(Article 244(2))* | ⭐ **Tribal areas in ASSAM, MEGHALAYA, TRIPURA and MIZORAM** — **Autonomous District Councils** with legislative, judicial and financial powers |
| **Article 239A / 239AA** | **Union Territories with legislatures** — Puducherry, and **Delhi** *(the NCT, with its own special scheme and the police/public order/land exclusions)* |

> ⚠️ ⭐ **The Fifth/Sixth Schedule swap is one of the most reliably tested traps in the whole syllabus** — it appeared in [Prelims 2025 Q6](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2025.md) and again in [Prelims 2026 Q3](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2026.md). ⭐ **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram = SIXTH.**

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The pairs
> ⭐ **371A Nagaland ≡ 371G Mizoram** *(customary law, land, no Act of Parliament unless the Assembly resolves)*
> ⭐ **371 Maharashtra/Gujarat ≡ 371J Karnataka** *(development boards for backward regions)*
> ⭐ **371B Assam ≡ 371C Manipur** *(committees of the Assembly for tribal/hill areas)*

### The Governor's law-and-order responsibility
> ⭐ **Only two: 371A NAGALAND and 371H ARUNACHAL PRADESH.**

### The minimum Assembly sizes
> ⭐ **Sikkim 30 · Mizoram 40 · Arunachal 30 · Goa 30.** *(Compare [Ch 33](33_State_Legislature.md): the general minimum is **60**, and these are the constitutional exceptions.)*

### The newest one
> ⭐ **371J — Karnataka, 98th Amendment Act, 2012, Hyderabad-Karnataka (Kalyana-Karnataka).** ⚠️ **The only 371 provision added in this century**, and the one most likely to be missed.

### Article 370
> ⭐ Marginal note: **"Temporary provisions"** · **Article 35A** by a **1954 Presidential Order** *(permanent residents)* · **370(3)** needed the **Constituent Assembly's recommendation**, which had **dissolved in 1957** · ⭐ **August 2019 abrogation + J&K Reorganisation Act** ⇒ ⭐ **J&K = UT WITH legislature; Ladakh = UT WITHOUT** · ⭐ **upheld by the Supreme Court in December 2023**, with a direction to **restore statehood**.

### The J&K Assembly's limit
> ⭐ **It can legislate on the State and Concurrent Lists EXCEPT "public order" and "police."**

### The Schedule trap
> ⭐ **SIXTH Schedule = ASSAM, MEGHALAYA, TRIPURA, MIZORAM.** Everything else tribal is **FIFTH**.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "To what extent is Article 370 of the Indian Constitution, bearing marginal note 'Temporary provision with respect to the State of Jammu and Kashmir', temporary? Discuss the future prospects of this provision in the context of Indian polity." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2016 Q2](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2016.md) — i.e. **before** the 2019 abrogation. Answer it historically **and** bring it up to date.*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The textual case for "temporary"** — the marginal note; its placement in **Part XXI**, headed *"Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions"*; and **Article 370(3)**'s express mechanism for making it inoperative. (ii) **The case that it had become permanent in effect** — the **Constituent Assembly of J&K dissolved in 1957** without recommending its removal, so the exit route appeared closed; decades of practice; and judicial observations treating it as having acquired permanence. (iii) **What actually happened** — **August 2019**: Presidential Orders, the **Reorganisation Act**, ⭐ **two Union Territories**, and **Article 35A** ceasing to operate. (iv) **The judicial resolution** — ***In re: Article 370* (2023)** upheld the abrogation, held J&K retained **no internal sovereignty**, and directed **restoration of statehood** and elections. (v) Conclude: the provision was **temporary in text, durable in practice, and finally treated as temporary in law** — with the unresolved question now being **when statehood returns**.

**2. "Discuss the nature of Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly after the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019. Briefly describe the powers and functions of the Assembly of the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2025 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2025.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **Nature** — J&K is a ⭐ **UT with a legislature**, in the same class as **Delhi and Puducherry**, under **Article 239A** and the 2019 Act; **Ladakh has no legislature**; the **Legislative Council was abolished**. (ii) **Powers** — may legislate on **State and Concurrent List** subjects ⭐ **except "public order" and "police"**; passes the budget; the **Council of Ministers** aids and advises the **Lieutenant Governor**. (iii) ⚠️ **Limits** — the LG's substantial powers *(All-India Services, Anti-Corruption Bureau)*; the Centre's reach over the excluded subjects; and the general subordination of a UT legislature to Parliament. (iv) **Comparison** — set it against a **full state Assembly** *(Ch 33)* and against **Delhi's Article 239AA** scheme. (v) Conclude on the **pending restoration of statehood** directed by the Supreme Court in 2023.

**3. "Asymmetric federalism is not a deviation from the Indian constitutional scheme but a condition of it." Discuss with reference to Articles 371–371J. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The claim** — formal equality of states coexists with **twelve tailored provisions**. (ii) **The categories** — customary law and land *(371A, 371G)*; development boards *(371, 371J)*; Assembly committees for tribal and hill areas *(371B, 371C)*; employment and education equity *(371D)*; minimum Assembly size *(371F, G, H, I)*; the Governor's law-and-order responsibility *(371A, 371H)*. (iii) **Why each exists** — accession terms, peace accords *(the 16-point agreement, the 1986 Mizo Accord)*, merger settlements, and regional development grievances. (iv) **The argument** — these provisions were **the price of integration**; uniformity imposed at the outset would have prevented accession, not produced it. (v) **The counter and the balance** — asymmetry can breed **demands for parity** and complicate administration; and Article 370's fate shows such provisions are **politically revisable**. Conclude: ⭐ **India's unity has rested on differentiated accommodation rather than uniform treatment** — the same argument as [Ch 13](../Part_II_System_of_Government/13_Federal_System.md)'s "holding-together" federation.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Articles 371–371J cover TWELVE states.** ⭐ **371 Maharashtra & Gujarat** *(development boards — Vidarbha, Marathwada; Saurashtra, Kutch)* · ⭐ **371A Nagaland** · **371B Assam** · **371C Manipur** · ⭐ **371D/E Andhra Pradesh & Telangana** · ⭐ **371F Sikkim** · ⭐ **371G Mizoram** · **371H Arunachal Pradesh** · **371I Goa** · ⭐ **371J Karnataka (98th Amendment, 2012 — Hyderabad-Karnataka)**.
- ⭐ **371A and 371G are structurally identical:** no Act of Parliament applies to **religious/social practices, customary law and procedure, administration of civil and criminal justice under customary law, and ownership and transfer of land** ⚠️ **unless the state Assembly so resolves**.
- ⭐ **Governor's special responsibility for LAW AND ORDER: only Nagaland (371A) and Arunachal Pradesh (371H).**
- ⭐ **Minimum Assembly sizes: Sikkim 30 · Mizoram 40 · Arunachal 30 · Goa 30** *(the general minimum in Article 170 is **60**)*.
- ⭐ **371D** — equitable **public employment and education** in AP and Telangana; local cadres; ⭐ an **Administrative Tribunal outside the High Court's jurisdiction**.
- ⭐ **371F Sikkim** — MLAs elect the **Lok Sabha member**; ⭐ **no court has jurisdiction over pre-merger treaties or agreements**.
- ⭐ **Article 370:** marginal note **"Temporary provisions"**; **Article 35A** *(1954 Presidential Order, permanent residents)*; **370(3)** required the **Constituent Assembly's** recommendation, ⚠️ **dissolved 1957**. ⭐ **August 2019** — Presidential Orders + ⭐ **J&K Reorganisation Act**: ⭐ **J&K = UT WITH legislature, Ladakh = UT WITHOUT**; **Legislative Council abolished**. ⭐ **Upheld by the Supreme Court in December 2023**, with a direction to **restore statehood**.
- ⭐ **The J&K Assembly may legislate on State and Concurrent List subjects EXCEPT "public order" and "police."** The **LG** retains substantial powers.
- ⚠️ ⭐ **Fifth Schedule = scheduled areas in other states. SIXTH Schedule = ASSAM, MEGHALAYA, TRIPURA, MIZORAM** *(Autonomous District Councils)*.
