# Chapter 33 — State Legislature

[← Chapter 32](32_State_Council_of_Ministers.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 34 →](34_High_Court.md)

**Read time ~18 min** · **Articles 168–212** · 🔴 Prelims: **2019** and recurring · Mains: **2010, 2012, 2021, 2022, 2023**

> 📌 **Most of this chapter is [Ch 22 Parliament](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md) transposed — devices, motions, budget stages, privileges are all identical.** ⭐ **The genuinely new material is the LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL**, which has no parallel at the Centre: a state may or may not have one, may create or abolish it, and it is **far weaker than the Rajya Sabha**. Spend your time there.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **Parliament is always bicameral; a state legislature may have one House or two — and can change its mind.** Article 169 lets a state **create or abolish** its Legislative Council. And when a state does have one, that Council is **not a second chamber in the Rajya Sabha sense at all**: it can delay an ordinary bill by a maximum of **four months**, and then the Assembly simply overrides it. **There is no joint sitting, because none is needed.**

---

## 📖 The story

The framers gave the states a choice they did not give the Union.

Parliament **must** have two Houses. A state **may** — and Article 169 lets it switch, in either direction, whenever its Assembly resolves to.

**Why the asymmetry?** Because the Rajya Sabha exists to represent **the states in the Union** — a genuine federal purpose that cannot be dispensed with. A Legislative Council represents nothing comparable; it is a **revising chamber**, and the framers thought states should decide for themselves whether the extra deliberation was worth the extra cost.

> 💡 **The analogy:** the Rajya Sabha is a **second signature required on a contract** — the other party's consent, structurally necessary. A Legislative Council is a **proofreader**. Useful, and a good proofreader catches real errors — but if the author disagrees, the author's version goes to print. **You can run a publishing house without one, which is why states are allowed to.**

**And states have exercised the choice repeatedly.** Andhra Pradesh **abolished** its Council in 1985 and **recreated** it in 2007. Tamil Nadu abolished its Council in 1986; a 2010 law to revive it was never brought into force. **Only six states have one today.**

**The consequence for the exam:** the Council's weakness is not an incidental detail — it is the whole reason the chapter exists separately from Ch 22.

## PART A — Organisation and composition

### Article 168 — the definition

> *"For every State there shall be a Legislature which shall consist of the **Governor** and, in some States, **two Houses**, and in other States, **one House**."*

⭐ **As with Parliament, the Governor is a part of the state legislature** though not a member of either House — same logic as [Ch 22](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md).

- The **Legislative Assembly** *(Vidhan Sabha)* is the **lower and popular** House — it exists in every state.
- The **Legislative Council** *(Vidhan Parishad)* is the **upper** House, where it exists.

### ⭐ The six bicameral states

**Andhra Pradesh · Bihar · Karnataka · Maharashtra · Telangana · Uttar Pradesh.**

> ⚠️ **This list changes** — check it before your exam. **Jammu & Kashmir's Council was abolished** by the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019** ([Ch 36](36_Special_Provisions.md)). Several states have passed resolutions to create Councils without the process being completed.

### ⭐⭐ Article 169 — creation and abolition

> Parliament may by law **abolish an existing Legislative Council or create one** where none exists, **if the Legislative Assembly of the state passes a resolution** to that effect by a ⭐ **special majority — a majority of the total membership of the Assembly AND not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting.**

**Three points that are asked:**
1. ⭐ **The initiative lies with the STATE Assembly**, but the **actual creation or abolition is by an Act of PARLIAMENT**.
2. ⭐ **Such a law is NOT deemed to be a constitutional amendment under Article 368** — Parliament passes it by a **simple majority**, like an ordinary law.
3. ⚠️ **Parliament is not bound** to act on the Assembly's resolution; it **may or may not** pass the law.

> 🧠 **The asymmetry to remember: the Assembly needs a SPECIAL majority to ask; Parliament needs only a SIMPLE majority to grant.** The hard part is at the state end.

### Composition of the Legislative Assembly — Article 170

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Maximum** | ⭐ **500** |
| **Minimum** | ⭐ **60** |
| ⚠️ **Exceptions below 60** | ⭐ **Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim and Goa — 30 each**; **Mizoram — 40**; **Nagaland — 46** *(also, by separate law, Puducherry 30 and Delhi 70)* |
| **Election** | **Direct election** from **territorial constituencies**, on **universal adult franchise** |
| **Reservation** | Seats reserved for **SCs and STs** on a population basis — ⭐ **reservation of seats, not of electorates** |
| ⚠️ **Anglo-Indian** | ⭐ The Governor's power to nominate **one Anglo-Indian** member was **ABOLISHED by the 104th Amendment Act, 2019** |
| **Delimitation** | Same freeze as the Lok Sabha — **42nd → 84th → 87th Amendments** ([Ch 22](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md)) |

⭐ **The 106th Amendment Act, 2023** reserves **one-third of seats for women** in the state Legislative Assemblies as well, on the same delayed trigger *(after the first census following commencement and the delimitation based on it)*.

### ⭐⭐ Composition of the Legislative Council — Article 171

**The size:**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Maximum** | **One-third of the total strength of the Legislative Assembly** |
| ⭐ **Minimum** | **40** |
| **Who fixes the actual strength** | ⭐ **Parliament** |

> ⭐ **The maximum is a fraction, the minimum is a number** — that combination is the examinable shape. A Council can never be as large as the Assembly; the design is that of a **small revising chamber**.

**⭐⭐ The five-fold composition — learn these fractions exactly:**

| Fraction | Elected by / nominated from |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1/3** | Elected by **members of local bodies** — municipalities, district boards etc. |
| ⭐ **1/3** | Elected by the **members of the Legislative Assembly**, ⚠️ **from among persons who are NOT members of the Assembly** |
| ⭐ **1/12** | Elected by **graduates of three years' standing** residing in the state |
| ⭐ **1/12** | Elected by **teachers of three years' standing** in institutions **not lower than secondary school** |
| ⭐ **1/6** | **Nominated by the Governor** from persons with special knowledge or practical experience in ⭐ **literature, science, art, the co-operative movement and social service** |

> 🧠 **The memory device: 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/12 + 1/12 + 1/6 = 1.** Check it — **4/12 + 4/12 + 1/12 + 1/12 + 2/12 = 12/12.** If your recalled fractions do not sum to one, you have mis-remembered one of them.
>
> ⭐ **So 5/6 are indirectly elected and 1/6 are nominated.** Election is by **proportional representation with the single transferable vote**.
>
> ⚠️ **The graduates' and teachers' constituencies are unique to the Legislative Council** — nothing like them exists anywhere else in Indian representation, and they are a favourite Prelims item.
>
> ⚠️ **This manner of composition is not entrenched** — ⭐ **Parliament may alter it by law**.

## PART B — Duration and membership

### ⭐ Duration — the same contrast as Parliament

| | **Legislative Assembly** | **Legislative Council** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | **Five years** from the date of its first meeting; **dissolvable** by the Governor at any time | ⭐ **A continuing chamber — NOT subject to dissolution** |
| **Retirement** | — | ⭐ **One-third retire every second year**; term **six years** |
| **Emergency extension** | ⭐ Extendable **one year at a time**, but **not beyond six months** after the Emergency ceases | Not applicable |

### Qualifications and disqualifications

| | **Legislative Assembly** | **Legislative Council** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Minimum age** | **25 years** | ⭐ **30 years** |
| **Other** | Citizen; oath before the Election Commission's appointee; ⭐ must be **an elector for any constituency in the STATE** | Same |

**Disqualifications — Article 191**, mirroring Article 102: **office of profit** · **unsound mind** · **undischarged insolvent** · **not a citizen / voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship** · disqualified by any law of Parliament *(the Representation of the People Act, 1951, adds the same list as for Parliament — see [Ch 22](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md))*.

### ⭐ Who decides a disqualification — the same split

| Ground | Decided by |
|---|---|
| **Article 191 grounds** | ⭐ The **GOVERNOR**, whose decision is final — but he must act **in accordance with the opinion of the Election Commission** *(Article 192)* |
| ⭐ **Defection — Tenth Schedule** | ⭐ The **Speaker / Chairman** of the House, **not** the Governor — reviewable per ***Kihoto Hollohan* (1992)** |

> 🧠 **Exactly parallel to the Centre:** at the Union it is the **President** on the **EC's** opinion; in a state it is the **Governor** on the **EC's** opinion. **Defection always goes to the presiding officer, at both levels.**

⚠️ **A seat is vacated on 60 days' absence** without permission, as in Parliament.

## PART C — ⭐⭐ Presiding officers, and the trap

| | **Rajya Sabha Chairman** *(Ch 22)* | ⭐ **Legislative Council Chairman** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Membership** | ⚠️ **NOT a member** — he is the **Vice-President of India** | ⭐⭐ **IS a member** — **elected by the Council from among its own members** |
| **Removal** | Only by removal from the office of Vice-President | ⭐ By a resolution of the Council passed by an **effective majority**, with **14 days' notice** |

> ⭐⭐ **This is the single most tested difference in the chapter.** The Rajya Sabha's Chairman comes from outside the House; the Legislative Council's Chairman is **one of its own members**, elected by it and removable by it. **Do not transpose the Rajya Sabha rule.**

**The Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Assembly** mirror their Lok Sabha counterparts exactly — elected from among members, removable by **effective majority** with **14 days' notice**, ⭐ **the Speaker does not vacate office on dissolution** *(he continues until immediately before the first meeting of the new Assembly)*, has a **casting vote only**, and ⭐ **certifies Money Bills, his decision being final**.

⭐ **A Speaker *pro tem*** is appointed by the **Governor** to preside over the first sitting and administer the oath.

> ⭐ **[Mains 2023 Q5](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md) asked about the role of presiding officers of state legislatures** in maintaining order and impartiality. The answer runs on **three legs**: their **powers** *(order, points of order, Money Bill certification, Tenth Schedule decisions)*; the **strains** *(partisanship, delay in deciding defection petitions, mass suspensions)*; and the **reforms** *(the British "once a Speaker, always a Speaker" convention, and a time limit on anti-defection decisions)*.

⚠️ **Quorum — the difference from Parliament:** ⭐ **Article 189(3)** — the quorum is ⭐ **10 members or one-tenth of the total number of members of the House, whichever is GREATER.** *(Parliament's is simply one-tenth.)*

## PART D — ⭐⭐ Legislative procedure: the weakness of the Council

**This is the core of the chapter and the substance of [Mains 2010 Q8(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2010.md) and [2021 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2021.md).**

### ⭐ Ordinary bills — Article 197

**A bill passed by the Assembly and transmitted to the Council.** The Council may:
1. **Pass** the bill; or
2. **Reject** it; or
3. **Pass it with amendments** not acceptable to the Assembly; or
4. ⭐ **Not pass it for three months**

**In cases 2, 3 and 4 the Assembly may pass the bill again** and transmit it once more. If the Council then **rejects it**, **amends it unacceptably**, or ⭐ **does not pass it within one month**, ⭐ **the bill is deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form in which the Assembly passed it the second time.**

> ⭐⭐ **Therefore the Legislative Council can delay an ordinary bill by a maximum of THREE MONTHS + ONE MONTH = FOUR MONTHS.** After that the Assembly's will prevails automatically.
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **There is NO provision for a joint sitting in the states.** None is needed — the Assembly overrides the Council by simply passing the bill again. *(Contrast **Article 108** at the Centre, where the Rajya Sabha's rejection produces a genuine deadlock requiring a joint sitting.)*

⭐ **And the reverse case is decisive:** a bill **originating in the Legislative Council** and **rejected by the Assembly** ⭐ **ends there — the Assembly's rejection is final**, and there is no mechanism for the Council to press it further.

### Money bills — Article 198

Identical in structure to Article 110 at the Centre:
- Introduced **only in the Legislative Assembly**, on the **Governor's recommendation**.
- ⭐ The **Speaker of the Assembly certifies** whether it is a Money Bill — **his decision is final**.
- The Council must **return it within 14 days** with or without recommendations; ⭐ the **Assembly may accept or reject** them.
- ⭐ If not returned within **14 days**, it is **deemed passed by both Houses**.

### ⭐⭐ Rajya Sabha vs Legislative Council — the comparison that answers the Mains question

| | **Rajya Sabha** | **Legislative Council** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Ordinary bills** | ⭐ **Equal powers** with the Lok Sabha; a deadlock goes to a **joint sitting** | ⭐ **Can only delay by 4 months**; the Assembly then overrides it — ⚠️ **no joint sitting** |
| **Money bills** | 14 days, recommendations only | 14 days, recommendations only *(same)* |
| ⭐ **Special powers** | ⭐ **Articles 249 and 312** — can authorise Parliament to legislate on the **State List** and to create **All-India Services** | ⚠️ ⭐ **NONE** |
| **Removing the government** | Cannot *(collective responsibility runs to the Lok Sabha)* | Cannot *(runs to the Assembly)* |
| **Electing the President** | ⭐ Participates *(elected members)* | ⚠️ ⭐ **Does not participate at all** |
| ⭐ **Existence** | **Permanent** — cannot be abolished | ⭐ **Can be created or abolished** under **Article 169** |

> ⭐ **The one-line verdict for an answer:** *"The Rajya Sabha is a **co-equal federal chamber**; the Legislative Council is a **subordinate revising chamber**. The Rajya Sabha can stop the Lok Sabha; the Council can only slow the Assembly down."*

### The arguments about Legislative Councils *(for [Mains 2021 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2021.md))*

| **For retaining/creating** | **Against** |
|---|---|
| A **revising chamber** that checks hasty and ill-considered legislation | ⚠️ It can only **delay by four months** — a weak check that rarely changes outcomes |
| Gives representation to **graduates, teachers and local bodies** — otherwise unrepresented interests | ⚠️ Those constituencies have **low turnout** and are open to manipulation |
| Provides a place for **experts and eminent persons** who cannot win a direct election | ⚠️ Used to **accommodate defeated politicians** and to make a **non-elected person a minister or CM** |
| Allows more **detailed scrutiny** than an overloaded Assembly | ⚠️ **Expensive** and duplicative for the value added |
| **Continuity** — it is never dissolved | ⚠️ **Not directly elected**, so it lacks democratic legitimacy to obstruct an elected Assembly |

## PART E — Privileges and immunity of proceedings

- ⭐ **Article 194** — the powers, privileges and immunities of the state legislature and its members are the **same in structure as Article 105** for Parliament: **freedom of speech in the House**, ⭐ **no liability in any court for anything said or any vote given**, freedom from arrest in **civil cases 40 days before and after** a session, and the collective privileges of the House. ⚠️ **Equally uncodified.**
- ⭐ **Article 212** — courts **shall not enquire into the proceedings** of a state legislature **on the ground of any alleged irregularity of procedure**. *(The counterpart of Article 122.)*

> ⚠️ **The trap:** the privileges are **the same as Parliament's** — ⭐ but they extend to the **Advocate General and ministers**, and ⚠️ **not to the Governor**, exactly as at the Centre the President is excluded.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The Council's arithmetic — the five fractions
> ⭐ **1/3 local bodies · 1/3 MLAs (from non-members) · 1/12 graduates · 1/12 teachers · 1/6 Governor's nominees.**
> 🧠 **Check: 4+4+1+1+2 = 12 twelfths.** If yours doesn't sum to 1, one fraction is wrong.

### The Council's size
> ⭐ **Maximum = one-third of the Assembly. Minimum = 40.** *(A fraction and a number — that is the shape.)*

### Article 169
> ⭐ **Assembly resolves by SPECIAL majority → Parliament legislates by SIMPLE majority → NOT a constitutional amendment under Article 368.**

### The delay limit
> ⭐⭐ **3 months + 1 month = 4 months, and that is all the Council can do.** ⚠️ **No joint sitting in the states.** A Council bill rejected by the Assembly **dies**.

### The presiding-officer trap
> ⭐⭐ **The Legislative Council's Chairman IS a member of the Council** — unlike the **Rajya Sabha's Chairman**, who is the **Vice-President** and **not a member**.

### The quorum
> ⭐ **10 members or one-tenth, WHICHEVER IS GREATER** *(Article 189(3))* — ⚠️ **Parliament's is simply one-tenth.**

### The ages and the sizes
> **Assembly: 25 years · max 500 · min 60** *(exceptions: **Goa, Sikkim, Arunachal 30**; **Mizoram 40**; **Nagaland 46**)*.
> **Council: 30 years · max ⅓ of the Assembly · min 40 · six-year terms · one-third retire every two years.**

### The disqualification split
> ⭐ **Article 191 grounds → the GOVERNOR, on the Election Commission's opinion. Defection → the SPEAKER/CHAIRMAN.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Explain the constitutional provisions under which Legislative Councils are established. Review the working and current status of Legislative Councils with suitable illustrations." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2021 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2021.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The provisions** — Article 168 *(a state may have one or two Houses)*; ⭐ **Article 169** *(Assembly's special-majority resolution → Parliament's simple-majority law → **not** an Article 368 amendment)*; **Article 171** *(the five fractions, max one-third of the Assembly, min 40)*. (ii) **Current status** — ⭐ **only six states** have Councils; ⚠️ **J&K's was abolished in 2019**; several resolutions elsewhere remain unimplemented. (iii) **The working** — illustrate the churn: **Andhra Pradesh abolished (1985) and recreated (2007)**; **Tamil Nadu abolished (1986)**, a revival law never brought into force. (iv) **Assess** — use the for/against table: revising value and representation of graduates, teachers and local bodies, against a **four-month delay limit**, cost, low-turnout constituencies, and use as a **back door for defeated politicians and non-elected ministers**. (v) Conclude: the Council is **worth having only where it is used as a genuine revising chamber**, and the constitutional design — deliberately making it easy to abolish — shows the framers thought so too.

**2. "How is disagreement between the Legislative Council and the Legislative Assembly of a State in passing of an ordinary Bill, resolved?" (150 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2010 Q8(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2010.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **Article 197** in full — the Council's four options; the Assembly's re-passage; the **one-month** second window; **deemed passage in the Assembly's form**. State the ⭐ **four-month maximum** explicitly, and the ⚠️ **absence of any joint sitting**. Then the reverse case — ⭐ **a Council bill rejected by the Assembly simply ends**. Close with the contrast: **Article 108's joint sitting exists at the Centre precisely because the Rajya Sabha can create a real deadlock; the Council cannot.**

**3. "Discuss the role of Presiding Officers of state legislatures in maintaining order and impartiality in conducting legislative work and in facilitating best democratic practices." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2023 Q5](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **Powers** — final interpreter of the rules within the House; order and discipline; points of order; ⭐ **Money Bill certification**; ⭐ **Tenth Schedule disqualification decisions**; casting vote; committee appointments. (ii) **The strains** — ⭐ the Speaker **retains party membership** and contests the next election on a ticket, so structural impartiality is not secured; **delay in deciding defection petitions** *(the political incentive to sit on them, and the courts' response in *Keisham Meghachandra* and after)*; **mass suspensions**; **falling sittings** in most state assemblies. (iii) **Reforms** — the **British "once a Speaker, always a Speaker"** convention; a **statutory time limit** for anti-defection decisions; transferring that jurisdiction to an **independent tribunal or the Election Commission**, as recommended repeatedly. (iv) Conclude: the office's authority rests on **perceived neutrality**, and the constitutional design does not currently protect it.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Article 168:** the state legislature = the **Governor + one or two Houses**. ⭐ **Six bicameral states: Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh.** ⚠️ **J&K's Council abolished in 2019.**
- ⭐⭐ **Article 169:** the **Assembly resolves by SPECIAL majority**; **Parliament creates or abolishes by SIMPLE majority**; ⭐ **NOT deemed a constitutional amendment under Article 368**; Parliament is **not bound** to act.
- **Assembly (Art 170):** **max 500, min 60** *(⚠️ **Goa, Sikkim, Arunachal 30; Mizoram 40; Nagaland 46**)*; direct election; SC/ST **seats** reserved; ⚠️ **Anglo-Indian nomination abolished, 104th Amendment 2019**; **106th Amendment (2023)** reserves **one-third for women** on the delayed trigger.
- ⭐⭐ **Council (Art 171): max ONE-THIRD of the Assembly, min 40**, strength fixed by **Parliament**. **1/3 local bodies · 1/3 MLAs from non-members · 1/12 graduates · 1/12 teachers · 1/6 Governor's nominees** *(literature, science, art, **co-operative movement**, social service)*. **5/6 indirectly elected, 1/6 nominated**, by **PR–STV**. ⚠️ **Parliament may alter this composition.**
- **Duration:** Assembly **5 years**, dissolvable, extendable **one year at a time** in an Emergency, not beyond **6 months** after. ⭐ **Council is a continuing chamber — one-third retire every 2 years, 6-year terms.**
- **Age: Assembly 25 · Council 30.** ⭐ **Disqualification: Article 191 grounds → the GOVERNOR on the EC's opinion; defection → the SPEAKER/CHAIRMAN.** **60 days' absence** vacates a seat.
- ⭐⭐ **Presiding officers: the Legislative Council's Chairman IS a member of the Council** *(elected by it, removable by effective majority with 14 days' notice)* — ⚠️ **unlike the Rajya Sabha Chairman.** The **Speaker** mirrors the Lok Sabha Speaker — **certifies Money Bills**, **does not vacate on dissolution**, **casting vote only**. **Speaker *pro tem* appointed by the Governor.**
- ⭐ **Quorum (Art 189(3)): 10 members or one-tenth, WHICHEVER IS GREATER.**
- ⭐⭐ **Article 197:** the Council may pass, reject, amend unacceptably, or sit on a bill for **3 months**; the Assembly re-passes; if the Council again fails within **1 month**, the bill is **deemed passed in the Assembly's form**. ⭐ **Maximum delay 4 months.** ⚠️ **No joint sitting in the states.** ⭐ **A Council bill rejected by the Assembly dies.**
- **Article 198 Money Bills:** Assembly only, on the **Governor's recommendation**; ⭐ **the Speaker certifies**; Council has **14 days**, recommendations only; **deemed passed** if not returned.
- **Privileges: Article 194** *(same as 105, equally uncodified)*; ⭐ **Article 212** — courts cannot enquire into proceedings for **procedural irregularity**.
