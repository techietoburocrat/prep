# Chapter 35 — Subordinate Courts

[← Chapter 34](34_High_Court.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 36 →](36_Special_Provisions.md)

**Read time ~8 min** · **Articles 233–237** · 🟡 Prelims: occasional · ⚠️ **Mains: heavy — 2012, 2016, 2018, 2019, 2024, 2025**, almost all through **TRIBUNALS**

> 📌 **Rated 🟡, but do not skim it.** ⭐ The constitutional provisions are thin, but this chapter is the home of **tribunals, Lok Adalats and the All-India Judicial Service** — which together account for **six Mains questions in the last thirteen years**. Give the last two Parts of this chapter the attention the rating does not suggest.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The Constitution says very little about the courts where almost all Indian litigation actually happens** — five articles, mostly about who appoints district judges. ⭐ **The examinable weight has shifted entirely to what has grown up alongside them:** tribunals that took jurisdiction away from the ordinary courts, and alternative forums built to relieve them.

---

## PART A — The constitutional provisions

### ⭐ Appointment — the split that is tested

| Post | Who appoints | Who is consulted | Article |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **District judges** | The **GOVERNOR** of the state | ⭐ The **HIGH COURT** | **233** |
| ⭐ **Other judicial service** *(below district judge)* | The **GOVERNOR** | ⭐ The **HIGH COURT *and* the STATE PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION** | **234** |
| **Control over district and subordinate courts** *(posting, promotion, leave)* | ⭐ The **HIGH COURT** | — | **235** |

> ⭐ **The pattern to hold:** ⚠️ **the Governor APPOINTS, the High Court CONTROLS.** And note that the **State PSC is involved only for the lower judicial service**, ⚠️ **not for district judges**.

### ⭐ Qualifications for a district judge — Article 233(2)

A person **not already in the service of the Union or of the State** is eligible to be appointed a district judge if he:
1. Has been an **advocate or pleader for not less than seven years**; **and**
2. Is **recommended by the High Court**

> ⚠️ **Article 236 definitions:** "district judge" includes a **judge of a city civil court, additional district judge, joint district judge, assistant district judge, chief judge of a small cause court, chief presidency magistrate, additional chief presidency magistrate, sessions judge, additional sessions judge and assistant sessions judge.** "Judicial service" means a service consisting **exclusively of persons intended to fill the post of district judge and other civil judicial posts inferior to it**.

⭐ **Article 237** — the Governor may, by public notification, direct that these provisions shall apply to **any class or classes of magistrates** in the state.

### The structure of subordinate courts

| Level | Civil side | Criminal side |
|---|---|---|
| **Highest at district level** | ⭐ **District Judge** — the highest civil court in the district, with **appellate and original jurisdiction** | ⭐ **Sessions Judge** — the same officer, sitting on the criminal side |
| **Below** | Subordinate judge · Munsiff *(the lowest civil court)* | Chief Judicial Magistrate · Judicial Magistrate |

> ⭐ **The point to remember:** ⚠️ **the District Judge and the Sessions Judge are usually the SAME PERSON** — he is called *District Judge* when presiding over a civil case and *Sessions Judge* over a criminal one.
>
> ⭐ **A Sessions Judge may pass any sentence including a death sentence — ⚠️ but it must be CONFIRMED by the High Court before execution** *(see [Ch 34](34_High_Court.md))*.

### ⭐ The All-India Judicial Service — a standing proposal

- ⭐ **Article 312** permits the creation of an **All-India Judicial Service** *(not including any post inferior to that of a district judge)* if the **Rajya Sabha** passes a resolution supported by ⭐ **two-thirds of members present and voting** ([Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md)).
- ⭐ **Recommended by the Sarkaria Commission** and repeatedly since.
- ⚠️ **It has never been created.** **Objections:** it would erode the **High Courts' control** over the subordinate judiciary under Article 235; **language barriers** in trial courts; and **state opposition** on federal grounds.

## PART B — ⭐⭐ Tribunals *(where the Mains marks are)*

### The constitutional basis — the 42nd Amendment, 1976

| Article | What it allows |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **323A** | ⭐ **PARLIAMENT** may provide for **administrative tribunals** for disputes relating to **recruitment and conditions of service** of persons appointed to public services of the Union or of a state |
| ⭐ **323B** | ⭐ The **appropriate legislature — Parliament OR a state legislature** — may provide for tribunals for **other matters**: taxation, foreign exchange, industrial and labour disputes, land reforms, ceiling on urban property, elections to Parliament and state legislatures, food, rent and tenancy rights |

> ⭐⭐ **The three differences between 323A and 323B — a certain Prelims item:**
> 1. ⭐ **323A: only PARLIAMENT can legislate. 323B: Parliament OR the state legislatures.**
> 2. ⭐ **323A: only for PUBLIC SERVICE matters. 323B: for the nine listed matters.**
> 3. ⭐ **323A: only ONE tribunal for the Centre and one for each state (no hierarchy). 323B: a HIERARCHY of tribunals may be created.**

**Under Article 323A**, Parliament enacted the **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985**, creating the ⭐ **Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)** and providing for State Administrative Tribunals.

### ⭐⭐ The constitutional validity question — *L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)

**The original scheme excluded the jurisdiction of the High Courts** over matters given to tribunals — that was the point of the 42nd Amendment.

⭐ In ***L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India* (1997)**, a seven-judge bench held:
1. ⭐ The power of **judicial review under Articles 32 and 226** is part of the ⭐ **BASIC STRUCTURE** and **cannot be excluded**.
2. ⭐ Tribunals may function as **courts of first instance**, ⚠️ **but their decisions are subject to scrutiny by a DIVISION BENCH of the High Court** within whose jurisdiction the tribunal falls.
3. ⭐ Litigants **cannot approach the Supreme Court directly** from a tribunal, bypassing the High Court.

> ⭐ **The formulation for an answer:** *"Tribunals **supplement** the ordinary courts; they do not **supplant** them. After *L. Chandra Kumar*, a tribunal is a **court of first instance whose orders remain reviewable by the High Court** — so the jurisdiction of the ordinary courts is **redistributed, not extinguished.**"*

### ⚠️ The problems with tribunalisation

1. ⭐ **Independence** — tribunals are often housed in, and administratively controlled by, the **very ministry** whose decisions they review
2. **Appointments and tenure** — short terms, and the prospect of **reappointment by the executive**, compromise independence *(the subject of repeated litigation, including ***Rojer Mathew* (2019)** and the *Madras Bar Association* cases)*
3. ⭐ **They add a layer rather than removing one** — after *L. Chandra Kumar*, an appeal still goes to the High Court, so the promised speed is not always delivered
4. **Vacancies and infrastructure** — many tribunals function well below sanctioned strength
5. ⚠️ **Fragmentation** — a proliferation of specialised tribunals with inconsistent procedures

## PART C — Alternative forums

### ⭐ Lok Adalats

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Statutory basis** | ⭐ The **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** *(which also gave statutory status to the **National Legal Services Authority — NALSA**)* |
| **Constitutional root** | ⭐ **Article 39A** — a **DPSP** requiring the state to secure **equal justice and free legal aid** ([Ch 8](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/08_DPSP.md)) |
| **Nature** | ⭐ Settlement by **conciliation and compromise** — **no adjudication**; the presiding officers **persuade**, they do not decide |
| ⭐ **Award** | ⭐ **Deemed to be a decree of a civil court**, ⭐ **FINAL and binding**, and ⚠️ ⭐ **NO APPEAL lies against it** |
| ⚠️ **Consent** | ⭐ **Both parties must agree** — a Lok Adalat cannot impose a settlement |
| **Court fee** | ⭐ **Court fee already paid is REFUNDED** if the dispute is settled |
| ⚠️ **Jurisdiction** | Any case pending before a court, or any matter **not yet brought** to court — ⚠️ **except non-compoundable offences** |

⭐ **Permanent Lok Adalats** *(added in 2002)* deal with **public utility services** — transport, postal, telegraph, power, water, sanitation, insurance — and ⚠️ ⭐ **can decide the merits if the parties fail to reach a settlement**, unlike an ordinary Lok Adalat.

> ⭐ **The distinction [Mains 2024 Q2](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md) asked for:** a ⭐ **Lok Adalat is conciliatory, free, non-appealable and requires consent**; an ⭐ **Arbitration Tribunal is adjudicatory, arises from a contractual arbitration agreement, is governed by the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, is paid for by the parties, and its award is challengeable in court on limited grounds.**

### Other forums

- ⭐ **Gram Nyayalayas** — under the **Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008**, at the intermediate panchayat level, to provide access to justice at the doorstep; ⚠️ **implementation has been very poor**.
- **Family Courts**, **Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions**, **Fast Track Courts**, and ⭐ **Parivarik Mahila Lok Adalats** *(organised by the National Commission for Women to address delays in matrimonial and family disputes — asked in [Mains 2012 Q3(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2012.md))*.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The appointment split
> ⭐ **Governor APPOINTS, High Court CONTROLS.** District judges *(Art 233)*: Governor + **High Court** consultation, **7 years as an advocate**, HC recommendation. Lower judicial service *(Art 234)*: Governor + **High Court AND State PSC**.

### The same person, two names
> ⭐ **District Judge (civil) = Sessions Judge (criminal).** ⭐ A sessions judge may award death — ⚠️ **but the High Court must confirm it**.

### 323A vs 323B — the three differences
> ⭐ **A: Parliament only · public service only · no hierarchy.**
> ⭐ **B: Parliament OR states · nine listed matters · hierarchy permitted.**

### The case
> ⭐ ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)** — judicial review under **32 and 226 is basic structure**; tribunals are **courts of first instance**; ⭐ **their orders go to a DIVISION BENCH of the High Court**, ⚠️ **not directly to the Supreme Court**.

### Lok Adalat — the five facts
> ⭐ **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** · rooted in ⭐ **Article 39A** · ⭐ **award = decree of a civil court, FINAL, NO APPEAL** · ⭐ **requires the consent of both parties** · ⭐ **court fee refunded**. **Permanent Lok Adalats** *(public utility services)* ⭐ **can decide on merits**.

### The service that does not exist
> ⭐ **All-India Judicial Service — Article 312, needs a Rajya Sabha resolution by two-thirds present and voting. Recommended by Sarkaria. Never created.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "How far do you agree with the view that tribunals curtail the jurisdiction of ordinary courts? In view of the above, discuss the constitutional validity and competency of the tribunals in India." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2018 Q12](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2018.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The design did curtail** — Articles **323A and 323B** *(42nd Amendment)* expressly excluded High Court jurisdiction over the transferred matters. (ii) ⭐ **But *L. Chandra Kumar* (1997) reversed that** — review under **32 and 226 is basic structure**; tribunals are **courts of first instance** and their orders are **reviewable by a High Court division bench**. So jurisdiction was **redistributed, not extinguished**. (iii) **Competency** — specialised expertise, informal procedure, speed, reduced court burden. (iv) ⚠️ **The problems** — administrative control by the **parent ministry**; short tenures and executive reappointment; **an added layer** rather than a substitute; vacancies; fragmentation. (v) Conclude: tribunals are **constitutionally valid but institutionally under-designed** — the fix is an **independent National Tribunals Commission** for appointments and administration, which the Supreme Court has repeatedly recommended.

**2. "Comment on the need of administrative tribunals as compared to the court system." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2025 Q2](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2025.md).*
> *Skeleton:* **Why needed** — the volume of service disputes; **technical subject matter**; ordinary courts' **pendency**; informal procedure and lower cost; **Article 323A** and the **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985** creating the **CAT**. **Compared with courts** — tribunals are **not bound by the Civil Procedure Code or the Evidence Act**, may include **technical members**, and offer faster disposal; but courts offer **greater independence, precedent and appellate structure**. Conclude with *L. Chandra Kumar*'s balance and the reform agenda.

**3. "Explain and distinguish between Lok Adalats and Arbitration Tribunals. Whether they entertain civil as well as criminal cases?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2024 Q2](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md).*
> *Skeleton:* **Lok Adalat** — statutory *(1987 Act)*, rooted in **Article 39A**, ⭐ **conciliatory not adjudicatory**, needs **consent**, **free**, award is a **civil decree with no appeal**, ⚠️ **cannot take non-compoundable offences** *(so: civil cases and **compoundable** criminal cases only)*. **Arbitration Tribunal** — contractual, under the **Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996**, **adjudicatory**, party-funded, award challengeable on **limited statutory grounds**, ⚠️ **for civil/commercial disputes only — criminal matters are not arbitrable**. Conclude on their shared purpose *(decongesting courts)* and their opposite methods *(persuasion vs decision)*.

**4. "The Central Administrative Tribunal which was established for redressal of grievances and complaints by or against central government employees nowadays is exercising its powers as an independent judicial authority. Explain." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2019 Q2](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md).*
> *Skeleton:* CAT's creation under **Article 323A** and the **1985 Act**; its composition *(judicial and administrative members)*; original jurisdiction over service matters; freedom from the CPC and the Evidence Act; and how ⭐ ***L. Chandra Kumar*** simultaneously **confirmed its judicial character** and **subjected it to High Court review** — the source of its present standing as a judicial rather than administrative body. Note the continuing problem of **executive control over appointments**.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- **Articles 233–237.** ⭐ **District judges (233): appointed by the GOVERNOR in consultation with the HIGH COURT; 7 years as an advocate + HC recommendation.** ⭐ **Other judicial service (234): Governor + HIGH COURT + STATE PSC.** ⭐ **Control (235) lies with the HIGH COURT** — posting, promotion, leave.
- ⭐ **District Judge = Sessions Judge** *(same person, civil and criminal)*. ⭐ **A death sentence must be CONFIRMED by the High Court.**
- ⭐ **All-India Judicial Service — Article 312, Rajya Sabha resolution by two-thirds present and voting; recommended by Sarkaria; NEVER CREATED** *(objections: erosion of Article 235 control, language, state opposition)*.
- ⭐⭐ **Tribunals — 42nd Amendment (1976).** **323A: PARLIAMENT only · public service matters only · no hierarchy.** **323B: Parliament OR states · nine listed matters · hierarchy allowed.** ⭐ **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 → CAT.**
- ⭐⭐ ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997):** review under **Articles 32 and 226 is BASIC STRUCTURE**; tribunals are **courts of first instance**; ⭐ **appeals go to a DIVISION BENCH of the High Court**, ⚠️ **not directly to the Supreme Court**. ⇒ **Tribunals supplement, they do not supplant.**
- ⚠️ **Tribunal problems:** ministry control · short tenure and executive reappointment · **an extra layer** · vacancies · fragmentation.
- ⭐ **Lok Adalats — Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**, rooted in **Article 39A**; **conciliatory**; ⭐ **award = civil decree, FINAL, NO APPEAL**; ⭐ **needs both parties' consent**; ⭐ **court fee refunded**; ⚠️ **no non-compoundable offences**. ⭐ **Permanent Lok Adalats** *(public utility services)* **can decide on merits**.
- **Also:** **Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008** *(poorly implemented)* · Family Courts · Consumer Commissions · Fast Track Courts · **Parivarik Mahila Lok Adalats** *(NCW)*.
