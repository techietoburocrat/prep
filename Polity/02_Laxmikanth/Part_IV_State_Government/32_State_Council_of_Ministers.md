> 📌 **This is [Ch 20 Central Council of Ministers](../Part_III_Central_Government/20_Central_Council_of_Ministers.md) with three differences.** Learn the three and skip the rest.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **Three differences from the Union Council of Ministers, and one of them is a number you will be asked:** the size cap has a **floor of 12** that the Union cap does not have; four specified states must have a **tribal welfare minister**; and the whole body is responsible to the **Legislative Assembly**, not to the Council.

---

## ⭐ The three differences — the whole chapter

| # | Difference | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐⭐ **The size cap has a MINIMUM** | **Article 164(1A)** *(91st Amendment, 2003)* — the total number of ministers **including the Chief Minister** shall **not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Legislative Assembly**, ⭐ **but shall not be less than 12**. ⚠️ **The Union cap (Article 75(1A)) has NO minimum** |
| 2 | ⭐ **The tribal welfare minister** | **Article 164(1)** — in ⭐ **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha** there shall be a **Minister in charge of tribal welfare**, who may in addition be in charge of the welfare of the **Scheduled Castes and backward classes** |
| 3 | **Responsibility runs to the Assembly** | **Article 164(2)** — the Council is **collectively responsible to the LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY**, ⚠️ **not to the Legislative Council**, even in a bicameral state |

> ⭐ **On difference 2 — the states have changed, and that is the trap.** The provision **originally named Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha**. The ⭐ **94th Amendment Act, 2006** ⭐ **freed Bihar from the obligation** and ⭐ **extended it to the newly created Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand**. **The current four are Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha.** Anyone answering "Bihar" is using a pre-2006 list.

## PART A — The rest, in brief

### Appointment and composition

- ⭐ **Article 164(1):** the **Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor**; the **other ministers are appointed by the Governor on the Chief Minister's advice**.
- Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the Governor**.
- A minister who is **not a member of the state legislature for six consecutive months** ceases to be a minister.
- ⭐ **Article 177:** every minister has the **right to speak and take part in the proceedings of either House**, but ⭐ **can vote only in the House of which he is a member**.
- **Salaries and allowances** are determined by the **state legislature**.

**The ranks** mirror the Union: **Cabinet Ministers · Ministers of State · Deputy Ministers**. ⭐ **The Cabinet is a part of the Council**, is much smaller, meets frequently, and takes the real decisions — exactly as at the Centre ([Ch 20](../Part_III_Central_Government/20_Central_Council_of_Ministers.md)).

### ⭐ The 91st Amendment Act, 2003 — both limbs

| Limb | Union *(Ch 20)* | State |
|---|---|---|
| **Size cap** | **Article 75(1A)** — ≤ **15% of the Lok Sabha**, ⚠️ **no minimum** | ⭐ **Article 164(1A)** — ≤ **15% of the Legislative Assembly**, ⭐ **minimum 12** |
| **Defectors barred** | **Article 75(1B)** | ⭐ **Article 164(1B)** — a member **disqualified on the ground of defection** is **also disqualified to be appointed a minister** |

> ⭐ **Why the state cap needed a floor.** Fifteen per cent of a very small Assembly would produce an unworkable ministry — **Sikkim's Assembly has 32 seats**, Goa's 40, Mizoram's 40. Without the floor of 12, such states could have had **five or six ministers** for an entire government. **The minimum protects small states from the cap designed to restrain large ones** — and that reasoning is what makes the fact memorable.

### Responsibility

| Type | Provision |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Collective** | **Article 164(2)** — collectively responsible to the **Legislative Assembly**. A no-confidence motion carried there means **the entire Council must resign**, including ministers drawn from the **Legislative Council** |
| **Individual** | **Article 164(1)** — ministers hold office **during the Governor's pleasure**, exercised **on the Chief Minister's advice** |
| ⚠️ **Legal** | ⭐ **None** — as at the Centre, there is **no countersignature requirement**, and ⭐ **Article 163(3)** bars any court from enquiring into **what advice ministers tendered** to the Governor |

> ⭐ **Article 163(3) is the state twin of Article 74(2)** — the **advice** is non-justiciable; the **action taken** on it is not. Keep the two apart.

### Article 166 — conduct of business

- All **executive action of the state government** is expressed to be taken **in the name of the Governor**.
- Orders made in his name must be **authenticated** as the rules specify — ⭐ and their **validity cannot be questioned on the ground that they are not orders made by the Governor**.
- The Governor makes rules for the **more convenient transaction of business** and for the **allocation of business among ministers**.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The three differences
> ⭐ **15% cap with a FLOOR OF 12** *(Union has no floor)* · ⭐ **tribal welfare minister in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, MP and Odisha** · **collective responsibility to the LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY only**.

### The 94th Amendment trap
> ⭐ **Bihar OUT, Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand IN (2006).** Original three: **Bihar, MP, Odisha**. Current four: ⭐ **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha.**

### The article pairs — Union vs State
> **74 ↔ 163** *(advice; the state version adds **discretion**)* · **75 ↔ 164** · **77 ↔ 166** · **78 ↔ 167** · **88 ↔ 177** · **74(2) ↔ 163(3)**.

### The size cap, both levels
> ⭐ **Union: ≤15% of the Lok Sabha, no minimum. State: ≤15% of the Assembly, minimum 12.** Both from the **91st Amendment, 2003**, which **also barred defectors** from being ministers at both levels.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. Examine the significance of the 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 for both the Union and the states. (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Both limbs at both levels — **Articles 75(1A)/(1B)** and **164(1A)/(1B)**; the **15% cap** and the ⭐ **state minimum of 12**; and the bar on **defectors becoming ministers**. Purpose: to end **jumbo ministries** as the currency of defection, complementing the **Tenth Schedule**. Assessment: it curbed ministry size, but defection persists through **mass splits** and resignation-and-re-election, so the amendment addressed a **symptom**.

**2. "Collective responsibility is the bedrock of parliamentary government, but it operates differently in a bicameral state." Discuss. (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Article 164(2) — responsibility runs ⭐ **only to the Legislative Assembly**; the **Legislative Council cannot remove a government**, and a no-confidence motion there has no constitutional effect. Yet **ministers may be drawn from the Council** and must resign with the rest — so ⭐ **a minister can be brought down by a House he does not sit in**. Compare the Rajya Sabha's identical position at the Centre. Conclude: the **weaker second chamber** in the states makes the asymmetry sharper — see [Ch 33](33_State_Legislature.md).

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- **Articles 163, 164, 166, 167, 177.** Structure mirrors the Union Council ([Ch 20](../Part_III_Central_Government/20_Central_Council_of_Ministers.md)) — **Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers**; the **Cabinet is a small part** of the Council and takes the real decisions.
- ⭐ **Article 164(1A) (91st Amendment, 2003): ministers including the CM ≤ 15% of the Legislative Assembly, MINIMUM 12.** ⚠️ **The Union cap has no minimum.** ⭐ **Article 164(1B): a defector cannot be appointed a minister.**
- ⭐ **Article 164(1): a Minister in charge of TRIBAL WELFARE is mandatory in CHHATTISGARH, JHARKHAND, MADHYA PRADESH and ODISHA.** ⚠️ **94th Amendment (2006)** removed **Bihar** and added **Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand**.
- ⭐ **Article 164(2): collectively responsible to the LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY** — not the Council. **Individual responsibility:** Governor's pleasure, on the **CM's advice**. ⚠️ **No legal responsibility**; ⭐ **Article 163(3)** bars courts from enquiring into the **advice** *(not the action)*.
- ⭐ **Article 177:** a minister may **speak in either House** but **vote only in his own**.
- **Article 166:** business in the **Governor's name**; authentication rules mean validity **cannot be challenged** on the ground the Governor did not personally make the order.
- **Six-month rule** for a non-member minister. Salaries fixed by the **state legislature**.
