> 📌 **This chapter is [Ch 19 Prime Minister](../Part_III_Central_Government/19_Prime_Minister.md) with the names changed.** Read that chapter, then read only the differences below. It genuinely takes ten minutes.

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **Everything true of the Prime Minister is true of the Chief Minister, with one asymmetry: the CM's appointer has express discretion and the PM's does not.** The President must appoint whoever commands the Lok Sabha; the Governor, under **Article 163**, has a discretion whose scope he himself defines — which makes the CM's position at the moment of appointment structurally weaker than the PM's.

> ⭐ **The two facts most likely to be asked:**
> 1. ⭐ **A member of the Legislative Council can be Chief Minister.** *(Real examples are common — several CMs have been Council members rather than MLAs.)*
> 2. ⭐ **A non-member can be CM for six months**, exactly as with the PM.

## PART A — Appointment

**Article 164(1):** *"The Chief Minister shall be appointed by the Governor."*

⭐ **The Governor's discretion is real and has been exercised.** Where no party has a clear majority in the Legislative Assembly, the Governor decides **whom to invite**. Post-***S.R. Bommai* (1994)**, the settled position is that:
- The Governor should invite the leader of the **single largest party or pre-poll alliance** first, and
- ⭐ **Majority must be tested on the FLOOR OF THE HOUSE**, within a short and specified time — **not** determined by the Governor's own assessment of letters of support.

> ⚠️ **This is where Governor controversies actually bite.** Several recent disputes have turned not on *whom* the Governor invited but on **how long he gave them before a floor test** — the Supreme Court has repeatedly ordered floor tests within **24 to 48 hours** to prevent horse-trading.

⚠️ **A person who is not a member of the state legislature** can be appointed Chief Minister, but must become a member **within six months**, or cease to hold office.

**Oath, term and salary:**
- Oath of **office and secrecy**, administered by the **Governor**.
- ⚠️ **Term is not fixed.** He holds office **during the pleasure of the Governor** — but ⭐ **cannot be dismissed so long as he commands a majority** in the Legislative Assembly.
- ⭐ **Salary and allowances are determined by the STATE LEGISLATURE** *(not Parliament)*.

## PART B — Powers and functions

### ① In relation to the Council of Ministers

- The Governor appoints ⭐ **only those persons as ministers who are recommended by the Chief Minister**.
- He **allocates and reshuffles portfolios**.
- He can **ask a minister to resign**, or advise the Governor to dismiss him.
- He **presides over the Cabinet** and guides, directs, controls and coordinates its activities.
- ⭐ **By resigning, he dissolves the entire Council of Ministers** — no other minister's resignation has that effect.

### ⭐ ② In relation to the Governor — Article 167

**The same three duties as Article 78:**
1. To **communicate to the Governor all decisions** of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the state and proposals for legislation
2. To **furnish such information** relating to administration and legislation **as the Governor may call for**
3. ⭐ If the Governor requires, to **submit for the consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a minister but which has not been considered by the Council**

He also **advises the Governor** on the appointment of the Advocate General, the Chairman and members of the State Public Service Commission, the State Election Commissioner, and others.

### ③ In relation to the state legislature

- **Leader of the House.**
- **Advises the Governor** on summoning and proroguing sessions.
- ⭐ Can **recommend dissolution of the Legislative Assembly** to the Governor at any time.
- **Announces government policies** on the floor of the House.

### ④ Other roles

- **Chairman of the State Planning Board.**
- ⭐ Acts as **Vice-Chairman of the concerned Zonal Council by rotation, holding office for a period of one year at a time** *(see [Ch 15](../Part_II_System_of_Government/15_Inter_State_Relations.md))*.
- ⭐ **Member of the Inter-State Council** and of the **Governing Council of NITI Aayog**, both chaired by the **Prime Minister**.
- Chief spokesman of the state government; political head of the services; chief crisis manager.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The four differences from the PM
> ⭐ **Appointed by the Governor, who has EXPRESS DISCRETION** · ⭐ **may belong to the LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL** · ⭐ **salary fixed by the STATE LEGISLATURE** · **duties under Article 167** *(not 78)*.

### The article block
> **163** aid, advice and discretion · **164(1)** appointment · ⭐ **164(1A)** the **15% cap with a minimum of 12** *(Ch 32)* · **167** the CM's duties towards the Governor.

### The floor-test rule
> ⭐ ***Bommai* (1994)** — **majority is tested in the House, not in Raj Bhavan.** Recent practice: floor tests ordered within **24–48 hours**.

### The two roles outside the state
> ⭐ **Vice-Chairman of his Zonal Council, by ANNUAL ROTATION** *(the Union Home Minister chairs)*; **member of the Inter-State Council and the NITI Aayog Governing Council** *(the PM chairs both)*.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The Chief Minister is the real executive head of a state, yet his position is weaker than that of the Prime Minister." Examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The similarities** — Articles 163/164 mirror 74/75; the same powers over the Council; the same asymmetry of resignation; Article 167 mirrors Article 78. (ii) ⭐ **Why the CM is structurally weaker** — his appointer has **express discretion** *(Article 163)*, whose scope the **Governor himself determines** as final; the Governor can **reserve his government's bills** for the President *(Article 200)*, and **recommend President's Rule** *(Article 356)* — the PM faces no equivalent; the state's legislative field is **limited by the Seventh Schedule and the five doors** ([Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md)); and the state depends on the Centre financially. (iii) **The counter** — a CM with a solid majority is politically dominant, and ***Bommai***, ***Nabam Rebia*** and ***B.P. Singhal*** have narrowed the Governor's room considerably. (iv) Conclude: the weakness is **federal, not personal** — it comes from the state's position in the Union, not from Article 164.

**2. "Discuss the role of the Governor in the appointment of the Chief Minister in a hung Assembly." (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Article 164(1) and the discretion under Article 163; the convention of inviting the **largest party or pre-poll alliance**; ⭐ ***Bommai*** and the **floor test** as the constitutional answer to competing claims of support; the recent practice of **court-ordered floor tests within 24–48 hours**; and the Sarkaria/Punchhi recommendation of a **published order of preference** for inviting claimants.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Article 164(1): appointed by the Governor.** Convention — leader of the majority party in the **Legislative Assembly**. ⭐ **The Governor has real discretion in a hung Assembly**, subject to ***Bommai*'s floor test**.
- ⭐ **A non-member may be CM for six months.** ⭐ **He may belong to EITHER House — including the Legislative Council.**
- **Holds office during the Governor's pleasure**, but **cannot be dismissed while he holds a majority**. ⭐ **Salary fixed by the STATE LEGISLATURE.** Oath by the **Governor**.
- **Over the Council:** recommends every minister · allocates portfolios · can demand resignation · presides over the Cabinet · ⭐ **his resignation dissolves the whole Council**.
- ⭐ **Article 167 — three duties towards the Governor:** **communicate** decisions · **furnish** information called for · **submit** to the Council any matter decided by a single minister.
- **Over the legislature:** leader of the House · advises on summoning and proroguing · ⭐ **can recommend dissolution** · announces policy.
- **Other roles:** Chairman of the **State Planning Board** · ⭐ **Vice-Chairman of the Zonal Council by ANNUAL ROTATION** · member of the **Inter-State Council** and the **NITI Aayog Governing Council**.
