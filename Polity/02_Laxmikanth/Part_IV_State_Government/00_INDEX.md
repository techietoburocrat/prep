## 🎯 How to read this Part ⭐

**Part IV mirrors [Part III](../Part_III_Central_Government/00_INDEX.md) almost exactly.** The Governor corresponds to the President, the Chief Minister to the Prime Minister, the State Legislature to Parliament, the High Court to the Supreme Court.

⚠️ **Do not re-learn the similarities — they are assumed. UPSC examines only the differences.** Every chapter here is written as a **contrast table** against its Part III counterpart, and the ⭐ marks sit on the points where the two diverge.

**The four divergences that carry most of the marks:**

| # | The difference | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐ **The Governor has express DISCRETION; the President does not** | **Article 163** carves out discretionary functions and makes the Governor's own view **final**. Article 74 gives the President no such clause. **This single difference generates most Centre–state litigation** |
| 2 | ⭐ **The Governor holds office during the President's pleasure** | No security of tenure at all — whereas the President can only be **impeached** |
| 3 | ⭐ **The Governor cannot pardon a death sentence** | And has no power over **court martial** cases. Both belong to the President alone |
| 4 | ⭐ **A state may have one House or two** | Parliament is always bicameral. **Article 169** lets a state **create or abolish** its Legislative Council — and the Council is far weaker than the Rajya Sabha |

## ⭐ Where the marks are

**Ch 30 (Governor)** is the highest-yield chapter in this Part by a wide margin — the office is the single most politically contested institution in Indian federalism right now, and it has appeared in Prelims in **2013, 2014, 2018, 2019 and 2025** and in Mains in **2018, 2022 and 2023**.

After that, **Ch 33 (State Legislature)** — mainly for the **Legislative Council** provisions, which have their own Mains questions *(2010, 2021)* and have no parallel in Part III.

**Ch 35 (Subordinate Courts)** is 🟡 but carries a disproportionate Mains load through **tribunals** — asked in **2016, 2018, 2019, 2024 and 2025**.

## 🧵 Threads running through Part IV

| Thread | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The Governor as the Centre's agent** | **Ch 30** *(discretion, reserving bills, recommending President's Rule)* → connects to [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md) *(Article 365, Sarkaria and Punchhi)* and [Ch 16](../Part_II_System_of_Government/16_Emergency_Provisions.md) *(Article 356 and Bommai)* |
| **The floor test** | **Ch 30** and **Ch 31** — ***S.R. Bommai* (1994)** settled that majority is tested **in the House, not in the Governor's judgement** |
| ⭐ **The weaker second chamber** | **Ch 33** — the Legislative Council can delay an ordinary bill by only **3 months + 1 month**, and has **no** say on Money Bills beyond 14 days. Contrast the Rajya Sabha in [Ch 22](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md) |
| **Judicial hierarchy** | **Ch 34** and **Ch 35** — the High Court's **wider writ jurisdiction** *(Article 226)*, and the tribunal question after ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)** |
| **Asymmetric federalism** | **Ch 36** — Articles **371–371J**, and the reorganisation of **Jammu & Kashmir in 2019** |
