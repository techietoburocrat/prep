# Chapter 30 — Governor

[← Chapter 29](../Part_III_Central_Government/29_Public_Interest_Litigation.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 31 →](31_Chief_Minister.md)

**Read time ~15 min** · **Articles 153–167, 213, 361** · 🔴 **The highest-yield chapter in Part IV** · Prelims: **2013, 2014, 2018, 2019, 2025** · Mains: **2018, 2022, 2023**

> 📌 **Read this beside [Ch 17 President](../Part_III_Central_Government/17_President.md).** Roughly 70% of the content is identical; the marks are entirely in the 30% that differs — and the biggest difference, **express discretion under Article 163**, is why this office is the most litigated institution in Indian federalism today.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The Governor is the President's counterpart with one decisive addition: express constitutional discretion.** Article 163 says he acts on the Council of Ministers' advice **"except in so far as he is required to exercise his functions in his discretion"** — and Article 163(2) makes **his own view on what is discretionary final and non-justiciable**. Article 74 gives the President no such clause. **That single sentence is the source of every Governor-versus-state-government controversy.**

---

## 📖 The story

Two offices, written a few pages apart, that look almost identical.

The President: appointed by an elaborate electoral college, removable only by impeachment, bound by Article 74 to act on advice.

The Governor: **appointed by the President**, holding office **during his pleasure**, bound by Article 163 to act on advice — **except where he acts in his discretion**.

**Those two differences are not decorative.** An officer who owes his appointment to the Centre, can be removed by the Centre at any moment without cause, and possesses a discretion whose scope he himself defines, is structurally something other than a ceremonial head of state.

> 💡 **The analogy:** the President is like a **chairman appointed by the shareholders for a fixed term** — largely ceremonial, but secure, and therefore genuinely neutral. The Governor is like a **branch head posted by head office and recallable at a week's notice**, who also holds a discretionary override he can invoke on his own assessment. **Even a scrupulous person in that position is not structurally neutral** — and the office does not depend on scruples.

**The framers knew.** In the Constituent Assembly there was a serious proposal for an **elected Governor**, rejected on the ground that an elected Governor would rival the Chief Minister and destabilise the state. **The Canadian model — a centrally appointed Lieutenant-Governor — was chosen instead.**

**The cost has been paid ever since.** The Governor's office sits at the centre of Article 356 dismissals ([Ch 16](../Part_II_System_of_Government/16_Emergency_Provisions.md)), floor-test controversies, and — most recently — the practice of **sitting on state bills indefinitely**, because **Article 200 prescribes no time limit at all**.

## PART A — Appointment and conditions

### ⭐ The differences from the President — start here

| | **President** *(Ch 17)* | **Governor** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **How chosen** | **Elected** by an electoral college of MPs and MLAs | ⭐ **APPOINTED by the President**, by warrant under his hand and seal — **not elected** |
| ⭐ **Tenure** | 5 years; removable **only by impeachment** | 5 years, ⭐ **but holds office DURING THE PLEASURE OF THE PRESIDENT** — no security of tenure |
| ⭐ **Minimum age** | **35** | **35** *(same)* |
| **Qualified for election to** | The **Lok Sabha** | ⚠️ **No such requirement** — only citizenship and age |
| ⭐ **Discretion** | ⚠️ **No express discretion clause** | ⭐ **Article 163 — express discretion, and Article 163(2) makes his own view FINAL** |
| ⭐ **Pardon** | ⭐ Can pardon a **death sentence**; has power in **court martial** cases | ⚠️ ⭐ **CANNOT pardon a death sentence**; ⭐ **no power in court martial cases** |
| **Oath administered by** | The **Chief Justice of India** | ⭐ The **Chief Justice of the High Court** of that state |
| **Resignation to** | The **Vice-President** | The **President** |

### The mechanics

- **Article 153:** *"There shall be a Governor for each State."* ⭐ The **7th Amendment Act, 1956** added that **the same person may be appointed Governor of two or more states**.
- ⭐ He is **neither directly elected nor indirectly elected** by an electoral college. He is a **nominee of the Central government** — which the Supreme Court has held does not make him an employee or servant of it.
- **Qualifications:** ⭐ only two — a **citizen of India**, and **completed 35 years of age**.

### ⚠️ The two conventions — and their fate

| Convention | Status |
|---|---|
| ⭐ He should be an **outsider** — not belonging to the state where he is appointed | **Frequently violated** |
| ⭐ The **President should consult the Chief Minister** of the state before appointing | **Frequently violated** |

> ⭐ **These two conventions, plus Sarkaria's formula — "an eminent person from outside the state, detached from local politics"** — are the reform package for this office. *(See [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md), and [Prelims 2019 Q10](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2019.md), which asked who recommended a detached Governor.)*

### Conditions of office

- ⚠️ **Must not be a member of Parliament or of a state legislature** — if appointed, he **vacates that seat** on the date he enters office.
- Must **not hold any office of profit**.
- Entitled to emoluments, allowances and privileges determined by **Parliament**; ⭐ **his emoluments and allowances CANNOT be diminished during his term**.
- ⭐ Where the **same person is Governor of two or more states**, the emoluments are **shared between the states** in a proportion the **President** determines.

### ⭐ Immunities — Article 361 *(identical to the President's)*

1. He is **not answerable to any court** for the exercise and performance of the powers and duties of his office.
2. ⭐ **No criminal proceedings** shall be instituted or continued against him **during his term of office**.
3. **No process for his arrest or imprisonment** shall issue during his term.
4. **Civil proceedings** for acts done in his **personal capacity** may be instituted only after **two months' notice**.

> ⭐ **Note the limit that is always tested:** the immunity is **personal**. ⚠️ **His official ACTIONS remain judicially reviewable** — it is the person, not the decision, that is protected. *(Tested in [Prelims 2018 Q1](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2018.md) and [Prelims 2025 Q8](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2025.md).)*

## PART B — Powers ⭐

*Only the differences from [Ch 17](../Part_III_Central_Government/17_President.md) are marked. Everything unmarked mirrors the President.*

### ① Executive powers

- All **executive action of the state government** is formally taken **in his name**.
- **Appoints** the **Chief Minister and other ministers**, who hold office during his pleasure.
- Appoints the ⭐ **Advocate General**, and determines his remuneration.
- Appoints the **State Election Commissioner** — ⭐ who can be **removed only in the manner and on the grounds of a High Court judge**.
- Appoints the **Chairman and members of the State Public Service Commission** — ⚠️ ⭐ **but they can be removed only by the PRESIDENT**, not by him.
- ⚠️ ⭐ **He does NOT appoint the judges of the High Court.** The **President** appoints them; the Governor is only **consulted**. *(A very common trap — see [Prelims 2013 Q9](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2013.md).)*
- Acts as ⭐ **Chancellor of the universities** in the state and appoints their Vice-Chancellors — ⚠️ **a statutory, not constitutional, role**, and currently a major flashpoint between Governors and state governments.
- ⭐ **Can recommend the imposition of President's Rule** under Article 356; during President's Rule he enjoys **extensive executive powers as the President's agent** ([Ch 16](../Part_II_System_of_Government/16_Emergency_Provisions.md)).

### ② Legislative powers

- He is **a part of the state legislature**, though not a member of the House.
- **Summons, prorogues** and can **dissolve the Legislative Assembly**.
- **Addresses** the legislature at the first session after each general election and the first session of each year.
- ⭐ **Nominates one-sixth of the members of the Legislative Council** from persons with special knowledge or practical experience in ⭐ **literature, science, art, the co-operative movement and social service**.
  > ⚠️ **Note the difference from the President**, who nominates **12** to the Rajya Sabha from **literature, science, art and social service** — the state list adds **"the co-operative movement."**
- ⚠️ ⭐ The power to nominate **one Anglo-Indian member** to the Legislative Assembly was **ABOLISHED by the 104th Amendment Act, 2019**.
- Decides on the **disqualification of members** of the state legislature, in consultation with the **Election Commission**.

### ⭐⭐ ③ Assent to bills — Article 200, the live controversy

When a bill passed by the state legislature is presented to him, the Governor has ⭐ **four options**:

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Give assent** | The bill becomes an Act |
| **Withhold assent** | The bill ends |
| **Return the bill for reconsideration** *(⚠️ not if it is a Money Bill)* | ⭐ **If the House passes it again, with or without amendments, the Governor MUST give assent** |
| ⭐ **Reserve the bill for the President's consideration** | It then goes to Article 201 — and ⭐ **the President may withhold assent even after the state legislature re-passes it**, giving an **absolute veto** |

**When reservation is compulsory:** ⭐ where the bill **endangers the position of the High Court** — the Governor **must** reserve it. He **may** also reserve a bill that is ultra vires, against the DPSP, against the larger interest of the country, of grave national importance, or dealing with compulsory acquisition of property under Article 31A.

> ⭐⭐ **The controversy that defines this office today.** ⚠️ **Article 200 prescribes NO TIME LIMIT** within which the Governor must act. Governors in several states have kept bills pending for **months and years**, effectively exercising a **pocket veto** the Constitution never granted them.
>
> - In ***State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary to the Governor* (2023)**, the Supreme Court held that a Governor **cannot sit indefinitely** on bills — the expression *"as soon as possible"* in the first proviso to Article 200 carries **constitutional content**, and inaction is not an option.
> - In **2025** the Court went further in the **Tamil Nadu Governor case**, prescribing **outer time limits** for the Governor and the President to act, and treating prolonged inaction as capable of resulting in **"deemed assent."** A **Presidential Reference under Article 143** was subsequently made on these questions.
>
> ⚠️ **This is live litigation.** State the 2023 principle firmly; treat the 2025 timelines and the Reference as **evolving**, and **check the current position before your exam** — this is exactly the sort of area where a stale answer costs marks.

### ④ Ordinance-making power — Article 213 ⭐

Mirrors Article 123 ([Ch 17](../Part_III_Central_Government/17_President.md)), with **one crucial addition**:

| Feature | Rule |
|---|---|
| **When** | When the **legislature is not in session** *(or, where bicameral, when both Houses are not in session)* and immediate action is necessary |
| **Force** | ⭐ Same force and effect as an **Act of the state legislature** |
| **Duration** | Ceases **six weeks** from the reassembly of the legislature; **maximum life 6 months + 6 weeks** |
| ⭐⭐ **The extra condition** | ⚠️ **The Governor CANNOT promulgate an ordinance without the PRESIDENT'S INSTRUCTIONS** in three cases: **(a)** if a **bill containing the same provisions would have required the President's previous sanction** for introduction; **(b)** if he would have deemed it necessary to **reserve a bill containing the same provisions** for the President; **(c)** if an **Act containing the same provisions would have been invalid without the President's assent** |

> ⭐ **That third row is the whole difference from the President's ordinance power, and it is exactly what [Mains 2022 Q12](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2022.md) asked** — *"the essential conditions for exercise of the legislative powers by the Governor"*, together with **the legality of re-promulgation**.
>
> **On re-promulgation:** ***D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar* (1987)** — Bihar had re-promulgated ordinances for years; the Court called it a **"fraud on the Constitution."** ***Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar* (2017)**, a seven-judge bench, held **re-promulgation unconstitutional** and **laying before the legislature mandatory**.

### ⑤ Financial powers

- **Money bills** can be introduced in the state legislature **only with his prior recommendation**.
- **No demand for a grant** can be made except on his recommendation.
- He constitutes a ⭐ **State Finance Commission every five years** to review the financial position of **panchayats and municipalities** *(Articles 243I and 243Y)*.
- The **Contingency Fund of the State** is at his disposal, and he can make advances from it to meet unforeseen expenditure.

> ⭐ **The trap:** ⚠️ **It is the GOVERNOR — not the Chief Minister — who constitutes the State Finance Commission.** This was tested directly in [Prelims 2025 Q13](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2025.md), where the statement attributing it to the Chief Minister was one of three incorrect statements about Panchayats.

### ⭐ ⑥ Judicial powers — Article 161

The Governor may grant **pardons, reprieves, respites and remissions** of punishment, or **suspend, remit or commute** the sentence of any person convicted of an offence **against a law relating to a matter to which the executive power of the state extends**.

⚠️ **Two things he CANNOT do, which the President can:**

| | **President (Art 72)** | **Governor (Art 161)** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Death sentence** | ⭐ **Can PARDON** | ⚠️ ⭐ **CANNOT pardon** — but **can suspend, remit or commute** it |
| ⭐ **Court martial** | ⭐ **Has power** | ⚠️ ⭐ **NO power whatsoever** |

> ⭐ **This comparison is one of the most reliable Prelims questions in the entire book.** The Governor's clemency power is **narrower in two specific ways**, and both are asked.

## PART C — ⭐⭐ The constitutional position: Article 163

**This is the heart of the chapter.**

> **Article 163(1):** *"There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Chief Minister at the head to aid and advise the Governor in the exercise of his functions, **except in so far as he is by or under this Constitution required to exercise his functions or any of them in his discretion**."*
>
> ⭐ **Article 163(2):** *"If any question arises whether any matter is or is not a matter as respects which the Governor is by or under this Constitution required to act in his discretion, **the decision of the Governor in his discretion shall be final**, and the validity of anything done by the Governor shall not be called in question on the ground that he ought or ought not to have acted in his discretion."*

⭐ **Compare Article 74**, which after the 42nd and 44th Amendments makes the President's compliance with advice **binding**, with only **one reconsideration**. ⚠️ **There is no equivalent of Article 163's discretion clause for the President.**

### ⭐ The two kinds of discretion

**① Constitutional discretion — expressly provided:**
1. ⭐ **Reservation of a bill for the President's consideration** *(Article 200)*
2. ⭐ **Recommendation for the imposition of President's Rule** *(Article 356)*
3. Exercising functions as **administrator of an adjoining Union Territory** *(where he holds additional charge)*
4. ⭐ Determining the amount payable by **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram** to an **autonomous Tribal District Council** as royalty from licences for mineral exploration
5. **Seeking information** from the Chief Minister regarding administrative and legislative matters *(Article 167)*

**② Situational discretion — implied by circumstance:**
1. ⭐ **Appointment of the Chief Minister** when no party has a clear majority, or when a sitting CM dies suddenly with no obvious successor
2. ⭐ **Dismissal of the Council of Ministers** when it **cannot prove the confidence** of the Legislative Assembly
3. ⭐ **Dissolution of the Legislative Assembly** when the Council of Ministers has lost its majority

### ⭐ Special responsibilities — Article 371 *(individual judgement)*

In certain states the Governor has **special responsibilities**, discharged in his **individual judgement** — a wider power still. *(Detail in [Ch 36](36_Special_Provisions.md).)*

**Maharashtra** *(Vidarbha and Marathwada)* · **Gujarat** *(Saurashtra and Kutch)* · **Nagaland** *(371A — law and order while internal disturbance continues)* · **Assam** *(371B)* · **Manipur** *(371C)* · **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana** *(371D)* · **Sikkim** *(371F)* · **Arunachal Pradesh** *(371H — law and order)*.

### ⚠️ What the courts have settled

| Case | Held |
|---|---|
| ***Shamsher Singh* (1974)** | ⭐ The Governor exercises his powers **on the aid and advice** of the Council of Ministers, **except in the few well-known exceptional situations** — he is a **constitutional head**, not a parallel executive |
| ⭐ ***S.R. Bommai* (1994)** | ⭐ **Majority must be tested on the FLOOR OF THE HOUSE**, not determined by the Governor's subjective satisfaction; Article 356 is **justiciable** ([Ch 16](../Part_II_System_of_Government/16_Emergency_Provisions.md)) |
| ***Rameshwar Prasad* (2006)** | Dissolution of the **Bihar Assembly** on the Governor's report of anticipated horse-trading was **unconstitutional** |
| ⭐ ***B.P. Singhal v. Union of India* (2010)** | Though the Governor holds office at pleasure and **no reasons need be assigned**, ⭐ **he cannot be removed arbitrarily, capriciously, or merely because the government at the Centre has changed** or because his ideology differs from the new ruling party |
| ***Nabam Rebia* (2016)** | ⭐ The Governor **cannot summon, prorogue or advance a session against the advice** of the Council of Ministers; his discretion is **limited, not unlimited** |
| ***State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary to the Governor* (2023)** | ⭐ A Governor **cannot indefinitely withhold action** on bills |

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The four differences from the President
> ⭐ **APPOINTED, not elected** · ⭐ **holds office at the President's PLEASURE** · ⭐ **has EXPRESS DISCRETION (Art 163), and his own view on it is FINAL** · ⭐ **CANNOT pardon a death sentence, and has NO court-martial power**.

### The article block
> **153** the office · **155** appointment · **156** term and pleasure · **161** pardon · ⭐ **163** aid, advice **and discretion** · **164** ministers · **167** CM's duties · **200/201** assent and reservation · ⭐ **213** ordinance *(with the President's-instruction condition)* · **361** immunity.

### Nomination to the Legislative Council
> ⭐ **One-sixth**, from **literature, science, art, the CO-OPERATIVE MOVEMENT and social service.** *(The President nominates **12** to the Rajya Sabha, and the co-operative movement is **not** on that list.)*

### The Article 213 extra condition
> ⭐ **The Governor needs the President's INSTRUCTIONS** where a corresponding bill would have needed the **President's previous sanction**, would have been **reserved**, or would have been **invalid without the President's assent**.

### The appointments trap
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The Governor does NOT appoint High Court judges — the President does.** The Governor appoints the **Advocate General**, the **State Election Commissioner** and the **State PSC** members — ⚠️ **but SPSC members can be removed only by the PRESIDENT.**

### The Finance Commission trap
> ⚠️ ⭐ **The GOVERNOR — not the Chief Minister — constitutes the State Finance Commission** *(Articles 243I and 243Y)*.

### The cases
> ***Shamsher Singh* 1974** constitutional head · ⭐ ***Bommai* 1994** **floor test** · ***Rameshwar Prasad* 2006** Bihar · ⭐ ***B.P. Singhal* 2010** **no arbitrary removal** · ***Nabam Rebia* 2016** discretion limited · ***Punjab* 2023** no indefinite delay.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Discuss the essential conditions for exercise of the legislative powers by the Governor. Discuss the legality of re-promulgation of ordinances by the Governor without placing them before the Legislature." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2022 Q12](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2022.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The Governor's legislative powers** — part of the legislature; summoning, proroguing, dissolving; addressing; nominating one-sixth of the Council; assent under **Article 200** with its four options; and **Article 213** ordinances. (ii) ⭐ **The essential conditions for Article 213** — the legislature **not in session**, **satisfaction that immediate action is necessary**, the subject must be within the **state legislature's competence**, and ⭐ **the President's instructions** in the three specified situations. (iii) **Re-promulgation** — ***Wadhwa* (1987)** called it a **"fraud on the Constitution"**; ***Krishna Kumar Singh* (2017)**, seven judges, held it **unconstitutional** and **laying mandatory**, and that an ordinance creates no enduring rights once it lapses. (iv) Conclude: the ordinance is an **emergency power, not an alternative legislature** — the constitutional defect is not the ordinance but its **repetition**.

**2. "Discuss the role of the Governor in the Indian federal system. Has the office lived up to the framers' intentions?" (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The design** — a **constitutional head** on the Canadian model, plus a **link with the Centre**. (ii) **Where it has strained** — appointment of politically active persons; the two conventions *(outsider, consult the CM)* routinely ignored; **removal at pleasure** on changes of government at the Centre; **Article 356 recommendations**; **indefinite delay under Article 200**; the **Chancellor** role in state universities. (iii) **The corrective jurisprudence** — *Shamsher Singh*, ⭐ *Bommai* **floor test**, *Rameshwar Prasad*, ⭐ *B.P. Singhal* **no arbitrary removal**, *Nabam Rebia*, *Punjab* (2023). (iv) **Reforms** — **Sarkaria**: an eminent outsider, detached from politics, appointed after consulting the CM; **Punchhi**: a **fixed five-year tenure**, removal only by a formal process, **no post-retirement office**, and ⭐ **time limits on assent**. Conclude: the office was designed as a **bridge** and has too often functioned as an **instrument** — and the remedy is **procedural certainty**, chiefly a **statutory time limit under Article 200**.

**3. "Account for the legal and political factors responsible for the reduced frequency of using Article 356 by the Union Governments since the mid-1990s." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2023 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md) — see the fuller skeleton in [Ch 16](../Part_II_System_of_Government/16_Emergency_Provisions.md).* The Governor's **report** is the trigger, so this chapter supplies half the answer.

**4. "Whether the Supreme Court Judgement (July 2018) can settle the political tussle between the Lt. Governor and elected government of Delhi? Examine." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2018 Q11](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2018.md).*
> *Skeleton:* Delhi's **special status under Article 239AA**; the Lt. Governor is **not a Governor** but an **Administrator** with wider powers; the 2018 judgment held the LG is **bound by the aid and advice** of the Council of Ministers except on **police, public order and land**, has **no independent decision-making power**, and cannot refer **every** matter to the President. Then the limits: the judgment settled **principle** but not **allocation of services**, which required further litigation and legislation. Conclude on the structural problem — **an elected government without control over its own administration** is the unresolved question.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Four differences from the President: APPOINTED not elected · holds office at the President's PLEASURE · has EXPRESS DISCRETION under Article 163 with his own view FINAL · cannot pardon a death sentence and has no court-martial power.**
- **Article 153**; ⭐ **7th Amendment (1956)** — one person may be Governor of **two or more states**. **Qualifications: citizen + 35 years only.** ⚠️ **Two conventions — outsider, and consult the CM — both frequently violated.**
- **Term five years, but "during the pleasure of the President."** Emoluments ⭐ **cannot be diminished during the term**. Oath by the ⭐ **Chief Justice of the High Court**. Resigns to the **President**.
- **Article 361 immunity:** not answerable to any court; **no criminal proceedings during term**; no arrest; civil proceedings after **two months' notice**. ⚠️ **His ACTIONS remain reviewable.**
- **Appoints:** CM and ministers · **Advocate General** · **State Election Commissioner** *(removable like a HC judge)* · **SPSC members** ⚠️ *(removable only by the **President**)*. ⚠️ ⭐ **Does NOT appoint High Court judges.** Acts as **Chancellor** of state universities *(statutory)*.
- **Legislative:** part of the legislature; ⭐ **nominates one-sixth of the Legislative Council** from **literature, science, art, the CO-OPERATIVE MOVEMENT and social service**; ⚠️ **Anglo-Indian nomination abolished — 104th Amendment, 2019**.
- ⭐ **Article 200 — four options: assent · withhold · return *(not a Money Bill)* · RESERVE for the President.** If returned and re-passed, **he must assent**; but under **Article 201** the **President may still withhold**. ⚠️ **Must reserve a bill endangering the High Court's position.** ⚠️ ⭐ **No time limit prescribed** — *Punjab* **(2023)**: he cannot sit indefinitely; **2025** Tamil Nadu case set timelines and raised **"deemed assent"**, followed by a **Presidential Reference** — **live litigation, verify before the exam**.
- ⭐ **Article 213 ordinance** — same as Article 123, **plus** the requirement of the **President's INSTRUCTIONS** in three cases. **Re-promulgation:** *Wadhwa* **(1987)** "fraud on the Constitution"; *Krishna Kumar Singh* **(2017)** unconstitutional, laying mandatory.
- **Financial:** money bills and demands for grants need his **prior recommendation**; ⭐ **he constitutes the State Finance Commission every five years** *(243I / 243Y)* — ⚠️ **not the Chief Minister**; Contingency Fund of the State at his disposal.
- ⭐ **Article 161 pardon:** ⚠️ **cannot pardon a death sentence** *(but can suspend, remit or commute it)*; ⚠️ **no court-martial power**.
- ⭐ **Article 163:** aid and advice **except** in his discretion; **163(2)** makes his own view **final and non-justiciable**. **Constitutional discretion:** reservation of bills · President's Rule recommendation · adjoining UT administration · **tribal district council royalties (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram)** · seeking information from the CM. **Situational discretion:** appointing a CM in a hung House · dismissal · dissolution.
- **Cases:** *Shamsher Singh* (1974) constitutional head · ⭐ *Bommai* (1994) **floor test** · *Rameshwar Prasad* (2006) · ⭐ *B.P. Singhal* (2010) **no arbitrary removal** · *Nabam Rebia* (2016) · *Punjab* (2023).
