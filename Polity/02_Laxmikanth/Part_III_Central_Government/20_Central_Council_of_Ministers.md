# Chapter 20 — Central Council of Ministers

[← Chapter 19](19_Prime_Minister.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 21 →](21_Cabinet_Committees.md)

**Read time ~9 min** · **Articles 74, 75, 77, 78, 88** · 🟠 Prelims: the **Cabinet vs Council** distinction is a recurring trap · Mains: **2014, 2024**

> 📌 **The one thing to get right here is that "Council of Ministers" and "Cabinet" are NOT the same body.** The Constitution mentions the Council; the word *Cabinet* was inserted only in **1978**. Nearly every Prelims question on this chapter turns on that distinction.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The Council of Ministers is the constitutional body; the Cabinet is the body that actually governs.** The Council has 60–70 members and has effectively **never met as a whole**; the Cabinet is 15–20 senior ministers who take every real decision. The Constitution vests power in the larger body and reality vests it in the smaller one — and the smaller one is answerable through the larger.

---

## 📖 The story

Article 74 creates a **Council of Ministers**. It does not create a Cabinet.

Yet everything you read about Indian government talks about Cabinet decisions, Cabinet meetings, Cabinet reshuffles. Where did the Cabinet come from?

**It came from practice, and the Constitution caught up 28 years later.** The word *Cabinet* was inserted into **Article 352** by the **44th Amendment Act, 1978** — and even then only to specify **whose written recommendation the President needs to declare an Emergency** (a direct response to 1975, when the Prime Minister advised alone). That is the **only** place the Constitution mentions it.

> 💡 **The analogy:** the Council of Ministers is the **full board of a company**; the Cabinet is its **executive committee**. The full board is where authority formally vests and to whom everyone is accountable — but it is too large to run anything, so a small committee meets weekly and decides. The board's role becomes ratification and, crucially, **shared liability**: when the executive committee's decision goes wrong, the **whole board** answers for it.
>
> That last part is **collective responsibility** — and it is why the distinction matters rather than being mere terminology.

## PART A — Composition ⭐

### The three (sometimes four) ranks

| Rank | What they are |
|---|---|
| **Cabinet Ministers** | Head the **important ministries** — Home, Defence, Finance, External Affairs and so on. They are **members of the Cabinet**, attend its meetings and play a decisive role in policy |
| **Ministers of State** | Either given **independent charge** of a ministry/department, or **attached to Cabinet Ministers**. ⚠️ **They are NOT members of the Cabinet** and attend its meetings **only when specially invited** |
| **Deputy Ministers** | ⚠️ **Not given independent charge**. Attached to Cabinet Ministers or Ministers of State to assist them. **Not members of the Cabinet** |
| *Parliamentary Secretaries* | The **last category**, with no department of their own; attached to senior ministers to assist them in parliamentary duties. *(No Parliamentary Secretaries have been appointed at the Centre since 1967.)* |

> ⚠️ **A "Deputy Prime Minister" is appointed occasionally, for political reasons.** The post is **not mentioned in the Constitution** and carries no additional constitutional power.

### ⭐⭐ Council of Ministers vs Cabinet — the table that carries the chapter

| | **Council of Ministers** | **Cabinet** |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | **60–70** ministers | **15–20** ministers |
| **Composition** | **All** three categories — Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers | ⭐ **Only Cabinet Ministers** — a **part** of the Council |
| **Meetings** | ⚠️ **Does not meet as a body** to transact government business. **It has no collective function** | **Meets frequently**, usually once a week, to take decisions |
| **Constitutional mention** | **Article 74** — described in detail | ⭐ Inserted only by the **44th Amendment (1978)** into **Article 352**; the Constitution **does not otherwise describe it** |
| **Functions** | ⭐ **Vested with all powers** — but **in theory** | ⭐ **Exercises, in practice, the powers of the Council**, and acts for it |
| **Role** | Its collective functions are **enforced through collective responsibility** | ⭐ **Directs the Council** by taking policy decisions that are **binding on all ministers** |
| **Implementation** | Implements the Cabinet's decisions | Supervises implementation by the Council |

> 🧠 **One line to hold it:** the **Council has the power, the Cabinet uses it, and the Council carries the responsibility.**

## PART B — Appointment and the 91st Amendment ⭐

### Appointment (Article 75)

- The **Prime Minister is appointed by the President**; **other ministers are appointed by the President on the PM's advice**. ⭐ The President can appoint **only those persons the PM recommends**.
- A minister who is **not a member of either House for six consecutive months** ceases to be a minister.
- A minister who is a member of **one House** has the **right to speak and take part in the proceedings of the other House**, but ⭐ **can vote only in the House of which he is a member** *(Article 88)*.

### ⭐ The 91st Amendment Act, 2003 — two changes to memorise

| Change | Provision |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Size cap** | **Article 75(1A)** — the total number of ministers, **including the Prime Minister**, shall **not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha** |
| ⭐ **Defectors barred** | **Article 75(1B)** — a member disqualified on the ground of **defection** shall also be **disqualified to be appointed a minister** |

> **The parallel state provision:** for states, the cap is **15% of the total strength of the Legislative Assembly**, but with a **minimum of 12 ministers** *(Article 164(1A))*.
>
> **Why the amendment happened:** before 2003 there was **no limit at all**, and jumbo ministries were used to buy loyalty — the surest way to keep a defector on side was to make him a minister. The 91st Amendment closed **both halves** of that practice at once: it capped the number of posts available, and it disqualified defectors from holding them. **Note the pairing — it is the sort of paired reform UPSC likes to test.**

**Oath, term and salary:**
- Oath of **office and secrecy**, administered by the **President**.
- Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President** — which in practice means **so long as the Prime Minister wants them**.
- **Salaries and allowances** are determined by **Parliament**.

## PART C — Responsibility ⭐

### ① Collective responsibility — Article 75(3)

> **"The Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People."**

**This is the bedrock principle of parliamentary government.** Three consequences:

1. ⭐ **They swim or sink together.** A no-confidence motion carried against the Council — or even against **one minister** — means the **entire Council must resign**, including ministers drawn from the **Rajya Sabha**, who are not members of the House that voted them out.
2. **Cabinet decisions bind every minister**, whether or not he attended the meeting or agreed. A minister who **cannot defend a decision publicly must resign**. *"They sink or swim together."*
3. The Council can be removed by the Lok Sabha, and the Council can advise dissolution of the Lok Sabha — the accountability runs both ways.

### ② Individual responsibility — Article 75(2)

> **"The Ministers shall hold office during the pleasure of the President."**

The President may **remove a minister even when the Council enjoys the Lok Sabha's confidence** — but ⭐ **only on the advice of the Prime Minister**. This is the PM's real disciplinary instrument over his colleagues.

### ③ ⚠️ No legal responsibility

⭐ **There is no provision in the Constitution for the system of legal responsibility of a minister.**

**In Britain**, every order of the Head of State for a public act must be **countersigned by a minister**, who is then legally answerable for it in court.

**In India**, there is **no requirement of countersignature**, and **the courts are barred from enquiring into the nature of the advice** tendered by ministers to the President *(Article 74(2))*.

> ⭐ **Article 74(2) is heavily tested:** *"the question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the President **shall not be inquired into in any court**."* The **advice** is non-justiciable — but the **action taken** on it remains reviewable. Keep those two apart.

### Article 77 — conduct of business

- All executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken **in the name of the President**.
- Orders and instruments made in his name must be **authenticated** in the manner specified by rules he makes — ⭐ and **their validity cannot be questioned on the ground that they are not orders made by the President**.
- The President makes rules for the **more convenient transaction of business** and for the **allocation of business among ministers**.

## PART D — The Kitchen Cabinet

An **informal body** consisting of the **Prime Minister and two to four influential colleagues** in whom he has faith and with whom he discusses every problem.

| Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|
| **Smaller** — a more efficient decision-making body than a large Cabinet | ⚠️ **Reduces the authority and status** of the Cabinet as the highest decision-making body |
| Can meet **more often** and deal with business **more expeditiously** | ⚠️ **Circumvents the legal process** by allowing decisions to be taken by persons outside it |
| Helps the PM **maintain secrecy** in making important decisions | ⚠️ May include **non-ministers** — friends, family members and outside advisers with no accountability |

> ⚠️ **The extra-constitutional problem:** the Kitchen Cabinet can include people who **are not ministers at all**, and who therefore bear **no collective responsibility** to the Lok Sabha. Decisions get made by people the House cannot remove — which is the sharpest argument in any answer on the decline of Cabinet government. *(See [Ch 19](19_Prime_Minister.md).)*

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The five articles
> **74** Council with the PM at the head, advice binding · **75** appointment, **15% cap**, **collective responsibility** · **77** business conducted in the President's name · **78** the PM's duties to the President · **88** ministers may **speak in either House, vote only in their own**.

### ⭐ Council vs Cabinet
> **Council: 60–70 · all three ranks · never meets · Article 74 · has the power in theory.**
> **Cabinet: 15–20 · Cabinet Ministers only · meets weekly · mentioned only in Article 352 via the 44th Amendment · exercises the power in practice.**

### The 91st Amendment (2003) — the paired reform
> ⭐ **Cap: 15% of the Lok Sabha, including the PM** *(states: 15% of the Assembly, minimum 12)* **AND a defector cannot be made a minister.** Two halves of one problem.

### The three responsibilities
> **Collective (75(3))** to the **Lok Sabha** — all resign together.
> **Individual (75(2))** — pleasure of the President, exercised **on the PM's advice**.
> ⚠️ **Legal — none.** No countersignature; **Article 74(2)** bars courts from enquiring into the **advice** *(but not the action)*.

### The six-month rule
> A non-member may be a minister for **six months**. Applies to the **PM too** *(Narasimha Rao, 1991)*.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The size of the cabinet should be as big as governmental work justifies and as big as the Prime Minister can manage as a team. How far is the efficacy of a government then inversely related to the size of the cabinet?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2014 Q6](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2014.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The constitutional position** — no cap before 2003; the **91st Amendment** capped it at **15%** of the Lok Sabha, precisely because jumbo ministries had become instruments of patronage. (ii) **The case that efficacy falls as size rises** — coordination costs; overlapping ministries; dilution of collective deliberation; ministries created for political accommodation rather than function; the drift of real decisions to the **Cabinet Committees** and the **Kitchen Cabinet**, which is itself evidence that the full body is too large to decide. (iii) **The case against a simple inverse relation** — India's scale and diversity need **representational breadth** (region, community, alliance partner); specialised ministries can improve delivery; **efficacy depends more on portfolio design and the PM's coordination than on headcount**. (iv) Conclude: size matters **through the quality of coordination**, not directly; the 15% cap addressed **patronage**, not efficiency, and the real lever is **rational allocation of business** under Article 77.

**2. "Distinguish between the Council of Ministers and the Cabinet. Is the distinction merely one of terminology?" (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Reproduce the Part A table. Then answer the "merely terminology" hook: **no** — the distinction carries real constitutional weight, because **power is vested in the Council but exercised by the Cabinet**, and **responsibility under Article 75(3) attaches to the Council**. So the body that decides is **not** the body that formally answers — and that gap is exactly what collective responsibility is designed to close.

**3. Examine the significance of the 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003. (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Both limbs — the **15% cap** (Articles 75(1A) and 164(1A), with the states' minimum of 12) and the **bar on defectors becoming ministers** (Article 75(1B)). Explain the shared purpose: cutting off ministerial office as the **currency of defection**. Link to the **Tenth Schedule**. Assess: it curbed jumbo ministries, but defection persists through **mass splits** and resignation-and-re-election, so the amendment treated a symptom.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- **Articles 74, 75, 77, 78, 88.** Article 74: Council of Ministers **with the PM at the head**; advice **binding** (42nd), with **one reconsideration** (44th).
- ⭐ **Council ≠ Cabinet.** Council **60–70**, all three ranks, **never meets as a body**, described in **Article 74**, has the powers **in theory**. Cabinet **15–20**, **Cabinet Ministers only**, meets **weekly**, mentioned in the Constitution **only in Article 352 via the 44th Amendment (1978)**, exercises the powers **in practice**.
- **Ranks:** Cabinet Ministers *(in the Cabinet)* · **Ministers of State** *(independent charge or attached — **not** in the Cabinet)* · **Deputy Ministers** *(no independent charge)* · Parliamentary Secretaries *(none at the Centre since 1967)*. **Deputy PM is not a constitutional post.**
- **Appointment:** President appoints on the **PM's advice** — and **only** those the PM recommends. **Six-month rule** for non-members. **Article 88** — speak in either House, **vote only in your own**.
- ⭐ **91st Amendment, 2003:** **Article 75(1A)** — ministers including the PM **≤ 15% of the Lok Sabha**; **Article 164(1A)** for states, **minimum 12**. **Article 75(1B)** — a **defector cannot be appointed a minister**.
- **Responsibility:** **collective** to the **Lok Sabha** *(75(3))* — one falls, all fall, including Rajya Sabha ministers; **individual** *(75(2))* — pleasure of the President, exercised **on the PM's advice**; ⚠️ **legal responsibility does not exist** — no countersignature, and **Article 74(2)** bars courts from enquiring into the **advice** *(the action remains reviewable)*.
- **Article 77:** business in the **President's name**; authentication rules mean validity **cannot be challenged** on the ground that the order was not made by the President personally.
- **Kitchen Cabinet:** PM + 2–4 trusted colleagues, may include **non-ministers**. Efficient and secret — but **bypasses the Cabinet** and places decisions with people who bear **no responsibility to the House**.
