# Chapter 19 — Prime Minister

**Read time ~8 min** · **Articles 74, 75, 78** · 🟠 Prelims: usually via the parliamentary-system route · Mains: **2002, 2024**

> 📌 **Read this chapter together with [Ch 17](17_President.md) and [Ch 20](20_Central_Council_of_Ministers.md).** They are three views of one arrangement: where authority formally sits (President), who actually decides (Prime Minister), and who is collectively answerable (Council of Ministers).

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The Constitution says almost nothing about the Prime Minister — and that silence is the point.** Article 75 gives one sentence on appointment; Article 78 lists three duties. Everything else about the most powerful office in India comes from **convention and political reality**, which is exactly why its power has grown so far beyond the text.

---

## 📖 The story

Open the Constitution looking for the Prime Minister's powers and you will find a surprisingly thin file. **Article 74** says there shall be a Council of Ministers **with the Prime Minister at the head**. **Article 75** says the President appoints him. **Article 78** gives him three duties towards the President. That is essentially it.

Compare that with the President, who gets **Articles 52 to 62 and 72 and 123 and 143** — pages of detail for an office that decides very little.

**The inversion is deliberate.** The framers were writing down what could be written down: the formal architecture. The Prime Minister's power does not come from the text — it comes from the fact that **he is the person the majority in the Lok Sabha follows.** You cannot draft that.

> 💡 **The analogy:** the Constitution describes the **wiring** of the house — where the sockets are, what voltage runs through them. The Prime Minister is **whoever holds the plug**. The wiring diagram tells you almost nothing about how much electricity that person actually commands, because that depends on something outside the diagram: the size and discipline of his majority.

**And that is why the same office has felt so different at different times.** Nehru with an overwhelming majority; the fragile coalitions of 1996–1998 where a Prime Minister could be unseated by a single ally; and majority governments since. **Same articles, radically different offices** — which is the substance of [Mains 2024 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md) on cabinet growth marginalising parliamentary supremacy.

## PART A — Appointment

**Article 75(1):** *"The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President."*

That is the whole procedure. The Constitution prescribes **no method** of selection.

**The convention:** the President appoints the **leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha**.

### ⭐ Where the President's discretion enters

| Situation | What the President does |
|---|---|
| **A party has a clear majority** | No discretion — he **must** appoint its leader |
| ⭐ **No party has a clear majority** | He exercises **personal discretion** in selecting and appointing the PM — usually inviting the leader of the **largest party or pre-poll alliance**, and requiring a **floor test** |
| **A sitting PM dies in office with no obvious successor** | He may exercise discretion; but if the ruling party **elects a new leader**, the President has no choice but to appoint him |

> This is the **most important discretionary power in the Constitution**, and it is the same one discussed in [Ch 17](17_President.md). It has decided who governed India in **1979, 1989, 1996, 1998 and 1999**.

### ⭐ Two facts that are constantly tested

1. **A person who is not a member of either House can be appointed Prime Minister** — but **must become a member of either House within six months**, or cease to be PM. *(Article 75(5).)*
   > **The example:** **P.V. Narasimha Rao** became Prime Minister in **1991** without being a member of either House, and was later elected from **Nandyal**.
2. ⭐ **The PM may belong to EITHER House of Parliament.**
   > **The example:** **Manmohan Singh** was Prime Minister for ten years (**2004–2014**) as a member of the **Rajya Sabha**.
   > ⚠️ **In Britain, by convention, the Prime Minister must be a member of the House of Commons.** That contrast is a standard Prelims trap — see [Ch 12](../Part_II_System_of_Government/12_Parliamentary_System.md).

**Oath, term and salary:**
- Oath of **office and secrecy**, administered by the **President**.
- ⚠️ **Term is not fixed.** He holds office **during the pleasure of the President** — but this does **not** mean the President can dismiss him at will. **So long as he enjoys the confidence of the Lok Sabha, he cannot be dismissed.** If he loses that confidence, he must resign or be dismissed.
- **Salary and allowances** are determined by **Parliament** from time to time.

## PART B — Powers and functions ⭐

Group them into four relationships.

### ① In relation to the Council of Ministers *(the real source of his power)*

| Power | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Recommends who becomes a minister** | ⭐ The President appoints **only those persons as ministers who are recommended by the Prime Minister** |
| **Allocates and reshuffles portfolios** | He decides who gets which ministry, and can change it |
| **Can demand a resignation** | He can ask a minister to resign, or **advise the President to dismiss him**, in case of difference of opinion |
| **Presides over the Cabinet** | He **guides, directs, controls and coordinates** the activities of all ministers |
| ⭐ **Can bring down the entire government** | ⚠️ **By resigning, he dissolves the Council of Ministers** — because the Council exists only with a PM at its head. **No other minister's resignation has this effect** |

> ⭐ **That last row is the whole answer to "why is the PM more than *primus inter pares*?"** Every other minister is dispensable; **the PM's departure kills the government**. The relationship is not symmetrical.

### ② In relation to the President — Article 78 ⭐

**Article 78 lists three duties of the Prime Minister towards the President.** Learn all three:

1. To **communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers** relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation
2. To **furnish such information** relating to the administration of the Union and proposals for legislation **as the President may call for**
3. ⭐ If the President so requires, to **submit for the consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a minister but which has not been considered by the Council**

> **Point 3 is a genuine presidential check hiding in a PM's duty clause** — it lets the President force a single minister's decision back to the whole Cabinet. Cite it whenever asked whether the President is purely ceremonial.

**He also advises the President on the appointment of** the Attorney General, the CAG, the Chairman and members of the UPSC, the Election Commissioners, the Chairman and members of the Finance Commission, and others.

### ③ In relation to Parliament

- He is the **leader of the Lower House**.
- He **advises the President on summoning and proroguing** the sessions of Parliament.
- ⭐ He can **recommend dissolution of the Lok Sabha to the President at any time** — a power that is his alone, and a formidable disciplinary weapon over his own party.
- He **announces government policies** on the floor of the House.

### ④ Other roles

- **Chairman of NITI Aayog**, the Inter-State Council, the National Integration Council, the National Water Resources Council, and the National Development Council *(historically)*.
- Plays a significant role in **shaping India's foreign policy**.
- Chief spokesman of the Union government; **chief crisis manager** at the political level; leader of the party in power; **political head of the services**.

## PART C — What the office is called, and the debate ⭐

### The descriptions worth quoting

| Author | Description |
|---|---|
| **Lord Morley** | *"**Primus inter pares**" — first among equals* — and the **"keystone of the Cabinet arch"** |
| **Sir Ivor Jennings** | *"The **sun** around which planets revolve"* — describing the PM as central to the Cabinet system |
| **H.J. Laski** | The PM is *"central to the formation of the Council of Ministers, central to its life, and central to its death"* |
| **William Munro** | The PM is *"the **captain of the ship of state**"* |
| **Sir William Vernon Harcourt** | *"An **inch of the Prime Minister's territory** is worth a mile of anyone else's"* — i.e. *inter stellas luna minores*, **"a moon among lesser stars"** |

### ⭐ "Cabinet government" or "Prime Ministerial government"?

**The argument that the office has outgrown the Cabinet:**
- The PM alone decides **who is in the Cabinet and what they do**
- **Kitchen Cabinets** — small informal groups of trusted advisers, often including non-ministers — take real decisions outside the Cabinet
- The PMO's growth as a parallel centre of policy
- Increasing use of **ordinances** and **Money Bill routing** to bypass parliamentary scrutiny
- Party discipline plus the **anti-defection law** mean MPs of the ruling party cannot vote against him

**The argument that it is overstated:**
- **Coalition governments (1989–2014)** repeatedly showed a PM constrained by allies — a single party's withdrawal brought down governments in **1990, 1997, 1999**
- **Collective responsibility** under Article 75(3) remains the constitutional position
- Judicial review, federalism and a free press impose real limits
- The 1999 defeat by **one vote** showed the Lok Sabha can still remove a PM

> **The formulation for Mains:** India has moved from **Cabinet government towards Prime Ministerial government**, but the movement is **driven by the party system, not by constitutional change** — the text of Articles 74, 75 and 78 has not altered. **When majorities are large, the office looks prime-ministerial; when they are thin, it looks collegial.**

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The three articles
> **74** — Council of Ministers **with the PM at the head**, to aid and advise · **75** — appointment, six-month rule, collective responsibility · **78** — the **three duties** towards the President.

### The two appointment facts
> **Not a member? Six months** *(Narasimha Rao, 1991, later elected from Nandyal)*. **Either House** *(Manmohan Singh, Rajya Sabha, 2004–14)* — ⚠️ **Britain: Commons only.**

### Article 78's three duties — C-F-S
> **C**ommunicate decisions · **F**urnish information called for · **S**ubmit to the Council any matter decided by a single minister.

### The asymmetry that defines the office
> ⭐ **If a minister resigns, the government continues. If the PM resigns, the government falls.** That is why he is more than *primus inter pares*.

### The quotes
> Morley: **"keystone of the Cabinet arch"** and ***primus inter pares*** · Jennings: **the sun** · Laski: **central to its formation, life and death** · Munro: **captain of the ship of state** · Harcourt: **an inch of his territory is worth a mile of anyone else's**.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The growth of cabinet system has practically resulted in the marginalisation of the parliamentary supremacy." Elucidate. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2024 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The design** — fusion of powers means the executive is drawn from and answerable to Parliament ([Ch 12](../Part_II_System_of_Government/12_Parliamentary_System.md)). (ii) **How the Cabinet came to dominate** — party discipline plus the **anti-defection law** removes the ruling MP's independence; the Cabinet controls the legislative agenda and House time; **ordinances**; **Money Bill routing** to bypass the Rajya Sabha; the guillotine; declining sittings and bills passed without committee referral. (iii) **The shift within the executive too** — from Cabinet government to **Prime Ministerial government**, with Kitchen Cabinets and the PMO. (iv) **The counter** — coalition eras constrained the Cabinet; committees remain effective; judicial review and federalism persist; Parliament removed governments in **1990, 1997, 1999**. (v) Conclude: supremacy has been **marginalised in practice while intact in form**, and the remedies are procedural — guaranteed opposition time, mandatory committee referral, limits on ordinances.

**2. "The issue of a hung Parliament adversely affects the stability of Indian Government." Discuss, and consider whether the Presidential form would be a solution. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2002 Q6(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2002.md) — see the fuller skeleton in [Ch 12](../Part_II_System_of_Government/12_Parliamentary_System.md).*

**3. "The Prime Minister is *primus inter pares* in name only." Critically examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Set up the classical formulation (Morley), then dismantle it with the powers in Part B — recommending ministers, allocating portfolios, demanding resignations, recommending dissolution, and above all the **asymmetry of resignation**. Then restore balance: coalition constraints, collective responsibility, floor accountability. Conclude that the phrase describes a **constitutional relationship**, not a **political one**, and the gap between the two is set by the majority.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- **Articles 74, 75, 78.** The Constitution says **very little** about the office — its power is **conventional and political**, not textual.
- **Appointment:** Article 75(1) — appointed by the **President**, no procedure prescribed. Convention: **leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha**. ⭐ **Real presidential discretion when the House is hung** — 1979, 1989, 1996, 1998, 1999.
- ⭐ **Non-member may be PM for six months** *(Narasimha Rao, 1991)*. ⭐ **May belong to either House** *(Manmohan Singh, Rajya Sabha)* — ⚠️ **Britain requires the Commons.**
- **Holds office during the President's pleasure** — but **cannot be dismissed while he holds the Lok Sabha's confidence**. Salary fixed by **Parliament**.
- **Over the Council:** recommends **every** minister · allocates portfolios · can demand resignation · presides over the Cabinet · ⭐ **his resignation dissolves the whole Council**.
- ⭐ **Article 78 — three duties:** **communicate** all Council decisions · **furnish** information the President calls for · **submit** to the Council any matter decided by a single minister but not considered by it.
- **Over Parliament:** leader of the Lower House · advises on **summoning and proroguing** · ⭐ can **recommend dissolution at any time** · announces policy on the floor.
- **Also chairs:** NITI Aayog, Inter-State Council, National Integration Council, National Water Resources Council.
- **The quotes:** *primus inter pares* and **"keystone of the Cabinet arch"** (Morley) · **the sun** (Jennings) · **central to its formation, life and death** (Laski) · **captain of the ship of state** (Munro).
- ⭐ **The debate:** Cabinet government → **Prime Ministerial government**, driven by **party majorities, not constitutional change**. Large majority ⇒ prime-ministerial; thin majority ⇒ collegial.
