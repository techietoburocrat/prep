# Chapter 18 — Vice-President

[← Chapter 17](17_President.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 19 →](19_Prime_Minister.md)

**Read time ~7 min** · **Articles 63–71, 89** · 🟠 Prelims: 1 question in most cycles · Mains: **2022**

> 📌 **A short chapter, but a dense one.** Almost everything examinable here is a **contrast with the President** — different electoral college, different qualification, different removal procedure. Learn it as a comparison table and it takes fifteen minutes.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The Vice-President has two jobs that have nothing to do with each other:** he is the **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (his actual day job), and he is the **standby who acts as President** if that office falls vacant. He is *not* a spare President, and unlike the American Vice-President, **he does not automatically become President** — he only acts, and only for six months.

---

## 📖 The story

The framers copied the office from the **American Vice-President**, who presides over the Senate. But they made one decisive change.

In the USA, if the President dies, the Vice-President **becomes** President for the rest of the term. In India, he only **acts** as President, and a **fresh election must be held within six months**.

> 💡 **The analogy:** the American VP is a **designated successor** — the office passes to him. The Indian VP is a **caretaker with a deadline** — he holds the keys until a proper election can be arranged, and then hands them over. The difference matters because India's President is elected by a wide electoral college including all the state assemblies; letting one person inherit that office without that election would bypass the states entirely.

**And it has happened.** When **Dr. Zakir Husain died in office in 1969**, Vice-President **V.V. Giri acted as President**, then resigned to contest the election — which he won. The design worked exactly as intended.

## PART A — Election ⭐

### The electoral college — and how it differs from the President's

| | **President** | **Vice-President** |
|---|---|---|
| **Elected MPs** | ✅ Included | ✅ Included |
| ⭐ **Nominated MPs** | ❌ **Excluded** | ✅ ⭐ **INCLUDED** |
| ⭐ **State legislative assemblies** | ✅ Included | ❌ ⭐ **EXCLUDED entirely** |
| **Delhi and Puducherry MLAs** | ✅ Included | ❌ Excluded |

> ⭐⭐ **This is the single most tested fact in the chapter.** The Vice-President's electoral college is **only Parliament — both Houses, elected *and* nominated members.** No state has any say at all.
>
> **The logic:** his primary function is to preside over the **Rajya Sabha**, a body of Parliament — so Parliament alone chooses him. The President, by contrast, is head of the whole Union, so the states participate.

**Method:** **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**, by **secret ballot** — the same as for the President.

> ⚠️ **A historical point worth knowing:** originally the Vice-President was elected by the members of both Houses assembled at a **joint meeting**. The **11th Amendment Act, 1961** changed this to an **electoral college**, and also provided that the election **cannot be challenged on the ground that the electoral college was incomplete** (i.e. because of vacancies).

### Qualifications — one crucial difference

| Must | Detail |
|---|---|
| Be a **citizen of India** | Same as President |
| Have completed **35 years** of age | Same as President |
| ⭐ Be **qualified for election as a member of the RAJYA SABHA** | ⚠️ **The President must be qualified for the LOK SABHA.** This is the difference — and it follows from the job: he presides over the Rajya Sabha |
| **Not hold any office of profit** | Same exception — a sitting President, Vice-President, Governor or Minister is not disqualified |

**Nomination:** at least **20 electors as proposers** and **20 as seconders**, with a security deposit of **₹15,000**. *(For the President it is 50 and 50.)*

## PART B — Term and removal

- **Term: five years**, and he is **eligible for re-election any number of times**.
- He may **resign to the President**.
- He **continues in office beyond his term until his successor assumes charge**.

### ⭐ Removal — the procedure that is unlike anything else

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Where it starts** | ⭐ **ONLY the Rajya Sabha** — the Lok Sabha cannot initiate |
| **Majority in the Rajya Sabha** | An **effective majority** — a majority of **all the then members** of the House |
| **Then** | Must be **agreed to by the Lok Sabha**, by a **simple majority** |
| **Notice** | **14 days' advance notice** |
| ⭐ **Ground** | ⚠️ **The Constitution mentions NO ground.** Unlike the President, who can be removed only for *"violation of the Constitution"*, the Vice-President can be removed **without any stated reason** |

> ⭐ **Three contrasts to hold together:**
> 1. The **President** is removed by **impeachment** for *violation of the Constitution*, by a **two-thirds special majority in both Houses**.
> 2. The **Vice-President** is removed by a resolution **originating only in the Rajya Sabha**, by **effective majority**, **agreed to by the Lok Sabha**, and for **no stated ground**.
> 3. **No Vice-President has ever been removed.**

## PART C — The two functions

### ① Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha *(the real job)*

This is where he spends his time, and it is what [Mains 2022 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2022.md) asked about.

**His powers as Chairman are broadly those of the Speaker in the Lok Sabha**, with important differences:

| | **Speaker (Lok Sabha)** | **Chairman (Rajya Sabha)** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Membership of the House** | **Is a member** of the House he presides over | ⚠️ **Is NOT a member** of the Rajya Sabha |
| **Vote** | Has a **casting vote** in case of a tie *(no vote in the first instance)* | Has a **casting vote** in case of a tie |
| ⭐ **Money Bill** | ⭐ **Certifies** whether a bill is a Money Bill — **his decision is final** | ⚠️ **No such power** |
| **Joint sitting** | ⭐ **Presides** over a joint sitting of both Houses | ⚠️ **Does not preside** — the Speaker does |
| **Removal** | Removed by the Lok Sabha by **effective majority**, 14 days' notice | Removed only along with removal from the office of Vice-President |

**As Chairman he:**
- Maintains order and decorum, and is the **final interpreter of the Constitution, the Rules of Procedure and parliamentary precedents** within the House
- **Adjourns** the House or suspends a sitting in the absence of a quorum
- Decides on **points of order**
- Refers matters to **committees**, and appoints their chairpersons
- Exercises **disciplinary powers** over members
- Decides questions of **disqualification of members under the Tenth Schedule** *(anti-defection)* for the Rajya Sabha

> ⚠️ **When the Vice-President is acting as President, he does NOT perform the duties of Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** — the **Deputy Chairman** takes over — and he is **not entitled to the Chairman's salary**; he draws the **President's** salary and allowances instead.

### ② Acting as President

He acts as President when a **vacancy** arises in that office by **resignation, removal, death or otherwise**, or when the President is **unable to discharge his functions** due to absence, illness or any other cause.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Maximum period** | ⭐ **Six months** — within which a new President **must** be elected |
| **What he gets** | ⭐ **All the powers, immunities and privileges of the President**, and the **President's emoluments** |
| ⭐ **He does NOT become President** | He only **acts**. Contrast the **USA**, where the Vice-President **succeeds to the office** for the remainder of the term |

> ⚠️ **The gap the Constitution leaves:** it does **not** provide for who discharges the President's functions when **both** the President and Vice-President are unavailable. Parliament filled this by the **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969** — the **Chief Justice of India**, and in his absence the **seniormost judge of the Supreme Court**, discharges the functions. **This happened in 1969**, when **CJI M. Hidayatullah** acted as President after Zakir Husain died and V.V. Giri resigned to contest.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The comparison that carries the chapter
> **Electoral college:** President = **elected** MPs + **elected** MLAs. Vice-President = **all** MPs, **elected and nominated**, and **no MLAs at all**.
> **Qualification:** President → **Lok Sabha**. Vice-President → **Rajya Sabha**.
> **Proposers:** President **50+50**. Vice-President **20+20**. Deposit **₹15,000** for both.

### Removal
> **Rajya Sabha only** initiates · **effective majority** there · **simple majority** in the Lok Sabha · **14 days' notice** · ⭐ **no ground specified**.

### The Chairman vs the Speaker
> ⭐ The Chairman is **not a member** of the House he presides over. He **cannot certify a Money Bill** and **does not preside over a joint sitting** — both belong to the **Speaker**.

### Acting as President
> **Maximum six months** · gets the **President's emoluments** · ⭐ **acts, does not succeed** *(unlike the USA)*. If **both** offices are vacant → the **CJI** *(President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969; Hidayatullah, 1969)*.

### The two people to remember
> **Dr. S. Radhakrishnan** — the first Vice-President, and the only one to serve **two full terms** before becoming President.
> **V.V. Giri** — acted as President in **1969**, resigned to contest, and won.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Discuss the role of the Vice-President of India as the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2022 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2022.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **Article 64** makes him ex-officio Chairman; **Article 89**. (ii) **Functions** — presiding, maintaining order, final interpreter of rules within the House, points of order, casting vote, referring bills to committees, disciplinary powers, deciding **Tenth Schedule** disqualifications for the House. (iii) ⭐ **The structural peculiarity** — he is **not a member** of the House he presides over, which makes him more detached than the Speaker but also gives him no constituency inside it. (iv) **What he lacks** — no power to **certify Money Bills**, no role in a **joint sitting**. (v) **The federal significance** — the Rajya Sabha is the House of the States, so its presiding officer matters to Centre–state balance; note his role in Articles **249 and 312** resolutions. (vi) Conclude on the modern strain: rising disruptions, mass suspensions, and the resulting contest over the Chair's neutrality.

**2. Compare the offices of the President and the Vice-President with respect to election, qualification and removal. (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Straight comparison table — electoral college (states in / states out; nominated out / nominated in), qualification (Lok Sabha / Rajya Sabha), proposers (50 / 20), removal (impeachment for violation of the Constitution / resolution of the Rajya Sabha with no stated ground). Conclude on **why** each difference exists: the President represents the **Union including the states**; the Vice-President exists to preside over **one House of Parliament**.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- **Articles 63–71**, plus **89** (Chairman of the Rajya Sabha). **Second highest office** in India.
- ⭐ **Electoral college = members of BOTH Houses of Parliament, elected AND nominated. NO state legislatures.** PR by single transferable vote, secret ballot. **11th Amendment (1961)** replaced the joint-meeting method and barred challenge on the ground of an **incomplete electoral college**.
- **Qualifications:** citizen · **35** years · ⭐ qualified for the **RAJYA SABHA** · no office of profit. **20 proposers + 20 seconders**, **₹15,000** deposit.
- **Term five years**, re-eligible; resigns to the **President**; continues till his successor takes charge.
- ⭐ **Removal:** resolution **only in the Rajya Sabha**, by **effective majority**, **agreed to by the Lok Sabha** by simple majority, **14 days' notice**, ⚠️ **no ground specified**. Never used.
- **Function 1 — ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.** ⚠️ **Not a member** of the House. **Cannot certify a Money Bill**; **does not preside over a joint sitting** — both are the **Speaker's**. Has a **casting vote**; decides **Tenth Schedule** disqualifications for the House.
- **Function 2 — acts as President** on a vacancy or the President's inability, for a **maximum of six months**, with **all the President's powers and emoluments**. ⭐ **He acts; he does not succeed** *(unlike the US Vice-President)*. While acting, the **Deputy Chairman** presides over the Rajya Sabha.
- ⚠️ **If both offices are vacant:** the **Chief Justice of India** discharges the functions — **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969**; **CJI Hidayatullah did so in 1969**.
