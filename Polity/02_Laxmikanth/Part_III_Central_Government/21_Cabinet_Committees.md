## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The Cabinet is too big and too busy to examine every issue, so it delegates to small committees of ministers — which then take the decision the Cabinet merely ratifies.** They are **extra-constitutional**, created entirely under the Rules of Business, and the Prime Minister makes and unmakes them at will.

---

## The essentials

### Nature ⭐

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional status** | ⚠️ **They are extra-constitutional — NOT mentioned in the Constitution.** The **Rules of Business** provide for their establishment *(made by the President under Article 77)* |
| **Who creates them** | ⭐ **The Prime Minister**, according to the exigencies of the time and the requirements of the situation. He can **add or reduce** their number and **alter their functions** |
| **Membership** | Usually **three to eight** members |
| **Who can be a member** | Normally **only Cabinet Ministers** — ⚠️ but **non-Cabinet Ministers can also be members** |
| **Composition rule** | They **invariably include the minister in charge of the subject** concerned |
| **Who chairs** | The **Prime Minister**, or a **senior Cabinet Minister** if he is not a member |
| **Two kinds** | **Standing** *(permanent)* and **ad hoc** *(temporary, disbanded once the task is complete)* |

### Functions

1. **Resolve problems** and formulate proposals for the Cabinet's consideration
2. **Take decisions** — ⭐ the Cabinet may review their decisions, but in practice they are usually final
3. Sort out issues and formulate proposals in a way the full Cabinet cannot, given its size and workload

### The standing committees

| Committee | Chaired by |
|---|---|
| **Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs** ⭐ | **Prime Minister** |
| **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs** | **Prime Minister** |
| **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet** | **Prime Minister** |
| **Cabinet Committee on Security** | **Prime Minister** |
| **Cabinet Committee on Investment and Growth** | **Prime Minister** |
| **Cabinet Committee on Employment and Skill Development** | **Prime Minister** |
| **Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs** | ⚠️ **Home Minister** |
| **Cabinet Committee on Accommodation** | ⚠️ **Home Minister** |

> ⭐ **The Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs is described as a "Super-Cabinet"** — it deals with all major policy questions and is the most powerful of the lot.
>
> ⚠️ **The two the Prime Minister does NOT chair are Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation** — both go to the **Home Minister**. That is the most likely Prelims hook in the chapter.

### Groups of Ministers (GoMs)

Besides Cabinet Committees, several **Groups of Ministers** have been constituted to look into different issues and problems.

- Some are **empowered** to take decisions on behalf of the Cabinet; others make **recommendations** to it.
- They are **ad hoc bodies**, and are **wound up** once their task is done.
- ⚠️ Like Cabinet Committees, they have **no constitutional basis**.

---

## ⚠️ The five things to actually remember

1. ⭐ **Extra-constitutional** — created by the **Rules of Business**, not the Constitution.
2. ⭐ **The Prime Minister constitutes them**, decides their number, membership and functions, and can change all of it.
3. ⚠️ **Non-Cabinet Ministers can be members** — membership is not restricted to the Cabinet.
4. ⚠️ **Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation are chaired by the Home Minister**, not the PM.
5. ⭐ **The Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs is the "Super-Cabinet."**

---

## The one analytical point worth carrying

**Cabinet Committees are where "Cabinet government" quietly becomes something narrower.** A decision taken by a five-member committee chaired by the Prime Minister is, formally, a decision the full Cabinet may review — and in practice almost never does.

Combine this with the **Kitchen Cabinet** from [Ch 20](20_Central_Council_of_Ministers.md) and the picture is consistent: **real decision-making has migrated inward**, from the Council of Ministers → to the Cabinet → to Cabinet Committees → to an informal inner circle. **Collective responsibility under Article 75(3) still attaches to the outermost ring, while the decision is taken in the innermost one.**

> That single sentence is the only thing from this chapter likely to earn marks in a Mains answer — use it in [Mains 2024 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md) on cabinet growth marginalising parliamentary supremacy.

## ⚡ 30-second revision

**Extra-constitutional**, under the **Rules of Business** *(Article 77)*. **Constituted by the PM**; **3–8 members**; may include **non-Cabinet Ministers**; always include the **minister in charge of the subject**; chaired by the **PM** or a senior Cabinet Minister. **Standing and ad hoc.** They **take decisions**, which the Cabinet may review but rarely does. ⭐ **CCPA = "Super-Cabinet."** ⚠️ **Home Minister chairs Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation.** **GoMs** are ad hoc, sometimes empowered to decide, and equally **non-constitutional**.
