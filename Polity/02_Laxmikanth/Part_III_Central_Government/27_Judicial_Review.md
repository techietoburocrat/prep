# Chapter 27 — Judicial Review

[← Chapter 26](26_Supreme_Court.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 28 →](28_Judicial_Activism.md)

**Read time ~9 min** · **Articles 13, 32, 131–136, 143, 226, 227, 245, 246, 251, 254, 372** · 🔴 Prelims: 1 most years · Mains: **2005, 2013, 2016, 2019**

> 📌 **Short chapter, heavy weight.** Judicial review is the hinge on which [Ch 11 Basic Structure](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/11_Basic_Structure.md), [Ch 26 Supreme Court](26_Supreme_Court.md) and [Ch 28 Judicial Activism](28_Judicial_Activism.md) all turn.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **Judicial review is the power to declare a law or an executive order unconstitutional and therefore void.** ⚠️ **The phrase appears nowhere in the Constitution** — but the power is conferred explicitly by a dozen articles, and since *Minerva Mills* it is itself part of the **basic structure**, so **Parliament cannot amend it away.**

---

## 📖 The story

A written constitution that limits government is only as good as the answer to one question: **who decides when a limit has been crossed?**

If Parliament decides, the limits are meaningless — the body being limited is judging its own case. If nobody decides, the limits are decorative.

**So the power has to sit with the courts**, and the Constitution places it there through a series of specific provisions rather than one grand declaration.

> 💡 **The analogy:** the Constitution is the **rulebook**, Parliament is a **player**, and judicial review makes the court the **referee**. The obvious objection — *who elected the referee?* — is the whole of the debate in [Ch 28](28_Judicial_Activism.md). The obvious answer is that **a game where a player rules on his own fouls is not a game at all**.
>
> The Indian refinement is that the referee's authority itself became **unamendable**: *Minerva Mills* held that a Parliament which abolished judicial review would be destroying the basic structure. **The referee cannot be sent off by the players.**

---

## PART A — The basis

### ⚠️ The phrase is not in the Constitution

⭐ The expression **"judicial review"** is **nowhere used** in the Constitution. But the power is **explicitly conferred** by:

| Article | What it does |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **13** | Laws **inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights** are **void**; the State **shall not make** any law taking away or abridging them |
| ⭐ **32** | The **Supreme Court's** writ jurisdiction — itself a Fundamental Right |
| ⭐ **226** | The **High Courts'** writ jurisdiction — **wider**, extending to "any other purpose" |
| **227** | High Courts' power of **superintendence** over all subordinate courts |
| **131–136** | The Supreme Court's original, appellate and special-leave jurisdictions |
| **143** | Advisory jurisdiction |
| **245, 246** | The **territorial and subject-matter limits** on Union and state legislative power |
| **251, 254** | **Repugnancy** — central law prevails over an inconsistent state law |
| ⭐ **372** | **Pre-constitutional laws continue in force** only to the extent they are **not inconsistent** with the Constitution |

> ⭐ **Article 13 is the engine.** Everything else supplies jurisdiction; Article 13 supplies the **consequence** — inconsistency means **voidness**.

### ⭐ Judicial review is basic structure

- ***Minerva Mills* (1980)** — held **judicial review** to be part of the **basic structure**; struck down **Article 368(4) and (5)** *(inserted by the 42nd Amendment)*, which had purported to bar any court from questioning an amendment.
- ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)** — the power of **judicial review under Articles 32 and 226** is an **integral and essential feature** of the Constitution, forming part of its **basic structure**. Tribunals may act as courts of first instance, but **their decisions remain subject to High Court review** under Articles 226/227.
- ***I.R. Coelho* (2007)** — laws placed in the **Ninth Schedule after 24 April 1973** are **open to basic-structure review**. *(See [Ch 11](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/11_Basic_Structure.md).)*

## PART B — Importance, scope and grounds

### ⭐ The three purposes

1. To uphold the **principle of supremacy of the Constitution**
2. To maintain **federal equilibrium** — the balance between the Centre and the states
3. To **protect the Fundamental Rights** of citizens

### ⭐ The three grounds on which a law may be struck down

A legislative enactment or executive order may be declared unconstitutional if it:
1. **Infringes the Fundamental Rights** *(Part III)*
2. **Is outside the competence of the authority** which framed it — i.e. beyond its legislative competence under the **Seventh Schedule**
3. **Is repugnant to the constitutional provisions**

### ⚠️ Scope — narrower than in the USA, and why

> ⭐ **The scope of judicial review in India is narrower than in the USA**, because the American Constitution provides for **"due process of law"**, while the Indian Constitution says **"procedure established by law"** *(Article 21)*.
>
> ⚠️ **But add the qualifier, or the point is out of date:** after ***Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978)**, the Supreme Court held that the procedure must be **"right, just and fair, and not arbitrary, fanciful or oppressive"** — importing the substance of **due process** into Article 21. **In practice the gap has largely closed.** See [Ch 7](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/07_Fundamental_Rights.md).

### ⭐ The doctrines that soften the blow

| Doctrine | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Severability** | Only the **offending part** of a law is struck down, **if it is severable** from the rest; the remainder survives |
| ⭐ **Eclipse** | A **pre-constitutional** law inconsistent with a Fundamental Right is **not dead but dormant** — it is "eclipsed", and **revives** if the inconsistency is removed *(e.g. by a constitutional amendment)*. ⚠️ **Applies to pre-constitutional laws; post-constitutional laws violating FRs are void *ab initio*** |
| ⭐ **Prospective overruling** | The Court's ruling applies only to **future** cases, leaving past transactions undisturbed. ⭐ First used in ***Golaknath* (1967)** |
| **Presumption of constitutionality** | A law is **presumed valid**; the burden of proving unconstitutionality lies on the challenger |

### ⚠️ The limitations on the power

1. ⭐ The Court **cannot initiate review on its own** — it needs a **case or controversy** properly before it
2. Laws carry a **presumption of constitutionality**
3. The Court generally avoids **political questions**
4. It reviews **the validity of a law, not the wisdom of the policy** behind it
5. ⚠️ **Article 74(2)** bars enquiry into the **advice** tendered by ministers to the President *(the action taken remains reviewable — see [Ch 20](20_Central_Council_of_Ministers.md))*
6. ⭐ **Article 122 and 212** bar courts from enquiring into the **proceedings** of Parliament and state legislatures **on the ground of alleged irregularity of procedure**

## PART C — Judicial review of administrative action

Distinct from review of **legislation**, and increasingly the bulk of the courts' work. The recognised grounds:

| Ground | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Illegality** | The authority **misunderstood the law** regulating its power, or acted **beyond its jurisdiction** *(ultra vires)* |
| ⭐ **Irrationality** | ***Wednesbury* unreasonableness** — a decision **so outrageous in its defiance of logic** that no sensible person could have arrived at it |
| ⭐ **Procedural impropriety** | Failure to observe **statutory procedure** or the **principles of natural justice** — *audi alteram partem* *(hear the other side)* and *nemo judex in causa sua* *(no one a judge in his own cause)* |
| **Proportionality** | The measure must be **proportionate** to the object; increasingly applied in rights cases *(and central to the privacy and Aadhaar judgments)* |

## PART D — The tug of war, in one place

*This is the sequence that appears across [Ch 10](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/10_Amendment_of_the_Constitution.md), [Ch 11](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/11_Basic_Structure.md), [Ch 16](../Part_II_System_of_Government/16_Emergency_Provisions.md) and this chapter. Learn it once, use it everywhere.*

| Year | Event | Effect on judicial review |
|---|---|---|
| **1951** | ***Shankari Prasad*** | Parliament **can** amend Fundamental Rights |
| **1967** | ***Golaknath*** | Parliament **cannot** abridge FRs *(and the Court first uses **prospective overruling**)* |
| **1971** | **24th Amendment** | Asserts Parliament **can** amend any part |
| ⭐ **1973** | ***Kesavananda Bharati*** | Can amend — **but not the basic structure** |
| **1975** | ***Indira Nehru Gandhi*** | **39th Amendment** clause struck down — the doctrine's **first use** |
| ⚠️ **1976** | **42nd Amendment** | **Article 368(4) and (5)** — no amendment shall be **questioned in any court** |
| ⭐ **1980** | ***Minerva Mills*** | **Struck those down.** ⭐ **Judicial review is basic structure** |
| **1997** | ***L. Chandra Kumar*** | **Articles 32 and 226 review is basic structure**; tribunals are **subject to High Court review** |
| **2007** | ***I.R. Coelho*** | Ninth Schedule laws **post-24 April 1973** are **reviewable** |
| **2015** | ***NJAC*** | **99th Amendment struck down** — independence of the judiciary is basic structure |

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The definition
> ⭐ **The power of the judiciary to examine the constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders of both the Central and State governments** — and to declare them **void** if they violate the Constitution.

### The engine and the consequence
> ⭐ **Article 13 supplies the consequence (voidness); every other article supplies jurisdiction.** The phrase "judicial review" **appears nowhere** in the Constitution.

### The three purposes — S-F-R
> **S**upremacy of the Constitution · **F**ederal equilibrium · **R**ights protection.

### The three grounds
> **Infringes FRs · beyond legislative competence · repugnant to the Constitution.**

### The four doctrines
> ⭐ **Severability** *(cut out the bad part)* · **Eclipse** *(pre-constitutional laws go dormant, and revive)* · **Prospective overruling** *(first used in **Golaknath**, 1967)* · **Presumption of constitutionality**.

### The three cases that made it unamendable
> ⭐ ***Minerva Mills* (1980)** — judicial review is basic structure · ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)** — Articles 32 and 226 are basic structure, tribunals reviewable · ***I.R. Coelho* (2007)** — Ninth Schedule post-**24 April 1973**.

### India vs USA
> ⭐ **"Procedure established by law"** *(India)* vs **"due process of law"** *(USA)* ⇒ narrower in scope — ⚠️ **but *Maneka Gandhi* (1978) required the procedure to be "right, just and fair", largely closing the gap.**

### Administrative review — I-I-P-P
> **I**llegality · **I**rrationality *(Wednesbury)* · **P**rocedural impropriety *(natural justice)* · **P**roportionality.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Is it possible to distinguish between judicial review and judicial activism in India? Does the recent behaviour of the Indian judiciary partake more of judicial activism? Argue with illustrations." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2005 Q6](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2005.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **Distinguish** — ⭐ **judicial review is a constitutional POWER** *(to test validity against the Constitution)*; **judicial activism is a JUDICIAL ATTITUDE** *(a willingness to use that power expansively and to direct the other organs)*. Review is **reactive and legal**; activism is **proactive and often remedial**. (ii) **The overlap** — activism is usually **exercised through** review, so they are not opposites but a **power and a posture**. (iii) **Illustrations** — review: *Golaknath*, *Minerva Mills*, NJAC. Activism: *Vishaka* guidelines, continuing mandamus in environmental cases, 2G and coal-block cancellations, the CNG order. (iv) Conclude with the boundary: activism becomes **overreach** when the Court **legislates or administers** rather than adjudicates. *(See [Ch 28](28_Judicial_Activism.md).)*

**2. "The Supreme Court of India keeps a check on arbitrary power of the Parliament in amending the Constitution." Discuss critically. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2013 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md).*
> *Skeleton:* Trace the Part D table — *Shankari Prasad* → *Golaknath* → 24th → **Kesavananda** → 39th and 42nd → **Minerva Mills** → *Coelho* → NJAC. **Critically:** the doctrine has **no textual basis** and is **open-ended**; the Court has expanded its own reviewing power; but the counter is decisive — the **42nd Amendment's claim to unlimited amending power** shows the check was **necessary**, and the doctrine has been used **sparingly**. Conclude: the check is **functionally legitimate rather than textually derived**, and depends on continued judicial restraint.

**3. "From the resolution of contentious issues regarding distribution of legislative powers by the courts, the 'Principle of Federal Supremacy' and 'Harmonious Construction' have emerged. Explain." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2019 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md) — see the fuller skeleton in [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md).*

**4. "What was held in the Coelho case? In this context, can you say that judicial review is of key importance amongst the basic features of the Constitution?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2016 Q6](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2016.md) — see [Ch 11](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/11_Basic_Structure.md).*
> *The line to close on:* **without judicial review, every other basic feature is unenforceable — review is the feature that protects the features.**

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Definition:** the power to examine the constitutionality of **legislative enactments and executive orders** of **both the Centre and the states**, and declare them **void**.
- ⚠️ **The phrase "judicial review" appears nowhere** in the Constitution. Conferred by **13, 32, 131–136, 143, 226, 227, 245, 246, 251, 254, 372**. ⭐ **Article 13** supplies **voidness**.
- ⭐ **Basic structure:** ***Minerva Mills* (1980)** struck down **Article 368(4) and (5)**; ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)** — **Articles 32 and 226 are basic structure**, and **tribunals are subject to HC review**; ***I.R. Coelho* (2007)** — Ninth Schedule laws **after 24 April 1973** are reviewable.
- **Three purposes:** supremacy of the Constitution · **federal equilibrium** · protection of Fundamental Rights.
- **Three grounds:** infringes **FRs** · beyond **legislative competence** · **repugnant** to the Constitution.
- ⭐ **Scope narrower than the USA** — **"procedure established by law"** vs **"due process"** — ⚠️ **but *Maneka Gandhi* (1978)** required procedure to be **"right, just and fair"**, largely closing the gap.
- **Doctrines:** ⭐ **severability** · **eclipse** *(pre-constitutional laws only; post-constitutional FR violations are void ab initio)* · **prospective overruling** *(first used in **Golaknath**, 1967)* · **presumption of constitutionality**.
- **Limits:** ⭐ **cannot act suo motu without a case** · presumption of validity · avoids **political questions** · reviews **validity, not policy wisdom** · **Article 74(2)** *(advice not enquirable)* · **Articles 122 and 212** *(no review of legislative proceedings for procedural irregularity)*.
- **Administrative review — four grounds:** ⭐ **illegality · irrationality (*Wednesbury*) · procedural impropriety (natural justice) · proportionality**.
