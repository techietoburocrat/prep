---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **Parliament is not the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha — it is the President plus both Houses.** That single definitional fact explains the veto, the ordinance, the joint sitting and the Money Bill route. And the chapter's real theme is a gap: **Parliament has enormous formal power over the executive and, in practice, exercises very little of it** — because the executive commands the majority that controls the House.

---

## 📖 The story

Every democracy has to answer: **who checks the government?**

In a presidential system the answer is a separately elected legislature that the President cannot dissolve and does not control. In a parliamentary system the answer is stranger — **the legislature checks a government drawn from its own majority.** The watchdog and the watched are the same set of people.

That works only if MPs of the ruling party are willing, sometimes, to act against their own government. **Two things have made that nearly impossible in India:** the party whip, and the **anti-defection law**, which makes voting against your party a route to losing your seat.

> 💡 **The analogy:** Parliament is designed like an **audit function inside a company** — the auditors sit inside the organisation, which gives them access nobody outside could have. The whole arrangement depends on the auditors being able to write an unfavourable report without being fired. **India's anti-defection law effectively lets the management fire the auditor for an adverse finding.** The access remains; the independence does not.
>
> **And this is why the committees matter so much** ([Ch 23](23_Parliamentary_Committees.md)). They meet in private, off-camera, without whips being issued — so the audit that cannot happen on the floor still happens in the committee room. When examiners ask about Parliament's decline, **the committees are the answer to "so what survives?"**

**The numbers make the decline concrete.** The first Lok Sabha sat for over **130 days a year**; recent Lok Sabhas average around **60–70**. Bills that once went routinely to committees are increasingly passed without referral, and sometimes within hours of introduction.

## PART A — Organisation ⭐

### Article 79 — the definition that unlocks the chapter

> **"There shall be a Parliament for the Union which shall consist of the President and two Houses to be known respectively as the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and the House of the People (Lok Sabha)."**

⭐ **The President is a part of Parliament, though he is not a member of either House and does not sit in them.** Why it matters:

- **No bill becomes law without his assent** ([Ch 17](17_President.md))
- He **summons, prorogues and dissolves** the Houses
- He **promulgates ordinances** when they are not in session

| System | Composition of the legislature |
|---|---|
| **India** | ⭐ **President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha** |
| **Britain** | King/Queen + House of Lords + House of Commons *(same pattern)* |
| ⚠️ **USA** | **Congress = Senate + House of Representatives only.** ⭐ **The President is NOT part of the legislature** — the separation-of-powers logic |

> **The Prelims trap:** *"The President is not a member of Parliament"* — **true**. *"The President is not a part of Parliament"* — **false**. Both statements appear; they are not the same claim.

## PART B — Composition ⭐

### The Rajya Sabha — Council of States

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Maximum strength** | **250** — **238** representatives of states and UTs + **12** nominated by the President |
| **Present strength** | **245** — 229 from states + 4 from UTs + 12 nominated |
| **Which Schedule** | ⭐ The **Fourth Schedule** deals with the allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha |
| **Election from states** | By the **elected members** of the **state legislative assemblies**, by **proportional representation with the single transferable vote**. Seats are allotted **on the basis of population**, so states are **not equally represented** |
| **Election from UTs** | **Indirectly**, by members of an **electoral college specially constituted** for the purpose, by the same method. ⚠️ **Only Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir** have representation — the others are too small |
| ⭐ **Nominated members** | **12**, by the President, from persons with **special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service** |

> ⚠️ **Two contrasts with the US Senate:** (i) American states have **equal** representation (two each) regardless of size; Indian states are represented **by population**. (ii) The US Senate has **no nominated members**; it is wholly elected.
>
> ⭐ **The domicile question** — the requirement that a Rajya Sabha candidate be **ordinarily resident in the state** he represents was **removed in 2003**, and the removal was **upheld in *Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India* (2006)**. The Court held that federalism does not require a domicile condition. This is a favourite Prelims item.

### The Lok Sabha — House of the People

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Maximum strength** | **552** — **530** from states + **20** from UTs |
| **Present strength** | **543** — 524 from states + 19 from UTs |
| ⚠️ **Anglo-Indian nomination** | ⭐ The provision for the President to nominate **2 Anglo-Indians** was **ABOLISHED by the 104th Amendment Act, 2019**. **A very common stale-fact trap** |
| **Election** | **Direct election** by the people from **territorial constituencies**, on the basis of **universal adult franchise** |
| ⭐ **Voting age** | Reduced from **21 to 18** by the **61st Amendment Act, 1988** |

### Reservation of seats

- Seats are reserved for **Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes** in the Lok Sabha **on the basis of population ratios**.
- ⭐ **Reservation is of *seats*, not of *electorates*.** A candidate in a reserved constituency must be from the SC/ST, but **all voters of that constituency vote** — there is **no separate electorate**. *(This is the Poona Pact settlement, and it is examinable.)*
- Originally provided for **10 years**; extended repeatedly, most recently by the **104th Amendment Act, 2019, until 2030**.
- ⭐ **The 106th Amendment Act, 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam)** reserves **one-third of seats for women** in the Lok Sabha and the state legislative assemblies, **including within the SC/ST quota** — but it takes effect only **after the first census following commencement and the delimitation carried out on its basis**, and lasts **15 years**. *(Tested in [Prelims 2024 Q13](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2024.md).)*

### ⭐ The delimitation freeze — a chain of amendments

| Amendment | What it did |
|---|---|
| **42nd (1976)** | Froze the allocation of seats and the division into constituencies on the **1971 Census**, until after the **2000 census** |
| **84th (2001)** | Extended the freeze on **seat allocation** until the **first census after 2026**, but allowed **re-drawing of constituency boundaries** on the **1991 census** |
| **87th (2003)** | Changed that delimitation base from the **1991** census to the **2001** census |

> **Why the freeze exists:** states that controlled their population growth would otherwise **lose seats** to states that did not. The freeze protects them — at the cost of leaving representation increasingly unequal. **This is the single biggest looming federal controversy of the late 2020s**, and it links directly to [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md).

## PART C — Duration and membership

### ⭐ Duration — the fundamental contrast

| | **Rajya Sabha** | **Lok Sabha** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | ⭐ **A continuing chamber — a permanent body. NOT subject to dissolution** | Continues for **five years** from the date of its **first meeting**; **dissolution** ends it |
| **Retirement** | ⭐ **One-third of members retire every second year**; each member serves **six years** | — |
| **Early end** | Cannot be dissolved | The **President may dissolve it at any time**, even before five years |
| **Extension** | Not applicable | ⭐ During a **National Emergency**, extendable by law **one year at a time**, but **not beyond six months** after the Emergency ceases ([Ch 16](../Part_II_System_of_Government/16_Emergency_Provisions.md)) |

### Qualifications (Article 84)

| Requirement | Rajya Sabha | Lok Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| **Citizenship** | Citizen of India | Citizen of India |
| ⭐ **Minimum age** | **30 years** | **25 years** |
| **Oath** | Must make and subscribe an oath before the Election Commission's appointee | Same |
| **Other** | Must be a **registered elector** in **any** parliamentary constituency *(RPA, 1951)* | Same |

### ⭐ Disqualifications (Article 102)

Under the **Constitution**, a person is disqualified if he:
1. Holds any **office of profit** under the Union or a state *(other than an office exempted by Parliament)*
2. Is of **unsound mind** and stands so declared by a court
3. Is an **undischarged insolvent**
4. Is **not a citizen of India**, or has **voluntarily acquired the citizenship of a foreign state**, or is under any acknowledgment of allegiance to a foreign state
5. Is so disqualified under **any law made by Parliament**

Under the **Representation of the People Act, 1951**, he is additionally disqualified if he:
- Is found guilty of certain **election offences or corrupt practices**
- Is **convicted and sentenced to imprisonment for two years or more** *(⚠️ **preventive detention is not a disqualification**)*
- Has **failed to lodge an account of election expenses** within the time
- Has an **interest in government contracts, works or services**
- Is a **director or managing agent, or holds an office of profit, in a corporation in which the government has at least 25% share**
- Has been **dismissed from government service for corruption or disloyalty** to the state
- Has been convicted for **promoting enmity between groups** or for the **offence of bribery**
- Has been punished for **preaching and practising social crimes** such as untouchability, dowry and sati

### ⭐ Who decides a disqualification — the split that is always tested

| Ground | Decided by |
|---|---|
| **Article 102 grounds** *(office of profit, insolvency, etc.)* | ⭐ **The President**, whose decision is **final** — but he must act **in accordance with the opinion of the Election Commission** *(Article 103)* |
| ⭐ **Defection — the Tenth Schedule** | ⚠️ **The Speaker / Chairman of the House** — **not** the President, and **not** on the EC's opinion |

> ⭐ ***Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu* (1992)** held that the presiding officer, when deciding a Tenth Schedule question, acts as a **tribunal**, and the decision is therefore **subject to judicial review** — though the Court will not intervene before a decision is made. This case is asked repeatedly.
>
> ***Ravi S. Naik*** and later cases have added that **inordinate delay** by the Speaker is itself reviewable — a live issue given the political incentive to sit on defection petitions.

### Vacation of seats

A member's seat becomes vacant on: **double membership** *(cannot be a member of both Houses, or of Parliament and a state legislature)* · **disqualification** · **resignation** *(to the Speaker/Chairman)* · ⭐ **absence from all meetings of the House for 60 days without permission** · and other cases *(election declared void, expulsion, election as President or Vice-President, appointment as Governor)*.

## PART D — The presiding officers ⭐⭐

### The Speaker of the Lok Sabha

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Election** | Elected by the **Lok Sabha from among its own members**, on a date fixed by the President |
| **Vacates office** | If he **ceases to be a member** of the Lok Sabha; on **resignation** *(addressed to the Deputy Speaker)*; on **removal** |
| **Removal** | By a resolution passed by an ⭐ **effective majority** *(a majority of all the then members)*, after **14 days' notice** |
| ⭐ **On dissolution** | ⚠️ **He does NOT vacate office when the Lok Sabha is dissolved.** He continues **until immediately before the first meeting of the newly elected Lok Sabha** |
| ⭐ **While a removal resolution is pending** | **Article 96** — he **shall NOT preside**; but he **retains the right to speak and take part** in the proceedings, and **may vote in the first instance** *(though not a casting vote)*. ⚠️ Candidates routinely get the last two wrong — see [Prelims 2024 Q17](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2024.md) |

**His powers:**
- ⭐ **Final interpreter** within the House of the Constitution's provisions relating to it, the **Rules of Procedure**, and **parliamentary precedents**
- **Adjourns** the House or suspends a sitting in the absence of a **quorum** — ⭐ the quorum is **one-tenth of the total number of members** of the House, including the presiding officer
- ⭐ **Does not vote in the first instance**, but exercises a **casting vote** in the case of a tie
- ⭐ **Presides over a joint sitting** of the two Houses
- ⭐ **Certifies whether a bill is a Money Bill — and his decision is FINAL** *(Article 110(4))*
- Decides **disqualification under the Tenth Schedule**
- Acts as **ex-officio chairman** of the **Business Advisory Committee**, the **Rules Committee** and the **General Purposes Committee**, and **appoints the chairmen of all parliamentary committees**
- May allow a **secret sitting** of the House

**How his independence is secured:**
1. **Security of tenure** — removable only by an effective majority of the House
2. ⭐ **His salary and allowances are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** — **not subject to the annual vote of Parliament**
3. His **conduct cannot be discussed** except on a **substantive motion**
4. His **powers of regulating procedure are not subject to the jurisdiction of any court**
5. He **cannot vote in the first instance**
6. He is given a **very high position in the order of precedence — seventh rank, along with the Chief Justice of India**

> ⭐ **"Once a Speaker, always a Speaker"** — the **British convention** under which the Speaker **resigns from his party** on election and, by convention, is **re-elected unopposed** thereafter. ⚠️ **India has NOT adopted it.** The Indian Speaker **retains party membership** and contests the next election on a party ticket — which is the structural source of every complaint about the Chair's partisanship, and which [Mains 2020 Q5](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2020.md) asked about directly.

### The Deputy Speaker

- Elected by the Lok Sabha **after the Speaker**, from among its members.
- ⭐ **He is NOT subordinate to the Speaker — he is directly responsible to the House.**
- ⭐ **Special privilege:** whenever he is appointed a **member of a parliamentary committee**, he **automatically becomes its chairman**.
- By a convention since 1990, the post goes to the **main opposition party**. ⚠️ **The Constitution sets no time limit for filling the office**, and it has been left **vacant for extended periods** — a live constitutional criticism.

### The Speaker *pro tem*

⭐ Appointed by the **President** — usually the **seniormost member** of the newly elected House. He **presides over the first sitting**, **administers the oath** to new members, and **enables the House to elect its new Speaker**, after which his office ceases.

### Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

Covered in [Ch 18](18_Vice_President.md). ⭐ **The key contrasts:** the **Chairman is not a member** of the House he presides over; he **cannot certify a Money Bill**; and he **does not preside over a joint sitting**.

### Leaders and the Whip

| Office | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Leader of the House** | In the Lok Sabha, the **Prime Minister** if he is a member of that House, or a **minister nominated by him**. In the Rajya Sabha, a **minister** who is a member |
| ⭐ **Leader of the Opposition** | Recognised in **1969**; given **statutory recognition** by the **Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament Act, 1977**. ⚠️ Requires a party with at least **one-tenth of the total strength of the House** — which is why **some Lok Sabhas have had no recognised Leader of the Opposition** |
| ⭐ **The Whip** | ⚠️ **Mentioned nowhere** — not in the Constitution, not in the Rules of the House, not in any parliamentary statute. **It is based entirely on conventions.** But defying a whip attracts the **Tenth Schedule** |

> ⭐ **The Whip's absence from the statute book, combined with the Tenth Schedule's teeth, is the sharpest single point about the decline of the individual MP.** An office with no legal existence can effectively end a member's career. Use it in [Mains 2013 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md) and [2019 Q15](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md).

## PART E — Sessions, and the four ways a sitting ends

### Sessions

- Summoned by the **President**. ⭐ **The gap between two sessions must not exceed six months** — so Parliament must meet **at least twice a year**.
- Three sessions by convention: **Budget** *(February–May, the longest)*, **Monsoon** *(July–September)*, **Winter** *(November–December)*.
- ⚠️ **Lame-duck session:** the last session of an existing Lok Sabha **after a new House has been elected** — its "lame ducks" are members who failed to get re-elected.

### ⭐ Adjournment vs prorogation vs dissolution — always tested

| Device | Who does it | What it ends | Effect on pending business |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment** | The **presiding officer** | A **sitting**, for a specified time *(hours, days, weeks)* | ⭐ **No effect** — pending business survives |
| **Adjournment *sine die*** | The **presiding officer** | A sitting **for an indefinite period** | No effect on pending business |
| **Prorogation** | ⭐ The **President** | The **session** | ⭐ **Pending bills do NOT lapse**; but pending **notices** *(motions, resolutions, questions)* lapse |
| ⭐ **Dissolution** | The **President** | The **Lok Sabha itself** — the House ceases to exist | ⭐ **Most pending business lapses** — see the table below |

### ⭐⭐ What lapses on dissolution of the Lok Sabha

| Lapses ❌ | Does NOT lapse ✅ |
|---|---|
| A bill **pending in the Lok Sabha** | A bill **pending in the Rajya Sabha** which has **not been passed by the Lok Sabha** |
| A bill **passed by the Lok Sabha but pending in the Rajya Sabha** | A bill **passed by both Houses but pending the President's assent** |
| — | A bill **passed by both Houses but returned by the President** for reconsideration |
| — | ⭐ A bill in respect of which the **President has notified his intention to summon a joint sitting**, if the notification was issued **before dissolution** |

> ⭐ **This exact table was tested in [Prelims 2024 Q18](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2024.md).** The logic that makes it memorable: **a bill lapses if the Lok Sabha still had work to do on it.** Once the Lok Sabha's role is finished — or if the bill never involved the Lok Sabha — it survives.

**Also:** all **pending assurances** to be examined by the Committee on Government Assurances lapse; and any **notices** lapse.

## PART F — The devices of parliamentary proceedings ⭐⭐

*This section is Prelims gold. Learn the definitions precisely — the questions are almost always about a single distinguishing feature.*

### Question Hour — the first hour of every sitting

| Type | Answer sought | Supplementary questions | Notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Starred** | **ORAL** | ⭐ **Allowed** | 10 days |
| ⭐ **Unstarred** | **WRITTEN** | ⭐ **NOT allowed** | 10 days |
| **Short notice** | **Oral** | Allowed | ⭐ **Less than 10 days** — for matters of urgent public importance |
| **Question to a private member** | Oral/written | — | Asked when the subject relates to a **bill or resolution for which that member is responsible** |

> 🧠 **A *star* is something you say out loud → starred = oral.** ⚠️ **Unstarred questions get no supplementaries precisely because there is no oral answer to supplement** — which is what [Prelims 2026 Q4](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2026.md) tested.

### ⭐ Zero Hour — an Indian innovation

> **Mentioned NOWHERE in the Rules of Procedure.** It begins **immediately after the Question Hour** and lasts until the **regular agenda is taken up** — i.e. from noon. Members raise matters **without prior notice**.
>
> ⭐ **It is an Indian innovation in parliamentary procedure and has existed since 1962.**

### The motions

| Motion | Key feature |
|---|---|
| **Adjournment motion** | ⭐ To draw attention to a **definite matter of urgent public importance**. **Involves an element of censure**, so ⚠️ **the Rajya Sabha does not permit it**. Needs the support of **50 members**; discussion must last **at least 2 hours 30 minutes** |
| ⭐ **No-confidence motion** | ⚠️ **Only in the Lok Sabha.** Needs the support of **50 members**. **No grounds need be stated.** If passed, the **entire Council of Ministers must resign** *(Article 75(3))* |
| **Censure motion** | ⚠️ Must **state the reasons**; may be moved **against an individual minister, a group, or the whole Council**. Can be moved **only in the Lok Sabha**. ⭐ **Passing it does not require the Council to resign** |
| **Confidence motion** | Moved **by the government** to prove its majority |
| **Privilege motion** | Moved **against a minister** for a **breach of parliamentary privilege** by withholding facts or giving wrong information |
| ⭐ **Calling attention motion** | An **Indian innovation**; calls a minister's attention to a matter of urgent public importance and seeks an **authoritative statement** from him. ⚠️ **Not mentioned in the Rules — but it is more advantageous than an adjournment motion** because it does not involve censure |
| **Motion of thanks** | On the **President's address**; ⭐ **must be passed** — its defeat amounts to the government's defeat |
| **No-day-yet-named motion** | Admitted by the Speaker but **without a date fixed** for discussion |
| **Point of order** | ⭐ Relates to the **interpretation or enforcement of the Rules** or the Constitution's articles regulating House business. ⚠️ **It suspends the proceedings**, and **no debate is allowed on it**. Usually raised by an **opposition member to control the government** |

### The closure motions

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Simple closure** | A member moves that a matter **having been sufficiently discussed be put to vote** |
| **Closure by compartments** | Clauses of a bill are **grouped**, and each group is voted on as a whole |
| **Kangaroo closure** | ⭐ Only **important clauses are taken up** for debate and voting; the rest are **skipped** |
| ⭐ **Guillotine closure** | Undiscussed clauses of a bill or demands for grants are **put to vote along with the discussed ones** because **time has run out** |

> ⚠️ **The guillotine is the most consequential of the four** — in practice, the vast majority of **demands for grants are guillotined** each year, meaning Parliament votes hundreds of thousands of crores without discussing them. **Cite this in any answer on financial control.**

### The discussions

- **Short duration discussion** *(two-hour discussion)* — on a matter of urgent public importance; **no formal motion and no voting**.
- **Half-an-hour discussion** — on a matter of sufficient public importance that has **already been the subject of a question**, where the answer needs elucidation on a matter of fact.
- **Special mention** — a matter that is **not a point of order** and cannot be raised under other devices; called **'Notice (Mention) Under Rule 377'** in the Lok Sabha, and **Special Mention** in the Rajya Sabha.
- **Resolutions** — **private members'**, **government**, and **statutory** *(moved in pursuance of a provision of the Constitution or an Act)*. ⚠️ **All resolutions are substantive motions and must be voted upon**, unlike motions generally.

## PART G — Legislative procedure ⭐⭐

### The four kinds of bills

**Ordinary bills · Money bills · Financial bills · Constitutional amendment bills.**

### ① Ordinary bills

Introduced in **either House**, by a minister *(public bill)* or a private member *(private member's bill)*.

**Three readings:**
1. **First reading** — introduction; the title and objectives are read; published in the Gazette
2. **Second reading** — the most important stage; three sub-stages: **general discussion** → **committee stage** *(usually a select or joint committee)* → **consideration stage** *(clause-by-clause)*
3. **Third reading** — debate confined to acceptance or rejection **as a whole**; no amendments allowed

Then the **second House** repeats the three stages, and may **pass**, **reject**, **keep it pending**, or **pass with amendments**.

### ⭐ Joint sitting (Article 108) — a Prelims certainty

**Called by the President** when, after a bill has been passed by one House and transmitted to the other, the other House:
1. **Rejects** the bill; **or**
2. **Disagrees on the amendments** to be made; **or**
3. ⭐ **More than six months elapse** without the bill being passed *(excluding any period the House is prorogued or adjourned for more than four consecutive days)*

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Who presides** | **The Speaker of the Lok Sabha.** In his absence, the **Deputy Speaker**; in his absence, the **Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**. ⚠️ **The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha does NOT preside** |
| **Quorum** | **One-tenth** of the total number of members of **both Houses** |
| **Decision** | By a **majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting** — which in practice **favours the Lok Sabha**, being more than twice the size |
| ⚠️ **Not available for** | ⭐ **Money bills** *(the Rajya Sabha has no power to block them)* and ⭐ **Constitutional amendment bills** *(both Houses must pass them separately by special majority)* |

> ⭐ **Held only three times:**
> 1. **Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1960**
> 2. **Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977**
> 3. **Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002**
>
> *Tested in [Mains 2017 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2017.md).*

### ② ⭐⭐ Money bills (Article 110)

**A bill is a Money Bill if it contains ONLY provisions dealing with all or any of:**
1. The **imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any tax**
2. The **regulation of borrowing** by the Government of India
3. The **custody of the Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund**, and payments into or withdrawals from them
4. The **appropriation of moneys** out of the Consolidated Fund of India
5. **Declaring any expenditure to be charged** on the Consolidated Fund, or increasing the amount of such expenditure
6. The **receipt of money on account of the Consolidated Fund or the public account**, or their custody or audit
7. Any **matter incidental** to the above

⚠️ **A bill is NOT a Money Bill merely because it provides for** the imposition of **fines or other pecuniary penalties**, the demand or payment of **fees for licences or services rendered**, or the imposition or regulation of a **tax by a local authority**.

| Stage | Rule |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Certification** | **The Speaker certifies** whether a bill is a Money Bill, and **his decision is FINAL** |
| **Introduction** | ⭐ **Only in the Lok Sabha**, and **only on the President's recommendation** — it is a **government bill** |
| ⭐ **Rajya Sabha's power** | **Cannot reject or amend it.** It **must return the bill within 14 days**, with or without recommendations |
| **If recommendations are made** | ⭐ The **Lok Sabha may accept or reject** any or all of them |
| **If not returned in 14 days** | ⭐ **Deemed to have been passed by both Houses** in the form the Lok Sabha passed it |
| **President** | ⭐ **May assent or withhold — but CANNOT return it** for reconsideration |

> ⚠️ **The live controversy:** because the Speaker's certification is final and the Rajya Sabha cannot block a Money Bill, governments have been accused of **routing ordinary legislation through the Money Bill channel** to bypass the Upper House — most prominently the **Aadhaar Act, 2016**. In ***Rojer Mathew* (2019)** a five-judge bench **doubted** the correctness of the earlier Aadhaar ruling on this point and referred the Money Bill question to a **larger bench**, where it remains pending. **This is the most current, most citable controversy in the chapter.**

### ③ Financial bills — the distinction most candidates get wrong ⭐

| | **Money Bill** *(Art 110)* | **Financial Bill (I)** *(Art 117(1))* | **Financial Bill (II)** *(Art 117(3))* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contents** | ⭐ **ONLY** matters in Article 110 | ⭐ Article 110 matters **PLUS other general legislation** | ⭐ Involves **expenditure from the Consolidated Fund**, but **contains none** of the Article 110 matters |
| **Where introduced** | **Lok Sabha only** | **Lok Sabha only** | ⭐ **Either House** |
| **President's recommendation** | Required for **introduction** | Required for **introduction** | ⭐ Required only for **consideration** — not for introduction |
| **Rajya Sabha's powers** | ⭐ **Restricted** — 14 days, recommendations only | ⭐ **FULL powers** — may reject or amend | ⭐ **FULL powers** |
| **Joint sitting** | ❌ Not possible | ✅ **Possible** | ✅ **Possible** |

> 🧠 **The one-line rule:** ⭐ **Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill, but not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill.** Financial Bill (I) resembles a Money Bill at the door and an ordinary bill thereafter.

### ④ Constitutional amendment bills

See [Ch 10](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/10_Amendment_of_the_Constitution.md). ⭐ Key points for this chapter: introduced in **either House**, **no President's recommendation needed**, must be passed **separately by each House by a special majority** — ⚠️ **no joint sitting** — and the **President must give assent** *(24th Amendment)*.

## PART H — The Budget and financial procedure ⭐⭐

### The Annual Financial Statement (Article 112)

⚠️ **The Constitution does not use the word "budget"** — it says **"Annual Financial Statement."**

### ⭐ Charged vs made expenditure

| | **Charged on the Consolidated Fund** | **Made from the Consolidated Fund** |
|---|---|---|
| **Voting** | ⭐ **NON-votable** — discussed but **not submitted to Parliament's vote** | ⭐ **Votable** — Parliament votes on the demands for grants |

**⭐ What is charged (learn this list — it is asked directly):**
1. **Emoluments and allowances of the President**, and other expenditure relating to his office
2. **Salaries and allowances of the Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**, and the **Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha**
3. **Debt charges** for which the Government of India is liable — interest, sinking fund charges, redemption charges
4. ⭐ **Salaries, allowances and pensions of the judges of the Supreme Court**, and **pensions of High Court judges**
5. ⭐ **Salary, allowances and pension of the Comptroller and Auditor General**
6. ⭐ **Salary, allowances and pension of the Chairman and members of the UPSC**
7. **Administrative expenses** of the Supreme Court, the CAG and the UPSC
8. Sums required to satisfy any **judgment, decree or award** of any court or arbitral tribunal
9. Any other expenditure **declared by Parliament** to be so charged

> 🧠 **The logic behind the list: everything charged belongs to an office whose independence depends on not having to ask Parliament for its salary each year.** President, presiding officers, judges, CAG, UPSC. Learn the reason and the list follows.

### ⭐ The six stages of the budget

1. **Presentation** of the budget
2. **General discussion** — the budget as a whole; **no motions, no voting**
3. **Scrutiny by departmental standing committees** — since 1993; see [Ch 23](23_Parliamentary_Committees.md)
4. ⭐ **Voting on demands for grants** — ⚠️ **the exclusive privilege of the LOK SABHA**; the Rajya Sabha has **no power** to vote on demands
5. **Passing of the Appropriation Bill** — legalises withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund
6. **Passing of the Finance Bill** — legalises the **taxation** proposals

### ⭐ Cut motions — a certain Prelims item

| Motion | What it asks | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Policy cut** | That the amount of the demand be **reduced to ₹1** — expressing **disapproval of the policy** underlying the demand | **₹1** |
| ⭐ **Economy cut** | That the amount be **reduced by a specified amount** — to effect **economy** | A specified amount |
| ⭐ **Token cut** | That the amount be **reduced by ₹100** — to ventilate a **specific grievance** within the government's responsibility | **₹100** |

> ⚠️ **In practice, cut motions are almost never passed** — passing one would mean the government has lost the confidence of the House.

### The other grants

| Grant | When |
|---|---|
| **Supplementary grant** | The amount authorised for a service in the current year is found **insufficient** |
| **Additional grant** | **New service** expenditure arises during the year, not contemplated in the budget |
| ⭐ **Excess grant** | Money has been spent **in excess** of the amount granted — voted **after the end of the financial year**, and ⭐ **must first be approved by the Public Accounts Committee** |
| **Vote of credit** | For an **unexpected demand** of such magnitude or indefinite character that it cannot be stated in the usual detail — a **blank cheque** |
| **Exceptional grant** | For a **special purpose**, forming no part of the current service of any financial year |
| ⭐ **Vote on account** *(Article 116)* | An **advance grant** to keep government running until the budget is enacted — ⭐ granted **only for the votable part**, and normally for **two months at one-sixth** of the total estimate |

> **Cited directly in [Mains 2000 Q9(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2000.md), which asked "What is vote on account?"**

### ⭐ The three funds

| Fund | Article | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Consolidated Fund of India** | **266(1)** | All revenues, loans raised and receipts from loan repayments. ⭐ **No money can be withdrawn except by parliamentary appropriation** |
| **Public Account of India** | **266(2)** | Public money **other than** that credited to the Consolidated Fund — provident fund, small savings. ⭐ **Operated by executive action**; parliamentary appropriation **not needed** |
| ⭐ **Contingency Fund of India** | **267** | For **unforeseen expenditure**; ⭐ **held by the Finance Secretary on behalf of the PRESIDENT**; the President can make advances from it **without parliamentary authorisation**, subject to **subsequent** approval |

## PART I — Privileges, sovereignty and decline

### ⭐ Parliamentary privileges (Article 105)

**Individual privileges:**
- ⭐ **Freedom from arrest in CIVIL cases** during the session and **40 days before and after** it. ⚠️ **This does NOT apply to criminal cases or preventive detention.**
- **Freedom of speech in Parliament** — ⭐ **no member is liable to any proceedings in any court** in respect of anything **said or any vote given** in Parliament or its committees *(Article 105(2))*
- **Exemption from jury service**

**Collective privileges:** the right to publish its reports and to prohibit others from publishing; the right to exclude strangers and hold secret sittings; the right to make rules regulating its own procedure; the right to punish members and outsiders for **breach of privilege or contempt**; the right to receive immediate information of a member's arrest; and courts are **prohibited from inquiring into the proceedings** of a House or its committees.

> ⚠️ ⭐ **Privileges have never been codified.** Article 105(3) says they shall be such as Parliament may **from time to time define**, and until then, those which existed **before the coming into force of the 44th Amendment**. **Parliament has deliberately not defined them** — which is itself the controversy, since an uncodified privilege sits uneasily with **Article 19(1)(a)**. *(Asked in [Mains 2014 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2014.md).)*
>
> ⚠️ **Article 361A** *(added by the 44th Amendment)* protects the **publication of substantially true reports of parliamentary proceedings** — except reports of a **secret sitting**.
>
> ⚠️ **Privileges do NOT extend to the President**, though he is a part of Parliament. They **do extend to the Attorney General and ministers**, who have the right to speak in the House.

### ⭐ Sovereignty of Parliament — the British contrast

**The British Parliament is sovereign.** The Indian Parliament is **not**, and is limited by:

1. ⭐ **A written Constitution** with a defined framework
2. ⭐ **The federal division of powers** — the Seventh Schedule ([Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md))
3. ⭐ **Judicial review**
4. ⭐ **Fundamental Rights** — Article 13
5. ⭐ **The basic structure doctrine** ([Ch 11](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/11_Basic_Structure.md))

> *Directly asked in [Mains 2023 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md) — "Compare and contrast the British and Indian approaches to Parliamentary sovereignty."*

### ⚠️ The decline of Parliament — the evidence

| Indicator | The trend |
|---|---|
| **Sittings** | From over **130 days a year** in the first Lok Sabha to roughly **60–70** now |
| **Scrutiny** | A falling share of bills **referred to committees**; some passed within hours of introduction |
| **Question Hour** | Frequently **lost to disruptions**, and occasionally curtailed |
| **Financial control** | Most **demands for grants are guillotined** without discussion |
| **Bypassing the Rajya Sabha** | The **Money Bill route** *(Aadhaar; *Rojer Mathew*, 2019 — pending before a larger bench)* |
| **Executive legislation** | Recourse to **ordinances**, and **re-promulgation** *(*Wadhwa*, 1987; *Krishna Kumar Singh*, 2017)* |
| **The individual MP** | The **whip** plus the **Tenth Schedule** leave almost no space for independent judgement |

> ⭐ **What survives — and the answer to any "so what can be done?" question:** the **Departmentally Related Standing Committees** ([Ch 23](23_Parliamentary_Committees.md)). They meet **in private**, **no whip is issued**, and they work **across party lines** — so the scrutiny that has become impossible on the floor still happens in the committee room. **Every reform proposal starts there:** mandatory referral of bills, guaranteed opposition days, a minimum number of sittings fixed by law, and an independent Speaker on the British model.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The definition
> ⭐ **Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha.** He is **not a member** but **is a part**. *(USA: the President is **not** part of Congress.)*

### The numbers
> **Rajya Sabha:** max **250** = **238 + 12** · actual **245** · **Fourth Schedule** · **⅓ retire every 2 years**, term **6 years** · ⭐ **never dissolved**.
> **Lok Sabha:** max **552** = **530 + 20** · actual **543** · ⚠️ **Anglo-Indian seats abolished — 104th Amendment, 2019** · voting age **18** since the **61st Amendment, 1988**.
> **Age:** Rajya Sabha **30**, Lok Sabha **25**. **Quorum: one-tenth.** **Absence for 60 days** vacates a seat.

### Who decides a disqualification
> ⭐ **Article 102 grounds → the PRESIDENT, on the ELECTION COMMISSION's opinion.**
> ⭐ **Tenth Schedule (defection) → the SPEAKER/CHAIRMAN** — reviewable per ***Kihoto Hollohan*** (1992).

### The Speaker
> **Effective majority + 14 days' notice** to remove · ⭐ **does not vacate on dissolution** · **casting vote only** · ⭐ **certifies Money Bills — final** · **presides over joint sittings** · **salary charged on the Consolidated Fund** · **7th in the order of precedence, with the CJI** · ⚠️ **India has NOT adopted "once a Speaker, always a Speaker."**

### The four endings — A-P-D
> **Adjournment** (presiding officer, sitting, nothing lapses) · **Prorogation** (President, session, **bills survive, notices lapse**) · **Dissolution** (President, the House itself, **most business lapses**).
> ⭐ **Survives dissolution:** bills pending **in the Rajya Sabha alone** · bills **awaiting assent** · bills **returned by the President** · bills where a **joint sitting has been notified**.

### The devices
> ⭐ **Starred = oral + supplementaries. Unstarred = written, none.** **Zero Hour** = Indian innovation, **not in the Rules**, since **1962**. **Adjournment motion** = censure, **Lok Sabha only, 50 members, 2½ hours**. **No-confidence** = **Lok Sabha only, 50 members, no grounds needed**. **Censure** = **must state grounds**, and **passing it does not force resignation**. **Calling attention** = Indian innovation, **not in the Rules**. **Point of order** = **suspends proceedings, no debate**.

### Joint sitting
> ⭐ **Speaker presides** *(never the Chairman)* · quorum **one-tenth of both Houses** · ⚠️ **not for Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills** · held **three times: 1960 Dowry Prohibition · 1977 Banking Service Commission (Repeal) · 2002 POTA**.

### Money Bill
> **Speaker certifies — final** · **Lok Sabha only, on the President's recommendation** · **Rajya Sabha: 14 days, recommendations only** · **deemed passed if not returned** · **President may assent or withhold, not return** · ⚠️ ***Rojer Mathew* (2019) — pending before a larger bench.**

### The budget
> ⭐ **"Annual Financial Statement"** — the word *budget* is not used. **Six stages.** ⭐ **Only the Lok Sabha votes on demands for grants.** **Cut motions: policy ₹1 · economy specified · token ₹100.** ⭐ **Excess grant → must go through the Public Accounts Committee first.** **Vote on account = 2 months at one-sixth.**
> **Funds: 266(1) Consolidated · 266(2) Public Account · 267 Contingency, held by the Finance Secretary for the President.**

### Privileges
> ⭐ **Freedom from arrest in CIVIL cases only, 40 days either side.** **No court proceedings for anything said or any vote given.** ⚠️ **Never codified.** **Article 361A** protects true reports **except of secret sittings**. **Do not extend to the President; do extend to the Attorney General.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "To what extent, in your view, the Parliament is able to ensure accountability of the executive in India?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2021 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2021.md); the same theme in [2000 Q7(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2000.md) and [2003 Q7(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2003.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The instruments** — Question Hour, Zero Hour, half-an-hour and short-duration discussions, calling attention, adjournment and censure motions, **no-confidence motion**, financial control *(demands for grants, cut motions, Appropriation and Finance Bills)*, and the **committees**. (ii) **Why they under-perform** — the executive commands the majority; **the whip plus the Tenth Schedule**; falling sittings; disruptions consuming Question Hour; **the guillotine**; ordinances; the Money Bill route. (iii) ⭐ **Where accountability survives** — the **DRSCs and the financial committees**, which work off-camera and across party lines; the **CAG–PAC chain**. (iv) Conclude: accountability is **strongest in committee and weakest on the floor**; reforms should mandate **committee referral**, fix a **minimum number of sittings**, and protect **opposition days**.

**2. "Individual parliamentarian's role as the national law maker is on a decline, which in turn has adversely impacted the quality of debates and their outcome." Discuss. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2019 Q15](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md); earlier as [2013 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The design** — an MP is meant to legislate, represent and scrutinise. (ii) **The causes of decline** — ⭐ the **Tenth Schedule** removes the freedom to vote on conviction; the **whip** has no statutory basis yet decisive effect; **private members' bills** almost never pass *(only 14 have ever become law, none since 1970)*; **MPLADS** shifting the MP's self-image from legislator to local executive *(see [Mains 2001 Q9(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2001.md))*; **falling sittings** and lost Question Hours; bills passed **without committee referral**. (iii) **Consequences** — thinner debate, weaker laws, more litigation. (iv) **Reforms** — confine the whip to **confidence and money votes** *(as recommended repeatedly)*, mandatory committee referral, more private members' time, research support for MPs.

**3. "Compare and contrast the British and Indian approaches to Parliamentary sovereignty." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2023 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md).*
> *Skeleton:* **Britain** — no written constitution, **no judicial review of primary legislation**, Parliament may make or unmake any law, no entrenched rights *(qualified by the Human Rights Act and devolution)*. **India** — a **written Constitution**, **federal division of powers**, **Fundamental Rights and Article 13**, **judicial review**, and ⭐ **the basic structure doctrine**, which limits even the amending power. Conclude: **India transplanted the Westminster *form* while rejecting its *foundational premise*** — sovereignty rests with the **Constitution**, not with Parliament, and *Kesavananda* is where that difference becomes explicit.

**4. "The 'Powers, Privileges and Immunities of Parliament and its Members' as envisaged in Article 105 of the Constitution leave room for a large number of un-codified and un-enumerated privileges to continue. Assess the reasons for the absence of legal codification of the 'parliamentary privileges'. How can this be addressed?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2014 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2014.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The position** — Article 105(3) leaves privileges to be defined by Parliament; it never has, so they remain those of the **House of Commons as they stood earlier**. (ii) **Why Parliament has not codified** — codification would make privileges **justiciable** and subject to Article 19(1)(a) scrutiny; the House prefers to be the **sole judge of its own contempt**; and any definition would be under-inclusive against unforeseen situations. (iii) **The problems** — conflict with **free speech and press freedom**; no clear procedure for the accused; the House acting as **complainant, judge and prosecutor**. (iv) **The way forward** — codify by statute with defined offences and penalties; provide a right of appeal; restrict privilege to what is **necessary for the functioning of the House**.

**5. "Define Money-bill. Discuss how it is passed in the Parliament." (150 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2004 Q8(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2004.md).*
> *Skeleton:* Article 110's exhaustive list and the three exclusions; the **Speaker's final certification**; introduction **only in the Lok Sabha on the President's recommendation**; the **Rajya Sabha's 14 days** and recommendation-only power; **deemed passage**; the President may **assent or withhold but not return**. Close with the ***Rojer Mathew*** controversy over misuse.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Article 79: Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha.** He is **part of** it but **not a member**. *(USA: President not part of Congress.)*
- **Rajya Sabha:** max **250** *(238 elected + 12 nominated)*, actual **245**; **Fourth Schedule**; elected by **state assemblies' elected members** by **PR–STV**, seats by **population**; only **Delhi, Puducherry, J&K** among UTs; ⭐ **permanent body, ⅓ retire every 2 years, 6-year terms**; **domicile requirement removed 2003**, upheld in ***Kuldip Nayar* (2006)**.
- **Lok Sabha:** max **552** *(530 + 20)*, actual **543**; ⚠️ **Anglo-Indian nomination abolished — 104th Amendment 2019**; voting age **18** *(61st Amendment, 1988)*; **five years**, dissolvable, extendable **one year at a time** during a National Emergency, **not beyond 6 months** after it ends.
- **Delimitation freeze:** **42nd → 84th → 87th**; seats frozen on the **1971 census** until the **first census after 2026**; boundaries redrawn on the **2001** census.
- **Qualification: 30** *(RS)* / **25** *(LS)*. **Disqualification: Article 102 → President on the EC's opinion; Tenth Schedule → Speaker/Chairman**, reviewable *(**Kihoto Hollohan**, 1992)*. Seat vacates on **60 days' absence**.
- **Speaker:** effective majority + **14 days**; ⭐ **does not vacate on dissolution**; **casting vote only**; ⭐ **certifies Money Bills — final**; **presides over joint sittings**; salary **charged** on the CFI; **7th in precedence with the CJI**; ⚠️ **"once a Speaker, always a Speaker" NOT adopted**. **Deputy Speaker is responsible to the House, not the Speaker**, and **automatically chairs any committee he joins**. **Speaker pro tem** appointed by the **President**.
- ⭐ **Leader of the Opposition needs one-tenth of the House** *(statutory recognition, 1977)*. ⭐ **The Whip appears nowhere in law** — pure convention, but enforced through the **Tenth Schedule**.
- **Sessions:** gap **≤ 6 months**; three by convention. **Adjournment** *(officer, sitting)* · **prorogation** *(President, session; **bills survive**, notices lapse)* · **dissolution** *(President, the House; most business lapses)*. ⭐ **Survives dissolution: bills pending in the RS alone · awaiting assent · returned by the President · joint sitting notified.**
- **Devices:** ⭐ **starred = oral + supplementaries; unstarred = written, none**; **Zero Hour** = Indian innovation, **not in the Rules**, since **1962**; **adjournment motion** = **LS only, 50 members, 2½ hrs, censure**; **no-confidence** = **LS only, 50 members, no grounds**; **censure must state grounds and does not force resignation**; **calling attention** = Indian innovation; **point of order suspends proceedings, no debate**; **guillotine** = undiscussed items put to vote for want of time.
- ⭐ **Joint sitting (108):** **Speaker presides**; quorum **one-tenth of both Houses**; ⚠️ **not for Money or Constitutional Amendment Bills**; **three times — 1960, 1977, 2002**.
- ⭐ **Money Bill (110):** Speaker certifies **finally**; **LS only, President's recommendation**; **RS 14 days, recommendations only**; **deemed passed**; President **cannot return**. ⚠️ ***Rojer Mathew* (2019)** pending before a larger bench. **Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill; not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill.**
- **Budget:** ⭐ **"Annual Financial Statement" (112)**; **charged = non-votable** *(President, presiding officers, debt, SC judges, CAG, UPSC, decrees)*; **six stages**; ⭐ **only the Lok Sabha votes demands for grants**; **cut motions ₹1 / specified / ₹100**; ⭐ **excess grant needs PAC approval first**; **vote on account = 2 months at one-sixth**; **funds 266(1), 266(2), 267**.
- **Privileges (105):** ⭐ **arrest immunity in CIVIL cases only, 40 days either side**; **no proceedings for anything said or voted**; ⚠️ **never codified**; **Article 361A** protects true reports **except secret sittings**; **do not extend to the President**, **do extend to the Attorney General**.
- ⭐ **Not sovereign** — limited by the written Constitution, federalism, judicial review, Fundamental Rights and **basic structure**.
