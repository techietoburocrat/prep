# Chapter 23 — Parliamentary Committees

[← Chapter 22](22_Parliament.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 24 →](24_Parliamentary_Forums.md)

**Read time ~14 min** · **Articles 105 and 118** · 🔴 Prelims: 1 most years · Mains: **2011, 2017, 2018, 2021, 2023** — a **very** frequent topic

> 📌 **Read this immediately after [Ch 22](22_Parliament.md).** That chapter documented Parliament's decline as a check on the executive. **This chapter is where the check survives** — and that framing is worth more marks than any list of committees.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **Committees do what the floor of the House no longer can.** They meet **in private**, **no whip is issued**, they work **across party lines**, they can **summon officials and examine documents**, and they are not performing for cameras. Woodrow Wilson's line is the standard quotation: **"Congress in session is Congress on public exhibition, whilst Congress in its committee rooms is Congress at work."**

---

## 📖 The story

A modern legislature has an impossible workload. Hundreds of bills, a budget running to lakhs of crores, dozens of ministries — and roughly sixty sitting days a year to examine all of it.

Worse, the floor of the House is the **worst possible place** to examine anything carefully. It is adversarial, televised, whipped, and frequently disrupted. A minister answering on the floor is performing for an audience; the opposition questioning him is doing the same.

**The committee room inverts every one of those conditions.**

> 💡 **The analogy:** the floor of the House is a **public trial** — theatrical, adversarial, and shaped by who is watching. The committee is the **private examination beforehand** — where documents are actually read, officials are questioned without an audience, and people on opposite sides can agree on facts without it looking like surrender.
>
> **That privacy is the feature, not a defect.** An MP who cannot criticise his own government on television can do it in a committee room, and the report goes out **in the committee's name**, not his.

**And it demonstrably works.** Committee reports have forced the withdrawal or substantial amendment of major bills. The **Public Accounts Committee**, chaired by an **opposition member** by convention, has produced findings that governments could not brush aside — the **2G spectrum** and **Bofors** examinations among them.

**The catch:** committees only bite if bills are **referred** to them, and referral is **not mandatory**. That single fact is the crux of nearly every Mains question on this chapter.

## PART A — The basics

### Constitutional and legal basis

- Parliamentary committees draw authority from **Article 105** *(privileges of members)* and **Article 118** *(power of each House to make rules regulating its procedure)*.
- ⭐ A parliamentary committee is one which is **appointed or elected by the House, or nominated by the Speaker/Chairman**; **works under the direction of the Speaker/Chairman**; **presents its report to the House or to the Speaker/Chairman**; and has a **secretariat provided by the Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha Secretariat**.

> ⚠️ **Consultative Committees of Parliament are NOT parliamentary committees.** They consist of MPs attached to various ministries, are constituted by the **Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs**, and provide an informal forum for discussion — but they do **not** satisfy the definition above. **A standard Prelims trap.**

### ⭐ The two broad kinds

| | **Standing Committees** | **Ad hoc Committees** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | **Permanent** — constituted every year or periodically, working on a **continuous basis** | **Temporary** — cease to exist on completing the task assigned |
| **Examples** | Financial committees, DRSCs, committees to enquire, to scrutinise, relating to the day-to-day business of the House, House-keeping committees | **Select or Joint Committees on Bills**, and committees like the **Railway Convention Committee** and the Joint Committee on Food Management |

## PART B — ⭐⭐ The three financial committees

**These are the most examinable committees in the chapter.** Build the comparison table until you can write it from memory.

| | **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** | **Estimates Committee** | **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Established** | ⭐ **1921** — the oldest, under the Government of India Act, 1919 | **1950** | ⭐ **1964**, on the recommendation of the **Krishna Menon Committee** |
| ⭐ **Strength** | **22** | ⭐ **30** | ⭐ **22** |
| ⭐ **Composition** | **15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha** | ⚠️ **ALL 30 from the LOK SABHA — no Rajya Sabha members at all** | **15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha** |
| **Term** | **One year** | **One year** | **One year** |
| **Election** | By the members of Parliament, by **proportional representation with the single transferable vote** | Same | Same |
| ⭐ **Chairman** | ⭐ Appointed by the **Speaker**; since **1967, by convention, from the OPPOSITION** | Appointed by the **Speaker**, always **from the ruling party** | Appointed by the **Speaker**; may be from the Lok Sabha or the Rajya Sabha |
| ⚠️ **Ministers** | **A minister cannot be elected** to it | **A minister cannot be elected** | **A minister cannot be elected** |
| **Function** | ⭐ Examines the **annual audit reports of the CAG**, which the President lays before Parliament | ⭐ Examines the **estimates** in the budget and suggests **economies** | Examines the **reports and accounts of public undertakings** and the CAG's reports on them |
| **Nickname** | — | ⭐ **"The continuous economy committee"** | — |

### ⭐ The Public Accounts Committee — the details that matter

**What it examines:**
- The **appropriation accounts** and the **CAG's annual audit reports**, satisfying itself that the money was **legally available** for the purpose, that the expenditure **conformed to the authority governing it**, and that reappropriations were made in accordance with the rules
- The accounts of **state corporations, trading and manufacturing schemes** and **autonomous bodies** *(except those allotted to COPU)*
- Money spent **in excess** of the amount granted — ⭐ **an excess grant must be approved by the PAC before it goes to Parliament** ([Ch 22](22_Parliament.md))

⭐ **The CAG is its "friend, philosopher and guide."** The relationship is the chapter's most citable fact: **the CAG audits, the PAC examines, Parliament acts.** Without the PAC, a CAG report is a document nobody is obliged to read.

**⚠️ Its four limitations — reproduce these in any critical answer:**
1. It examines matters ***post facto*** — **after the money has already been spent**
2. It **cannot intervene** in matters of day-to-day administration
3. Its recommendations are **advisory, not binding** on the ministries
4. ⭐ It is **not vested with the power of disallowance of expenditure** by the departments
5. It is **not an executive body** and so **cannot issue an order** — only Parliament can take a final decision

> ⭐ **Why the Chairman comes from the opposition** — a convention since **1967** — is worth a sentence in every answer: a committee auditing government spending is credible only if it is not chaired by the government. **It is the single most effective accountability convention in Indian parliamentary practice**, and it is precisely what [Mains 2017 Q5](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2017.md) asked about.

### The Estimates Committee — the two things to remember

- ⭐ **The largest committee**, with **30 members, all from the Lok Sabha.**
- It examines estimates and suggests **economies, improvements in organisation, efficiency and administrative reform**; suggests **alternative policies** to bring about efficiency and economy; and examines whether the money is **well laid out** within the limits of the policy implied in the estimates.
- ⚠️ **It cannot question the policy laid down by Parliament** — only its **execution**.
- ⚠️ It examines **only a few selected ministries each year**, so its coverage is thin.

## PART C — ⭐⭐ The Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs)

**This is the modern heart of the chapter and the most likely Mains topic.**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Established** | **1993** — a landmark reform. Originally **17**, restructured to **24 in 2004** |
| ⭐ **Strength** | **31 members each — 21 from the Lok Sabha + 10 from the Rajya Sabha** |
| **Which House services them** | ⭐ **16 committees** are under the **Lok Sabha**, **8** under the **Rajya Sabha** |
| **Appointment** | Lok Sabha members **nominated by the Speaker**; Rajya Sabha members **nominated by the Chairman** |
| ⚠️ **Ministers** | ⭐ **A minister cannot be a member** of any DRSC |
| **Term** | **One year** |

**Their four functions:**
1. To consider the **demands for grants** of the related ministries **before they are discussed and voted** in the Lok Sabha — ⭐ their report **must not suggest cut motions**
2. To examine **bills referred to them** by the Speaker/Chairman
3. To consider the **annual reports** of the ministries
4. To consider **national basic long-term policy documents** presented to the House

⚠️ **What they may NOT do:** consider matters of **day-to-day administration**, or matters under consideration by **other parliamentary committees**. ⭐ **Their recommendations are advisory and not binding.**

### ⭐ Why the DRSCs matter — the argument for Mains

| Advantage | Why |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Detailed scrutiny of the budget** | Before 1993 the demands for grants of most ministries were **guillotined without discussion**. The DRSCs give every ministry's demands an examination somewhere |
| ⭐ **Expertise develops** | Members stay with one sector and build knowledge no generalist debate can |
| ⭐ **Non-partisan working** | ⚠️ **No whip is issued in a committee**, and proceedings are **in private** — so members can act on evidence rather than party position |
| **Access to officials and papers** | Committees can **summon officials**, take evidence, and call for documents |
| **Continuity** | They work **throughout the year**, including when the House is not sitting |

### ⚠️ Why they under-perform — the other half of the answer

1. ⭐ **Referral of bills is NOT mandatory** — it is at the discretion of the **Speaker/Chairman**, and the proportion of bills referred has **fallen sharply**; several major laws have been passed with **no committee scrutiny at all**
2. **Recommendations are not binding** on the government
3. **One-year terms** limit the accumulation of expertise the design intends
4. **Low attendance** at many meetings
5. They **cannot examine day-to-day administration**, which is where much accountability actually lies
6. **No technical or research support** comparable to legislative committees elsewhere

> ⭐ **The reform that every answer should end with: make committee referral of bills the default**, with the House required to record reasons for **not** referring. That single change would restore most of what [Ch 22](22_Parliament.md) documents as lost.

## PART D — The other committees

*Know the names and one distinguishing feature each. Do not memorise strengths beyond the financial committees and the DRSCs.*

### Committees to enquire

| Committee | Function |
|---|---|
| **Committee on Petitions** | Considers petitions on bills and on matters of general public importance; also considers **representations from individuals** |
| ⭐ **Committee of Privileges** | **Semi-judicial** in nature; examines **breach of privilege** of the House or its members and **recommends appropriate action**. **Lok Sabha: 15 members; Rajya Sabha: 10** |
| **Committee on Ethics** | ⭐ Enforces the **code of conduct of members** and examines cases of **misconduct**. *(Tested in [Prelims 2024 Q12](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2024.md).)* Initially an **ad hoc** committee in the Lok Sabha, later made **standing** |

### Committees to scrutinise and control

| Committee | Function |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Committee on Government Assurances** | ⭐ Examines the **assurances, promises and undertakings given by ministers** on the floor and reports on the **extent to which they have been implemented**. **Lok Sabha 15, Rajya Sabha 10.** ⚠️ *A very commonly asked committee* |
| ⭐ **Committee on Subordinate Legislation** | ⭐ Examines whether the powers of **delegated legislation** conferred by the Constitution or by Parliament are being **properly exercised** by the executive. **15 members in each House** |
| **Committee on Papers Laid on the Table** | Examines all papers laid on the table by ministers to see whether there has been **compliance with the relevant provisions** |
| ⭐ **Committee on the Welfare of SCs and STs** | **30 members — 20 Lok Sabha + 10 Rajya Sabha.** Considers the reports of the **National Commissions for SCs and STs** and reviews their welfare. ⚠️ *See the composition trap in [Prelims 2026 Q5](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2026.md)* |
| **Committee on Empowerment of Women** | **30 members — 20 LS + 10 RS**; reviews measures for women's empowerment |
| **Joint Committee on Offices of Profit** | ⭐ **15 members — 10 LS + 5 RS.** Examines the composition of committees and bodies appointed by government and recommends **which offices should disqualify** a member |

### Committees relating to the day-to-day business of the House

**Business Advisory Committee** *(allocates time for legislative business — chaired by the Speaker)* · **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions** *(⚠️ **a Lok Sabha committee only**; the Rajya Sabha's function is performed by its Business Advisory Committee)* · **Rules Committee** *(chaired by the Speaker)* · **Committee on Absence of Members** *(⚠️ **Lok Sabha only**)*.

### House-keeping committees

**General Purposes Committee** *(chaired by the Speaker)* · **House Committee** · **Library Committee** · **Joint Committee on Salaries and Allowances of Members**.

## PART E — ⚠️ Committees you must not confuse

| | **Departmentally Related Standing Committee** | **Select / Joint Committee on a Bill** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | ⭐ **Standing** — permanent, reconstituted annually | ⭐ **Ad hoc** — dissolved once it reports |
| **Scope** | The **whole sector** of the related ministries — bills, demands for grants, annual reports, policy documents | ⭐ **One specific bill only** |
| **Composition** | **31 members** *(21 LS + 10 RS)* | Varies; a **Select Committee** is of one House, a **Joint Committee** of both |
| **How constituted** | Nominated **annually** by the Speaker/Chairman | Constituted by a **motion in the House** for that bill |

> ⭐ **This exact distinction was asked in [Mains 2011 Q4(d)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2011.md)** — *"Distinction between 'Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committees' and 'Parliamentary Forums'."* Know the DRSC/Select-Committee distinction **and** the DRSC/Forum distinction ([Ch 24](24_Parliamentary_Forums.md)).

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The three financial committees — the numbers
> ⭐ **PAC: 1921 · 22 members · 15 LS + 7 RS · Chairman from the OPPOSITION since 1967 · examines the CAG's reports.**
> ⭐ **Estimates: 1950 · 30 members · ALL from the LOK SABHA · "the continuous economy committee."**
> ⭐ **COPU: 1964 (Krishna Menon Committee) · 22 members · 15 LS + 7 RS.**
>
> 🧠 **22 · 30 · 22** — the odd one out is the Estimates Committee, which is also the only one with **no Rajya Sabha members**.

### The DRSCs
> ⭐ **1993** · originally **17**, now **24** *(since 2004)* · **31 members = 21 LS + 10 RS** · **16 under the Lok Sabha, 8 under the Rajya Sabha** · ⚠️ **a minister cannot be a member** · term **one year** · **recommendations advisory** · ⭐ **their reports must not suggest cut motions**.

### The rule that applies to all of them
> ⚠️ ⭐ **A minister cannot be a member of the financial committees or the DRSCs.** *(He is being scrutinised, not scrutinising.)*

### The quotation
> ⭐ **Woodrow Wilson: "Congress in session is Congress on public exhibition, whilst Congress in its committee rooms is Congress at work."**

### The single most important structural fact
> ⭐ **Referral of a bill to a committee is NOT mandatory.** Everything that is wrong with parliamentary scrutiny in India follows from that one discretionary decision.

### The traps
> ⚠️ **Consultative Committees are NOT parliamentary committees** *(Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs)*.
> ⚠️ **Committee on Private Members' Bills and Committee on Absence of Members are LOK SABHA ONLY.**
> ⚠️ **Welfare of SCs and STs = 30 members, 20 LS + 10 RS**, chaired by a person appointed by the **Speaker**.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Explain the structure of the Parliamentary Committee system. How far have the financial committees helped in the institutionalisation of the Indian Parliament?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2023 Q16](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **Structure** — standing vs ad hoc; then the families: **financial** *(PAC, Estimates, COPU)*, **DRSCs**, enquiry, scrutiny, day-to-day, house-keeping. (ii) **How the financial committees institutionalised Parliament** — the **PAC–CAG chain** converts an audit report into political accountability; the **opposition chairmanship since 1967** made scrutiny credible; the Estimates Committee introduced **ex-ante** examination of spending; **COPU** brought public undertakings, otherwise outside ministerial answerability, within Parliament's reach; the **excess-grant route** through the PAC gives Parliament a formal hold on unauthorised spending. (iii) **Limits** — *post facto* examination, advisory recommendations, no power of disallowance, thin coverage. (iv) Conclude: they institutionalised **process and expertise**, not **enforcement** — Parliament gained a memory, not a sanction.

**2. "Do Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees keep the administration on its toes and inspire reverence for parliamentary control?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2021 Q15](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2021.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **Yes, in design and often in fact** — pre-1993 the demands for grants were guillotined; DRSCs give every ministry annual scrutiny; **no whip, private sittings, cross-party working**; power to summon officials; reports have forced amendment and withdrawal of bills. (ii) **But the reverence is qualified** — ⭐ **referral is discretionary** and falling; recommendations are **not binding**; one-year terms; low attendance; **no research support**; they cannot touch day-to-day administration. (iii) Conclude with a reform list: **mandatory referral with recorded reasons for exceptions**, longer terms, research staff, and a requirement that government **respond to recommendations within a fixed period**.

**3. "Why do you think the committees are considered to be useful for parliamentary work? Discuss, in this context, the role of the Estimates Committee." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2018 Q4](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2018.md).*
> *Skeleton:* **Why useful** — workload, expertise, privacy, absence of whip, continuity, access to officials *(the Wilson quotation)*. **The Estimates Committee specifically** — 30 members all from the Lok Sabha; examines estimates to suggest **economies and administrative reform**; the **"continuous economy committee"**; ⚠️ it may question **execution but not policy**, and covers only a few ministries a year. Conclude by comparing it with the PAC: **the Estimates Committee looks forward at what is proposed; the PAC looks back at what was spent** — together they cover both ends of the financial cycle.

**4. "Discuss the role of the Public Accounts Committee in establishing accountability of the government to the people." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2017 Q5](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2017.md).*
> *Skeleton:* Composition and the **opposition chairmanship since 1967**; the **CAG as "friend, philosopher and guide"**; what it examines *(appropriation accounts, CAG reports, excess grants, autonomous bodies)*; landmark examinations. Then the **four limitations** *(post facto, no day-to-day role, advisory, no power of disallowance)*. Conclude: the PAC converts **audit into accountability**, but the final sanction rests with Parliament and ultimately the electorate — the committee supplies the **evidence**, not the **punishment**.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- **Basis: Articles 105 and 118.** ⚠️ **Consultative Committees are NOT parliamentary committees** *(Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs)*.
- **Two kinds: standing** *(permanent)* and **ad hoc** *(Select/Joint Committees on Bills, Railway Convention Committee)*.
- ⭐ **PAC — 1921 · 22 members (15 LS + 7 RS) · Chairman from the OPPOSITION since 1967 · examines the CAG's audit reports · approves excess grants first.** CAG is its **"friend, philosopher and guide."** ⚠️ Limits: **post facto**, no day-to-day role, **advisory**, **no power of disallowance**, not an executive body.
- ⭐ **Estimates Committee — 1950 · 30 members · ALL LOK SABHA · "continuous economy committee"** · examines estimates for **economies and administrative reform** · ⚠️ **cannot question policy**, only execution · covers few ministries a year.
- ⭐ **COPU — 1964, on the Krishna Menon Committee's recommendation · 22 members (15 LS + 7 RS)** · examines public undertakings and the CAG's reports on them.
- ⚠️ **A minister cannot be a member of any financial committee or DRSC.** All three financial committees: **one-year term**, elected by **PR–STV**, chairman appointed by the **Speaker**.
