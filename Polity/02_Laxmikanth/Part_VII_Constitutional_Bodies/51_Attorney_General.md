# Chapter 51 — Attorney General of India

[← Chapter 50](50_CAG.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 52 →](52_Advocate_General.md)

**Read time ~8 min** · **Article 76** · 🟠 Prelims: **2013, 2022** · ⚠️ **Mains: 2019 AND 2025** — asked twice in seven years, unusual for a "small" chapter

> 📌 **Rated 🟠, and asked like a 🔴.** ⭐ **[Mains 2019 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md) and [Mains 2025 Q5](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2025.md) both asked about this one office.** ⚠️ **Do not skim it.** The examiner's angle is the paradox in Part C: **the government's own lawyer who is not a government servant, and who may take private clients.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **The Attorney General is the highest law officer in India — and deliberately not a member of the government.** ⚠️ **He advises the executive, appears for it, sits in Parliament without a vote, and yet holds office at pleasure with no fixed term.** ⭐ **The office was designed for independent legal counsel; whether it delivers that is the whole Mains debate.**

---

## PART A — The office

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Article** | ⭐ **76** *(Part V — the Union Executive)*. ⭐ **He is the HIGHEST LAW OFFICER of the country** |
| ⭐ **Appointed by** | ⭐ **The PRESIDENT** |
| ⭐⭐ **Qualification** | ⭐ **A person QUALIFIED TO BE APPOINTED A JUDGE OF THE SUPREME COURT** — i.e. a citizen of India who has been a **judge of a High Court for 5 years**, or an **advocate of a High Court for 10 years**, or is in the President's opinion a **distinguished jurist** |
| ⚠️ ⭐ **Term** | ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The Constitution fixes NO term** |
| ⚠️ ⭐ **Removal** | ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The Constitution prescribes NO grounds and NO procedure.** He ⭐ **holds office during the PLEASURE OF THE PRESIDENT** and may be removed at any time |
| **Remuneration** | ⚠️ **Not fixed by the Constitution — determined by the PRESIDENT** |
| ⭐ **Convention** | ⭐ **He resigns when the government that appointed him resigns or is replaced**, since his appointment is made on that government's advice |

> ⭐⭐ **Compare this with the CAG in one line and the point lands:** ⭐ **the CAG has a fixed term, judge-grade removal, charged expenditure and a bar on future office; the Attorney General has NONE of the four.** ⚠️ **The Constitution protected the auditor of the government and left the lawyer of the government at its pleasure — because one is a watchdog and the other is counsel.**

---

## PART B — ⭐ Duties and rights

### ⭐ Duties — Article 76(2)

1. ⭐ **To give ADVICE to the Government of India on such legal matters as are REFERRED to him by the President**;
2. ⭐ **To perform such other duties of a LEGAL CHARACTER as are assigned to him by the President**;
3. ⭐ **To discharge the functions conferred on him by the Constitution or any other law.**

⭐ **The three duties the President has in fact assigned:**
> ⭐ **(a)** To appear on behalf of the Government of India in **all cases in the SUPREME COURT** in which the Government is concerned;
> ⭐ **(b)** To represent the Government of India in any ⭐ **REFERENCE made by the President to the Supreme Court under ARTICLE 143**;
> ⭐ **(c)** To appear in **any HIGH COURT** in any case in which the Government of India is concerned.

### ⭐⭐ Rights and privileges

| Right | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Right of audience** | ⭐⭐ **In ALL COURTS in the territory of India** — a right no other advocate has by virtue of office |
| ⭐⭐ **Parliamentary rights *(Article 88)*** | ⭐ **He may SPEAK IN, and TAKE PART IN the proceedings of, BOTH HOUSES of Parliament, their JOINT SITTING, and any COMMITTEE of Parliament of which he is named a member** — ⚠️ ⭐ **but he has NO RIGHT TO VOTE** |
| ⭐ **Privileges** | ⭐ **He enjoys all the privileges and immunities of a MEMBER OF PARLIAMENT** *(Article 105)* |
| ⭐ **Contempt** | ⭐ Under the **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971**, ⭐ **criminal contempt proceedings in the Supreme Court initiated by a private person require the CONSENT of the Attorney General** *(or the Solicitor General)* |

> ⭐⭐ **Article 88 is the examinable one.** ⚠️ **"Speak and take part, but not vote" is the exact formula** — and note that it applies to ⭐ **ministers who are not members of a House as well**. *(See [Ch 22](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md).)*

---

## PART C — ⚠️⚠️ The limitations, and the paradox

### ⭐ The four self-denying rules

⭐ **To avoid a conflict between duty and interest, the Attorney General:**
1. ⚠️ ⭐ **Should NOT advise or hold a brief AGAINST the Government of India**;
2. ⚠️ **Should NOT advise or hold a brief in cases in which he is called upon to advise or appear FOR the Government of India**;
3. ⚠️ ⭐ **Should NOT defend accused persons in criminal prosecutions without the permission of the Government of India**;
4. ⚠️ **Should NOT accept appointment as a DIRECTOR in any company or corporation without the permission of the Government of India.**

### ⭐⭐ The paradox that makes this a Mains chapter

| ⚠️ **What he is NOT** | ⭐ **Consequence** |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ ⭐ **NOT a full-time counsel for the government** | ⭐ **He is NOT debarred from PRIVATE LEGAL PRACTICE** — subject only to the four rules above |
| ⚠️ ⭐ **NOT a member of the Cabinet** | ⚠️ **Unlike the BRITISH Attorney General, who is a member of the ministry and answerable in the House of Commons** |
| ⚠️ **NOT a government servant** | He is not subject to service rules or to Article 311 |
| ⚠️ **NOT the only law officer** | ⭐⭐ **The SOLICITOR GENERAL and the ADDITIONAL SOLICITORS GENERAL assist him — and they are NOT mentioned in the Constitution at all.** ⚠️ **Article 76 creates ONE office only** *(exactly the distinction [Prelims 2022 Q7](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2022.md) tested)* |

> ⭐⭐ **The line to use in an answer:** ⚠️ **"The Attorney General is the government's lawyer, not the government's officer."** ⭐ **He owes a duty to the court as an advocate and a duty to the Union as counsel, and the Constitution resolves the tension by keeping him outside the executive rather than inside it.**

### ⚠️ The criticisms, in one list

1. ⚠️ ⭐ **Pleasure tenure with no fixed term** — independence rests entirely on personal standing;
2. ⚠️ ⭐ **Private practice** raises a continuing conflict-of-interest question, and the permission requirements are administered by the very government he advises;
3. ⚠️ **No obligation to publish his opinions**, so Parliament rarely sees the legal advice on which major decisions rest;
4. ⚠️ **The parliamentary right under Article 88 is almost never used**, so the accountability channel the Constitution opened lies unused;
5. ⚠️ **Ambiguity about whom he serves** — the *Union of India* as a legal person, or the *government of the day* — surfaces whenever he must advise on a politically contested question.

⭐ **The reform suggestions worth naming:** a **fixed term**, a **bar on private practice** with compensating remuneration, **publication of formal opinions** on constitutional questions, and **statutory recognition of the Solicitor General's office**.

---

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The four "nots"
> ⚠️ ⭐ **NOT a Cabinet member · NOT a government servant · NOT a full-time counsel *(private practice allowed)* · NOT the only law officer — but the ONLY one in the Constitution.**

### The article set
> ⭐ **76 — the office · 88 — speak and take part in Parliament, NO VOTE · 105 — privileges of an MP · 143 — Presidential references, which he argues.**

### Qualification and tenure
> ⭐ **Qualified to be a SUPREME COURT JUDGE.** ⚠️ ⭐ **No term, no removal procedure — office held at the PRESIDENT'S PLEASURE.** ⭐ **Convention: he goes when the government goes.**

### The four self-denying rules
> ⚠️ ⭐ **No brief against the Union · no brief where he advises the Union · no defending the accused without permission · no company directorship without permission.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The Attorney General of India plays a crucial role in guiding the legal framework of the Union Government and ensuring sound governance through legal counsel." Discuss his responsibilities, rights and limitations in this regard. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2025 Q5](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2025.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **The office** — Article 76; **highest law officer**; appointed by the **President**; qualified to be a **Supreme Court judge**; ⚠️ **pleasure tenure, no fixed term**. (ii) ⭐ **Responsibilities** — advice on legal matters referred by the President; other legal duties assigned; functions under the Constitution and law; in practice, appearing for the Union in **all Supreme Court cases**, in ⭐ **Article 143 references**, and in the High Courts; ⭐ **consent for contempt proceedings** under the 1971 Act. (iii) ⭐ **Rights** — ⭐ **audience in ALL courts in India**; ⭐ **Article 88 — speak and take part in both Houses, joint sittings and committees, WITHOUT a vote**; ⭐ **Article 105 privileges**. (iv) ⚠️ ⭐ **Limitations** — the **four self-denying rules**; **not a Cabinet member**, **not a government servant**, **not full-time**; ⚠️ **removable at pleasure**. (v) ⭐ **How he "ensures sound governance"** — by testing executive proposals against constitutional limits *before* they become litigation, by defending the Union's position coherently across courts, and by advising on **Article 143** references that pre-empt constitutional crises. ⚠️ **Where the design is weak** — pleasure tenure, private practice and unpublished opinions. **Conclude** with the reform list.

**2. "The Attorney-General is the chief legal adviser and lawyer of the Government of India." Discuss. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2019 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md).*
> *Skeleton:* Take the statement in two halves. ⭐ **As "chief legal ADVISER"** — Article 76(2)(a); advice on references from the President; the practical role in vetting Bills, ordinances and constitutional questions; ⭐ **his Article 88 access to Parliament**, which no other adviser has. ⭐ **As "LAWYER"** — the three assigned duties *(Supreme Court, Article 143 references, High Courts)*; **audience in all courts**. ⚠️ **Then complicate the statement** — he is **not the government's employee**; he may take **private clients**; the **Solicitor General** does much of the actual arguing; and ⚠️ **his advice binds nobody**. **Conclude:** the description is accurate but incomplete — ⭐ **he is the Union's counsel, an officer of the court, and a participant in Parliament, and the Constitution deliberately made him all three at once.**

**3. "Should the Attorney General of India be given a fixed tenure?" (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* **For** — protects independent advice on politically inconvenient questions; parity with the **CAG, CEC and UPSC** model; ends the perception that counsel serves the party rather than the Union; would sit naturally with a **bar on private practice**. **Against** — he is the government's **chosen counsel**, and a client must be able to change its lawyer; a fixed-term AG whom the government does not trust would simply be **bypassed by the Solicitor General**; and the office is **advisory, not adjudicatory**, so the independence argument is weaker than for the CAG. ⭐ **Conclude:** the stronger reform is **transparency of formal opinions and a bar on conflicting private practice**, rather than tenure — because the risk here is **conflict of interest**, not dismissal.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Article 76 — the highest law officer of India**, appointed by the **President**, ⭐ **qualified to be a SUPREME COURT JUDGE**. ⚠️ ⭐ **No fixed term, no removal procedure — holds office at the President's PLEASURE**; remuneration fixed by the President; ⭐ **by convention resigns with the government**.
- ⭐ **Duties (76(2)):** advise on legal matters **referred by the President** · other duties of a **legal character** assigned by the President · functions under the Constitution or any law. ⭐ **In practice:** all **Supreme Court** cases concerning the Union · ⭐ **Article 143 references** · any **High Court** case concerning the Union.
- ⭐⭐ **Rights: audience in ALL courts in India** · ⭐ **Article 88 — speak and take part in both Houses, joint sittings and any committee of which he is a member, but NO VOTE** · ⭐ **Article 105 privileges of an MP** · ⭐ **consent for Supreme Court contempt proceedings** *(Contempt of Courts Act, 1971)*.
- ⚠️ ⭐ **Four limitations:** no brief **against** the Union · no brief where he advises the Union · **no defending the accused** without permission · **no company directorship** without permission.
- ⚠️ ⭐ **NOT a Cabinet member · NOT a government servant · NOT full-time — private practice is allowed.** ⭐⭐ **The SOLICITOR GENERAL and Additional Solicitors General are NOT in the Constitution.**
- ⚠️ **Criticisms:** pleasure tenure · private practice conflicts · unpublished opinions · Article 88 rights unused · ambiguity between the *Union* and the *government of the day*.
