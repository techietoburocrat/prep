# Chapter 44 — Finance Commission

[← Chapter 43](43_State_Public_Service_Commission.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 45 →](45_GST_Council.md)

**Read time ~13 min** · **Article 280 (and 281)** · 🔴 Prelims: recurring · ⚠️ **Mains: 2013, 2018, 2021** — asked on a roughly four-year cycle, always about a *specific* Commission

> 📌 **The examiner never asks "what is the Finance Commission".** ⭐ **He asks what the 13th, 14th or 15th Commission DID differently.** So learn the institution in ten minutes and then spend your time on the **award-by-award table in Part C** — that is where every Mains mark in this chapter sits.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐ **The Constitution gave the Union the productive taxes and gave the states the expensive responsibilities.** That mismatch is deliberate and permanent. ⭐⭐ **The Finance Commission is the constitutional device for correcting it every five years — the balancing wheel of Indian fiscal federalism.**

---

## PART A — The institution

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Article** | ⭐ **280** *(reporting: **281**)* |
| **Nature** | ⭐ **QUASI-JUDICIAL** body — it hears the Union and the states as parties and makes a reasoned award |
| ⭐ **Constituted by** | ⭐ **The PRESIDENT — every FIFTH year, or earlier if he considers it necessary** |
| ⭐ **Composition** | ⭐⭐ **A Chairman + FOUR other members** |
| **Term** | Specified by the President in his order; members are **eligible for reappointment** |
| ⭐ **Qualifications** | ⚠️ **Not in the Constitution** — Parliament laid them down in the ⭐ **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951** |

### ⭐ The qualifications — a neat, examinable list

| Post | Must be |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Chairman** | A person having **experience in PUBLIC AFFAIRS** |
| ⭐ **Member 1** | A **judge of a High Court**, or one **qualified to be appointed** as such |
| ⭐ **Member 2** | A person with **special knowledge of the FINANCES AND ACCOUNTS of government** |
| ⭐ **Member 3** | A person with **wide experience in FINANCIAL MATTERS AND ADMINISTRATION** |
| ⭐ **Member 4** | A person with **special knowledge of ECONOMICS** |

> ⭐ **Notice what that list is doing:** ⚠️ **a judge, an accountant, an administrator and an economist — the Commission is built to be a TRIBUNAL, not a committee of the finance ministry.** That is the textual basis for calling it *quasi-judicial*, and it is worth one line in any answer on its independence.

## PART B — ⭐⭐ Functions: Article 280(3), all four clauses

The Commission **recommends to the President**:

| Clause | Recommendation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **(a)** | ⭐ **The DISTRIBUTION between the Union and the states of the net proceeds of taxes** to be divided between them, **and the allocation of the states' share AMONG the states** | ⭐ **This is the "vertical" and "horizontal" devolution — the Commission's core work** |
| ⭐ **(b)** | ⭐ **The PRINCIPLES governing GRANTS-IN-AID to the states** out of the Consolidated Fund of India | These are the ⭐ **Article 275** statutory grants |
| ⭐ **(bb)** | ⭐ **Measures needed to augment the Consolidated Fund of a STATE to supplement the resources of the PANCHAYATS**, on the basis of the recommendations of the **State Finance Commission** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Added by the 73rd Amendment, 1992** |
| ⭐ **(c)** | ⭐ **The same, for MUNICIPALITIES** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Added by the 74th Amendment, 1992** |
| **(d)** | ⭐ **Any other matter referred to it by the PRESIDENT in the interests of SOUND FINANCE** | ⚠️ **This is the elastic clause** — the terms of reference travel through it, and it is where controversy usually starts |

> ⭐⭐ **Clauses (bb) and (c) are the constitutional bridge to [Part V](../Part_V_Local_Government/00_INDEX.md).** ⚠️ **The Union Finance Commission does not fund local bodies directly** — it recommends how a **state's** Consolidated Fund should be augmented so that the **state** can pass money down. ⭐ **This indirect route is exactly why local bodies remain fiscally starved**, and it is the point [Mains 2013 Q7](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md) was probing.

### ⭐ Article 281 — the report

⭐ **The President lays every recommendation of the Commission, together with an EXPLANATORY MEMORANDUM as to the ACTION TAKEN on it, before BOTH HOUSES of Parliament.**

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The recommendations are ADVISORY, not binding.** ⭐ **But the convention is near-absolute on the tax-devolution part: no government has ever rejected the vertical share.** Grants and conditionalities are where governments have deviated. ⭐ **Use that split — "binding by convention on devolution, discretionary on grants" — rather than a flat "not binding".**

### ⚠️⚠️ The distinction that examiners love: Article 275 vs Article 282

| | ⭐ **Article 275 grants** | ⭐ **Article 282 grants** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | ⭐ **STATUTORY** — given **on the Finance Commission's recommendation** | ⭐ **DISCRETIONARY** — given by the Union for **"any public purpose"**, even outside its legislative competence |
| **Route** | ⭐ **The Finance Commission** | ⭐ **The executive** — historically the **Planning Commission**, today the line ministries |
| **What it funds** | General fiscal need; specific tribal-welfare and Scheduled-Area grants | ⭐ **CENTRALLY SPONSORED SCHEMES** |
| **Federal effect** | ⭐ **Predictable, rule-based, untied** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Tied, conditional, and expandable at the Centre's will** |

> ⭐⭐ **This one table answers most "fiscal federalism" questions.** ⚠️ **Article 282 was intended as a minor, incidental power. It became the constitutional basis of the entire centrally sponsored scheme architecture** — which is how the Union came to influence subjects on the **State List** without amending the Seventh Schedule. *(See [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md).)*

### ⚠️ The other structural grievance: cesses and surcharges

⭐ **Article 270 divides the "net proceeds" of Union taxes — but expressly EXCLUDES cesses and surcharges** *(Article 271 lets the Union levy a surcharge for its own purposes)*.

> ⚠️ ⭐ **Consequence:** every rupee the Union raises as a **cess** or **surcharge** stays **entirely with the Union** and never enters the divisible pool. ⭐ **So the states' effective share is materially lower than the headline devolution percentage** — the single most-cited state complaint before every recent Commission, and an essential paragraph in any fiscal-federalism answer.

## PART C — ⭐⭐ The awards you must be able to name

| Commission | Chairman | Award period | ⭐ **What made it different** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **K.C. Neogy** | 1952–57 | The first; established the method |
| **11th** | **A.M. Khusro** | 2000–05 | First after the **80th Amendment** created a **single divisible pool of ALL central taxes** *(before this, income tax and Union excise were shared under separate articles)* |
| **12th** | **C. Rangarajan** | 2005–10 | Linked debt relief to enacting **fiscal responsibility (FRBM) legislation** |
| ⭐ **13th** | ⭐ **Vijay Kelkar** | 2010–15 | ⭐⭐ **The departure asked about in [Mains 2013 Q7](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md): for the first time it gave LOCAL BODIES a share LINKED TO THE DIVISIBLE POOL** *(a basic grant plus a performance grant, instead of an ad-hoc lump sum)*, and imposed conditions such as constituting **State Finance Commissions on time**, putting local-body accounts in order and setting up an **ombudsman** and a **property tax board** |
| ⭐⭐ **14th** | ⭐ **Y.V. Reddy** | 2015–20 | ⭐⭐ **Raised tax devolution from 32% to 42% — the largest single jump ever.** ⭐ **Abolished the plan/non-plan distinction**, ⭐ **stopped recommending sector-specific and state-specific grants**, and treated the states as **equal partners** free to spend untied money. Coincided with the **abolition of the Planning Commission** |
| ⭐⭐ **15th** | ⭐ **N.K. Singh** | 2021–26 *(after a one-year 2020–21 report)* | ⭐ **Devolution 41%** — a **1 percentage-point reduction from 42%**, ⚠️ **because Jammu and Kashmir ceased to be a state**. ⭐ **Shifted the population base from 1971 to the 2011 CENSUS** — bitterly contested by the southern states — and offset it with a ⭐ **"demographic performance" criterion rewarding fertility decline**. Introduced **revenue deficit grants**, sector- and state-specific grants, and a **non-lapsable fund for defence and internal security modernisation** |
| ⭐ **16th** | ⭐ **Arvind Panagariya** | ⭐ **2026–27 to 2030–31** | Constituted **31 December 2023**; terms of reference cover devolution, grants, local-body augmentation and a review of **disaster-management financing**. ⚠️ **Verify the finally accepted devolution share and horizontal formula from the tabled report and the Action Taken memorandum before quoting a number** |

### ⭐ The 15th Commission's horizontal formula — the one set of weights worth memorising

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Income distance** *(the gap from the richest state's per-capita income)* | ⭐ **45%** |
| ⭐ **Area** | **15%** |
| ⭐ **Population (2011)** | **15%** |
| ⭐ **Demographic performance** | ⭐ **12.5%** |
| ⭐ **Forest and ecology** | **10%** |
| **Tax and fiscal effort** | **2.5%** |

> ⭐ **The logic in one line:** ⚠️ **income distance is equalising** *(the poorer the state, the larger its share)*, ⭐ **while demographic performance and fiscal effort are rewarding** *(they compensate the states that controlled population and collected taxes well)*. ⭐ **Every Finance Commission is a negotiation between EQUITY and EFFICIENCY, and the weights are where that negotiation is recorded.**

## PART D — ⚠️ The criticisms and the reform debate

1. ⚠️ ⭐ **The Union writes the terms of reference alone.** Clause (d) lets it steer the Commission — the **15th's** original ToR asking it to consider the 2011 census, and to examine whether **revenue deficit grants** should exist at all, drew protest resolutions from several state assemblies.
2. ⚠️ ⭐ **Cesses and surcharges outside the divisible pool** erode the award from outside it *(above)*.
3. ⚠️ **Advisory status** — grants and conditionalities have been trimmed in the Action Taken memorandum.
4. ⚠️ **Two channels of transfer, one Commission.** Before 2015 the **Planning Commission** transferred sums comparable to the Finance Commission's, with **no constitutional standing**. ⭐ **Its abolition made the Finance Commission the sole statutory channel** — but **centrally sponsored schemes under Article 282 continue**, so the second channel never really closed.
5. ⚠️ **No permanent secretariat** — each Commission is assembled and dissolved, losing institutional memory. ⭐ **A standing "advance cell" or permanent Finance Commission secretariat is the most commonly recommended fix.**
6. ⚠️ **Local bodies still receive money via the states**, so clauses (bb) and (c) remain the weakest part of the award *(the 13th's conditionalities were the strongest attempt to change this)*.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The article and the shape
> ⭐ **Article 280 — the President constitutes it EVERY FIFTH YEAR or earlier. CHAIRMAN + FOUR members.** ⭐ **Article 281 — report laid before both Houses with an ACTION TAKEN memorandum.**

### The four qualifications
> ⭐ **A JUDGE · an ACCOUNTS person · an ADMINISTRATOR · an ECONOMIST** — chaired by someone with **experience in public affairs**. *(Hence: quasi-judicial.)*

### The four clauses
> ⭐ **(a) tax devolution · (b) grants-in-aid principles · (bb) PANCHAYATS (73rd) · (c) MUNICIPALITIES (74th) · (d) anything the President refers.**

### 275 vs 282
> ⭐⭐ **275 = statutory, on the FC's recommendation.** ⭐ **282 = discretionary, "any public purpose" — the home of CENTRALLY SPONSORED SCHEMES.**

### The three awards that get asked
> ⭐ **13th (Kelkar) — local bodies get a SHARE OF THE DIVISIBLE POOL.**
> ⭐ **14th (Y.V. Reddy) — 32% → 42%, plan/non-plan abolished, untied money.**
> ⭐ **15th (N.K. Singh) — 41%, 2011 census, demographic-performance weight, defence fund.**
> ⭐ **16th (Panagariya) — award period 2026–31.**

### The grievance
> ⚠️ ⭐ **CESSES AND SURCHARGES are outside the divisible pool** *(Articles 270 and 271)* — so **41% of the pool is not 41% of central tax revenue.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "How is the Finance Commission of India constituted? What do you know about the terms of reference of the recently constituted Finance Commission? Discuss." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2018 Q14](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2018.md); update the "recent" Commission each time you revise.*
> *Skeleton:* **Constitution** — Article 280; the **President**, every **fifth year or earlier**; **Chairman + 4 members**; qualifications under the ⭐ **1951 Act** *(judge, accounts, administration, economics)*; quasi-judicial character; **Article 281** reporting with the **action-taken memorandum**. **Terms of reference** — the four heads of 280(3) plus whatever the President refers under **(d)**. ⭐ **For the 15th:** the **2011 population base**, a review of **revenue deficit grants**, **performance incentives**, a proposed **defence and internal security fund**, and the treatment of **cesses and surcharges**. ⚠️ **The critical comment** — the ToR is drafted by the **Union alone**, which lets one party to the dispute frame the questions put to the arbitrator; several state assemblies passed resolutions against the 15th's ToR. ⭐ **Suggest** consultation with the **Inter-State Council** before ToR are notified.

**2. "How have the recommendations of the 14th Finance Commission enabled the states to improve their fiscal position?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2021 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2021.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The headline — devolution raised from 32% to 42%**, the largest increase ever recommended. **How that improved state finances:** (i) ⭐ **untied money** — devolution is unconditional, unlike scheme funds, so states gained **spending autonomy**; (ii) ⭐ **abolition of the plan/non-plan distinction** ended the bias against maintenance and salaries; (iii) **predictability** for five years aided state budgeting; (iv) **revenue deficit grants** for states with a post-devolution gap; (v) the Commission **stopped recommending sector-specific grants**, treating states as **mature spenders**. ⚠️ **The qualifications** — the higher share was partly offset by the **restructuring of centrally sponsored schemes** and higher state matching ratios; ⚠️ **cesses and surcharges grew**, shrinking the divisible pool; and states with weak absorptive capacity did not convert autonomy into outcomes. **Conclude:** the 14th changed the **form** of transfers more than the total, and its real legacy is the principle that **the states are partners, not claimants.**

**3. "Discuss the recommendations of the 13th Finance Commission which have been a departure from the previous commissions for strengthening local government finances." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2013 Q7](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2013.md).*
> *Skeleton:* **The background** — Articles **280(3)(bb) and (c)** *(73rd and 74th Amendments)* make local-body augmentation a mandatory head, but earlier Commissions gave **ad-hoc lump sums**. ⭐ **The 13th's departure** — (i) ⭐ **it linked local-body grants to a SHARE OF THE PREVIOUS YEAR'S DIVISIBLE POOL**, so the amount grows automatically with central tax revenue; (ii) it split the grant into a ⭐ **BASIC grant plus a PERFORMANCE grant**; (iii) it attached ⭐ **conditions** — timely constitution of the **State Finance Commission** and action on its report, audited accounts, a **local-body ombudsman**, a **property tax board**, and electronic transfer of funds; (iv) it recognised that the binding constraint is **state-level devolution**, not the size of the central grant. ⚠️ **Assessment** — conditionalities were only partly met, and later Commissions retained the share-linked design but varied the conditions. **Conclude:** the 13th converted local-body funding from **charity into entitlement**, which is the correct constitutional reading of clauses (bb) and (c).

**4. "Cesses, surcharges and centrally sponsored schemes have quietly undone what the Finance Commission awards." Critically examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Explain the **award** *(41–42% of the divisible pool, untied)*; then the **three leakages** — ⭐ **cesses and surcharges outside Article 270**; ⭐ **Article 282 discretionary grants funding centrally sponsored schemes on State List subjects with matching contributions**; and **conditional grants** that re-tie money the Commission untied. Add the ⚠️ **GST effect** — states surrendered independent indirect-tax powers, and the compensation mechanism was time-limited *(see [Ch 45](45_GST_Council.md))*. **Balance it:** the Union's own committed expenditure and the need for national minimum standards. ⭐ **Conclude** with the reform list — cap cesses or share them, put centrally sponsored schemes before the **Inter-State Council**, and give the Finance Commission a **permanent secretariat**.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Article 280** — the **President** constitutes it **every fifth year or earlier**; ⭐ **Chairman + 4 members**; **quasi-judicial**; qualifications under the ⭐ **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951** — **judge · accounts · administration · economics**, chaired by a person of **public affairs** experience.
- ⭐⭐ **280(3): (a) tax devolution, vertical AND horizontal · (b) principles of grants-in-aid (Article 275) · (bb) PANCHAYATS (73rd Amendment) · (c) MUNICIPALITIES (74th) · (d) anything referred by the President.**
- ⭐ **Article 281** — laid before **both Houses** with an **explanatory memorandum on action taken**. ⚠️ **Advisory, not binding — but devolution has never been rejected.**
- ⭐⭐ **275 = statutory grants on the FC's recommendation. 282 = discretionary grants for "any public purpose" → CENTRALLY SPONSORED SCHEMES.**
- ⚠️ ⭐ **Cesses and surcharges are OUTSIDE the divisible pool** *(Articles 270, 271)* — the states' biggest grievance.
- ⭐ **13th (Kelkar, 2010–15)** — local-body grants **linked to the divisible pool**, basic + performance, with conditions *(SFC on time, ombudsman, property tax board)*.
- ⭐⭐ **14th (Y.V. Reddy, 2015–20)** — **32% → 42%**; **plan/non-plan abolished**; **no sector-specific grants**; untied money; the **Planning Commission was abolished in the same period**.
- ⭐⭐ **15th (N.K. Singh, 2021–26)** — **41%** *(minus 1 point because **J&K became a UT**)*; ⭐ **2011 census** with a ⭐ **12.5% demographic-performance** offset; **income distance 45%, area 15%, population 15%, forest and ecology 10%, tax effort 2.5%**; revenue deficit grants; **non-lapsable defence and internal security fund**.
- ⭐ **16th (Arvind Panagariya)** — constituted **31 Dec 2023**, award period ⭐ **2026–27 to 2030–31**. ⚠️ **Check the accepted devolution share against the tabled report.**
- ⚠️ **Criticisms:** Union-drafted ToR · cesses and surcharges · advisory status · surviving **Article 282** transfers · **no permanent secretariat** · local bodies still funded **through** the states.
