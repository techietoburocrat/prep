# Chapter 45 — GST Council

[← Chapter 44](44_Finance_Commission.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 46 →](46_National_Commission_SCs.md)

**Read time ~8 min** · **Article 279A (101st Amendment Act, 2016)** · 🟠 Prelims: in the "constitutional bodies" format · Mains: as a **federalism** question, not a tax one

> 📌 **The newest constitutional body in the Constitution, and the only one whose creation required states to give up a taxing power they had held since 1950.** ⭐ **Treat this as a chapter on FEDERALISM that happens to be about tax** — that is how GS-II asks it.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **The GST Council is India's only permanent institution where the Union and every state sit as co-legislators over a single tax.** ⚠️ **Laxmikanth's phrase for it — and a good one for an answer — is that it makes India "one nation, one tax, one market" by converting independent taxing powers into POOLED SOVEREIGNTY.**

---

## PART A — The constitutional architecture around it

The **101st Amendment Act, 2016** did four things. ⭐ **Learn all four — the Council is only the fourth.**

| Article | What it did |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **246A** | ⭐ **Gives Parliament AND every state legislature SIMULTANEOUS power to make laws on GST.** ⚠️ **This is a new kind of entry — it is not in the Union List, the State List or the Concurrent List.** ⭐ **On INTER-STATE supply, only Parliament may legislate** |
| ⭐ **269A** | ⭐ **IGST on inter-state supply is levied and collected by the UNION and APPORTIONED between the Union and the states** as Parliament provides on the Council's recommendation |
| ⭐ **279A** | ⭐ **Creates the GST COUNCIL** |
| ⭐ **366(12A)** | Defines GST as a tax on the **supply of goods or services or both, except the supply of ALCOHOLIC LIQUOR for human consumption** |

> ⚠️ ⭐ **Two exclusions worth knowing precisely:** ⭐ **alcoholic liquor for human consumption is OUTSIDE GST altogether** *(constitutionally excluded)*; and ⭐ **five petroleum products — crude, high-speed diesel, motor spirit, natural gas and aviation turbine fuel — are inside GST in principle but at a ZERO date until the Council recommends one.** ⚠️ **They are not "excluded" — they are deferred.**

## PART B — ⭐ The Council itself

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Article** | ⭐ **279A** — the President was required to constitute it **within 60 days** of the article's commencement |
| ⭐⭐ **Chairperson** | ⭐ **The UNION FINANCE MINISTER** |
| ⭐ **Members** | ⭐ **The Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue or Finance**, and ⭐ **the Minister in charge of Finance or Taxation, or any other Minister, nominated by EACH state government** |
| ⭐ **Vice-Chairperson** | ⭐⭐ **Chosen by the STATE members from among themselves** — an unusual and deliberately federal touch |
| ⭐ **Quorum** | ⭐ **One-half of the total number of members** |
| ⭐⭐ **Decision rule** | ⭐⭐ **A majority of NOT LESS THAN THREE-FOURTHS of the WEIGHTED VOTES of the members present and voting**, where — ⭐ **the CENTRE's vote = ONE-THIRD** of the total votes cast, and ⭐ **ALL the STATES together = TWO-THIRDS** |
| **Secretariat** | The **Union Revenue Secretary** is the ex-officio Secretary; the **Chairperson of the CBIC** is a **permanent invitee without voting right** |

### ⭐⭐ Do the arithmetic once — it is the most examinable fact in the chapter

> - The Centre holds **1/3 = 33.3%**. A decision needs **75%**. ⭐ **So the Centre alone can BLOCK any proposal** *(since 100 − 33.3 = 66.7% < 75%)* — ⚠️ **it holds a veto.**
> - The states together hold **2/3 = 66.7%**, which is also **less than 75%**. ⭐ **So the states cannot pass anything without the Centre either.**
> - ⭐ **Neither side can act alone. Every decision requires the Centre PLUS a substantial majority of the states.** ⚠️ **That is the definition of pooled sovereignty, and it is the sentence to write in an answer.**

### ⭐ What the Council recommends *(Article 279A(4))*

> ⭐ **Taxes, cesses and surcharges to be SUBSUMED** into GST · ⭐ **goods and services that may be subjected to, or exempted from, GST** · ⭐ **model GST laws, principles of levy, apportionment of IGST and the principles governing place of supply** · ⭐ **the TURNOVER THRESHOLD for exemption** · ⭐ **RATES, including FLOOR RATES WITH BANDS** · ⭐ **special rates for a specified period to raise resources during a NATURAL CALAMITY OR DISASTER** · ⭐ **special provision for the North-Eastern states, Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand** · ⭐ **the DATE from which GST is levied on the five petroleum products**.

⭐ **Article 279A(11)** — the Council **establishes a mechanism to adjudicate disputes** between the Union and states, or between states, arising out of its own recommendations.

> ⚠️ **That dispute-resolution mechanism has never been constituted** — an easy, specific criticism to deploy.

## PART C — ⚠️⚠️ *Union of India v Mohit Minerals* (2022) — the case that changed the answer

⭐ **A three-judge bench held that the GST Council's recommendations are NOT BINDING on the Union or the states.**

| The Court's reasoning | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Article 246A gives Parliament and the state legislatures SIMULTANEOUS legislative power** — neither derives its authority from the Council | ⭐ **A recommendation cannot bind a legislature that holds independent power** |
| ⭐ The Council's recommendations have ⭐ **PERSUASIVE value** and are a product of **"collaborative dialogue"** | The federal structure survives the amendment |
| ⚠️ The Constitution uses "recommendation" deliberately; where it wanted a binding effect it said so | ⭐ **Indian federalism is described as a dialogue between "unequal federal units"** |

> ⭐⭐ **Before 2022, the standard answer was "GST ended state fiscal autonomy." After 2022, the correct answer is more interesting:** ⚠️ **a state MAY legislate against a Council recommendation — but doing so would fracture the single national market that is the whole point of GST, so the constraint is POLITICAL and ECONOMIC rather than legal.** ⭐ **Say exactly that, and cite the case.**

## PART D — ⭐ The federalism balance sheet

### What the states gained

- ⭐ A **seat and a vote** in setting tax rates on **services**, which they could never tax before;
- ⭐ **Destination-based taxation** — consuming states gain, which helps the poorer, populous states;
- ⭐ A **guaranteed compensation** for revenue loss, at **14% annual growth over a 2015–16 base**, ⭐ **for five years to June 2022**, funded by the **GST Compensation Cess** *(cess collection was later extended to service the borrowings raised during the pandemic shortfall)*;
- ⭐ A **wider tax base** and the end of cascading taxes.

### ⚠️ What the states gave up

- ⚠️ ⭐ **The independent power to fix rates on most goods** — VAT, entry tax, octroi, luxury and entertainment taxes were all subsumed;
- ⚠️ **Fiscal flexibility in a crisis** — a state can no longer raise its own rate to meet a local shock;
- ⚠️ **The compensation ended in June 2022**, and the states' demand for extension was not accepted;
- ⚠️ ⭐ **The Centre's one-third veto** means no reform passes over its objection;
- ⚠️ **Cesses and surcharges outside the divisible pool** compound this — see [Ch 44](44_Finance_Commission.md).

> ⭐ **The balanced conclusion for an answer:** ⚠️ **GST converted a vertical relationship into a horizontal forum, and simultaneously reduced the range of decisions the states can take alone.** ⭐ **The Council is genuinely federal in FORM — one member per state, an elected Vice-Chairperson, a three-fourths rule — and asymmetric in POWER, because one member holds a third of the votes.**

### ⭐ Related institutions worth a line

- ⭐ **GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT)** — the statutory appellate forum, with a **Principal Bench in New Delhi** and state benches; it addresses the long gap during which taxpayers had to go straight to the High Courts;
- ⭐ **GST Network (GSTN)** — the IT backbone, a not-for-profit company, now majority government-owned.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The four articles of the 101st Amendment
> ⭐ **246A — simultaneous power** *(Parliament alone for inter-state)* · ⭐ **269A — IGST, levied by the Union, apportioned** · ⭐ **279A — the Council** · ⭐ **366(12A) — the definition, excluding alcoholic liquor.**

### The composition
> ⭐ **Chair = UNION FINANCE MINISTER. Members = the Union MoS (Revenue/Finance) + one minister per state.** ⭐ **The VICE-CHAIR is elected BY THE STATES.** ⭐ **Quorum = half.**

### The arithmetic
> ⭐⭐ **75% of weighted votes. Centre = 1/3. All states = 2/3.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Neither side can pass anything alone — but the Centre alone can block.**

### The two exclusions
> ⭐ **ALCOHOL is constitutionally OUT. The five petroleum products are IN but DEFERRED — crude, HSD, petrol, natural gas, ATF** *(the Council fixes the date)*.

### The case
> ⭐⭐ ***Union of India v Mohit Minerals* (2022)** — recommendations are **NOT binding**; they are **persuasive**; Article **246A** gives **simultaneous** power; federalism as a **"dialogue between unequal units."**

### The compensation
> ⭐ **14% growth over a 2015–16 base, for FIVE YEARS, ending JUNE 2022**, funded by the **compensation cess**.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The GST Council is a model of cooperative federalism in form and of centralised bargaining in substance." Critically examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* **The form** — Article **279A**; every state has a **vote**; the ⭐ **Vice-Chairperson is elected by the states**; a **three-fourths** rule forces consensus; the Council has met continuously and mostly decided **by consensus rather than by vote**. **The substance** — ⭐ **the Centre's one-third share is a veto**; ⚠️ the **compensation guarantee lapsed in 2022** despite state demands; the ⭐ **Article 279A(11) dispute-resolution mechanism has never been set up**; and the **Union's cesses stay outside the divisible pool**. ⭐ **The legal turn** — ***Mohit Minerals* (2022)** restored the states' formal freedom by holding the recommendations **non-binding**, ⚠️ **but exercising that freedom would break the single market, so it is a right that cannot be used.** **Conclude:** the Council is India's most successful federal institution *by usage* and its most asymmetric *by design* — the fix is a **functioning dispute mechanism**, a **rules-based compensation formula**, and **placing cesses inside the divisible pool.**

**2. "Discuss the constitutional changes brought about by the 101st Amendment Act and their implications for the fiscal autonomy of states." (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* The **four articles** *(246A, 269A, 279A, 366(12A))* and the **taxes subsumed** on both sides. **Implications** — gains *(a share in taxing services; destination basis; wider base; a seat at the table)* and losses *(rate-setting autonomy; crisis flexibility; the end of compensation)*. ⭐ **Bring in [Ch 44](44_Finance_Commission.md)** — the state's revenue now depends on **two central decisions**: the Finance Commission's devolution and the GST Council's rates. ⚠️ **Conclude:** state fiscal autonomy has shifted from **independent power** to **participation in a joint forum** — a real change in kind, not just degree.

**3. "The GST Council's recommendations are not binding. Does that strengthen or weaken cooperative federalism?" (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* State ***Mohit Minerals* (2022)** and its reasoning from Article **246A**. **Strengthens** — it confirms states are **co-equal legislators**, keeps the Council a **deliberative** body, and gives a dissenting state a constitutional voice. **Weakens** — it introduces the ⚠️ **risk of a fragmented tax structure**, uncertainty for business, and disputes with **no adjudicating forum** because 279A(11) is unimplemented. ⭐ **Conclude:** the judgment is best read as a **reminder rather than a licence** — it restores the states' bargaining position inside the Council without any state actually intending to legislate against it.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **101st Amendment Act, 2016** → ⭐ **246A** *(simultaneous power; **Parliament alone for inter-state supply**)* · ⭐ **269A** *(IGST — levied and collected by the Union, apportioned)* · ⭐ **279A** *(the Council)* · ⭐ **366(12A)** *(definition; ⚠️ **excludes alcoholic liquor for human consumption**)*.
- ⭐ **Constituted by the President within 60 days.** ⭐ **Chair = Union Finance Minister; members = Union MoS (Revenue/Finance) + one minister per state; VICE-CHAIR ELECTED BY THE STATES; quorum = half.**
- ⭐⭐ **Decisions need 75% of weighted votes: CENTRE 1/3, ALL STATES 2/3.** ⚠️ ⭐ **The Centre alone can block; the states alone cannot pass.**
- ⭐ **Recommends:** taxes subsumed · goods/services taxed or exempt · model laws, IGST apportionment, place of supply · **threshold** · **rates with floor rates and bands** · **special rates during a natural calamity** · **special provision for the NE states, J&K, HP, Uttarakhand** · ⭐ **the date GST applies to the five petroleum products**.
- ⭐ **279A(11)** — a **dispute-resolution mechanism**; ⚠️ **never constituted.**
- ⭐⭐ ***Union of India v Mohit Minerals* (2022)** — recommendations are **NOT binding**, only **persuasive**; Article 246A confers **simultaneous** power; federalism is a **"dialogue between unequal federal units."**
- ⭐ **Compensation — 14% growth over a 2015–16 base for FIVE YEARS, ended JUNE 2022**, funded by the **GST compensation cess** *(collection extended to repay pandemic-era borrowing)*.
- ⭐ **GSTAT** — the appellate tribunal, **Principal Bench at New Delhi**. **GSTN** — the IT backbone.
