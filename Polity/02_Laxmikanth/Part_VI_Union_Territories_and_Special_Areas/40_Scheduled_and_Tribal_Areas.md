# Chapter 40 — Scheduled and Tribal Areas

[← Chapter 39](39_Union_Territories.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 41 →](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/41_Election_Commission.md)

**Read time ~14 min** · **Article 244 (Part X) · Fifth and Sixth Schedules** · 🟠 ⚠️ **Prelims: 2013, 2015, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026** — one of the most reliably asked topics in the whole book

> 📌 **This is a small chapter with an enormous Prelims footprint.** ⭐ The **Fifth-vs-Sixth Schedule swap** was the trap in **Prelims 2015 Q9, 2022 Q10, 2023 Q8, 2025 Q6** — and again in 2026. ⚠️ **If you learn only one thing from this chapter, learn which four states are in the Sixth Schedule.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> Ordinary state administration was thought unsuitable for areas where tribal communities predominate. ⭐ **So the Constitution built two escape hatches: the Fifth Schedule, where the GOVERNOR gets extraordinary personal powers over an otherwise ordinary state administration; and the Sixth Schedule, where tribal communities get their OWN elected law-making councils.**
>
> ⭐⭐ **Fifth Schedule = protection from above. Sixth Schedule = self-rule from within.** That contrast answers most questions here.

---

## PART A — ⭐⭐ The distinction that decides the marks

| | ⭐ **FIFTH SCHEDULE** | ⭐ **SIXTH SCHEDULE** |
|---|---|---|
| **Constitutional hook** | **Article 244(1)** | **Article 244(2)** *(and Article 275(1))* |
| ⭐⭐ **Where it applies** | **"Scheduled Areas" and Scheduled Tribes in any state OTHER than the four below** | ⚠️ ⭐ **"Tribal Areas" in ASSAM, MEGHALAYA, TRIPURA and MIZORAM — and nowhere else** |
| **The governing idea** | ⭐ **Paternalist protection** — the state administers, but the Governor holds a personal shield | ⭐ **Devolved self-government** — elected councils with real legislative power |
| **Key institution** | ⭐ **Tribes Advisory Council (TAC)** — *advisory only* | ⭐ **Autonomous District Councils (ADCs)** — *elected and law-making* |
| **Who declares the area** | ⭐ The **PRESIDENT** *(by order, after consulting the Governor)* | ⭐ The **GOVERNOR** *(who may create, alter, unite or divide autonomous districts)* |
| **The Governor's role** | ⭐ **Very strong** — may switch off central and state laws; makes **regulations** | Strong, but as **supervisor** of the councils rather than as a substitute for them |
| **Annual report** | ⭐ The **Governor reports ANNUALLY to the PRESIDENT** on the administration of Scheduled Areas | ⚠️ **No such general annual-report duty** |

> ⭐⭐ **The mnemonic for the Sixth Schedule four:** ⚠️ **"MA-TM" — Meghalaya, Assam, Tripura, Mizoram.** Or: **the Sixth Schedule is the North-East MINUS Manipur, Nagaland and Arunachal Pradesh** *(those three are dealt with under [Article 371A/371B/371C etc.](../Part_IV_State_Government/36_Special_Provisions.md), not the Sixth Schedule)*.
>
> ⚠️ **The classic wrong answer is to put Nagaland or Manipur in the Sixth Schedule. They are not there.**

---

## PART B — The Fifth Schedule

### ⭐ Who declares a Scheduled Area, and on what criteria

⭐ The **President** may, by order, **declare an area to be a Scheduled Area**, and may **increase, decrease, alter or rescind** it — **in consultation with the Governor** of the state concerned.

⚠️ **The criteria are NOT in the Constitution.** They are administrative, and settled by practice:
1. **Preponderance of tribal population**
2. **Compactness and reasonable size**
3. **Under-developed nature of the area**
4. **Marked disparity in economic standard** of the people

> ⭐ **Ten states have Scheduled Areas:** **Andhra Pradesh · Telangana · Chhattisgarh · Gujarat · Himachal Pradesh · Jharkhand · Madhya Pradesh · Maharashtra · Odisha · Rajasthan.**
>
> ⚠️ **Note the two surprises: HIMACHAL PRADESH and GUJARAT are in the list; KERALA, TAMIL NADU, KARNATAKA and WEST BENGAL are not.** Examiners like the surprises.

### ⭐⭐ The Governor's three extraordinary powers

| # | Power | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐⭐ **To switch off a law** | The Governor may direct that an **Act of Parliament or of the state legislature shall NOT apply** to a Scheduled Area, or shall apply **with exceptions and modifications** |
| 2 | ⭐⭐ **To make regulations** | For **peace and good government**, the Governor may make regulations to — ⭐ **(a) prohibit or restrict the TRANSFER OF LAND by or among members of Scheduled Tribes; (b) regulate the ALLOTMENT of land to Scheduled Tribes; (c) regulate MONEY-LENDING to Scheduled Tribes** |
| 3 | ⭐ **To report** | Submit a **report to the President ANNUALLY**, or whenever required, on the administration of the Scheduled Areas |

⚠️ **Two conditions on the regulation power:** the Governor must **consult the Tribes Advisory Council**, and the regulation requires the ⭐ **assent of the PRESIDENT**. A regulation may **repeal or amend** an Act of Parliament or of the state legislature applicable to that area.

> ⭐ **Read power (2) carefully — LAND, LAND, MONEY.** ⚠️ **Land alienation and indebtedness are the two historical causes of tribal dispossession, and the Constitution names exactly those two.** That is the sentence to open a Mains answer with.

### ⭐ The Tribes Advisory Council (TAC)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Every state with **Scheduled Areas**; and the **President may direct** one for a state with STs but **no** Scheduled Areas |
| ⭐ **Size** | ⭐ **Not more than 20 members**, of whom ⭐ **about three-fourths must be representatives of the Scheduled Tribes in the state legislative assembly** |
| ⚠️ **Function** | ⭐ **To ADVISE on matters referred to it by the Governor** relating to the welfare and advancement of STs |
| ⚠️ **The weakness** | ⭐ **It is purely advisory, it is chaired by the Chief Minister in practice, and it meets rarely.** ⚠️ **This is the standard criticism of the Fifth Schedule** |

### ⭐ Executive power — Centre and state

- The **executive power of the STATE** extends to the Scheduled Areas;
- ⭐ **But the executive power of the UNION extends to giving DIRECTIONS to the state** as to the administration of those areas;
- ⭐ **Article 275(1)** — the Union gives **grants-in-aid** to states for **schemes of tribal welfare** and for **raising the level of administration of Scheduled Areas**.

### ⭐ Article 339 — the review mechanism

- **339(1):** the **President may at any time**, and ⭐ **shall at the expiration of ten years from the commencement of the Constitution**, appoint a **Commission** to report on the administration of Scheduled Areas and the welfare of STs. *(The **Dhebar Commission, 1960** was appointed under it.)*
- ⭐ **339(2):** the **executive power of the Union extends to giving directions to a state** as to the drawing up and execution of **schemes essential for the welfare of the Scheduled Tribes**.

## PART C — The Sixth Schedule

### ⭐ The architecture

⭐ Tribal areas in **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram** are constituted into ⭐ **autonomous districts** — and where a district has **more than one tribe**, into ⭐ **autonomous regions** within it.

⚠️ **They remain part of the state**, but are administered by their own councils.

| Element | ⭐ **District Council** | **Regional Council** |
|---|---|---|
| **Membership** | ⭐ **Not more than 30 members** — ⭐ **4 NOMINATED by the Governor** and ⭐ **26 ELECTED on adult franchise** | As determined for the autonomous region |
| ⭐ **Term** | ⭐ **Elected members: 5 years.** ⚠️ **Nominated members hold office during the GOVERNOR'S PLEASURE** | Same |
| **Who creates it** | ⭐ The **GOVERNOR** — he may **create, alter the areas of, unite, divide** autonomous districts and regions, and change their names | The Governor |

> ⭐ **"30 = 4 + 26, five years, nominated at pleasure"** — this exact breakdown has been asked. ⚠️ **Do not confuse the nominated members' tenure with the elected members'.**

### ⭐⭐ What the Councils can actually do

**Legislative** — subject to the ⭐ **Governor's assent**, a District or Regional Council may make laws on:
> ⭐ **Land** *(other than reserved forest)* · **management of any forest not a reserved forest** · **use of canal water for agriculture** · ⭐ **shifting cultivation (jhum)** · **village or town administration, including village or town police and public health and sanitation** · **appointment of chiefs or headmen** · ⭐ **inheritance of property** · ⭐ **marriage and divorce** · ⭐ **social customs**

**Judicial** — the Councils may constitute ⭐ **village councils and courts** to try suits and cases **between parties all of whom belong to Scheduled Tribes** within the district. ⚠️ **Serious offences — those punishable with death, transportation for life or imprisonment for five years or more — are outside their jurisdiction** unless the Governor specifically confers it. The **High Court** has such jurisdiction over these cases as the Governor specifies.

**Financial** — the Councils may ⭐ **assess and collect LAND REVENUE and impose specified TAXES**, levy tolls on goods and ferries, and grant licences or leases for the **extraction of minerals**; they receive a **share of royalties** on minerals from the state.

**Applicability of other laws** — ⚠️ ⭐ **Acts of Parliament and of the state legislature do not automatically apply to an autonomous district, or apply with specified exceptions and modifications, as directed by notification.** ⭐ **This is the Sixth Schedule's version of the Fifth Schedule's "switch-off" power.**

> ⚠️ **Edition-sensitive detail:** the exact allocation of the switch-off notification (Governor vs President) differs between Assam and the other three states and has been amended several times. ⭐ **For the exam, hold the PRINCIPLE — general laws do not apply of their own force inside an autonomous district — rather than the sub-paragraph number.**

### ⭐ The ten Autonomous District Councils today

| State | Councils |
|---|---|
| **Assam** *(3)* | ⭐ **Bodoland Territorial Council** · **Karbi Anglong** · **Dima Hasao** |
| **Meghalaya** *(3)* | **Khasi Hills** · **Jaintia Hills** · **Garo Hills** |
| **Tripura** *(1)* | **Tripura Tribal Areas ADC** |
| **Mizoram** *(3)* | **Chakma** · **Lai** · **Mara** |

⭐ **Article 244A** *(22nd Amendment, 1969)* — **Parliament may by law create an AUTONOMOUS STATE within Assam**, with its own legislature or council of ministers. ⚠️ **It has never been used** — but it is the constitutional basis repeatedly invoked in North-East statehood demands.

⭐ **The 125th Constitutional Amendment Bill (2019)** — proposes to **strengthen the ADCs** with greater financial and executive powers, elected village and municipal councils inside them, and a fixed share of state finance-commission funds. ⚠️ **Still pending.**

## PART D — ⭐ The two statutes that sit on top

### ⭐⭐ PESA — the Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996

The **73rd Amendment** *(see [Ch 37](../Part_V_Local_Government/37_Panchayati_Raj.md))* ⚠️ **did not automatically extend to Fifth Schedule areas.** ⭐ **PESA, based on the Bhuria Committee (1995), extended Part IX to them — with modifications that make the Gram Sabha unusually powerful.**

| ⭐ Under PESA, the **Gram Sabha** | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Approves plans and beneficiaries** | Every village development plan and the selection of beneficiaries under poverty-alleviation programmes |
| ⭐⭐ **Must be CONSULTED before land acquisition** | And before **resettlement or rehabilitation** of displaced persons |
| ⭐⭐ **Must be consulted for MINOR MINERAL** licences | Prospecting licences and mining leases for **minor minerals** need its recommendation |
| ⭐ **Safeguards custom** | Competent to safeguard **community resources, customs, traditions and cultural identity** |
| ⭐ **Controls** | **Minor forest produce · minor water bodies · local plans · money-lending to STs · village markets · institutions in the social sector** |
| ⚠️ **Reservation** | ⭐ **Reservation of seats is in proportion to the tribal population, but NOT LESS THAN HALF the total seats**; ⚠️ ⭐ **ALL chairperson posts are RESERVED for Scheduled Tribes** |

> ⭐ ***Prelims 2013 Q17*** asked directly on PESA, and ***Prelims 2025 Q89*** turned on the Centre-vs-state position on **minor minerals**. ⚠️ **The consultation-not-consent gap is PESA's known weakness** — the Gram Sabha must be *consulted*, and consultation can be recorded and ignored.

### ⭐ The Forest Rights Act, 2006

Formally the ⭐ **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006**.

- ⭐ Recognises **individual rights** *(to cultivate and live on forest land)* and ⭐ **COMMUNITY forest rights**, including the right to **protect and manage community forest resource**;
- ⭐ **The Gram Sabha is the authority that initiates the determination of claims** — a real transfer of power, not an advisory role;
- ⚠️ ⭐ **The nodal ministry is the MINISTRY OF TRIBAL AFFAIRS**, ⚠️ **not Environment and Forests** — ⭐ *exactly what [Prelims 2021 Q8](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2021.md) tested*;
- ⚠️ Also covers **"other traditional forest dwellers"** who have lived there for **three generations (75 years)** — so it is **not tribes-only**.

> ⭐ **Also worth two lines each:** **PVTGs — Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups** *(75 groups, formerly "primitive tribal groups"; asked in [Prelims 2019](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2019.md))*; and ⭐ **Article 342 — the President specifies Scheduled Tribes for a state by public notification, and only PARLIAMENT may thereafter include or exclude a tribe from that list** *(asked in [Prelims 2024 Q84](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2024.md))*.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The one distinction that matters
> ⭐⭐ **FIFTH = everywhere else, the GOVERNOR protects.** ⭐⭐ **SIXTH = Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram, the PEOPLE govern.** ⚠️ **Nagaland, Manipur, Arunachal are NOT Sixth Schedule** — they are Article 371-series.

### The Governor's Fifth Schedule powers
> ⭐ **LAND transfer · LAND allotment · MONEY-LENDING** — plus the power to **switch off any law**, and the **annual report to the President**. ⚠️ **Regulations need TAC consultation + PRESIDENT'S ASSENT.**

### The TAC
> ⭐ **Max 20 members · ~3/4 from ST MLAs · ADVISORY ONLY.**

### The District Council
> ⭐ **30 = 26 elected + 4 nominated · 5 years · nominated members at the Governor's PLEASURE.** ⭐ **Laws need the Governor's ASSENT.**

### Ten and ten
> ⭐ **TEN states have Scheduled Areas. TEN Autonomous District Councils exist** *(Assam 3, Meghalaya 3, Mizoram 3, Tripura 1)*.

### The statutes
> ⭐ **PESA 1996** *(Bhuria Committee)* — Gram Sabha **consulted** on land acquisition and **minor minerals**; ⭐ **all chairperson posts reserved for STs**; ST seats **not less than half**.
> ⭐ **FRA 2006** — individual + **community** forest rights; ⭐ **Gram Sabha determines claims**; ⚠️ ⭐ **nodal ministry = TRIBAL AFFAIRS**.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The Fifth Schedule was designed to protect tribal communities, but protection has not translated into empowerment." Critically examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* **The design** — Article 244(1); Presidential declaration of Scheduled Areas; the Governor's three regulation powers over **land transfer, land allotment and money-lending**; the power to **disapply laws**; the **annual report**; **Article 275(1)** grants; the **TAC**. ⭐ **Why protection has not become empowerment** — (a) the **TAC is advisory, chaired by the CM, and rarely convened**; (b) the Governor's disapplication power is ⚠️ **almost never used**, so mining, forest and land laws apply unmodified; (c) **PESA's "consultation" is not consent**, and state PESA rules were delayed by years; (d) ⭐ **displacement** — tribal communities are a disproportionate share of those displaced by dams, mines and industry; (e) **FRA implementation** — high rejection rates and near-neglect of **community** forest rights. **Way forward:** make the TAC's advice binding on notified subjects, activate the Governor's power to modify laws, move PESA from consultation to **consent**, and prioritise **community forest rights**. **Conclude:** the Fifth Schedule gives the Governor a **shield he does not lift.**

**2. "Compare the Fifth and the Sixth Schedules of the Constitution as instruments of tribal self-governance." (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Build the **comparison table in Part A** into prose — coverage, the declaring authority, the institution *(TAC vs ADC)*, the nature of power *(advisory vs legislative, judicial and financial)*, and the safeguard mechanism. ⭐ **Argue that the Sixth Schedule is genuinely self-governing** — elected councils legislating on land, jhum, inheritance, marriage and custom, running village courts, and collecting land revenue — ⚠️ **while the Fifth Schedule is protective custody**. **Then note the Sixth Schedule's own problems:** friction with the state government, weak finances, urban areas inside districts having no elected local body, and the pending ⭐ **125th Amendment Bill**. **Conclude:** the future of Fifth Schedule areas lies in moving *towards* the Sixth Schedule model — which is precisely what PESA attempted and only half-achieved.

**3. "PESA, 1996 promised the Gram Sabha a veto and delivered a consultation." Discuss with reference to land acquisition and mining in Scheduled Areas. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* PESA's origin *(the 73rd Amendment's exclusion of Scheduled Areas + the **Bhuria Committee**)*; the powers it gives the Gram Sabha; ⭐ **the critical wording — "consulted" for land acquisition and "recommendation" for minor minerals**, which courts have read as procedural. **Illustrate** with the *Samatha* judgment (1997) on leasing tribal land in Scheduled Areas, the **Niyamgiri Gram Sabha referendum (2013)** — where the Supreme Court did require the Gram Sabhas to decide on religious and cultural rights — and the **FRA 2006** community-rights route as the stronger alternative. **Conclude:** the fix is statutory *(consent, not consultation)*, and a live model already exists in the **FRA's Gram Sabha determination**.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Article 244** — **244(1) → FIFTH Schedule**; **244(2) → SIXTH Schedule**. ⭐ **Article 244A** — an **autonomous state within Assam** *(22nd Amendment, 1969; never used)*.
- ⭐⭐ **SIXTH Schedule = ASSAM, MEGHALAYA, TRIPURA, MIZORAM only.** Everything else with Scheduled Areas is **FIFTH**.
- ⭐ **Scheduled Areas are declared by the PRESIDENT** *(after consulting the Governor)*; **autonomous districts are created by the GOVERNOR**.
- ⭐ **Ten states have Scheduled Areas:** AP, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, **HP**, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan.
- ⭐⭐ **Governor's Fifth Schedule regulation power: LAND TRANSFER · LAND ALLOTMENT · MONEY-LENDING**; needs ⭐ **TAC consultation + PRESIDENT'S ASSENT**; may **repeal an Act of Parliament** for that area. Plus the power to **disapply any law**, and an ⭐ **ANNUAL report to the President**.
- ⭐ **TAC — max 20, about 3/4 ST MLAs, ADVISORY ONLY.**
- ⭐⭐ **District Council — 30 members = 26 elected + 4 NOMINATED; 5-year term; nominated members at the Governor's PLEASURE.** Laws on ⭐ **land, non-reserved forest, jhum, canal water, village administration, inheritance, marriage and divorce, social customs** — all requiring the ⭐ **Governor's assent**. Village courts for ST-vs-ST disputes; ⚠️ **serious offences excluded**. Land revenue and specified taxes.
- ⭐ **Ten ADCs** — Assam 3 *(Bodoland, Karbi Anglong, Dima Hasao)*, Meghalaya 3, Mizoram 3, Tripura 1. ⭐ **125th Amendment Bill (2019) still pending.**
- ⭐ **Article 275(1)** — grants-in-aid for tribal welfare. ⭐ **Article 339** — a Commission on Scheduled Areas *(Dhebar, 1960)* + Union directions on ST welfare schemes.
- ⭐⭐ **PESA 1996** *(Bhuria Committee)* — Gram Sabha **approves plans and beneficiaries**, is **consulted on land acquisition** and **recommends minor-mineral leases**; ST seats **≥ half**; ⭐ **ALL chairperson posts reserved for STs**.
- ⭐ **FRA 2006** — individual + **community** forest rights; ⭐ **Gram Sabha initiates claims**; ⚠️ ⭐ **nodal ministry = MINISTRY OF TRIBAL AFFAIRS**; also covers **other traditional forest dwellers (75 years / three generations)**.
- ⭐ **Article 342** — the **President** notifies STs; ⚠️ **only PARLIAMENT can add or remove a tribe** thereafter.
