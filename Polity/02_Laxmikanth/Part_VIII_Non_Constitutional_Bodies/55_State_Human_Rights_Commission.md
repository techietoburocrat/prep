# Chapter 55 — State Human Rights Commission

[← Chapter 54](54_NHRC.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 56 →](56_Central_Information_Commission.md)

**Read time ~4 min** · **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** · 🟡 Prelims: as a contrast with the NHRC · Mains: inside NHRC answers

> 📌 **Same Act, same design, four differences.** ⭐ **Read [Ch 54](54_NHRC.md) first.** ⚠️ **The one difference that decides questions is the JURISDICTION LIMIT — an SHRC cannot touch a Union List subject.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐ **A state-level mirror of the NHRC, confined to what a state government can actually control** — ⚠️ **which is why it is weaker: the most serious human-rights complaints in India involve the police, the prisons and the district administration, and those are exactly the subjects the SHRC does cover.** ⭐ **Its weakness is not its jurisdiction. It is its resourcing.**

---

## ⭐⭐ The four differences

| # | Element | ⭐ **NHRC** | ⭐ **SHRC** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐ **Chairperson** | A retired **CJI or Supreme Court judge** | ⭐ **A retired CHIEF JUSTICE *or* JUDGE of a HIGH COURT** *(the 2019 amendment widened this too)* |
| 2 | ⭐ **Members** | 5 + deemed members | ⭐ **TWO** — one serving or retired **judge of a High Court**, or a **District Judge with at least 7 years' experience as a District Judge**; and one person with **knowledge or practical experience of human rights** |
| 3 | ⭐ **Appointed by** | The **President**, on a **six**-member committee | ⭐ **The GOVERNOR**, on a committee of the ⭐ **CHIEF MINISTER *(chair)*, the SPEAKER of the Legislative Assembly, the STATE HOME MINISTER and the LEADER OF OPPOSITION in the Assembly** — ⭐ **plus, where there is a Legislative Council, its CHAIRMAN and the Leader of Opposition in the Council** |
| 4 | ⭐⭐ **Jurisdiction** | Anything within the Act's definition | ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Only matters in the STATE LIST and the CONCURRENT LIST** — ⚠️ **and it cannot inquire into a matter already being inquired into by the NHRC or another statutory commission** |

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Difference 4 is the examinable one.** ⭐ **But notice what falls INSIDE the State and Concurrent Lists: POLICE and PUBLIC ORDER *(State List entries 2 and 1)*, PRISONS *(entry 4)*, and CRIMINAL LAW and PROCEDURE *(Concurrent List)*.** ⭐ **So an SHRC's jurisdiction covers custodial deaths, police excesses and prison conditions — the very core of Indian human-rights work.**

### ⚠️ The one thing that does NOT change

⭐⭐ **A member of a State Human Rights Commission is removed by the PRESIDENT — not by the Governor** — on the same grounds and, for misbehaviour, only ⭐ **after an inquiry and report by the SUPREME COURT.**

> ⭐ **You have now met this design three times: the State PSC ([Ch 43](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/43_State_Public_Service_Commission.md)), the SHRC, and — with a variation — the State Information Commission ([Ch 57](57_State_Information_Commission.md)).** ⚠️ **The rule to carry: for a state body meant to check the state government, the Constitution or the statute puts REMOVAL beyond the state's reach.**

**Everything else is identical:** ⭐ **term of 3 years or 70**, reappointment permitted *(2019)*; ⭐ **powers of a civil court**; ⭐ **the one-year limitation**; ⭐ **recommendatory findings**; ⭐ **the power to approach the Supreme Court or the High Court**; ⭐ **an annual report to the state government, laid before the state legislature with an action-taken memorandum**.

---

## ⭐ Two provisions added in 2019

1. ⭐ **A State Human Rights Commission may be given charge of one or more UNION TERRITORIES** — ending the anomaly of UTs having no commission at all;
2. ⭐⭐ **Human-rights cases relating to DELHI are dealt with by the NHRC itself**, not by a Delhi commission.

---

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The four differences
> ⭐ **HIGH COURT chairperson · TWO members · appointed by the GOVERNOR on a CM-headed committee · STATE and CONCURRENT LIST only.**

### The one that stays the same
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **REMOVED BY THE PRESIDENT, on a Supreme Court inquiry — not by the Governor.**

### The 2019 additions
> ⭐ **An SHRC may cover a UT.** ⭐ **DELHI's cases go to the NHRC.**

---

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **Same Act — Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.** ⭐ **Chairperson: a retired CHIEF JUSTICE or JUDGE of a HIGH COURT** *(widened in 2019)*, plus ⭐ **TWO members** — a High Court judge or a **District Judge with 7 years' service**, and a **human-rights expert**.
- ⭐ **Appointed by the GOVERNOR** on a committee of the ⭐ **CM (chair), Assembly Speaker, State Home Minister and Leader of Opposition**, ⭐ **+ the Council Chairman and the Council's Leader of Opposition where a Legislative Council exists.**
- ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Removed by the PRESIDENT, after a SUPREME COURT inquiry — not by the Governor.**
- ⭐ **Term 3 years or 70**, reappointment allowed; ⭐ **civil-court powers**; ⚠️ **one-year limitation**; ⚠️ **recommendations not binding**; ⭐ **may approach the Supreme Court or the High Court**.
- ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Jurisdiction limited to the STATE and CONCURRENT Lists** — and ⚠️ **not over a matter already before the NHRC or another statutory commission.**
- ⭐ **2019: an SHRC may be given charge of a UT; DELHI's cases go to the NHRC.**
