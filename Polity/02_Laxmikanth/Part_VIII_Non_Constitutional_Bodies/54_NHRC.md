# Chapter 54 — National Human Rights Commission

[← Chapter 53](53_NITI_Aayog.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 55 →](55_State_Human_Rights_Commission.md)

**Read time ~13 min** · **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** *(amended 2006 and 2019)* · 🔴 ⚠️ **Mains: 2000, 2014, 2018, 2021** — asked four times

> 📌 ⭐ **This is the most heavily examined chapter in Part VIII, and the questions are remarkably consistent: the NHRC does good work and cannot enforce anything — why, and what would fix it?** ⚠️ **Learn the THREE structural limitations in Part C by heart. They are the answer to every one of the four questions asked so far.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐ **The NHRC is a STATUTORY body with the powers of a civil court, an independent investigating wing, and judges of the Supreme Court on it** — ⚠️ **and at the end of an inquiry all it can do is RECOMMEND.** ⭐ **Laxmikanth's own verdict, worth quoting: it is "a watchdog of human rights in the country" whose recommendations are not binding — the gap between those two halves is the chapter.**

---

## PART A — Origin and status

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Status** | ⚠️ ⭐ **STATUTORY, not constitutional** — established under the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** |
| ⭐ **Established** | **1993** *(the Act was preceded by an Ordinance)* |
| ⭐ **International backdrop** | ⭐ **The PARIS PRINCIPLES (1991, endorsed by the UN General Assembly in 1993)** — the global benchmark for national human rights institutions: **pluralist composition, a broad mandate, adequate funding, and independence from the executive** |
| ⭐⭐ **Definition of "human rights"** *(s.2(1)(d))* | ⭐ **Rights relating to LIFE, LIBERTY, EQUALITY and DIGNITY of the individual, guaranteed by the CONSTITUTION or embodied in the INTERNATIONAL COVENANTS and ENFORCEABLE BY COURTS IN INDIA** |

> ⭐⭐ **That definition is worth memorising word-for-word.** ⚠️ **Note its reach: it includes rights from the international covenants — but only those "enforceable by courts in India."** ⭐ **So the NHRC's jurisdiction is wider than Part III of the Constitution, and narrower than international human rights law.**

---

## PART B — Composition and appointment

### ⭐⭐ Composition after the 2019 amendment

| Post | Who may hold it |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Chairperson** | ⭐⭐ **A retired CHIEF JUSTICE OF INDIA *or* a retired JUDGE OF THE SUPREME COURT** — ⚠️ **the 2019 amendment widened this; before 2019 only a former CJI was eligible** |
| **Member 1** | A serving or retired **Judge of the Supreme Court** |
| **Member 2** | A serving or retired **Chief Justice of a High Court** |
| ⭐ **Members 3, 4, 5** | ⭐ **THREE persons having knowledge of, or practical experience in, human rights** — ⚠️ ⭐ **of whom at least ONE shall be a WOMAN** *(the 2019 amendment raised this from two members to three and added the woman requirement)* |
| ⭐⭐ **Deemed (ex-officio) members** | ⭐ **The chairpersons of the National Commissions for MINORITIES, SCs, STs, WOMEN — and, added in 2019, BACKWARD CLASSES, the NCPCR, and the Chief Commissioner for PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES** |

⭐ **A Secretary-General** is the chief executive officer of the Commission.

> ⭐ **The deemed members are the Paris Principles' "pluralism" requirement in action** — the NHRC is deliberately built to contain the specialised commissions rather than compete with them. ⚠️ **Which makes [Mains 2018 Q16](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2018.md)'s "merge them all" question sharper: the architecture for coordination already exists and is under-used.**

### ⭐⭐ Appointment — the six-member committee

⭐ **Appointed by the PRESIDENT on the recommendation of a committee consisting of:**
> ⭐ **the PRIME MINISTER *(chairperson)* · the SPEAKER of the Lok Sabha · the DEPUTY CHAIRMAN of the Rajya Sabha · the LEADERS OF THE OPPOSITION in BOTH Houses · the UNION HOME MINISTER.**

⚠️ ⭐ **A sitting judge of the Supreme Court or a sitting Chief Justice of a High Court can be appointed only after CONSULTATION WITH THE CHIEF JUSTICE OF INDIA.**

### ⭐ Tenure and removal

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Term** | ⭐ **THREE years, or until the age of 70, whichever is earlier** — ⚠️ **the 2019 amendment reduced it from five years to three** |
| ⭐ **Reappointment** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Now permitted — the 2019 amendment removed the bar** |
| ⭐ **After demitting office** | Not eligible for further employment under the Union or a state government |
| ⭐⭐ **Removal** | ⭐ **By the PRESIDENT.** For ⭐ **proved misbehaviour or incapacity, only after a reference to the SUPREME COURT, which holds an inquiry and reports** — ⚠️ **and the President may remove only if the Court so advises.** Also removable directly for **insolvency, paid employment outside the office, unsoundness of mind, or conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude** |

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The 2019 amendment is a two-sided fact and you should present it as such.** ⭐ **It WIDENED eligibility** *(any retired Supreme Court judge could chair, ending long vacancies caused by waiting for a willing former CJI)* **and it ADDED members and a woman's seat.** ⚠️ **But it also SHORTENED the term to three years and PERMITTED REAPPOINTMENT — and a shorter term with the prospect of reappointment by the government is exactly the design that weakens independence.** ⭐ **That is the sophisticated point in any answer on the NHRC's autonomy.**

## PART C — ⭐ Functions, powers, and the three limitations

### ⭐ Functions

1. ⭐ **Inquire, SUO MOTU or on a petition, into a violation of human rights or abetment thereof, or NEGLIGENCE IN THE PREVENTION of such violation by a public servant**;
2. ⭐ **Intervene in any proceeding involving an allegation of human rights violation pending before a court**, with the court's approval;
3. ⭐ **VISIT jails and any state-controlled institution** where persons are detained, to study living conditions and make recommendations;
4. ⭐ **Review the constitutional and legal SAFEGUARDS** for the protection of human rights and recommend measures;
5. ⭐ **Review the factors, including acts of TERRORISM, that inhibit the enjoyment of human rights**;
6. **Study treaties and international instruments** and make recommendations for their effective implementation;
7. **Undertake and promote research**;
8. ⭐ **Spread human rights LITERACY** and promote awareness of the available safeguards;
9. ⭐ **Encourage the efforts of NGOs and institutions working in the field.**

### ⭐ Powers

- ⭐ **The powers of a CIVIL COURT trying a suit**, and its proceedings have a **judicial character**;
- ⭐ **It has its OWN investigating staff**, headed by a **Director-General of Police**, and may also ⭐ **utilise the services of any officer or investigating agency of the Centre or a state**;
- ⭐ It may look into a matter **within one year** of the violation *(see below)*;
- ⭐ **During or after an inquiry it may recommend** the payment of ⭐ **compensation or damages**, the ⭐ **initiation of prosecution**, or **immediate interim relief** to the victim or family;
- ⭐ It may ⭐ **approach the SUPREME COURT or the HIGH COURT** concerned for such directions, orders or writs as that court may deem necessary.

> ⭐⭐ **That last power is the NHRC's real weapon and it is routinely forgotten.** ⚠️ **It cannot enforce its own findings — but it can go to court to get them enforced.** ⭐ **Any answer that says "the NHRC is toothless" without mentioning this is incomplete.**

### ⭐ Reporting

⭐ The Commission submits **annual or special reports to the CENTRAL GOVERNMENT and to the STATE GOVERNMENT concerned**, which must lay them before **Parliament or the state legislature** ⭐ **together with a memorandum of action taken and the REASONS FOR NON-ACCEPTANCE of any recommendation.**

### ⚠️⚠️ THE THREE LIMITATIONS — memorise these

| # | Limitation | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **1** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Its functions are RECOMMENDATORY.** It cannot punish the violator, nor award relief itself — including compensation | ⭐ **The single most-cited criticism.** The remedy lies with the government or, on the NHRC's application, with the courts |
| ⭐⭐ **2** | ⚠️ ⭐ **It CANNOT inquire into a complaint made MORE THAN ONE YEAR after the incident** | ⚠️ **A hard statutory bar — many custodial and communal-violence complaints surface later than that** |
| ⭐⭐ **3** | ⚠️ ⭐ **A LIMITED ROLE regarding the ARMED FORCES.** It may only **seek a report from the Central Government** and then **make its recommendations**, on which the government must report back within **three months** | ⚠️ ⭐ **It cannot summon witnesses or conduct an independent investigation** into the armed forces — the largest hole in its jurisdiction, and the one that matters most in **conflict areas** |

⭐ **Add the practical limitations** — ⚠️ **chronic vacancies**; a staff drawn largely on **deputation from the police and the government**, which victims of police excess find hard to trust; **dependence on the government for funds**; and ⚠️ **the deferral of the NHRC's "A" status accreditation by the global alliance of national human rights institutions in 2023** over concerns about pluralism in appointments and police involvement in investigations. ⚠️ **Verify the current accreditation status before quoting it in an answer.**

### ⭐ Human Rights Courts

⭐ The Act provides that a **state government, with the CONCURRENCE of the CHIEF JUSTICE of the High Court, may specify a COURT OF SESSION in each district as a HUMAN RIGHTS COURT** for the speedy trial of offences arising out of human rights violations. ⚠️ **Implementation has been patchy, and a Court of Session so designated tries the same offences under ordinary law** — which is the standard criticism of the provision.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The status and the definition
> ⚠️ ⭐ **STATUTORY — Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.** ⭐ **"Human rights" = LIFE, LIBERTY, EQUALITY and DIGNITY, guaranteed by the Constitution or in the international covenants and ENFORCEABLE BY COURTS IN INDIA.**

### The composition, post-2019
> ⭐ **Chairperson = a retired CJI *or* a retired Supreme Court judge.** ⭐ **+ an SC judge, a HC Chief Justice, and THREE human-rights experts (at least one a WOMAN).** ⭐ **Deemed members = the chairpersons of the Minorities, SC, ST, Women, BC and Child Rights commissions + the Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities.**

### The committee of six
> ⭐ **PM (chair) · Speaker · Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha · both Leaders of Opposition · the Union HOME MINISTER.**

### The 2019 amendment, both ways
> ⭐ **Widened eligibility · more members · a woman member.** ⚠️ ⭐ **BUT: term cut from 5 years to 3, and REAPPOINTMENT now allowed.**

### The three limitations
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **RECOMMENDATORY · ONE-YEAR limitation · ARMED FORCES only by report.**

### The forgotten power
> ⭐ **It may approach the SUPREME COURT or a HIGH COURT for directions, orders or writs.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Though the Human Rights Commissions have contributed immensely to the protection of human rights in India, yet they have failed to assert themselves against the mighty and powerful. Analysing their structural and practical limitations, suggest remedial measures." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2021 Q12](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2021.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **The contribution** — suo motu inquiries into custodial deaths *(mandatory reporting within 24 hours is an NHRC-created norm)*, jail visits, interventions in court, compensation recommendations, guidelines on encounter deaths, and human-rights literacy. (ii) ⚠️ ⭐ **Structural limitations** — **recommendatory powers only**; the ⭐ **one-year limitation**; ⭐ **the armed-forces restriction**; **statutory, not constitutional, status**; ⚠️ **the 2019 amendment's shorter term with reappointment**; a **government-appointed** commission with a **Home Minister on the selection committee**. (iii) ⚠️ **Practical limitations** — vacancies, a police-dominated investigating wing, budget dependence, huge pendency, weak state commissions. (iv) ⭐ **Remedies** — make recommendations **binding unless rejected in a reasoned order laid before Parliament within a fixed time**; ⭐ **remove or extend the one-year bar**; ⭐ **allow independent inquiry into armed-forces complaints, at least where a civilian death is alleged**; **charge the expenditure on the Consolidated Fund**; **an independent investigating cadre**; a **fixed five-year non-renewable term**; and **constitutional status**, as the NCSC/NCST/NCBC have. **Conclude** with the Paris Principles as the standard against which each fix is justified.

**2. "National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) in India can be most effective when its tasks are adequately supported by other mechanisms that ensure the accountability of a government." Assess the role of the NHRC as an effective complement to the judiciary and other institutions. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2014 Q9](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2014.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **Frame the NHRC as a COMPLEMENT, not a substitute** — that is the question's own word. **Where it complements the judiciary** — ⭐ **suo motu jurisdiction** *(courts must wait to be moved)*; ⭐ **inquisitorial rather than adversarial** procedure; free and accessible; ⭐ **it can approach the Supreme Court or a High Court** to convert a finding into an order; **jail visits and systemic review**, which no court does routinely. **Where it depends on others** — ⚠️ **enforcement lies with the executive**; **prosecution with the state**; **compensation with the government**; **funding and staff with the Home Ministry**; and **the state human rights commissions** must carry the state-list burden. ⭐ **The other accountability mechanisms** — the **CAG**, **CVC and Lokpal**, **RTI and the Information Commissions**, **parliamentary committees**, a free press and civil society. **Conclude:** ⭐ **the NHRC is a diagnostic institution in a system where the curative power lies elsewhere** — its effectiveness is therefore a function of the health of the whole accountability chain, exactly as the question suggests.

**3. "Discuss the provisions of the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 — the definition of human rights, the composition of the NHRC, its functions, and suggestions for amendment." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2000 Q10(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2000.md), and answerable today with the 2006 and 2019 amendments as the "suggestions" that were adopted.*
> *Skeleton:* Definition *(s.2(1)(d))* → composition *(post-2019)* → appointment committee → term and removal → the nine functions → civil-court powers and the investigating wing → the three limitations → the reporting chain. ⭐ **For "suggestions for amendment"**: extend or remove the one-year bar; enforceable recommendations; independent investigation into the armed forces; a dedicated investigating cadre; charged expenditure; constitutional status; **and a critical note that the 2019 amendment moved in the wrong direction on tenure.**

**4. "Multiplicity of various commissions for the vulnerable sections of society leads to overlapping jurisdiction. Is it better to merge all commissions into an umbrella Human Rights Commission?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2018 Q16](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2018.md). ⭐ **Full skeleton in [Ch 46](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/46_National_Commission_SCs.md).*** ⭐ **Add from this chapter:** the ⭐ **deemed-member architecture already places seven commission chairpersons inside the NHRC**, so the coordination mechanism exists and is simply not used — which is the strongest argument for **reform without merger**.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⚠️ ⭐ **STATUTORY body — Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** *(amended **2006** and **2019**)*; benchmarked against the ⭐ **PARIS PRINCIPLES**.
- ⭐ **"Human rights" = life, liberty, equality and dignity, guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in the international covenants and ENFORCEABLE BY COURTS IN INDIA.**
- ⭐⭐ **Composition (post-2019):** Chairperson — a retired **CJI or Supreme Court judge**; + a **Supreme Court judge**; + a **High Court Chief Justice**; + ⭐ **THREE human-rights experts, at least one a WOMAN**; + ⭐ **seven deemed members** *(chairs of the Minorities, SC, ST, Women, BC and Child Rights commissions and the Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities)*.
- ⭐ **Appointed by the PRESIDENT on a SIX-member committee: PM (chair), Speaker, Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha, both Leaders of Opposition, Union HOME MINISTER.** ⭐ A **sitting** judge or Chief Justice needs **consultation with the CJI**.
- ⭐⭐ **Term: 3 years or 70** *(reduced from 5 in 2019)*; ⚠️ **reappointment now allowed**. ⭐ **Removed by the PRESIDENT, on misbehaviour, only after a SUPREME COURT inquiry and report.**
- ⭐ **Functions:** suo motu or petition inquiry, including **negligence in prevention** by a public servant · intervene in court proceedings · ⭐ **visit jails** · review safeguards · review **terrorism** as an inhibiting factor · treaties · research · ⭐ **human-rights literacy** · encourage NGOs.
- ⭐ **Powers of a CIVIL COURT** · its own investigating staff under a **DGP** · may recommend **compensation, prosecution or interim relief** · ⭐ **may approach the Supreme Court or a High Court for directions.**
- ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **THE THREE LIMITATIONS: recommendations are NOT BINDING · no complaint older than ONE YEAR · the ARMED FORCES only by seeking a report from the Central Government** *(which must reply within three months)*.
- ⭐ **Reports to the Central and state governments → laid before Parliament / the state legislature with an action-taken memorandum and reasons for non-acceptance.**
- ⭐ **Human Rights Courts** — a state government may designate a **Court of Session** in each district, ⭐ **with the concurrence of the High Court's Chief Justice.**
