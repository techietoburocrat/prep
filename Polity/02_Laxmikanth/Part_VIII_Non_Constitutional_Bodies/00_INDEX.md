## PART B — ⭐ The three arguments this Part is built on

### ⭐⭐ 1. Recommendatory power, and the memorandum technique

⚠️ **Almost every body here can inquire like a court and decide nothing.** ⭐ **The Constitution's and Parliament's standard device is the ACTION-TAKEN MEMORANDUM: the government must lay before the legislature a written explanation of every recommendation it rejected, and the reasons.**

> ⭐⭐ **The exception is worth knowing precisely: the CENTRAL and STATE INFORMATION COMMISSIONS.** ⭐ **Their orders BIND, and they can fine an erring officer ₹250 a day up to ₹25,000 — out of his own pocket.** ⚠️ **No other body in Part VIII can impose a personal consequence on an individual.**

### ⭐⭐ 2. The three levers of institutional independence

| Lever | ⭐ Strong here | ⚠️ Weak here |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Appointment** | ⭐ **Lokpal** — a five-member committee in which the government holds **one seat**, and the fifth member is chosen by the other four | ⚠️ **CIC and CVC** — PM + a minister + LoP · ⚠️ **CBI** — the government appoints, though on a PM/LoP/CJI committee |
| ⭐ **Tenure** | ⭐ **CVC** — 4 years, bar on future office · ⭐ **Lokpal** — 5 years, extensive post-office bars | ⚠️ ⭐ **CIC after 2019** — term prescribed by the **Central Government** · ⚠️ **NHRC after 2019** — 3 years **with reappointment allowed** · ⚠️ **CBI Director** — annual extensions to 5 years |
| ⭐ **Finance** | ⭐ **CVC** — expenditure **charged** on the Consolidated Fund | ⚠️ **NHRC, CIC, NDMA** — dependent on ministry budgets and deputationist staff |

> ⭐ **This table is reusable across the whole book.** ⚠️ **Whenever a question asks about the autonomy of ANY body, test it on appointment, tenure and finance — and then add the fourth, unwritten lever: whether it has its OWN investigating and secretarial capacity, or borrows the government's.**

### ⭐⭐ 3. The statute always answers the judgment

⭐ **You will see the same sequence three times in this Part and twice more elsewhere in the book:**

| The Court acts | ⚠️ Parliament or the executive answers |
|---|---|
| ⭐ ***Vineet Narain* (1997)** — give the CVC statutory status and the CBI Director a 2-year tenure | ⭐ **CVC Act, 2003** *(complied)* — but ⚠️ **the DSPE (Amendment) Act, 2021 added annual extensions to 5 years** |
| ⭐ ***Subramanian Swamy* (2014)** — strikes down the "single directive" prior approval under **Article 14** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Section 17A of the PC Act (2018) restores prior approval by statute** |
| ⭐ ***Anoop Baranwal* (2023)** — ECI appointments by PM + LoP + CJI | ⚠️ ⭐ **The 2023 Act replaces the CJI with a Union Cabinet Minister** *(see [Ch 41](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/41_Election_Commission.md))* |
| ⭐ **May 2023 Constitution Bench** — services belong to the elected Delhi government | ⚠️ ⭐ **GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2023** *(see [Ch 39](../Part_VI_Union_Territories_and_Special_Areas/39_Union_Territories.md))* |

> ⭐⭐ **Making this observation explicitly — "a judgment interpreting a statute can always be answered by a new statute; only a BASIC STRUCTURE finding cannot" — connects Part VIII back to [Ch 11](../Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/11_Basic_Structure.md) and lifts an ordinary answer into a good one.**

## PART C — ⭐ The anti-corruption architecture, on one page

| Body | ⭐ Investigates? | ⭐ Supervises? | ⭐ Directs? | ⭐ Punishes? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **CVC** | ⚠️ No own machinery | ⭐ **Yes — the CBI, for PC Act cases only** | Advises | ⚠️ **No** |
| ⭐ **CBI** | ⭐ **Yes** | — | — | ⚠️ **No — it prosecutes; the court punishes** |
| ⭐ **Lokpal** | ⭐ **Own Inquiry and Prosecution Wings** | ⭐ **Yes — the CBI, in cases it refers** | ⭐ **Yes — including a bar on transferring the investigating officer** | ⚠️ **No** |
| ⭐ **Courts** | — | — | — | ⭐ **Yes** |

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Read the last column: NO investigating or oversight body in India can punish.** ⭐ **Punishment requires a Special Court, and getting to that court requires a SANCTION FOR PROSECUTION from the government** *(Section 19 of the PC Act; Section 17A for even beginning an inquiry)*. ⭐ **That is where the anti-corruption chain actually breaks — not at the investigation stage — and saying so is the mark of an answer written by someone who understands the system.**
