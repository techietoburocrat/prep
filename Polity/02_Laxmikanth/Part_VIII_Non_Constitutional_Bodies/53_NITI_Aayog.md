# Chapter 53 — NITI Aayog

[← Chapter 52](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/52_Advocate_General.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 54 →](54_NHRC.md)

**Read time ~8 min** · **No article, no statute — a CABINET RESOLUTION** · 🟠 Prelims: **2013, 2014, 2015** · Mains: through federalism and governance questions

> 📌 ⭐ **The single most useful fact in this chapter is what NITI Aayog is NOT.** ⚠️ **It is neither a constitutional body nor a statutory one — it exists by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet, exactly as the Planning Commission did.** ⭐ **That fact alone has decided several Prelims questions.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **The Planning Commission decided how much money each state got. NITI Aayog decides nothing.** ⚠️ **It advises, benchmarks, ranks and convenes — and the money now moves entirely through the Finance Commission and the line ministries.** ⭐ **Whether that is a gain for federalism or a loss of leverage for the states is the whole debate.**

---

## PART A — What it replaced

| | ⭐ **Planning Commission *(1950–2014)*** | ⭐ **NITI Aayog *(from 1 January 2015)*** |
|---|---|---|
| **Basis** | ⚠️ **A Cabinet resolution** *(March 1950)* | ⚠️ ⭐ **A Cabinet resolution — the same non-status** |
| ⭐⭐ **Financial power** | ⭐ **Allocated funds to states and to central ministries; approved state plans** | ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **NONE. It cannot allocate a rupee** |
| **Planning direction** | ⚠️ **Top-down** — the Centre wrote the plan | ⭐ **Bottom-up** — states as participants |
| **Its apex forum** | ⭐ **National Development Council** *(PM, CMs, Union ministers, Planning Commission members)* | ⭐ **Governing Council** *(PM, all CMs, LGs of UTs)* |
| **Output** | ⭐ **Five Year Plans** *(the 12th, 2012–17, was the last)* | ⭐ **A 15-year Vision, a 7-year Strategy and a 3-year Action Agenda**; indices and reports |
| **Membership** | Full-time members with fixed positions | A leaner core plus **ex-officio and part-time** members |
| **Relation to states** | ⚠️ Approver and paymaster | ⭐ **Convener and evaluator** |

> ⭐⭐ **The sentence that captures the change:** ⚠️ **the Planning Commission's authority came from its cheque book; NITI Aayog has no cheque book, so its authority must come from the quality of its ideas and the Prime Minister's chairmanship.** ⭐ **Everything anyone says for or against NITI Aayog follows from that.**

⚠️ **Do not miss the parallel move:** ⭐ **the 14th Finance Commission raised devolution from 32% to 42% in the very same period** *(see [Ch 44](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/44_Finance_Commission.md))*. ⭐ **The states received more untied money and lost the forum where they used to bargain for tied money.** ⚠️ **Whether that was a net gain depends on the state — a well-run state gained autonomy, a weak one lost an advocate.**

---

## PART B — Structure

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Chairperson** | ⭐⭐ **The PRIME MINISTER** |
| ⭐ **Vice-Chairperson** | ⭐ **Appointed by the Prime Minister** *(holds Cabinet-minister rank)* |
| ⭐⭐ **Governing Council** | ⭐ **The Chief Ministers of ALL states and the Lieutenant Governors of Union Territories** |
| ⭐ **Regional Councils** | ⭐ **Formed by the Prime Minister for a specific period, to address issues affecting more than one state or region** — convened by the PM, comprising the **CMs and LGs of the region** |
| ⭐ **Full-time members** | A small number of domain experts |
| ⭐ **Ex-officio members** | ⭐ **Not more than FOUR Union ministers**, nominated by the Prime Minister |
| ⭐ **Part-time members** | ⭐ **Not more than TWO**, from leading universities and research organisations, **on a rotational basis** |
| ⭐ **Chief Executive Officer** | ⭐ **Appointed by the Prime Minister for a fixed tenure, in the rank of SECRETARY to the Government of India** |
| **Special invitees** | Experts and practitioners nominated by the Prime Minister |
| **Internal hubs** | ⭐ **Team India Hub** *(the interface with states)* and ⭐ **Knowledge and Innovation Hub** *(the think-tank function)* |

## PART C — ⭐ What it actually does, and what it is criticised for

### ⭐ The functions worth naming

1. ⭐ **COOPERATIVE FEDERALISM** — structured engagement with the states through the Governing Council and Regional Councils;
2. ⭐ **A think tank** — policy advice to the Centre and the states, and a "directional and strategic" input on national priorities;
3. ⭐ **MONITORING AND EVALUATION** — the **Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office**;
4. ⭐ **Competitive federalism through RANKING** — the ⭐ **SDG India Index**, **Composite Water Management Index**, **Health Index**, **School Education Quality Index**, **India Innovation Index**, and the **National Multidimensional Poverty Index**;
5. ⭐ **The Aspirational Districts Programme** *(2018)* — ranking the most backward districts on measurable indicators and publishing the league table;
6. ⭐ **Atal Innovation Mission** — innovation and entrepreneurship promotion.

> ⭐ **"Competitive federalism" is NITI Aayog's real instrument.** ⚠️ **It cannot make a state do anything — so it publishes a ranking and lets political competition do the work.** ⭐ **That is a genuinely new technique of Indian governance and worth a sentence in any federalism answer.**

### ⚠️ The criticisms

1. ⚠️ ⭐ **No financial powers at all**, so its recommendations carry no consequence for a state that ignores them;
2. ⚠️ ⭐ **No constitutional or statutory status** — it can be reconstituted or abolished by another Cabinet resolution;
3. ⚠️ **The Governing Council meets rarely**, and its meetings have been boycotted by some Chief Ministers, weakening the cooperative-federalism claim;
4. ⚠️ ⭐ **The states lost a forum for negotiating allocations without gaining a forum for negotiating policy** — the Inter-State Council *(Article 263)* remains under-used *(see [Ch 15](../Part_II_System_of_Government/15_Inter_State_Relations.md))*;
5. ⚠️ **Ranking without capacity-building** can punish poor states for structural disadvantage;
6. ⚠️ **Perceived proximity to the government of the day** — as an advisory body inside the executive, it lacks the arm's-length position of a Finance Commission.

⭐ **The standard reform suggestion:** ⭐ **give NITI Aayog statutory status, revive the Inter-State Council as the political forum, and route centrally sponsored schemes through a body where states have a vote** — the same conclusion the GST Council chapter reaches from the other direction.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The status
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **NEITHER constitutional NOR statutory — a CABINET RESOLUTION, 1 January 2015.** ⭐ *(So was the Planning Commission, March 1950.)*

### The one real difference
> ⭐⭐ **The Planning Commission ALLOCATED FUNDS. NITI Aayog does NOT.**

### The composition
> ⭐ **Chair = PM.** ⭐ **Governing Council = all CMs + UT LGs.** ⭐ **Ex-officio ≤ 4 Union ministers · part-time ≤ 2 · CEO of Secretary rank, appointed by the PM.**

### The forums, paired
> ⭐ **Planning Commission → NATIONAL DEVELOPMENT COUNCIL.** ⭐ **NITI Aayog → GOVERNING COUNCIL.** *(Both chaired by the PM; the NDC also included Union ministers and Planning Commission members.)*

### The technique
> ⭐ **"Competitive federalism" — it cannot direct, so it RANKS.** SDG India Index · Water · Health · School Education · Innovation · **Aspirational Districts**.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The replacement of the Planning Commission by NITI Aayog strengthened federalism in form and weakened the states' bargaining power in substance." Critically examine. (250 words)**
> *Skeleton:* **What changed** — a body that **allocated funds and approved state plans** was replaced by one that **advises and evaluates**; the **NDC** by the **Governing Council**; **Five Year Plans** by a **Vision–Strategy–Action Agenda**. ⭐ **The federal gains** — an end to the Centre "approving" state plans; ⭐ **the 14th Finance Commission's 42% devolution** giving states untied money; **bottom-up planning**; **competitive federalism** through indices; **Regional Councils** for cross-state issues. ⚠️ **The losses** — ⭐ **no forum with money attached**, so states cannot bargain; **centrally sponsored schemes under Article 282 continue** with matching shares, and are now negotiated with **line ministries individually rather than collectively**; ⚠️ the **Governing Council meets infrequently**; and NITI Aayog's **advisory status inside the executive** limits its ability to arbitrate. **Conclude:** the reform correctly removed a body with **no constitutional warrant for the power it exercised** — ⭐ **but it left a vacuum that only a revived Inter-State Council, or a statutory NITI Aayog with state representation, can fill.**

**2. "NITI Aayog governs by ranking rather than by directing. Assess this technique." (150 words)**
> *Skeleton:* Describe **competitive federalism** and name the indices, especially the ⭐ **Aspirational Districts Programme**. **Strengths** — transparent, data-driven, creates political incentives, builds state statistical capacity, avoids coercion in a federal system. ⚠️ **Weaknesses** — rankings reward **starting position** unless measured on *improvement*; data are self-reported; ⚠️ **no resources accompany the ranking**; and a poor rank can demoralise rather than mobilise. **Conclude:** ranking works as an **accelerator where capacity exists** and is no substitute for **capacity-building where it does not.**

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **NITI Aayog is NEITHER constitutional NOR statutory — created by a CABINET RESOLUTION on 1 January 2015**, replacing the **Planning Commission** *(itself a 1950 resolution)*.
- ⭐⭐ **The decisive difference: the Planning Commission ALLOCATED FUNDS; NITI Aayog has NO financial powers.**
- ⭐ **Chairperson = PRIME MINISTER; Vice-Chairperson appointed by the PM; CEO of SECRETARY rank appointed by the PM.**
- ⭐ **Governing Council = all Chief Ministers + LGs of UTs.** ⭐ **Regional Councils formed by the PM for specific issues.** ⭐ **Ex-officio members ≤ 4 Union ministers; part-time members ≤ 2, from universities and research bodies, by rotation.**
- ⭐ **Two hubs — Team India Hub *(state interface)* and Knowledge and Innovation Hub *(think tank)*.**
- ⭐ **Planning output: the 12th Five Year Plan (2012–17) was the LAST.** Replaced by a ⭐ **15-year Vision, 7-year Strategy and 3-year Action Agenda.**
- ⭐ **Instruments: SDG India Index · Composite Water Management Index · Health Index · School Education Quality Index · India Innovation Index · National MPI · ASPIRATIONAL DISTRICTS PROGRAMME · Atal Innovation Mission.**
- ⚠️ **Criticisms:** no money · no legal status · infrequent Governing Council · states lost a bargaining forum · rankings without resources · perceived closeness to the executive.
