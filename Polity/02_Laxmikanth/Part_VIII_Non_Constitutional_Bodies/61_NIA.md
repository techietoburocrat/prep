# Chapter 61 — National Investigation Agency

[← Chapter 60](60_Lokpal.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 62 →](62_NDMA.md)

**Read time ~5 min** · **National Investigation Agency Act, 2008** · 🟡 Prelims: constitutional-vs-statutory format · Mains: through federalism and internal security

> 📌 ⭐ **Read this chapter as the mirror image of [Ch 59](59_CBI.md).** ⚠️ **The CBI has no Act of its own and needs state consent. The NIA has its own Act and needs NO state consent.** ⭐ **That single contrast is the chapter's entire exam value.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **After 26/11, Parliament decided that terrorism could not be investigated by an agency that had to ask a state for permission** — ⭐ **so it created a federal agency with statutory suo motu jurisdiction over scheduled offences anywhere in India.** ⚠️ **Which is exactly why the NIA is the most federally contested investigating agency in the country.**

---

## PART A — The Act and its jurisdiction

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Act** | ⭐⭐ **National Investigation Agency Act, 2008**, enacted weeks after the ⭐ **26 November 2008 Mumbai attacks** |
| ⭐ **Status** | ⭐ **STATUTORY**, under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** |
| **Head** | A **Director-General**, with the powers of a Director-General of Police |
| ⭐⭐ **What it investigates** | ⭐ **"SCHEDULED OFFENCES" — offences under the Acts listed in the SCHEDULE to the Act**, which include the ⭐ **Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967**, the **Atomic Energy Act**, the **Anti-Hijacking Act**, the **SAARC Convention (Suppression of Terrorism) Act**, and the **Explosive Substances Act** |
| ⭐⭐ **Consent** | ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **NONE required.** ⭐ **The Central Government may direct the NIA to take up a scheduled offence ANYWHERE IN INDIA, and the state must transfer the case and hand over the records.** The NIA may also act on a **state's own request**, or on the **Central Government's suo motu direction** |
| ⭐ **Special Courts** | ⭐ **The Central Government designates SESSIONS COURTS as NIA SPECIAL COURTS** for the trial of scheduled offences |

### ⭐ The NIA (Amendment) Act, 2019 — three changes

1. ⭐⭐ **Extra-territorial jurisdiction** — ⭐ **the NIA may investigate scheduled offences committed OUTSIDE INDIA against Indian citizens or affecting India's interests**, subject to international treaties and the domestic law of the country concerned;
2. ⭐ **New scheduled offences added** — ⭐ **HUMAN TRAFFICKING · counterfeit currency · manufacture or sale of prohibited arms · CYBER-TERRORISM · offences under the Explosive Substances Act, 1908**;
3. ⭐ **The Central Government may designate Sessions Courts as Special Courts**, and **state governments may do so with the Centre's concurrence.**

> ⭐ **Learn the 2019 additions as a list — "trafficking, counterfeit currency, prohibited arms, cyber-terrorism, explosives" — because they are exactly the sort of five-item set a statement question is built from.**

⭐ **In the same year, the ⭐ Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Amendment Act, 2019 allowed the Central Government to designate an INDIVIDUAL — not just an organisation — as a terrorist**, and empowered an **NIA officer of Inspector rank or above** to investigate UAPA offences and, with the DG's approval, to **attach property** representing proceeds of terrorism.

## PART B — ⚠️ The federalism objection

| ⚠️ **The objection** | ⭐ **The justification** |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **"Police" and "public order" are State List entries 2 and 1** — a central agency investigating inside a state without consent invades state jurisdiction | ⭐ **Terrorism is trans-state and trans-national by nature; an offence planned in one state and executed in another cannot be investigated state by state** |
| ⚠️ **The Centre alone decides when to take a case over**, with no state veto and no independent trigger | ⭐ **The Union has legislative competence over ⭐ "defence", "foreign affairs" and ⭐ Union List entry 8 — "Central Bureau of Intelligence and Investigation"** |
| ⚠️ **A state loses the case, the records and the investigating officers** | ⭐ **Entry 93 of the Union List covers "offences against laws with respect to any of the matters in this List"** |

> ⭐ **Chhattisgarh challenged the NIA Act before the Supreme Court under ARTICLE 131** *(the Court's original jurisdiction over Centre–state disputes — see [Ch 26](../Part_III_Central_Government/26_Supreme_Court.md))*, arguing that the Act is beyond Parliament's competence and destroys the federal structure. ⭐ **Cite the challenge as evidence that the federal objection is live and litigated, not theoretical.**

⭐ **The comparison to write out:**

| | ⭐ **CBI** | ⭐ **NIA** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Own statute?** | ⚠️ **No** — a 1963 resolution + the DSPE Act, 1946 | ⭐ **Yes — the NIA Act, 2008** |
| ⭐⭐ **State consent?** | ⭐ **Required *(Section 6, DSPE Act)*** | ⚠️ ⭐ **NOT required** |
| **Parent ministry** | ⭐ **DoPT** *(Ministry of Personnel)* | ⭐ **Ministry of Home Affairs** |
| **Supervised by** | ⭐ **CVC**, for PC Act cases | The Central Government |
| **Court-ordered probes** | ⭐ **Supreme Court / High Courts may order one without consent** | Not needed — the Centre may direct it directly |

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The trigger and the contrast
> ⭐ **NIA Act, 2008 — after 26/11.** ⭐⭐ **The CBI needs state consent; the NIA does NOT.**

### The 2019 amendment
> ⭐ **Offences OUTSIDE India · human trafficking · counterfeit currency · prohibited arms · CYBER-TERRORISM · Explosive Substances Act · state Special Courts with the Centre's concurrence.**

### The companion law
> ⭐ **UAPA (Amendment) Act, 2019 — an INDIVIDUAL may be designated a terrorist; NIA officers of Inspector rank and above may investigate; property attachment with the DG's approval.**

### The constitutional peg
> ⭐ **Union List entry 8 — "Central Bureau of Intelligence and Investigation."** ⚠️ **Article 131 challenge by Chhattisgarh.**

---

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **National Investigation Agency Act, 2008**, enacted after the ⭐ **26/11 Mumbai attacks**; ⭐ **STATUTORY**, under the ⭐ **Ministry of Home Affairs**; headed by a **Director-General**.
- ⭐ **Investigates "SCHEDULED OFFENCES"** — under the **UAPA, 1967**, the Atomic Energy Act, the Anti-Hijacking Act, the SAARC Convention Act, the Explosive Substances Act and others in the Schedule.
- ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **NO state consent required** — the Central Government may direct the NIA to take up a scheduled offence anywhere in India, and the state must hand over the case and records. ⭐ **Sessions Courts are designated NIA SPECIAL COURTS.**
- ⭐ **NIA (Amendment) Act, 2019:** ⭐ **jurisdiction over offences committed OUTSIDE India** · new scheduled offences — ⭐ **human trafficking, counterfeit currency, prohibited arms, cyber-terrorism, Explosive Substances Act** · ⭐ **states may designate Special Courts with the Centre's concurrence.**
- ⭐ **UAPA (Amendment) Act, 2019 — an INDIVIDUAL may be designated a terrorist**; NIA Inspectors and above may investigate; property attachment with the DG's approval.
- ⚠️ **Federal objection:** police and public order are **State List** subjects; the Centre alone triggers a takeover; ⭐ **Chhattisgarh's Article 131 challenge**. ⭐ **Justification: Union List entries 8 and 93, and the trans-state nature of terrorism.**
