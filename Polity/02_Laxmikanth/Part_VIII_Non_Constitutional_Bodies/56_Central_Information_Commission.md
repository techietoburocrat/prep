# Chapter 56 — Central Information Commission

[← Chapter 55](55_State_Human_Rights_Commission.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md) · [Chapter 57 →](57_State_Information_Commission.md)

**Read time ~9 min** · **Right to Information Act, 2005** *(amended 2019)* · 🟠 Prelims: RTI recurs · ⚠️ **Mains: 2012, 2020** — the 2020 question was *entirely* about this Commission's autonomy

> 📌 ⭐ **The CIC is the exception in Part VIII: its decisions are BINDING.** ⚠️ **Every other body in this Part recommends; the CIC orders — it can direct disclosure, impose a penalty on an officer, and award compensation.** ⭐ **That makes the 2019 amendment, which put its tenure and salary in the government's hands, a far bigger deal than it looks.**

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> ⭐⭐ **The Right to Information Act converted a Fundamental Right into a procedure with a deadline** — ⚠️ **and the CIC is the appellate authority that makes the deadline mean something.** ⭐ **Its power to fine the erring officer personally is the sharpest enforcement tool any Indian oversight body possesses.**

---

## PART A — ⭐ The right behind the Act

⭐ **The RTI Act did not create the right to information.** ⚠️ ⭐ **The Supreme Court had already read it into ARTICLE 19(1)(a) — the freedom of speech and expression — because you cannot form or express an opinion without access to facts** *(the line of cases from ***State of UP v Raj Narain* (1975)** and ***S.P. Gupta* (1981)**)*. ⭐ **The Act supplied the machinery.**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Act** | ⭐ **Right to Information Act, 2005** *(in full force from 12 October 2005)* |
| ⭐ **Who must disclose** | ⭐ **"Public authorities"** — any authority or body established by the Constitution, by law of Parliament or a state legislature, or by government notification; ⭐ **including bodies OWNED, CONTROLLED OR SUBSTANTIALLY FINANCED by the government, and NGOs substantially financed by it** |
| ⭐ **The officers** | ⭐ **Public Information Officers (PIOs)** in every public authority, plus **Assistant PIOs**; ⭐ **a first appeal lies to a senior officer, and the SECOND APPEAL to the Information Commission** |
| ⭐ **Timelines** | ⭐ **30 days** ordinarily; ⭐ **48 hours** where the information concerns the **life or liberty of a person**; 35 days if routed through an APIO |
| ⭐ **Section 4 — suo motu disclosure** | ⭐ **Every public authority must proactively publish 17 categories of information**, so that citizens have minimum resort to the Act. ⚠️ **The most under-implemented section in the Act** |
| ⭐ **Section 8** | The **exemptions** — sovereignty and integrity, security, foreign relations, contempt of court, breach of parliamentary privilege, commercial confidence and trade secrets, fiduciary relationships, personal information with no public interest, cabinet papers *(⭐ **disclosable once the decision is taken and the matter complete**)* |
| ⭐ **Section 8(2) — the public interest override** | ⭐⭐ **Information may be disclosed if the PUBLIC INTEREST IN DISCLOSURE OUTWEIGHS the harm to the protected interests — notwithstanding the Official Secrets Act, 1923** |
| ⭐ **Section 24** | ⭐ **Intelligence and security organisations in the Second Schedule are EXEMPT** — ⚠️ **except for information on allegations of CORRUPTION and HUMAN RIGHTS VIOLATIONS**, and human-rights information requires the **Commission's approval** |

> ⭐⭐ **Section 8(2) is the provision to quote.** ⚠️ **It expressly overrides the Official Secrets Act, 1923 — a colonial statute that had governed government information for eighty years.** ⭐ **That single sub-section is the strongest statement of the transparency principle in Indian law.**

---

## PART B — The Commission

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Composition** | ⭐ **The CHIEF INFORMATION COMMISSIONER + not more than TEN Information Commissioners** |
| ⭐⭐ **Appointment** | ⭐ **By the PRESIDENT, on the recommendation of a committee of the ⭐ PRIME MINISTER *(chair)*, the LEADER OF OPPOSITION in the LOK SABHA, and a UNION CABINET MINISTER nominated by the Prime Minister** |
| **Who may be appointed** | Persons of eminence in public life with wide knowledge and experience in **law, science and technology, social service, management, journalism, mass media or administration and governance**; ⚠️ **they must not be MPs or MLAs, hold any other office of profit, be connected with a political party, or carry on a business or profession** |
| ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Term** | ⭐ **Originally 5 years or 65, and salary at parity with the CEC and Election Commissioners.** ⚠️ ⭐ **The RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 REMOVED both — the TERM and the SALARY are now "as PRESCRIBED BY THE CENTRAL GOVERNMENT"** *(the rules made under it set a three-year term)* |
| **Reappointment** | ⚠️ **Not eligible for reappointment** |
| ⭐ **Removal** | ⭐ **By the PRESIDENT** — on **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**, ⭐ **after a reference to the SUPREME COURT and an inquiry by it**; and directly for **insolvency, conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude, paid employment, infirmity, or acquiring a financial or other interest** likely to affect his functions |

### ⭐⭐ Powers — and why they are different from everything else in this Part

| Power | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Inquiry** | ⭐ **Powers of a CIVIL COURT** — summons, discovery, evidence on affidavit, requisition of public records |
| ⭐⭐ **Access** | ⭐ **During an inquiry it may EXAMINE ANY RECORD under the control of a public authority — and ⚠️ NO SUCH RECORD MAY BE WITHHELD FROM IT ON ANY GROUND** |
| ⭐⭐ **Binding orders** | ⭐ **It may REQUIRE the public authority to provide information in a particular form, appoint a PIO, publish information, make changes to record-keeping, or provide training** |
| ⭐⭐ **Penalty** | ⭐ **A penalty on the PIO PERSONALLY — ₹250 PER DAY of delay, up to a maximum of ₹25,000** — for refusing an application, not furnishing information within time, malafide denial, giving false information, or destroying information |
| ⭐ **Compensation and discipline** | ⭐ **It may require the public authority to COMPENSATE the complainant for any loss**, and ⭐ **recommend disciplinary action** against the PIO |

> ⭐⭐ **Read the penalty row again.** ⚠️ **The fine falls on the officer, out of his own salary — not on the department.** ⭐ **No other body in Part VIII can impose a personal financial consequence on an individual public servant.** ⭐ **That is why the RTI regime changed behaviour where recommendatory bodies did not.**

## PART C — ⚠️⚠️ The 2019 amendment, and the autonomy question

⭐ **What the original Act did:** it fixed the Information Commissioners' **term at 5 years** and their **salary at parity with the Chief Election Commissioner / Election Commissioners** — ⭐ **deliberately, so that the CIC's status was set by PARLIAMENT and could not be adjusted by the executive.**

⚠️ ⭐ **What the RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 did:** it deleted both, and provided that the ⭐ **term, salary, allowances and other terms of service shall be "as may be prescribed by the Central Government."**

| ⚠️ **The criticism** | ⭐ **The government's justification** |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **A body that adjudicates against the government now has its tenure and pay set by that government** | The CEC is a **constitutional** authority and the CIC a **statutory** one; equating them was a **drafting anomaly** |
| ⚠️ **Shorter, renewable-free but government-fixed terms invite anticipatory caution in Commissioners** | Flexibility to rationalise service conditions across statutory bodies |
| ⚠️ ⭐ **It applies to the STATE Information Commissions too — the CENTRE now prescribes the terms of a body appointed by a GOVERNOR**, which is a federal objection | Uniformity across the RTI regime |

> ⭐⭐ **The answer [Mains 2020 Q2](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2020.md) wanted:** ⭐ **the amendment does not touch the Commission's POWERS — the binding orders and the penalty survive intact.** ⚠️ **What it changes is the INCENTIVE STRUCTURE of the people exercising them.** ⭐ **Independence in oversight bodies is manufactured through fixed terms, protected salaries and secure removal — the 2019 Act removed two of the three legs, leaving only removal-by-Supreme-Court-inquiry.** ⭐ **And the federal objection about state commissions is the point most answers miss.**

### ⚠️ The other problems worth naming

1. ⚠️ ⭐ **Vacancies and pendency** — the CIC and several SICs have functioned below strength with backlogs running into years, which defeats an Act built on a 30-day timeline;
2. ⚠️ ⭐ **Section 4 proactive disclosure is largely ignored**, so citizens must file applications for information that should already be published;
3. ⚠️ **Threats to RTI activists** — the **Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014** remains substantially un-operational;
4. ⚠️ ⭐ **The 2023 data-protection legislation amended Section 8(1)(j)** to broaden the exemption for personal information, ⚠️ **which transparency advocates argue removes the public-interest test that used to let citizens obtain information about public servants.** ⭐ **This is the newest and most examinable RTI controversy** — check the current position before writing on it.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The right and the Act
> ⭐ **The RIGHT comes from ARTICLE 19(1)(a); the ACT (2005) supplies the machinery.** ⭐ **Section 8(2) overrides the OFFICIAL SECRETS ACT, 1923.**

### The numbers
> ⭐ **30 days · 48 hours for life and liberty · ₹250 per day · ₹25,000 maximum · Chief + 10 Commissioners · 17 categories under Section 4.**

### The appointment committee of three
> ⭐ **PM (chair) + LEADER OF OPPOSITION in the Lok Sabha + a UNION CABINET MINISTER nominated by the PM.** *(Note how closely this mirrors the **2023 ECI Selection Committee** — see [Ch 41](../Part_VII_Constitutional_Bodies/41_Election_Commission.md).)*

### The 2019 change
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **Term and salary moved from the STATUTE to CENTRAL GOVERNMENT RULES.** ⭐ **Powers untouched; independence weakened.**

### The one that binds
> ⭐⭐ **The CIC ORDERS. Everyone else in Part VIII RECOMMENDS.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "Recent amendments to the Right to Information Act will have a profound impact on the autonomy and independence of the Information Commission." Discuss. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2020 Q2](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2020.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **What the original design protected** — a **statutory** 5-year term and **salary parity with the CEC**, both fixed by **Parliament**. (ii) ⭐ **What the 2019 amendment did** — moved **term, salary and service conditions** to **rules made by the Central Government**, for both the CIC and the **State** Information Commissions. (iii) ⚠️ ⭐ **Why that affects independence** — the Commission adjudicates **against** the government; **security of tenure and unalterable pay** are the classic devices for insulating an adjudicator *(compare the ⭐ **CAG**, whose salary is fixed by Parliament and cannot be varied to his disadvantage, and whose expenditure is **charged**)*; a **shorter, executive-set term** creates anticipatory caution. (iv) ⭐ **The federal objection** — the **Centre** now prescribes conditions for commissioners appointed by a **Governor** on a **Chief Minister's** committee. (v) ⚠️ **The counter-argument** — the CIC is statutory, not constitutional, and parity with a constitutional authority was anomalous; the **powers are untouched**. (vi) ⭐ **Add the practical picture** — vacancies and pendency do more damage than the amendment, and ⭐ **Section 4 proactive disclosure remains the real unimplemented reform.** **Conclude:** the amendment does not weaken the Act's **teeth**; it weakens the **spine of the people who must use them** — and the fix is a statutory term, protected salary, and time-bound filling of vacancies.

**2. "Comment on the significance of the Preamble contained in the Right to Information Act." (150 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2012 Q3(e)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2012.md).*
> *Skeleton:* ⭐ **The Preamble frames RTI not as a favour but as a necessary condition of democracy** — it recites that **democracy requires an informed citizenry and transparency of information**, which are **vital to the functioning of the government and to containing corruption**, and that **revelation of information in actual practice is likely to conflict with other public interests including the efficient operation of governments, optimum use of limited fiscal resources, and the preservation of confidentiality of sensitive information.** ⭐ **Its significance:** (i) it makes RTI a **derivative of Article 19(1)(a)**, not a statutory concession; (ii) ⭐ **it is the interpretive key to Section 8** — the Act itself acknowledges the competing interests, so exemptions are to be read as **narrow carve-outs from a general rule of disclosure**; (iii) it grounds the ⭐ **Section 8(2) public-interest override**; (iv) it links transparency to **accountability and anti-corruption**, which is how courts have used it.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **The right flows from ARTICLE 19(1)(a); the RTI Act, 2005 supplies the machinery.** ⭐ **"Public authority" includes bodies substantially financed by government.**
- ⭐ **30 days · 48 hours where life or liberty is involved · first appeal to a senior officer · SECOND APPEAL to the Information Commission.**
- ⭐ **Section 4 — 17 categories of proactive disclosure** *(the most ignored provision)*. ⭐ **Section 8 — exemptions** *(cabinet papers disclosable once the decision is complete)*. ⭐⭐ **Section 8(2) — public-interest override, notwithstanding the OFFICIAL SECRETS ACT, 1923.** ⭐ **Section 24 — intelligence and security bodies exempt EXCEPT on corruption and human-rights violations.**
- ⭐ **CIC = Chief Information Commissioner + up to TEN Information Commissioners**, appointed by the ⭐ **PRESIDENT** on a committee of the ⭐ **PM, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM.**
- ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **The RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 removed the statutory 5-year term and the CEC salary parity — term and salary are now PRESCRIBED BY THE CENTRAL GOVERNMENT**, for the **State** Commissions too.
- ⭐ **Removal by the PRESIDENT on proved misbehaviour or incapacity, after a SUPREME COURT inquiry**; also for insolvency, moral-turpitude conviction, paid employment, infirmity or a conflicting interest. ⚠️ **No reappointment.**
- ⭐⭐ **Powers: civil-court powers · may examine ANY record, and NONE may be withheld from it · BINDING orders to disclose · ⭐ a PERSONAL PENALTY on the PIO of ₹250 per day up to ₹25,000 · compensation to the complainant · recommendation of disciplinary action.**
- ⚠️ **Problems:** vacancies and pendency · Section 4 unimplemented · un-operational whistle-blower protection · ⚠️ **the 2023 broadening of the Section 8(1)(j) personal-information exemption.**
