# Chapter 38 — Municipalities

[← Chapter 37](37_Panchayati_Raj.md) · [Part Index](00_INDEX.md) · [Master](../00_INDEX.md)

**Read time ~12 min** · **Part IX-A, Articles 243P–243ZG, Twelfth Schedule** · **The last chapter of Part V** · 🟠 Prelims: recurring · Mains: **2013, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024**

> 📌 **Structurally this is [Ch 37](37_Panchayati_Raj.md) transposed to towns — same tenure, same age, same one-third reservation, the same State Election Commission and State Finance Commission.** ⭐ **Read only the differences**, which are concentrated in **four places**: the three *types* of municipality, the **Wards Committee**, and the **two planning committees (DPC and MPC)** that have no rural counterpart.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The 74th Amendment gave cities a constitutional local government just as urbanisation was making them the most important units of Indian administration — and then left them the weakest.** ⭐ **Urban local bodies raise a smaller share of their own revenue than panchayats' rural counterparts in most comparable countries, and the functions the Twelfth Schedule lists are largely performed by parastatals — development authorities, water boards and transport corporations that the elected mayor does not control.**

---

## 📖 The story

Urban local government in India is **older than the Republic** — the **Madras Municipal Corporation of 1687** is the oldest municipal body in the country, and **Lord Ripon's Resolution of 1882**, the ⭐ **"Magna Carta of local self-government"**, established elected local bodies with elected non-official majorities.

**So why did cities need a constitutional amendment in 1992?** For the same reason villages did: state governments **superseded municipal bodies and did not hold elections**, sometimes for years, because nothing obliged them to.

The 74th Amendment fixed that. But it created a peculiar situation.

> 💡 **The analogy:** imagine a city where the **elected council** is constitutionally guaranteed — but the **water supply** is run by a state water board, **housing and land** by a development authority, **buses** by a state transport corporation, and **electricity** by a state utility. The council is real, its elections are guaranteed, its mayor is elected — and almost nothing a resident complains about is actually within its control.
>
> ⭐ **That is the substance of [Mains 2023 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md):** *"The states in India seem reluctant to empower urban local bodies both functionally as well as financially."*

**And the stakes keep rising.** India's urban population is heading past 40%, cities generate the majority of GDP, and the institutions responsible for them are the least empowered tier of Indian government.

## PART A — Evolution *(brief — know the four landmarks)*

| Year | Landmark |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1687** | **Madras Municipal Corporation** — the **first** municipal corporation in India |
| **1726** | Municipal corporations set up in **Bombay and Calcutta** |
| **1870** | ⭐ **Lord Mayo's Resolution** — financial decentralisation, the beginnings of local self-government |
| ⭐ **1882** | ⭐ **Lord Ripon's Resolution** — the ⭐ **"Magna Carta of local self-government"**; elected non-official majorities and elected chairpersons |
| **1907** | **Royal Commission on Decentralisation** *(chaired by Hobhouse)* |
| **1919** | **Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms** — local self-government became a ⭐ **transferred subject** under dyarchy in the provinces |
| **1935** | **Government of India Act** — provincial autonomy; local self-government a **provincial subject** |
| ⭐ **1992** | ⭐ **74th Amendment Act** — in force ⭐ **1 June 1993** |

## PART B — ⭐⭐ What is different from panchayats

### ⭐ ① Three TYPES, not three tiers — Article 243Q

⚠️ **This is the biggest structural difference.** Panchayats have **three tiers in a hierarchy**; municipalities have **three types for different sizes of settlement**, and they do not sit above one another.

| Type | For |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Nagar Panchayat** | ⭐ A **TRANSITIONAL AREA** — an area **in transition from a rural area to an urban area** |
| ⭐ **Municipal Council** | A **smaller urban area** |
| ⭐ **Municipal Corporation** | A **larger urban area** |

⭐ **The Governor specifies** which area falls into which category, having regard to **population, density, revenue generated for local administration, percentage of employment in non-agricultural activities, economic importance** and other factors.

> ⚠️ ⭐ **The exception that is asked:** a **municipality need NOT be constituted** in an urban area which the **Governor specifies as an INDUSTRIAL TOWNSHIP** — where municipal services are being provided or proposed to be provided by an **industrial establishment**.

### ⭐ ② Composition — Article 243R

- ⭐ **All seats are filled by DIRECT election**, and the municipal area is divided into **wards**.
- ⭐ The **state legislature may provide** for the representation of:
  - ⭐ Persons with **special knowledge or experience in municipal administration** — ⚠️ ⭐ **WITHOUT the right to vote**
  - **MPs, MLAs and MLCs** representing constituencies comprising the municipal area
  - The **chairpersons of Wards Committees**
- The manner of election of the **chairperson** is left to the **state legislature**.

> ⭐ **The "without the right to vote" clause is a favourite Prelims item** — experts may sit and speak but **cannot vote**. Panchayats have no equivalent provision.

### ⭐ ③ Wards Committees — Article 243S

> ⭐ **Constituted within the territorial area of a municipality having a population of THREE LAKH OR MORE**, consisting of one or more wards.

⭐ The **state legislature** decides their composition, territorial area, and the manner of filling seats. If a committee covers **one ward**, the member representing that ward is its **chairperson**; if it covers **two or more wards**, one of those members **elected by them** is the chairperson.

> ⭐ **Purpose:** to bring decision-making closer to the resident in large cities — the urban analogue of the **Gram Sabha**, though ⚠️ **much weaker**, since the Gram Sabha consists of **all voters** while a Wards Committee consists of **elected representatives**.

### ⭐⭐ ④ The two planning committees — the real distinctives

**These have no rural counterpart and are heavily examined.**

| | ⭐ **District Planning Committee (DPC)** | ⭐ **Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Article** | ⭐ **243ZD** | ⭐ **243ZE** |
| **Where** | ⭐ **In EVERY STATE, at the DISTRICT level** | ⭐ In **every METROPOLITAN AREA** — ⭐ population **10 LAKH OR MORE** |
| **Purpose** | ⭐ To **consolidate the plans prepared by the PANCHAYATS AND THE MUNICIPALITIES** in the district and prepare a ⭐ **draft development plan for the DISTRICT AS A WHOLE** | ⭐ To prepare a **draft development plan for the METROPOLITAN AREA as a whole** |
| ⭐ **Elected proportion** | ⭐ **FOUR-FIFTHS** elected by and from among the elected members of the **district panchayat and the municipalities** in the district | ⭐ **TWO-THIRDS** elected by and from among the elected members of the **municipalities and chairpersons of the panchayats** in the metropolitan area |
| **Basis of proportion** | ⭐ In proportion to the ratio of **RURAL to URBAN population** in the district | ⭐ In proportion to the ratio of the **population** of the municipalities and panchayats |
| **Who forwards the plan** | ⭐ The **Chairperson** forwards it to the **state government** | The **Chairperson** forwards it to the **state government** |

> 🧠 **The two fractions are the exam point: DPC = four-fifths; MPC = two-thirds.** And the two triggers: **DPC in every district; MPC where population is 10 lakh or more.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **In practice both are among the least implemented provisions of the Constitution** — many states have not constituted them at all, or constitute them without giving them any role in actual planning, which continues to be done by **development authorities**. **This is the concrete evidence for any answer on urban governance failure**, and it directly serves [Mains 2019 Q16](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md) on multi-level planning.

## PART C — What is the SAME as panchayats

*Do not re-learn these — just note that they carry over.*

| Element | Article | Rule *(identical to Part IX)* |
|---|---|---|
| **Reservation** | **243T** | SC/ST **in proportion to population**; ⭐ **not less than one-third of SC/ST seats for SC/ST women**; ⭐ **not less than one-third of total seats for women**; backward classes at the **state's discretion**; chairperson offices as the state provides |
| **Duration** | **243U** | **Five years**; fresh elections **before expiry** or **within six months** of dissolution; a body constituted on dissolution serves only the **remainder** |
| ⭐ **Disqualification** | **243V** | ⭐ **Minimum age 21** |
| **Powers** | **243W** | The state legislature **may** endow municipalities with powers to function as **institutions of self-government**, in relation to the ⭐ **18 matters in the TWELFTH SCHEDULE** |
| **Finances** | **243X** | The state legislature **may** authorise taxes, duties, tolls and fees; assign taxes; provide grants-in-aid from the **Consolidated Fund of the State** |
| ⭐ **Finance Commission** | **243Y** | ⭐ **The SAME State Finance Commission** constituted under **Article 243-I** reviews municipal finances too |
| ⭐ **Election Commission** | **243ZA** | ⭐ **The SAME State Election Commission** conducts municipal elections |
| **Audit** | **243Z** | As the state legislature provides |
| **Bar to courts** | **243ZG** | Delimitation and seat allotment **not questionable**; elections only by **election petition** |

> ⭐ **The compulsory/voluntary split from [Ch 37](37_Panchayati_Raj.md) applies identically here** — **existence, elections, tenure and reservations are mandatory; functions, funds and functionaries are discretionary.** ⭐ **That is why [Mains 2023 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md) can ask about "functional as well as financial" reluctance in one breath.**

## PART D — ⭐ The eight types of urban local body

*Municipalities under Part IX-A are only one of these. The others are statutory or executive creations, and the distinction is examinable.*

| Body | For |
|---|---|
| **Municipal Corporation** | The **largest** cities |
| **Municipality** *(Municipal Council/Board/Committee)* | **Smaller** towns |
| **Notified Area Committee** | ⭐ A **fast-developing town** or one that does **not fulfil the conditions** for a municipality but is still considered important — ⚠️ ⭐ created by a **notification in the government gazette**, and it is ⭐ **entirely NOMINATED**, so it is **neither an elected body nor a statutory body** |
| **Town Area Committee** | ⭐ A **small town** — a semi-municipal authority with limited functions *(drainage, roads, street lighting, conservancy)*; created by a separate **Act of the state legislature**; may be elected, nominated or both |
| ⭐ **Cantonment Board** | ⭐ Set up for a **civilian population living in a cantonment area**. ⚠️ ⭐ **Created by a CENTRAL Act — the Cantonments Act, 2006 — and administered by the MINISTRY OF DEFENCE.** ⭐ The **military officer commanding the station is the ex-officio PRESIDENT** of the Board; the ⭐ **executive officer is appointed by the PRESIDENT OF INDIA**. Elected members hold office for **five years** |
| **Township** | Established by a **public enterprise** to provide civic amenities to its staff in a township near the plant — ⚠️ ⭐ **no elected members; it is an extension of the bureaucratic structure** |
| **Port Trust** | Established in **port areas** by an Act of Parliament; manages the port and provides civic amenities; has **both elected and nominated members** |
| **Special Purpose Agency** | Single-purpose or multi-purpose statutory or departmental bodies — **town improvement trusts, urban development authorities, water supply and sewerage boards, housing boards, pollution control boards, electricity supply boards, city transport boards** |

> ⭐⭐ **The Special Purpose Agencies are the analytical point, not just a list.** They are ⭐ **"functional local bodies" rather than "area-based local bodies"** — they take **specific functions away** from the municipality across the whole area. ⚠️ **They are the single biggest structural reason urban local bodies are weak**, and naming them is what makes a Mains answer concrete rather than generic.
>
> ⭐ **The Cantonment Board facts are a reliable Prelims item:** **central Act · Ministry of Defence · station commander is ex-officio President · executive officer appointed by the President of India.**

## PART E — ⚠️ Why urban local bodies are weak, and the reforms

### ⭐ Functionally

1. ⭐ **Article 243W says "may."** Most states have **not devolved all 18 Twelfth Schedule functions**; ⚠️ **urban planning, water supply, fire services and land use** — the most important — are frequently retained by **parastatals**.
2. ⭐ **Parallel bodies** — **development authorities**, water and sewerage boards, transport corporations and housing boards report to the **state government**, not the elected council.
3. ⭐ **DPCs and MPCs are largely unconstituted or non-functional**, so the constitutionally mandated **integrated planning never happens**.
4. ⭐ **The Mayor is usually weak** — in most states the mayor has a **one-year term** and is **indirectly elected**, while the **Municipal Commissioner**, a state-appointed IAS officer, holds real executive power.

### ⭐ Financially

1. ⭐ **Own-source revenue is low** — property tax collection efficiency is poor, valuations are outdated, and exemptions are wide.
2. ⚠️ **Octroi and entry taxes were subsumed into GST** *(101st Amendment — see [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md))*, removing a major municipal revenue source with **compensation arrangements that vary by state**.
3. ⚠️ **State Finance Commissions** are constituted late and their **recommendations frequently not implemented**.
4. **Grants are tied** to schemes *(AMRUT, Smart Cities, Swachh Bharat)*, leaving little untied money.
5. **Municipal borrowing and municipal bonds** remain marginal, because weak accounts and credit ratings limit market access.

### ⭐ The reforms to propose

- ⭐ **Directly elected Mayor with a five-year term** and executive authority, on the model recommended by the **Second ARC**.
- ⭐ **Constitute and empower DPCs and MPCs**, and route parastatal plans through them.
- ⭐ **Activity mapping** for all **18 Twelfth Schedule** functions, with matching funds and functionaries.
- **Property tax reform** — GIS-based mapping, periodic revaluation, fewer exemptions.
- ⭐ **Accrual-based double-entry accounting** and credit ratings to open the **municipal bond** market.
- **Timely State Finance Commissions**, with a statutory obligation to act on their reports.
- ⭐ **Wind up or subordinate parastatals** to the elected body.

## 🧠 Memory hooks

### The amendment pair
> ⭐ **73rd → Part IX → Articles 243–243-O → Eleventh Schedule → 29 items → in force 24 April 1993.**
> ⭐ **74th → Part IX-A → Articles 243P–243ZG → Twelfth Schedule → 18 items → in force 1 June 1993.**

### The three types
> ⭐ **Nagar Panchayat = TRANSITIONAL area · Municipal Council = SMALLER urban area · Municipal Corporation = LARGER urban area.** ⚠️ ⭐ **No municipality need be constituted in an INDUSTRIAL TOWNSHIP.**

### The two thresholds
> ⭐ **Wards Committee — population 3 LAKH or more.** ⭐ **Metropolitan Planning Committee — population 10 LAKH or more.**

### The two fractions
> ⭐ **DPC = FOUR-FIFTHS elected · MPC = TWO-THIRDS elected.**

### The "no vote" clause
> ⭐ Persons with **special knowledge of municipal administration** may be given representation ⚠️ **WITHOUT the right to vote**. *(No panchayat equivalent.)*

### The Cantonment Board
> ⭐ **Central Act (Cantonments Act, 2006) · Ministry of DEFENCE · the station commander is ex-officio PRESIDENT · the executive officer is appointed by the PRESIDENT OF INDIA.**

### The colonial landmark
> ⭐ **Lord Ripon, 1882 — the "Magna Carta of local self-government."** *(Mayo 1870 was the financial-decentralisation precursor; **Madras 1687** was the first corporation.)*

### The one-line diagnosis
> ⭐ **Area-based local bodies were constitutionalised; function-based parastatals kept the functions.**

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The states in India seem reluctant to empower urban local bodies both functionally as well as financially." Comment. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2023 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2023.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **Frame with the compulsory/voluntary split** — the 74th Amendment made **elections and structure** mandatory and left **Article 243W functions** and **Article 243X finances** to state discretion. (ii) ⭐ **Functional reluctance** — incomplete devolution of the **18 Twelfth Schedule** items; **urban planning, water and land use** retained by **development authorities and water boards**; ⭐ **DPCs and MPCs largely unconstituted**; a **weak, indirectly elected, one-year Mayor** against a **state-appointed Municipal Commissioner**. (iii) ⭐ **Financial reluctance** — low property tax efficiency; **octroi subsumed into GST**; **State Finance Commission** reports ignored; **tied scheme grants**; negligible municipal bond issuance. (iv) **Why states resist** — cities are the **revenue base** and the site of **land value**, and empowering a rival elected authority in the state's largest cities is politically costly. (v) ⭐ **Reforms** — directly elected Mayor with a five-year term, activity mapping, empowered DPCs/MPCs, property tax reform, accrual accounting and credit ratings. Conclude: ⭐ *"The 74th Amendment created city governments without transferring city government."*

**2. "Analyse the role of local bodies in providing good governance at local level and bring out the pros and cons of merging the rural local bodies with the urban local bodies." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2024 Q5](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2024.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **Role in good governance** — proximity, participation, responsiveness, local knowledge, and the **Eleventh/Twelfth Schedule** functions. (ii) ⭐ **Pros of merging** — India's **peri-urban** areas fit neither category; **census towns** remain governed as villages though they are functionally urban; a single body would end jurisdictional gaps in **water, waste and transport**, allow **integrated planning** *(which is what **DPCs** were meant to achieve)*, and pool scarce technical capacity. (iii) ⚠️ **Cons** — rural and urban functions genuinely differ *(agriculture and common lands vs sewerage and building bylaws)*; ⭐ the **Gram Sabha's direct democracy has no urban equivalent** and would be lost; **PESA and Fifth Schedule protections** could be diluted; **urban dominance** over rural representation in a merged body; and the Constitution itself provides **two separate Parts**. (iv) ⭐ **The middle path** — strengthen the **DPC** as the integrating institution rather than merging the bodies, and create a **statutory category for census towns**. Conclude on that.

**3. "In the context of neo-liberal paradigm of developmental planning, multi-level planning is expected to make operations cost-effective and remove many implementation blockages." Discuss. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2019 Q16](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md).*
> *Skeleton:* **The constitutional design for multi-level planning** — Article **243G** and **243W** *(plans for economic development and social justice)*, ⭐ **Article 243ZD (DPC)** consolidating rural and urban plans, and ⭐ **Article 243ZE (MPC)** for metropolitan areas. **How it should work** — bottom-up plans aggregated at district level, then state, then national. ⚠️ **Why it does not** — **DPCs and MPCs unconstituted or ornamental**; planning captured by **parastatals**; **tied scheme funds** removing local discretion; weak local **capacity**; and the abolition of the Planning Commission leaving the vertical chain unclear. **Reforms** — empower DPCs, untie funds, build local planning capacity.

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **74th Amendment Act, 1992 — in force 1 June 1993. Part IX-A, Articles 243P–243ZG, Twelfth Schedule with 18 items.**
- **History:** ⭐ **Madras 1687** *(first corporation)* · **Mayo 1870** · ⭐ **Ripon 1882 — "Magna Carta of local self-government"** · Royal Commission on Decentralisation 1907 · **Montagu–Chelmsford 1919** *(transferred subject)* · GoI Act 1935.
- ⭐ **Three TYPES (243Q): Nagar Panchayat (transitional area) · Municipal Council (smaller urban area) · Municipal Corporation (larger urban area)**, specified by the **Governor**. ⚠️ ⭐ **No municipality need be constituted in an INDUSTRIAL TOWNSHIP.**
- ⭐ **Composition (243R):** all seats by **direct election**; the state may add **MPs/MLAs/MLCs**, **Wards Committee chairpersons**, and ⭐ **persons with special knowledge of municipal administration — WITHOUT the right to vote**.
- ⭐ **Wards Committees (243S): population 3 LAKH or more.**
- ⭐⭐ **DPC (243ZD): every district · consolidates PANCHAYAT and MUNICIPALITY plans · FOUR-FIFTHS elected · proportion = rural : urban population.**
- ⭐⭐ **MPC (243ZE): metropolitan area of 10 LAKH or more · TWO-THIRDS elected.** ⚠️ **Both are largely unconstituted in practice.**
- **Same as panchayats:** reservation *(SC/ST by population; **one-third for women**)* · **5-year** duration with the **6-month** rule · ⭐ **age 21** · **243W powers ("may", 18 items)** · **243X finances** · ⭐ **the SAME State Finance Commission (243Y)** and ⭐ **the SAME State Election Commission (243ZA)** · **243ZG** bar to courts.
- ⭐ **Eight urban body types:** Corporation · Municipality · **Notified Area Committee** *(gazette notification, **wholly nominated**)* · **Town Area Committee** *(separate state Act)* · ⭐ **Cantonment Board** *(**central** Cantonments Act 2006, **Ministry of Defence**, station commander **ex-officio President**, executive officer appointed by the **President of India**)* · **Township** *(no elected members)* · **Port Trust** *(Act of Parliament)* · ⭐ **Special Purpose Agencies** *(development authorities, water boards, housing boards — "**functional**" rather than "**area-based**" bodies)*.
- ⚠️ **Why weak — functionally:** *"may"* in 243W · **parastatals hold the key functions** · **DPCs/MPCs unconstituted** · ⭐ **weak one-year, indirectly elected Mayor vs a state-appointed Municipal Commissioner**.
- ⚠️ **Why weak — financially:** poor **property tax** efficiency · ⭐ **octroi subsumed into GST** · SFC reports ignored · **tied** scheme grants · negligible **municipal bonds**.
- ⭐ **Reforms:** directly elected **Mayor with a 5-year term** *(Second ARC)* · **activity mapping** for all 18 functions · **empower DPCs and MPCs** · property tax reform · **accrual accounting and credit ratings** · subordinate the parastatals.
