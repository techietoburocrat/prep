# Chapter 37 — Panchayati Raj

**Read time ~18 min** · **Part IX, Articles 243–243-O, Eleventh Schedule** · 🔴 Prelims: **2013, 2016, 2025, 2026** · Mains: **2005, 2008, 2013, 2015 (×2), 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2024**

> 📌 **This chapter has produced more Mains questions than almost any other in the book**, and they are all variations on one question: **the system exists constitutionally and does not work practically — why?** ⭐ **The answer is in Part B: the compulsory/voluntary split.** Get that section right and you can answer any of them.

---

## 🎯 The one-line point

> **The 73rd Amendment made panchayats permanent but not powerful.** It guaranteed their **existence, elections, tenure and reservations** — all compulsory. It left their **functions, funds and functionaries** to the discretion of each state legislature — all voluntary. ⭐ **India constitutionalised the *body* and left the *power* to be given away later. Most states never gave it.**

---

## 📖 The story

Article 40 — a **Directive Principle** — asked the state to organise village panchayats as **units of self-government**. Non-justiciable, and for four decades largely ignored.

Several committees tried. **Balwant Rai Mehta (1957)** produced the three-tier design, and Nehru inaugurated it at **Nagaur, Rajasthan, on 2 October 1959**. It flourished briefly and then withered — because everything depended on state governments that had **no obligation** to sustain it. Elections were postponed indefinitely; bodies were superseded and not reconstituted.

**The 73rd Amendment (1992) was designed to fix exactly that failure.** It made the things states had been neglecting **mandatory**: elections must be held, tenure is five years, a body dissolved early must be reconstituted within six months, an independent **State Election Commission** runs the polls.

> 💡 **The analogy:** the Amendment is like a law guaranteeing that a company **must hold shareholder meetings on schedule, with an independent registrar, and cannot be wound up at the board's convenience.** All genuinely valuable — the meetings now happen.
>
> ⭐ **But it did not transfer any assets to the shareholders.** What the meeting can actually *decide* was left to the board — the state legislature — to determine later, and mostly it declined.
>
> **That gap between guaranteed procedure and discretionary substance is the entire examinable content of this chapter.**

**The scale of what did work should not be understated.** There are roughly **2.6 lakh panchayats** and over **31 lakh elected representatives**, of whom ⭐ **more than 14 lakh are women** — the largest experiment in representative local democracy anywhere. **The failure is one of power, not of participation.**

## PART A — Evolution: the committees ⭐

*Learn these as a chain, each fixing the previous one's diagnosis.*

| Committee | Year | ⭐ The recommendation that matters |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Balwant Rai Mehta** | **1957** | ⭐ **"Democratic decentralisation"** — the ⭐ **THREE-TIER** system: **Gram Panchayat (village) · Panchayat Samiti (block) · Zila Parishad (district)**, organically linked by **indirect elections** at the higher levels. ⭐ **Rajasthan implemented it first — inaugurated at Nagaur on 2 October 1959**; Andhra Pradesh followed |
| ⭐ **Ashok Mehta** | **1977** | ⭐ **TWO-TIER — Zila Parishad (district) and Mandal Panchayat** (a group of villages, 15,000–20,000 population). ⭐ **The DISTRICT should be the first point of decentralisation.** Also: ⭐ **open participation by political parties**; compulsory powers of taxation; **social audit**; reservation for SCs/STs by population; ⭐ **a minister for panchayati raj** in the state council of ministers |
| **G.V.K. Rao** | **1985** | Found the bureaucratisation of development had made panchayats ⭐ **"grass without roots."** The **district** level is the most important; a **District Development Commissioner**; regular elections |
| ⭐ **L.M. Singhvi** | **1986** | ⭐ **The FIRST to recommend CONSTITUTIONAL recognition** of panchayati raj. Also **Nyaya Panchayats**, the village as a **distinct unit**, and the ⭐ **Gram Sabha as the embodiment of direct democracy** |
| **Thungon** | **1988** | Constitutional recognition; **three-tier** with the **Zila Parishad as the pivot**; a **five-year tenure**; the **District Collector as chief executive officer** of the Zila Parishad |
| **Gadgil** | **1988** | Constitutional status; five-year term; reservations |

**The legislative attempts:**
- ⚠️ **64th Amendment Bill, 1989** *(Rajiv Gandhi)* — **passed the Lok Sabha but failed in the Rajya Sabha**.
- ⚠️ **74th Amendment Bill, 1990** *(V.P. Singh government)* — **lapsed** with the dissolution of the Lok Sabha.
- ⭐ **73rd Amendment Act, 1992** — enacted, and **came into force on 24 April 1993** *(now observed as **National Panchayati Raj Day**)*.

> 🧠 **The chain in one line:** ⭐ **Mehta gave the structure (1957) · Ashok Mehta gave the district focus and politics (1977) · Singhvi gave the demand for constitutional status (1986) · the 73rd Amendment delivered it (1992).**

## PART B — ⭐⭐ Compulsory vs voluntary: the heart of the chapter

**This table is the answer to most of the fourteen Mains questions. Reproduce it from memory.**

| ⭐ **COMPULSORY** *(the state must)* | ⭐ **VOLUNTARY** *(the state may)* |
|---|---|
| Organisation of a **Gram Sabha** in every village | Giving representation to **MPs, MLAs and MLCs** in panchayats |
| Establishment of panchayats at the **village, intermediate and district** levels | Providing **reservation for backward classes** |
| ⭐ **Direct elections** to all seats at all three levels | ⭐ **Granting powers and authority to function as institutions of SELF-GOVERNMENT** |
| **Indirect election** of chairpersons at the intermediate and district levels | ⭐ **Devolution of powers to prepare plans for economic development and social justice, and to implement schemes** |
| ⭐ **21 years** as the minimum age to contest | ⭐ **Granting financial powers** — to levy, collect and appropriate **taxes, duties, tolls and fees** |
| ⭐ **Reservation for SCs and STs** at all three levels | — |
| ⭐ **Reservation of one-third of seats for WOMEN** | — |
| **Five-year tenure**, and fresh elections **within six months** of supersession | — |
| ⭐ Establishment of a **State Election Commission** | — |
| ⭐ Establishment of a **State Finance Commission every five years** | — |

> ⭐⭐ **Read the two columns against each other and the diagnosis writes itself.**
> **The left column is entirely about EXISTENCE — who is elected, when, for how long, with what reservations.**
> **The right column is entirely about POWER — what they may actually do, and with whose money.**
>
> ⭐ **The Constitution made the body compulsory and the power optional.** That is the sentence to open any answer on why panchayati raj underperforms.

## PART C — The salient features, article by article ⭐

### ⭐ Gram Sabha — Article 243A

> A body consisting of **all persons registered in the electoral rolls of a village** comprised within the area of a panchayat.

⚠️ ⭐ **Its powers and functions are determined by the STATE LEGISLATURE** — the Constitution creates it and says nothing about what it can do. *(Singhvi had called it "the embodiment of direct democracy"; the Amendment left its content to the states.)*

### ⭐ Three-tier system — Article 243B

Panchayats at the **village, intermediate and district** levels.

> ⭐⭐ **The exception that is asked repeatedly:** ⚠️ **a state having a population NOT EXCEEDING TWENTY LAKH may choose NOT to constitute panchayats at the INTERMEDIATE level.**
>
> *This was one of three incorrect statements in [Prelims 2025 Q13](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2025.md) — the statement claimed intermediate panchayats exist "in all States."*

### ⭐ Election of members and chairpersons — Article 243C

- ⭐ **All seats at all three levels are filled by DIRECT election** from territorial constituencies.
- The chairperson at the **village** level is elected as the **state legislature determines**.
- ⭐ The chairperson at the **intermediate and district** levels is elected **by and from among the elected members** — i.e. **indirectly**.
- The state legislature **may** provide for the representation of **MPs, MLAs and MLCs** in panchayats *(voluntary)*.

### ⭐⭐ Reservation of seats — Article 243D

| Reservation | Rule |
|---|---|
| **SC and ST** | In **proportion to their population** in the panchayat area; ⭐ allotted **by rotation** to different constituencies |
| ⭐ **SC/ST women** | ⭐ **Not less than ONE-THIRD of the seats reserved for SCs and STs** shall be reserved for **SC/ST women** |
| ⭐ **Women overall** | ⭐ **Not less than ONE-THIRD of the TOTAL number of seats** — ⚠️ **including the seats reserved for SC/ST women** — shall be reserved for women, by rotation |
| **Chairperson offices** | Reserved for SCs, STs and women **in the manner the state legislature provides** |
| ⚠️ **Backward classes** | ⭐ The state legislature **may** provide for it — **voluntary**, and many states have |

> ⭐ **The phrasing that matters: "NOT LESS THAN one-third."** It is a **floor, not a ceiling** — which is why over **twenty states have legislated 50% reservation for women** in panchayats, entirely constitutionally.

### ⭐ Duration — Article 243E

- **Five years** from the date of its first meeting.
- May be **dissolved earlier** in accordance with state law.
- ⭐ **Fresh elections must be completed:** *(a)* **before the expiry of the five-year term**; or *(b)* ⭐ **before the expiration of SIX MONTHS from the date of dissolution**.
- ⚠️ ⭐ **A panchayat constituted upon dissolution continues only for the REMAINDER of the period** the dissolved panchayat would have continued — it does **not** get a fresh five years.
- ⚠️ ⭐ **No election is necessary if the remainder of the period is less than six months.**

> ⭐ **This four-part rule is a Prelims favourite** — it was tested in [Prelims 2016 Q1](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2016.md) on panchayat membership and dissolution.

### ⭐ Disqualifications — Article 243F

As provided by the law of the state legislature, or under any law relating to elections to the state legislature. ⭐ **But the minimum age to contest is TWENTY-ONE**, ⚠️ **not 25** — and a person **shall not be disqualified on the ground that he is less than 25 years of age if he has attained 21**.

> ⭐ **[Prelims 2025 Q13](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2025.md) tested this too** — the statement claiming a minimum age of **thirty** was incorrect.

### ⭐ Powers and authority — Article 243G

The **state legislature MAY** endow panchayats with such powers and authority as may be necessary to enable them to **function as institutions of self-government** — including:
- The preparation of **plans for economic development and social justice**
- The **implementation of schemes** for economic development and social justice
- In relation to the **29 matters listed in the ELEVENTH SCHEDULE**

⚠️ ⭐ **Note the word "may."** This is the **voluntary** limb, and it is where devolution fails.

### ⭐ Finances — Articles 243H and 243-I

**Article 243H** — the state legislature **may**:
- Authorise a panchayat to **levy, collect and appropriate** taxes, duties, tolls and fees
- **Assign** to a panchayat taxes collected by the state government
- Provide for **grants-in-aid** from the **Consolidated Fund of the State**
- Provide for the constitution of **funds** for crediting and withdrawing money

⭐⭐ **Article 243-I — the State Finance Commission:**
- ⭐ Constituted by the **GOVERNOR** — ⚠️ **NOT the Chief Minister** — **every five years**
- Reviews the **financial position of the panchayats** and recommends the **distribution of the net proceeds of taxes** between the state and panchayats, the **taxes assignable** to them, and **grants-in-aid**
- ⭐ The Governor places its **recommendations, with an action-taken memorandum, before the STATE LEGISLATURE**
- ⭐ The **Central Finance Commission** *(Article 280)* also suggests measures to **augment the Consolidated Fund of a state to supplement the resources of panchayats** — added by the **73rd Amendment** *(see [Ch 14](../Part_II_System_of_Government/14_Centre_State_Relations.md))*

> ⭐ **The "Governor, not Chief Minister" point was tested directly in [Prelims 2025 Q13](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2025.md)** — one of the three incorrect statements. **Learn it as a trap, not a detail.**

### ⭐ State Election Commission — Article 243K

- ⭐ **Superintendence, direction and control** of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of **all elections to panchayats** is vested in a **State Election Commission**, consisting of a **State Election Commissioner appointed by the GOVERNOR**.
- ⭐ ⚠️ **He shall not be removed from office EXCEPT in the manner and on the grounds prescribed for the removal of a JUDGE OF A HIGH COURT** — and his **conditions of service shall not be varied to his disadvantage** after appointment.

> ⭐ **This is the strongest independence guarantee in the whole chapter**, and it is deliberate: the failure the Amendment was fixing was **states postponing elections indefinitely**. ⚠️ **Note it is the STATE Election Commission — entirely separate from the Election Commission of India.**

### ⚠️ Areas exempted — Article 243M

⭐ **Part IX does NOT apply to:**
1. ⭐ **Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram**
2. The **hill areas of Manipur** for which a **District Council** exists
3. ⭐ The **Scheduled Areas and Tribal Areas** *(Fifth and Sixth Schedules)*
4. **Darjeeling district of West Bengal**, in respect of the areas for which the **Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council** exists *(as to its functions)*

⭐ **But Parliament may extend Part IX to the Scheduled Areas and Tribal Areas, subject to exceptions and modifications** — ⭐ **and it did, through PESA.**

### Bar to interference by courts — Article 243-O

⭐ The validity of any law relating to the **delimitation of constituencies or the allotment of seats** cannot be questioned in any court; and ⭐ **no election to a panchayat shall be questioned except by an ELECTION PETITION** presented as the state legislature provides.

## PART D — ⭐⭐ PESA, 1996

*The **Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996** — asked directly in [Prelims 2013 Q17](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2013.md).*

**What it does:** extends **Part IX** to the ⭐ **Fifth Schedule Scheduled Areas**, with modifications — because a uniform panchayat structure would have overridden tribal self-governance traditions.

⭐ **It applies to ten states:** **Andhra Pradesh · Telangana · Chhattisgarh · Gujarat · Himachal Pradesh · Jharkhand · Madhya Pradesh · Maharashtra · Odisha · Rajasthan.**

### ⭐ The provisions that matter

1. ⭐ **State legislation on panchayats in Scheduled Areas shall be in consonance with CUSTOMARY LAW**, social and religious practices, and traditional management practices of community resources.
2. ⭐ **A village** shall ordinarily consist of a **habitation, a group of habitations or a hamlet** comprising a community managing its affairs **in accordance with traditions and customs**. **Every village shall have a Gram Sabha.**
3. ⭐ **The Gram Sabha shall APPROVE the plans, programmes and projects for social and economic development BEFORE they are taken up for implementation.**
4. ⭐ **The Gram Sabha shall be responsible for the IDENTIFICATION OF BENEFICIARIES** under poverty alleviation and other programmes.
5. ⭐⭐ **Consultation with the Gram Sabha or panchayat is MANDATORY before:** *(a)* **acquiring land** in Scheduled Areas for development projects and resettling or rehabilitating persons affected; and *(b)* **granting a prospecting licence or mining lease for MINOR MINERALS**, and before auctioning minor minerals concessions.
6. **Planning and management of MINOR WATER BODIES** is entrusted to panchayats.
7. ⭐ **The Gram Sabha/panchayat is given powers to:** enforce prohibition or regulate the sale and consumption of **intoxicants**; own **minor forest produce**; ⭐ **prevent alienation of land** and **restore unlawfully alienated land** of a Scheduled Tribe; **manage village markets**; **control money lending** to STs; **control institutions and functionaries** in all social sectors; and **control local plans and resources, including tribal sub-plans**.
8. ⭐ **Reservation of seats** in Scheduled Areas at every panchayat shall be **in proportion to the population**, ⚠️ **but not less than ONE-HALF of the total seats**; and ⭐ **ALL the chairpersons at all levels shall be reserved for Scheduled Tribes.**
9. ⭐ The state government **may nominate** unrepresented Scheduled Tribes, ⚠️ **but such nomination shall not exceed ONE-TENTH of the total members elected**.

> ⭐ **The framing for a Mains answer:** ⚠️ **PESA is India's most far-reaching statutory devolution of power — and among its least implemented.** Most of the ten states have **not amended their subordinate rules** to give effect to it, and the **mandatory consultation on land acquisition and mining** has repeatedly been diluted in practice. Connect this to [Ch 36](../Part_IV_State_Government/36_Special_Provisions.md) and to the **Forest Rights Act, 2006** *(asked in [Prelims 2013 Q3](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2013.md) and [2021 Q8](../../03_PYQ_Prelims/2021.md))*.

## PART E — ⚠️ Why it underperforms, and what would fix it

*This is the substance of nearly every Mains question in this chapter.*

### ⭐ The 3 Fs — and the fourth

| F | The failure |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Functions** | **Article 243G says "may."** Most states have devolved only a few of the **29 Eleventh Schedule** subjects, and often on paper only — without ⭐ **activity mapping** *(specifying which tier does what within a subject)* |
| ⭐ **Funds** | Own-source revenue is negligible; most states have **not given real taxation powers**; transfers are largely **tied** to schemes; ⚠️ **State Finance Commissions are often constituted late, and their reports frequently not acted upon** |
| ⭐ **Functionaries** | Panchayats have **no staff of their own** — they depend on **state line-department officials** who report to their own departments, not to the elected body |
| ⭐ **Functionality** *(the 2020 framing)* | Even where the 3 Fs exist on paper, the **capacity to plan, execute, procure and audit** is missing — [Mains 2020 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2020.md) asked precisely about this shift |

### The other structural problems

1. ⭐ **Parallel bodies** — District Rural Development Agencies, water and sanitation missions, and centrally sponsored scheme committees that **bypass the elected panchayat** entirely.
2. ⭐ **Proxy representation** — the ***"Sarpanch Pati"*** phenomenon, where an elected woman's husband exercises the office. *(Asked in [Mains 2008 Q11(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2008.md) and [2019 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md).)*
3. **Bureaucratic dominance** — the Block Development Officer and District Collector often outrank the elected chairperson in practice.
4. **Irregular elections** in some states, and prolonged supersession.
5. ⚠️ **Rotation of reserved seats** disrupts continuity — a representative who builds capacity in one term may be unable to contest the next from that seat.
6. **Elite capture** and low Gram Sabha attendance.

### ⭐ The reforms to propose

- ⭐ **Activity mapping** for each of the 29 subjects, specifying **which tier does what**, with corresponding **funds and functionaries**.
- ⭐ **Untied funds** as the default; **timely constitution of State Finance Commissions** and a requirement to **act on their reports**.
- ⭐ **Own staff** for panchayats, or at least **dual reporting** of line-department officials to the elected body.
- **Capacity building** — training, digital accounting *(eGramSwaraj)*, and **social audit** as a statutory requirement.
- ⭐ **Winding up parallel bodies** and routing scheme funds through panchayats.
- **Longer or staggered rotation** of reserved seats to preserve continuity.
- ⭐ **Full implementation of PESA**, including subordinate rules in all ten states.

## ❓ Expected Mains questions

**1. "The local self-government system in India has not proved to be an effective instrument of governance." Critically examine and give your views to improve the situation. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2017 Q1](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2017.md); the same charge in [2015 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2015.md) and [2022 Q3](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2022.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) ⭐ **Open with the compulsory/voluntary split** — the Constitution guaranteed **existence** and left **power** to state discretion. (ii) **What has worked** — regular elections under an independent SEC; **2.6 lakh panchayats, 31 lakh representatives, over 14 lakh women**; a genuine transformation in **who** holds office. (iii) **What has not** — the **3 Fs**, parallel bodies, bureaucratic dominance, **proxy representation**, State Finance Commission reports ignored, PESA unimplemented. (iv) ⭐ **Reforms** — activity mapping, untied funds, own functionaries, statutory social audit, winding up parallel agencies, full PESA implementation. (v) Conclude: ⭐ *"The 73rd Amendment democratised local **representation** without decentralising local **power**. The remaining task is not another amendment but the exercise of the discretion Article 243G already grants."*

**2. "The strength and sustenance of local institutions in India has shifted from their formative phase of 'functions, functionaries and funds' to the contemporary stage of 'functionality'. Highlight the critical challenges faced by local institutions in terms of their functionality in recent times." (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2020 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2020.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **Acknowledge the shift** — the 3 Fs framed the debate for two decades; the question is now whether devolved powers are **usable**. (ii) ⭐ **Functionality challenges** — **capacity** *(planning, procurement, accounting)*; **data and digital divide** despite eGramSwaraj; **staff vacancies**; **tied funds** leaving no discretionary space; **audit and social audit** weakness; **parallel bodies** fragmenting authority; **elite capture** and low Gram Sabha attendance; **proxy representation**. (iii) **What functionality would require** — trained cadres, untied resources, real-time accounting, **statutory social audit**, and outcome-based rather than input-based schemes. (iv) Conclude: ⭐ *"Devolution on paper produced representation; functionality requires capability — and capability, unlike structure, cannot be legislated in a single amendment."*

**3. "The reservation of seats for women in the institutions of local self-government has had a limited impact on the patriarchal character of the Indian political process." Comment. (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2019 Q13](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2019.md); the earlier version in [2008 Q11(a)](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2008.md).*
> *Skeleton:* (i) **The scale of the change** — **not less than one-third** mandated; over **twenty states legislated 50%**; ⭐ **more than 14 lakh elected women representatives**. (ii) **The evidence of real impact** — studies showing shifts in **public-goods priorities** *(water, sanitation, schools)*; changed aspirations for girls in villages with women leaders; ⭐ **rotation exposing more women to office over time**. (iii) ⭐ **Why the impact is limited** — the ***"Sarpanch Pati"*** phenomenon; **rotation preventing incumbency and experience**; lower literacy and mobility; social constraints on attending meetings; ⚠️ and the structural point — **the office itself is weak**, so capturing it yields limited power. (iv) **What would deepen it** — capacity building, longer rotation cycles, penalties for proxy functioning, and ⭐ **strengthening the office itself**. (v) Conclude: reservation **changed who sits in the chair before it changed what the chair can do** — and connect to the **106th Amendment** ([Ch 22](../Part_III_Central_Government/22_Parliament.md)).

**4. "Assess the importance of the Panchayat system in India as a part of local government. Apart from government grants, what sources can the Panchayats look to for financing developmental projects?" (250 words)**
> *Actually asked — [Mains 2018 Q15](../../04_PYQ_Mains/2018.md).*
> *Skeleton:* **Importance** — Article 40's DPSP realised; constitutional third tier; participatory planning under Article 243G; the Eleventh Schedule's 29 subjects; social justice through reservation. ⭐ **Non-grant financing sources** — **own taxes** *(property tax, tax on land and buildings, professions, entertainment, markets, vehicles, water and lighting rates)*; **tolls and user fees**; **assigned taxes** shared by the state; **State Finance Commission** devolution; **Central Finance Commission** grants under **Article 280(3)(bb)**; income from **panchayat property** *(ponds, markets, common lands, plantations)*; **CSR funds**; **borrowing** where state law permits; and **MGNREGA and scheme convergence**. Conclude on why own-source revenue matters: ⭐ **untied money is the only money that buys autonomy.**

## ⚡ 60-second revision

- ⭐ **73rd Amendment Act, 1992 — in force 24 April 1993. Part IX, Articles 243–243-O, Eleventh Schedule with 29 items.** Rooted in **Article 40** *(a DPSP)*.
- **Committees:** ⭐ **Balwant Rai Mehta 1957** *(three-tier; Rajasthan, **Nagaur, 2 Oct 1959**)* · **Ashok Mehta 1977** *(**two-tier**, district first, political parties, social audit)* · **G.V.K. Rao 1985** *("grass without roots")* · ⭐ **L.M. Singhvi 1986** *(**first to demand constitutional status**, Nyaya Panchayats, Gram Sabha)* · Thungon and Gadgil 1988. ⚠️ **64th Amendment Bill 1989 failed in the Rajya Sabha.**
- ⭐⭐ **COMPULSORY:** Gram Sabha · three tiers · **direct election to all seats at all levels** · indirect election of chairpersons at intermediate/district · **age 21** · SC/ST reservation · ⭐ **one-third for women** · **5-year tenure and re-election within 6 months** · ⭐ **State Election Commission** · ⭐ **State Finance Commission every 5 years**.
- ⭐⭐ **VOLUNTARY:** representation of MPs/MLAs/MLCs · **backward-class reservation** · ⭐ **powers to function as institutions of self-government** · ⭐ **devolution of planning and scheme implementation** · ⭐ **financial powers**.
- ⭐ **243B:** a state with population **not exceeding 20 lakh** need not have the **intermediate** tier. ⭐ **243D:** *"not less than"* one-third for women — a **floor**; twenty-plus states have legislated **50%**. ⭐ **243E:** 5 years · re-election before expiry or within **6 months** of dissolution · the new body serves only the **remainder** · **no election if under 6 months remain**. ⭐ **243F:** age **21**.
- ⭐ **243-I: the GOVERNOR constitutes the State Finance Commission** — ⚠️ **not the Chief Minister.** ⭐ **243K: the State Election Commissioner is removable only like a HIGH COURT JUDGE.**
- ⚠️ **243M exempts: Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram · hill areas of Manipur with a District Council · Scheduled and Tribal Areas · Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council areas.** ⭐ **Parliament extended Part IX to Scheduled Areas via PESA.**
- ⭐⭐ **PESA, 1996 — 10 states** *(AP, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, HP, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan)*. **Gram Sabha approves plans and identifies beneficiaries**; ⭐ **consultation MANDATORY before land acquisition and before minor-mineral licences**; powers over **intoxicants, minor forest produce, land alienation, markets, money lending**; ⭐ **reservation not less than ONE-HALF**; ⭐ **all chairpersons reserved for STs**; **nomination capped at one-tenth**.
- ⚠️ **Why it underperforms — the 3 Fs plus functionality:** *"may"* in **243G** · negligible own revenue and **ignored SFC reports** · **no own functionaries** · **parallel bodies** · ⭐ ***"Sarpanch Pati"*** · bureaucratic dominance · **rotation breaking continuity** · elite capture.
- ⭐ **The line to close on:** *the 73rd Amendment democratised **representation** without decentralising **power**.*
