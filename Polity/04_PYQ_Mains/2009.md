# UPSC Mains — Polity Questions: 2009

[← 2008](2008.md) · [Index](00_INDEX.md) · [2010 →](2010.md)

**Paper:** General Studies **Paper I** (old pattern) · **Polity marks:** ~66 / 300

> A turning point. From 2009 the polity block stops being a neat Q6–Q9 section and dissolves into the paper. Institutional questions ("how is X constituted?") give way to governance and current-affairs framing — the direction the exam would formalise in 2013.

---

## Law, Elections & Corruption

### Q6 · 2 × 15 = 30 marks · ~150 words each · *answer any two*
**(a)** What are your views on the features and impact of the Domestic Violence Act, 2005?

**(b)** Are the traditional determinants of voting behaviour in India changing? Examine in the context of the last General Elections.

**(c)** Examine corruption as a serious development challenge in Indian Polity.

`Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act` `Voting behaviour` `Corruption`

> Q6(b) is a direct follow-up to **2007** Q7(b). Q6(c) anticipates the Lokpal question in **2013** (GS-II Q17) and the Transparency International question in **2016** (GS-II Q12).

---

## Constitutional Bodies

### Q7(b) · 15 marks · ~150 words
In the changing context of governance in the country, what should be the role of the UPSC?

`UPSC` `Civil services`

> Follows **2004** Q7(b) and **2008** Q6(c) on the UPSC; the civil-services reform theme returns in **2014** (GS-II Q14), **2017** (GS-II Q18) and **2020** (GS-II Q7).

---

## Free Speech

### Q9(c) · 15 marks · ~150 words
'As we live in a plural society we need the greatest freedom to express our opinions even if others find it offensive' — Do you agree? Discuss with reference to some recent incidents in the Indian context.

`Article 19(1)(a)` `Free speech` `Pluralism`

> Reappears as **2013** GS-II Q2 (Section 66A) and **2014** GS-II Q4 (hate speech and films).

**(a)** Significance of 26th November in the country's polity
