### 1. Ordinances *(Paper Q51)*
With reference to the Indian polity, consider the following statements :

I. An Ordinance can amend any Central Act.
II. An Ordinance can abridge a Fundamental Right.
III. An Ordinance can come into effect from a back date.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) I and II only
- (b) II and III only
- (c) I and III only
- (d) I, II and III

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) I and III only** · `Ordinance` `Article 123` `Article 13`

> An Ordinance has **the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament** — so it can amend a Central Act (I ✓) and can operate retrospectively (III ✓).
>
> But statement II fails on **Article 13**: an Ordinance is "law" for the purposes of Article 13, so it is void to the extent it abridges a Fundamental Right. An Ordinance can do anything Parliament can do — and Parliament cannot abridge an FR by ordinary law either.

</details>

### 2. States and their descriptions *(Paper Q52)*
Consider the following pairs :

| | State | Description |
|---|---|---|
| I. | Arunachal Pradesh | The capital is named after a fort, and the State has two National Parks |
| II. | Nagaland | The State came into existence on the basis of a Constitutional Amendment Act |
| III. | Tripura | Initially a Part 'C' State, it became a centrally administered territory with the reorganization of States in 1956 and later attained the status of a full-fledged State |

How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All the three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) All the three** · `Reorganisation of States` `Article 371A`

> I — **Itanagar** is named after the **Ita Fort**; Arunachal has **Namdapha** and **Mouling** National Parks.
> II — Nagaland was carved out with the **State of Nagaland Act, 1962**, alongside the **13th Amendment Act** which inserted **Article 371A**.
> III — Tripura: Part 'C' State → Union Territory (1956) → full State in **1972**.

</details>

### 3. Which bodies are constitutional? *(Paper Q53)*
With reference to India, consider the following :

I. The Inter-State Council
II. The National Security Council
III. Zonal Councils

How many of the above were established as per the provisions of the Constitution of India?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All the three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Only one** · `Inter-State Council` `Article 263` `Constitutional vs statutory bodies`

> Only the **Inter-State Council** is constitutional — **Article 263**, set up by Presidential order in 1990 on the Sarkaria Commission's recommendation.
>
> **Zonal Councils** are **statutory** (States Reorganisation Act, 1956) and the **National Security Council** is a purely **executive** body (1998). This constitutional / statutory / executive three-way split is one of the highest-yield distinctions in the whole subject.

</details>

### 4. Governor's discretion and reservation of Bills *(Paper Q54)*
Consider the following statements :

I. The Constitution of India explicitly mentions that in certain spheres the Governor of a State acts in his/her own discretion.
II. The President of India can, of his/her own, reserve a bill passed by a State Legislature for his/her consideration without it being forwarded by the Governor of the State concerned.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) I only
- (b) II only
- (c) Both I and II
- (d) Neither I nor II

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) I only** · `Governor` `Article 163` `Article 200` `Article 201`

> I is correct — **Article 163(1)** expressly carves out functions the Governor exercises "in his discretion", and Article 163(2) makes the Governor's own view on what is discretionary final.
>
> II is wrong: reservation of a State Bill for the President runs **only through the Governor** under **Article 200**. The President has no power to reach down and pull up a State Bill directly.

</details>

### 5. Where each provision sits *(Paper Q55)*
Consider the following pairs :

| | Provision in the Constitution of India | Stated under |
|---|---|---|
| I. | Separation of Judiciary from the Executive in the public services of the State | The Directive Principles of the State Policy |
| II. | Valuing and preserving of the rich heritage of our composite culture | The Fundamental Duties |
| III. | Prohibition of employment of children below the age of 14 years in factories | The Fundamental Rights |

How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All the three
- (d) None

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) All the three** · `DPSP` `Fundamental Duties` `Article 24`

> I — **Article 50**, a DPSP. II — **Article 51A(f)**, a Fundamental Duty. III — **Article 24**, a Fundamental Right.
>
> The trap UPSC keeps setting here is **Article 24 vs Article 45**: prohibition of child labour under 14 is an enforceable **Right** (Art. 24); free and compulsory education / early childhood care is the **DPSP** side (Art. 45). See [`08_DPSP`](../02_Laxmikanth/Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/08_DPSP.md) and [`09_Fundamental_Duties`](../02_Laxmikanth/Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/09_Fundamental_Duties.md).

</details>

### 6. Fifth Schedule — Scheduled Areas *(Paper Q56)*
Consider the following statements :

With reference to the Constitution of India, if an area in a State is declared as Scheduled Area under the Fifth Schedule

I. the State Government loses its executive power in such areas and a local body assumes total administration
II. the Union Government can take over the total administration of such areas under certain circumstances on the recommendations of the Governor

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) I only
- (b) II only
- (c) Both I and II
- (d) Neither I nor II

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither I nor II** · `Fifth Schedule` `Scheduled Areas` `Article 244`

> Declaring a Scheduled Area **does not displace the State Government at all**. The executive power of the State continues; what the Fifth Schedule adds is a layer of **Governor's oversight** — an annual report to the President, a **Tribes Advisory Council**, and the Governor's power to direct that a Central or State law shall not apply, or shall apply with modifications.
>
> The Union's role is to give **directions to the State** as to administration of those areas — not to take over administration itself.

</details>

### 7. Amendments needing State ratification *(Paper Q58)*
Consider the following subjects under the Constitution of India :

I. List I—Union List, in the Seventh Schedule
II. Extent of the executive power of a State
III. Conditions of the Governor's office

For a constitutional amendment with respect to which of the above, ratification by the Legislatures of not less than one-half of the States is required before presenting the bill to the President of India for assent?

- (a) I and II only
- (b) II and III only
- (c) I and III only
- (d) I, II and III

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) I and II only** · `Article 368` `Federal amendments`

> The proviso to **Article 368(2)** lists exactly what needs half the States: election of the President (Arts. 54, 55); **extent of the executive power of the Union and the States** (Arts. 73, 162); High Courts for Union Territories; Chapter IV of Part V, Chapter V of Part VI, Chapter I of Part XI; **any of the Lists in the Seventh Schedule**; representation of States in Parliament; and Article 368 itself.
>
> **Conditions of the Governor's office is not on that list** — that is the whole question. See [`10_Amendment_of_the_Constitution`](../02_Laxmikanth/Part_I_Constitutional_Framework/10_Amendment_of_the_Constitution.md).

</details>

### 8. Immunities of the Governor and legislators *(Paper Q59)*
With reference to the Indian polity, consider the following statements :

I. The Governor of a State is not answerable to any court for the exercise and performance of the powers and duties of his/her office.
II. No criminal proceedings shall be instituted or continued against the Governor during his/her term of office.
III. Members of a State Legislature are not liable to any proceedings in any court in respect of anything said within the House.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) I and II only
- (b) II and III only
- (c) I and III only
- (d) I, II and III

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) I, II and III** · `Article 361` `Article 194` `Privileges`

> I and II are **Article 361(1) and 361(2)** — personal immunity of the President and Governors. III is **Article 194(2)** — the State-legislature twin of Article 105(2) for Parliament.
>
> Note what the immunity does *not* cover: the Governor's **actions remain judicially reviewable** even though the Governor personally is not answerable, and civil proceedings can be brought after **two months' notice**.

</details>

### 9. Pardoning power of the President *(Paper Q86)*
Consider the following statements with regard to pardoning power of the President of India :

I. The exercise of this power by the President can be subjected to limited judicial review.
II. The President can exercise this power without the advice of the Central Government.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) I only
- (b) II only
- (c) Both I and II
- (d) Neither I nor II

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) I only** · `Article 72` `Pardoning power` `Article 74`

> I is correct — **Epuru Sudhakar v. Government of A.P. (2006)** confirmed limited judicial review of clemency (for mala fides, irrelevant considerations, arbitrariness or non-application of mind), though the merits are not re-examined.
>
> II is wrong for the same reason as always: under **Article 74** the President acts on the **aid and advice of the Council of Ministers**. Article 72 is no exception — the Home Ministry processes mercy petitions.

</details>

### 10. The Speaker of the House of the People *(Paper Q87)*
Consider the following statements :

I. On the dissolution of the House of the People, the Speaker shall not vacate his/her office until immediately before the first meeting of the House of the People after the dissolution.
II. According to the provisions of the Constitution of India, a Member of the House of the People on being elected as Speaker shall resign from his/her political party immediately.
III. The Speaker of the House of the People may be removed from his/her office by a resolution of the House of the People passed by a majority of all the then Members of the House, provided that no resolution shall be moved unless at least fourteen days' notice has been given of the intention to move the resolution.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) I and II only
- (b) II and III only
- (c) I and III only
- (d) I, II and III

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) I and III only** · `Speaker` `Article 94`

> I and III are both straight out of **Article 94**: continuity of office across dissolution, and removal by an **effective majority** with **14 days' notice**.
>
> II is wrong — and note the exact wording of the trap: "**According to the provisions of the Constitution of India**". Resigning from one's party is a **British convention** that India has never adopted; the Constitution says nothing of the kind.

</details>

### 11. Tenth Schedule and 'political party' *(Paper Q88)*
Consider the following statements :

I. If any question arises as to whether a Member of the House of the People has become subject to disqualification under the 10th Schedule, the President's decision in accordance with the opinion of the Council of Union Ministers shall be final.
II. There is no mention of the word 'political party' in the Constitution of India.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) I only
- (b) II only
- (c) Both I and II
- (d) Neither I nor II

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither I nor II** · `Tenth Schedule` `Anti-defection` `Article 103`

> I confuses two different routes. Under **Article 103**, other disqualifications are decided by the **President in accordance with the opinion of the Election Commission** — not the Council of Ministers. And **Tenth Schedule** questions are carved out of Article 103 entirely: they are decided by the **Speaker/Chairman**.
>
> II is wrong because the **Tenth Schedule itself repeatedly uses "political party"** — inserted by the 52nd Amendment, 1985.

</details>

### 12. Minor minerals — Centre vs State *(Paper Q89)*
Consider the following statements :

**Statement I :** In India, State Governments have no power for making rules for grant of concessions in respect of extraction of minor minerals even though such minerals are located in their territories.

**Statement II :** In India, the Central Government has the power to notify minor minerals under the relevant law.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

- (a) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct and Statement II explains Statement I
- (b) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct but Statement II does not explain Statement I
- (c) Statement I is correct but Statement II is not correct
- (d) Statement I is not correct but Statement II is correct

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Statement I is not correct but Statement II is correct** · `Federalism` `MMDR Act` `Seventh Schedule`

> This is the neatest Centre–State split in the paper. Under the **MMDR Act, 1957**, the **Centre decides what counts as a "minor mineral"** (Statement II ✓), but once so notified, **the States make the rules** for granting concessions in them (so Statement I ✗).
>
> Classification is central; administration is State. Read the two halves separately and the Statement I/II format resolves itself.

</details>

### 13. Panchayats — what is NOT correct *(Paper Q91)*
Consider the following statements :

I. Panchayats at the intermediate level exist in all States.
II. To be eligible to be a Member of a Panchayat at the intermediate level, a person should attain the age of thirty years.
III. The Chief Minister of a State constitutes a commission to review the financial position of Panchayats at the intermediate levels and to make recommendations regarding the distribution of net proceeds of taxes and duties, leviable by the State, between the State and Panchayats at the intermediate level.

Which of the statements given above are **not** correct?

- (a) I and II only
- (b) II and III only
- (c) I and III only
- (d) I, II and III

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) I, II and III** · `73rd Amendment` `Part IX` `Article 243I`

> All three are wrong — read the question stem carefully, it asks which are **not** correct.
>
> I — **Article 243B** exempts States with a population **not exceeding twenty lakhs** from having an intermediate level.
> II — the minimum age is **21**, not 30 (Article 243F).
> III — the **State Finance Commission** is constituted by the **Governor** under **Article 243I**, not the Chief Minister.
>
> See [`../01_NCERT/`](../01_NCERT/) Class 11 *Local Governments* for the 73rd/74th Amendment structure.

</details>

### 14. Lokpal *(Paper Q98)*
Consider the following statements about Lokpal :

I. The power of Lokpal applies to public servants of India, but not to the Indian public servants posted outside India.
II. The Chairperson or a Member shall not be a Member of the Parliament or a Member of the Legislature of any State or Union Territory, and only the Chief Justice of India, whether incumbent or retired, has to be its Chairperson.
III. The Chairperson or a Member shall not be a person of less than forty-five years of age on the date of assuming office as the Chairperson or Member, as the case may be.
IV. Lokpal cannot inquire into the allegations of corruption against a sitting Prime Minister of India.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) III only
- (b) II and III
- (c) I and IV
- (d) None of the above statements is correct

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) III only** · `Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013`

> I ✗ — the Act reaches Indian public servants **wherever posted**, including outside India.
> II ✗ — the Chairperson may be a **former CJI, a former Supreme Court judge, or an eminent person** meeting the prescribed criteria; it is not CJI-only.
> III ✓ — minimum age **45**.
> IV ✗ — the Lokpal **can** inquire against a sitting PM, but with safeguards: matters of international relations, security, public order, atomic energy and space are excluded, and it needs a **two-thirds** vote of the full bench with the inquiry held in camera.

</details>

## What 2025 tested

| Theme | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Executive** — Ordinance, Governor's discretion, pardoning power, Governor's immunity | 4 |
| **Federalism & Centre-State** — Inter-State Council, Fifth Schedule, State ratification, minor minerals, State formation | 5 |
| **Parliament** — Speaker, Tenth Schedule | 2 |
| **Rights, Duties & DPSP** — where each provision sits | 1 |
| **Local government** — Panchayats | 1 |
| **Statutory bodies** — Lokpal | 1 |

**The lesson of this paper:** 2025 was a **federalism year**. Five of fourteen questions sat on the Centre–State seam, and three of them turned on the same distinction — *who classifies* versus *who administers* (minor minerals), *who is constitutional* versus *statutory* versus *executive* (Inter-State Council), *what needs State ratification* versus what does not.

Also worth internalising: **three separate questions were decided by Article 74**. Ordinance, pardoning power, and the reservation of Bills all came back to "the President acts on advice." If you know that one principle cold, you bank marks every single year.
