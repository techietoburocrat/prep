### 1. Forced Labour (Vishti) in the Gupta Period
With reference to forced labour (Vishti) in India during the Gupta period, which one of the following statements is correct?

- (a) It was considered a source of income for the State, a sort of tax paid by the people.
- (b) It was totally absent in the Madhya Pradesh and Kathiawar regions of the Gupta Empire.
- (c) The forced labourer was entitled to weekly wages.
- (d) The eldest son of the labourer was sent as the forced labourer.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) It was considered a source of income for the State, a sort of tax paid by the people** · `Ancient India` `Gupta administration`

</details>

### 2. Harappan Sites
Which one of the following is not a Harappan site?

- (a) Chanhudaro
- (b) Kot Diji
- (c) Sohgaura
- (d) Desalpur

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Sohgaura** · `Ancient India` `Harappan Civilisation` — Sohgaura (Uttar Pradesh) is famous instead for a **Mauryan-era copper-plate inscription** on famine relief, not Harappan archaeology.

</details>

### 3. Delhi Sultanate Administrative Terms
Consider the following statements:

1. In the revenue administration of the Delhi Sultanate, the in-charge of revenue collection was known as 'Amil'.
2. The Iqta system of the Sultans of Delhi was an ancient indigenous institution.
3. The office of 'Mir Bakshi' came into existence during the reign of the Khalji Sultans of Delhi.

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 1 and 2 only
- (c) 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 only** · `Medieval India` `Delhi Sultanate administration` — the Iqta system was **imported from Central/West Asia**, not indigenous, and 'Mir Bakshi' is a **Mughal**-era office, not Khalji.

</details>

### 4. Mian Tansen
With reference to Mian Tansen, which one of the following statements is not correct?

- (a) Tansen was the title given to him by Emperor Akbar.
- (b) Tansen composed Dhrupads on Hindu gods and goddesses.
- (c) Tansen composed songs on his patrons.
- (d) Tansen invented many Ragas.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Tansen was the title given to him by Emperor Akbar** *(the statement that is NOT correct)* · `Medieval India` `Mughal court culture` — "Tansen" was a title conferred by his earlier patron, **Raja Ramchandra of Rewa**, before he joined Akbar's court.

</details>

### 5. Jagirdar vs Zamindar
With reference to Mughal India, what is/are the difference/differences between Jagirdar and Zamindar?

1. Jagirdars were holders of land assignments in lieu of judicial and police duties, whereas Zamindars were holders of revenue rights without obligation to perform any duty other than revenue collection.
2. Land assignments to Jagirdars were hereditary and revenue rights of Zamindars were not hereditary.

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) Both 1 and 2
- (d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** · `Medieval India` `Mughal land-revenue system` — Jagirs were typically **non-hereditary** and transferable, while zamindari rights were often **hereditary**, the reverse of what statement 2 claims; statement 1 also mischaracterises the Jagirdar's duties (administrative/military, not merely judicial/police).

</details>

### 6. Gandhi and Colonial-Era Events
With reference to British colonial rule in India, consider the following statements:

1. Mahatma Gandhi was instrumental in the abolition of the system of 'indentured labour'.
2. In Lord Chelmsford's 'War Conference', Mahatma Gandhi did not support the resolution on recruiting Indians for World War.
3. Consequent upon the breaking of the Salt Law by Indian people, the Indian National Congress was declared illegal by the colonial rulers.

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 1 and 3 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 1 and 3 only** · `Modern India` `Gandhi` `Civil Disobedience Movement` — Gandhi actually **supported** the War Conference's recruitment resolution (a controversial, less-known episode where he backed British war recruitment), making statement 2 false.

</details>

### 9. The Charter Act of 1813
Consider the following statements about the Charter Act of 1813:

1. It ended the trade monopoly of the East India Company in India except for trade in tea and trade with China.
2. It asserted the sovereignty of the British Crown over the Indian territories held by the Company.
3. The revenues of India were now controlled by the British Parliament.

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 and 2 only** · `Modern India` `Charter Acts` — cross-link: [Spectrum file 12](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Modern_India_Spectrum/Notes/12_Topup_Tables.md), Part I, on the 1813 Act's education-funding clause; [Laxmikanth Ch.1](../../../Polity/02_Laxmikanth/01_Historical_Background.md) for the full Acts sequence. Statement 3 is false — Indian revenues **remained under Company control** at this stage, still short of full Parliamentary control.

</details>

### 12. Nimbarka and Kabir
Consider the following statements:

1. Saint Nimbarka was a contemporary of Akbar.
2. Saint Kabir was greatly influenced by Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) Both 1 and 2
- (d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** · `Medieval India` `Bhakti and Sufi saints` — Nimbarka predates Akbar by centuries (traditionally placed much earlier); Kabir (15th century) predates Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi (16th-17th century), making the influence chronologically impossible.

</details>
