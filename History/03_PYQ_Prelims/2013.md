### 1. Hiuen Tsang's Account of India
The Chinese traveller Yuan Chwang (Hiuen Tsang), who visited India, recorded the general conditions and culture of India at that time. In this context, which of the following statements is/are correct?

1. The roads and river-routes were completely immune from robbery.
2. As regards punishment for offences, ordeals by fire, water and poison were the instruments for determining the innocence or guilt of a person.
3. Tradesmen had to pay duties at ferries and barrier stations.

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 1 and 2 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) 2 and 3 only** · `Ancient India` `Foreign travellers' accounts` `Harshavardhana era` — Hiuen Tsang actually recorded significant **robbery risk** on the roads (he himself was robbed), making statement 1 false.

</details>

### 2. The Indus Civilisation's Characteristics
Which of the following characterises/characterise the people of the Indus Civilisation?

1. They possessed great palaces and temples.
2. They worshipped both male and female deities.
3. They employed horse-drawn chariots in warfare.

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) None of the statements given above is correct

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** · `Ancient India` `Harappan Civilisation` — no unambiguous great palace/temple structures have been identified (unlike Mesopotamia), and there is **no clear evidence of horses or horse-drawn chariots** in Harappan contexts.

</details>

### 9. "Tribhanga" Pose
In the context of the cultural history of India, a pose in dance and dramatics called 'Tribhanga' has been a favourite of Indian artists from ancient times till today. Which one of the following statements best describes this pose?

- (b) Facial expressions, hand gestures and make-up are combined to symbolise certain epic or historic characters

### 13. Rock-Cut Architecture
With reference to the history of Indian rock-cut architecture, consider the following statements:

1. The caves at Badami are the oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India.
2. The Barabar rock-cut caves were originally made for the Ajivikas by Emperor Chandragupta Maurya.
3. At Ellora, caves were made for different faiths.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 1 and 2 only
- (c) 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) 3 only** · `Art & Culture` `Rock-cut architecture` — the Barabar caves (Bihar) are earlier than Badami, so Badami is not the oldest, making statement 1 false; the Barabar caves were commissioned by **Ashoka** (and later Dasharatha), not Chandragupta Maurya, making statement 2 false. Ellora genuinely has Buddhist, Hindu and Jain caves side by side.

</details>

### 17. Bhakti Saints Contemporary with the Lodi-to-Mughal Transition
Consider the following Bhakti Saints:

1. Dadu Dayal
2. Guru Nanak
3. Tyagaraja

Who among the above was/were preaching when the Lodi dynasty fell and Babur took over?

- (a) 1 and 3
- (b) 2 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1 and 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** · `Medieval India` `Bhakti saints` `Chronology` — Guru Nanak (b.1469) was active at the time of the 1526 Lodi-Mughal transition; Dadu Dayal (16th-17th c.) and Tyagaraja (18th-19th c.) both postdate it by a wide margin.

</details>
