### 1. Elements of the Feudal System
With reference to Indian history, which of the following is/are the essential elementary elements of the feudal system?

1. A very strong centralised political authority and a very weak provincial or local political authority
2. The emergence of an administrative structure based on control and possession of the land
3. Creation of a lord-vassal relationship between the feudal lord and his overlord

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 and 3 only** · `Ancient/Early Medieval India` `Feudalism debate` — feudalism is characterised by a **weak central authority and strong local/provincial power**, the reverse of statement 1.

</details>

### 2. Medieval Indian States and Present Regions
Consider the following pairs — medieval Indian state and present region:

| Medieval Indian State | Present Region |
|---|---|
| 1. Champaka | Central India |
| 2. Durgara | Jammu |
| 3. Kuluta | Malabar |

Which of the above pairs is/are correctly matched?

- (a) 1 and 2
- (b) 2 only
- (c) 1 and 3
- (d) 3 only

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** · `Medieval India` `Historical geography` — Kuluta actually corresponds to **Kullu (Himachal Pradesh)**, not Malabar.

</details>

### 4. Babur's Arrival in India
Consider the following. The arrival of Babur into India led to the —

1. Introduction of gunpowder in the subcontinent
2. Introduction of the arch and dome in the region's architecture
3. Establishment of the Timurid dynasty in the region

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 1 and 3 only
- (c) 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) 3 only** · `Medieval India` `Mughal founding` — gunpowder and the arch-and-dome technique were **both already present in India before Babur** (via the Delhi Sultanate/earlier contacts), making 1 and 2 false; only the Timurid dynastic establishment is genuinely new with Babur.

</details>

### 12. The Rowlatt Satyagraha
With reference to the Rowlatt Satyagraha, which of the following statements is/are correct?

1. The Rowlatt Act was based on the recommendations of the 'Sedition Committee'.
2. In the Rowlatt Satyagraha, Gandhi tried to use the Home Rule League.
3. Demonstrations against the Simon Commission coincided with the Rowlatt Satyagraha.

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 1 and 2 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 1 and 2 only** · `Modern India` `Rowlatt Satyagraha` — the Simon Commission (1928) came nearly a decade after the Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919), making statement 3 chronologically false.

</details>

### 13. Kingdoms Associated with the Buddha's Life
Which of the following kingdoms were associated with the life of the Buddha?

1. Avanti
2. Gandhara
3. Kosala
4. Magadha

- (a) 1, 2 and 3
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1, 3 and 4
- (d) 3 and 4 only

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 3 and 4 only** *(corrected — see below)* · `Ancient India` `Buddha's biography` — Kosala (Shravasti/Jetavana) and Magadha (Bodh Gaya, Rajgir) were the Buddha's actual spheres of activity. Neither Avanti nor Gandhara had a direct personal association with his life. ⚠️ **This file's first-pass source (superkalam.com) reported (c) as correct; cross-checking against multiple independent sources confirms the official UPSC key is (d) — corrected here rather than left standing.**

</details>

### 15. Earliest Among Four Monuments
With reference to the art and archaeological history of India, which one among the following was made earliest?

- (a) Lingaraja Temple at Bhubaneswar
- (b) Rock-cut Elephant at Dhauli
- (c) Rock-cut Monuments at Mahabalipuram
- (d) Varaha Image at Udayagiri

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Rock-cut Elephant at Dhauli** · `Art & Culture` `Chronology of monuments` — a Mauryan-era (Ashokan) work, predating the Gupta-era Udayagiri Varaha, the Pallava Mahabalipuram monuments, and the later Kalinga-era Lingaraja Temple.

</details>

- (d) A hand-painted decorative silk cloth in North-Western India
