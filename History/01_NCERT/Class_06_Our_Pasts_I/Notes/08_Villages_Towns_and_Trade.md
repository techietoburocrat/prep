> 🔑 **The point worth carrying into an answer:** the Silk Route's lasting legacy was **cultural, not commercial**. > 🌏 **Live IR relevance:** China's Belt and Road Initiative explicitly invokes Silk Route imagery. # PART A — 🌾 VILLAGES: Who Produced the Wealth

## 1️⃣ The Technology That Made Surplus Possible

Two innovations, already met in Chapter 5, deserve restating because everything here rests on them:

| Innovation | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Iron ploughshare** ⭐ | Turned over the heavy, clayey alluvial soil of the Ganga valley that wooden ploughs couldn't break. **Much higher yields** |
| **Paddy transplantation** ⭐⭐ | Saplings grown in a nursery, then **transplanted** into flooded fields. Dramatically increases yield — **but requires enormous labour** |
| **Irrigation** ⭐ | Canals, wells, tanks and **artificial lakes**. Kings and rich landowners funded these — and gained prestige and control by doing so |

> ⚠️ **NCERT's crucial rider, and the reason this chapter matters:** the yield gains from transplantation were captured by landowners, while the **labour** was supplied by **slaves and landless labourers who received no share of the increase.** Technological progress **widened** inequality rather than reducing it. **That's a genuinely strong, evidence-based line for Mains and Essay.**

## 2️⃣ Who Lived in a Village — Learn Both Regions

### 🌴 In the TAMIL region (South) ⭐⭐

| Group | Who they were |
|---|---|
| **VELLALAR** ⭐ | **Large landowners** — the dominant landholding class |
| **UZHAVAR** ⭐ | **Ploughmen** — ordinary cultivators who worked their own land |
| **KADAISIYAR** and **ADIMAI** ⭐ | **Landless labourers, including slaves** — the bottom of the order |

### 🌾 In the NORTHERN region ⭐⭐

| Group | Who they were |
|---|---|
| **GRAMA BHOJAKA** ⭐⭐ | **The village headman.** Usually the **largest landowner**; the post was often **HEREDITARY** ⭐. He had slaves and hired workers to cultivate his land; the king used him to **collect taxes**; he also functioned as **judge** and sometimes **policeman** |
| **GRIHAPATI** ⭐ | **Independent farmers** — smaller landowners who cultivated their own land |
| **DASA KARMAKARA** ⭐ | **Slaves and hired workers** — landless, working others' fields for wages or as bondsmen |
| **Artisans** | Blacksmith, potter, carpenter, weaver — village craftspeople |

> ⭐ **The *grama bhojaka* is the key figure to understand.** Look at the roles he combined: **biggest landowner + tax collector + judge + policeman**, and the position was **hereditary**. That is enormous concentrated local power — economic, fiscal, judicial and coercive, all in one hereditary office.
>
> 💻 **He's the single point of contact between the state and the village — and he holds all four capabilities with no separation of concerns and no rotation.** The state gained cheap reach; the village gained an unchallengeable local authority. **This is the deep historical root of rural power structures that Indian land reform and Panchayati Raj legislation have been trying to unpick ever since.**
>
> 🔗 **Cross-link to your Polity notes:** the 73rd Amendment's design — elected panchayats, reservation of seats, a separate State Election Commission — is in part a deliberate answer to exactly this hereditary-headman concentration. **A great connective point for a GS2 answer.**

## 3️⃣ 📜 The SANGAM AGE — South India Enters the Syllabus

**High-yield. UPSC asks about the Sangam Age regularly, and most aspirants under-prepare it.**

### What were the Sangams?

**Sangam** = an **assembly/academy of Tamil poets**, traditionally held under the patronage of the **Pandya kings at Madurai**. Tradition speaks of **three Sangams**; the literature of the **third** largely survives.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Period** | c. **300 BCE – 300 CE** |
| **Language** | **Tamil** |
| **Patrons** | The **Pandyas** of **Madurai** |
| **Nature** | Overwhelmingly **secular** poetry — about love, war, kings, ethics and everyday life. **Not primarily religious**, unlike most contemporary north Indian literature ⭐ |
| **Authors** | ~**473 poets**, including **~30 women poets** ⭐ — most famously **AVVAIYAR** |

### The literature ⭐⭐

| Work | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ettuthogai** | "The Eight Anthologies" |
| **Pattuppattu** | "The Ten Idylls" |
| **Tolkappiyam** ⭐⭐ | **The earliest Tamil grammar** — by **Tolkappiyar**; also covers poetics and sociology. **The oldest extant Tamil work** |
| **Pathinenkilkanakku** | "The Eighteen Minor Works" — includes the **Tirukkural** |
| **TIRUKKURAL** ⭐⭐ | By **THIRUVALLUVAR** — 1,330 couplets on ethics, polity and love. Often called the "Tamil Veda"; a universal ethical classic. ⭐ **Excellent GS4 Ethics citation** |
| **Silappadikaram** ⭐⭐ | By **ILANGO ADIGAL** — "The Tale of the Anklet"; the story of Kovalan, Kannagi and Madhavi. One of the **five great Tamil epics** |
| **Manimekalai** ⭐⭐ | By **SATTANAR (Sittalai Sattanar)** — sequel to *Silappadikaram*, strongly **Buddhist** in outlook |

> ⚠️ **Note:** *Silappadikaram* and *Manimekalai* are strictly **post-Sangam** (c. 5th–6th century CE), but are universally taught alongside Sangam literature and asked together. **Know the author-work pairs — that's the most common question format.**

> ⭐ **KARIKALA CHOLA** deserves a line of his own: he won the **Battle of Venni**, founded **Puhar/Kaveripattinam**, and built the **Kallanai (Grand Anicut)** across the Kaveri — **one of the oldest water-diversion structures still in use anywhere in the world.** That's a superb, specific example for an Economy/Geography answer on ancient irrigation.

### 🌄 The *Tinai* system — Sangam poetry's eco-zones ⭐

A genuinely distinctive idea: Sangam poetry classifies landscapes into **five *tinai***, each with its own deity, people, occupation and associated emotional theme.

| Tinai | Landscape | Deity | People/occupation | Love-theme |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kurinji** | Hills/mountains | Murugan | Hunters, gatherers | Union of lovers |
| **Mullai** | Forest/pastoral | Mayon (Vishnu) | Herders | Patient waiting |
| **Marutham** | Agricultural plains | Indra | Farmers | Lovers' quarrels |
| **Neithal** | Coastal | Varunan | Fisherfolk, salt makers | Pining/separation |
| **Palai** | Arid/desert | Kotravai | Robbers, wanderers | Separation, hardship |

**Poems also split by theme:** ***Aham*** (interior — love and personal life) and ***Puram*** (exterior — war, kings, public life).

> 🎯 **This is genuinely sophisticated literary architecture** — an ecological classification mapped onto emotional states. **It's a strong Art & Culture point** showing that ancient Indian literary theory was systematic, not merely devotional.

# PART B — 🏙️ TOWNS AND TRADE

## 4️⃣ The Archaeology of Towns

### 🔵 Ring wells ⭐

Rings of **baked clay arranged one on top of another**, sunk into the ground. Found in many settlements.

**Uses:** as **toilets/soak pits**, as **drains**, as **garbage dumps**, and (in some cases) as **wells**.

> 🎯 A modest artefact with outsized meaning: ring wells indicate **sanitation arrangements in ordinary households** — not palaces. It's archaeology speaking for people no text mentions.

### 🖤 NBPW — Northern Black Polished Ware ⭐⭐

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | A fine, **glossy black (sometimes steel-grey/silvery) pottery** with a distinctive lustrous surface |
| **When** | c. **700–200 BCE** — the classic marker of the **Second Urbanisation** and the Mauryan period |
| **Where** | Mainly the Ganga valley, but traded very widely |
| **Significance** | **A luxury/deluxe ware** — its wide distribution maps trade networks, and its presence dates a layer |

> 💻 **NBPW is the archaeologist's version-string.** Find it in a layer and you've dated the layer and identified it as connected to the Gangetic trade network. Chapter 2 told you pottery is archaeology's most useful fossil — **NBPW is the flagship example.**

### 🪙 Punch-marked coins ⭐

- India's **earliest coins**, mostly **silver** (some copper)
- Called ***PANA*** or ***karshapana***
- Made by **punching symbols** (sun, wheel, hill, tree, animals) onto metal pieces — **no ruler's name or portrait** ⭐
- Circulated from ~600 BCE, becoming widespread in the Mauryan period

> ⚠️ **Prelims trap:** punch-marked coins bear **symbols, not names or portraits.** The first coins with **rulers' names and portraits** in India come from the **Indo-Greeks**. **Kanishka's gold coins** later show him and various deities.

### 🏭 Shrenis — Guilds ⭐⭐

A **SHRENI** was an **association of craftspersons or merchants** — the corporate backbone of the ancient Indian economy.

**What a shreni actually did:**

| Function | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trained craftspersons** | Apprenticeship and skill transmission |
| **Procured raw material** | Bulk purchase and supply |
| **Distributed finished products** | Marketing and sale |
| **Set standards** | Quality and price regulation for the trade |
| **Acted as BANKS** ⭐ | Received deposits from rich merchants and religious institutions and paid **interest**; the corpus funded monasteries and temples in perpetuity |
| **Made religious donations** ⭐ | Guild donations are recorded in **inscriptions at Sanchi, Bharhut, Karle, Nasik and Amaravati** — a major source of evidence |
| **Self-regulation** | Had their own rules, headmen and internal dispute resolution, often recognised by the state |

> ⭐ **The banking function deserves emphasis.** A donor could deposit a sum with a guild on the condition that the **interest** perpetually funded, say, lamps or robes for a monastery. **That is an endowment with a perpetual income stream — ancient India's version of a trust fund**, and it was legally durable enough that inscriptions record the terms.
>
> 🎯 **Guild inscriptions are also a *source* in the Chapter 1 sense** — they record artisans and merchants (weavers, oil-pressers, potters, ivory-carvers) by name and trade. **Non-royal people entering the historical record through their own donations.** Beautiful example for Mains.

## 5️⃣ ⚓ ARIKAMEDU — The Roman Connection ⭐⭐⭐

**Arikamedu, near Puducherry (Pondicherry), on the Ariyankuppam river** — occupied c. 1st century BCE – 2nd century CE. Excavated notably by **Mortimer Wheeler**.

**What was found there — learn this list, it's asked directly:**

| Find | Significance |
|---|---|
| **A massive brick structure** — possibly a **warehouse** | A trading depot, not just a village |
| **AMPHORAE** ⭐⭐ | Tall, double-handled **Roman jars** used to carry **wine and olive oil** |
| **ARRETINE WARE** ⭐⭐ | Stamped **red-glazed Roman pottery**, made in **Arezzo, Italy** — unmistakably imported |
| **ROULETTED WARE** ⭐ | Fine pottery with rouletted (wheel-impressed) decoration |
| **Roman lamps, glassware, gems** | Roman luxury imports |
| **Bead-making and dyeing facilities** | Local **production for export** — Arikamedu made goods *for* the Roman market |
| **Roman coins** | Direct evidence of the trade balance |

> ⭐ **The Tamil word *YAVANA*** was used for Greeks and then Romans/westerners generally. Sangam poems describe **yavanas arriving in ships, paying in gold, and taking away pepper** — and even mention yavanas employed as **bodyguards** by Tamil kings. **The literature and the archaeology corroborate each other precisely** — which is exactly the methodological ideal Chapter 1 set up.

### 💰 The Indo-Roman trade

**India exported:** ⭐ **PEPPER** (Romans called it *"black gold"* — the single biggest item), spices (cardamom, cinnamon, ginger), **muslin** and fine cotton textiles, **pearls** (from Korkai), **gemstones** (beryl from Coimbatore), ivory, **teak**, indigo, sandalwood, and **exotic animals**.

**India imported:** **GOLD and SILVER COINS** ⭐ (in enormous quantity), wine, olive oil, glassware, coral, some slaves, and lead/tin.

> ⭐⭐ **The trade balance was so heavily in India's favour** that the Roman writer **PLINY THE ELDER** complained that India was **"the sink of the world's gold"** — draining the Roman treasury of precious metal. **Huge hoards of Roman gold coins have been found in South India**, especially Tamil Nadu and Kerala, which archaeologically confirms Pliny's complaint.
>
> 🎯 **This is a fantastic fact.** It shows ancient India as a **major exporter of high-value manufactured and agricultural goods running a large trade surplus with the era's superpower.** It's quotable in Economy answers on India's historical trade position and in Essay on India's economic history.

### 🌬️ Hippalus and the Monsoon ⭐⭐

Around the **1st century CE**, a Greek sailor named **HIPPALUS** is credited (in Greco-Roman sources) with recognising the **regularity of the monsoon winds** across the Arabian Sea.

```
   BEFORE                              AFTER
   ══════                              ═════
   Ships hugged the coastline,         Ships sailed DIRECT across the
   creeping around Arabia and          open Arabian Sea:
   Persia. Slow, dangerous,            • SW monsoon (summer) → to India
   many months, many middlemen.        • NE monsoon (winter) → back
                                       
                                       ~40 DAYS instead of many months.
                                       Middlemen bypassed. Volume soars.
```

> 💻 **This is a latency-and-hop-count optimisation.** He didn't invent a new ship — he discovered that a *predictable* natural pattern let you take a direct route instead of many short hops through intermediaries. **Fewer hops, lower latency, no rent-seeking middlemen, dramatically higher throughput.** Indo-Roman trade volume exploded as a direct result.

**Also know:** the ***Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*** ⭐ — an anonymous Greek merchant's handbook (1st century CE) listing Indian Ocean ports, goods and sailing conditions. **A superb primary source** for ancient Indian trade.

## 6️⃣ 🐫 THE SILK ROUTE

### The commodity

**Silk** was made in **China**, which guarded the secret of sericulture (silkworm rearing) fiercely for centuries. Roman elites paid extraordinary prices for it — so much so that the Roman Senate periodically tried to ban it as a drain on the treasury and an affront to modesty.

### The route

```
   ╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
   ║                       THE SILK ROUTE(S)                               ║
   ╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

   🇨🇳 CHINA ──► Central Asia ──► 🏔️ over the mountains ──► NW INDIA
   (silk)         (Kashgar,          (Karakoram,             (Taxila,
                   Samarkand,         Hindu Kush)             Peshawar,
                   Bactria)                                   Mathura)
                       │                                          │
                       │                                          ▼
                       ▼                                    Indian ports
                  ──► PERSIA ──► 🇸🇾 SYRIA ──► 🇷🇴 ROME     (Bharuch,
                                                             Muziris)
                                                                  │
                                                                  ▼
                                                          SEA ROUTE via
                                                          Arabian Sea &
                                                          Red Sea → Egypt
                                                          → Mediterranean
                                                          → ROME

   ⭐ THE CRUCIAL POINT: a long stretch of the overland route passed
      through NORTH-WESTERN INDIA. Whoever controlled that stretch
      collected the TAXES on everything moving along it.

      → THE KUSHANAS controlled it. → THE KUSHANAS GOT VERY RICH.
      → Which is why Kanishka's coins are GOLD.
```

**Also travelling the route:** horses (from Central Asia — always in demand in India), glass, gold, gems, spices, and — most consequentially — **Buddhism, art styles, mathematics and astronomy**.

> ⭐ **Buddhism travelled the Silk Route** with merchants and monks into Central Asia, then **China, Korea and Japan**. Monasteries were established along the route as **rest stops for traders** — a symbiosis in which merchants funded monasteries and monasteries provided safe lodging, credit and legitimacy. **The Bamiyan Buddhas** in Afghanistan (destroyed 2001) stood on this route.

## 7️⃣ 👑 THE KUSHANAS AND KANISHKA

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | A branch of the **Yuezhi**, a Central Asian people |
| **Empire** | Central Asia and north-west India — Afghanistan, Pakistan, north India down to the Ganga valley |
| **Two capitals** ⭐⭐ | **PESHAWAR (Purushapura)** and **MATHURA** |
| **Greatest ruler** | **KANISHKA** ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **Era** ⭐⭐ | Kanishka began the **SAKA ERA in 78 CE** — ⭐ **this is INDIA'S NATIONAL CALENDAR (the Saka Samvat), adopted 22 March 1957** ⭐ |
| **Religion** | Patronised **Buddhism** (especially **Mahayana**); his coins also show Greek, Persian, Zoroastrian and Indian deities — an eclectic, cosmopolitan pantheon |
| **Fourth Buddhist Council** ⭐⭐ | Convened by Kanishka in **Kashmir (Kundalvana)**; presided over by **Vasumitra**; **Ashvaghosha** as deputy; Buddhism split formally into **Hinayana and Mahayana**; texts compiled in **Sanskrit** |
| **Coins** ⭐ | **GOLD coins** of high purity, bearing his image and a wide range of deities — evidence of enormous trade wealth |
| **Court figures** ⭐⭐ | **ASHVAGHOSHA** (poet; wrote ***Buddhacharita***, a life of the Buddha, and ***Saundarananda***), **CHARAKA** (the great physician; ***Charaka Samhita***), **NAGARJUNA** (Mahayana philosopher; founder of the *Madhyamaka*/Shunyavada school), **VASUMITRA**, **Sushruta** (associated) |
| **Earlier Kushana** | **Kujula Kadphises**, **Vima Kadphises** (first to issue gold coins) |

> ⭐⭐ **The Saka era = India's national calendar** is a genuinely high-value cross-syllabus fact (History → Polity → General Awareness). India's official calendar, used in the *Gazette of India* and by All India Radio, is the **Saka Samvat**, whose year 1 is **78 CE** — the era conventionally associated with Kanishka. **Adopted alongside the Gregorian calendar on 22 March 1957.**

### 🎨 Gandhara vs Mathura — The Art & Culture Comparison ⭐⭐⭐

Under the Kushanas, the **first images of the Buddha in human form** were made (ending the aniconic phase of Chapter 6). Two schools emerged, and **comparing them is a standard question.**

| | **GANDHARA SCHOOL** | **MATHURA SCHOOL** |
|---|---|---|
| **Region** | North-west (Taxila, Peshawar, Swat, Afghanistan) | **Mathura**, UP |
| **Influence** | ⭐ **GRECO-ROMAN (Hellenistic)** — hence "Greco-Buddhist" | ⭐ **INDIGENOUS Indian** — descended from yaksha figures |
| **Material** | **Grey/blue-grey schist** (and stucco) | ⭐ **Spotted RED SANDSTONE** (from Sikri) |
| **Buddha's face** | Sharp, **Apollo-like** features; wavy hair; moustache sometimes | Rounder, fuller, **smiling**; shaven head or *ushnisha* |
| **Drapery** | **Thick, heavy, with realistic folds** (like a Roman toga), covering both shoulders | **Thin, clinging, transparent**, folds shown lightly; right shoulder often bare |
| **Mood** | **Spiritual, meditative, calm** | **Energetic, robust, muscular, joyful** |
| **Body** | Anatomically naturalistic, muscular in a Greek idiom | Broad-shouldered, powerful, less anatomically naturalistic |
| **Subjects** | Mainly the **Buddha and Bodhisattvas** | Buddha, **and also** Jain tirthankaras, Hindu deities, **yakshis**, and **royal portraits** (the famous headless **Kanishka statue**) |
| **Patrons** | Kushanas (esp. Kanishka), Shakas | Kushanas |

> ⭐ **The third school to know: AMARAVATI** (Andhra Pradesh, on the Krishna; under the **Satavahanas**). **White marble/limestone**; narrative relief panels teeming with slender, dynamic, expressive figures; **Jataka stories** in stone. It influenced **Sri Lankan and Southeast Asian art**.
>
> 🧠 **Three-way hook: "Gandhara = GREY schist + GREEK. Mathura = RED sandstone + INDIGENOUS. Amaravati = WHITE marble + NARRATIVE."** Colour-code them; it works.

### The Satavahanas (worth knowing alongside)

- Ruled the **Deccan** (c. 1st century BCE – 2nd century CE); capital **Pratishthana (Paithan)**
- Greatest ruler: **Gautamiputra Satakarni** ⭐ — known from the **Nasik prashasti** issued by his mother **Gautami Balashri**
- Patronised **Buddhism** — the **Amaravati** stupa and the **Karle, Bhaja, Nasik** cave complexes
- ⭐ Notable for **matronymics** (kings named after their mothers — *Gautami*putra, *Vasishthi*putra), which is socially significant and often asked

## 8️⃣ 🚶 The Chinese Pilgrims ⭐⭐

Three Chinese Buddhist monks travelled to India and left invaluable accounts. **Learn who came when and under whom.**

| Pilgrim | When | Ruler in India | Came for | Wrote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FA XIAN (Fa-Hien)** ⭐⭐ | **c. 399–414 CE** | **Chandragupta II** (Gupta) | Buddhist texts, esp. the **Vinaya** | *Fo-Kuo-Ki* ("Record of Buddhist Kingdoms") |
| **XUAN ZANG (Hiuen Tsang)** ⭐⭐⭐ | **c. 630–645 CE** | **Harshavardhana** | Study at **Nalanda**; collect texts | *Si-Yu-Ki* ("Records of the Western World") |
| **I-TSING (Yijing)** ⭐ | **c. 671–695 CE** | Post-Harsha | Study at **Nalanda** | Accounts of Buddhist practice in India and the archipelago |

> ⭐ **Xuan Zang is the most important.** He studied at **Nalanda** for years under **Shilabhadra**, travelled the length of India, and his account is our single richest description of 7th-century India — cities, roads, monasteries, customs, and Harsha's administration. He is the basis of the Chinese classic *Journey to the West*.
>
> 🎯 **A Chapter 1 callback:** these are **foreign literary sources** — outsiders describing India, so they notice what insiders take for granted. **But they were Buddhist monks**, so they emphasise Buddhism and may understate the vitality of other traditions. **Naming that bias in a Mains answer shows source-criticism.**

## 9️⃣ 🙏 New Religious Developments

### The rise of BHAKTI ⭐⭐

Alongside all this trade and empire, a major religious shift was underway.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Bhakti** | **Devotion** — a personal, loving, direct relationship between devotee and deity |
| **The key idea** ⭐ | **No priest, no elaborate ritual, no sacrifice, and NO BARRIER OF CASTE OR GENDER** is needed. The devotee approaches the deity directly |
| **Deities** | **Shiva, Vishnu** (and his incarnations, esp. **Krishna** and **Rama**), and goddesses — **Durga** and other forms of the Devi |
| **Key text** ⭐⭐ | The **BHAGAVAD GITA** (part of the *Mahabharata*) — Krishna teaches Arjuna that devotion and duty, rather than ritual, are the path |
| **Consequence** ⭐ | **TEMPLES** and **IMAGES** become central to worship (rather than the fire altar). This drives the temple architecture of Ch.9–10 |
| **Later flowering** | The **Alvars** (Vishnu) and **Nayanars** (Shiva) in Tamil country from ~6th century CE, and then the great medieval Bhakti movement |

> 🎯 **Why Bhakti mattered socially:** it offered spiritual access to those excluded by Vedic ritual — **women and lower castes could be devotees**, and some became celebrated saints. It's a recurring GS1 theme (social reform through religion) and connects forward to the medieval Bhakti-Sufi movements. **Also note it's part of the answer to "why did Buddhism decline" (Ch.6) — Bhakti offered comparable accessibility from within the Hindu fold.**

### ⛪ Christianity arrives ⭐

- Tradition holds that **St Thomas the Apostle** arrived on the **Malabar coast (Kerala) around 52 CE**, establishing Christian communities
- The **Syrian Christians / St Thomas Christians** of Kerala trace their origin to this
- ⭐ **The route was the trade route** — Christianity reached India by the same sea lanes that carried pepper to Rome

> 🎯 **A neat, high-value observation:** India had Christian communities **centuries before most of Europe was Christianised.** It's a strong point for answers on India's religious pluralism and its ancient cosmopolitan connections. **Judaism (the Cochin Jews) and later Islam and Zoroastrianism (Parsis) arrived along the same Malabar trade routes.**

### 🕉️ Also in this period

- **Hinduism** as we recognise it takes shape — image worship, temples, the Puranas being compiled
- **Mahayana Buddhism** develops the **Bodhisattva** ideal and Buddha images (Ch.6)
- Buddhism and Jainism continue to receive **merchant and guild patronage** — inscriptions at Sanchi, Bharhut, Karle and Amaravati record donations by traders, artisans and their guilds

## 🔁 2-MINUTE RECAP

1. **Iron ploughshare + paddy transplantation + irrigation** produced surplus — but the gains went to landowners while the labour came from slaves and the landless. **Progress widened inequality.**
2. **Tamil villages:** **vellalar** (large landowners) → **uzhavar** (ploughmen) → **kadaisiyar/adimai** (landless/slaves).
3. **Northern villages:** **grama bhojaka** (hereditary headman — landowner + tax collector + judge + policeman), **grihapati**, **dasa karmakara**.
4. **Sangam Age (300 BCE–300 CE):** Tamil, **secular**, patronised by the **Pandyas at Madurai**; ~473 poets including ~30 women (**Avvaiyar**).
5. **Texts:** *Tolkappiyam* (Tolkappiyar), *Tirukkural* (**Thiruvalluvar**), *Silappadikaram* (**Ilango Adigal**), *Manimekalai* (**Sattanar**).
6. **Three kingdoms:** **Chera** (Vanji, Muziris, **bow**), **Chola** (Uraiyur, Puhar, **tiger**), **Pandya** (Madurai, Korkai, **fish**). **Karikala Chola** built the **Kallanai**.
7. **Tinai** — five poetic eco-zones; **aham/puram** thematic split.
8. **Town archaeology:** **ring wells**, **NBPW**, **punch-marked coins** (symbols, no names).
9. **Shrenis (guilds)** trained, procured, set standards, **acted as banks paying interest**, and made recorded donations — putting non-royal people into the historical record.
10. **Arikamedu:** **amphorae, Arretine ware, rouletted ware**, warehouse, bead-making — the Indo-Roman trading station.
11. **India exported pepper ("black gold"), muslin, pearls, gems; imported Roman GOLD.** **Pliny**: India is **"the sink of the world's gold."**
12. **Hippalus** and the **monsoon winds** → direct Arabian Sea crossing → trade volume explodes. See the ***Periplus***.
13. **Silk Route** carried silk, horses, gems — **and Buddhism, art styles and mathematics** into Central Asia, China, Korea, Japan.
14. **Kushanas** controlled the north-west stretch and grew rich. **Kanishka:** capitals **Peshawar and Mathura**; **Saka era 78 CE = India's national calendar**; **Fourth Buddhist Council** in Kashmir (Vasumitra) → Hinayana/Mahayana split; gold coins; court of **Ashvaghosha, Charaka, Nagarjuna**.
15. **First Buddha images** appear now. **Gandhara** = grey schist, Greco-Roman, heavy folds, meditative. **Mathura** = **red sandstone**, indigenous, thin clinging drapery, energetic. **Amaravati** = white marble, narrative, Satavahana.
16. **Satavahanas:** **Gautamiputra Satakarni**, **Nasik prashasti**, **matronymics**.
17. **Chinese pilgrims:** **Fa Xian** (Chandragupta II), **Xuan Zang** (Harsha, Nalanda), **I-Tsing**.
18. **Bhakti** rises — direct devotion, **no priest or caste barrier**, **Bhagavad Gita**, temples and images become central.
19. **St Thomas** reaches **Kerala c. 52 CE** — Christianity arrives by the pepper route.
