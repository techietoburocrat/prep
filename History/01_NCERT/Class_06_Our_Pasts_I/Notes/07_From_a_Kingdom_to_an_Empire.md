## 🎬 Start Here: The Most Extraordinary Thing Any King Ever Did

Every empire in history announces its victories. Rock Edict XIII announces something else entirely:

> *"When he had been consecrated eight years, the Beloved of the Gods, the king Piyadassi, conquered Kalinga. **A hundred and fifty thousand people were deported, a hundred thousand were killed and many times that number perished.** Afterwards, now that Kalinga was annexed, the Beloved of the Gods very earnestly practised Dhamma, desired Dhamma, and taught Dhamma. **On conquering Kalinga the Beloved of the Gods felt remorse**, for, when an independent country is conquered, the slaughter, death, and deportation of the people is extremely grievous to the Beloved of the Gods, and weighs heavily on his mind..."*

**Read that again, and notice what is happening.**

```
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  WHAT EVERY OTHER ANCIENT KING CARVED IN STONE:                    │
   │                                                                    │
   │     "I conquered. I destroyed. I am glorious. Fear me."            │
   │                                                                    │
   ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │  WHAT ASHOKA CARVED IN STONE:                                      │
   │                                                                    │
   │     "I conquered. I counted the dead. I WAS WRONG.                 │
   │      I will not do it again. Here is what I will do instead."      │
   │                                                                    │
   │  ...and then he PUBLISHED IT across his entire empire,             │
   │  IN THE LANGUAGES HIS SUBJECTS SPOKE, permanently, in rock.        │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> 🎯 **H. G. Wells's often-quoted line:** *"Amidst the tens of thousands of names of monarchs that crowd the columns of history... the name of Ashoka shines, and shines almost alone, a star."* **Keep this for an Ethics or Essay answer** — but pair it with the critical view further down, because uncritical hagiography is weaker than balanced assessment.

> 💻 **What Ashoka actually built is a public, immutable, distributed, multilingual communication layer for state policy.** Content authored once at the centre, replicated to ~40 locations across the subcontinent, **localised into the local script and language**, engraved on the most durable medium available, and sited deliberately at high-traffic nodes — trade routes, pilgrimage centres, river crossings. **It is state communication designed for permanence, reach and comprehension.** Two thousand three hundred years later, we are still reading the deployment.

## 1️⃣ Sources — Where Mauryan History Comes From

Chapter 1's lesson pays off here: this is the first Indian empire where we have **abundant and varied** sources.

| Source | Author | Value | ⚠️ Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ASHOKAN EDICTS** ⭐⭐⭐ | **Ashoka himself** | **The gold standard.** Contemporary, unaltered, in the king's own voice. Our single best source | They are **royal propaganda** — they say what Ashoka *wanted* known. Absence of a topic ≠ absence of the thing |
| **ARTHASHASTRA** ⭐⭐⭐ | **Kautilya / Chanakya / Vishnugupta** | A detailed treatise on statecraft, economy, law, espionage, diplomacy (*saptanga* theory, *mandala* theory) | ⚠️ It is a **prescriptive manual of how a state *should* work**, not a description of the Maurya state. Its date is debated (later recensions exist). **Do not treat it as a Mauryan census** |
| **INDICA** ⭐⭐ | **Megasthenes**, Seleucus Nikator's ambassador at Chandragupta's court | Eyewitness account of Pataliputra, the army, the administration, society | ⚠️ **Survives only in fragments quoted by later Greek writers** (Arrian, Strabo, Diodorus). Contains clear errors — e.g. his claim that Indian society had **seven castes** (not four), and that there was no slavery |
| **MUDRARAKSHASA** ⭐ | **Vishakhadatta** | Sanskrit play on Chandragupta's rise and Chanakya's stratagems | Written ~700 years later. **Literary, not historical** |
| **Buddhist texts** | *Dipavamsa*, *Mahavamsa* (Sri Lankan chronicles), *Divyavadana*, *Ashokavadana* | Ashoka's Buddhism, the Third Council, Mahinda's mission | Devotional; they portray Ashoka as an ideal Buddhist king |
| **Jaina texts** | *Parishishtaparvan* | Chandragupta's Jainism and his end at Shravanabelagola | Sectarian |
| **Puranas** | — | Dynastic lists and regnal years | Later compilation |
| **Archaeology** | Pataliputra excavations (Kumrahar), NBPW pottery, punch-marked coins | Material confirmation | — |

> 🎯 **The *Arthashastra* caveat is genuinely important and separates good answers from average ones.** Students routinely write "Mauryan administration had X because the *Arthashastra* says so." **Correct framing:** *"The Arthashastra, though prescriptive and of debated date, describes an elaborate bureaucratic apparatus which likely reflects Mauryan practice in broad outline."* That hedge is worth marks.

### Chandragupta Maurya — the founder

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Came to power** | c. **321 BCE**, overthrowing **Dhana Nanda** of Magadha, guided by **Chanakya (Kautilya/Vishnugupta)** |
| **Extent** | From Afghanistan to Bengal, and south into the Deccan — the first genuinely subcontinental Indian empire |
| **Seleucus Nikator** ⭐ | Alexander's general; defeated by Chandragupta around **305 BCE**. Treaty: Seleucus **ceded** Kabul (Paropanisadae), Kandahar (Arachosia), Herat (Aria) and Baluchistan (Gedrosia); Chandragupta gave **500 war elephants** |
| **Megasthenes** ⭐ | Seleucus's ambassador; wrote ***Indica*** |
| **His end** ⭐ | Jaina tradition: he abdicated, embraced Jainism, accompanied **Bhadrabahu** south to **Shravanabelagola (Karnataka)**, and died by ***sallekhana*** (Ch.6 link) |

> 🎯 **The Seleucus episode deserves emphasis:** an Indian ruler defeated one of Alexander's own generals and **gained territory in modern Afghanistan.** That's a significant, concrete fact and a corrective to the notion that Alexander's campaign left India subordinate.

## 3️⃣ Administering an Empire — And Why It Was Uneven

**NCERT's central analytical point in this chapter: the empire was NOT governed uniformly.** This is a genuinely sophisticated idea for a Class 6 book, and UPSC likes it.

```
  ╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
  ║        THREE ZONES OF MAURYAN CONTROL — decreasing tightness        ║
  ╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  ZONE 1: THE CAPITAL REGION — PATALIPUTRA and around                │
  │  ████████████████████████████  DIRECT, TIGHT CONTROL                │
  │  • Ruled directly by the king and his ministers                     │
  │  • Full bureaucracy: tax collection, judiciary, spies, army         │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                                                        │
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  ZONE 2: THE PROVINCES — 4 (or 5) provincial capitals               │
  │  ████████████████  GOVERNED BY ROYAL PRINCES (KUMARAS)              │
  │                                                                     │
  │     ★ TAXILA        (north-west)                                    │
  │     ★ UJJAIN        (west) — Ashoka was governor here as a prince   │
  │     ★ SUVARNAGIRI   (south — "golden mountain," near gold mines)    │
  │     ★ TOSALI        (east — in Kalinga, after the conquest)         │
  │     (★ Kalinga/Samapa sometimes counted separately)                 │
  │                                                                     │
  │  • Local customs and laws often continued                           │
  │  • Central control was real but looser                              │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                                                        │
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  ZONE 3: OUTLYING & FOREST AREAS                                    │
  │  ████  LOOSE / INDIRECT CONTROL                                     │
  │  • The empire mainly wanted RESOURCES: timber, elephants,           │
  │    honey, wax, precious stones — and safe passage along ROADS       │
  │  • ATAVIKA (forest) peoples were largely left alone, but WARNED     │
  │    (Rock Edict XIII explicitly tells forest tribes to reform or     │
  │     face consequences — a rare hard edge in Ashoka's rhetoric)      │
  │  • Control was concentrated along ROADS and RIVERS, not spread      │
  │    evenly over territory                                            │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> 💻 **This is not a uniformly provisioned system — it's a core with tightly-managed nodes, a middle tier of delegated regional control, and an edge where you only maintain the routes and extract the resources you need.** The map looks like a solid block; the actual control was a **network of corridors and hubs**, not an area. **Say this in a Mains answer and it reads as genuine understanding rather than memorised extent.**

### Officials and machinery

| Office | Function |
|---|---|
| **Mantri Parishad** | Council of ministers |
| **Kumara / Aryaputra** ⭐ | **Royal princes as provincial governors** |
| **Mahamatta / Mahamatra** ⭐ | High officers with varied duties |
| **DHAMMA MAHAMATTA** ⭐⭐⭐ | **A new office created by Ashoka** — officers to teach, propagate and supervise *dhamma*, look after the welfare of women, prisoners, the aged, and various sects |
| **Rajuka** ⭐ | Officers in charge of rural administration and justice; Ashoka gave them **independence in judicial matters** (Pillar Edict IV) |
| **Yukta** | Subordinate revenue officials |
| **Pradeshika** | District-level officers; conducted **quinquennial tours** (every 5 years) |
| **Samaharta / Sannidhata** | Chief revenue collector / treasurer (*Arthashastra*) |
| **Nagaraka** | City administrator |
| **Adhyaksha** | Superintendents of departments |
| **Gupta-charas / Spies** ⭐ | An extensive espionage network (*Arthashastra*) |

**Megasthenes on Pataliputra** ⭐: he describes a city on the Ganga, ~**9 miles long and 1.5 miles wide**, surrounded by a **wooden palisade with 570 towers and 64 gates**, and a deep moat. He describes a **municipal committee of 30 members in 6 boards of 5**, handling crafts, foreigners, births/deaths, trade, manufactured goods, and sales tax. He also describes a **war office of 30 in 6 boards** (infantry, cavalry, elephants, chariots, navy, transport). *(Archaeology at Kumrahar has confirmed wooden palisade remains.)*

**The army:** Pliny, citing Megasthenes, gives Chandragupta **600,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry, 9,000 elephants**. Treat these numbers as impressive but not precise.

**Roads:** the **Uttarapatha** ran from the north-west to Pataliputra and on to Tamralipti — the ancestor of the Grand Trunk Road. Ashoka claims to have planted **banyan trees for shade**, dug **wells**, built **rest-houses (*dharmashalas*)** and planted **medicinal herbs** along the roads.

## 4️⃣ ⚔️ The Kalinga War (261 BCE) — The Turning Point

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **When** | c. **261 BCE**, in Ashoka's **8th regnal year** |
| **Where** | **Kalinga** — modern coastal **Odisha** |
| **Why** | Kalinga controlled the **eastern coastal trade route** to South India and Southeast Asia, and had remained independent. It was strategically and commercially valuable |
| **The cost** ⭐⭐ | By **Ashoka's own account** in **Rock Edict XIII**: **100,000 killed, 150,000 deported, and "many times that number perished"** |
| **The result** ⭐ | **Remorse (*anusochana*)** — and a public renunciation of war as an instrument of policy |
| **Where it's recorded** ⭐⭐ | **Major Rock Edict XIII** |

> ⭐ **The detail that historians find most telling:** **Rock Edict XIII — the Kalinga edict — is deliberately NOT inscribed in Kalinga itself.** In Kalinga, two **Separate Rock Edicts** were installed instead (at **Dhauli** and **Jaugada**), addressed to the officials there and concerned with the just and humane treatment of the newly conquered people (*"All men are my children"*).
>
> **Why?** Almost certainly because publishing a triumphal casualty count in the conquered province would have inflamed it. **Ashoka's communication was audience-aware — he varied the message by region.** That's a genuinely sharp observation and a strong point for a Mains answer.

**After Kalinga:** *"Bherighosha"* (the sound of the war drum) was replaced by *"Dhammaghosha"* (the sound of dhamma) — a phrase worth quoting.

> ⚠️ **A necessary critical note:** Ashoka **did not dismantle the empire, disband the army, or restore Kalinga's independence.** He renounced *further* conquest. He also retained capital punishment (though he granted a three-day respite to the condemned) and issued warnings to the forest peoples. **A balanced Mains answer states both the sincerity of the transformation and its limits.** That balance scores better than either uncritical praise or cynicism.

## 5️⃣ ☸️ Ashoka's DHAMMA — Be Precise Here

**The single most misunderstood topic in this chapter.**

> ⚠️ ***Dhamma* (Prakrit for Sanskrit *dharma*) was NOT Buddhism.** Ashoka was personally a Buddhist, but the *dhamma* he propagated through the edicts was a **broad, non-sectarian code of ethical and social conduct** intended for **all his subjects, of every faith**. The edicts do **not** mention the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, nirvana, or ask anyone to become Buddhist.

### What dhamma actually asked of people

| Principle | In practice |
|---|---|
| **Respect for elders** | Obedience to parents, teachers and elders |
| **Respect for brahmanas and shramanas** ⭐ | Note: **BOTH** — the orthodox priesthood *and* the heterodox renunciants |
| **Kindness to servants and slaves** | Humane treatment of dependants |
| **Ahimsa / non-injury to living beings** | Restrictions on animal slaughter; Ashoka reduced killing in the royal kitchen |
| **TOLERANCE of all sects** ⭐⭐ | **Rock Edict XII** — the great edict on religious tolerance |
| **Generosity (*dana*)** | Giving to shramanas, brahmanas, the poor |
| **Truthfulness, purity, gentleness** | Personal virtue |
| **Little sin, many good deeds** | The general formula |

> ⭐⭐ **Major Rock Edict XII deserves to be known almost verbatim** — it is one of the earliest state declarations of religious tolerance anywhere in the world:
>
> *"One should not honour only one's own sect and condemn the sects of others... **He who honours his own sect and condemns the sects of others** wholly from devotion to his own sect... **injures his own sect very severely.** Concord alone is commendable... let all listen and be willing to listen to the doctrines professed by others."*
>
> Note the *argument*, not just the sentiment: **sectarian aggression harms your own sect.** It is a reasoned case for tolerance, not merely a pious wish. **This is prime GS4 Ethics and Essay material** — quote it for answers on secularism, tolerance, pluralism and communal harmony.

### How Ashoka propagated dhamma

1. **INSCRIPTIONS** ⭐ — carved on rocks and pillars across the empire, **in local languages and scripts**
2. **DHAMMA MAHAMATTAS** ⭐⭐ — a **new cadre of officers** created specifically to spread dhamma and oversee welfare
3. **DHAMMA YATRAS** ⭐ — the king replaced royal pleasure tours (*vihara yatras*, i.e. hunting) with **tours to meet people, teach dhamma and give gifts**
4. **Public works** — wells, rest houses, shade trees, **medical treatment for humans AND animals** (Rock Edict II mentions medicinal herbs and treatment provided even beyond his borders, in the Chola, Pandya, Satiyaputra, Keralaputra and Tamraparni territories, and in Antiochus's realm) ⭐
5. **Missions abroad** ⭐ — Rock Edict XIII names five Hellenistic kings to whom he sent envoys: **Antiochus II (Syria), Ptolemy II (Egypt), Antigonus (Macedon), Magas (Cyrene), Alexander (Epirus)**; missions also went to **Sri Lanka (Mahinda and Sanghamitta — his son and daughter), Suvarnabhumi (Southeast Asia), Kashmir, Gandhara**
6. **Restricted animal sacrifice and festive gatherings (*samajas*)** involving killing
7. **Third Buddhist Council** at Pataliputra under **Moggaliputta Tissa**

> 🌏 **Rock Edict XIII is remarkable as a diplomatic document too** — it names contemporary Greek kings by name, which is how historians **anchor Mauryan chronology to the well-dated Hellenistic world.** Without those five names, our dating of Ashoka would be far shakier. **A beautiful example of Chapter 1's cross-source dating in action.**

## 6️⃣ 🪨 The Edicts — Classification, Languages, Decipherment

### Classification

| Type | Number | Where | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MAJOR ROCK EDICTS** ⭐ | **14** | On large boulders at ~8–10 sites | The core dhamma programme |
| **MINOR ROCK EDICTS** | Several | ~15+ sites | Ashoka's personal commitment to Buddhism; **the only ones naming him "ASHOKA"** ⭐ |
| **MAJOR PILLAR EDICTS** ⭐ | **7** | On polished sandstone pillars | Later in his reign; review of dhamma policy |
| **MINOR PILLAR EDICTS** | — | Sarnath, Sanchi, Allahabad, Rummindei, Nigalisagar | Schism edicts; commemorative |
| **CAVE INSCRIPTIONS** ⭐ | — | **Barabar caves (Bihar)** | Donated to the **AJIVIKAS** ⭐ |
| **SEPARATE ROCK EDICTS** ⭐ | 2 | **Dhauli** and **Jaugada** (Kalinga) | Instructions on humane administration of the conquered |

### Key sites

| Site | Location | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Girnar** ⭐ | Junagadh, Gujarat | Major Rock Edicts. **Same rock also bears the later Rudradaman and Skandagupta inscriptions** about the **Sudarshana lake** ⭐ |
| **Dhauli** ⭐ | Odisha | Separate Rock Edicts; famous **rock-cut ELEPHANT** sculpture |
| **Jaugada** | Odisha | Separate Rock Edicts |
| **Kalsi** | Uttarakhand | Major Rock Edicts |
| **Shahbazgarhi & Mansehra** ⭐ | Pakistan (NW) | Major Rock Edicts — **in KHAROSHTHI script** ⭐ |
| **Sopara** | Maharashtra | Major Rock Edicts |
| **Yerragudi** | Andhra Pradesh | Major + Minor Rock Edicts |
| **Maski, Gujarra, Nettur, Udegolam** ⭐ | Karnataka / MP | **Minor Rock Edicts that name "ASHOKA" directly** ⭐ |
| **Kandahar** ⭐⭐ | **Afghanistan** | **Bilingual GREEK–ARAMAIC** inscription |
| **Sarnath** ⭐⭐⭐ | UP | The **LION CAPITAL** pillar; schism edict |
| **Sanchi** | MP | Pillar; schism edict |
| **Rummindei (Lumbini)** ⭐ | Nepal | Pillar recording Ashoka's visit; **tax on the village reduced to 1/8** ⭐ |
| **Nigalisagar** | Nepal | Pillar; enlargement of the stupa of Buddha Konakamana |
| **Topra & Meerut** | Delhi | Pillars **moved to Delhi by Firoz Shah Tughlaq** in the 14th century ⭐ |
| **Allahabad-Kosam** | UP | Pillar bearing Ashoka's edicts **and** Samudragupta's later **prashasti** (Ch.9) ⭐ |
| **Lauriya-Nandangarh, Lauriya-Araraj, Rampurva** | Bihar | Pillars with animal capitals |
| **Barabar caves** ⭐ | Bihar | Donated to the **Ajivikas**; **earliest rock-cut caves in India**; mirror-polished interiors |

### Languages and scripts ⭐⭐

| Script | Where | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **BRAHMI** ⭐ | Most of the subcontinent | Left to right. **The ancestor of nearly all Indian scripts** (Devanagari, Tamil, Bengali, Telugu, Kannada, and also Tibetan, Thai, Burmese) |
| **KHAROSHTHI** ⭐ | **North-west** (Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra) | **RIGHT to LEFT** (derived from Aramaic) |
| **GREEK** ⭐ | **Kandahar, Afghanistan** | — |
| **ARAMAIC** ⭐ | **Kandahar, Taxila, Laghman** | — |

**Language:** overwhelmingly **PRAKRIT** (the spoken vernacular), *not* Sanskrit — the same accessibility principle you saw with the Buddha in Chapter 6.

> ⭐ **The Kandahar bilingual (Greek + Aramaic) is a superb detail.** In a region where people spoke Greek and Aramaic, Ashoka **localised his message into their languages.** That is deliberate, audience-aware statecraft — and it also proves the reach of Mauryan authority into Afghanistan.

### 🔓 Decipherment — the Chapter 1 payoff

By the medieval period **nobody could read Brahmi.** Firoz Shah Tughlaq moved two Ashokan pillars to Delhi in the 14th century and had scholars try — none could.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Deciphered by** ⭐⭐ | **JAMES PRINSEP**, in **1837** — an official of the East India Company's mint in Calcutta and secretary of the Asiatic Society |
| **How** | He worked from short donative inscriptions on Sanchi's railings, many ending in the same two characters — correctly guessed to be *"danam"* (gift) — and worked outward |
| **The twist** ⭐ | Prinsep could now read the edicts, but they were issued by a king calling himself **"Devanampiya Piyadassi"** — a name matching no known king. It was only later, when **Minor Rock Edicts at Maski (1915), and subsequently Gujarra, Nettur and Udegolam**, were found naming **"Ashoka" directly**, that the identification was confirmed against the Sri Lankan chronicles |

> 🎯 **Two facts here get asked constantly: James Prinsep, 1837; and Maski as the edict that names Ashoka.** Also note the elegance of the story — **one man's decipherment recovered an entire emperor whom Indian tradition had almost forgotten.** Ashoka's reputation in modern India is, in a real sense, a 19th-century archaeological recovery.

## 7️⃣ 🦁 Mauryan Art — Ch.10 Preview, But Learn It Here

**Mauryan art is a Mains favourite. Do not skip this.**

### The pillars

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Material** | **Chunar sandstone** (from near Varanasi) — quarried at one place and **transported hundreds of kilometres** ⭐ |
| **Height** | Typically **12–15 m**; weight **~50 tonnes** |
| **Structure** | **MONOLITHIC** — shaft carved from a **single stone** ⭐; separate capital on top |
| **The polish** ⭐⭐ | A brilliant, glass-like **"Mauryan polish"** — a surface finish whose technique remains **not fully understood** and which was **not replicated in later periods** |
| **Parts** | **Shaft** (plain, tapering) → **Inverted lotus/bell capital** → **Abacus** (drum with animal/floral reliefs) → **Crowning animal** (lion, bull, elephant, horse) |

**Other capitals:** the **Rampurva Bull**, the **Lauriya-Nandangarh Lion**, the **Sankissa Elephant**, the **Rampurva Lion**.

### 🕳️ The Barabar Caves ⭐

- Near Gaya, Bihar. **The earliest surviving rock-cut caves in India**
- Excavated under **Ashoka** and his grandson **Dasharatha**, and donated to the **AJIVIKAS** (Ch.6 link)
- Famous for the **mirror-like Mauryan polish** on the interior granite walls
- **Lomas Rishi** cave has a facade imitating a **wooden thatched hut** in stone — showing the transition from wooden to stone architecture ⭐
- *(Also the "Marabar Caves" of E. M. Forster's* A Passage to India.*)*

> 🎯 **Ashoka, a Buddhist, donated caves to the Ajivikas.** That single fact is the strongest material evidence of the tolerance he preached in Rock Edict XII. **Use it as your example when asked to show that Ashoka's tolerance was practised, not merely proclaimed.**

**Also Mauryan:** the **Didarganj Yakshi** (a polished chauri-bearer statue from Patna — though its date is debated between Mauryan and later), and the original brick core of the **Sanchi Stupa**, which Ashoka built (later enlarged by the Shungas → Ch.10).

## 8️⃣ 📉 Decline of the Mauryan Empire

Ashoka died c. **232 BCE**; the empire ended in **185 BCE** — barely **50 years** later. Why so fast?

| Cause | Argument | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Weak successors** ⭐ | No ruler of comparable ability after Ashoka; the empire was reportedly partitioned among heirs | Strong — a highly centralised system depends on a capable centre |
| **2. Over-centralisation** ⭐ | Everything depended on the king personally; there was no institutional continuity independent of him | Strong. **The system had a single point of failure** |
| **3. Vast size, slow communication** | Managing Afghanistan to Bengal with pre-modern communications was inherently fragile | Strong |
| **4. Financial strain** | An enormous army and bureaucracy, plus lavish donations to sects and extensive public works, strained the treasury | Plausible |
| **5. Ashoka's pacifism** ⭐ | *(H. P. Sastri's view)* The renunciation of war weakened the army's edge | ⚠️ **Contested.** Ashoka never disbanded the army; the empire held for decades after him. **Present it as a view, not a fact** |
| **6. Brahmanical reaction** ⭐ | *(H. P. Sastri)* Ashoka's ban on sacrifice and support for heterodoxy antagonised brahmanas; **Pushyamitra Shunga**, who killed the last Maurya, was himself a brahmana who revived the **Ashvamedha** | Debated. Ashoka's edicts explicitly enjoin respect for brahmanas, which cuts against a simple anti-brahmanical reading |
| **7. Provincial oppression** ⭐ | Ashoka's own edicts record **revolts at Taxila** (which he was sent to quell as a prince) caused by misrule by officials | Well-evidenced from the edicts themselves |
| **8. Foreign invasions** | Bactrian Greeks pressing the north-west after Ashoka | Contributory |
| **9. Economic factors** *(D. D. Kosambi)* | Currency debasement and fiscal pressure | Debated |

**The end:** in **185 BCE**, the last Maurya, **Brihadratha**, was **assassinated during a military review by his own commander-in-chief, Pushyamitra Shunga**, who founded the **Shunga dynasty**.

> 🎯 **The best framing for a Mains answer:** *"The Mauryan empire's rapid collapse reflects the structural fragility of a highly centralised, personality-dependent polity rather than any single cause; the absence of institutional mechanisms capable of surviving a weak monarch proved decisive."* **Then** add the debated factors with attribution.

## ⚖️ Assessing Ashoka — The Balanced View

Because uncritical praise reads as unsophisticated, here is both sides.

| ✅ The case for | ⚠️ The case for caution |
|---|---|
| Publicly acknowledged the horror of his own war — **unprecedented in ancient kingship** | He **kept** Kalinga; he did not restore its independence |
| Institutionalised welfare: hospitals, wells, roads, shade trees, veterinary care | **Retained capital punishment** (with a three-day respite) |
| **Rock Edict XII** — a reasoned, early state doctrine of religious tolerance | **Warned forest peoples** of consequences in RE XIII — coercion remained available |
| Practised tolerance materially — **Barabar caves to the Ajivikas** | The edicts are **his own account**; we lack an independent check |
| Localised his message into subjects' languages and scripts | Dhamma may also have served as an **integrative ideology** for a diverse empire — pragmatic as well as moral |
| Sent missions that carried Buddhism across Asia — a lasting civilisational effect | The empire collapsed within 50 years of his death |

> 🎯 **The mature conclusion:** *"Ashoka's dhamma was simultaneously a genuine ethical commitment and an instrument of imperial integration; the two readings are not mutually exclusive, and the evidence supports both."* **That sentence will serve you in any Ashoka question.**

## 🔁 2-MINUTE RECAP

1. **Chandragupta Maurya, 321 BCE**, overthrew the Nandas with **Chanakya**'s help; defeated **Seleucus Nikator** and gained Afghan territories; **Megasthenes** at his court; ended as a Jain at **Shravanabelagola**.
2. **Bindusara** ("Amitrochates") extended into the Deccan and patronised the **Ajivikas**.
3. **Ashoka (268–232 BCE)** — titles **Devanampiya Piyadassi**.
4. **Sources:** *Arthashastra* (prescriptive, contested date), *Indica* (fragmentary), *Mudrarakshasa* (literary), Buddhist chronicles — and above all **the edicts**.
5. **Administration was UNEVEN:** tight at Pataliputra, delegated to **kumaras** at **Taxila, Ujjain, Suvarnagiri, Tosali**, and loose at the forested edges — control ran along **roads and rivers**, not evenly over territory.
6. **Kalinga war, 261 BCE:** **100,000 killed, 150,000 deported**, recorded by Ashoka himself in **Major Rock Edict XIII** — and **deliberately omitted in Kalinga**, where the **Separate Edicts at Dhauli and Jaugada** were installed instead.
7. **Dhamma is NOT Buddhism** — a broad ethical code: respect for elders, for **both** brahmanas and shramanas, kindness to servants, *ahimsa*, generosity, and above all **tolerance (RE XII)**.
8. **Propagated by:** inscriptions, **dhamma mahamattas**, dhamma yatras, public works, and **missions abroad** (Mahinda and Sanghamitta to Sri Lanka; five Hellenistic kings named).
9. **Edicts:** 14 Major Rock Edicts, 7 Major Pillar Edicts, Minor Rock and Pillar Edicts, cave inscriptions. In **Prakrit**, using **Brahmi**, **Kharoshthi** (NW), and **Greek/Aramaic** (Kandahar).
10. **Deciphered by James Prinsep in 1837.** The **Maski** edict identified "Devanampiya Piyadassi" as **Ashoka**.
11. **Mauryan art:** monolithic **Chunar sandstone** pillars with the unreplicated **Mauryan polish**; the **Sarnath Lion Capital** → **State Emblem of India (26 Jan 1950)**, three lions visible, *Satyameva Jayate* from the **Mundaka Upanishad**, Ashoka Chakra on the flag.
12. **Barabar caves** — earliest rock-cut caves, donated to the **Ajivikas**; **Lomas Rishi**'s wood-imitating facade.
13. **Decline:** weak successors, over-centralisation (single point of failure), size, finances, provincial misrule (Taxila revolts), foreign pressure. **Brihadratha killed by Pushyamitra Shunga, 185 BCE.**
14. **Assess Ashoka in balance:** genuine ethical transformation *and* an integrative imperial ideology — both readings hold.
