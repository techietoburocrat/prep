> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE OPENING PARAGRAPH — it states the whole problem, so learn its shape.**
> *"**EAST ASIA at the beginning of the nineteenth century was DOMINATED BY CHINA.** The **Qing dynasty**, heir to a long tradition, **seemed secure in its power**, while **Japan, a small island country, seemed to be locked in isolation.** ⭐ **Yet, within a few decades CHINA was thrown into turmoil unable to face the colonial challenge…** and **JAPAN… was successful in building a modern nation-state, creating an industrial economy and even establishing a COLONIAL EMPIRE by incorporating TAIWAN (1895) and KOREA (1910). It defeated CHINA, the land that had been the source of its culture and ideals, in 1894, and RUSSIA, a European power, in 1905.**"*

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE PUZZLE IN ONE PICTURE

   c. 1800   CHINA -- vast, secure, the centre of East Asian
                      civilisation
             JAPAN -- small, insular, borrowing its culture from
                      China
                          |
                 THE SAME SHOCK HITS BOTH
                 (Western gunboats, unequal treaties, opium)
                          |
   ------------------------------------------------------------
   CHINA                          |  JAPAN
   - imperial government loses    |  - builds a MODERN NATION-STATE
     political control            |  - builds an INDUSTRIAL ECONOMY
   - unable to reform effectively |  - builds a COLONIAL EMPIRE
   - convulsed by CIVIL WAR       |  - DEFEATS CHINA (1894) and
   - revolution as the answer     |    RUSSIA (1905)
     (CCP victorious 1949)        |  - then war, and DEFEAT (1945)
   ------------------------------------------------------------
                          |
   ⭐⭐ AND THE TWIST AT THE END -- by the 1970s BOTH are major
      economic powers, having arrived by OPPOSITE routes:
        JAPAN -- capitalism, then defeat, then democracy
        CHINA -- revolution, then Party control, then a market
                 economy WITHOUT democracy
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Do not treat this as "Japan succeeded, China failed" — that is the schoolboy answer and NCERT explicitly refuses it.** The chapter's own title is ***PATHS* to modernisation, plural**, and its closing section is titled ***"Two Roads"***. ⭐ **Japan's success produced an aggressive empire, a repressive state at home and a legacy of hatred across Asia, ending in atomic bombs.** ⭐ **China's convulsion produced a revolution that "did remove centuries' old inequalities, spread education and raise consciousness" even as it "turned the ideals of liberation and equality into slogans to manipulate the people."** **Each path bought something and paid for it.** Write it that way.

## 1️⃣ The Political System before Meiji ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE TOKUGAWA STRUCTURE (1603-1867)

   THE EMPEROR -- ruled from KYOTO, but "by the TWELFTH CENTURY
   the imperial court LOST POWER to shoguns"
                          |
   THE SHOGUN -- "who in theory ruled IN THE NAME OF the emperor";
   from 1603 to 1867 the office was held by the TOKUGAWA family
                          |
   ⭐⭐ THE DAIMYO -- the country was divided into OVER 250 DOMAINS
   under these lords.
   ⭐ HOW THE SHOGUN CONTROLLED THEM: "ordering them to STAY AT THE
     CAPITAL EDO (modern Tokyo) FOR LONG PERIODS so that they
     WOULD NOT POSE A THREAT." He also controlled THE MAJOR CITIES
     AND MINES.
                          |
   THE SAMURAI -- "the WARRIOR CLASS... THE RULING ELITE", who
   served the shoguns and daimyo
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three late-sixteenth-century changes that "laid the pattern for future development" — this is NCERT's own exam question ("What were the major developments BEFORE the Meiji restoration that made it possible for Japan to modernise rapidly?"):**

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ ONE -- THE PEASANTRY WAS DISARMED
   "only the samurai could carry swords. This ensured PEACE AND
    ORDER, ENDING THE FREQUENT WARS OF THE PREVIOUS CENTURY."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ TWO -- THE DAIMYO WERE ORDERED TO LIVE IN THE CAPITALS OF
   THEIR DOMAINS, "each with a large degree of AUTONOMY"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THREE -- LAND SURVEYS
   "identified OWNERS AND TAXPAYERS and GRADED LAND PRODUCTIVITY
    to ensure A STABLE REVENUE BASE."
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ And what those three produced — the real answer to the question:**

| Consequence | NCERT's evidence |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The most urbanised country in the world** | *"by the mid-seventeenth century, Japan not only had **THE MOST POPULATED CITY IN THE WORLD — EDO** — but also two other large cities, **OSAKA and KYOTO**, and **at least half a dozen castle-towns with populations of over 50,000.**"* ⚠️ *"**By contrast, most European countries of the time had ONLY ONE LARGE CITY.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **A commercial economy** | *"This led to the growth of a **COMMERCIAL ECONOMY**, and created **FINANCIAL AND CREDIT SYSTEMS**"* — plus **the increased use of money** and ⭐ **the creation of a STOCK MARKET IN RICE** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Merit beginning to outrank status** | *"**A PERSON'S MERIT BEGAN TO BE MORE VALUED THAN HIS STATUS.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A reading public** | *"a **vibrant culture blossomed in the towns**, where the fast-growing class of **merchants patronised theatre and the arts.** As people enjoyed reading, it became possible for **gifted writers TO EARN A LIVING SOLELY BY WRITING.** ⭐⭐ **In Edo, people could 'RENT' A BOOK FOR THE PRICE OF A BOWL OF NOODLES.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Import substitution, already** | Japan paid for **Chinese silk and Indian textiles** in **gold and silver**, which strained the economy — so the Tokugawa **restricted the export of precious metals** and **developed the silk industry at NISHIJIN in Kyoto**, whose silk *"came to be known as **the best in the world**"* |

> ⭐⭐ **The Nishijin detail is worth keeping.** In the sixteenth century it had a weavers' guild of **31 households**; by the end of the seventeenth the community numbered **over 70,000 people**. An order of **1713** required that **only domestic yarn be used**. ⭐ Silk production created **a class of REGIONAL ENTREPRENEURS who challenged the Tokugawa order** — and when foreign trade opened in **1859**, **Japan's silk exports became a major source of profit.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This section is the single most important thing in the chapter, and most candidates skip it because it is "background".** ⭐ **Japan did not modernise in 1868 from a standing start. By the time Perry arrived it ALREADY had: internal peace, a stable land-revenue base, the largest city on earth, a monetised commercial economy with credit and a rice futures market, a merchant class that patronised the arts, mass literacy cheap enough to rent a book for the price of noodles, and a domestic silk industry built deliberately to replace imports.**
> ⭐⭐ **The Meiji reforms did not CREATE a modern society; they REMOVED THE POLITICAL LID from one that had largely already formed.** That is why they worked in decades rather than centuries. **Contrast China, where the state that had to reform was also the state whose legitimacy depended on not reforming.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐ **Hold this beside Class 10 Ch.4's account of India.** India too had commercial wealth, credit networks, skilled artisans and finer cotton than anyone. **What India lacked was not the ingredients but the SOVEREIGNTY to direct them** — its surplus was being carried away as home charges, and its policy was made in London. ⭐⭐ **The comparison Japan/China/India is really a comparison of who controlled the state.**

> ⭐⭐ **Also worth knowing — the intellectual shift.** *"the study of **ancient Japanese literature** led people to **QUESTION THE DEGREE OF CHINESE INFLUENCE** and to argue that **the essence of being Japanese could be found LONG BEFORE THE CONTACT WITH CHINA**"* — in classics like the ***Tale of the Genji*** and in the **myths that the islands were created by the gods and the emperor descended from the Sun Goddess.**
> ⭐ ***The Tale of the Genji*** — a **fictionalised diary of the Heian court** by **MURASAKI SHIKIBU**, *"the central work of fiction in Japanese literature"*. ⭐⭐ **Note the gender detail: many women writers of that period wrote in the JAPANESE script, while men wrote in the CHINESE script used for education and government** — and the novel *"shows **the independence that women had in choosing their husbands and living their lives**."*
> 🎯 **A nationalism needs a past that is its OWN.** Japanese scholars found one by reaching behind Chinese influence to a pre-Chinese Japan — exactly what Bengali historical novelists did behind colonial history *(Class 10 supplement Part C §5)*.

## 2️⃣ The Meiji Restoration ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1853 -- the USA sends COMMODORE MATTHEW PERRY (1794-1858)
   to demand a treaty permitting TRADE and DIPLOMATIC RELATIONS.
   ⭐ Japan signed THE FOLLOWING YEAR (1854).

   WHY THE USA WANTED IT:
     - "Japan lay on THE ROUTE TO CHINA which the USA saw as
        A MAJOR MARKET"
     - "their WHALING SHIPS in the Pacific needed A PLACE TO
        REFUEL"
   ⭐ At that time there was ONLY ONE Western country trading
     with Japan -- HOLLAND.
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE POLITICAL EFFECT -- and this is the causal hinge
   "Perry's arrival had an important effect on Japanese politics.
    ⭐ THE EMPEROR, WHO TILL THEN HAD HAD LITTLE POLITICAL POWER,
    NOW RE-EMERGED AS AN IMPORTANT FIGURE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1868 -- "a movement FORCIBLY REMOVED THE SHOGUN FROM
   POWER, and BROUGHT THE EMPEROR TO EDO. This was made THE
   CAPITAL and RENAMED TOKYO, which means 'EASTERN CAPITAL'."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The "black ships" — a detail worth quoting.** The Japanese called Perry's vessels ***black ships*** because *"tar was used to seal the joints of the wood"*. They *"are depicted in paintings and cartoons showing the strange foreigners and their habits"*, and became **a powerful symbol of Japan's "opening"**.
> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's own correction, and it is a genuine Prelims trap:** *"(**Today, scholars would argue that Japan had NOT been 'CLOSED'**, took part in the **east Asian trade** and had access to knowledge of the wider world both **through the Dutch and the Chinese**.)"* ⭐ **"The opening of Japan" is a phrase written from the deck of an American ship.** Japan was selectively connected, not sealed.

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why the fear was real — and note that INDIA is the example they used:**

> *"Officials and the people were aware that **some European countries were building COLONIAL EMPIRES IN INDIA AND ELSEWHERE.** News of **China being defeated by the British** was flowing in, and this was **even depicted in POPULAR PLAYS**, so that there was **A REAL FEAR THAT JAPAN MIGHT BE MADE A COLONY.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The slogan, and it is the single most quotable phrase in the chapter:**
> ***`FUKOKU KYOHEI`* — "RICH COUNTRY, STRONG ARMY".**
> *"They realised that they needed to **develop their economy and build a strong army, OTHERWISE THEY WOULD FACE THE PROSPECT OF BEING SUBJUGATED LIKE INDIA.** To do this they needed to **create a sense of NATIONHOOD among the people, and to TRANSFORM SUBJECTS INTO CITIZENS.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Subjects into citizens" is the whole Meiji project in three words, and it tells you why modernisation had to be more than machinery.** ⭐ **A subject obeys; a citizen IDENTIFIES.** An army of conscripts, a national school system and a tax base all require people who believe the state is theirs. **That is why the Meiji reforms attack schooling, conscription, administrative boundaries and the calendar all at once** — they are not separate reforms but one operation.
> ⭐⭐ **And note the motive: they modernised BECAUSE OF what they saw happening to India and China.** **Japan's modernisation was a defensive reaction to colonialism elsewhere** — which is exactly why it later justified its own empire as "liberating Asia".

### 👑 2.1 The "emperor system"

> ⭐⭐⭐ *"the new government also worked to build what they called the **'EMPEROR SYSTEM'.** (**Japanese scholars use this term as the emperor was PART OF A SYSTEM, along with the BUREAUCRACY and the MILITARY, that exercised power.**)"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The double image of the emperor — this is the chapter's neatest example of tradition put to modern work:**

```
   THE EMPEROR AS TRADITION        |  THE EMPEROR AS MODERNITY
   -------------------------------|------------------------------
   "treated with REVERENCE as he  |  "he was also shown as
    was considered A DIRECT       |   ⭐ THE LEADER OF
    DESCENDANT OF THE SUN         |   WESTERNISATION"
    GODDESS"                      |  - he wore WESTERN-STYLE
   - his BIRTHDAY became a        |    MILITARY UNIFORMS
     NATIONAL HOLIDAY             |  - EDICTS were issued in his
                                  |    name TO SET UP MODERN
                                  |    INSTITUTIONS
```

> ⭐⭐ **Officials were sent to study the European monarchies on which they planned to model their own**, and the ⭐⭐⭐ **IMPERIAL RESCRIPT ON EDUCATION of 1890** urged people to *"**pursue learning, advance public good and promote common interests**."*

### 🏫 2.2 Education, administration and the army

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ SCHOOLING -- a new system from the 1870s
     - COMPULSORY FOR BOYS AND GIRLS, and ⭐ "BY 1910 ALMOST
       UNIVERSAL"
     - TUITION FEES WERE MINIMAL
     - the curriculum was based on WESTERN MODELS, but by the
       1870s, "while emphasising modern ideas, ⭐⭐ STRESS WAS
       PLACED ON LOYALTY AND THE STUDY OF JAPANESE HISTORY"
     - the ministry of education controlled THE CURRICULUM, THE
       SELECTION OF TEXTBOOKS and TEACHERS' TRAINING
     - ⭐ "MORAL CULTURE" had to be taught -- texts urged children
       to "REVERE THEIR PARENTS, BE LOYAL TO THE NATION, and
       BECOME GOOD CITIZENS"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ ADMINISTRATION -- "the Meiji government imposed a NEW
   ADMINISTRATIVE STRUCTURE BY ALTERING OLD VILLAGE AND DOMAIN
   BOUNDARIES."
   ⭐ THE TEST OF A NEW UNIT: "revenue adequate to maintain the
     LOCAL SCHOOLS AND HEALTH FACILITIES, as well as serve as
     A RECRUITMENT CENTRE FOR THE MILITARY"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ CONSCRIPTION -- "ALL YOUNG MEN OVER TWENTY had to do a
   period of MILITARY SERVICE"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ AND CONTROL -- "A LEGAL SYSTEM was set up to REGULATE THE
   FORMATION OF POLITICAL GROUPS, CONTROL THE HOLDING OF MEETINGS
   and IMPOSE STRICT CENSORSHIP."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE FATAL DESIGN FLAW
   "THE MILITARY AND THE BUREAUCRACY WERE PUT UNDER THE DIRECT
    COMMAND OF THE EMPEROR. ⭐ THIS MEANT THAT EVEN AFTER A
    CONSTITUTION WAS ENACTED THESE TWO GROUPS REMAINED OUTSIDE
    THE CONTROL OF THE GOVERNMENT."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That last box is the most consequential sentence in the Japanese half of the chapter, and it is the answer to "why did a modernising Japan end up at Pearl Harbor?"** ⭐ **The army was made answerable to the emperor, not to the cabinet — so a civilian government could never control it.** NCERT draws the conclusion itself: *"the tension between these different ideals represented by **a democratic constitution and a modern army** was to have **far-reaching consequences. THE ARMY PRESSED FOR A VIGOROUS FOREIGN POLICY TO ACQUIRE MORE TERRITORY.**"*
> ⭐⭐ **This is a constitutional-design point worth carrying into GS2:** **where the coercive apparatus is placed outside the accountable executive, the constitution is decorative in exactly the area that matters most.** *(Note the reinforcing rule of **1899**: only **serving generals and admirals** could become ministers — so the services could bring down any cabinet simply by refusing to nominate one.)*

**⭐⭐ A neat aside worth a Prelims mark — the Japanese script:**

> The Japanese **borrowed their written script from the Chinese in the sixth century**, but since the languages differ they developed **two phonetic alphabets — HIRAGANA and KATAKANA.** ⭐ **Hiragana is considered feminine because it was used by many women writers in the Heian period** (such as Murasaki). ⭐⭐ *"The existence of a phonetic syllabary meant that **KNOWLEDGE SPREAD FROM THE ELITES TO THE WIDER SOCIETY RELATIVELY QUICKLY.**"* In the **1880s** it was suggested that Japan adopt **a completely phonetic script, or a European language** — ⚠️ **neither was done.**

## 3️⃣ Modernising the Economy ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ HOW IT WAS PAID FOR -- "Funds were raised by levying AN
   AGRICULTURAL TAX."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1870-72 -- Japan's FIRST RAILWAY LINE, between TOKYO and
   the port of YOKOHAMA
                          |
   ⭐⭐ TECHNOLOGY IMPORTED, THEN INTERNALISED
     - TEXTILE MACHINERY imported from Europe
     - FOREIGN TECHNICIANS employed "to TRAIN WORKERS, as well as
       to TEACH IN UNIVERSITIES AND SCHOOLS"
     - and ⭐ JAPANESE STUDENTS WERE SENT ABROAD
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1872 -- MODERN BANKING INSTITUTIONS were launched
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE ZAIBATSU -- "large business organisations CONTROLLED
   BY INDIVIDUAL FAMILIES"
   "Companies like ⭐ MITSUBISHI and SUMITOMO were helped through
    SUBSIDIES AND TAX BENEFITS to become major SHIPBUILDERS so
    that ⭐⭐ JAPANESE TRADE WAS FROM NOW ON CARRIED IN JAPANESE
    SHIPS."
   ⭐ The zaibatsu "DOMINATED THE ECONOMY TILL AFTER THE SECOND
     WORLD WAR."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two things here are worth more than the dates.**
> ⭐ **(1) The peasantry paid for industrialisation.** An **agricultural tax** funded the railways, the banks and the subsidies. **Japan extracted its own development capital from its own countryside** — which is what a sovereign state can do and a colony cannot, because in a colony that surplus leaves. 🔗 *(Set this directly against Class 10 Ch.3's **home charges**.)*
> ⭐⭐ **(2) "Japanese trade was from now on carried in Japanese SHIPS."** This is deliberate: the state **picked firms, subsidised them, gave them tax breaks, and aimed at controlling the CARRYING TRADE, not merely production.** ⭐ **That is industrial policy in the modern sense**, and it is the direct ancestor of Korea's *chaebol* strategy later in this same chapter. 🔗 *(Compare Class 10 Ch.4's **European Managing Agencies**, where Indian financiers supplied capital but Europeans made every decision — the mirror image.)*

**⭐⭐⭐ Population and migration:**

```
   POPULATION      1872 ......  35 MILLION
                   1920 ......  55 MILLION
                          |
   ⭐⭐ TO REDUCE POPULATION PRESSURE THE GOVERNMENT ACTIVELY
      ENCOURAGED MIGRATION:
        first  -> HOKKAIDO, the northern island, "a largely
                  autonomous area where the indigenous people
                  called the ⭐ AINU lived"
        then   -> HAWAII and BRAZIL
        and    -> "the growing COLONIAL EMPIRE of Japan"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ URBANISATION      by 1925 .... 21% of the population
                         by 1935 .... 32% (22.5 million)
```

> ⚠️ 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **Do not pass over Hokkaido.** Japanese settlers were **moved into the land of the AINU**, an indigenous people, exactly as NCERT's **Theme 6** describes white settlers moving into North America and Australia. ⭐ **A country that modernised in order to avoid being colonised proceeded to colonise — internally first (the Ainu), then Taiwan (1895) and Korea (1910).** **That is not an incidental cruelty; it is the argument of the chapter's conclusion.**

### 👷 3.1 Industrial workers — and who they actually were ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   MANUFACTURING EMPLOYMENT
        1870 ......   700,000
        1913 ......   4 MILLION
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ BUT LOOK AT THE UNIT SIZE
   "MOST OF THEM WORKED IN UNITS EMPLOYING LESS THAN FIVE PEOPLE
    AND USING NEITHER MACHINERY NOR ELECTRIC POWER."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND WHO STAFFED THE MODERN FACTORIES
   "OVER HALF OF THOSE EMPLOYED IN MODERN FACTORIES WERE WOMEN.
    ⭐ AND IT WAS WOMEN WHO ORGANISED THE FIRST MODERN STRIKE
    IN 1886."
   "After 1900 the number of men began to increase but ⭐⭐ ONLY IN
    THE 1930s DID MALE WORKERS BEGIN TO OUTNUMBER WOMEN."
                          |
   FACTORIES EMPLOYING MORE THAN 100 WORKERS
        1909 ...... just over 1,000
        1920 ...... over 2,000
        1930s ..... 4,000
   ⚠️ "YET EVEN IN 1940, THERE WERE OVER 550,000 WORKSHOPS THAT
      EMPLOYED LESS THAN FIVE EMPLOYEES."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE SOCIAL CONSEQUENCE NCERT DRAWS
   "This SUSTAINED THE FAMILY-CENTRED IDEOLOGY, just as NATIONALISM
    WAS SUSTAINED BY A STRONG PATRIARCHAL SYSTEM UNDER AN EMPEROR
    WHO WAS LIKE A FAMILY PATRIARCH."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two findings here are directly transferable to other answers.**
> ⭐ **(1) The small workshop survived industrialisation — 550,000 of them still had under five employees in 1940.** *(Precisely Class 10 Ch.4's finding for both Britain and India: the factory is the visible face of an economy whose bulk lies elsewhere. NCERT states this pattern in three different chapters so that you generalise it.)*
> ⭐⭐ **(2) Japan's modern industry was built mainly by WOMEN, and women organised its first modern strike in 1886** — yet the ideology the same economy sustained was **patriarchal**, with the emperor cast as the family's patriarch. ⭐ **The factory floor and the official ideology were describing two different countries.** *(Compare Class 10 Ch.4 and the Bombay mills: women were 23% of the workforce and then displaced "by machines or by men".)*

### 🌱 3.2 Tanaka Shozo and the first pollution agitation ⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐ *"The **rapid and unregulated growth of industry** and the demand for natural resources such as **timber** led to **ENVIRONMENTAL DESTRUCTION. TANAKA SHOZO, elected to the first House of Representatives, launched THE FIRST AGITATION AGAINST INDUSTRIAL POLLUTION IN 1897 with 800 VILLAGERS in a mass protest forcing the government to take action.**"*
>
> **Who he was:** ⭐ **Tanaka Shozo (1841–1913)**, *"the **SELF-TAUGHT SON OF A FARMER**"*, who rose to become a major political figure, participated in the **Popular Rights Movement of the 1880s** demanding constitutional government, and was **elected to the first Diet**. ⭐⭐ **His belief: *"ORDINARY PEOPLE SHOULD NOT BE SACRIFICED FOR INDUSTRIAL PROGRESS."***
>
> **The case:** the ⭐ **ASHIO MINE** was polluting the **WATARASE RIVER**, *"ruining **100 SQUARE MILES OF FARMLAND** and affecting **A THOUSAND FAMILIES**"*. ⭐ **The agitation forced the company to take pollution-control measures so that BY 1904 HARVESTS WERE NORMAL.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is one of the most useful paragraphs in the whole NCERT series for GS3, and almost nobody uses it.** ⭐ **An environmental mass movement, led by an elected representative, winning measurable remediation — in 1897.** It predates almost everything candidates cite. **And the framing is exactly the modern one:** *ordinary people should not be sacrificed for industrial progress* — the displacement-versus-development question, stated in a sentence.
> 🔗 **Pair it with the chapter's later account of MINAMATA mercury poisoning in the 1960s and CADMIUM poisoning**, and you have **Japan as a complete arc: pollution → grass-roots pressure → litigation and compensation → "some of the strictest environmental controls in the world."** *(And with Class 10 supplement Part B's Bengal Smoke Nuisance Commission for the colonial-era comparison.)*

## 5️⃣ "Westernisation" and "Tradition" — the intellectual debate ⭐⭐⭐

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Three positions. Learn them as a spectrum — this is a ready-made Mains answer:**

| Thinker | Position |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FUKUZAWA YUKICHI (1835–1901)** — *total Westernisation* | Japan must ⭐ **"EXPEL ASIA"** — *"he meant that Japan must **shed its 'Asian' characteristics and become part of the West**."* Born in **an impoverished samurai family**, studied **Dutch and Western sciences** at Nagasaki and Osaka, then English; went to the USA in **1860 as a translator** for the first Japanese embassy; founded the school that is today ⭐ **KEIO UNIVERSITY**; a core member of the ⭐ **MEIROKUSHA**, a society to promote Western learning. In ***The Encouragement to Learning* (1872–76)** he was scathing: ⭐⭐ *"**All that Japan has to be proud of is its scenery**."* He wanted not just factories but ⭐ **"the CULTURAL ESSENCE of the West — THE SPIRIT OF CIVILISATION"**, to build **a new citizen.** His principle: ⭐⭐⭐ *"**Heaven did not create men above men, nor set men below men.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **MIYAKE SETSUREI (1860–1945)** — *national distinctiveness* | The next generation **questioned this total acceptance** and urged that **national pride be built on indigenous values.** Each nation must **develop its special talents IN THE INTEREST OF WORLD CIVILISATION**: ⭐⭐ *"**To devote oneself to one's country is to devote oneself to the world.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **UEKI EMORI (1857–1892)** — *liberal democracy* | A leader of the ⭐ **POPULAR RIGHTS MOVEMENT** demanding **constitutional government**; admired **the French Revolution's doctrine of the natural rights of man and of popular sovereignty**; wanted **a liberal education that would develop each individual**: ⭐⭐ *"**Freedom is more precious than order.**"* ⭐ **Others even advocated VOTING RIGHTS FOR WOMEN.** *"This pressure led the government to announce a constitution."* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **These three exhaust the options available to any society modernising under external pressure, which is why the section is worth memorising as a structure rather than as three biographies.**
> ⭐ **Fukuzawa: the problem is US — copy the West wholesale, including its spirit.**
> ⭐ **Miyake: the problem is IMITATION — a nation contributes to the world by being distinctively itself.**
> ⭐ **Ueki: the problem is POWER — modernity means rights, not battleships.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Now map them onto India and the answer writes itself:** **Fukuzawa ≈ the early Anglicists and Rammohun Roy's confidence in Western science**; **Miyake ≈ the Swadeshi and cultural-nationalist strand, Vivekananda and Tagore's civilisational argument**; **Ueki ≈ the moderate constitutionalists, and later the liberal-democratic core of the Congress.** ⭐ **And note who won in Japan: NEITHER the Westernisers nor the liberals, but the state, which took Fukuzawa's technology, Miyake's pride and none of Ueki's freedom.**
> 🔗 **Also compare Class 10 supplement Part A: Phan Chu Trinh (accept Western ideals, indict the French for betraying them) is Ueki's Vietnamese counterpart; Phan Boi Chau is closer to Miyake.**

| Group | Who | Their answer |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The early reformers** | **KANG YOUWEI (1858–1927)**, **LIANG QICHAO (1873–1929)** | *"tried to **USE TRADITIONAL IDEAS IN NEW AND DIFFERENT WAYS** to meet the challenges posed by the West"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The republican revolutionaries** | **SUN YAT-SEN**, *"the first president of the republic"* | *"were **INSPIRED BY IDEAS FROM JAPAN AND THE WEST**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Communist Party (CCP)** | Mao Zedong and others | *"wanted to **END AGE-OLD INEQUALITIES AND DRIVE OUT THE FOREIGNERS**"* |

> ⭐⭐ **Where modern China begins:** *"with its **first encounter with the West in the SIXTEENTH AND SEVENTEENTH CENTURIES when JESUIT MISSIONARIES introduced Western sciences such as ASTRONOMY AND MATHEMATICS.** ⭐ **Limited though its immediate impact was, it set in motion events that gathered momentum in the nineteenth century when Britain used force to expand its lucrative trade in OPIUM** leading to the **FIRST OPIUM WAR (1839–42).** This **undermined the ruling Qing dynasty and strengthened demands for reform and change.**"*

## 9️⃣ The Opium Trade ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE TRIANGULAR TRADE -- reproduce this exactly

   THE PROBLEM: "The demand for Chinese goods such as TEA, SILK
   and PORCELAIN created a serious BALANCE-OF-TRADE PROBLEM.
   ⭐ WESTERN GOODS DID NOT FIND A MARKET IN CHINA, SO PAYMENT HAD
   TO BE IN SILVER."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ THE COMPANY'S SOLUTION: "a new option -- OPIUM, WHICH GREW
   IN INDIA."
                          |
   THE CIRCUIT:
     1. Grow OPIUM in INDIA
     2. Sell it in CHINA
     3. Give the SILVER earned to Company agents in CANTON
        in return for LETTERS OF CREDIT
     4. Use the silver to buy TEA, SILK and PORCELAIN
     5. Sell those in BRITAIN
   ⭐⭐ "This was the 'TRIANGULAR TRADE' between BRITAIN, INDIA
      and CHINA."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the same triangular trade you met in Class 9's supplement and Class 10 Ch.3 — and here you are seeing it from the CHINESE end.** ⭐ **The point to make in an answer: Britain solved its trade deficit with China by manufacturing an addiction, and it used INDIAN land, INDIAN peasants and INDIAN soldiers to do it.** 🔗 **NCERT itself supplies the sting a page later — ordinary Chinese *"could see that the British used INDIAN SOLDIERS in their wars on China."*** ⭐⭐ **India was simultaneously a victim of the system and, in Chinese eyes, an instrument of it.** That single observation is worth a paragraph in any question on Indo-Chinese historical relations.

**⭐⭐⭐ What the Qing reformers tried:**

> **Kang Youwei and Liang Qichao** *"realised the need to **strengthen the system**"* and initiated policies to build **a modern administrative system, a new army and an educational system**, and **set up local assemblies to establish constitutional government.** *"They saw the need to **PROTECT CHINA FROM COLONISATION.**"*

**⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ The "negative examples" — Poland and India. This is a striking passage; do not skip it:**

> *"The **NEGATIVE EXAMPLE OF COLONISED COUNTRIES worked powerfully on Chinese thinkers.** The **partition of POLAND** in the eighteenth century was a much-discussed example. **So much so that by the late 1890s it came to be used as A VERB: ⭐ 'TO POLAND US' (bolan wo).**"*
>
> *"**INDIA was another such example.** In **1903**, the thinker **LIANG QICHAO**, who believed that **only by making people aware that China was a nation would they be able to resist the West**, wrote that India was ⭐⭐ *'**a country that was destroyed by a NON-COUNTRY that is the East India Company**'*."* ⚠️ *"He **criticised Indians for being CRUEL TO THEIR OWN PEOPLE and SUBSERVIENT TO THE BRITISH.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read Liang's phrase carefully, because it is an argument and not an insult.** ⭐ *"**Destroyed by a non-country**"* means: **India did not fall to a rival NATION — it fell to a trading company**, because there was no Indian nation to resist as one. **That was precisely Liang's warning to China**: nationhood is not a sentiment, it is the practical capacity to act collectively, and without it you can be dismantled by a corporation.
> ⚠️ **Be even-handed about the second half.** His charge that Indians were *"cruel to their own people"* points at **caste and internal hierarchy** — and the uncomfortable truth is that **Class 10 Ch.2's account of how differently swaraj was understood by peasants, tribals, plantation workers and Dalits** shows he was not simply wrong. ⭐ **A candidate who can quote a Chinese reformer using India as a cautionary tale, and then engage with the charge honestly, is writing at a different level from one who recites the freedom struggle.**

**⭐⭐⭐ And the target of reform: Confucianism.**

> *"**CONFUCIANISM**, developed from the teachings of **CONFUCIUS (551–479 BCE)** and his disciples, was concerned with **good conduct, practical wisdom and proper social relationships.** It influenced the Chinese attitude toward life, provided social standards and laid the basis for political theories and institutions. ⚠️ **IT WAS NOW SEEN AS A MAJOR BARRIER TO NEW IDEAS AND INSTITUTIONS.**"*

**⭐⭐⭐ Learning from Japan — and note the reversal:**

> *"To train people in modern subjects **students were sent to study in JAPAN, BRITAIN and FRANCE.** ⭐ **Many Chinese students went to Japan in the 1890s.** They not only brought back new ideas but **many became leading republicans.** ⭐⭐ **The Chinese borrowed even JAPANESE TRANSLATIONS of European words such as JUSTICE, RIGHTS and REVOLUTION because they used the same ideographic script — A REVERSAL OF THE TRADITIONAL RELATIONSHIP.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **China had given Japan its script; now it took back, in that script, the vocabulary of modern politics.** **The direction of cultural borrowing had flipped within a single generation** — which is exactly how humiliating the situation felt, and why the reaction was so radical.

### 📜 9.1 The Examination System ⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐ ENTRY TO THE ELITE RULING CLASS (about 1.1 MILLION till
   1850) had been LARGELY THROUGH AN EXAMINATION.
                          |
   ⭐⭐ THE FORMAT: writing an "EIGHT-LEGGED ESSAY" (pa-ku wen) in
   CLASSICAL CHINESE in a prescribed form
                          |
   HELD TWICE EVERY THREE YEARS, at different levels
   ⭐⭐⭐ "of those allowed to sit ONLY 1-2 PER CENT PASSED THE
      FIRST LEVEL, usually BY THE AGE OF 24, to become what was
      called ⭐ 'BEAUTIFUL TALENT'"
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE OVERSUPPLY OF DEGREE-HOLDERS
   Before 1850 there were about
        526,869 CIVIL and 212,330 MILITARY provincial (sheng-yuan)
        degree holders
   ⭐ "SINCE THERE WERE ONLY 27,000 OFFICIAL POSITIONS, MANY
     LOWER-LEVEL DEGREE HOLDERS DID NOT HAVE JOBS."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ WHY IT WAS ABOLISHED IN 1905
   "The examination ACTED AS A BARRIER TO THE DEVELOPMENT OF
    SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY AS IT DEMANDED ONLY LITERARY SKILLS.
    In 1905 it was abolished as it was based on skills in classical
    Chinese learning that had, it was felt, NO RELEVANCE FOR THE
    MODERN WORLD."
   ⭐ NOTE THE TIMING: abolished JUST AFTER the RUSSO-JAPANESE WAR
     of 1905 -- "a war fought ON CHINESE SOIL AND OVER CHINESE
     TERRITORY."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This box repays thought, and it is uncomfortably close to home for anyone preparing for a competitive examination.** ⭐ **The Chinese examination was the most meritocratic recruitment system in the pre-modern world** — it selected on demonstrated ability rather than birth, and it staffed an empire for centuries. **It was abolished anyway**, for two reasons NCERT states plainly: **it tested the wrong thing** (literary skill in a classical language, not science or administration of a modern kind), and **it produced far more qualified people than there were posts** — over 700,000 degree-holders for 27,000 positions.
> ⭐⭐ **The transferable lesson: an examination system is only as good as the correspondence between what it MEASURES and what the job REQUIRES.** When that correspondence breaks, the exam stops selecting talent and starts absorbing it. ⭐ **A generation of frustrated, credentialled, unemployed degree-holders is not a neutral fact — it is a revolutionary constituency**, and many of them became exactly that.
> 🔗 *(Worth setting against the Vietnamese case in Class 10 supplement Part A, where the French DELIBERATELY failed two-thirds of candidates to limit the supply of educated natives. Same instrument, opposite abuse.)*

|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1. NATIONALISM** | *"**overthrowing the MANCHU who were seen as A FOREIGN DYNASTY, as well as other FOREIGN IMPERIALISTS**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **2. DEMOCRACY** | *"establishing **DEMOCRATIC GOVERNMENT**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **3. SOCIALISM** | *"**REGULATING CAPITAL and EQUALISING LANDHOLDINGS**"* |

> ⭐⭐ **Note the first principle carefully:** the Manchu (Qing) were themselves regarded as **a foreign dynasty**, so Chinese nationalism had **two targets at once — the internal alien ruler and the external imperialist.**

### ✊ 10.1 The May Fourth Movement ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 4 MAY 1919 -- "an angry demonstration was held in
   BEIJING to protest against THE DECISIONS OF THE POST-WAR PEACE
   CONFERENCE."
   ⚠️ THE GRIEVANCE: "DESPITE BEING AN ALLY OF THE VICTORIOUS SIDE
      LED BY BRITAIN, CHINA DID NOT GET BACK THE TERRITORIES
      SEIZED FROM IT."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ "The protest BECAME A MOVEMENT. It GALVANISED A WHOLE
   GENERATION TO ATTACK TRADITION and to call for SAVING CHINA
   THROUGH ⭐ MODERN SCIENCE, DEMOCRACY AND NATIONALISM."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ WHAT IT DEMANDED -- learn the list
     - DRIVING OUT THE FOREIGNERS "who were controlling the
       country's resources"
     - REMOVING INEQUALITIES and REDUCING POVERTY
     - ⭐ THE USE OF SIMPLE LANGUAGE IN WRITING
     - ⭐ ABOLISHING THE PRACTICE OF FOOT-BINDING and THE
       SUBORDINATION OF WOMEN
     - ⭐ EQUALITY IN MARRIAGE
     - ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT TO END POVERTY
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **May Fourth is the hinge of modern Chinese history and the most likely single item from this chapter to be asked.** ⭐ **The trigger is the deepest humiliation available: China fought on the WINNING side and was still not given back its own territory.** That destroyed the reformers' premise — that the international order could be appealed to — and made the revolutionaries' case for them.
> ⭐⭐ **Note what the movement bundled together: anti-imperialism AND language reform AND the end of foot-binding AND equality in marriage.** **Nothing was regarded as merely "cultural".** The claim was that **a nation cannot resist externally while half its population is bound at the feet and its written language is inaccessible to ordinary people.** ⭐ **National strength and social reform were treated as a single project** — which is precisely the argument Indian reformers were having at the same moment, and usually losing. 🔗 *(Compare Class 10 Ch.2 on how the Congress kept its social questions unresolved to hold its coalition together.)*

### 🏛️ 10.2 The Guomindang, and why it failed ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐ "Sun Yat-sen's ideas became the basis of the political
   philosophy of the GUOMINDANG (the NATIONAL PEOPLE'S PARTY)."
   ⭐ THEY IDENTIFIED THE "FOUR GREAT NEEDS":
        CLOTHING · FOOD · HOUSING · TRANSPORTATION
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AFTER SUN'S DEATH -- CHIANG KAI-SHEK (1887-1975) emerged
   as leader, launching a military campaign
     - to control the ⭐ "WARLORDS", "regional leaders who had
       USURPED AUTHORITY"
     - and TO ELIMINATE THE COMMUNISTS
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ HIS PROGRAMME
   "He advocated a SECULAR AND RATIONAL 'THIS-WORLDLY'
    CONFUCIANISM, but ALSO SOUGHT TO MILITARISE THE NATION. The
    people, he said, must develop a ⭐ 'HABIT AND INSTINCT FOR
    UNIFIED BEHAVIOUR'."
   ⚠️ ON WOMEN: he encouraged them "to cultivate THE FOUR VIRTUES
      of ⭐ 'CHASTITY, APPEARANCE, SPEECH AND WORK' and RECOGNISE
      THEIR ROLE AS CONFINED TO THE HOUSEHOLD.
      ⭐ EVEN THE LENGTH OF HEMLINES WAS PRESCRIBED."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The Guomindang's social base — the numbers matter:**
> *"The Guomindang's social base was **IN URBAN AREAS. Industrial growth was SLOW AND LIMITED.** In cities such as **SHANGHAI**, which became the centres of modern growth, **by 1919 an industrial working class had appeared numbering 500,000.** ⚠️ Of these, however, **only a small percentage were employed in modern industries such as shipbuilding. MOST WERE 'PETTY URBANITES' (xiao shimin), TRADERS AND SHOPKEEPERS.** ⭐ **Urban workers, particularly women, earned very low wages. Working hours were long and conditions of work bad.**"*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ NCERT's verdict — this is the exam sentence:**

> *"The Guomindang **despite its attempts to unite the country FAILED because of its NARROW SOCIAL BASE and LIMITED POLITICAL VISION.** ⭐⭐ **A major plank in Sun Yat-sen's programme — REGULATING CAPITAL AND EQUALISING LAND — WAS NEVER CARRIED OUT BECAUSE THE PARTY IGNORED THE PEASANTRY AND THE RISING SOCIAL INEQUALITIES. IT SOUGHT TO IMPOSE MILITARY ORDER RATHER THAN ADDRESS THE PROBLEMS FACED BY THE PEOPLE.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the cleanest cause-and-effect in the chapter, and the whole Chinese revolution turns on it.** ⭐ **Sun Yat-sen's third principle was land reform. The Guomindang inherited the principle and dropped the practice.** In a country that was overwhelmingly peasant, **a party based on urban traders and shopkeepers that would not touch land had no route to a majority** — and it substituted **military order** for a social programme.
> ⭐⭐ **Then look at what Mao did instead: he went to the peasants.** ⭐ **The CCP did not defeat the Guomindang because communism was more persuasive than nationalism; it won because ONE party offered land to the people who worked it and the other did not.** **State that, and you have answered "why did the CCP win?" properly.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐ **Now bring it home: the Indian National Congress DID go to the countryside** — Champaran, Kheda, Bardoli, the Awadh kisan sabhas *(Class 10 Ch.2)*. ⭐⭐ **That is a large part of why India's transfer of power came through a mass movement rather than through a peasant revolution.** **The Guomindang is the counterfactual India avoided.**

> ⭐⭐ **The intellectual ferment, worth a Prelims fact:** social and cultural change was helped by the spread of schools and universities — ⭐ **Peking University was established in 1902** — and by journalism. The popular ***LIFE WEEKLY***, edited by ⭐ **ZAO TAOFEN (1895–1944)**, introduced readers to new ideas and to leaders such as ⭐⭐ **Mahatma Gandhi and Kemal Ataturk**, and its circulation rose from **just 2,000 in 1926 to a massive 200,000 copies in 1933.**

## 1️⃣1️⃣ The Rise of the Communist Party of China ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE SETTING -- "When the JAPANESE INVADED CHINA IN 1937,
   the Guomindang RETREATED. The long and exhausting war weakened
   China."
   ⚠️ "PRICES ROSE 30 PER CENT PER MONTH BETWEEN 1945 AND 1949,
      and UTTERLY DESTROYED THE LIVES OF ORDINARY PEOPLE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ RURAL CHINA FACED TWO CRISES -- learn both
     1. ⭐ ECOLOGICAL -- "SOIL EXHAUSTION, DEFORESTATION and
        FLOODS"
     2. ⭐ SOCIO-ECONOMIC -- "caused by EXPLOITATIVE LAND-TENURE
        SYSTEMS, INDEBTEDNESS, PRIMITIVE TECHNOLOGY and POOR
        COMMUNICATIONS"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE CCP WAS FOUNDED IN 1921, "soon after the RUSSIAN
   REVOLUTION."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The Comintern, and what happened to it:** *"leaders such as **LENIN and TROTSKY** went on to establish the **COMINTERN or the THIRD INTERNATIONAL** … **to help bring about a WORLD GOVERNMENT that would end exploitation.** The Comintern and the Soviet Union **supported communist parties around the world** but they worked within the traditional Marxist understanding that ⭐⭐ **REVOLUTION WOULD BE BROUGHT ABOUT BY THE WORKING CLASS IN CITIES.** Its initial appeal across national boundaries was immense but ⚠️ **it soon became A TOOL FOR SOVIET INTERESTS and was DISSOLVED IN 1943.**"*
>
> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **A DATE DISCREPANCY WORTH KNOWING.** NCERT prints the Comintern's founding as **"March 1918"**. **The standard date accepted everywhere else is MARCH 1919.** ⭐ **For Prelims, if a question turns on this, the Comintern was founded in 1919** — but be aware that the NCERT text says 1918, and do not be thrown if a coaching source quotes it that way. *(The dissolution date of **1943** is correct.)*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mao's break with orthodoxy — the single most important idea in the Chinese half:**

> *"**MAO ZEDONG (1893–1976)**, who emerged as a major CCP leader, **TOOK A DIFFERENT PATH BY BASING HIS REVOLUTIONARY PROGRAMME ON THE PEASANTRY. ⭐ HIS SUCCESS MADE THE CCP A POWERFUL POLITICAL FORCE THAT ULTIMATELY WON AGAINST THE GUOMINDANG.**"*

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ JIANGXI, 1928-1934 -- in the mountains, "secure from
   Guomindang attacks"
     - a STRONG PEASANTS' COUNCIL (SOVIET) was organised, "united
       through ⭐ CONFISCATION AND REDISTRIBUTION OF LAND"
     - ⭐⭐ "Mao, UNLIKE OTHER LEADERS, STRESSED THE NEED FOR AN
       INDEPENDENT GOVERNMENT AND ARMY"
     - ⭐ ON WOMEN: he "supported the emergence of RURAL WOMEN'S
       ASSOCIATIONS, promulgated a NEW MARRIAGE LAW that FORBADE
       ARRANGED MARRIAGES, STOPPED PURCHASE OR SALE OF MARRIAGE
       CONTRACTS and SIMPLIFIED DIVORCE"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE LONG MARCH (1934-35)
   "The Guomindang BLOCKADE of the Communists' Soviet forced the
    party to seek another base." -- ⭐ 6,000 GRUELLING AND
    DIFFICULT MILES to SHANXI
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE NEW BASE AT YANAN -- where they developed their
   programme to
        END WARLORDISM · CARRY OUT LAND REFORMS ·
        FIGHT FOREIGN IMPERIALISM
   ⭐ "THIS WON THEM A STRONG SOCIAL BASE."
                          |
   "In the difficult years of the war the Communists and the
    Guomindang WORKED TOGETHER, but after the end of the war the
    Communists ESTABLISHED THEMSELVES IN POWER and the Guomindang
    was DEFEATED."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The Xunwu survey of 1930 — quote this if you want to show what Mao's method actually was.** He examined *"everyday commodities such as **salt and soya beans**, the relative strengths of **local organisations**, **petty traders and craftsmen, ironsmiths and prostitutes**, and the strength of **religious organisations**, to examine **the different levels of exploitation.**"* ⚠️ He even *"gathered statistics of the number of peasants **who had sold their children** and found out what price they received — **boys were sold for 100–200 yuan** but ⭐ **there were NO instances of the sale of girls because the need was for HARD LABOUR, not sexual exploitation.**"* *"It was **on the basis of these studies** that he advocated ways of solving social problems."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Xunwu survey is the detail that separates a good answer from a recited one, because it shows the CCP's advantage was EMPIRICAL before it was ideological.** ⭐ **Mao counted things — prices, traders, debts, children sold — and built a programme from what he found.** The Guomindang, by contrast, *"sought to impose military order rather than address the problems faced by the people."* ⭐⭐ **One party studied the countryside; the other garrisoned it.**
> 🔗 **Note the parallel with Class 10 supplement Part B: Charles Booth's survey made London's poverty into an object of policy by counting it.** **Counting is not a neutral act; it decides what a state is capable of seeing.**

## 1️⃣2️⃣ New Democracy, the Great Leap and the Cultural Revolution ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 🏗️ 12.1 Establishing the New Democracy, 1949–65

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1949 -- THE PEOPLE'S REPUBLIC OF CHINA is established,
   based on the principles of ⭐ "NEW DEMOCRACY", "an ALLIANCE OF
   ALL SOCIAL CLASSES, ⭐⭐ UNLIKE THE 'DICTATORSHIP OF THE
   PROLETARIAT' that the Soviet Union said it had established"
     - CRITICAL AREAS OF THE ECONOMY put under government control
     - PRIVATE ENTERPRISE and PRIVATE OWNERSHIP OF LAND
       "GRADUALLY ENDED"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ 1953 -- the programme ends; the government declares
   "a programme of SOCIALIST TRANSFORMATION"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 1958 -- THE GREAT LEAP FORWARD, "a policy to GALVANISE
   THE COUNTRY TO INDUSTRIALISE RAPIDLY"
     - ⭐ "People were encouraged to SET UP STEEL FURNACES IN THEIR
       BACKYARDS"
     - ⭐ IN RURAL AREAS, "PEOPLE'S COMMUNES (where land would be
       COLLECTIVELY OWNED AND CULTIVATED) were started"
     - ⭐⭐ "BY 1958 THERE WERE 26,000 COMMUNES COVERING 98 PER CENT
       OF THE FARM POPULATION"
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ AND THE VERDICT, IN ONE LINE
   "THE STEEL PRODUCED IN THE BACKYARD FURNACES WAS UNUSABLE
    INDUSTRIALLY."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The "socialist man" — Mao's stated goal:** someone who would have ⭐⭐ **FIVE LOVES: *"FATHERLAND, PEOPLE, LABOUR, SCIENCE and PUBLIC PROPERTY."*** ⭐ **Mass organisations** were created for farmers, women, students and other groups — the **All-China Democratic Women's Federation had 76 MILLION members**, the **All-China Students Federation 3.29 MILLION.**
>
> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **The internal dissent, which sets up the Cultural Revolution:** *"These objectives and methods **DID NOT APPEAL TO EVERYONE IN THE PARTY.** In **1953–54**, some were **urging more attention to INDUSTRIAL ORGANISATION AND ECONOMIC GROWTH. ⭐ LIU SHAOCHI (1896–1969) and DENG XIAOPING (1904–97) tried to MODIFY THE COMMUNE SYSTEM as it was NOT WORKING EFFICIENTLY.**"*

### 🔥 12.2 Conflicting Visions, 1965–78 — the Cultural Revolution

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE CONFLICT, stated exactly
   "between the MAOISTS wanting to create a 'SOCIALIST MAN' and
    those who OBJECTED TO HIS EMPHASIS ON IDEOLOGY RATHER THAN
    EXPERTISE"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1965 -- Mao launches THE GREAT PROLETARIAN CULTURAL
   REVOLUTION "TO COUNTER HIS CRITICS"
     - ⭐ THE RED GUARDS, "mainly STUDENTS AND THE ARMY", used for
       a campaign against ⭐⭐ "OLD CULTURE, OLD CUSTOMS AND OLD
       HABITS"
     - "STUDENTS AND PROFESSIONALS WERE SENT TO THE COUNTRYSIDE TO
       LEARN FROM THE MASSES"
     - ⭐⭐ "IDEOLOGY (BEING COMMUNIST) WAS MORE IMPORTANT THAN
       HAVING PROFESSIONAL KNOWLEDGE"
     - ⚠️ "DENUNCIATIONS AND SLOGANS REPLACED RATIONAL DEBATE"
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE COST
   "The Cultural Revolution BEGAN A PERIOD OF TURMOIL, WEAKENED THE
    PARTY and SEVERELY DISRUPTED THE ECONOMY AND EDUCATIONAL
    SYSTEM."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ 1975 -- "the Party ONCE AGAIN LAID EMPHASIS ON GREATER
   SOCIAL DISCIPLINE and THE NEED TO BUILD AN INDUSTRIAL ECONOMY
   so that China could BECOME A POWER BEFORE THE END OF THE
   CENTURY."
```

> ⚠️⭐⭐ **A second date to be aware of:** NCERT's body text dates the launch of the Cultural Revolution to **1965**, while its own timeline and most standard accounts give **1966**. ⭐ **Know both; if forced to choose in Prelims, 1966 is the conventional answer.** *(The chapter is dating the beginning of the internal conflict rather than the formal launch.)*

### 💹 12.3 Reforms from 1978 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ DENG XIAOPING'S FORMULA -- state it as a paradox,
   because that is what it is
   "Deng Xiaoping KEPT PARTY CONTROL STRONG WHILE INTRODUCING
    A SOCIALIST MARKET ECONOMY."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1978 -- the Party declares its goal as
   ⭐⭐ THE FOUR MODERNISATIONS:
        SCIENCE · INDUSTRY · AGRICULTURE · DEFENCE
   ⚠️ "DEBATE WAS ALLOWED AS LONG AS THE PARTY WAS NOT QUESTIONED."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 5 DECEMBER 1978 -- a wall-poster, ⭐ "THE FIFTH
   MODERNISATION", proclaimed that "WITHOUT DEMOCRACY THE OTHER
   MODERNISATIONS WOULD COME TO NOTHING."
   ⭐ It criticised the CCP "for NOT SOLVING THE PROBLEM OF POVERTY
     or ENDING SEXUAL EXPLOITATION, even CITING CASES OF SUCH ABUSE
     FROM WITHIN THE PARTY."
                          |
   ⚠️ "THESE DEMANDS WERE SUPPRESSED"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1989 -- "on the SEVENTIETH ANNIVERSARY OF THE MAY FOURTH
   MOVEMENT many intellectuals called for GREATER OPENNESS and an
   end to ⭐ 'OSSIFIED DOGMAS' (su shaozhi).
   ⚠️ STUDENT DEMONSTRATORS AT TIANANMEN SQUARE IN BEIJING WERE
      BRUTALLY REPRESSED. This was STRONGLY CONDEMNED AROUND THE
      WORLD."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The Fifth Modernisation" is the best single item in this chapter for a GS2 or Essay answer on development and democracy.** ⭐ **The Party named four modernisations — all of them technical. An anonymous wall-poster named the fifth and argued that without it the other four "would come to nothing."** **That is the entire development-versus-democracy debate compressed into a poster.**
> ⭐⭐ **And the chapter lets you test the claim against evidence rather than assert it.** **China's four modernisations did NOT come to nothing** — it became economically powerful without the fifth. **But** NCERT records the cost: *"increasing **inequalities between social groups, between regions and between men and women** are creating social tensions"*, and the political system *"continues to be tightly controlled."* ⭐ **So the honest formulation is: authoritarian modernisation can deliver growth, but it forecloses the mechanism by which a society corrects itself.** 🔗 **Then bring in KOREA below, where growth eventually FORCED democratisation — the same question, the opposite outcome.**
> ⭐⭐ **Note the recurring date: 1919, 1978, 1989 — the May Fourth anniversary keeps being used as the occasion for demands for openness.** **A movement can become a calendar that later generations use to speak.**

**⭐⭐⭐ The post-reform debate — three positions NCERT reports:**

| Position | Content |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The dominant view (Party)** | *"**strong political control, ECONOMIC LIBERALISATION and INTEGRATION INTO THE WORLD MARKET**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **The critics** | *"increasing **inequalities between social groups, between regions and between men and women** are creating social tensions"*, and they **question the heavy emphasis on the market** |
| ⭐⭐ **The traditionalists** | *"a **growing revival of earlier so-called 'traditional' ideas, of CONFUCIANISM**, and arguments that **China can build a modern society FOLLOWING ITS OWN TRADITIONS rather than simply copying the West**"* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **Note the irony and use it — Confucianism was identified as "a major barrier to new ideas and institutions" at the start of this chapter, attacked by May Fourth, and assaulted by the Red Guards as "old culture". It is now being revived as the basis of a distinctively Chinese modernity.** ⭐ **A tradition rejected in the name of strength returns once strength has been achieved.** *(Exactly Miyake Setsurei's argument in the Japanese half, arriving in China eighty years later.)*

## 1️⃣3️⃣ The Story of Taiwan ⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1949 -- CHIANG KAI-SHEK, defeated by the CCP, FLED TO
   TAIWAN "with OVER US$300 MILLION IN GOLD RESERVES and CRATES OF
   PRICELESS ART TREASURES", and established THE REPUBLIC OF CHINA
                          |
   ⭐⭐ THE PRIOR STATUS: "Taiwan had been A JAPANESE COLONY since
   the Chinese CEDED IT after the 1894-95 war with Japan."
   Sovereignty was restored to China by THE CAIRO DECLARATION
   (1943) and THE POTSDAM PROCLAMATION.
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE REPRESSION FIRST -- do not omit this
   "Massive demonstrations in FEBRUARY 1947 had led the GMD to
    ⭐ BRUTALLY KILL A WHOLE GENERATION OF LEADING FIGURES."
   The GMD under Chiang "went on to establish A REPRESSIVE
   GOVERNMENT FORBIDDING FREE SPEECH AND POLITICAL OPPOSITION and
   EXCLUDING THE LOCAL POPULATION FROM POSITIONS OF POWER."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND YET -- THE ECONOMIC RECORD
   "HOWEVER, THEY CARRIED OUT LAND REFORMS THAT INCREASED
    AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTIVITY AND MODERNISED THE ECONOMY so that
    ⭐ BY 1973 TAIWAN HAD A GNP SECOND ONLY TO THAT OF JAPAN IN
    ASIA."
   ⭐⭐ "The economy, largely dependent on trade, has been steadily
      growing, but WHAT IS IMPORTANT IS THAT THE GAP BETWEEN THE
      RICH AND POOR HAS BEEN STEADILY DECLINING."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE DEMOCRATIC TRANSFORMATION -- "EVEN MORE DRAMATIC"
     - began slowly after CHIANG'S DEATH IN 1975
     - ⭐ grew "when MARTIAL LAW WAS LIFTED IN 1987 and OPPOSITION
       PARTIES WERE LEGALLY PERMITTED"
     - "THE FIRST FREE ELECTIONS began the process of BRINGING
       LOCAL TAIWANESE TO POWER"
```

> ⚠️⭐⭐ **A date error in the printed text worth knowing about.** NCERT gives *"the Potsdam Proclamation (1949)"*. **The Potsdam Proclamation was issued in JULY 1945.** ⭐ **The Cairo Declaration (1943) is correctly dated; treat the Potsdam year in this book as a misprint.**

> ⭐⭐ **The diplomatic position:** *"Diplomatically **most countries have only TRADE MISSIONS in Taiwan. FULL DIPLOMATIC RELATIONS AND EMBASSIES ARE NOT POSSIBLE AS TAIWAN IS CONSIDERED TO BE PART OF CHINA.**"* Re-unification *"remains a contentious issue"*, but **"Cross Strait" relations have been improving**, with **massive Taiwanese trade and investment in the mainland**; ⭐ *"China may be willing to tolerate **a semi-autonomous Taiwan as long as it gives up any move to seek independence.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Taiwan is the control experiment that makes sense of the whole chapter, and hardly anyone uses it.** ⭐ **The SAME party — the Guomindang — that ignored the peasantry and lost China carried out LAND REFORM in Taiwan and produced Asia's second-highest GNP.** **The party did not change; the constraint did.** On the mainland it was defending landlords whose support it needed; on an island where it arrived as an outside force with no obligations to the local landholding class, it could expropriate freely. ⭐⭐ **Land reform is politically easy for a regime that owes nothing to the landowners — which is the general lesson, and it applies directly to India's own patchy record on land reform.**
> ⭐⭐ **Second point: Taiwan grew AND its inequality fell** — NCERT flags this as *"what is important"*. **Growth with declining inequality is not automatic; it followed from having redistributed the main asset first.** 🔗 *(Set beside China's post-1978 growth, where NCERT reports inequality rising.)*

## 1️⃣4️⃣ The Story of Korea ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐ **Note: this Korea section is NEW material added in the revised edition, and it runs right up to 2017.** It is the most contemporary content in any NCERT History textbook, and it is unusually rich for **GS2 (democratisation)** and **GS3 (export-led growth, the 1997 crisis)**.

### 🇰🇷 14.1 Colony, division and war

```
   ⭐⭐ THE JOSEON DYNASTY (1392-1910) faced, in the late
   nineteenth century, "INTERNAL POLITICAL AND SOCIAL STRIFE and
   INCREASING FOREIGN PRESSURE FROM CHINA, JAPAN AND THE WEST."
   Korea implemented modernisation reforms in GOVERNMENTAL
   STRUCTURES, DIPLOMATIC RELATIONS, INFRASTRUCTURE and SOCIETY.
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1910 -- "imperial Japan ANNEXED KOREA AS ITS COLONY,
   bringing the OVER 500-YEAR LONG JOSEON DYNASTY TO ITS END."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE RESISTANCE -- Koreans were "angry about JAPAN'S
   SUPPRESSION OF THEIR CULTURE and FORCED ASSIMILATION". They
     - DEMONSTRATED against colonial rule
     - SET UP A PROVISIONAL GOVERNMENT
     - ⭐ SENT DELEGATIONS to appeal to foreign leaders at the
       CAIRO, YALTA and POTSDAM conferences
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AUGUST 1945 -- Japanese colonial rule ends AFTER 35 YEARS
   with Japan's defeat.
   ⭐ NCERT is careful to credit Koreans: "it was THE CONTINUED
     EFFORTS OF INDEPENDENCE ACTIVISTS both inside and outside
     Korea that ENSURED KOREA'S INDEPENDENCE after Japan's defeat."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE DIVISION -- "the Korean Peninsula was TEMPORARILY
   DIVIDED ALONG THE 38TH PARALLEL", the SOVIETS managing the
   NORTH and the U.N. the SOUTH.
   ⭐⭐ "HOWEVER, THIS DIVISION BECAME PERMANENT AS SEPARATE
      GOVERNMENTS WERE ESTABLISHED IN BOTH THE NORTH AND THE SOUTH
      IN 1948."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ JUNE 1950 -- THE KOREAN WAR breaks out. South Korea
   received support from THE US-LED UNITED NATIONS FORCES, North
   Korea from COMMUNIST CHINA -- ⭐ "it developed into A VINTAGE
   PROXY WAR OF THE COLD WAR ERA."
   ⭐ JULY 1953 -- after three years, "the war ended in AN
     ARMISTICE AGREEMENT. KOREA REMAINED DIVIDED."
```

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **What the war cost, beyond lives** — NCERT lists three distinct damages, which is exactly the kind of layered answer examiners reward:
> **(1)** *"massive losses of **life and property**"*; **(2)** *"a **DELAY IN FREE-MARKET ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT AND DEMOCRATISATION**"*; **(3)** ⭐ *"**prices suddenly rose due to INFLATION** caused by increased national expenses and currency issued during the war"*, and *"**industrial facilities constructed during the colonial period had been DESTROYED ENTIRELY**."* As a result **South Korea was forced to rely on US economic assistance.**

### 📈 14.2 Rapid industrialisation under strong leadership ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐ 1948 -- SYNGMAN RHEE elected first president "through
   democratic process", but "he EXTENDED HIS ADMINISTRATION,
   TWICE THROUGH ILLEGAL CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS"
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why it worked — NCERT gives five ingredients. This is a ready-made answer:**

| Ingredient | NCERT's words |

| ⭐⭐ **1. Leadership and bureaucracy** | *"a combination of **STRONG LEADERS, WELL-TRAINED BUREAUCRATS, AGGRESSIVE INDUSTRIALISTS and A CAPABLE LABOUR FORCE**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **2. Incentives that were answered** | *"**Ambitious entrepreneurs RESPONDED WELL TO GOVERNMENT INCENTIVES** to increase exports and develop new industries"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **3. Education, and this is the one to stress** | *"**At the dawn of Korea's industrialisation, ALMOST ALL KOREAN WORKERS WERE ALREADY LITERATE AND COULD EASILY ACQUIRE NEW SKILLS.**"* |

| ⭐⭐⭐ **5. Capital from three sources** | *"**FOREIGN INVESTMENT** and Korea's **HIGH DOMESTIC SAVINGS RATE** helped develop the heavy industrial sector, while **REMITTANCES from South Korean workers overseas** also contributed"* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Ingredient 3 is the one that should change how you write about development.** ⭐ **Korea was already literate BEFORE it was rich.** Literacy was not a reward for growth; **it was the precondition that made the growth strategy executable** — you cannot move a workforce from garments to shipbuilding and electronics in fifteen years unless it can be retrained.
> ⭐⭐ **Now run the same test on the rest of the chapter and a pattern appears: JAPAN had near-universal schooling by 1910 and a book-renting public in Edo; KOREA was literate at the dawn of industrialisation; TAIWAN did land reform first.** ⭐⭐⭐ **In every East Asian success the human-capital or land-asset investment came BEFORE the industrial push, not after it.** 🔗 **That is the sharpest available criticism of India's own sequencing, and it is the single most useful idea to take from this chapter into GS3.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT asks directly: *"Did economic growth in South Korea contribute to its democratisation?"* Here is the answer, and it is more interesting than a yes.**
> ⭐ **YES, but not as a favour and not by intention.** Growth was used, explicitly, **as the justification for suspending democracy** — the Yusin Constitution was the price of development. **But growth also manufactured the conditions of its own political defeat**: NCERT spells out the chain — **urbanisation → better education → a developed media → citizens' self-awareness of political rights → the demand for direct elections.**
> ⭐⭐ **The decisive detail is WHO joined in June 1987: "not only students, but THE MIDDLE CLASS as well."** **Students had protested for decades and been crushed; what changed was that the class created BY the growth model turned against the regime that produced it.** ⭐ **An authoritarian developmental state builds, as a by-product, the constituency that will demand its end.**
> ⚠️ **But do not make it a law of nature — the chapter itself supplies the counter-example.** **China grew even faster after 1978 and the demand at Tiananmen in 1989 was crushed.** ⭐⭐ **So the honest conclusion: economic growth generates the SOCIAL PRECONDITIONS for democratisation but does not guarantee the POLITICAL OUTCOME — that depends on whether the regime's coercive capacity and legitimacy can absorb the pressure.** **Korea and China, two years apart, are the controlled comparison.**

## 1️⃣5️⃣ Two Roads to Modernisation — NCERT's own conclusion ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The thesis sentence of the entire chapter:**
> *"**INDUSTRIAL SOCIETIES FAR FROM BECOMING LIKE EACH OTHER HAVE FOUND THEIR OWN PATHS TO BECOMING MODERN.** The histories of Japan and China, along with the stories of Taiwan and Korea, show how **DIFFERENT HISTORICAL CONDITIONS LED THEM ON WIDELY DIVERGENT PATHS to building independent and modern nations.**"*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ On JAPAN — the balance sheet, in NCERT's own words:**

| The gain | The cost |
|---|---|
| *"successful in **retaining its independence** and **using traditional skills and practices in new ways**"* | ⚠️ *"its **ELITE-DRIVEN modernisation** generated **an AGGRESSIVE NATIONALISM**, helped to sustain **a REPRESSIVE REGIME that STIFLED DISSENT and demands for democracy**, and established **a COLONIAL EMPIRE that LEFT A LEGACY OF HATRED IN THE REGION**, as well as **DISTORTED INTERNAL DEVELOPMENTS**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **And the double-edged nationalism:** *"Japan's programme of modernisation was carried out **in an environment dominated by Western imperial powers. While it IMITATED them, it also attempted to FIND ITS OWN SOLUTIONS.** Japanese nationalism was marked by these different compulsions — ⭐⭐ **while MANY JAPANESE HOPED TO LIBERATE ASIA FROM WESTERN DOMINATION, FOR OTHERS THESE IDEAS JUSTIFIED BUILDING AN EMPIRE.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The methodological point — this is the sentence to quote in an Essay on tradition and modernity:**
> *"the transformation of social and political institutions and daily life was ⭐ **NOT JUST A QUESTION OF REVIVING TRADITIONS, OR TENACIOUSLY PRESERVING THEM, BUT RATHER OF CREATIVELY USING THEM IN NEW AND DIFFERENT WAYS.**"* **The example given:** the **Meiji school system**, modelled on European and American practice, introduced new subjects — *"but the curriculum's **main objective was TO MAKE LOYAL CITIZENS**. A course on **morals that stressed loyalty to the emperor was compulsory**."*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ On CHINA — the balance sheet:**

```
   ⚠️ THE STARTING CONDITION
   "FOREIGN IMPERIALISM, BOTH WESTERN AND JAPANESE, COMBINED WITH
    A HESITANT AND UNSURE QING DYNASTY to WEAKEN GOVERNMENT CONTROL
    and SET THE STAGE FOR A BREAKDOWN OF POLITICAL AND SOCIAL
    ORDER leading to IMMENSE MISERY FOR MOST OF THE PEOPLE."
   ⭐ "WARLORDISM, BANDITRY AND CIVIL WAR exacted a heavy toll on
     human lives, as did THE SAVAGERY OF THE JAPANESE INVASION.
     NATURAL DISASTERS ADDED TO THIS BURDEN."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT THE CCP DID -- and NCERT gives BOTH sides in one
      paragraph
   THE CLAIM: it "fought to PUT AN END TO TRADITION, which they saw
     as KEEPING THE MASSES IN POVERTY, THE WOMEN SUBJUGATED AND THE
     COUNTRY UNDEVELOPED"
   ⚠️ THE CONTRADICTION: "WHILE CALLING FOR POWER TO THE PEOPLE,
     IT BUILT A HIGHLY CENTRALISED STATE."
   ⚠️ THE FAILURE: "its REPRESSIVE POLITICAL SYSTEM TURNED THE
     IDEALS OF LIBERATION AND EQUALITY INTO SLOGANS TO MANIPULATE
     THE PEOPLE."
   ⭐⭐ THE ACHIEVEMENT, STATED ANYWAY: "YET IT DID REMOVE
     CENTURIES' OLD INEQUALITIES, SPREAD EDUCATION AND RAISE
     CONSCIOUSNESS AMONG THE PEOPLE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE PRESENT QUESTION
   "The Party has now carried out MARKET REFORMS and has been
    successful in MAKING CHINA ECONOMICALLY POWERFUL but ITS
    POLITICAL SYSTEM CONTINUES TO BE TIGHTLY CONTROLLED. The
    society now faces GROWING INEQUALITIES, as well as A REVIVAL OF
    TRADITIONS LONG SUPPRESSED. ⭐ THIS NEW SITUATION AGAIN POSES
    THE QUESTION OF HOW CHINA CAN DEVELOP WHILE RETAINING ITS
    HERITAGE."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Notice what NCERT does in that last box, and imitate it.** It states **the contradiction**, **the failure** and **the achievement** of the Chinese revolution **in three consecutive sentences, without resolving them.** ⭐ **That is what a balanced answer looks like — not a hedge ("there were merits and demerits"), but three specific claims that happen to point in different directions.** *"Yet it did remove centuries' old inequalities, spread education and raise consciousness"* is a real concession made by a text that has just called the system repressive. **Learn the move, not just the content.**

### 🟢 Mains angles

- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What were the major developments before the Meiji restoration that made it possible for Japan to modernise rapidly?"** *(NCERT's own.)* — **The three late-16th-century changes** (disarming the peasantry, daimyo in domain capitals, land surveys for a stable revenue base) and **what they produced**: internal peace, **Edo as the world's largest city**, a **commercial economy with credit and a rice stock market**, **merit valued over status**, a **reading public** ("rent a book for the price of a bowl of noodles"), and **import substitution in silk at Nishijin**. ⭐ **Conclusion: the Meiji reforms removed a political lid from a society that had largely already modernised.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Discuss how daily life was transformed as Japan developed."** *(NCERT's own.)* — The shift from the **multi-generation patriarchal household** to the **nuclear *homu*** with **breadwinner and homemaker**; the resulting **demand for domestic goods, entertainment and housing**; the **housing instalment figures against a bank clerk's 40 yen a month**; **electric trams, public parks (1878), department stores, radio (1925), cinema (1899), the Ginza and *ginbura***; and the ***moga***. ⭐ **Close on Matsui Sumako's Nora — a society arguing with itself.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"How did the Qing dynasty try and meet the challenge posed by the Western powers?"** *(NCERT's own.)* — **Kang Youwei and Liang Qichao**: a **modern administrative system, a new army, a new educational system, local assemblies and constitutional government**, driven by the **negative examples of Poland and India**; **students sent to Japan, Britain and France**; the **examination system abolished in 1905**; and the recognition that **Confucianism was now "a major barrier"**. ⚠️ **Note the limit: reform was attempted by the dynasty whose legitimacy depended on the system being reformed.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What were Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles?"** *(NCERT's own — nationalism, democracy, socialism, with the glosses.)*
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Did Japan's policy of rapid industrialisation lead to wars with its neighbours and destruction of the environment?"** *(NCERT's own.)* **Wars — yes, and structurally:** the **army answerable to the emperor not the cabinet (1890)**, **only serving officers as ministers (1899)**, the **fear of Western powers used to silence dissent**, and **empire in Taiwan (1895) and Korea (1910)**. **Environment — yes, twice:** **Tanaka Shozo and the Ashio mine (1897)**, then **cadmium, Minamata mercury and air pollution** in the 1960s–70s. ⭐ **But note the second half of both stories: the wars ended in defeat and a "no war" constitution; the pollution ended in some of the strictest environmental controls in the world.** **Both crises produced correction — through catastrophe in one case and through civil society in the other.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Do you think Mao Zedong and the CCP were successful in liberating China and laying the basis for its current success?"** *(NCERT's own.)* Use the textbook's own three-part verdict: **the contradiction** (power to the people, but a highly centralised state), **the failure** (a repressive system that turned liberation into manipulative slogans), and **the achievement** (*"it did remove centuries' old inequalities, spread education and raise consciousness"*). ⭐ **Then the honest link to "current success": land reform and mass literacy were the preconditions the 1978 reforms built on — so the answer is that the CCP laid the basis at a human cost the chapter does not conceal.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Did economic growth in South Korea contribute to its democratisation?"** *(NCERT's own — see the worked answer in §14.3: growth was used to JUSTIFY suspending democracy, and then produced the urbanised, educated, media-connected middle class that forced it. Use CHINA in 1989 as the counter-example that stops the argument becoming a law.)*
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GS1 flagship — "Compare the paths to modernisation of Japan, China and India."** Build it on **three variables**: ⭐ **(1) SOVEREIGNTY** — Japan kept it, China lost it partially, India lost it entirely, so only Japan could tax its own peasantry to fund its own railways; ⭐ **(2) THE PRIOR SOCIAL BASE** — Japan's literacy, commercial economy and urbanisation preceded reform, India's artisanal and commercial strength was dismantled *(Class 10 Ch.4)*, China's elite was locked into an examination system that barred science; ⭐ **(3) WHO WAS MOBILISED** — Japan's elite-driven reform, China's peasant revolution, India's mass movement. **Conclude with the cost side: Japan's path produced empire and Pearl Harbor, China's produced the Cultural Revolution, India's produced a democracy that has had to postpone redistribution.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GS3 — "The East Asian development model."** ⭐ **State-led, export-oriented, sequenced.** Korea: **ISI → exports → light industry → heavy and chemical industry**, with **five-year plans, support for large firms, high domestic savings, FDI and remittances** — and, decisively, **a workforce already literate**. Japan: **agricultural tax → railways → banks → subsidised zaibatsu → national carrying trade**. Taiwan: **land reform first → growth with declining inequality**. ⭐⭐ **The transferable finding: the human-capital and land-asset investments came BEFORE the industrial push in every case.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Essay — "Tradition and modernity."** ⭐ Use NCERT's own formulation: modernisation was *"**not just a question of reviving traditions, or tenaciously preserving them, but rather of CREATIVELY USING THEM in new and different ways**"* — the **emperor as both Sun Goddess descendant and leader of westernisation**; the **Meiji school teaching modern subjects to make loyal citizens**; **Nishitani's three streams**; and **China's post-1978 revival of the very Confucianism May Fourth had attacked.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **GS2/Essay — "Development versus democracy."** ⭐ **The Fifth Modernisation poster** as the statement of the problem; **Korea (growth → middle class → June 1987)** against **China (growth → Tiananmen 1989 → tighter control)**; **Park's Yusin Constitution** as the explicit bargain; and **Taiwan's transition after 1987** as the third case.

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The puzzle:** around 1800 **China was dominant and Japan insular**; within decades **Japan built a modern nation-state, an industrial economy and a colonial empire (Taiwan 1895, Korea 1910), defeating China in 1894 and Russia in 1905**, while **China lost political control and was convulsed by civil war.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Japan's head start:** **Tokugawa shoguns 1603–1867**, **over 250 daimyo domains**, **samurai** elite. Three late-16th-century changes — **peasantry disarmed, daimyo settled in domain capitals, land surveys for stable revenue** — produced **peace, EDO as the world's largest city**, a **commercial economy with credit and a rice stock market**, **merit over status**, and a reading public where **a book cost the price of a bowl of noodles to rent**.
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **Nishijin silk:** a guild of **31 households** in the 16th century, **over 70,000 people** by the end of the 17th; **only domestic yarn** from **1713**; and **silk exports became a major profit source when trade opened in 1859**.
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Perry arrived 1853, treaty 1854** (only **Holland** traded before). **1868: the shogun removed, the emperor brought to Edo, renamed TOKYO.** ⚠️ **Scholars dispute that Japan had been "closed" at all.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***FUKOKU KYOHEI* — "rich country, strong army"** — adopted for fear of being **"subjugated like India"**, with the aim of **transforming SUBJECTS INTO CITIZENS**. The **"emperor system"** made him both **descendant of the Sun Goddess** and **leader of westernisation**; **Imperial Rescript on Education 1890**.
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **Schooling compulsory for boys and girls from the 1870s, almost universal by 1910**, fees minimal, stressing **loyalty and Japanese history**; **conscription for all men over 20**; **strict censorship**. ⚠️⭐ **The army and bureaucracy were placed under the emperor, so they stayed OUTSIDE government control** — reinforced in **1899** when **only serving generals and admirals could be ministers**.
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The economy:** funded by an **AGRICULTURAL TAX**; **first railway Tokyo–Yokohama 1870-72**; **modern banking 1872**; **zaibatsu** (**Mitsubishi, Sumitomo**) subsidised so **Japanese trade travelled in Japanese ships**. Population **35m (1872) → 55m (1920)**; migration to **Hokkaido (the AINU), Hawaii, Brazil**; urban **21% (1925) → 32% (1935)**.
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Workers:** manufacturing **700,000 (1870) → 4 million (1913)**, but **most in units of under five people with no machinery**, and **over 550,000 such workshops even in 1940**. ⭐ **Over half of modern-factory workers were WOMEN, who organised the first modern strike in 1886**; men outnumbered them **only in the 1930s**.
9. ⭐⭐⭐ **TANAKA SHOZO**, self-taught farmer's son and member of the first Diet, launched **the first agitation against industrial pollution in 1897** with **800 villagers** against the **Ashio mine** poisoning the **Watarase** — **100 sq miles**, **1,000 families** — and **harvests were normal by 1904**. *"Ordinary people should not be sacrificed for industrial progress."*
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Aggressive nationalism:** a **restricted franchise**, a **Diet** (German word, German legal influence) with **limited powers**; **elected PMs only between 1918 and 1931**; and **the fear of Western powers used to silence opposition** to military expansion and higher taxes. **Empire abroad suppressed democracy at home.**
11. ⭐⭐⭐ **Three intellectual positions:** **FUKUZAWA YUKICHI** — *"expel Asia"*, Keio University, Meirokusha, *"Heaven did not create men above men"*; **MIYAKE SETSUREI** — *"to devote oneself to one's country is to devote oneself to the world"*; **UEKI EMORI** — Popular Rights Movement, *"Freedom is more precious than order"*, some even urging **votes for women**.
12. ⭐⭐ **Daily life:** the **nuclear *homu*** replaces the multi-generation household; **housing at 200 yen down and 12 yen a month when a bank clerk earned 40**; the ***moga***; **parks 1878, cinema 1899, radio 1925**, the **Ginza** and ***ginbura***; **Matsui Sumako as Nora**.
13. ⭐⭐⭐ **"Overcoming Modernity" (1943):** how to **combat the West while being modern**. **NISHITANI KEIJI defined modern as the unity of the RENAISSANCE, the PROTESTANT REFORMATION and the RISE OF NATURAL SCIENCES**, claiming Japan's **"moral energy"** gave it a duty to build **a Greater East Asia**.
14. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Defeat and recovery:** **US Occupation (1945-47)** — **demilitarisation, a new constitution with ARTICLE 9 the "no war clause", agrarian reform, trade unions restored, an attempt to dismantle the zaibatsu**; **women voted in 1946**. The **"miracle" was "firmly rooted in its long history"** — popular struggle, social cohesion, and government–bureaucracy–industry coordination, helped by **US support and demand from the Korean and Vietnam wars**. **Tokyo Olympics and the Shinkansen, both 1964.** ⚠️ Then **cadmium, Minamata mercury, air pollution** → grass-roots pressure → **some of the strictest environmental controls in the world**.
15. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **China's three groups:** **reformers (Kang Youwei, Liang Qichao)** using tradition in new ways; **republican revolutionaries (Sun Yat-sen)** inspired by Japan and the West; and the **CCP**, which wanted to **end inequalities and drive out foreigners**.
16. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The opium triangular trade:** Chinese **tea, silk and porcelain** had to be paid for in **silver** because **Western goods found no market**; so the Company grew **opium in India**, sold it in China, and used the silver for tea — **Britain, India and China**. **First Opium War 1839–42.**
17. ⭐⭐⭐ **The negative examples:** **Poland's partition became the verb *bolan wo*, "to Poland us"**; and **Liang Qichao in 1903 called India "a country destroyed by a NON-COUNTRY that is the East India Company"**, noting that **Indian soldiers fought Britain's wars on China**.
18. ⭐⭐⭐ **The examination system:** the **eight-legged essay** in classical Chinese; **1–2% passed the first level**, usually **by 24**; **526,869 civil and 212,330 military degree-holders for 27,000 posts**; **abolished 1905** as **"a barrier to the development of science and technology"**.
19. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1911: the republic under SUN YAT-SEN**, with the **THREE PRINCIPLES — NATIONALISM, DEMOCRACY, SOCIALISM** (the last meaning **regulating capital and equalising landholdings**).
20. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MAY FOURTH, 1919:** China, **though on the victorious side, did not get its territories back**. The movement demanded **modern science, democracy and nationalism**, plus **simple language in writing, an end to foot-binding and the subordination of women, and equality in marriage**.
21. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Guomindang failed** through a **narrow urban social base and limited political vision** — it **ignored the peasantry** and **never carried out land reform**, imposing **military order** instead. **Chiang Kai-shek** prescribed women's **four virtues** and even **hemlines**. Shanghai's industrial working class was **500,000 by 1919**, mostly **"petty urbanites"**.
22. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The CCP (founded 1921)** won because **MAO based the revolution on the PEASANTRY**, breaking with Comintern orthodoxy. **Jiangxi 1928–34** (land confiscated and redistributed; **a new marriage law** forbidding arranged marriage and simplifying divorce); the **XUNWU SURVEY of 1930**; the **LONG MARCH 1934–35, 6,000 miles to Shanxi**; the base at **YANAN**, promising **an end to warlordism, land reform and resistance to imperialism**.
23. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1949: the PRC, on "NEW DEMOCRACY" — an alliance of all classes, NOT the dictatorship of the proletariat.** **1958: the GREAT LEAP FORWARD** — backyard steel furnaces and **26,000 communes covering 98% of the farm population**; ⚠️ **the steel was "unusable industrially"**. **1965/66: the CULTURAL REVOLUTION**, Red Guards against **"old culture, old customs and old habits"**, where **ideology outranked expertise** and **"denunciations and slogans replaced rational debate."**
24. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1978: DENG XIAOPING kept Party control while introducing a socialist market economy**, declaring the **FOUR MODERNISATIONS — science, industry, agriculture, defence.** ⭐ **On 5 December 1978 a wall-poster demanded "THE FIFTH MODERNISATION": without democracy the others "would come to nothing."** Suppressed — as were the **Tiananmen demonstrators in 1989**, on the **seventieth anniversary of May Fourth**.
25. ⭐⭐⭐ **TAIWAN:** Chiang fled in **1949** with **over US$300m in gold**; the GMD **killed a generation of local leaders after February 1947** and ruled repressively — **yet carried out the LAND REFORM it never attempted on the mainland**, giving Taiwan by **1973 a GNP second only to Japan's in Asia** with **falling inequality**. **Martial law lifted 1987.**
26. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **KOREA:** **Joseon dynasty 1392–1910**; **annexed by Japan 1910**, freed **August 1945 after 35 years**; **divided at the 38th parallel**, permanent from **1948**; the **Korean War, June 1950 – armistice July 1953**, a **proxy war of the Cold War**. **Rhee forced out by the APRIL REVOLUTION of 1960**; **Park Chung-hee's coup May 1961**, elected **October 1963**.
27. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Park's model:** a shift **from import substitution to EXPORTS**, five-year plans favouring large firms, **light industry (textiles, garments) then heavy and chemical industry**, and the **SAEMAUL (New Village) Movement of 1970**. It worked because of **strong leaders, trained bureaucrats, aggressive industrialists, a capable labour force**, ⭐ **a workforce ALREADY LITERATE**, openness to technology, **FDI, high domestic savings and overseas remittances**.
28. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Growth bought authoritarianism, then destroyed it.** The **YUSIN CONSTITUTION (October 1972)** made **permanent presidency** possible; **Park was assassinated in October 1979**; **Chun Doo-hwan's coup followed in December**; **GWANGJU (May 1980) was suppressed**. But growth produced **urbanisation → education → media → political self-awareness**, and in the **JUNE DEMOCRACY MOVEMENT of 1987 the MIDDLE CLASS joined the students**, forcing **direct elections**.
29. ⭐⭐⭐ **Consolidation:** **Kim Young-sam, a civilian, elected December 1992**; **OECD 1996**; the **1997 foreign currency crisis** met by the **IMF** and by citizens' **GOLD COLLECTION MOVEMENT**; **Kim Dae-jung elected December 1997** in the first peaceful transfer; **Park Geun-hye, first female president (2012), impeached March 2017** after the **candlelight protests**; **Moon Jae-in, May 2017**.
30. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The conclusion:** *"**Industrial societies far from becoming like each other have found their own paths to becoming modern.**"* **Japan** kept independence and used tradition creatively — but produced **aggressive nationalism, a repressive regime and an empire that left a legacy of hatred**. **China's** revolution **built a highly centralised state while calling for power to the people** and **turned liberation into slogans** — ⭐ **"yet it did remove centuries' old inequalities, spread education and raise consciousness."**
