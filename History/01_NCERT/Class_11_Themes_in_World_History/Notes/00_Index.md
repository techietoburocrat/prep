# Class XI — *Themes in World History*

> **What this book actually is:** the only **proper world history** in the NCERT series. Classes 9 and 10 gave you world history *as it touches India*; this one steps outside India altogether — Mesopotamia, Rome, the Mongols, feudal Europe, the Renaissance, the destruction of indigenous America and Australia, and the modernisation of Japan and China.

---

## ⚠️ Read this before you start — an honest assessment of its value

**This book is NOT uniformly high-yield, and you should not read it the way you read Class 10.** Be clear about what the syllabus actually asks for.

> **GS1 Mains, world history line:** *"History of the world will include events from the **18TH CENTURY** such as industrial revolution, world wars, redrawal of national boundaries, colonization, decolonization, political philosophies like communism, capitalism, socialism etc. — their forms and effect on the society."*

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note the words "from the 18th century".** That single phrase splits this book in half:

```
   THEMES 6 AND 7  ->  squarely inside the Mains GS1 world-history line
                       (colonisation, indigenous displacement, and the
                        NON-EUROPEAN paths to modernisation)

   THEMES 1 TO 5   ->  OUTSIDE it. Mesopotamia, Rome, the Mongols,
                       feudalism and the Renaissance are pre-18th-century.
                       They are NOT dead weight -- but their value is
                       PRELIMS, ART & CULTURE, ESSAY and OPTIONAL,
                       not the GS1 world-history question.
```

**So the correct posture is:**

| Themes | How to read them | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **6 and 7** | **Properly, like a Class 10 chapter** | They answer a syllabus line directly, and **Theme 7 is the best material in the whole NCERT series on why Japan modernised and China convulsed** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **4 and 5** | **Read once, carefully, for concepts** | **Feudalism** and the **Renaissance** are the background to every "rise of the modern West" answer, and Theme 5 quietly demolishes the idea that the Renaissance was purely European |
| ⭐⭐ **1, 2 and 3** | **Read once for Prelims facts and Essay material** | **Mesopotamia, Rome and the Mongols** yield clean factual questions and superb Essay illustrations, but do not repay a second reading before Prelims |

⚠️ **Do not let this book eat the time that belongs to Classes 8 and 10.** Modern India and the freedom struggle outrank all of it.

The book is organised in **four sections**, each opening with an **Introduction** and a **Timeline**. NCERT says plainly that *"these timelines are **not for you to memorise for exams**"* — they exist to let you see what was happening in different places at the same moment. Use them exactly that way.

## ⚠️ What NCERT removed — FOUR themes, and one of them is a syllabus keyword

The original edition had **11 themes**. The rationalised edition prints **7**. The four that were cut:

| Removed theme | Original # | UPSC value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **From the Beginning of Time** | 1 | ⭐ Human evolution, hominids, the debate on the origins of language | **Skip.** It is prehistory and anthropology; the ground it covers is better served by Geography and by Science & Tech |
| **The Central Islamic Lands** | 4 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The rise of Islam, the caliphate, the Crusades, Islamic learning and the transmission of Greek science.** This is a serious hole — it is the only NCERT treatment of the medieval Islamic world as a *world power* rather than as invaders of India | ⭐ **Restore** |
| **Confrontation of Cultures** | 8 | ⭐⭐⭐ The Spanish in the Americas, Columbus, Cortés, Pizarro, the destruction of the Aztecs and Incas | ⭐ **Restore** — and note it **pairs directly with Theme 6**, which now begins mid-argument without it |
| **The Industrial Revolution** | 9 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION" IS A GS1 SYLLABUS WORD.*** Coal, iron, steam, the factory, transport, and the debate over whether living standards rose | ⭐⭐ **Restore — highest priority of the four** |

## ⚠️⚠️ THE RENUMBERING TRAP — this book contains dangling cross-references

When NCERT deleted four themes it renumbered the rest **but did not fix every internal reference.** The current printed book therefore points you at themes that are not in it:

```
   IN THE PREFACE ("On Reading World History"):
   "the drama unfolds not only in Rome (Theme 2)... but in the
    CENTRAL ISLAMIC STATES (THEME 4), and the land of the
    Mongols (Theme 3)."
                          |
   ⚠️ Rome = Theme 2 and the Mongols = Theme 3 are CORRECT in the
      new numbering -- but "Theme 4" here means THE CENTRAL ISLAMIC
      LANDS, which HAS BEEN DELETED.
      In the new numbering Theme 4 is THE THREE ORDERS.

   INSIDE THEME 3 (Nomadic Empires):
   "In Theme 4 we studied state formations in the central Islamic
    lands..."
                          |
   ⚠️ Same dangling reference. You did NOT study it -- it is gone.

   INSIDE THEME 6 (Displacing Indigenous Peoples), first paragraph:
   "THEME 8 described the history of the Spanish and Portuguese
    colonisation of South America."
                          |
   ⚠️ That was CONFRONTATION OF CULTURES -- DELETED. So Theme 6
      BEGINS MID-ARGUMENT: the Spanish destruction of the Aztecs
      and Incas, the first half of the story, is simply absent.

   ALSO INSIDE THEME 6, on industry:
   "small peasants were losing their land to big farmers, and
    moving to jobs in factories (SEE THEME 9)."
                          |
   ⚠️ That was THE INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION -- DELETED.
      ⭐ Use CLASS 10 Ch.4 instead; it makes the same point.
```

⭐ **So: whenever this book says "Theme 4" in a sentence about Islam, it means a chapter you do not have** — do not go looking for it in *The Three Orders*. **The same applies to "Theme 8" (Spanish America) and "Theme 9" (the Industrial Revolution).** ⭐⭐ **Four themes were cut but the cross-references were only partly repaired, so the book points at chapters that no longer exist in at least four places.**

## 🧭 How this book connects to the rest of your History

| This book | Connects to |
|---|---|
| **Theme 1** — Mesopotamian cities, writing, the food surplus | **Class 6 Ch.3** (Harappan cities) and **Class 10 supplement Part B** (what makes a city) |
| **Theme 2** — Rome: empire, slavery, the late-antique transformation | **Class 11 Theme 4** (what replaced Rome in Europe) |
| **Theme 3** — the Mongols, Genghis Khan, steppe polity | **Class 7 Ch.3–4** (the Delhi Sultanate's Mongol problem; Babur's descent from Timur and Genghis) |
| **Theme 4** — feudalism, the three orders, the manor | **Class 9 Ch.1** (what the French Revolution abolished — the First, Second and Third Estates are *these* three orders) |
| **Theme 5** — the Renaissance, humanism, print | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Class 10 Ch.5** (*Print Culture*) — Gutenberg, the Reformation and the reading revolution are the SAME story told twice |
| **Theme 6** — settler colonialism, indigenous displacement | **Class 10 Ch.3** (*The Making of a Global World*) — smallpox in the Americas, and the migration flows |
| **Theme 7** — Japan and China modernising | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Class 10 Ch.3 and Ch.4** — and the comparison with **India's** colonial de-industrialisation is the answer examiners want |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The single strongest cross-link in this book:** **Theme 7 sets Japan against China**, one modernising under its own state and the other torn apart by foreign intervention and civil war. **Put INDIA beside them as a third case** — colonised outright, industrialised late and partially, independent only in 1947 — and you have a three-way comparison that answers a large family of GS1 questions on why some Asian societies modernised and others did not.

## ✍️ Study order

1. ⭐ **Theme 7 first** (*Paths to Modernisation*). It is the highest-yield chapter in the book and needs no earlier theme as background.
2. **Theme 6** (*Displacing Indigenous Peoples*) — pairs with Theme 7 in the same section, and with Class 10 Ch.3.
3. **Theme 5** (*Changing Cultural Traditions*) — read it right after **Class 10 Ch.5**, since they overlap heavily on print and the Reformation.
4. **Theme 4** (*The Three Orders*) — read before or alongside **Class 9 Ch.1**, because it defines the estates the French Revolution destroyed.
5. **Themes 1, 2 and 3** last, in one pass, for Prelims facts and Essay illustrations.
