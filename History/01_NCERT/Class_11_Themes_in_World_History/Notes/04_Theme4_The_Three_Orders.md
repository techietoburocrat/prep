## 🧭 Hold the whole chapter as ONE argument

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The chapter has a single shape, and everything in it hangs off that shape. Learn the shape first and the details will have somewhere to sit.**

```
   ⭐ THE ONE-LINE ARGUMENT
   A society organised around LAND and PERSONAL LOYALTY was
   dissolved by WARMER WEATHER, BETTER PLOUGHS, and MONEY --
   and then by a PLAGUE -- and what emerged from the wreckage
   was the MODERN STATE.

   THE FIVE STAGES

   1. AFTER ROME (5th-9th c.)  --  no central power, so land is
      the only security. Society organises itself around who
      controls land. THREE ORDERS: those who pray, those who
      fight, those who work.
                          |
   2. THE BOOM (11th-13th c.)  --  the climate warms, the forest
      line recedes, iron ploughs and the three-field system
      DOUBLE the food. Population 42m -> 73m. Towns revive.
      ⭐ And rent starts being paid in MONEY instead of labour,
        which quietly kills the personal bond feudalism ran on.
                          |
   3. A FOURTH ORDER  --  townspeople. Merchants, bankers,
      lawyers, guilds. "TOWN AIR MAKES FREE." Merchants come to
      rival the nobility in power.
                          |
   4. THE CRASH (14th c.)  --  the climate turns cold again, the
      silver runs out, and the BLACK DEATH kills a fifth of
      Europe. Population 73m -> 45m.
      ⭐ Fewer workers means LABOUR IS SUDDENLY EXPENSIVE.
        Wages up 250% in England; grain prices DOWN.
                          |
   5. THE OUTCOME  --  lords try to force serfdom back on. The
      peasants revolt (1323, 1358, 1381) and are CRUSHED --
      ⭐⭐ AND STILL WIN, because the money economy cannot be
      un-invented. Kings then build standing armies with guns,
      the castle stops being safe, and the "NEW MONARCHS"
      absorb the nobility into a COURT instead of a pyramid.
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The single most quotable idea in this chapter is the paradox at stage 5:** *"though the lords succeeded in crushing the revolts, the peasants ensured that the feudal privileges of earlier days could not be reinvented."* ⭐⭐ **A rebellion can lose every battle and still make the old order impossible.** That sentence is usable in an essay, in GS1 on social movements, and in almost any answer about peasant movements in India.

## 1️⃣ The setting: what was left after Rome ⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐ NCERT'S OPENING -- learn this causal chain, it explains
      why feudalism took the shape it did

   The Roman Empire falls.
   Many groups of GERMANIC PEOPLE from EASTERN AND CENTRAL
   EUROPE occupy regions of ITALY, SPAIN and FRANCE.
                          |
   ⭐ "In the ABSENCE OF ANY UNIFYING POLITICAL FORCE, military
     conflict was FREQUENT"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ "and the need to GATHER RESOURCES TO PROTECT ONE'S LAND
     became very important"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ "SOCIAL ORGANISATION WAS THEREFORE CENTRED ON THE
     CONTROL OF LAND."
                          |
   ⭐ ITS FEATURES CAME FROM TWO SOURCES:
     "derived from both IMPERIAL ROMAN TRADITIONS and
      GERMAN CUSTOMS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ AND ONE THING SURVIVED THE COLLAPSE:
     CHRISTIANITY -- "the official religion of the Roman Empire
     FROM THE FOURTH CENTURY, survived the collapse of Rome, and
     gradually spread to CENTRAL AND NORTHERN EUROPE.
     ⭐ The CHURCH ALSO BECAME A MAJOR LANDHOLDER AND POLITICAL
       POWER IN EUROPE."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why land, and not money or office?** Because **when there is no state, there is no enforcement** — and the only asset that cannot be stolen and carried away is **land that armed men are standing on**. ⭐ **Feudalism is what a society does about security when nobody can guarantee it centrally: it converts land into an army.** A lord gives land; in return he receives soldiers. **That single exchange is the engine of the entire chapter.**

**⭐⭐⭐ And note what this means for the Church.** Rome's political structure died; Rome's *religion* did not. **The institution that outlived the empire became the empire's heir** — with its own laws, its own lands, its own taxes and its own head in Rome. ⚠️ **This is why the medieval Church is a political subject, not only a religious one**, and why the first order outranks the second in the ordering.

**⭐⭐ The three orders, named:**

| Order | Who | NCERT's phrase | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | **Christian priests** (the clergy) | *"some **pray**"* | Intercession with God |
| **Second** | **Landowning nobles** | *"others **fight**"* | Protection |
| **Third** | **Peasants** | *"still others **work**"* | Production |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's framing sentence for the whole chapter:** *"The changing relationships between these three groups was an important factor in shaping European history for several centuries."* ⭐ **Note the word CHANGING** — the chapter is not a description of a fixed pyramid, it is the story of that pyramid coming apart.

## 2️⃣ Historiography: Marc Bloch, and where the evidence comes from ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⚠️ **Do not skip this section because it looks like preamble.** ⭐ **UPSC asks historiography questions** — *how* we know, and *who* decided what counted as history. **Bloch here does for social history exactly what Burckhardt (Theme 5) does for cultural history**, and the two make a matched pair worth carrying into the exam hall.

**⭐⭐⭐ Why medieval Europe can be studied village by village — the SOURCES:**

```
   ⭐ "Over the last 100 years, European historians have done
     DETAILED WORK ON THE HISTORIES OF REGIONS, EVEN OF
     INDIVIDUAL VILLAGES."

   WHY THAT WAS POSSIBLE -- "from the medieval period, there is
   a lot of material in the form of DOCUMENTS, DETAILS OF
   LANDOWNERSHIP, PRICES and LEGAL CASES":

     CHURCH REGISTERS of BIRTHS, MARRIAGES and DEATHS
        -> "made it possible to understand the STRUCTURE OF
           FAMILIES AND OF POPULATION"
     INSCRIPTIONS IN CHURCHES
        -> information about TRADERS' ASSOCIATIONS
     SONGS AND STORIES
        -> "a sense of FESTIVALS AND COMMUNITY ACTIVITIES"

   ⭐⭐ AND WHAT THEY LET YOU SEE -- two timescales
     LONG period changes ..... like INCREASE IN POPULATION
     SHORT period changes .... like PEASANT REVOLTS
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the whole method of social history in one box.** The parish priest recording a baptism was not writing history; he was doing paperwork. **Seven centuries later that paperwork becomes a population graph.** ⭐ **Administrative routine is the richest historical source there is** — the same reason Indian historians read revenue records, and the same reason a Harappan seal (Class 6 Ch.3) tells you more than a monument.

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ MARC BLOCH (1886–1944) — learn every line of this:**

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who** | *"one of the many scholars in **France** who have worked on feudalism, **one of the earliest**"* |
| **His argument** | ⭐⭐⭐ *"history consisted of **MUCH MORE THAN JUST POLITICAL HISTORY, INTERNATIONAL RELATIONS AND THE LIVES OF GREAT PEOPLE**"* |
| **What he emphasised** | ⭐⭐⭐ **(1)** *"the importance of **GEOGRAPHY** in shaping human history"*; **(2)** *"the need to understand the **COLLECTIVE BEHAVIOUR OR ATTITUDES** of groups of people"* |
| **His book** | ***Feudal Society*** — on **European, particularly FRENCH, society between 900 and 1300**, describing *"in remarkable detail **social relations and hierarchies, land management and the popular culture** of the period"* |
| **His death** | ⚠️ *"His career was cut short tragically when he was **SHOT BY THE NAZIS IN THE SECOND WORLD WAR**."* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two things make Bloch examinable.**
> ⭐ **(1) The method.** He is the founder-generation of what is called the **Annales** approach *(the name is beyond NCERT — the idea is not)*: history as **geography + collective mentality + the long term**, rather than kings and treaties. ⭐⭐ **Everything in this chapter about weather, ploughs, harnesses, crop rotation and life expectancy exists BECAUSE of that turn.** A historian of the older school would have given you the Hundred Years War and nothing about the mould-board.
> ⭐ **(2) The biography.** ⚠️ **He was shot by the Nazis** — he had joined the French Resistance. **Pair him with Class 9 Ch.3 (*Nazism*)**: the regime that industrialised murder also shot the historian who insisted that ordinary people had a history. **That is a legitimate, powerful line in an essay on the uses of history.**

**⭐⭐⭐ One definition to memorise before going further:**

> **The `MEDIEVAL ERA` = the period in European history between the FIFTH and the FIFTEENTH centuries.** ⚠️ **A Prelims-sized fact.** *(Note that Indian "medieval" is dated differently — roughly the eighth to the eighteenth century — so **the same word means different centuries in the two syllabi.** Do not let one contaminate the other.)*

## 3️⃣ What "feudalism" actually means ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE WORD
   'FEUDALISM' -- used by HISTORIANS (not by the people who
   lived it) to describe "the ECONOMIC, LEGAL, POLITICAL and
   SOCIAL relationships that existed in Europe in the medieval
   era."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ Derived from the GERMAN word 'FEUD', which means
     ⭐ 'A PIECE OF LAND'.
                          |
   ⭐ WHERE -- "the kind of society that developed in MEDIEVAL
     FRANCE, and LATER in ENGLAND and in SOUTHERN ITALY."

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ IN THE ECONOMIC SENSE -- the definition to write in an
      answer
     "a kind of AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION which is based on the
      RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN LORDS AND PEASANTS."
        - the peasants cultivated THEIR OWN LAND AS WELL AS
          THAT OF THE LORD
        - the peasants performed LABOUR SERVICES for the lords
        - the lords in exchange provided MILITARY PROTECTION
        - ⭐ the lords "also had EXTENSIVE JUDICIAL CONTROL over
          peasants"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ "Thus, feudalism WENT BEYOND THE ECONOMIC to cover the
     SOCIAL AND POLITICAL aspects of life as well."

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE DATING TRAP
     ROOTS -- traced to practices in the ROMAN EMPIRE and in the
       age of the French king CHARLEMAGNE (742-814)
     BUT as "an ESTABLISHED WAY OF LIFE in large parts of
       Europe" it "may be said to have EMERGED LATER, IN THE
       ⭐ ELEVENTH CENTURY."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **Note the three-part structure of the word's meaning, because a careless answer collapses it.** **Feudalism is (a) an ECONOMY** — who works whose land; **(b) a LAW** — the lord judges his own peasants; **(c) a POLITICS** — the lord raises troops, holds courts, mints coin. ⭐ **A lord is landlord, judge, general and mint in one person.** That fusion of roles is what the modern state will later pull apart and monopolise — and **that** is why this chapter ends with kings, taxes and standing armies.

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The cross-link worth building now — the INDIAN FEUDALISM debate** *(beyond NCERT, but a standard Mains and optional-paper theme):*

> Indian historians have long argued over whether **the land grants of early medieval India** — the *brahmadeya* and *agrahara* grants of **Class 6 Ch.9**, and the ***samanta*** chiefs of **Class 7 Ch.2** — amount to an Indian feudalism. **The similarities are real**: land given away in perpetuity, revenue and judicial rights transferred with it, service owed upward, a decline of towns and coinage. ⚠️ **The differences are equally real**: **no vassalage contract sworn on the Bible**, **no manor with the lord's demesne worked by servile labour**, **no serfdom in the European legal sense**, and above all **caste**, which organised Indian labour in a way no European category matches. ⭐ **The safe examination position: "feudal-like" relations of dependence existed, but the European model cannot be exported wholesale.** Use the comparison to show range; do not claim identity.

## 4️⃣ France and England — the two laboratories ⭐⭐⭐

**⭐⭐ FRANCE — from a Roman province to a Frankish kingdom:**

```
   ⭐ GAUL, a province of the ROMAN EMPIRE
     - TWO extensive coastlines
     - mountain ranges
     - long rivers
     - forests
     - ⭐ "large tracts of PLAINS SUITED TO AGRICULTURE"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ "The FRANKS, a GERMANIC TRIBE, gave their name to Gaul,
     making it 'FRANCE'."
                          |
   From the SIXTH CENTURY -- a kingdom ruled by Frankish/French
   kings, who were CHRISTIAN.
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE CHURCH-STATE BARGAIN
     "The French had very strong links with the Church, which
      were further strengthened when in ⭐ 800 THE POPE GAVE
      KING CHARLEMAGNE THE TITLE OF 'HOLY ROMAN EMPEROR',
      ⭐⭐ TO ENSURE HIS SUPPORT."
                          |
   ⚠️ NCERT'S FOOTNOTE -- easy to miss, examinable
     "The head of the EASTERN CHURCH, in CONSTANTINOPLE, had a
      SIMILAR RELATIONSHIP with the BYZANTINE EMPEROR."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read the Charlemagne sentence in the direction NCERT wrote it.** It does **not** say the Pope rewarded a great king. It says the Pope **gave a title in order to secure a protector**. ⭐ **The crown was the price of an alliance, and the alliance ran both ways**: the king got sacred legitimacy, the Pope got an army. **That reciprocal bargain — sacred sanction traded for armed protection — is the single most transferable idea in the chapter**, and it lets you compare, with care, the *sulh-i kul* politics of **Class 7 Ch.4**, Ashoka's use of *dhamma* (**Class 6 Ch.7**), and the Byzantine arrangement in the footnote.

**⭐⭐⭐ Early History of France — NCERT's timeline (learn the four starred entries):**

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **481** | **CLOVIS becomes king of the Franks** |
| **486** | Clovis and the Franks begin the **conquest of northern Gaul** |
| ⭐ **496** | **Clovis and the Franks CONVERT TO CHRISTIANITY** |
| **714** | **CHARLES MARTEL becomes mayor of the palace** |
| ⭐ **751** | **Martel's son PEPIN deposes the Frankish ruler**, becomes king and **establishes a dynasty**; wars of conquest **double the size of his kingdom** |
| **768** | Pepin succeeded by his son **CHARLEMAGNE / Charles the Great** |
| ⭐⭐ **800** | **POPE LEO III crowns Charlemagne HOLY ROMAN EMPEROR** |
| ⭐ **840 onwards** | **Raids by VIKINGS from NORWAY** |

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐ **Two traps in that table.** **(1) Charles Martel was NOT a king** — he was **"mayor of the palace"**, the office that really held power; ⭐ **it was his SON PEPIN who deposed the nominal ruler and took the crown**, and **Charlemagne is Pepin's son**. Three generations, only two of them kings. **(2) The Viking raids from 840 belong in the causal story**, not in a list of dates: **raids are exactly the "frequent military conflict" that made local protection worth more than a distant king** — ⭐ **they are part of the answer to "why did feudalism happen?"**

**⭐⭐ ENGLAND — one sentence here, developed in §9:**

> *"Across a narrow channel lay the island of **England-Scotland**, which in the **eleventh century** was **conquered by a DUKE from the French province of NORMANDY**."* ⭐ **Hold this fact: the English ruling class after 1066 is French-speaking.** It is why England and France then spend centuries at war *"because of disputes over territory and trade"* — **they are quarrelling relatives, not strangers.**

## 5️⃣ The idea of the three orders — hierarchy justified by function ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE DEFINING SENTENCE -- quote it verbatim in Mains

   "FRENCH PRIESTS believed in the concept that people were
    members of one of the three 'ORDERS', DEPENDING ON THEIR
    WORK."

   A BISHOP STATED:
     ⭐⭐⭐ "HERE BELOW, SOME PRAY, OTHERS FIGHT,
            STILL OTHERS WORK..."
                          |
     THE CLERGY  ....  those who PRAY    (First Order)
     THE NOBILITY ...  those who FIGHT   (Second Order)
     THE PEASANTRY ..  those who WORK    (Third Order)

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ NOTE WHO IS SPEAKING.
     It is PRIESTS who describe the scheme -- and priests who
     place THEMSELVES FIRST. "Priests placed themselves in the
     first order, and nobles in the second."
     ⭐ A social theory is never neutral about the position of
       the people who wrote it.
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ ABBESS HILDEGARD OF BINGEN, twelfth century — the chapter's most revealing quotation:**

> *"**Who would think of herding his entire cattle in one stable — cows, donkeys, sheep, goats, without difference?** Therefore it is necessary to **establish difference among human beings, so that they do not destroy each other** ... **God makes distinctions among his flock, in heaven as on earth. ⭐ All are loved by him, yet there is no equality among them.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Take this quotation apart, because its structure is the structure of every hierarchy-justifying argument you will meet in this syllabus.**
> ⭐ **Step 1 — an analogy from the farmyard**, which feels obvious and therefore goes unexamined.
> ⭐ **Step 2 — a claim that difference PREVENTS HARM** (*"so that they do not destroy each other"*): inequality is presented not as a privilege but as a **safety measure**.
> ⭐ **Step 3 — divine sanction**: God himself sorts the flock, so the arrangement is not human and cannot be renegotiated.
> ⭐⭐ **Step 4 — the softener**: *"all are loved by him"* — everyone is valued, **just not equally placed**. ⚠️ **That last move is the important one.** It lets a hierarchy describe itself as harmonious rather than exploitative. **You have met exactly this argument before** — in the *Purusha-sukta* justification of *varna* (Class 6), and you will meet it again in every defence of a graded order. ⭐⭐⭐ **The examinable point is the ARGUMENT-SHAPE, not the individual instance:** *natural analogy → order prevents chaos → divine authorship → everyone is loved.*

> ⭐⭐ **And notice who is making it.** **Hildegard is a WOMAN, an abbess, and a gifted musician** *(she reappears in §7 as the developer of community singing in church)* — **in a chapter that will tell you women could not become priests.** ⚠️ **The people who articulate a hierarchy most eloquently are often people whom that hierarchy limits.** That is a genuinely sophisticated observation and it will lift a Mains answer.

**🔑 Vocabulary note from the margin:** *"'**ABBEY**' is derived from the **Syriac abba, meaning FATHER**. An abbey was governed by an **ABBOT** or an **ABBESS**."*

## 6️⃣ The Second Order — the nobility ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 🤝 6.1 Vassalage — a contract, not merely a command

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ WHY THE NOBILITY MATTERED
   "The nobility had, in reality, a CENTRAL ROLE in social
    processes. This is because THEY CONTROLLED LAND. This
    control was the outcome of a practice called ⭐ 'VASSALAGE'."

   ⭐⭐⭐ THE CHAIN -- and note it repeats at every level
     THE KING
        | the big landowners -- the NOBLES -- are VASSALS of
        | the king
     THE NOBLES
        | the PEASANTS are vassals of the landowners
     THE PEASANTS

   ⭐⭐ Similar to "the practice among the GERMANIC PEOPLES, of
     whom the FRANKS WERE ONE."

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE MUTUAL PROMISE -- the heart of it
     A nobleman accepted the king as his ⭐ SEIGNEUR (SENIOR),
     and THEY MADE A MUTUAL PROMISE:
        the SEIGNEUR / LORD would PROTECT the vassal
        the VASSAL would be LOYAL to him
     ⭐ 'LORD' is "derived from a word meaning ONE WHO PROVIDED
       BREAD."

   ⭐⭐⭐ THE CEREMONY -- Prelims-friendly detail
     "elaborate RITUALS and EXCHANGE OF VOWS taken ON THE BIBLE
      IN A CHURCH."
     At the ceremony the vassal received, AS A SYMBOL OF THE
     LAND being given to him:
        a WRITTEN CHARTER,  or
        a STAFF,            or
        ⭐ EVEN A CLOD OF EARTH
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three things in that box repay a second look.**
> ⭐ **(1) It is RECIPROCAL.** Protection is owed downward, loyalty upward. **A lord who fails to protect has broken the contract.** This is not the same as slavery or absolute subjection, and Mains answers that flatten it into "oppression" lose the distinction NCERT is drawing.
> ⭐ **(2) It is SWORN ON THE BIBLE IN A CHURCH.** ⭐⭐ **With no state to enforce contracts, RELIGION is the enforcement mechanism** — breaking the vow is not illegal, it is a sin. **The first order underwrites the second order's property system.** That is why the two orders defend each other so consistently.
> ⭐ **(3) The CLOD OF EARTH.** In a society where **most people could not read**, a written charter is useless as a public act — so the transfer of land is made **visible and physical**. ⭐ **Handing over a lump of soil in front of witnesses IS the deed of title.** *(The same logic explains the Indian practice of recording grants on copper plates that could be produced and read aloud generations later.)*

### 👑 6.2 The noble's four privileges — a state in miniature

| Privilege | NCERT's wording | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Property** | *"**absolute control over his property, IN PERPETUITY**"* | ⭐⭐ Not a salary or a posting — **heritable, permanent**. A king cannot simply recall it |
| **Army** | *"He could **raise troops called 'FEUDAL LEVIES'**"* | ⭐⭐⭐ **Private military force.** Kill this and feudalism dies — which is exactly what §14 describes |
| **Justice** | *"The lord **held his own COURTS OF JUSTICE**"* | ⭐⭐⭐ The lord judges the people who work his land. **No appeal to a higher law** |
| **Money** | *"and could even **COIN HIS OWN MONEY**"* | ⭐⭐⭐ **The most startling one.** Minting is the sharpest possible marker of sovereignty |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Put those four together and read the result: the noble is a small sovereign.** He owns the land, commands the soldiers, runs the courts and issues the currency. ⚠️ **This is the correct way to understand what "the fall of Rome" meant in practice** — not that government disappeared, but that **it fragmented into hundreds of miniature governments**, each the size of an estate. ⭐⭐ **And it tells you exactly what the "new monarchs" of §14 had to take back: taxation, the army, the courts and the mint.** *(Compare the four with what a modern state reserves to itself, and you have the whole of the state-formation argument in one table.)*

**⭐⭐ What the lord physically held:** *"He was the **lord of all the people settled on his land**. He owned **vast tracts of land** which contained **his own dwellings, his private fields and pastures**, and **the homes and fields of his tenant-peasants**. **His house was called a MANOR.** His private lands were cultivated by peasants, **who were also expected to act as FOOT-SOLDIERS IN BATTLE when required, in addition to working on their own farms.**"*

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐ **Note that last clause and keep it for the knights section.** The peasant is **already** the lord's soldier — **an unpaid, untrained, part-time one.** ⭐ **The knight exists because that arrangement stopped working**, and §6.4 says so explicitly.

### 🏰 6.3 The manorial estate ⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐ SCALE
     "A lord had HIS OWN MANOR-HOUSE. He also CONTROLLED
      VILLAGES -- ⭐ SOME LORDS CONTROLLED HUNDREDS OF VILLAGES."
     a SMALL manorial estate .... about A DOZEN FAMILIES
     a LARGER estate ............ FIFTY OR SIXTY families

   ⭐⭐ WHAT WAS ON IT -- "almost everything needed for daily
      life was found on the estate"
     GRAIN grown in the fields
     BLACKSMITHS and CARPENTERS -- maintained the lord's
        implements, repaired his weapons
     STONEMASONS -- looked after his buildings
     WOMEN -- spun and wove fabric
     ⭐ CHILDREN -- worked in the lord's WINE-PRESSES
     WOODLANDS AND FORESTS -- where the lords HUNTED
     PASTURES -- where his CATTLE AND HORSES grazed
     A CHURCH on the estate
     A CASTLE for defence

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ BUT -- THE SENTENCE THAT MATTERS MOST
     "The manor COULD NOT BE COMPLETELY SELF-SUFFICIENT because
      ⭐ SALT, MILLSTONES and METALWARE had to be obtained from
      OUTSIDE SOURCES."
     And luxury-minded lords had to buy from elsewhere:
        RICH FURNISHINGS, MUSICAL INSTRUMENTS, ORNAMENTS
        "not locally produced."

   ⭐ CASTLES -- a dating point
     "From the THIRTEENTH CENTURY, some castles were made
      BIGGER for use as a RESIDENCE for a knight's family."
     ⚠️ "In fact, in ENGLAND CASTLES WERE PRACTICALLY UNKNOWN
        BEFORE THE NORMAN CONQUEST, and developed as CENTRES OF
        POLITICAL ADMINISTRATION AND MILITARY POWER under the
        feudal system."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The "not completely self-sufficient" line is a trap-answer waiting to happen, and it is also the hinge of the chapter.** The standard textbook picture of the manor is a **closed, self-feeding world** — and NCERT deliberately punctures it. ⭐ **Salt, millstones and metalware must come from outside.** ⭐⭐ **Which means TRADE never stopped, only shrank** — and therefore **the revival of towns in §11 is not a miracle, it is a re-expansion of something that had merely contracted.** ⚠️ **In an answer, never write that the manor was self-sufficient. Write that it aimed at self-sufficiency and could not achieve it — and name the three goods.**

> ⭐⭐ **On castles: notice that they are described as CENTRES OF ADMINISTRATION, not just fortresses.** A castle is the lord's office, court, treasury, garrison and home in one building — **the four privileges of §6.2 given an address.**

### ⚔️ 6.4 The knights ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ WHY KNIGHTS APPEARED -- this is NCERT's own exercise
      question, so build the answer here

   "From the NINTH CENTURY, there were FREQUENT LOCALISED WARS
    in Europe."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ "The AMATEUR PEASANT-SOLDIERS WERE NOT SUFFICIENT, and
     ⭐ GOOD CAVALRY WAS NEEDED."
                          |
   "This led to the growing importance of a NEW SECTION OF
    PEOPLE -- THE KNIGHTS."
                          |
   ⭐ THE SAME CHAIN, ONE LINK LOWER
     "They were LINKED TO THE LORDS, just as the latter were
      linked to the king."

   ⭐⭐⭐ THE FIEF -- the knight's equivalent of the noble's land
     The lord GAVE the knight a piece of land called a ⭐ 'FIEF'
     and PROMISED TO PROTECT IT.
     ⭐ "The fief COULD BE INHERITED."
     SIZE -- "anything between ⭐ 1,000 AND 2,000 ACRES OR MORE"
     ON IT --
        a HOUSE for the knight and his family
        a CHURCH
        "other establishments to house his DEPENDANTS"
        a WATERMILL
        a WINE-PRESS
     ⭐ "As in the feudal manor, THE LAND OF THE FIEF WAS
       CULTIVATED BY PEASANTS."

   ⭐⭐ WHAT THE KNIGHT OWED IN RETURN
        "paid his lord a REGULAR FEE"
        "PROMISED TO FIGHT FOR HIM IN WAR"

   ⭐⭐ TRAINING -- "To keep up their skills, knights spent time
     EACH DAY FENCING and PRACTISING TACTICS WITH DUMMIES."

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE DIVIDED-LOYALTY POINT -- a favourite of examiners
     "A KNIGHT MIGHT SERVE MORE THAN ONE LORD, but his
      ⭐ FOREMOST LOYALTY WAS TO HIS OWN LORD."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why the peasant-soldier failed, in one sentence: a cavalryman is a full-time professional and a farmer is not.** ⭐ **A horse must be bred, fed, shod and trained; armour must be bought; and the man must practise EVERY DAY** — the chapter says exactly that. **None of it is compatible with sowing and harvesting.** ⭐⭐ **So the society must find a way to support a man who does nothing but fight — and the only currency it has is LAND.** *That* is what a fief is: **not a reward, but a maintenance grant for a specialist.**

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The divided-loyalty clause is the structural weakness of the whole system, and NCERT drops it in almost casually.** If a knight may serve several lords, then **when two of his lords go to war with each other, the system has no answer.** ⭐⭐ **A pyramid of PERSONAL promises cannot resolve conflicts between the promises** — there is no higher authority, only more promises. ⭐ **This is precisely the gap that kings will exploit in §14 when they replace feudal levies with paid, trained infantry who have exactly ONE employer.**

**⭐⭐ ANSWERING NCERT'S QUESTION — *"Why did knights become a distinct group, and when did they decline?"***

> **Rise (from the 9th century):** frequent localised wars → amateur peasant-soldiers inadequate → **good cavalry needed** → a specialist warrior maintained by an inheritable **fief** of 1,000–2,000+ acres worked by peasants, in exchange for a regular fee and military service.
> **Decline (15th–16th centuries, from §14):** ⭐⭐ **"Rulers DISPENSED WITH THE SYSTEM OF FEUDAL LEVIES for their armies and introduced PROFESSIONALLY TRAINED INFANTRY EQUIPPED WITH GUNS AND SIEGE ARTILLERY directly under their control. ⭐ The resistance of the aristocracies CRUMBLED IN THE FACE OF THE FIREPOWER OF THE KINGS."** ⭐⭐⭐ **Gunpowder ends the knight** — armour stops being protection and the castle stops being safe. **(Cross-refer Theme 3, which NCERT itself cites here.)**

**🎵 The minstrels — the estate's newsroom and entertainment:**

> From the **twelfth century in France**, **minstrels travelled from manor to manor**, *"singing songs which told stories — **partly historical, partly invented** — about **brave kings and knights**."* ⭐ *"In an age when **not too many people could read and manuscripts were few**, these travelling bards were very popular."* Many manors had a **narrow balcony above the large hall** where people gathered for meals — **the MINSTRELS' GALLERY** — *"from where singers entertained nobles while they feasted."*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Do not treat this as decoration.** In a non-literate society, **the song IS the medium** — it carries news, reputation, genealogy and the code of conduct expected of a knight. ⭐ **"Partly historical, partly invented" is NCERT quietly telling you what kind of source this is**, and the phrase is a gift in any answer about oral tradition as historical evidence. *(Compare **Class 10 Ch.5** on print doing this job later, and the bardic traditions behind Rajput genealogies.)*

**⭐⭐ The loyalty ideal, in the knights' own literature —** ***Doon de Mayence***, a **thirteenth-century French poem (to be sung)**:

> *"**If my dear lord is slain, his fate I'll share, / If he is hanged, then hang me by his side. / If to the stake he goes, with him I'll burn; / And if he's drowned, then let me drown with him.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Set this beside the divided-loyalty clause above and you can see the culture doing repair work on the structure.** The system permits a knight to serve several lords; the songs insist that loyalty is absolute and to the death. ⭐ **When an ideal is proclaimed that loudly, it is usually because reality kept falling short of it.** **That is a sharp, defensible line for a Mains answer on chivalry.**

## 7️⃣ The First Order — the clergy ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ WHY THE CHURCH WAS INDEPENDENT OF THE KING
      -- three powers, learn them as a set

   1. "The Catholic Church had ⭐ ITS OWN LAWS"
   2. "OWNED LANDS given to it by rulers"
   3. "and could ⭐ LEVY TAXES"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ "It was thus a VERY POWERFUL INSTITUTION WHICH DID NOT
     DEPEND ON THE KING."

   ⭐ THE STRUCTURE
     THE POPE -- head of the western Church, LIVED IN ROME
        |
     BISHOPS and CLERICS -- "who constituted the first 'order'"
        |
     THE VILLAGE PRIEST -- "MOST VILLAGES HAD THEIR OWN CHURCH,
        where people assembled EVERY SUNDAY to listen to the
        SERMON by the priest and to pray together."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Those three powers are the definition of a rival state.** Own law, own land, own taxes — **and a chain of command that ends in Rome, not in Paris or London.** ⭐⭐ **This is the structural reason for every Church-versus-king conflict in European history**, and it is the thing **Henry VIII eventually cuts** (Theme 5: *"in England the rulers ended the connection with the Pope and the monarch became head of the Church"*). ⭐ **Notice also what it gives ordinary people: an institution present in EVERY VILLAGE, meeting EVERY SUNDAY.** No king had that reach. **The parish is the densest administrative network in medieval Europe** — which is exactly why its registers became the historian's best source (§2).

**⭐⭐⭐ WHO COULD NOT BECOME A PRIEST — a four-part exclusion, and a clean Prelims fact:**

| Excluded | NCERT's wording |
|---|---|
| **Serfs** | *"**Serfs were banned**"* |
| **The physically challenged** | *"as were **the physically challenged**"* |
| **Women** | ⭐ *"**Women could not become priests**"* |
| **(A condition, not an exclusion)** | *"**Men who became priests could not marry**"* |

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐ **The serf exclusion is the one to notice.** ⭐⭐ **The Church, which taught the spiritual equality of all souls, closed its own career ladder to the bottom order.** **The first order recruits from the second, not the third.** Hierarchy is not merely described by the clergy — **it is reproduced inside the clergy.** *(And the marriage ban has a hard institutional logic worth stating: **a celibate clergy has no legitimate heirs, so Church land cannot be privatised into a family inheritance.** Celibacy protects the endowment.)*

**⭐⭐⭐ The bishops — "the religious nobility":**

> *"Bishops were the **RELIGIOUS NOBILITY**. **Like lords who owned vast landed estates, the bishops also had the use of vast estates, and lived in grand palaces.**"* ⭐⭐ **The phrase is NCERT's own and it is worth quoting** — it tells you the first and second orders are **socially the same people in different clothes.**

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Church's two income streams — get the distinction exactly right:**

| | **TITHE** | **TAILLE** *(from §8, placed here for contrast)* |
|---|---|---|
| **Who takes it** | ⭐ **The CHURCH** | ⭐ **The KING** |
| **What it is** | *"entitled to **a TENTH SHARE of whatever the peasants produced from their land over the course of the year**"* | *"one **DIRECT TAX** that kings **sometimes** imposed on peasants"* |
| **Who pays** | Peasants | Peasants |
| **Who is exempt** | — | ⭐⭐ **"the CLERGY AND NOBLES were EXEMPTED from paying this"** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This single comparison is the highest-value thing in the chapter for Mains, because it is the direct ancestor of 1789.** ⭐⭐ **Class 9 Ch.1 lists exactly these burdens on the Third Estate — the tithe to the Church, the taille to the state — and exactly this exemption of the first two estates.** ⭐ **Learn the pair together and you can answer both chapters with one memory.** **The French Revolution's core fiscal grievance is written down here, five centuries early: the two orders that owned the land paid no direct tax, and the order that worked it paid both.**

**⭐⭐ The Church's second income: endowments.** *"Money also came in the form of **ENDOWMENTS made by the rich for THEIR OWN WELFARE and the welfare of their DECEASED RELATIVES IN THE AFTERLIFE**."* ⭐ **Keep this line — it is the seed of the INDULGENCES controversy in Theme 5.** Once salvation can be assisted by payment, the step to selling documents that *"freed the buyer from the burden of his sins"* is a short one, and **Luther takes his stand exactly there.**

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Feudal culture INSIDE the Church — a beautiful, examinable observation:**

```
   "Some of the important CEREMONIES conducted by the Church
    COPIED FORMAL CUSTOMS OF THE FEUDAL ELITE."

   ⭐⭐ THE ACT OF KNEELING WHILE PRAYING
        -- hands clasped, head bowed --
        "was AN EXACT REPLICA of the way in which A KNIGHT
         CONDUCTED HIMSELF WHILE TAKING VOWS OF LOYALTY TO
         HIS LORD."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ THE USE OF THE TERM ⭐ 'LORD' FOR GOD
        -- "another example of feudal culture that found its
           way into the practices of the Church."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ "Thus, THE RELIGIOUS AND THE LAY WORLDS OF FEUDALISM
     SHARED MANY CUSTOMS AND SYMBOLS."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the chapter's most elegant idea and it deserves a minute.** We usually assume religion shapes society. ⭐⭐ **Here the influence runs the OTHER WAY: a social arrangement — vassalage — reshapes how people pray.** The believer kneels before God **in the posture of a knight swearing fealty**, and calls God by **the title of a landholder**. ⭐⭐⭐ **Prayer is remodelled as an act of homage.** ⚠️ **In an answer, use it to make the general point that religious form encodes social structure** — and note that **Indian devotional vocabulary does the same thing**, addressing the deity as *swami*, *nath* or *raja*, with the temple organised as a royal household. *(That parallel is beyond NCERT; use it as illustration, not as a claim about influence.)*

### ⛪ 7.1 Monks, monasteries and friars ⭐⭐⭐

**🔑 Margin note:** *"The word '**MONASTERY**' is derived from the **GREEK word 'MONOS', meaning SOMEONE WHO LIVES ALONE**."*

```
   ⭐⭐ THE CONTRAST NCERT DRAWS
     CLERICS ... "lived AMONGST PEOPLE in towns and villages"
     MONKS .... "chose to live ISOLATED LIVES", in communities
                called ABBEYS or MONASTERIES, "often in places
                ⭐ VERY FAR FROM HUMAN HABITATION"

   ⭐⭐⭐ THE TWO FAMOUS FOUNDATIONS -- both are Prelims-sized
     529 -- ST BENEDICT, in ITALY
     910 -- CLUNY, in BURGUNDY

   ⭐⭐ THE VOWS
     "to REMAIN IN THE ABBEY FOR THE REST OF THEIR LIVES"
     "to spend their time in ⭐ PRAYER, STUDY AND MANUAL LABOUR,
      LIKE FARMING"

   ⭐⭐⭐ OPEN TO WOMEN -- the important difference from priesthood
     "UNLIKE PRIESTHOOD, THIS LIFE WAS OPEN TO BOTH MEN AND
      WOMEN -- men became MONKS and women NUNS."
     ⚠️ "Except in a few cases, ALL ABBEYS WERE SINGLE-SEX
        COMMUNITIES."
     "Like priests, MONKS AND NUNS DID NOT MARRY."

   ⭐⭐⭐ GROWTH -- from a retreat into an institution
     FROM small communities of 10 OR 20 men/women
     TO   communities "OFTEN OF SEVERAL HUNDRED", with
          - LARGE BUILDINGS and LANDED ESTATES
          - attached ⭐ SCHOOLS OR COLLEGES
          - and ⭐ HOSPITALS
     "They CONTRIBUTED TO THE DEVELOPMENT OF THE ARTS."
     ⭐ ABBESS HILDEGARD -- "a gifted MUSICIAN, and did much to
       develop the practice of ⭐ COMMUNITY SINGING OF PRAYERS
       IN CHURCH."

   ⭐⭐⭐ FRIARS -- from the THIRTEENTH CENTURY
     "some groups of monks -- called ⭐ FRIARS -- chose
      ⭐ NOT TO BE BASED IN A MONASTERY but to MOVE FROM PLACE
      TO PLACE, PREACHING TO THE PEOPLE and LIVING ON CHARITY."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ANSWERING NCERT'S QUESTION — *"What was the function of medieval monasteries?"*** Give it as **five functions, not one**: **(1) RELIGIOUS** — a life of prayer and withdrawal, under vows; **(2) ECONOMIC** — manual labour and farming on **large landed estates**, making the abbey a major producer and landholder; **(3) EDUCATIONAL** — **attached schools and colleges**, and the copying of manuscripts *(the Benedictine rule sets aside fixed hours for "sacred reading")*; **(4) SOCIAL WELFARE** — **hospitals**, and charity; **(5) CULTURAL** — *"they contributed to the development of the arts"*, with **Hildegard developing community singing**. ⭐⭐ **Add the sixth, which is really the point: it was one of the very few institutional lives OPEN TO WOMEN** in a Church that barred them from priesthood — **the abbess is the highest office a medieval woman could hold in her own right.**

**⭐⭐⭐ THE BENEDICTINE RULE — a manuscript of 73 chapters, "followed by monks for many centuries". NCERT prints five:**

| Chapter | Rule | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | *"**Permission to speak should RARELY be granted to monks**"* | Silence as discipline |
| **7** | ⭐ *"**HUMILITY MEANS OBEDIENCE**"* | The equation the whole order rests on |
| **33** | ⭐ *"**No monk should own PRIVATE PROPERTY**"* | ⭐⭐ **A propertyless elite inside a land-obsessed society** |
| **47** | ⭐⭐ *"**IDLENESS IS THE ENEMY OF THE SOUL**, so friars and sisters should be occupied at certain times in **MANUAL LABOUR**, and at fixed hours in **SACRED READING**"* | ⭐⭐⭐ **Manual work made HOLY** — in a society where the third order works and the first two do not |
| **48** | *"The monastery should be laid out in such a way that **ALL NECESSITIES BE FOUND WITHIN ITS BOUNDS: WATER, MILL, GARDEN, WORKSHOPS**"* | ⭐ **Designed self-sufficiency** — the aim the manor could not reach |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Chapter 47 is the one to quote.** In a society whose entire theory says *some pray, some fight, some work*, **the Benedictine rule tells the men who pray that they must also work** — ⭐ **and thereby ranks manual labour as spiritual discipline rather than as the mark of the lowest order.** ⚠️ **That is a real crack in the three-order theory, opened from inside the first order.** *(It also anticipates a much later and much larger argument about work and worth — worth a line in an essay on the dignity of labour.)*

**⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE FOURTEENTH-CENTURY REVOLT AGAINST MONASTICISM — do not skip this, it is where the chapter connects to Theme 5:**

```
   "By the FOURTEENTH CENTURY, there was a GROWING UNCERTAINTY
    ABOUT THE VALUE AND PURPOSE OF MONASTICISM."

   ⭐⭐ IN ENGLAND -- LANGLAND'S 'PIERS PLOWMAN' (c.1360-70)
     contrasted "the EASE AND LUXURY of the lives of some monks"
     with the ⭐ "'PURE FAITH' of 'SIMPLE PLOUGHMEN AND SHEPHERDS
     AND POOR COMMON LABOURERS.'"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ ALSO IN ENGLAND -- CHAUCER'S 'CANTERBURY TALES'
     "had ⭐ COMIC PORTRAITS OF A NUN, A MONK AND A FRIAR."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Look at what Langland is doing: he inverts the three orders.** The order that prays is accused of **ease and luxury**; the order that works is credited with **"pure faith."** ⭐⭐ **The hierarchy is being turned upside down USING ITS OWN VALUES** — and this is happening in the **1360s**, ⚠️ **immediately after the Black Death**, and **twenty years before the English peasants' revolt of 1381 (§13)**. ⭐⭐⭐ **Criticism of the Church, a labour shortage and a peasant rising are not three separate facts in this century — they are one situation.** **And the line runs straight on to Theme 5: the Protestant grievances of 1517 begin here, in satire, a century and a half early.**

### 🎄 7.2 The Church and society — how Christianity absorbed what it replaced ⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE CORE OBSERVATION
   "Though Europeans became Christian, they ⭐ STILL HELD ON TO
    SOME OF THEIR OLD BELIEFS IN MAGIC AND FOLK TRADITIONS."

   ⭐⭐⭐ CHRISTMAS -- "important from the FOURTH CENTURY"
     Christ's birth, celebrated on 25 DECEMBER,
     ⭐ "REPLACED AN OLD PRE-ROMAN FESTIVAL, the date of which
       was calculated by the ⭐ SOLAR CALENDAR."
     -> so the date is FIXED.
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ EASTER -- "marked the CRUCIFIXION of Christ and his
     RISING FROM THE DEAD."
     ⚠️ "But its date was NOT A FIXED ONE, because it replaced
        an OLDER FESTIVAL TO CELEBRATE THE COMING OF SPRING
        after a long winter, dated by the ⭐ LUNAR CALENDAR."
     -> so the date MOVES.

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE PARISH -- watch a village become an administrative
      unit
     "Traditionally, on that day, people of each village used to
      make A TOUR OF THEIR VILLAGE LANDS. With the coming of
      Christianity, THEY CONTINUED TO DO THIS, but they called
      the village the ⭐ 'PARISH' (THE AREA UNDER THE SUPERVISION
      OF ONE PRIEST)."

   ⭐⭐ HOLY DAYS
     "OVERWORKED PEASANTS WELCOMED 'HOLY DAYS'/HOLIDAYS because
      THEY WERE NOT EXPECTED TO WORK THEN. These days were meant
      for prayer, but ⭐ PEOPLE USUALLY SPENT A GOOD PART OF THEM
      HAVING FUN AND FEASTING."
     ⭐ THE WORD 'HOLIDAY' IS LITERALLY 'HOLY DAY'.
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why the two great festivals are dated differently is the best small puzzle in the chapter, and the answer is genuinely satisfying.** ⭐ **Christmas is fixed because the festival it replaced was SOLAR** — tied to the sun's own turning at midwinter, which falls on the same date every year. ⭐ **Easter moves because the festival it replaced was LUNAR** — tied to the spring moon, which does not. ⭐⭐ **The Christian calendar is carrying the shape of a pre-Christian one.** ⚠️ **The general principle is the examinable part: a new religion does not usually erase the old festivals, it OVERWRITES them — keeping the date, the season and the practice, and changing the meaning.** *(India offers the same pattern in abundance — harvest festivals, solstice observances and local deities absorbed into larger traditions — which makes this a strong, safe comparison in a GS1 culture answer.)*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **And notice the parish.** The **villagers' old boundary-walking custom survives intact** — but the ground they walk is renamed after **the priest who supervises it**. ⭐ **A ritual becomes a jurisdiction.** That is how the Church acquired the village-level administrative grid that made its registers (§2) so complete.

**⭐⭐ PILGRIMAGE:** *"Pilgrimage was an **important part of a Christian's life**, and **many people went on long journeys to shrines of MARTYRS or to BIG CHURCHES**."* **NCERT's footnote:** *"**PALMERS** — a monk who travels to distant shrines."*

> **⭐⭐ CHAUCER, *The Canterbury Tales* (c.1340–1400), the opening — written in Middle English, given here in modern translation:**
> *"When in April the sweet showers fall / And pierce the drought of March to the root / And the small birds are making melody / That sleep away the night with open eye... / (So Nature pricks them and their heart engages); / **Then people long to go on pilgrimages**, / And **palmers** long to seek the foreign shrines / Of far-off saints, revered in various lands. / And especially from every shire / **Of England, to CANTERBURY they make their journey.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Two examinable points from the Chaucer material, and they pull in opposite directions — which is why he is worth knowing.** ⭐ **(1)** He is the evidence for **pilgrimage as mass practice**, organised by season, drawing people *"from every shire."* ⭐ **(2)** He is simultaneously the evidence for **satire of the clergy** — the same book carries *"comic portraits of a nun, a monk and a friar."* ⭐⭐ **A society can be devout and sceptical at once**, and Chaucer is proof. *(For Prelims: **Chaucer c.1340–1400; The Canterbury Tales; Canterbury, England; written in MIDDLE ENGLISH** — the language point matters, because writing in the vernacular rather than Latin is itself part of the story Theme 5 finishes.)*

## 8️⃣ The Third Order — peasants, free and unfree ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐ **NCERT's own framing sentence — quote it:** *"Let us now turn to **the vast majority of people**, namely, **THOSE WHO SUSTAINED THE FIRST TWO ORDERS**."* ⭐ **The third order is not one group among three; it is the base that the other two stand on.**

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE CENTRAL DISTINCTION — free peasants versus serfs. Learn this table cold; it is the most likely Prelims question in the chapter:**

| | **FREE PEASANTS** | **SERFS** *(from the verb **"TO SERVE"**)* |
|---|---|---|
| **Whose land do they cultivate?** | ⭐ *"held their farms as **TENANTS OF THE LORD**"* | ⭐ *"cultivated plots of land, but **THESE BELONGED TO THE LORD**"* |
| **Produce** | Theirs, minus obligations | ⭐ *"**MUCH OF THE PRODUCE from this had to be given to the lord**"* |
| **Labour on the lord's own land** | ⭐ *"**usually THREE days** a week, **often more**"* — the output is **LABOUR-RENT**, going *"**directly to the lord**"* | *"They **also had to work on the land which belonged EXCLUSIVELY to the lord**"* |
| **Wages** | — | ⭐ *"They received **NO WAGES**"* |
| **Freedom of movement** | Free | ⚠️⭐⭐ *"**could NOT LEAVE THE ESTATE WITHOUT THE LORD'S PERMISSION**"* |
| **Military service** | ⭐ *"The men had to render military service **(AT LEAST FORTY DAYS EVERY YEAR)**"* | — |
| **Marriage** | Their own | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ *"The lord **COULD DECIDE WHOM A SERF SHOULD MARRY**, or might give his blessing to the serf's choice, **BUT ON PAYMENT OF A FEE**"* |
| **Monopolies owed to the lord** | — | ⭐⭐ Serfs could use **ONLY the lord's MILL** to grind flour, **his OVEN** to bake bread, **his WINE-PRESSES** to distil wine and beer |

**⭐⭐⭐ The free peasant's unpaid extras — NCERT lists them, and a good answer names them:**

> *"they could be required to do **other unpaid labour services**, like **DIGGING DITCHES, GATHERING FIREWOOD, BUILDING FENCES and REPAIRING ROADS AND BUILDINGS.**"*
> **And the household's work:** *"Besides helping in the fields, **women and children had to do other tasks. They SPUN THREAD, WOVE CLOTH, MADE CANDLES and PRESSED GRAPES to prepare wine FOR THE LORD'S USE.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read that last sentence carefully, because it is easy to skim past the important words: "FOR THE LORD'S USE."** ⭐⭐ **The obligation is not just the man's three days in the lord's fields — the whole household's labour is drawn in**, including **women's textile work and children's hands.** ⚠️ **When you write about feudal exploitation, do not describe it as a tax on the peasant; describe it as a claim on the FAMILY.** ⭐ **The unit of obligation is the household, not the individual** — the same correction that matters when writing about agrarian labour in India.

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the monopolies deserve a sentence of their own.** The mill, the oven and the wine-press are the **three pieces of capital equipment a village cannot do without** — and the lord owns all three. ⭐⭐ **This is not rent on land; it is a toll on PROCESSING.** Grain you grew yourself must still be ground on his stone; bread you kneaded yourself must still be baked in his oven. ⚠️ **A lord who controls the chokepoints does not need to own every field.** *(Keep the phrase "control of the processing chokepoint" — it travels well into economy answers.)*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE TAILLE — and the exemption that made a revolution:**

> *"There was **one DIRECT TAX called 'TAILLE'** that **kings SOMETIMES imposed on peasants** (⭐⭐ **the clergy and nobles were EXEMPTED from paying this**)."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Learn this beside the tithe (§7) and beside Class 9 Ch.1.** ⭐ **Two burdens, two beneficiaries, one payer:** the **tithe** takes a tenth of the harvest for the **Church**; the **taille** takes a direct tax for the **king** — ⭐⭐ **and the two orders that own most of the land pay neither.** ⚠️ **That is the fiscal architecture the French Revolution was called to demolish**, and being able to name it in the medieval chapter and then again in the 1789 chapter is exactly the kind of continuity that earns marks. *(The word "**taille**" appears in Class 9 Ch.1 with no explanation of where it came from. This is where it came from.)*

## 9️⃣ England — feudalism arrives by conquest ⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐ "FEUDALISM DEVELOPED IN ENGLAND FROM THE ELEVENTH
     CENTURY."

   ⭐ BEFORE THAT
     "The ANGLES and SAXONS, from CENTRAL EUROPE, had settled
      in England in the ⭐ SIXTH CENTURY. The country's name,
      ENGLAND, is a variant of ⭐ 'ANGLE-LAND'."

   ⭐⭐⭐ 1066 -- THE NORMAN CONQUEST
     "In the eleventh century, ⭐ WILLIAM, THE DUKE OF NORMANDY,
      crossed the ENGLISH CHANNEL with an army and DEFEATED THE
      SAXON KING OF ENGLAND."
     ⚠️ "From this time, FRANCE AND ENGLAND WERE OFTEN AT WAR
        because of DISPUTES OVER TERRITORY AND TRADE."

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ WHAT WILLIAM DID -- feudalism imposed in one stroke
     1. "William I had ⭐ THE LAND MAPPED"
     2. "and distributed it in sections to ⭐ 180 NORMAN NOBLES
        WHO HAD MIGRATED WITH HIM"
     3. "The lords became the ⭐ CHIEF TENANTS OF THE KING, and
        were expected to give him MILITARY HELP. They were
        obliged to ⭐ SUPPLY A CERTAIN NUMBER OF KNIGHTS TO THE
        KING."
     4. "They soon began to GIFT SOME OF THEIR OWN LANDS TO
        KNIGHTS who would serve them just as they in turn
        served the king."
     5. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ "They COULD NOT, HOWEVER, USE THEIR KNIGHTS FOR
        ⭐ PRIVATE WARFARE, WHICH WAS FORBIDDEN IN ENGLAND."
     6. "ANGLO-SAXON PEASANTS became TENANTS of various levels
        of landholders."

   ⚠️ NCERT'S FOOTNOTE: "The present QUEEN of England is
      descended from WILLIAM I."
      (Written before 2022 -- the present monarch is KING
       CHARLES III. The descent claim is unaffected.)
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The contrast between French and English feudalism is the sharpest comparative point in the chapter, and it decides the ending in §14. Get it clear now.**
>
> ⭐ **FRANCE — feudalism GREW.** It emerged gradually out of the wreckage of Rome over centuries, from the bottom up, with **no single moment of design**. Result: **lords with genuinely independent power**, private war, and a king who is one great lord among others.
>
> ⭐⭐ **ENGLAND — feudalism was INSTALLED.** In 1066 a conqueror **surveyed the whole country, allotted it to 180 followers, and set the terms.** Result: **every landholder holds from the king by an explicit contract**, the number of knights owed is specified, ⭐⭐ **and PRIVATE WARFARE IS SIMPLY BANNED.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **So the English king starts stronger — he is not the top of the pyramid, he is the ARCHITECT of it.** ⚠️ **Do not now conclude that England therefore became the more absolutist state — the opposite happened**, and §14 explains the paradox: **because the English king was strong enough to tax everyone, he had to bargain with everyone; that bargaining body became PARLIAMENT.** *(NCERT does not name the survey; it is the **Domesday Book** of 1086 — a useful fact, clearly beyond the textbook, and exactly the kind of administrative source §2 was talking about.)*

## 🔟 What broke the system open — environment, technology, money ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's hinge sentence — this is where the chapter stops describing and starts explaining:** *"While **members of the first two orders saw the social system as STABLE AND UNCHANGING**, there were several processes which were **TRANSFORMING** the system. Some of these, such as **changes in the environment, were GRADUAL AND ALMOST IMPERCEPTIBLE**. Others were **more DRAMATIC, like the changes in AGRICULTURAL TECHNOLOGY AND LAND USE.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That sentence is a thesis about how history works, and it is worth more than the details that follow it.** ⭐ **The people at the top believed they lived in an unchanging order — and were wrong**, because the forces dismantling it were **weather, ploughs, harnesses and coins**: things no chronicler thought worth recording and no bishop thought worth theorising. ⭐⭐ **This is Marc Bloch's argument (§2) demonstrated in practice.** ⚠️ **Use it as a general principle in essays: the changes that end an era are usually invisible to the people the era belongs to.**

### 🌡️ 10.1 The environment — a climate story with hard consequences

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE COLD PHASE -- FIFTH TO TENTH CENTURIES
     "MOST OF EUROPE WAS COVERED WITH VAST FORESTS."
        -> "Thus the LAND AVAILABLE FOR AGRICULTURE WAS
           LIMITED."
        -> ⭐ AND a social consequence: "peasants dissatisfied
           with their conditions COULD FLEE FROM OPPRESSION AND
           TAKE REFUGE IN THE FOREST."
     "Europe was undergoing an ⭐ INTENSELY COLD CLIMATIC SPELL"
        -> SEVERE AND PROLONGED WINTERS
        -> a SHORTENED GROWING SEASON for crops
        -> ⭐ REDUCED YIELDS from agriculture
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE WARM PHASE -- FROM THE ELEVENTH CENTURY
     "Europe entered a ⭐ WARM PHASE. AVERAGE TEMPERATURES
      INCREASED, which had a PROFOUND EFFECT ON AGRICULTURE."
        -> "peasants now had a ⭐ LONGER GROWING SEASON"
        -> "the soil, ⭐ NOW LESS SUBJECTED TO FROST, COULD BE
           MORE EASILY PLOUGHED"
        -> ⭐⭐ "ENVIRONMENTAL HISTORIANS have noted that there
           was a SIGNIFICANT RECEDING OF THE FOREST LINE in
           many parts of Europe. This made ⭐ EXPANSION OF THE
           AREA UNDER CULTIVATION POSSIBLE."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two things to carry away.** ⭐ **(1) The forest is a POLITICAL fact, not just an ecological one** — *"peasants dissatisfied with their conditions could flee... and take refuge in the forest."* **A forest is an escape route, and therefore a limit on how hard a lord can squeeze.** ⚠️ **You have met this argument before, from the other side: Class 9 Ch.4 (*Forest Society and Colonialism*) and Class 10's Gudem hill people, where the STATE closes the forest and the escape route disappears.** ⭐⭐ **Whoever controls the forest controls the peasant's alternatives.**
> ⭐ **(2) Climate is treated here as a HISTORICAL CAUSE, with named practitioners — "environmental historians."** **That is a live, current field and a good thing to be able to name in a GS3 answer**, where climate and agriculture meet.

### 🌾 10.2 Old land use — why yields were miserable

```
   ⭐⭐ THE TOOL
     "The ONLY MECHANICAL AID available to the peasant was the
      ⭐ WOODEN PLOUGH, DRAWN BY A TEAM OF OXEN."
     "This plough could at best ⭐ SCRATCH THE SURFACE OF THE
      EARTH and was UNABLE TO FULLY DRAW OUT THE NATURAL
      PRODUCTIVITY OF THE SOIL."
     -> "Agriculture was therefore ⭐ VERY LABOUR INTENSIVE."
     -> "FIELDS HAD TO BE DUG BY HAND, ⭐ OFTEN ONCE IN FOUR
        YEARS, and ENORMOUS MANUAL LABOUR WAS REQUIRED."

   ⭐⭐⭐ THE ROTATION -- the TWO-FIELD system
     "an INEFFECTIVE method of crop rotation was in use."
     The land was divided IN HALF:
        FIELD A -- planted in AUTUMN with WINTER WHEAT
        FIELD B -- left FALLOW
     The NEXT YEAR:
        RYE planted on the previously fallow land
        the other half put to fallow
     ⭐⭐ RESULT -- "the soil SLOWLY DETERIORATED, and FAMINES
       WERE NOT UNCOMMON. ⭐ CHRONIC MALNUTRITION ALTERNATED
       WITH DEVASTATING FAMINES and life was difficult for
       the poor."
     ⚠️ NOTE THE ARITHMETIC: HALF the land is idle EVERY YEAR.
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The squeeze, and the peasants' answer — PASSIVE RESISTANCE:**

```
   ⭐⭐ THE LORDS' PROBLEM
     "the lords were ANXIOUS TO MAXIMISE THEIR INCOMES. Since it
      was ⭐ NOT POSSIBLE TO INCREASE OUTPUT FROM THE LAND, the
      peasants were FORCED to
        - bring under cultivation ALL the land in the manorial
          estate
        - and ⭐ SPEND MORE TIME DOING THIS THAN THEY WERE
          LEGALLY BOUND TO DO."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE PEASANTS' ANSWER
     "The peasants DID NOT BOW QUIETLY TO OPPRESSION. ⭐ SINCE
      THEY COULD NOT PROTEST OPENLY, THEY RESORTED TO
      ⭐⭐ PASSIVE RESISTANCE."
        - "They spent MORE TIME CULTIVATING THEIR OWN FIELDS,
           and KEPT MUCH OF THE PRODUCT OF THAT LABOUR FOR
           THEMSELVES."
        - "They also ⭐ AVOIDED PERFORMING UNPAID EXTRA
           SERVICES."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE STRUCTURAL CONFLICT -- over the COMMONS
     "They came into conflict with the lords over ⭐ PASTURE AND
      FOREST LANDS, and saw these lands as ⭐⭐ RESOURCES TO BE
      USED BY THE WHOLE COMMUNITY, while the lords treated
      these as ⭐⭐ THEIR PRIVATE PROPERTY."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is one of the most useful paragraphs in the entire NCERT History series, and it is worth learning as a concept rather than as a fact.**
> ⭐⭐ **"Passive resistance"** — working slowly, working your own strip first, quietly not doing the unpaid extras — **is what resistance looks like when open protest is impossible.** ⚠️ **It leaves almost no record**, which is why older histories concluded that peasants were passive. **They were not; they were being deniable.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **And the commons conflict is a template you will use repeatedly.** Two incompatible ideas of the same land: **community resource** versus **private property.** ⭐ **You have already met it in Class 9 Ch.4 (forest laws), Class 10 Ch.2 (Gudem tribals and the right to enter forests), and the whole lineage running to the FRA 2006.** ⭐⭐ **Whenever a state or a landlord converts a commons into property, this exact conflict follows** — **medieval France, colonial India, and contemporary land-acquisition disputes are the same argument in three costumes.**

### ⚙️ 10.3 The new agricultural technology — the eleventh-century boom ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ FOUR CHANGES, "BY THE ELEVENTH CENTURY"

   1. THE PLOUGH
      FROM basic WOODEN ploughs
      TO   ⭐ HEAVY IRON-TIPPED PLOUGHS and MOULD-BOARDS
      "These ploughs could DIG MUCH DEEPER and the MOULD-BOARDS
       ⭐ TURNED THE TOPSOIL PROPERLY. With this the NUTRIENTS
       FROM THE SOIL WERE BETTER UTILISED."
                          |
   2. THE HARNESS -- a small change with big physics
      FROM the ⭐ NECK-HARNESS
      TO   the ⭐ SHOULDER-HARNESS
      "This ENABLED ANIMALS TO EXERT GREATER POWER."
      PLUS -- "HORSES WERE NOW BETTER SHOD, with ⭐ IRON
      HORSESHOES, WHICH PREVENTED FOOT DECAY."
                          |
   3. POWER
      "There was INCREASED USE OF ⭐ WIND AND WATER ENERGY for
       agriculture. More WATER-POWERED and WIND-POWERED MILLS
       were set up all over Europe for purposes like
       ⭐ MILLING CORN and PRESSING GRAPES."
                          |
   4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE MOST REVOLUTIONARY ONE -- LAND USE
      "the switch from a TWO-FIELD to a ⭐ THREE-FIELD SYSTEM."
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE THREE-FIELD SYSTEM — understand it, don't just memorise it:**

```
   ⭐ THE RULE
     "peasants could use a field TWO YEARS OUT OF THREE if they
      planted it with ONE CROP IN AUTUMN and A DIFFERENT CROP
      IN SPRING A YEAR AND A HALF LATER."

   THE THREE FIELDS
     FIELD 1 -- AUTUMN -- ⭐ WHEAT or RYE ....... for HUMANS
     FIELD 2 -- SPRING -- ⭐ PEAS, BEANS, LENTILS for HUMANS
                          ⭐ OATS and BARLEY .... for HORSES
     FIELD 3 -- ⭐ FALLOW
     "Each year they ROTATED THE USE among the three fields."

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE RESULTS -- four of them, all quotable
     1. "an ALMOST IMMEDIATE INCREASE in the amount of food
        produced from EACH UNIT OF LAND.
        ⭐⭐ FOOD AVAILABILITY DOUBLED."
     2. "The greater use of plants like PEAS AND BEANS meant
        ⭐ MORE VEGETABLE PROTEINS IN THE DIET of the average
        European and a ⭐ BETTER SOURCE OF FODDER for their
        animals."
     3. ⭐⭐ FARM SIZE SHRANK -- "The average size of a peasant's
        farm shrank from ⭐ ABOUT 100 ACRES TO 20 TO 30 ACRES BY
        THE THIRTEENTH CENTURY."
        "Holdings which were SMALLER could be MORE EFFICIENTLY
         CULTIVATED and REDUCED THE AMOUNT OF LABOUR NEEDED."
     4. ⭐⭐⭐ "This ⭐ GAVE THE PEASANTS TIME FOR OTHER
        ACTIVITIES."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why the two-field system was so wasteful, and the three-field so clever — in plain terms.** Under the old scheme **half the land rests every year**. Under the new one **only a third does** — so ⭐ **the cultivated area rises by half without clearing a single new acre.** ⭐⭐ **And the second field is the real invention:** it grows **peas, beans and lentils**, which **feed people protein** and **feed horses**, and *(a point NCERT leaves implicit)* **legumes restore the soil rather than exhausting it** — which is why the extra cropping does not immediately wreck the land. ⭐⭐⭐ **One change in the cropping calendar delivers more calories, more protein, better draught animals and better soil at once.** **That is why NCERT calls it "the most revolutionary."**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And result 4 is the one that changes history.** *"This gave the peasants time for other activities."* ⭐ **Spare time is what a subsistence society never has.** ⚠️ **Trace the chain and you have the second half of the chapter in a single line: warmer weather → better ploughs and rotation → more food from less land → SPARE TIME AND SURPLUS GRAIN → something to sell → a place to sell it → TOWNS → merchants, guilds, bankers → a FOURTH ORDER that the three-order theory has no room for.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Write that chain out in a Mains answer and you have answered "how did feudalism decline?" without needing a single date.**

**⭐⭐⭐ WHO PAID FOR THE NEW TECHNOLOGY — a small, precise, examinable point:**

| Improvement | Who took the initiative | NCERT's reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Watermills and windmills** | ⭐ **THE LORDS** | *"Some of these technological changes **cost a lot of money**. **Peasants did not have enough money** to set up watermills and windmills"* |
| **Extending arable land** | ⭐ **THE PEASANTS** | *"peasants were able to take the initiative in many things"* |
| **The three-field rotation** | ⭐ **THE PEASANTS** | *"They also **switched** to the three-field rotation of crops"* |
| **Forges and smithies** | ⭐ **THE PEASANTS** | *"set up **small forges and smithies in the villages**, where **iron-tipped ploughs and horseshoes were made and repaired CHEAPLY**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The pattern is worth stating out loud: the lords financed the CAPITAL-INTENSIVE improvements, the peasants drove the CHEAP and INCREMENTAL ones.** ⚠️ **And notice that the lords' investments — mill, oven, press — are exactly the monopolies of §8.** ⭐⭐ **The lord's "investment" and the lord's "extraction" are the same three machines.** *(That is the honest way to describe it: he did pay for the mill, and he did then compel everyone to use it.)*

### 💰 10.4 Money — the quiet solvent ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE SENTENCE THAT EXPLAINS THE END OF FEUDALISM

   "FROM THE ELEVENTH CENTURY, the ⭐ PERSONAL BONDS THAT HAD
    BEEN THE BASIS OF FEUDALISM WERE WEAKENING, because
    ⭐⭐ ECONOMIC TRANSACTIONS WERE BECOMING MORE AND MORE
    MONEY BASED."

   HOW IT WORKED
     "LORDS found it convenient to ask for ⭐ RENT IN CASH, NOT
      SERVICES"
                          |
     "and CULTIVATORS were ⭐ SELLING THEIR CROPS FOR MONEY
      (INSTEAD OF EXCHANGING THEM FOR OTHER GOODS) to TRADERS,
      who would then take such goods TO BE SOLD IN THE TOWNS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ THE PRICE EFFECT
     "The increasing use of money began to INFLUENCE PRICES,
      which became HIGHER IN TIMES OF POOR HARVESTS."
     ⭐ THE FIGURE TO REMEMBER -- "In ENGLAND, for instance,
       AGRICULTURAL PRICES DOUBLED BETWEEN THE 1270s AND THE
       1320s."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the deepest idea in the chapter, so slow down for it.** **Feudalism runs on PERSONAL relationships**: *this* peasant owes *this* lord three days a week; *this* knight owes *this* lord forty days' service; the vow was sworn face to face on the Bible. ⭐⭐ **Money dissolves the person out of the relationship.** Once the lord takes **cash** instead of **labour**, he no longer needs *that particular* peasant — **he needs the sum.** And the peasant no longer owes *service* — **he owes a number.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **The obligation becomes IMPERSONAL, TRANSFERABLE and MEASURABLE.** ⚠️ **And a relationship you can pay off is a relationship you can leave.**
> ⭐⭐ **Note that nobody intended this.** The lord asked for cash because it was *convenient*; the peasant sold grain because a trader was there. **No one set out to end feudalism. The convenience of coin did it** — which is exactly the "imperceptible transformation" NCERT flagged at the start of §10.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ A Fourth Order? Towns and townspeople ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's opening — three consequences of one cause:** *"**Expansion in agriculture was accompanied by growth in THREE related areas: POPULATION, TRADE and TOWNS.**"*

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE POPULATION FIGURES -- learn all three, they are
      the most likely numerical question in the chapter

     1000 AD .......... ⭐ 42 MILLION
     c.1200 ........... ⭐ 62 MILLION
     1300 ............. ⭐ 73 MILLION

   ⭐⭐ WHY -- "BETTER FOOD MEANT A LONGER LIFESPAN."
     "By the THIRTEENTH CENTURY, an average European could
      expect to live ⭐ 10 YEARS LONGER THAN IN THE EIGHTH
      CENTURY."

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ AND THE LINE NOT TO SKIP
     "⭐ WOMEN AND GIRLS HAD SHORTER LIFESPANS COMPARED TO MEN
      ⭐⭐ BECAUSE THE LATTER ATE BETTER FOOD."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Stop on that last sentence.** ⭐ **It is a demographic fact with a household cause: unequal food inside the family, showing up as unequal years of life.** ⚠️ **NCERT states it flatly, in one line, and moves on — which is exactly why it is worth marking.** ⭐⭐ **This is directly usable in GS1 Society and in any answer on gender and nutrition**, and it pairs with the Indian data you will meet in Economy and in the Social Justice papers, where **intra-household food allocation** is a standing theme. ⭐ **The general point: a life-expectancy gap can be produced not by biology but by who is served first at a meal.**

**⭐⭐⭐ How towns came back — two routes:**

```
   ⭐ THE STARTING POINT
     "The towns of the ROMAN EMPIRE had become ⭐ DESERTED AND
      RUINED after its fall."
     "But FROM THE ELEVENTH CENTURY, as agriculture increased
      and became able to SUSTAIN HIGHER LEVELS OF POPULATION,
      TOWNS BEGAN TO GROW AGAIN."

   ⭐⭐⭐ ROUTE 1 -- THE MARKET GROWS INTO A TOWN
     "Peasants who had ⭐ SURPLUS GRAIN TO SELL needed a place
      where they could SET UP A SELLING CENTRE and where they
      could BUY TOOLS AND CLOTH."
                          |
     "This led to the growth of ⭐ PERIODIC FAIRS and SMALL
      MARKETING CENTRES"
                          |
     "which GRADUALLY DEVELOPED TOWN-LIKE FEATURES":
        - a ⭐ TOWN SQUARE
        - a ⭐ CHURCH
        - ⭐ ROADS where merchants BUILT SHOPS AND HOMES
        - ⭐ AN OFFICE where those who governed the town
          could meet

   ⭐⭐ ROUTE 2 -- THE TOWN GROWS AROUND SOMETHING ALREADY THERE
     "In other places, towns grew around
        ⭐ LARGE CASTLES,
        ⭐ BISHOPS' ESTATES,
        or ⭐ LARGE CHURCHES."

   ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE CHANGE IN WHAT IS OWED
     "In towns, ⭐ INSTEAD OF SERVICES, PEOPLE PAID A TAX to the
      lords who owned the land on which the town stood."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read the last box again: "instead of services, people paid a TAX."** ⭐ **The town is where the money economy of §10.4 becomes a way of life rather than a convenience.** ⭐⭐ **In the countryside the lord takes your LABOUR; in the town he takes a PAYMENT.** ⚠️ **And a payment leaves you free the rest of the time** — which is precisely what the next passage says.

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ "TOWN AIR MAKES FREE" — the chapter's most famous line, and it is meant literally:**

```
   ⭐⭐ WHAT TOWNS OFFERED
     "the prospect of ⭐ PAID WORK and ⭐ FREEDOM FROM THE
      LORD'S CONTROL, for YOUNG PEOPLE FROM PEASANT FAMILIES."

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ "'TOWN AIR MAKES FREE' WAS A POPULAR SAYING."

   ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE ACTUAL RULE BEHIND THE SAYING
     "Many SERFS CRAVING TO BE FREE RAN AWAY AND HID IN TOWNS.
      ⭐⭐ IF A SERF COULD STAY FOR ONE YEAR AND ONE DAY WITHOUT
      HIS LORD DISCOVERING HIM, HE WOULD BECOME A FREE MAN."

   ⭐⭐ WHO LIVED IN TOWNS
     "Many people in towns were ⭐ FREE PEASANTS OR ESCAPED
      SERFS who provided ⭐ UNSKILLED LABOUR."
     "SHOPKEEPERS AND MERCHANTS were numerous."
     "LATER there was need for individuals with ⭐ SPECIALISED
      SKILLS, LIKE BANKERS AND LAWYERS."
     ⭐ "The BIGGER TOWNS had populations of ⭐ ABOUT 30,000."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ "THEY COULD BE SAID TO HAVE FORMED A ⭐ 'FOURTH'
      ORDER."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the passage that breaks the chapter's title, and NCERT knows it — hence the question mark in its own heading, *"A Fourth Order?"***
> ⭐ **"One year and one day" is a real legal rule, not a proverb.** It means **serfdom had a statute of limitations** — and therefore that **the town was a hole in the feudal system through which people could climb out.**
> ⭐⭐ **Now count what the three-order theory cannot classify:** a **banker** does not pray, fight or work the land. Neither does a **lawyer**, a **shopkeeper**, an **innkeeper** or a **guild master**. ⚠️ **A society explains itself with three categories while producing a fourth — and the theory never catches up.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **That is the sharpest possible answer to "why did feudalism decline?": not because it was defeated, but because it kept generating people it had no place for.** *(And when those people demand a place, in 1789, they call themselves — precisely — **the Third Estate**, and claim to be **the nation**. Class 9 Ch.1.)*

**⭐⭐⭐ THE GUILD — the basis of urban economic organisation:**

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ "THE BASIS OF ECONOMIC ORGANISATION WAS THE ⭐ GUILD."

     "EACH CRAFT OR INDUSTRY was organised into a guild, an
      ASSOCIATION WHICH CONTROLLED
         ⭐ THE QUALITY OF THE PRODUCT,
         ⭐ ITS PRICE, and
         ⭐ ITS SALE."

   ⭐⭐ THE GUILD-HALL -- "a feature of EVERY town"
     "a building for ⭐ CEREMONIAL FUNCTIONS, and where the
      ⭐ HEADS OF ALL THE GUILDS MET FORMALLY."

   ⭐ OTHER TOWN OCCUPATIONS NCERT NAMES
     GUARDS ....... patrolled the town walls
     MUSICIANS .... played at FEASTS and in CIVIC PROCESSIONS
     INNKEEPERS ... looked after TRAVELLERS
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three points about guilds that turn a definition into an answer.** ⭐ **(1) A guild is not a trade union — it is a CARTEL with a public face**: it fixes **quality, price and sale**, i.e. it **restricts competition on purpose**, on the argument that stable quality and stable prices serve the town. ⭐ **(2) The guild-hall being the place where "the heads of all the guilds met formally" means the guilds were the town's POLITICAL class** — economic organisation and civic government are the same body of men. ⭐⭐ **(3) Compare the Indian *SHRENI*** (Class 6 Ch.8, and the *nagaram* merchant bodies of Class 7 Ch.2): **craft and trade associations that likewise regulated their members, held funds, and dealt with rulers as corporate bodies.** ⚠️ **A genuine and safe comparison** — but note the difference: **the shreni operated within a caste-ordered society**, so membership and craft were bound up with birth in a way the European guild's apprenticeship system was not.

**⭐⭐⭐ TRADE REVIVES — the specifics NCERT gives:**

| Route / place | Detail |
|---|---|
| **West Asia** | *"By the **ELEVENTH CENTURY**, **new trade routes with WEST ASIA** were developing"* ⭐ *(NCERT cross-refers **Theme 5** here — and that is a correct reference)* |
| **Scandinavia** | ⭐ *"**Scandinavian merchants** were sailing south from the **NORTH SEA** to exchange **FURS and HUNTING-HAWKS for CLOTH**"* |
| **England** | *"**English traders came to sell TIN**"* |
| **France** | *"by the **TWELFTH CENTURY**, **commerce and crafts began to grow**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **And the change in the craftsman's life is a lovely small detail with a large meaning:** *"**Earlier, craftsmen used to travel from manor to manor; now they found it EASIER TO SETTLE IN ONE PLACE where goods could be PRODUCED AND TRADED FOR FOOD.**"* ⭐⭐ **The artisan stops going to the customer, because enough customers now come to him.** ⚠️ **That is the definition of a market town, expressed as a change in one man's routine.**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The political payoff, in NCERT's own words:** *"As the number of towns grew and trade continued to expand, ⭐ **TOWN MERCHANTS BECAME RICH AND POWERFUL, AND RIVALLED THE POWER OF THE NOBILITY.**"* ⭐⭐ **Hold this sentence for §14** — when the "new monarchs" need money, **these are the men who lend it**, and that loan is how **non-aristocratic elements gain access to the court.**

The three together make a clean three-stage answer on how ideas reached ordinary people before mass literacy.)*

> *"**Because of the inadequacy which we often felt on feast days, for the narrowness of the place forced the WOMEN TO RUN TOWARDS THE ALTAR UPON THE HEADS OF THE MEN with much anguish and noisy confusion**, [we decided] **to enlarge and amplify the noble church**...*
> *We also caused to be painted, by **the exquisite hands of many masters FROM DIFFERENT REGIONS**, a **splendid variety of new windows**... > ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Mine this quotation for two separate answers.**
> ⭐ **(1) As evidence of CROWDS and popular devotion** — the church had to be enlarged because it was physically overwhelmed on feast days. **The vivid, undignified image of women scrambling over men's heads is worth quoting**; it tells you these were mass events, not solemn elite rituals.
> ⭐⭐ **(2) As evidence of ART PATRONAGE AND CONSERVATION** — masters summoned **from different regions**, materials named and valued, and ⭐ **a salaried master craftsman and goldsmith appointed specifically to PROTECT the windows, paid in coins from the altar and flour from the storehouse.** ⚠️ **That is a twelfth-century conservation post with a defined budget** — a genuinely striking fact, and a good one for any answer on heritage conservation having a long institutional history.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ The Crisis of the Fourteenth Century ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's framing:** *"By the **early fourteenth century**, Europe's **economic expansion SLOWED DOWN**. This was due to **THREE FACTORS**."* ⭐ **Learn them as a numbered set — this is the most answerable structured question in the chapter.**

### ❄️ Factor 1 — the climate turns again

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE REVERSAL
     "In NORTHERN EUROPE, by the end of the THIRTEENTH CENTURY
      ⭐ THE WARM SUMMERS OF THE PREVIOUS 300 YEARS HAD GIVEN
      WAY TO BITTERLY COLD SUMMERS."
        -> "SEASONS FOR GROWING CROPS WERE ⭐ REDUCED BY A
           MONTH"
        -> "it became DIFFICULT TO GROW CROPS ON HIGHER GROUND"
        -> ⭐ "STORMS AND OCEANIC FLOODING DESTROYED MANY
           FARMSTEADS, which resulted in ⭐ LESS INCOME IN TAXES
           FOR GOVERNMENTS"

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND THE TRAP THE BOOM HAD SET -- read this twice
     "The opportunities offered by favourable climatic
      conditions before the thirteenth century had led to
      ⭐ LARGE-SCALE RECLAMATION OF THE LAND OF FORESTS AND
      PASTURES for agriculture.
      ⭐⭐ BUT INTENSIVE PLOUGHING HAD EXHAUSTED THE SOIL
      DESPITE THE PRACTICE OF THE THREE-FIELD ROTATION OF CROPS,
      ⭐ BECAUSE CLEARANCE WAS NOT ACCOMPANIED BY PROPER SOIL
      CONSERVATION."
        -> "The ⭐ SHORTAGE OF PASTURAGE REDUCED THE NUMBER OF
           CATTLE."
        -> ⭐⭐ "POPULATION GROWTH WAS OUTSTRIPPING RESOURCES,
           and the IMMEDIATE RESULT WAS FAMINE."

   ⭐⭐ THE DATES
     ⭐ 1315-17 -- "SEVERE FAMINES hit Europe"
     ⭐ THE 1320s -- "followed by ⭐ MASSIVE CATTLE DEATHS"
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the most sophisticated causal argument in the chapter and it is worth real attention, because it is a GS3 argument in medieval dress.** ⭐ **The boom contained the bust.** Warm weather made it possible to clear forest and pasture; **clearing without soil conservation exhausted the land**; **losing the pasture reduced the cattle**; **fewer cattle meant less manure and less draught power**, which further weakened yields — and **the population that the good years had grown was still there to be fed.** ⭐⭐ **When the weather turned, there was no slack left in the system.**
> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **State the principle plainly, because it transfers directly to modern answers: prosperity built on drawing down a natural resource is prosperity with a deadline.** **Land expansion without soil conservation, in the fourteenth century, is the same argument as groundwater depletion or soil-organic-carbon loss in a contemporary GS3 answer.** ⭐ **NCERT hands you a historical case study for sustainability — use it in an essay and it will not read as a cliché, which is precisely what makes it valuable.**

### 🪙 Factor 2 — the silver runs out

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ "In addition, TRADE WAS HIT BY A ⭐ SEVERE SHORTAGE OF
     METAL MONEY because of a ⭐ SHORTFALL IN THE OUTPUT OF
     SILVER MINES IN ⭐ AUSTRIA AND SERBIA."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ THE GOVERNMENTS' RESPONSE
     "This FORCED GOVERNMENTS TO ⭐ REDUCE THE SILVER CONTENT OF
      THE CURRENCY, AND TO MIX IT WITH CHEAPER METALS."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is debasement, and it is a Prelims-friendly economic idea.** ⭐ **When the coin runs on actual silver, the money supply is limited by MINING** — if the mines fail, the economy seizes up regardless of how much people want to trade. ⚠️ **Governments respond by putting less silver in each coin**, which is a hidden way of creating more coins from the same metal — ⭐⭐ **and the classic consequence is that prices rise, because the coin is worth less than it claims.** *(NCERT does not spell out the inflation; it gives you the mechanism and the earlier fact that English agricultural prices doubled between the 1270s and the 1320s.)* ⭐ **Name the two countries — AUSTRIA and SERBIA — they are the kind of specific that a Prelims statement-question likes.**

### ☠️ Factor 3 — the Black Death ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ HOW IT ARRIVED -- and note the irony
     "As TRADE EXPANDED in the thirteenth and fourteenth
      centuries, ⭐ SHIPS CARRYING GOODS FROM DISTANT COUNTRIES
      HAD STARTED ARRIVING IN EUROPEAN PORTS.
      ⭐⭐ ALONG WITH THE SHIPS CAME RATS -- carrying the deadly
      ⭐ BUBONIC PLAGUE INFECTION (THE 'BLACK DEATH')."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ "WESTERN EUROPE, ⭐ RELATIVELY ISOLATED IN EARLIER
     CENTURIES, was hit by the epidemic between
     ⭐⭐ 1347 AND 1350."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE MORTALITY -- both figures are examinable
     "The MODERN ESTIMATE of mortality in that epidemic is that
      ⭐⭐ 20 PER CENT OF THE PEOPLE OF THE WHOLE OF EUROPE
      DIED,
      ⭐ WITH SOME PLACES LOSING AS MUCH AS 40 PER CENT of the
      population."

   ⭐⭐⭐ WHO AND WHERE IT HIT HARDEST
     "⭐ AS TRADE CENTRES, CITIES WERE THE HARDEST HIT."
     "In ENCLOSED COMMUNITIES like ⭐ MONASTERIES AND CONVENTS,
      when ONE individual contracted the plague, it was not long
      before EVERYONE did.
      ⚠️ AND IN ALMOST EVERY CASE, ⭐ NONE SURVIVED."
     "The plague took its ⭐ WORST TOLL AMONG INFANTS, THE YOUNG
      AND THE ELDERLY."
     "There were other RELATIVELY MINOR EPISODES of plague in
      the ⭐ 1360s AND 1370s."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE DEMOGRAPHIC ARITHMETIC -- the single most
      important pair of numbers in the chapter
     ⭐ EUROPE'S POPULATION: 73 MILLION IN 1300
                          -> ⭐⭐ 45 MILLION IN 1400
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Notice the irony NCERT builds into the first line, because it is the whole point.** ⭐ **The plague arrives BY THE SAME ROUTE AS THE PROSPERITY.** The ships that carried the trade of §11 carried the rats. ⭐⭐ **Western Europe was hit so hard partly because it had been *"relatively isolated in earlier centuries"* — a population with no prior exposure meets a pathogen carried by its own new commerce.** ⚠️ **That is exactly the mechanism you have already met in Class 10 Ch.3 (*The Making of a Global World*), where SMALLPOX carried by the Spanish devastates the Americas.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Connection increases wealth and increases epidemic risk by the same act. Say that once, cleanly, and you have a line that works in a history answer, a GS2 global-governance answer, and an essay.**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **And the monastery detail deserves its own sentence:** an **enclosed community with shared meals, shared dormitories and a duty to nurse the sick** is the ideal environment for transmission — *"in almost every case, none survived."* ⚠️ **The institutions that had schools, hospitals and libraries (§7.1) were the ones wiped out most completely.** ⭐ **A very large part of the fourteenth century's cultural loss is contained in that one line.**

**⭐⭐⭐ GIOVANNI BOCCACCIO (1313–75), Italian author — the eyewitness voice:**

> *"**How many valiant men, how many fair ladies, (had) BREAKFAST WITH THEIR KINFOLK AND THE SAME NIGHT SUPPED WITH THEIR ANCESTORS IN THE NEXT WORLD!** The condition of the people was pitiable to behold. **They sickened by the thousands daily, and died UNATTENDED AND WITHOUT HELP.** Many died in the open street, others dying in their houses, **made it known by the stench of their rotting bodies. CONSECRATED CHURCHYARDS DID NOT SUFFICE for the burial of the vast multitude of bodies, which were HEAPED BY THE HUNDREDS IN VAST TRENCHES, LIKE GOODS IN A SHIP'S HOLD, and covered with a little earth.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two things to take from Boccaccio.** ⭐ **(1) The SPEED** — *"breakfast with their kinfolk and the same night supped with their ancestors"* is a single day from health to death, and it explains the panic better than any statistic. ⭐⭐ **(2) The COLLAPSE OF RITUAL** — *"died unattended"*, *"consecrated churchyards did not suffice"*, bodies *"heaped... like goods in a ship's hold."* ⚠️ **A society is defined partly by how it buries its dead; when that breaks down, the social order itself is visibly failing.** ⭐ **The ship's-hold simile is bleakly apt, given how the plague arrived.** *(For Prelims: **Boccaccio, 1313–75, Italian** — the same generation as **Chaucer (c.1340–1400)** and **Langland (c.1360–70)**. ⭐ **All three are witnesses to the same shaken century**, which is a neat way to hold the literary names together.)*

### 📉 12.1 The economic consequences — a labour shortage changes everything ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE CHAIN -- follow it link by link, it is the
      chapter's climax

   "This CATASTROPHE, COMBINED WITH THE ECONOMIC CRISIS, caused
    ⭐ IMMENSE SOCIAL DISLOCATION."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ "DEPOPULATION resulted in a ⭐ MAJOR SHORTAGE OF LABOUR."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ "SERIOUS IMBALANCES were created between ⭐ AGRICULTURE
     AND MANUFACTURE, because ⭐ THERE WERE NOT ENOUGH PEOPLE TO
     ENGAGE IN BOTH EQUALLY."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ PRICES FALL -- "Prices of agricultural goods ⭐ DROPPED
     AS THERE WERE FEWER PEOPLE TO BUY."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ WAGES RISE -- the headline figure
     "WAGE RATES INCREASED because the demand for labour,
      particularly AGRICULTURAL LABOUR, ⭐⭐ ROSE IN ENGLAND BY
      AS MUCH AS 250 PER CENT in the aftermath of the Black
      Death."
     ⭐ "The SURVIVING LABOUR FORCE COULD NOW DEMAND
       ⭐⭐ TWICE THEIR EARLIER WAGES."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Here is the pivot of the whole chapter, and it is genuinely counter-intuitive: the catastrophe STRENGTHENED the bargaining position of the survivors.**
> ⭐ **Feudalism rested on an assumption that never had to be stated — that there are always more peasants than the land needs.** **A serf who could be replaced had nothing to bargain with.**
> ⭐⭐ **The plague destroyed that assumption in three years.** With a fifth of Europe dead, **labour became the scarce factor and land the abundant one** — and **the price of scarce things rises.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Note the scissors: grain prices FALL (fewer mouths) while wages RISE (fewer hands).** ⚠️ **Both blades cut the lord**: his income falls and his costs rise at the same moment. **That is exactly why §13 happens.**
> ⭐⭐ **State the general law once and reuse it: whichever factor of production is scarce captures the gains.** *(It is the same logic behind agricultural wage movements after large out-migration, and it is a clean, defensible economic point in a history answer.)*

## 1️⃣3️⃣ Social Unrest — the revolts that lost and won ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE LORDS' PREDICAMENT
     "The INCOME OF LORDS WAS THUS BADLY HIT. It DECLINED as
      ⭐ AGRICULTURAL PRICES CAME DOWN and ⭐ WAGES OF LABOURERS
      INCREASED."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THEIR ATTEMPTED SOLUTION -- turn the clock back
     "⭐ IN DESPERATION, they tried to
        ⭐⭐ GIVE UP THE MONEY-CONTRACTS THEY HAD ENTERED INTO
        ⭐⭐ AND REVIVE LABOUR-SERVICES."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ WHO RESISTED -- and note WHICH peasants
     "This was ⭐ VIOLENTLY OPPOSED BY PEASANTS, particularly the
      ⭐⭐ BETTER-EDUCATED AND MORE PROSPEROUS ONES."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE THREE REVOLTS -- learn all three, they are a
      classic three-statement Prelims set
     ⭐ 1323 -- FLANDERS
     ⭐ 1358 -- FRANCE
     ⭐ 1381 -- ENGLAND
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two observations that turn this into a strong answer rather than a list of dates.**
> ⭐ **(1) It is the BETTER-OFF peasants who revolt, not the poorest.** NCERT says so directly — *"particularly the better-educated and more prosperous ones."* ⚠️ **This contradicts the intuitive picture of revolt as an explosion of the most desperate.** ⭐⭐ **People rebel when something they have GAINED is about to be taken away**, and they rebel most effectively when they have the literacy and resources to organise. **Rising expectations, then a threatened reversal — that is the classic trigger.**
> ⭐ **(2) And NCERT confirms the pattern geographically:** *"it is significant that they occurred with **the most violent intensity in THOSE AREAS WHICH HAD EXPERIENCED THE PROSPERITY OF THE ECONOMIC EXPANSION** — ⭐⭐ **a sign that peasants were attempting to PROTECT THE GAINS THEY HAD MADE in previous centuries.**"* **The revolt map matches the prosperity map.** ⭐⭐⭐ **This is directly transferable to Indian history — the Awadh peasants, the Kheda patidars, and the general observation that the most politically active peasantry is rarely the poorest.**

**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE PARADOX — the most important sentence in the chapter. Learn it verbatim:**

> *"Though these rebellions were **RUTHLESSLY CRUSHED**... **Despite the severe repression, ⭐ THE SHEER INTENSITY OF PEASANT OPPOSITION ENSURED THAT THE OLD FEUDAL RELATIONS COULD NOT BE REIMPOSED. ⭐⭐ THE MONEY ECONOMY WAS TOO FAR ADVANCED TO BE REVERSED.** Therefore, though **the lords SUCCEEDED IN CRUSHING THE REVOLTS**, the peasants ensured that **the feudal privileges of earlier days COULD NOT BE REINVENTED.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Take this apart, because it is the chapter's whole philosophy of change.**
> ⭐ **Militarily: the lords won every engagement.** The revolts of 1323, 1358 and 1381 were all put down, and put down brutally.
> ⭐⭐ **Structurally: the lords lost anyway** — for **two** reasons, and a good answer gives both. **(a) The economic reason:** *"the money economy was too far advanced to be reversed"* — **cash rents, markets and wage labour had become the medium of rural life, and you cannot un-invent them by decree.** **(b) The political reason:** *"the sheer intensity of peasant opposition"* — ⭐⭐ **the lords learned what re-imposing serfdom would COST, and the price was permanently too high.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **The general principle, and it is one of the most useful things in this entire book: a movement can be defeated and still succeed, by making the old arrangement too expensive to restore.** ⚠️ **Use it on the 1857 revolt, on peasant and tribal movements throughout the freedom struggle, and in any essay on the efficacy of protest.** **Repression that "works" can still lose the argument.**

**⭐⭐⭐ NCERT's timeline — Eleventh to Fourteenth Centuries:**

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **1066** | **Normans defeat Anglo-Saxons and conquer England** |
| **1100 onwards** | **Cathedrals being built in France** |
| ⭐ **1315–17** | **Great famine in Europe** |
| ⭐⭐ **1347–50** | **BLACK DEATH** |
| **1338–1461** | **Hundred Years War between England and France** |

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐ **A discrepancy worth knowing about, and knowing how to handle.** **NCERT prints the Hundred Years War as 1338–1461.** ⭐ **The conventional dating in almost all other sources is 1337–1453.** *(The 1461 date reflects a longer view that folds in the last Anglo-French fighting; 1453, the fall of Bordeaux, is the standard end point.)* ⚠️ **For a Prelims statement matched against NCERT, go with NCERT. If you are writing the date yourself in Mains, write "the Hundred Years War (fourteenth to fifteenth centuries)" and avoid the trap entirely.** ⭐ **Also note the war is listed but never discussed** — it is background, not content, so do not invest time in it here.

> ⭐⭐ **NCERT's Activity 4 is genuinely worth doing:** *"Read through the events and processes listed with dates, and **connect them into a narrative account**."* ⭐ **That is Mains answer-writing practice disguised as a school exercise** — and the narrative it wants is exactly the five-stage chain in the 🧭 box at the top of these notes.

## 1️⃣4️⃣ Political Change — the "new monarchs" ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE OPENING CLAIM
     "Developments in the POLITICAL sphere ⭐ PARALLELED SOCIAL
      PROCESSES."

> ⭐⭐ **In the COURT, everyone faces the king directly, and what they compete for is FAVOUR.** **The nobles are still there — still dominant, now with "permanent positions in the administrative service" — but they hold their standing at the king's pleasure rather than by inherited right.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Hence the phrase to memorise: "royal absolutism has been called a MODIFIED FORM OF FEUDALISM."** **Same class of people, different basis of power.** ⚠️ **A revolution in the STRUCTURE with barely any change in the PERSONNEL — which is exactly why 1789 was still necessary four centuries later.**
> ⭐⭐ **And note the door that patronage opens.** **Because favour can be bought, and kings need cash for soldiers, MERCHANTS AND BANKERS get into the court** — the fourth order of §11 arriving at the centre of the state with a loan in hand. ⭐ **The financiers who lend to the crown become permanent participants in government.** *(That relationship — sovereign debt as a route to political influence — is one of the most consequential in modern history, and this paragraph is where the NCERT series first shows it to you.)*

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ENGLAND -- "WHAT HAPPENED IN ENGLAND WAS VERY
      DIFFERENT."
     "⭐ EVEN BEFORE THE NORMAN CONQUEST, the ANGLO-SAXONS had a
      ⭐⭐ GREAT COUNCIL, ⭐ WHICH THE KING HAD TO CONSULT BEFORE

                          |
     "This developed into what was called ⭐ THE PARLIAMENT,
      which consisted of
        ⭐ THE HOUSE OF LORDS -- "the members of which were THE
          LORDS AND THE CLERGY"
        ⭐ THE HOUSE OF COMMONS -- "representing TOWNS AND RURAL
          AREAS"
                          |
     ⭐⭐⭐ "KING CHARLES I RULED FOR ⭐ 11 YEARS (1629-40)
       WITHOUT CALLING PARLIAMENT.
       ⭐ WHEN HE WAS FORCED TO CALL IT, BECAUSE HE NEEDED MONEY,
       a section of Parliament decided to ⭐ GO TO WAR AGAINST
       HIM, and later ⭐⭐ EXECUTED HIM AND ESTABLISHED A
       REPUBLIC."
                          |
     "⭐ THIS DID NOT LAST LONG, AND MONARCHY WAS RESTORED,
      ⭐⭐ BUT ON THE CONDITION THAT PARLIAMENT WOULD BE CALLED
      REGULARLY."

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE CHAPTER'S LAST LINE
     "TODAY, ⭐ FRANCE HAS A REPUBLICAN FORM OF GOVERNMENT and
      ⭐ ENGLAND HAS A MONARCHY. This is because of
      ⭐⭐ THE DIFFERENT DIRECTIONS THAT THE HISTORIES OF THE TWO
      COUNTRIES TOOK AFTER THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This ending is superb and is the single best thing to take from the chapter into Class 9 Ch.1 and into GS2. Read the irony slowly.**
> ⭐ **ENGLAND kept a body the king had to consult before taxing — and eventually FOUGHT AND EXECUTED a king who tried to rule without it. Result: the monarchy SURVIVED, permanently constrained.**
> ⭐ **FRANCE let its consultative assembly lapse for 175 years rather than share power. Result: when the crown finally had to summon the Estates-General again in 1789, it had no practice of compromise to fall back on — and the monarchy was DESTROYED.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **The paradox in one line: the crown that CONCEDED regularly survived; the crown that conceded NOTHING was abolished.** ⚠️ **Or, put as a principle for essays and GS2: institutions that absorb conflict continuously prevent it from accumulating. Pressure that has no outlet does not disappear — it stores.**
> ⭐⭐ **And note what 1614→1789 means for your Class 9 notes.** **When Louis XVI summons the Estates-General in May 1789, nobody alive has ever seen one meet.** ⭐ **The procedural quarrel that follows — voting by order or by head — happens precisely because the institution had been dead for six generations and its rules were an antique.** ⭐⭐ **The Third Estate's decision to declare itself the National Assembly is the fourth order of §11 finally refusing the three-order scheme altogether.** **That is the thread that ties this chapter to the French Revolution, and it is worth writing exactly that way.**

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **One factual caution about the last line.** NCERT's *"today"* is accurate as far as it goes, but write it carefully in an answer: ⭐ **the correct formulation is that the UNITED KINGDOM is a constitutional monarchy** — the monarch reigns, Parliament governs — **and France is a republic (currently the Fifth Republic).** ⚠️ **France did not go straight from 1789 to today's republic**; it passed through empires, restorations and five republics. **Do not let the neat closing sentence flatten that.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **After Rome fell, Germanic peoples occupied Italy, Spain and France.** With **no unifying political force**, military conflict was frequent and *"the need to gather resources to protect one's land became very important"* — so ⭐ **"social organisation was centred on the CONTROL OF LAND"**, drawing on **Roman traditions and German customs**. **Christianity, official since the fourth century, survived** — and ⭐ **the Church became a major landholder and political power.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MARC BLOCH (1886–1944)**, French, argued history is **more than political history, international relations and great lives**, stressing **geography** and **collective attitudes**. ***Feudal Society*** covers **French society 900–1300**. ⚠️ **He was SHOT BY THE NAZIS.** Evidence came from **parish registers of births, marriages and deaths, church inscriptions, songs and stories**.
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **'FEUDALISM' is from the German 'FEUD' = a piece of land.** Economically it is *"agricultural production based on the relationship between lords and peasants"* — peasants work **their own land and the lord's**, give **labour services**, receive **military protection**, and are under the lord's **judicial control**. ⭐ **Roots in Rome and Charlemagne (742–814), but established only in the ELEVENTH CENTURY.**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **The FRANKS gave Gaul its name.** In ⭐ **800 the Pope gave Charlemagne the title HOLY ROMAN EMPEROR "to ensure his support."** Timeline: **481 Clovis · 496 conversion · 714 Charles Martel (mayor of the palace) · 751 Pepin takes the crown · 768 Charlemagne · 800 crowned by Leo III · 840 onwards Viking raids.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE THREE ORDERS**, as a bishop put it: ⭐ *"Here below, some **pray**, others **fight**, still others **work**."* — **clergy, nobility, peasantry.** ⚠️ **Priests wrote the scheme and placed themselves first.** **Hildegard of Bingen**: difference is needed *"so that they do not destroy each other"*; ⭐ *"all are loved by him, **yet there is no equality among them**."*
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **VASSALAGE.** Nobles were vassals of the king, peasants of the landowners. **Seigneur = senior**; **'lord' = one who provided bread.** Vows on the **Bible in a church**; the symbol of land was **a charter, a staff, or a clod of earth.** **The noble had four powers: perpetual property, feudal levies, his own courts, and his own coinage** — ⭐ **a sovereign in miniature.**
7. ⭐⭐⭐ **THE MANOR.** Some lords held **hundreds of villages**; estates ran from **a dozen to fifty or sixty families**; grain, blacksmiths, carpenters, stonemasons, women weaving, **children at the wine-presses**, forests for hunting, a church and a castle. ⚠️⭐ **NOT self-sufficient — salt, millstones and metalware came from outside.** **In England castles were unknown before 1066.**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **KNIGHTS** arose from the **ninth century** because **peasant-soldiers were insufficient and cavalry was needed**. The **FIEF** — **1,000–2,000+ acres, inheritable**, with house, church, watermill and wine-press, worked by peasants — bought **a regular fee and war service**. Knights practised **daily**. ⚠️ **A knight might serve MORE THAN ONE LORD.** **Minstrels** carried songs *"partly historical, partly invented."*
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE CHURCH did not depend on the king: its own LAWS, its own LANDS, its own TAXES**, headed by the **Pope in Rome**. ⚠️ **Serfs, the physically challenged and women could not become priests; priests could not marry.** **Bishops were "the religious nobility."** ⭐ **TITHE = the Church's tenth of the produce.** Endowments were bought **for the afterlife** — the seed of **indulgences**.
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Feudal culture entered the Church**: ⭐ **kneeling in prayer was "an exact replica" of a knight's vow of loyalty**, and **God was called "lord."** *"The religious and the lay worlds of feudalism shared many customs and symbols."*
11. ⭐⭐⭐ **MONASTERIES: St Benedict, Italy, 529; Cluny, Burgundy, 910.** Vows of **lifelong residence, prayer, study and manual labour**; ⭐ **open to women as nuns, unlike priesthood**; they grew to hundreds, with **schools, colleges and hospitals**. **Benedictine rule: humility is obedience (7), no private property (33), ⭐ "idleness is the enemy of the soul" (47), all necessities within the walls (48).** **FRIARS (13th c.) left the monastery to preach and live on charity.** ⚠️ **By the 14th century Langland and Chaucer were satirising monks.**
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **Old beliefs survived.** ⭐ **Christmas is FIXED because it replaced a SOLAR festival; EASTER MOVES because it replaced a LUNAR spring festival.** The village became **the PARISH — "the area under the supervision of one priest."** **'Holy days' were welcomed because no work was expected.** **Pilgrimage** was central — **Chaucer's *Canterbury Tales*, in MIDDLE ENGLISH.**
13. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FREE PEASANTS vs SERFS.** Free: **tenants**, **40+ days' military service**, **usually three days a week** of labour-rent, plus ditches, firewood, fences and roads; **women and children spun, wove, made candles and pressed wine FOR THE LORD'S USE.** **Serfs: the land is the lord's, much of the produce goes to him, NO WAGES, cannot leave without permission**, must use **only his mill, oven and wine-presses**, and ⭐ **need his decision or a fee to marry.** ⭐⭐ **TAILLE = the king's direct tax — CLERGY AND NOBLES EXEMPT.**
14. ⭐⭐⭐ **ENGLAND.** Angles and Saxons settled in the **sixth century**; **England = "Angle-land."** ⭐ **1066: William, Duke of Normandy, defeated the Saxon king**, had **the land mapped** and distributed it to **180 Norman nobles** as **chief tenants owing knights** — ⚠️ **but PRIVATE WARFARE WAS FORBIDDEN.**
15. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ENVIRONMENT.** From the **fifth to tenth centuries** Europe was **forested and intensely cold** — limited farmland, **but forests were an escape route for oppressed peasants.** ⭐ **From the eleventh century a WARM PHASE** lengthened the growing season, reduced frost and **pushed back the forest line.**
16. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE OLD SYSTEM WAS POOR.** A **wooden plough** could only **scratch the surface**; fields were **dug by hand once in four years**; the **two-field rotation left half the land fallow**, the soil deteriorated and **famine alternated with chronic malnutrition.** Lords squeezed harder; ⭐⭐ **peasants replied with PASSIVE RESISTANCE** — working their own fields first and avoiding unpaid extras — and **fought the lords over pasture and forest, which they saw as COMMUNITY resources and the lords as PRIVATE PROPERTY.**
17. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ELEVENTH-CENTURY BREAKTHROUGH: heavy IRON-TIPPED PLOUGHS with MOULD-BOARDS · the SHOULDER-HARNESS · IRON HORSESHOES · water and wind MILLS · and the THREE-FIELD SYSTEM** (autumn wheat/rye; spring peas, beans, lentils, oats and barley; one fallow). ⭐⭐ **FOOD AVAILABILITY DOUBLED**; diets gained **vegetable protein**; farms **shrank from ~100 to 20–30 acres** and became more efficient; and ⭐ **peasants got TIME FOR OTHER ACTIVITIES.** **Lords funded the mills; peasants extended arable, adopted rotation and built village forges.**
18. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MONEY DISSOLVED THE PERSONAL BOND.** *"From the eleventh century, the personal bonds that had been the basis of feudalism were weakening, because economic transactions were becoming more and more money based."* **Lords took cash rent instead of services; peasants sold crops to traders.** ⭐ **English agricultural prices DOUBLED between the 1270s and the 1320s.**
19. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A FOURTH ORDER.** Population **42m → 62m → 73m (1000–1300)**; ⚠️ **women and girls lived shorter lives "because the latter ate better food."** Towns revived as **fairs and marketing centres** or around **castles, bishops' estates and churches**; townspeople paid **a tax, not services**. ⭐⭐ ***"TOWN AIR MAKES FREE"* — a serf undiscovered for ONE YEAR AND ONE DAY became free.** Big towns reached **30,000**. **GUILDS controlled quality, price and sale**; ⭐ **merchants "rivalled the power of the nobility."** **Cathedrals** from the twelfth century drew towns around them, and their **stained glass "narrated the stories in the Bible… which illiterate people could 'read'."**
20. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FOURTEENTH-CENTURY CRISIS — THREE CAUSES.** ⭐ **(1)** Bitterly cold summers, a growing season **shorter by a month**, and **soil exhausted by clearance without conservation** → **famines 1315–17** and **cattle deaths in the 1320s**. ⭐ **(2)** A **silver shortage** from mines in **Austria and Serbia** → **debased coinage**. ⭐⭐ **(3) The BLACK DEATH, 1347–50**, carried by **rats on trading ships** into a **relatively isolated** western Europe — **20% of Europe died, some places 40%**; ⚠️ **cities and enclosed monasteries were worst hit.** **Population 73m (1300) → 45m (1400).**
21. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE SURVIVORS GAINED.** Depopulation caused a **labour shortage**: **agricultural prices fell** while **demand for agricultural labour in England rose by up to 250%** and survivors **demanded twice their earlier wages.** Lords, squeezed from both sides, **tried to scrap money-contracts and revive labour-services.**
22. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE REVOLTS — 1323 FLANDERS · 1358 FRANCE · 1381 ENGLAND** — were led by ⭐ **the better-educated and more prosperous peasants**, and were fiercest **where the boom had brought prosperity**, because they were **protecting their gains**. ⚠️ **All were ruthlessly crushed.** ⭐⭐⭐ **And still: *"the money economy was too far advanced to be reversed… the peasants ensured that the feudal privileges of earlier days could not be reinvented."***
23. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE NEW MONARCHS — Louis XI (France), Maximilian (Austria), Henry VII (England), Isabelle and Ferdinand (Spain)** — built **standing armies, a permanent bureaucracy and national taxation**, and in **Spain and Portugal** began **overseas expansion**. They **dropped feudal levies for professionally trained infantry with GUNS AND SIEGE ARTILLERY**, and ⭐ **"the resistance of the aristocracies crumbled in the face of the firepower of the kings."** ⚠️ **Opposition everywhere turned on TAXATION** — English risings in **1497, 1536, 1547, 1549, 1553**; Louis XI's long struggle with dukes and princes; and ⭐ **France's "religious" wars as "in part a contest between royal privileges and regional liberties."**
24. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ABSOLUTISM AS "A MODIFIED FORM OF FEUDALISM."** The nobility switched **from opponents to loyalists** and took **permanent posts in the administrative service** — **the same class still dominant.** But the shape changed: ⭐ **from a PYRAMID of personal dependence to a COURT at whose centre the king sat**, running on **patronage** — ⭐⭐ **and because patronage could be obtained with money, MERCHANTS AND BANKERS lent to kings and gained access to the court**, so *"rulers made space for non-feudal elements in the state system."*
25. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ENDING.** ⭐ **France:** the **Estates-General** met in **1614** under the child-king **Louis XIII** and ⚠️ **was not summoned again for nearly two centuries — till 1789** — *"because the kings did not want to share power."* ⭐ **England:** the pre-Conquest **Great Council**, which **the king had to consult before imposing any tax**, became **Parliament** (Lords + Commons). **Charles I ruled 11 years (1629–40) without it; recalled it because he needed money; was fought, executed, and a republic declared** — and when monarchy returned it was ⭐ **"on the condition that Parliament would be called regularly."** ⭐⭐⭐ **Hence: "today France has a republican form of government and England has a monarchy."**

24. After 1066 William I distributed the land of England to — ***180 Norman nobles who had migrated with him***; *private warfare was **forbidden in England**.*

25. Under the two-field system the proportion of land left fallow each year was — ***one half***; *under the **three-field system, one third**.*

26. In the three-field system, the field sown in spring carried — ***peas, beans and lentils for human use, and oats and barley for the horses.***

27. Following the agricultural improvements, the average size of a peasant's farm — ***shrank from about 100 acres to 20 to 30 acres by the thirteenth century***, *because smaller holdings could be **more efficiently cultivated**.*

28. Watermills and windmills were set up on the initiative of — ***the lords***, *because peasants lacked the money; **peasants** took the initiative in **extending arable land, three-field rotation, and village forges and smithies**.*

29. In England, agricultural prices doubled between — ***the 1270s and the 1320s.***

30. Europe's population moved as follows — ***42 million in 1000, 62 million around 1200, 73 million in 1300, and 45 million in 1400.***

31. NCERT explains the shorter lifespans of women and girls by — ***the fact that men ate better food.***

32. A runaway serf became a free man if he remained undiscovered in a town for — ***one year and one day.***

33. A guild controlled — ***the quality of the product, its price and its sale.***

34. Stained glass windows in cathedrals served to — ***narrate the stories in the Bible through pictures, which illiterate people could "read"***, *appearing radiant to those **inside by day** and visible to those **outside by candlelight after sunset**.*

35. Abbot Suger enlarged the Abbey of St Denis because — ***the narrowness of the place forced women to run towards the altar upon the heads of the men***; *he appointed **a master craftsman and a goldsmith** to protect the windows.*

36. The three causes of the fourteenth-century crisis were — ***climatic reversal with soil exhaustion and famine; a shortage of silver from the mines of Austria and Serbia; and the Black Death.***

37. The Black Death struck western Europe between — ***1347 and 1350***, *killing an estimated **20 per cent of Europe**, with some places losing **40 per cent**.*

38. After the Black Death, the demand for agricultural labour in England rose by — ***as much as 250 per cent***, *while **agricultural prices fell**.*

39. The peasant revolts occurred in — ***Flanders in 1323, France in 1358 and England in 1381***, *led particularly by **the better-educated and more prosperous peasants**.*

40. NCERT's verdict on those revolts is that — ***though the lords crushed them, the peasants ensured that the feudal privileges of earlier days could not be reinvented***, *because **the money economy was too far advanced to be reversed**.*

41. Match the new monarchs: Louis XI, Maximilian, Henry VII, Isabelle and Ferdinand — ***France, Austria, England and Spain respectively.***

42. Royal absolutism has been called "a modified form of feudalism" because — ***the same class that had ruled under feudalism, the lords, continued to dominate, taking permanent positions in the administrative service.***

43. Under the new monarchies the king was — ***no longer at the apex of a pyramid of personal dependence, but at the centre of a courtier society and a network of patron–client relationships.***

44. The French Estates-General met in 1614 and then — ***not again for nearly two centuries, till 1789.***

45. The English Parliament developed from — ***the Anglo-Saxon Great Council, which the king had to consult before imposing any tax***, *and comprised the **House of Lords** (lords and clergy) and the **House of Commons** (towns and rural areas).*

46. Charles I ruled without summoning Parliament for — ***eleven years (1629–40)***, *and recalled it only **because he needed money**.*
