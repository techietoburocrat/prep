## 📖 Section IV Introduction — *Towards Modernisation* ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Read this before the theme itself.** The section introduction is only three pages, and it contains **two passages of genuinely high value** that most candidates never see because they skip section introductions.

### 🏭 A. What "modernisation" rests on

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ TWO DEVELOPMENTS "CREATED A CONTEXT FOR WHAT HAS BEEN
      CALLED 'MODERNISATION'":
     1. THE INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION
     2. ⭐ "A SERIES OF POLITICAL REVOLUTIONS THAT TRANSFORMED
        SUBJECTS INTO CITIZENS" --
          THE AMERICAN REVOLUTION (1776-81)
          THE FRENCH REVOLUTION (1789-94)
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ AND THE WARNING AGAINST A SINGLE MODEL
   "For long it was believed that BRITISH INDUSTRIALISATION
    PROVIDED THE MODEL for industrialisation in other countries.
    ⭐ HOWEVER, HISTORIANS HAVE BEGUN TO QUESTION some of the
    earlier ideas... EACH COUNTRY DREW UPON THE EXPERIENCES OF
    OTHER NATIONS, WITHOUT NECESSARILY REPRODUCING ANY MODEL."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE WORKED EXAMPLE -- learn this contrast
     BRITAIN -- "COAL AND COTTON TEXTILE industries were developed
        in THE FIRST PHASE"; "the invention of RAILWAYS initiated
        THE SECOND STAGE"
     RUSSIA (industrialising from the late nineteenth century) --
        ⭐ "THE RAILWAY AND OTHER HEAVY INDUSTRY EMERGED IN THE
        INITIAL PHASE OF INDUSTRIALISATION ITSELF"
   ⭐ "Likewise, THE ROLE OF THE STATE, AND OF BANKS, in
     industrialisation HAS DIFFERED FROM COUNTRY TO COUNTRY."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ AND THE COSTS, NAMED
   Britain's case "displayed THE HUMAN AND MATERIAL COSTS incurred
   on its industrialisation --
        THE PLIGHT OF THE LABOURING POOR, ESPECIALLY OF CHILDREN
        ENVIRONMENTAL DEGRADATION
        and THE CONSEQUENT EPIDEMICS OF CHOLERA AND TUBERCULOSIS"
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Subjects into citizens" appears here AND in Theme 7's account of Meiji Japan — NCERT is using it as the definition of political modernity, so adopt it.** ⭐ **A modern polity is one in which the people are the source of authority rather than the objects of it.** That is why the Industrial Revolution alone is not enough: **industry without the political revolution gives you a rich autocracy**, which is precisely what Theme 7's Japan and Korea had to work through.
> ⭐⭐ **And the "no single model" point is worth an Essay paragraph on its own**, because the late industrialiser does not repeat the pioneer's sequence — **Russia began with railways and heavy industry, exactly where Britain ended.** 🔗 *(Korea's ladder in Theme 7 — light industry first, then heavy and chemical — is a third sequence again. Three countries, three orders of operations, and none of them wrong.)*

### 🌍 B. THREE VARIANTS OF IMPERIALISM ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ NCERT distinguishes THREE. Learn all three -- the
      distinction is the point of the whole section.

   1. SETTLER COLONIES
      "Western capitalisms -- MERCANTILE, INDUSTRIAL AND FINANCIAL
       -- and early twentieth-century JAPANESE capitalism created
       colonies in large parts of the third world. ⭐ SOME OF THESE
       WERE SETTLER COLONIES."
       -> AMERICA, AUSTRALIA, NEW ZEALAND, SOUTH AFRICA
       -> the population is REPLACED
                          |
   2. DIRECT IMPERIAL CONTROL
      ⭐ "Others, such as BRITISH RULE IN INDIA, are examples of
        DIRECT IMPERIAL CONTROL."
       -> the population STAYS and is GOVERNED
                          |
   3. THE SEMI-COLONY
      ⭐⭐ "The case of nineteenth- and early twentieth-century
        CHINA illustrates A THIRD VARIANT. Here BRITAIN, FRANCE,
        GERMANY, RUSSIA, AMERICA AND JAPAN MEDDLED IN CHINESE
        AFFAIRS WITHOUT DIRECTLY TAKING OVER STATE POWER. They
        EXPLOITED THE COUNTRY'S RESOURCES to their own advantage,
        ⭐ SERIOUSLY COMPROMISING CHINESE SOVEREIGNTY AND REDUCING
        THE COUNTRY TO THE STATUS OF A SEMI-COLONY."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This three-way distinction is the single most useful analytical tool in the book, and it explains outcomes that otherwise look arbitrary.**
> ⭐ **Where the coloniser INTENDED TO STAY (settler colonies), the indigenous population was displaced or destroyed — because the land itself was the object.**
> ⭐ **Where the coloniser intended to RULE AND EXTRACT (India), the population was preserved — because it was the labour force and the taxpayer.** **India was not spared genocide by British benevolence; it was spared because 250 million people were more valuable alive and taxed than dead.**
> ⭐⭐ **Where the coloniser wanted NEITHER land nor administration but only access (China), sovereignty was hollowed out without being formally taken.**
> **Say that in an answer and you have explained why "colonialism" produced such different demographic outcomes in North America, India and China — a comparison that Theme 6 and Theme 7 together are built to support.**

### 🗳️ C. CIVIC vs ETHNIC NATIONALISM ⭐⭐⭐⭐ — the best short passage in the book

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The common ground first:** *"**ALL NATIONALISMS ARE DOCTRINES OF POPULAR SOVEREIGNTY.** Nationalist movements believe that **political power should rest with the people**, and ⭐ **THIS IS WHAT MAKES NATIONALISM A MODERN CONCEPT.**"*

| | **CIVIC NATIONALISM** | **ETHNIC / RELIGIOUS NATIONALISM** |
|---|---|---|
| **Who is sovereign** | ⭐⭐ *"vests sovereignty in **ALL PEOPLE regardless of LANGUAGE, ETHNICITY, RELIGION or GENDER**"* | *"tries to build national solidarities around **A GIVEN LANGUAGE, RELIGION OR SET OF TRADITIONS**"* |
| **How the nation is defined** | ⭐⭐⭐ *"defines nationhood **in terms of CITIZENSHIP, not ethnicity or religion**"* | ⭐ *"defining the people **ETHNICALLY, not in terms of common citizenship**"* |
| **What it seeks to create** | *"a **community of RIGHTS-EXERCISING CITIZENS**"* | ⚠️ *"in a multi-ethnic country, ethnic nationalists might **LIMIT THE EXERCISE OF SOVEREIGNTY TO A CHOSEN PEOPLE, often assumed to be SUPERIOR TO MINORITY COMMUNITIES**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Where each is found:** *"**Today, MOST WESTERN COUNTRIES DEFINE THEIR NATIONHOOD IN TERMS OF COMMON CITIZENSHIP and not by common ethnicity.** ⚠️ **One prominent exception is GERMANY**, where ideas of ethnic nationalism have had **a long and troubling career going back to the reaction against the FRENCH IMPERIAL OCCUPATION OF GERMAN STATES IN 1806.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the sentence that makes it examinable in an Indian context:** *"**Ideologies of civic nationalism have VIED WITH those of ethnic/religious nationalism THE WORLD OVER, and this has been so in MODERN INDIA, CHINA AND JAPAN AS WELL.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the most directly transferable passage in the entire book — it gives you a precise vocabulary for questions you will certainly face.**
> ⭐ **Note first what the two share: BOTH are doctrines of popular sovereignty, and both are modern.** Ethnic nationalism is **not** a survival of ancient tribalism; **it is a modern claim about who "the people" are.** That correction alone is worth marks.
> ⭐⭐ **The distinction is about the ANSWER to one question: who counts as the people?** Civic nationalism answers *"whoever holds citizenship"*; ethnic nationalism answers *"whoever shares the descent, language or faith"*. ⭐ **The first can absorb newcomers; the second cannot, by construction.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Now connect it to the rest of your syllabus and it does a great deal of work:** **Class 10 Ch.1** (Germany's ethnic route to unification, and the 1806 origin NCERT names); **Class 9 Ch.3** (Nazism as ethnic nationalism taken to its conclusion); **Class 10 supplement Part C** (*Anandamath* imagining the nation as a single religious community, and NCERT's own questions — *"Was India to be a nation of only a single religious community? Who had natural claims to belong?"*); and **the Indian Constitution's choice of citizenship over ethnicity.** ⭐⭐ **The Class 11 section introduction supplies the theory for which Classes 9 and 10 supply the cases.**

## 1️⃣ What the chapter is doing, and the historiography ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐ THE PROCESS -- "From the EIGHTEENTH CENTURY, more areas of
   SOUTH AMERICA, CENTRAL AMERICA, NORTH AMERICA, SOUTH AFRICA,
   AUSTRALIA and NEW ZEALAND came to be SETTLED BY IMMIGRANTS FROM
   EUROPE. ⭐ THIS LED TO MANY OF THE NATIVE PEOPLES BEING PUSHED
   OUT INTO OTHER AREAS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ THE VOCABULARY -- "The European settlements were called
   'COLONIES'. When THE EUROPEAN INHABITANTS of the colonies became
   INDEPENDENT OF THE EUROPEAN 'MOTHER-COUNTRY', these colonies
   became 'STATES' OR COUNTRIES."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE RESULT TODAY -- "Today, these EUROPEANS AND ASIANS
   FORM THE MAJORITY in these countries, and THE NUMBER OF THE
   NATIVE INHABITANTS ARE VERY SMALL. ⭐ THEY ARE HARDLY SEEN IN
   THE TOWNS, and PEOPLE HAVE FORGOTTEN THAT THEY ONCE OCCUPIED
   MUCH OF THE COUNTRY"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ EXCEPT IN THE NAMES: "the names of many rivers, towns, etc.
   are derived from 'NATIVE' NAMES" --
        USA ......... OHIO · MISSISSIPPI · SEATTLE
        CANADA ...... SASKATCHEWAN
        AUSTRALIA ... WOLLONGONG · PARRAMATTA
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read that last box carefully — it is doing something subtle.** ⭐ **The map still speaks the language of the people who were removed.** Every American who says "Mississippi" or "Ohio" is using a word from a people who were driven off it. **A place-name is the most durable form of evidence there is, because it survives in the mouths of the people who benefited from the erasure.** *(That is a genuinely good Essay opening.)*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The historiographical arc — and this answers NCERT's exercise "Why was the history of the Australian native peoples left out of history books?":**

```
   ⚠️ TILL THE MID-TWENTIETH CENTURY
   "American and Australian history textbooks used to describe how
    Europeans ⭐ 'DISCOVERED' THE AMERICAS AND AUSTRALIA. THEY
    HARDLY MENTIONED THE NATIVE PEOPLES EXCEPT TO SUGGEST THAT
    THEY WERE HOSTILE TO EUROPEANS."
                          |
   FROM THE 1840s -- "These peoples were, however, STUDIED BY
   ANTHROPOLOGISTS in America"
   ⚠️ NOTE THE CATEGORY: they were objects of SCIENCE, not
      subjects of HISTORY
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ MUCH LATER, FROM THE 1960s -- "the native peoples were
   ENCOURAGED TO WRITE THEIR OWN HISTORIES OR TO DICTATE THEM
   (⭐ THIS IS CALLED ORAL HISTORY)"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ TODAY -- "it is possible to read HISTORICAL WORKS AND
   FICTION WRITTEN BY THE NATIVE PEOPLES", and museums show
   galleries of 'native art'. ⭐ "The new NATIONAL MUSEUM OF THE
   AMERICAN INDIAN in the USA HAS BEEN CURATED BY AMERICAN INDIANS
   THEMSELVES."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The word "discovered" is the whole problem in one verb, and you should say so explicitly.** ⭐ **A land can only be "discovered" if the people already living on it do not count as people who know where they are.** **The vocabulary of the textbook did the ideological work before any argument had to be made** — which is exactly the point NCERT makes about *terra nullius* later in this chapter.
> ⭐⭐ **Then note the three-stage progression, because it is a model of how any suppressed history is recovered:** **(1) absence** — mentioned only as hostile; **(2) ANTHROPOLOGY** — studied as a curiosity, in the present tense, as specimens rather than actors; **(3) ORAL HISTORY and self-authorship** — from the 1960s, and finally **a national museum curated by the people it is about.** ⭐ **The decisive shift is from being DESCRIBED to being the DESCRIBER.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐ **This is precisely the arc of Class 10 supplement Part C's caste section** — Chandu Menon writing about a foolish landlord, then Potheri Kunjambu writing from a lower caste, then Advaita Malla Burman writing from inside the fisherfolk community. ⭐⭐ **"Who writes changes what can be seen" is the same finding in a different continent, and using both together is very strong.**

## 2️⃣ European Imperialism and its variations ⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐ "The American empires of SPAIN AND PORTUGAL DID NOT EXPAND
   AFTER THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY. From that time other countries
   -- ⭐ FRANCE, HOLLAND AND ENGLAND -- began to extend their
   trading activities and to establish colonies in AMERICA, AFRICA
   and ASIA."
   ⚠️ "IRELAND ALSO WAS VIRTUALLY A COLONY OF ENGLAND, as the
      landowners there were MOSTLY ENGLISH SETTLERS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE DRIVER AND THE VARIATION
   "it became obvious that while it was ⭐ THE PROSPECT OF PROFIT
    WHICH DROVE PEOPLE TO ESTABLISH COLONIES, there were
    SIGNIFICANT VARIATIONS IN THE NATURE OF THE CONTROL
    ESTABLISHED."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ SOUTH ASIA -- "trading companies like the EAST INDIA
   COMPANY MADE THEMSELVES INTO POLITICAL POWERS, DEFEATED LOCAL
   RULERS AND ANNEXED THEIR TERRITORIES.
   ⭐ THEY RETAINED THE OLDER WELL-DEVELOPED ADMINISTRATIVE SYSTEM
   AND COLLECTED TAXES FROM LANDOWNERS. Later they built RAILWAYS
   to make trade easier, excavated MINES and established big
   PLANTATIONS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ AFRICA -- "Europeans TRADED ON THE COAST, EXCEPT IN SOUTH
   AFRICA, and ONLY IN THE LATE NINETEENTH CENTURY did they
   VENTURE INTO THE INTERIOR. After this, some of the European
   countries REACHED AN AGREEMENT TO DIVIDE UP AFRICA as colonies
   for themselves."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ WHO COUNTS AS A "SETTLER"
   "The word 'SETTLER' is used for
        THE DUTCH in South Africa
        THE BRITISH in Ireland, New Zealand and Australia
        THE EUROPEANS in America"
   ⭐ "The official language in these colonies was ENGLISH (EXCEPT
     IN CANADA, WHERE FRENCH IS ALSO AN OFFICIAL LANGUAGE)."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **The sentence about India repays attention: the Company "RETAINED the older well-developed administrative system and collected taxes from landowners."** ⭐ **A settler colony destroys the existing order because it wants the land empty; an extractive colony PRESERVES the existing order because it wants the revenue machinery working.** **The same imperialism produced opposite treatments of local institutions, for the same reason — profit.**
> ⚠️ **And do not skip Ireland.** *"virtually a colony of England, as the landowners there were mostly English settlers"* — ⭐ **settler colonialism inside Europe, next door to the coloniser.** *(Recall Class 10 Ch.1: Ireland forcibly incorporated in 1801 after Wolfe Tone's failed revolt; and Class 10 Ch.5: the Vernacular Press Act was modelled on the IRISH Press Laws. Ireland was Britain's laboratory.)*

**⭐⭐ The names of the "New World" — a compact Prelims box:**

| Name | Origin |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **AMERICA** | *"First used after the publication of the travels of **AMERIGO VESPUCCI (1451–1512)**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **CANADA** | From ***kanata*** = **"village"** in the language of the **Huron-Iroquois**, *"as heard by the explorer **JACQUES CARTIER in 1535**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **AUSTRALIA** | *"Sixteenth-century name for land in the **Great Southern Ocean** (***austral* is Latin for 'SOUTH'**)"* |
| ⭐⭐ **NEW ZEALAND** | *"Name given by **TASMAN of Holland**, who was **the first to sight these islands in 1642** (***zee* is Dutch for 'SEA'**)"* |

> ⭐⭐ **And the detail that makes the point:** *"The Geographical Dictionary lists **OVER A HUNDRED PLACE-NAMES in the Americas and Australia which begin with 'NEW'.**"*
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **A hundred places called "New" something — New England, New York, New South Wales, New Orleans.** ⭐ **The settler names the land after the place he left, which is a way of announcing that nothing here had a name worth keeping.** **Set it against the surviving native names (Ohio, Saskatchewan, Wollongong) and you have the two impulses of settler colonialism side by side: erase, and yet unavoidably inherit.**

## 3️⃣ The Native Peoples of North America ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE PEOPLING OF THE CONTINENT
   "The earliest inhabitants of North America came FROM ASIA
    ⭐ OVER 30,000 YEARS AGO on A LAND-BRIDGE ACROSS THE BERING
    STRAITS, and during the LAST ICE AGE 10,000 YEARS AGO they
    MOVED FURTHER SOUTH."
   ⭐⭐ "The OLDEST ARTEFACT found in America -- AN ARROW-POINT --
      is 11,000 YEARS OLD."
   ⭐ "The population STARTED TO INCREASE ABOUT 5,000 YEARS AGO
     WHEN THE CLIMATE BECAME MORE STABLE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ HOW THEY LIVED -- "in BANDS, in VILLAGES ALONG RIVER
   VALLEYS. They ate FISH AND MEAT, and cultivated VEGETABLES AND
   MAIZE."
   ⭐ LONG JOURNEYS for meat, "chiefly that of BISON, the wild
     buffalo that roamed the grasslands" -- easier FROM THE
     SEVENTEENTH CENTURY, "when the natives started to RIDE
     HORSES, WHICH THEY BOUGHT FROM SPANISH SETTLERS"
   ⭐⭐ "BUT THEY ONLY KILLED AS MANY ANIMALS AS THEY NEEDED FOR
      FOOD."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **William Macleish, *The Day before America* — a passage worth quoting whole in an Essay:**
> *"At sunset on the day before America … **diversity lay at every hand. People spoke in MORE THAN A HUNDRED TONGUES. They lived by EVERY POSSIBLE COMBINATION OF HUNTING, FISHING, GATHERING, GARDENING, AND FARMING open to them.** The quality of soils and the effort required to open and tend them determined some of their choices of how to live. **Cultural and social biases determined others. Surpluses of fish or grain or garden plants or meats helped create POWERFUL, TIERED SOCIETIES HERE BUT NOT THERE. ⭐ SOME CULTURES HAD ENDURED FOR MILLENNIA…**"*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why they built no kingdoms — and this is the hinge of the whole chapter:**

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ "THEY DID NOT ATTEMPT EXTENSIVE AGRICULTURE AND SINCE
   THEY DID NOT PRODUCE A SURPLUS, THEY DID NOT DEVELOP KINGDOMS
   AND EMPIRES AS IN CENTRAL AND SOUTH AMERICA."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ "There were SOME INSTANCES OF QUARRELS BETWEEN TRIBES OVER
   TERRITORY, but ⭐ BY AND LARGE CONTROL OF LAND WAS NOT AN ISSUE.
   THEY WERE CONTENT WITH THE FOOD AND SHELTER THEY GOT FROM THE
   LAND WITHOUT FEELING ANY NEED TO 'OWN' IT."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE ECONOMY OF THE GIFT
   "An important feature of their tradition was that of MAKING
    FORMAL ALLIANCES AND FRIENDSHIPS, AND EXCHANGING GIFTS.
    ⭐⭐ GOODS WERE OBTAINED NOT BY BUYING THEM, BUT AS GIFTS."
   ⭐ WAMPUM BELTS, "made of COLOURED SHELLS SEWN TOGETHER, were
     EXCHANGED BY NATIVE TRIBES AFTER A TREATY WAS AGREED TO."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AND THEIR OWN FORM OF LITERACY
   "NUMEROUS LANGUAGES were spoken, THOUGH THESE WERE NOT WRITTEN
    DOWN. They believed that ⭐ TIME MOVED IN CYCLES, and each
    tribe had accounts about their origins PASSED ON FROM ONE
    GENERATION TO THE NEXT. They were SKILLED CRAFTSPEOPLE and
    WOVE BEAUTIFUL TEXTILES.
    ⭐⭐ THEY COULD READ THE LAND -- THEY COULD UNDERSTAND THE
    CLIMATES AND DIFFERENT LANDSCAPES IN THE WAY LITERATE PEOPLE
    READ WRITTEN TEXTS."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"They could read the land the way literate people read written texts" is the best sentence in the chapter, and it is doing precise analytical work.**
> ⭐ **Recall the European definition of civilisation given a few pages later: LITERACY, ORGANISED RELIGION, URBANISM.** By that test the natives failed on all three. **NCERT's reply is not to deny the test but to show that it was measuring the wrong thing** — a people who can read weather, tracks, soils and seasons possess a body of transmitted knowledge as complex as any written one. ⭐⭐ **"Uncivilised" turns out to mean "using a form of knowledge the observer cannot read."**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is EXACTLY Class 9 Ch.4–5 on India** — shifting cultivators and pastoralists declared primitive and wasteful by a colonial state that could not read their systems, and whose land was therefore classified as "waste". **Two continents, one manoeuvre.**
> ⭐⭐ **And note the causal chain in the "no kingdoms" box, because it is the tragedy of the chapter in three steps: NO SURPLUS → NO STATE → NO ARMY.** ⭐ **The very restraint that NCERT praises — *"they only killed as many animals as they needed"* — is what left them without the surplus, the standing armies and the fortified states that might have resisted.** **Sustainability and military survival pulled in opposite directions, and that is a genuinely tragic finding, not a moral failing.**

**⭐⭐ The vocabulary — worth knowing precisely, because the terms carry politics:**

| Term | NCERT's definition |
|---|---|

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **The Canadian shift from "bands" (1876) to "nations" (1980s) is a whole political history compressed into one word.** ⭐ **A "band" is a group; a "nation" is a sovereign entity that can hold rights and sign treaties.** **Changing the noun changed what could be claimed in court** — which is exactly what the 1982 Constitution Act then recognised.
> ⭐⭐ **And note the aside NCERT slips in:** names of native tribes *"are often given to things unconnected with them: **DAKOTA (an aeroplane), CHEROKEE (a jeep), PONTIAC (a car), MOHAWK (a haircut)!**"* ⭐ **The names survive as branding for the settler's machines.** **A culture can be simultaneously destroyed and merchandised.**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The Hopi account of first contact — Lee Brown, 1986. Read it once; it changes the emotional register of the chapter:**
> *"It was indicated on the stone tablets that the **Hopis** had that **the first brothers and sisters that would come back to them would come as TURTLES across the land.** They would be human beings, **but they would come as turtles.** So when the time came close **the Hopis were at a special village TO WELCOME the turtles** … they saw **the Spanish Conquistadores coming, COVERED IN ARMOUR, LIKE TURTLES ACROSS THE LAND.** So this was them. So they went out to the Spanish man and **they extended their hand hoping for the handshake** — ⭐ **but into the hand the Spanish man DROPPED A TRINKET.** And so word spread throughout North America that **there was going to be a hard time**…"*
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **An outstretched hand meeting a trinket is the entire encounter in one gesture — one side offering relationship, the other offering merchandise.** **That is the "competing notions of civilisation" the section introduction names, dramatised in a sentence.**

## 5️⃣ Mutual Perceptions ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE EUROPEAN TEST OF CIVILISATION -- three criteria,
      learn them
   "In the eighteenth century, western Europeans defined
    'CIVILISED' people in terms of
        1. LITERACY
        2. AN ORGANISED RELIGION
        3. URBANISM
    ⭐ TO THEM, THE NATIVES OF AMERICA APPEARED 'UNCIVILISED'."
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Four Europeans, four verdicts — this comparison is a ready-made answer:**

| Who | What they said | Had they met a native American? |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **JEAN-JACQUES ROUSSEAU** | *"such people were **TO BE ADMIRED, as they were UNTOUCHED BY THE CORRUPTIONS OF 'CIVILISATION'**"* — the popular term was ⭐ **"THE NOBLE SAVAGE"** | ⚠️ **No** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **WILLIAM WORDSWORTH** | They lived *"**amid wilds / Where fancy hath small liberty to grace / The affections, to exalt them or refine**"* — meaning ⚠️ *"people living close to nature had **ONLY LIMITED POWERS OF IMAGINATION AND EMOTION**"* | ⚠️ **No** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WASHINGTON IRVING** | *"The Indians I have had an opportunity of seeing in real life are **QUITE DIFFERENT FROM THOSE DESCRIBED IN POETRY** … **Taciturn they are, it is true, WHEN IN COMPANY WITH WHITE MEN, whose goodwill they DISTRUST and whose language they DO NOT UNDERSTAND; ⭐ BUT THE WHITE MAN IS EQUALLY TACITURN UNDER LIKE CIRCUMSTANCES.** When the Indians are among themselves, they are **GREAT MIMICS, and entertain themselves excessively AT THE EXPENSE OF THE WHITES** … ⚠️ **The white men (as I have witnessed) are prone to treat the poor Indians as LITTLE BETTER THAN ANIMALS.**"* | ⭐⭐ **YES** |
| ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **THOMAS JEFFERSON** *(third President of the USA)* | *"**This unfortunate race which we have been taking so much pains to civilise… have JUSTIFIED EXTERMINATION.**"* | — |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT has arranged these four deliberately, and the arrangement IS the argument. Notice who had actually met a native American.**
> ⭐ **Rousseau idealised them and Wordsworth patronised them — and NCERT points out, in one dry clause, that NEITHER HAD MET ONE.** ⭐⭐ **The "noble savage" and the emotionally stunted primitive are the same error with opposite signs: both are European ideas about nature, projected onto people who were not consulted.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Irving, who HAD met them, reports something neither ideal nor primitive — that natives were reserved around white men for the perfectly ordinary reason that they distrusted them and did not speak the language, and that white men behave identically in the same situation.** ⭐ **He also records that the natives found the Europeans funny, and mimicked them for entertainment — which quietly destroys the assumption that the natives were awestruck.** **Contact, in other words, produces accurate description; distance produces theory.**
> ⚠️⭐⭐ **And then Jefferson, who was neither ignorant nor cruel by the standards of his time, moves in one sentence from "civilising" to "extermination" — the two projects turn out to be compatible.** *(NCERT notes he was a contemporary of Wordsworth, and that his words "would lead to a public outcry today.")*
> 🔗 **Set the whole table against Class 10 supplement Part A's Vietnamese textbooks calling the Vietnamese "skilled copyists but not creative", and Class 9 Ch.4 on "criminal tribes".** ⭐⭐ **The coloniser's description of the colonised is never merely a description — it is the justification for what is about to be done.**

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The two incompatible economies — GIFT versus COMMODITY:**

```
   THE NATIVE VIEW           |  THE EUROPEAN VIEW
   --------------------------|---------------------------------
   "the goods they exchanged |  "For the Europeans, DREAMING OF
    with the Europeans were  |   BECOMING RICH, the fish and furs
    ⭐ GIFTS, GIVEN IN       |   were ⭐ COMMODITIES, WHICH THEY
    FRIENDSHIP"              |   WOULD SELL FOR A PROFIT IN
                             |   EUROPE"
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ AND SO THE PRICES MADE NO SENSE
   "The prices of the goods they sold VARIED FROM YEAR TO YEAR,
    DEPENDING ON THE SUPPLY. ⭐ THE NATIVES COULD NOT UNDERSTAND
    THIS -- THEY HAD NO SENSE OF THE 'MARKET' IN FARAWAY EUROPE.
    They were PUZZLED by the fact that the European traders
    SOMETIMES GAVE THEM A LOT OF THINGS in exchange for their
    goods, SOMETIMES VERY LITTLE."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ AND THE BEAVERS
   "They were also SADDENED BY THE GREED OF THE EUROPEANS. In their
    IMPATIENCE TO GET FURS, they had SLAUGHTERED HUNDREDS OF
    BEAVERS, and the natives were VERY UNEASY, ⭐ FEARING THAT THE
    ANIMALS WOULD TAKE REVENGE ON THEM FOR THIS DESTRUCTION."
```

> ⭐⭐ **A lovely detail on native resistance in words:** *"Many **folk tales of the natives MOCKED EUROPEANS and described them as GREEDY AND DECEITFUL**, but **because these were told as IMAGINARY STORIES, it was only MUCH LATER that the Europeans UNDERSTOOD THE REFERENCES.**"*
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **Satire under the cover of fiction, invisible to the powerful until long afterwards.** 🔗 *(Exactly the Vietnamese teachers "quietly modifying the text" in Class 10 supplement Part A — the weapons of the weak are usually deniable.)*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The gift-versus-commodity contrast is the intellectual core of the chapter and should anchor any answer on it.** ⭐ **A gift creates a RELATIONSHIP and an obligation that continues; a commodity closes a transaction and ends it.** The natives thought they were making allies; the Europeans thought they were making margins. **Neither side was lying, and that is why the misunderstanding was so total.**
> ⭐⭐ **Then note the price puzzle, which is genuinely poignant: the natives could not see why the same furs fetched different goods in different years, because THE CAUSE WAS IN EUROPE — a market they had never seen and could not have imagined.** ⭐ **Their terms of trade were being set by information they had no access to.** 🔗 **That is the identical asymmetry Class 10 Ch.3 describes for colonial primary producers and Class 10 Ch.4 for Indian weavers dealing with the gomastha — and it is still the structural complaint of commodity exporters today.**

**⭐⭐⭐ From traders to settlers — and the change of intent:**

> *"Following the first Europeans, who were **traders**, were those who came to **'SETTLE' in America.** From the seventeenth century, there were groups of Europeans **being persecuted because they were of a different sect of Christianity** — ⭐ **Protestants living in predominantly Catholic countries, or Catholics in countries where Protestantism was the official religion.** Many of them left Europe **to begin a new life.**"*
>
> ⚠️ *"**As long as there was VACANT LAND, this was not a problem**, but gradually the Europeans **moved further inland, near native villages. THEY USED THEIR IRON TOOLS TO CUT DOWN FORESTS TO LAY OUT FARMS.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The two ways of seeing a forest:** *"Natives and Europeans **saw different things when they looked at forests** — ⭐ **natives IDENTIFIED TRACKS INVISIBLE TO THE EUROPEANS. Europeans IMAGINED THE FORESTS CUT DOWN AND REPLACED BY CORNFIELDS.** **Jefferson's 'dream' was a country populated by Europeans with small farms.** The natives, who **grew crops for their own needs, not for sale and profit**, and thought it **wrong to 'own' the land**, could not understand this. ⚠️ **In Jefferson's view, THIS MADE THEM 'UNCIVILISED'.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Natives identified tracks invisible to the Europeans; Europeans imagined the forests cut down and replaced by cornfields" — one sees what IS THERE, the other sees what COULD BE THERE.** ⭐ **The settler's gaze is inherently a gaze at a future in which the present occupants do not appear.** ⭐⭐ **And note that a people were declared uncivilised for the specific offence of producing for use rather than for profit.** **"Civilisation" was being defined as participation in a market.**

## 6️⃣ The Native Peoples Lose their Land ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐ HOW THE STATES GREW -- "The countries known as CANADA and
   the UNITED STATES OF AMERICA came into existence AT THE END OF
   THE EIGHTEENTH CENTURY. At that time they occupied ONLY A
   FRACTION of the land they now cover."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ HOW THE USA ACQUIRED TERRITORY
     BY PURCHASE -- ⭐ land in the south from FRANCE (the
        'LOUISIANA PURCHASE', 1803) and ⭐ ALASKA from RUSSIA
     BY WAR -- "much of SOUTHERN USA was WON FROM MEXICO"
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND THE SENTENCE TO QUOTE
   "IT DID NOT OCCUR TO ANYONE THAT THE CONSENT OF NATIVES LIVING
    IN THESE AREAS SHOULD HAVE BEEN ASKED."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE FRONTIER -- "The western 'FRONTIER' of the USA was
   A SHIFTING ONE, and AS IT MOVED, THE NATIVES ALSO WERE FORCED
   TO MOVE BACK."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT asks directly: *"What did the 'frontier' mean to the Americans?"* Answer it in three layers, not one.**
> ⭐ **(1) Literally, a MOVING LINE, not a border** — *"a shifting one"*, pushed west decade by decade until **1892, when the USA declared continental expansion complete.**
> ⭐⭐ **(2) Economically, the promise of LAND THAT COULD BE OWNED** — which is exactly what drew the younger sons of Britain and France, the dispossessed of Germany, Sweden and Italy, and the Poles who found the prairies like their steppes.
> ⭐⭐⭐ **(3) Ideologically, an EMPTY SPACE.** That is the fiction the whole system rested on, and NCERT punctures it with one line: **the consent of the people living there was never considered.** ⚠️ **A "frontier" is a border with people on the other side; calling it a frontier rather than a border was itself the claim.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐ **Add Marx's line, printed in the margin of this very page:** the American frontier was *"**the last positive capitalist utopia … the limitless nature and space to which THE LIMITLESS THIRST FOR PROFIT ADAPTS ITSELF**"* *('Bastiat and Carey', **Grundrisse**)*. ⭐ **Note what happened when the limitless space ran out in 1892: "within a few years the USA was setting up its own colonies — in HAWAII and the PHILIPPINES."** **The frontier did not close; it went overseas.**

**⭐⭐⭐ Who the settlers were, and why they wanted land:**

| Group | Why they came |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **From Britain and France** | *"**YOUNGER SONS who would NOT INHERIT their fathers' property** and therefore were eager to **own land in America**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **From Germany, Sweden, Italy** | *"had **LOST THEIR LANDS TO BIG FARMERS**, and wanted **farms they could own**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **From Poland** | *"happy to work in the **prairie grasslands, WHICH REMINDED THEM OF THE STEPPES of their homes**, and were **excited at being able to buy huge properties AT VERY LOW PRICES**"* |

> ⭐⭐ **What they did to the land:** *"They cleared land and developed agriculture, **introducing crops (RICE AND COTTON) WHICH COULD NOT GROW IN EUROPE and therefore COULD BE SOLD THERE FOR PROFIT.** To protect their huge farms from wild animals — **WOLVES AND MOUNTAIN LIONS — THESE WERE HUNTED TO EXTINCTION.** ⭐ **They felt totally secure only with the INVENTION OF BARBED WIRE IN 1873.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **The settlers were, very largely, PEOPLE WHO HAD THEMSELVES BEEN DISPOSSESSED IN EUROPE** — younger sons excluded by primogeniture, peasants squeezed out by big farmers, the poor of Poland. ⭐⭐ **A dispossession in Europe was exported and became a dispossession in America.** **That does not excuse anything, but it explains the ferocity of the hunger for ownership: these were people for whom "owning land" was the thing that had been denied them.** 🔗 *(The enclosures of Class 9 Ch.4 and Class 10 Ch.4 are the other end of this migration — the same process seen from the side that emptied.)*

**⚠️⭐⭐⭐ Slavery — and why it was a Southern institution:**

> *"The **climate of the southern region was TOO HOT FOR EUROPEANS TO WORK OUTDOORS**, and the experience of South American colonies had shown that **the natives who had been enslaved HAD DIED IN LARGE NUMBERS. ⭐ PLANTATION OWNERS THEREFORE BOUGHT SLAVES IN AFRICA.** Protests by anti-slavery groups led to **a ban on the slave TRADE**, ⚠️ **but the Africans who were in the USA REMAINED SLAVES, AS DID THEIR CHILDREN.**"*
>
> *"The **NORTHERN states**, where **the economy did not depend on plantations (and therefore on slavery)**, argued for ending slavery **which they condemned as an inhuman practice. In 1861–65 there was a war** between the states that wanted to retain slavery and those supporting abolition. **The latter won. Slavery was abolished** — ⚠️ **though it was only in the TWENTIETH CENTURY that African Americans were able to win the battle for CIVIL LIBERTIES, and SEGREGATION between 'whites' and 'non-whites' in schools and public transport was ended.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **Two things to carry away.** ⭐ **(1) Banning the slave TRADE is not the same as abolishing SLAVERY** — the ban stopped imports and left everyone already enslaved, and their children, in bondage. **A reform aimed at the flow leaves the stock untouched.** ⭐⭐ **(2) NCERT is careful that the North's opposition tracked its economic structure** — *"where the economy did not depend on plantations (and therefore on slavery)"*. **That is not cynicism; it is the observation that moral positions are cheapest where they cost least**, and it is the same structure as the "deserving poor" and the fear-driven housing reforms of Class 10 supplement Part B. ⚠️ **And note the century-long gap between legal abolition (1865) and actual civil rights — emancipation on paper is the beginning of a struggle, not its end.**

### ⚖️ 6.1 The Cherokee case and the Trail of Tears ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE GENERAL METHOD
   "In the USA, as settlement expanded, the natives were INDUCED OR
    FORCED TO MOVE, AFTER SIGNING TREATIES SELLING THEIR LAND.
    ⚠️ THE PRICES PAID WERE VERY LOW, and there were instances when
    the Americans CHEATED THEM by TAKING MORE LAND OR PAYING LESS
    THAN PROMISED."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE GEORGIA CASE -- "Officials had argued that the
   CHEROKEE tribe WAS GOVERNED BY STATE LAWS, BUT COULD NOT ENJOY
   THE RIGHTS OF CITIZENS."
   ⭐⭐ AND THE DETAIL THAT MAKES IT WORSE: "This was DESPITE THE
      FACT THAT, OF ALL THE NATIVE PEOPLES, THE CHEROKEES WERE THE
      ONES WHO HAD MADE THE MOST EFFORT TO LEARN ENGLISH AND TO
      UNDERSTAND THE AMERICAN WAY OF LIFE; EVEN SO THEY WERE NOT
      ALLOWED THE RIGHTS OF CITIZENS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 1832 -- US CHIEF JUSTICE JOHN MARSHALL'S JUDGMENT
   The Cherokees were ⭐ "A DISTINCT COMMUNITY, OCCUPYING ITS OWN
   TERRITORY IN WHICH THE LAWS OF GEORGIA HAD NO FORCE", and
   "THEY HAD SOVEREIGNTY IN CERTAIN MATTERS."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND WHAT THE PRESIDENT DID
   "US President ANDREW JACKSON had A REPUTATION FOR FIGHTING
    AGAINST ECONOMIC AND POLITICAL PRIVILEGE, ⭐ BUT WHEN IT CAME
    TO THE INDIANS, HE WAS A DIFFERENT PERSON. HE REFUSED TO HONOUR
    THE CHIEF JUSTICE'S JUDGMENT, and ORDERED THE US ARMY TO EVICT
    THE CHEROKEES from their land and DRIVE THEM TO THE GREAT
    AMERICAN DESERT."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE TRAIL OF TEARS
   "OF THE 15,000 PEOPLE THUS FORCED TO GO,
    ⭐ OVER A QUARTER DIED ALONG THE 'TRAIL OF TEARS'."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This episode is worth learning in full, because it is a constitutional story as much as a humanitarian one, and it makes a point about the rule of law that transfers directly to GS2.**
> ⭐ **The Cherokees did everything assimilation demanded** — learned English, adopted American ways — **and were still refused citizenship.** ⚠️ **That is the crucial finding: the demand to assimilate was not an offer. It was a moving target.** *(Which is exactly why the natives later, in 1954, accepted citizenship only ON CONDITION that their reservations and traditions be left alone — see §8.)*
> ⭐⭐ **Then the constitutional core: THE SUPREME COURT RULED IN THEIR FAVOUR AND THE PRESIDENT SIMPLY IGNORED IT.** **A judgment is only as strong as the executive's willingness to enforce it**, and here the executive commanded the army. ⭐ **Rights recognised by a court but not enforced by a government are, at the moment of crisis, worth nothing** — and over 3,750 people died to prove it.
> ⭐⭐⭐ **And the detail about Jackson is the most instructive of all: a man with "a reputation for fighting against economic and political privilege" — a democrat, by the standards of his day — "BUT WHEN IT CAME TO THE INDIANS, HE WAS A DIFFERENT PERSON."** ⭐ **The democratic conscience of the age was real and it simply did not extend to people outside the political community.** **That is the theme of the next section, and it is the deepest point in the chapter.**

**⚠️⭐⭐⭐ How dispossession was justified — the arguments, which are worth knowing because they recur:**

> *"Those who took the land occupied by the tribes justified it by saying **the natives DID NOT DESERVE TO OCCUPY LAND WHICH THEY DID NOT USE TO THE MAXIMUM.** They went on to criticise them **for being LAZY, since they did not use their crafts skills to PRODUCE GOODS FOR THE MARKET**, for **not being interested in LEARNING ENGLISH** or **DRESSING 'CORRECTLY'** (which meant like the Europeans). ⚠️ **THEY DESERVED TO 'DIE OUT', they argued.** The prairies were **cleared for farmland**, and **wild bison killed off.** ⭐ *'**PRIMITIVE MAN WILL DISAPPEAR WITH THE PRIMITIVE ANIMAL**' wrote a visiting Frenchman."*

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **And the intellectual respectability supplied by the new science.** *"It is significant that it was at this time (**from the 1840s**) that the subject of **ANTHROPOLOGY** (which had been **developed in France**) was **introduced in North America**, out of **a curiosity to study the differences between native 'PRIMITIVE' communities and the 'CIVILISED' communities of Europe.** ⭐ **Some anthropologists argued that JUST AS THERE WERE NO 'PRIMITIVE' PEOPLE TO BE FOUND IN EUROPE, THE AMERICAN NATIVES TOO WOULD 'DIE OUT'.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Put the justification and the anthropology together and you see the mechanism.** ⭐ **First a moral claim: land not used "to the maximum" is forfeit — i.e. the test of a right to land is PRODUCTIVITY FOR THE MARKET.** **Then a scientific claim: these people are destined to die out anyway.** ⭐⭐ **The first makes the taking legitimate; the second makes it inevitable — and if extinction is inevitable, nobody need feel responsible for causing it.** **A prophecy of disappearance is an alibi for those doing the disappearing.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐ **This "productive use" test is exactly the colonial doctrine of *waste land* in India** *(Class 9 Ch.4–5)*: forests and pastures declared unproductive and therefore available. **Same argument, same decades, opposite hemispheres.**

**⭐⭐⭐ The population figures NCERT sets as an activity — and they repay comparing, since the chapter asks you to:**

```
                      USA, 1820      SPANISH AMERICA, 1800
   NATIVES ........   0.6 million     ⭐ 7.5 million
   WHITES .........   9.0 million        3.3 million
   MIXED EUROPEANS    0.1 million     ⭐ 5.3 million
   BLACKS .........   1.9 million        0.8 million
   TOTAL ..........  11.6 million       16.9 million
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This table answers NCERT's exercise "Comment on any points of difference between the native peoples of South and North America", and the contrast is stark.**
> ⭐ **In Spanish America the natives were still the LARGEST group (7.5m of 16.9m); in the USA they were 0.6m out of 11.6m — about 5 per cent.**
> ⭐⭐ **And look at "mixed Europeans": 5.3 million in Spanish America against 0.1 million in the USA.** ⭐⭐⭐ **That single row tells you the difference between the two colonialisms: the Spanish CONQUERED AND MIXED with a dense settled population that had states, cities and agriculture; the British and their successors DISPLACED AND REPLACED a thinner population that had none of those.** **Both were catastrophic — Theme 8 (deleted) covered the Spanish destruction of the Aztecs and Incas — but they were catastrophic in different shapes.**
> ⭐ **Note also that the underlying cause traces back to §3: the presence or absence of an agricultural SURPLUS.** **Where there were empires (Central and South America), the conquerors took them over; where there were none, the settlers took the land itself.**

**⚠️⭐⭐⭐ Reservations, and the last resistance:**

> *"the natives were **pushed westward, given land elsewhere ('THEIRS IN PERPETUITY') ⚠️ BUT OFTEN MOVED AGAIN IF ANY MINERAL — LEAD OR GOLD — OR OIL WAS FOUND ON THEIR LANDS.** Many tribes were **forced to share the land originally occupied by one tribe, thus leading to QUARRELS BETWEEN THEM.** They were **locked off in small areas called 'RESERVATIONS', ⭐ WHICH OFTEN WAS LAND WITH WHICH THEY HAD NO EARLIER CONNECTION.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐ *"**They did not give in without a fight.** The US army **crushed a series of rebellions FROM 1865 TO 1890**, and in Canada there were **armed revolts by the METIS (people of native European descent) BETWEEN 1869 AND 1885.** ⚠️ **But after that they gave up.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **"Theirs in perpetuity" — and then moved again whenever gold, lead or oil appeared underneath.** ⭐ **A guarantee that lapses whenever the land becomes valuable is not a guarantee; it is a lease terminable at the landlord's convenience.** **That is the precise legal texture of dispossession, and it is worth stating in those words.** 🔗 *(Compare Class 10 supplement Part B: the Bombay Improvement Trust displacing 64,000 and rehousing 14,000 — the promise and the delivery are different documents.)*

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CHIEF SEATTLE'S LETTER, 1854 — the most quotable passage in the book. The President had asked him to sign a treaty giving up a large part of their land. He replied:**
> *"**How can you BUY OR SELL THE SKY, THE WARMTH OF THE LAND? The idea is strange to us. IF YOU DO NOT OWN THE FRESHNESS OF THE AIR AND THE SPARKLE OF THE WATER, HOW CAN ONE BUY THEM?** ⭐ **Every part of the earth is sacred to my people. Every shining pine-needle, every sandy shore, every mist in the dark woods, every clearing and every humming insect is holy in the memory and experience of my people.** The sap which courses through the trees carries the memories of the red man…*
> *So, when the Great Chief in Washington sends word that he wishes to buy our land, **he asks much of us.** The Great Chief sends word that **he will reserve us a place so that we can live comfortably. HE WILL BE OUR FATHER AND WE WILL BE HIS CHILDREN.** So we will consider your offer to buy our land. **But it will not be easy. For this land is sacred to us.** ⭐⭐ **The shining water that moves in the streams and rivers IS NOT JUST WATER BUT THE BLOOD OF OUR ANCESTORS.** If we sell you land, you must remember that it is sacred and **you must teach your children that it is sacred**… **The water's murmur is the voice of my father's father…**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two things make this more than a beautiful lament.** ⭐ **First, it is an ARGUMENT, not a plea: Seattle is pointing out a category error.** Buying land presupposes that land is the kind of thing that can be owned and transferred — **and he is denying the premise, not refusing the price.** *(That is exactly the section introduction's point about "competing notions of civilisation".)* ⭐⭐ **Second, note the sentence about the Great Chief being "our father" and the natives "his children."** **Seattle repeats the offer's own language back at it — and paternalism, stated plainly, sounds like what it is.** 🔗 **Set this beside Class 10 supplement Part A's Tonkin Free School chant about "childish things": the colonised are addressed as children in Vietnam and in Washington, in the same decades, in the same tone.**

## 7️⃣ The Gold Rush, Industry, and Constitutional Rights ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE 1840s -- "traces of GOLD were found in the USA, in
   CALIFORNIA. This led to the 'GOLD RUSH', when THOUSANDS OF EAGER
   EUROPEANS HURRIED TO AMERICA in the hope of making a quick
   fortune."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AND WHAT IT BUILT -- "This led to THE BUILDING OF RAILWAY
   LINES ACROSS THE CONTINENT, for which ⭐ THOUSANDS OF CHINESE
   WORKERS WERE RECRUITED."
        THE USA'S RAILWAY was completed BY 1870
        CANADA'S ................................ BY 1885
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ WHY AMERICAN INDUSTRY AROSE FOR DIFFERENT REASONS
   In England, industrialisation happened partly because "SMALL
   PEASANTS WERE LOSING THEIR LAND TO BIG FARMERS, AND MOVING TO
   JOBS IN FACTORIES."
   ⭐ "IN NORTH AMERICA, INDUSTRIES DEVELOPED FOR VERY DIFFERENT
     REASONS --
        TO MANUFACTURE RAILWAY EQUIPMENT so that rapid transport
          could LINK DISTANT PLACES
        and TO PRODUCE MACHINERY WHICH WOULD MAKE LARGE-SCALE
          FARMING EASIER."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE TRANSFORMATION, IN ONE SENTENCE
   "IN 1860, THE USA HAD BEEN AN UNDEVELOPED ECONOMY.
    IN 1890, IT WAS THE LEADING INDUSTRIAL POWER IN THE WORLD."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ BY 1890 -- "THE BISON HAD ALMOST BEEN EXTERMINATED,
   THUS ENDING THE LIFE OF HUNTING THE NATIVES HAD FOLLOWED FOR
   CENTURIES."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1892 -- "the USA's CONTINENTAL EXPANSION WAS COMPLETE...
   THERE NO LONGER REMAINED THE 'FRONTIER'."
   ⚠️ "WITHIN A FEW YEARS THE USA WAS SETTING UP ITS OWN COLONIES
      -- IN HAWAII AND THE PHILIPPINES. ⭐ IT HAD BECOME AN
      IMPERIAL POWER."
```

> ⭐⭐ **Andrew Carnegie**, *"a poor immigrant from Scotland who became one of the first millionaire industrialists in the USA"*, caught the mood: *"**The old nations creep on at a snail's pace… THE REPUBLIC THUNDERS ON AT THE SPEED OF AN EXPRESS.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The industrialisation contrast is a genuinely important analytical point and it is easy to state.** ⭐ **Britain industrialised because DISPOSSESSION SUPPLIED LABOUR — enclosure pushed peasants into factories. America industrialised because SPACE DEMANDED MACHINERY — railways to cross the distance and machines to farm at scale.** ⭐⭐ **Britain's constraint was people with nothing to do; America's constraint was land with nobody to work it.** **Same outcome, opposite scarcities** — which is exactly the section introduction's warning that there is no single model. 🔗 *(And Class 10 Ch.4 gives you the third case: India, where the constraint was neither, because policy was made elsewhere.)*
> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Then the two dates that belong together, and the pairing is the argument of the chapter: BY 1890 THE BISON WERE GONE; BY 1892 THE FRONTIER WAS CLOSED.** ⭐ **The extermination of an animal and the completion of a nation are the same event described from two sides.** *"Primitive man will disappear with the primitive animal"* was not a prediction — **the bison were destroyed, and destroying them destroyed the economy of the people who lived on them.** **Ecological destruction was, in effect, a method of conquest.** 🔗 *(Compare Class 10 Ch.3's rinderpest in Africa: 90% of cattle dead, and Africans forced into wage labour as a result. The mechanism is identical.)*
> ⭐⭐ **And the closing irony — the frontier shuts in 1892 and within a few years the USA takes HAWAII and the PHILIPPINES.** **A country founded in revolt against empire became one as soon as it ran out of interior.**

### 🗳️ 7.1 Constitutional Rights — and who they were for ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT THE SETTLERS CLAIMED
   "The 'DEMOCRATIC SPIRIT' which had been the rallying cry of the
    settlers in their fight for independence in the 1770s, CAME TO
    DEFINE THE IDENTITY OF THE USA AGAINST THE MONARCHIES AND
    ARISTOCRACIES OF THE OLD WORLD."
   ⭐⭐ "Also important to them was that their constitution included
      THE INDIVIDUAL'S 'RIGHT TO PROPERTY', WHICH THE STATE COULD
      NOT OVERRIDE."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND THE SENTENCE THAT UNDOES IT
   "BUT BOTH DEMOCRATIC RIGHTS (THE RIGHT TO VOTE FOR
    REPRESENTATIVES TO CONGRESS AND FOR THE PRESIDENT) AND THE
    RIGHT TO PROPERTY WERE ONLY FOR WHITE MEN."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **And a striking reversal of influence, worth knowing:** **Daniel Paul, a Canadian native, pointed out in 2000** that **Thomas Paine**, *"the champion of democracy at the time of the War for American Independence and the French Revolution, ⭐ **'USED THE INDIANS AS MODELS OF HOW SOCIETY MIGHT BE ORGANIZED'**"* — and used this to argue that ⭐⭐ *"**the NATIVE AMERICANS BY THEIR EXAMPLE SOWED THE SEEDS FOR THE LONG-DRAWN-OUT MOVEMENT TOWARDS DEMOCRACY BY THE PEOPLE OF EUROPE**"* *(**We Were Not the Savages**)*.

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This short section contains the sharpest contradiction in the chapter, and it is prime Essay and GS4 material.**
> ⭐ **The USA defined itself against the monarchies of the Old World by its democratic spirit and its inviolable right to property — and both were reserved for white men.** ⭐⭐ **The right to property was made unbreakable by the state at the very moment the state was systematically breaking native property.** **The two facts are not in tension by accident: the guarantee was strong precisely because it applied to a defined class of persons, and natives and slaves were not in it.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Then Daniel Paul's claim turns the story inside out: the very people excluded from the democracy may have supplied the MODEL for it, via Paine.** **If true, the natives contributed the idea and received none of the benefit** — which is as concise a statement of historical injustice as you will find.
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And this is the point where the chapter speaks directly to India.** NCERT sets an activity quoting the American historian **Howard Spodek**: *"**For the indigenous [people] the effects of the American Revolution were EXACTLY OPPOSITE to those of the settlers — EXPANSION BECAME CONTRACTION, DEMOCRACY BECAME TYRANNY, PROSPERITY BECAME POVERTY, AND LIBERTY BECAME CONFINEMENT.**"* ⭐⭐ **That sentence — one event, two exactly inverted meanings depending on who you were — is the single best thing in this chapter to carry into an Essay.**

> ⭐⭐ **NCERT's own comparative table, which is worth reproducing because it puts India in the frame:**
>
> | Group | How they were treated, and the rationalisation |
> |---|---|
> | **Indians under British rule** | *"**Taxed arbitrarily; seen as NOT EQUAL** (rationalisation — **not ready for the responsibility of representative government**)"* |
> | **Natives in America and Australia** | *"**Not seen as citizens; not equal** (rationalisation — **'PRIMITIVE', as in no settled agriculture, provision for the future, towns**)"* |
> | **African slaves in America** | *"**Denied personal liberty; not equal** (rationalisation — **'Slavery is part of their own social system', black people are inferior**)"* |
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three different peoples, three different rationalisations, one identical conclusion — "not equal".** ⭐ **The reason offered changes to fit the case; the verdict never does.** **That tells you the reasons were being produced to justify a decision already taken, and it is exactly the lesson NCERT teaches about colonial sources throughout the series.**

## 8️⃣ The Winds of Change — North America ⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⚠️ "NOT TILL THE 1920s DID THINGS BEGIN TO IMPROVE for the
      native peoples of the USA and Canada."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1928 -- "THE PROBLEM OF INDIAN ADMINISTRATION", a survey
   directed by the social scientist ⭐ LEWIS MERIAM, "painted A
   GRIM PICTURE OF THE TERRIBLY POOR HEALTH AND EDUCATION
   FACILITIES FOR NATIVES IN RESERVATIONS"
   (⭐ published "only a few years before the USA was swept by a
     major economic depression")
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 1934 -- "a landmark law in the USA, THE INDIAN
   REORGANISATION ACT, WHICH GAVE NATIVES IN RESERVATIONS THE
   RIGHT TO BUY LAND AND TAKE LOANS."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ THE 1950s AND 1960s -- "the US and Canadian governments
   thought of ENDING ALL SPECIAL PROVISIONS for the natives in the
   hope that they would ⭐ 'JOIN THE MAINSTREAM', that is, ADOPT
   EUROPEAN CULTURE.
   ⭐⭐ BUT THE NATIVES DID NOT WANT THIS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 1954 -- THE 'DECLARATION OF INDIAN RIGHTS', prepared by
   the natives themselves: "a number of native peoples ACCEPTED
   CITIZENSHIP OF THE USA ⭐ BUT ON CONDITION THAT THEIR
   RESERVATIONS WOULD NOT BE TAKEN AWAY AND THEIR TRADITIONS WOULD
   NOT BE INTERFERED WITH."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ CANADA -- in 1969 the government announced it would
   ⚠️ "NOT RECOGNISE ABORIGINAL RIGHTS". "The natives, in A
   WELL-ORGANISED OPPOSITION MOVE, held A SERIES OF DEMONSTRATIONS
   AND DEBATES."
   ⭐⭐ 1982 -- "THE CONSTITUTION ACT ACCEPTED THE EXISTING
      ABORIGINAL AND TREATY RIGHTS OF THE NATIVES."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The 1954 Declaration is the intellectual heart of this section and maps almost exactly onto Indian debates about Scheduled Tribes, so learn its logic.**
> ⭐ **The government's offer was: give up your special status and become full citizens. The natives' answer was: we will take the citizenship AND keep the reservations and the traditions.** ⭐⭐ **They refused the trade-off between EQUALITY and DIFFERENCE — and the reason is in §6.1: the Cherokees had assimilated more than anyone and were still denied citizenship, so an offer of inclusion conditional on self-erasure had already been tested and found worthless.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Note the word "MAINSTREAM" and the section introduction's own question about it:** *"One might wonder what is meant by 'mainstream'. ⭐ **HOW DOES ECONOMIC AND POLITICAL POWER INFLUENCE THE MAKING OF 'MAINSTREAM CULTURES'?**" ⭐⭐ **A "mainstream" is not a neutral centre; it is whichever culture holds the power to describe itself as the default.** **That question, asked in an NCERT textbook, is directly usable in a GS1 answer on tribal integration or a GS4 answer on cultural rights.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Indian parallel is close enough to be worth stating explicitly:** **the Fifth and Sixth Schedules, the debate between "assimilation" and "isolation" resolved by Nehru's Panchsheel for tribals, and the Forest Rights Act 2006 recognising pre-existing rights rather than granting new ones.** ⭐ **Canada's 1982 Constitution Act did the same thing in the same language — it "ACCEPTED THE EXISTING aboriginal and treaty rights", i.e. recognised rights that were always there rather than conferring them.** **Recognition and conferment are different legal acts, and the difference is the whole argument.**

> ⭐⭐ **NCERT's balanced closing:** *"Today, it is clear that the native peoples of both countries, **though reduced so much in numbers from what they had been in the eighteenth century, HAVE BEEN ABLE TO ASSERT THEIR RIGHT TO THEIR OWN CULTURES and, particularly in Canada, TO THEIR SACRED LANDS, in a way their ancestors could not have done in the 1880s.**"*

## 9️⃣ AUSTRALIA ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ ANTIQUITY -- "the 'ABORIGINES' (a general name given to A
   NUMBER OF DIFFERENT SOCIETIES) began to arrive on the continent
   ⭐ OVER 40,000 YEARS AGO (possibly even earlier). They came from
   NEW GUINEA, WHICH WAS CONNECTED TO AUSTRALIA BY A LAND-BRIDGE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ BUT NOT IN THEIR OWN ACCOUNT
   "IN THE NATIVES' TRADITIONS, THEY DID NOT COME TO AUSTRALIA, BUT
    HAD ALWAYS BEEN THERE. The past centuries were called
    ⭐ 'THE DREAMTIME' -- something DIFFICULT FOR EUROPEANS TO
    UNDERSTAND, SINCE THE DISTINCTION BETWEEN PAST AND PRESENT IS
    BLURRED."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ DIVERSITY -- "In the late eighteenth century, there were
   BETWEEN 350 AND 750 NATIVE COMMUNITIES in Australia, EACH WITH
   ITS OWN LANGUAGE (⭐ EVEN TODAY 200 OF THESE LANGUAGES ARE
   SPOKEN)."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ A SEPARATE GROUP -- THE TORRES STRAIT ISLANDERS, living in
   the north. "The term 'ABORIGINE' IS NOT USED FOR THESE as they
   are believed to have MIGRATED FROM ELSEWHERE and belong to a
   different race."
   ⭐ TOGETHER, "they make up 2.4 PER CENT OF AUSTRALIA'S
     POPULATION IN 2005."
                          |
   ⭐ GEOGRAPHY -- "Australia is SPARSELY POPULATED, and even now
   MOST OF THE TOWNS ARE ALONG THE COAST (where the British first
   arrived in 1770) BECAUSE THE CENTRAL REGION IS ARID DESERT."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Dreamtime is not a quaint belief and you should not treat it as one — it is a rival account of the nature of time, and its political consequences are enormous.** ⭐ **European history is LINEAR: there was a before (arrival) and an after, so occupation has a date and therefore, in principle, a legal origin.** **In the Dreamtime the distinction between past and present is blurred and the people were ALWAYS there** — which means there is no moment of arrival, and therefore no prior claim that could ever have been superior.
> ⭐⭐ **Now see why this mattered in court.** ***Terra nullius*** **rested on the claim that nobody owned the land. The Dreamtime is the strongest possible denial of that claim** — and when the High Court finally overturned *terra nullius* in **1992**, it recognised **native title "from BEFORE 1770"**, i.e. it accepted that occupation preceded and survived the British arrival. ⭐ **A cosmology became a land title.**
> 🔗 **Note also the parallel with the natives of North America believing that *"time moved in cycles"* (§3).** **Both peoples were dispossessed partly because their way of holding the past could not be entered in a European register.**

**⭐⭐ The European arrival:**

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1606** | **Dutch travellers sight Australia** |
| **1642** | **TASMAN** lands on the island later named **TASMANIA** |
| ⭐⭐ **1770** | **JAMES COOK reaches BOTANY BAY**, named **NEW SOUTH WALES** |
| ⭐⭐ **1788** | **British PENAL COLONY formed. SYDNEY founded** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The friendliness, and the reversal — note how thin the justification was:** *"The story… has **many points of similarity to the story of the Americas, THOUGH IT BEGAN NEARLY 300 YEARS LATER.** Initial reports from **Captain Cook and his crew** about encounters with natives are **ENTHUSIASTIC ABOUT THEIR FRIENDLINESS.** ⚠️ **There was a SHARP REVERSAL of feeling on the part of the British when COOK WAS KILLED BY A NATIVE — NOT IN AUSTRALIA, BUT IN HAWAII.** ⭐ **As often happened, A SINGLE INCIDENT OF THIS NATURE WAS USED BY COLONISERS TO JUSTIFY SUBSEQUENT ACTS OF VIOLENCE TOWARDS OTHER PEOPLE.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Cook was killed in HAWAII and Australians paid for it.** ⭐ **The violence was not retaliation — it was a licence, and the licence was issued on the strength of an event on another archipelago involving different people.** **Keep this as the cleanest example of how a single incident is converted into a general warrant.**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **What the settlement looked like from the other side — a description of the Sydney area in 1790, from *Creating a Nation*:**
> *"**Aboriginal production had been dramatically disturbed by the British presence. THE ARRIVAL OF A THOUSAND HUNGRY MOUTHS, followed by hundreds more, PUT UNPRECEDENTED PRESSURE ON LOCAL FOOD RESOURCES.**
> So what would the **DARUK** people have thought of all this? To them **such large-scale destruction of sacred places and strange, violent behaviour towards their land was INEXPLICABLE. ⭐ The newcomers seemed to KNOCK DOWN TREES WITHOUT ANY REASON, for they were NOT MAKING CANOES, GATHERING BUSH HONEY OR CATCHING ANIMALS.** Stones were moved and stacked together, clay dug up, shaped and cooked, holes were made in the ground, large unwieldy structures built. **At first they may have equated the clearing with THE CREATION OF A SACRED CEREMONIAL GROUND… Perhaps they thought a huge ritual gathering was to be held, DANGEROUS BUSINESS from which they should steer well clear.** ⚠️ **There is no doubt the Daruks subsequently AVOIDED the settlement, for THE ONLY WAY TO BRING THEM BACK WAS BY AN OFFICIAL KIDNAPPING.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is a brilliant piece of historical imagination and shows you how to write about a people who left no written record.** ⭐ **The historians reason from what the Daruk would have been able to SEE: men felling trees for no purpose they could recognise, since no canoe, honey or animal resulted.** **Purposeless destruction is unintelligible, so they reached for the only category that fitted — ritual.** ⭐⭐ **And the last line is devastating in its administrative flatness: the settlement's response to being avoided was "an OFFICIAL kidnapping."**

**⚠️⭐⭐⭐ The demographic catastrophe, and who the settlers were:**

> *"They did not foresee that in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries ⭐⭐ **NEARLY 90 PER CENT OF THEM WOULD DIE — by EXPOSURE TO GERMS, by THE LOSS OF THEIR LANDS AND RESOURCES, and IN BATTLES AGAINST THE SETTLERS.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐ **The convict settlement:** *"The experiment of settling **BRAZIL with PORTUGUESE CONVICTS** had been **abandoned when their violent behaviour provoked angry reprisals from the natives.** The British had adopted **the same practice in the AMERICAN COLONIES until they became independent. THEN THEY CONTINUED IT IN AUSTRALIA.** ⭐ Most of the early settlers were **CONVICTS DEPORTED FROM ENGLAND** who, when their jail term ended, **were allowed to live as free people in Australia ON CONDITION THAT THEY DID NOT RETURN TO BRITAIN.** ⚠️ **With no recourse but to make a life for themselves in this land so different from their own, THEY FELT NO HESITATION ABOUT EJECTING NATIVES FROM LAND THEY TOOK OVER FOR CULTIVATION.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **Note the causal claim NCERT makes, and note that it explains without excusing.** ⭐ **The early settlers were themselves people the British state had discarded — convicts forbidden to return home.** **People with no way back and nothing to lose made the most ruthless dispossessors**, because the land they took was the only life available to them. 🔗 **It is the same finding as the American settlers being Europe's dispossessed younger sons (§6): settler colonialism was very often carried out by the losers of the metropolitan social order, at the expense of people who had nothing to do with it.**

**⭐⭐ The development of Australia:**

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1850** | **Self-government** granted to Australian colonies |
| ⭐ **1851** | **Chinese coolie immigration** — **stopped by law in 1855** |
| **1851–1961** | **Gold rushes** |
| ⭐⭐ **1901** | **Formation of the FEDERATION OF AUSTRALIA, with SIX STATES** |
| ⭐⭐ **1911** | **CANBERRA established as capital** |
| **1948–75** | **Two million Europeans migrate to Australia** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The economy and the name — a detail that is both funny and exact:** *"The economic development of Australia under European settlement was **NOT AS VARIED AS IN AMERICA. VAST SHEEP FARMS AND MINING STATIONS** were established, followed by **VINEYARDS AND WHEAT FARMING.** These came to form the basis of the country's prosperity. When the states were united and a new capital was to be built in **1911**, ⭐⭐ **ONE NAME SUGGESTED FOR IT WAS *WOOLWHEATGOLD*! Ultimately it was called CANBERRA (= *kamberra*, A NATIVE WORD MEANING 'MEETING PLACE').**"*
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ ***Woolwheatgold* would have named the capital after three commodities. They chose a native word instead — and the word means "meeting place".** ⭐ **A settler nation named its capital in the language of the people it had displaced, and picked the word for coming together.** **That irony is worth one sentence in an essay and no more, but it is worth that one.**

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Labour and the colour bar:** *"**Some natives were employed in farms, UNDER CONDITIONS OF WORK SO HARSH THAT IT WAS LITTLE DIFFERENT FROM SLAVERY.** Later, **CHINESE IMMIGRANTS provided cheap labour, AS IN CALIFORNIA**, but **unease about being dependent on non-whites led the governments in BOTH countries TO BAN CHINESE IMMIGRANTS.** ⚠️ **Till 1974, such was the popular fear that 'DARK' PEOPLE FROM SOUTH ASIA OR SOUTHEAST ASIA MIGHT MIGRATE to Australia in large numbers that there was A GOVERNMENT POLICY TO KEEP 'NON-WHITE' PEOPLE OUT.**"*
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **The "White Australia" policy lasted until 1974 — within living memory, and well after Indian independence.** ⭐ **Note the sequence, which recurs in every settler economy: import cheap non-white labour when it is needed, then legislate against it when its presence becomes uncomfortable.** 🔗 *(The Chinese built the American and Canadian transcontinental railways in §7 and were then excluded by both countries. Class 10 Ch.3's indentured labour is the same story with Indian workers.)*

## 🔟 The Winds of Change — Australia ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 1968 -- "people were ELECTRIFIED by a lecture by the
   anthropologist W.E.H. STANNER, entitled
   ⭐⭐ 'THE GREAT AUSTRALIAN SILENCE' -- THE SILENCE OF HISTORIANS
   ABOUT THE ABORIGINES."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ FROM THE 1970s -- "as was happening in North America,
   there was an eagerness to understand natives ⭐ NOT AS
   ANTHROPOLOGICAL CURIOSITIES BUT AS COMMUNITIES WITH DISTINCT
   CULTURES, unique ways of understanding nature and climate, with
   a sense of community which had VAST BODIES OF STORIES, TEXTILE
   AND PAINTING AND CARVING SKILLS, WHICH SHOULD BE UNDERSTOOD AND
   RECORDED AND RESPECTED."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ HENRY REYNOLDS'S BOOK -- ⭐ "WHY WEREN'T WE TOLD?"
   "This CONDEMNED THE PRACTICE OF WRITING AUSTRALIAN HISTORY AS
    THOUGH IT HAD BEGUN WITH CAPTAIN COOK'S 'DISCOVERY'."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ WHAT FOLLOWED -- university departments to study native
   cultures; galleries of native art; museums enlarged with
   dioramas; ⭐ "natives have begun WRITING THEIR OWN LIFE
   HISTORIES."
   ⭐⭐ AND WHY THE TIMING MATTERED: "It has also occurred AT A
      CRITICAL TIME, because IF NATIVE CULTURES HAD REMAINED
      IGNORED, BY THIS TIME MUCH OF SUCH CULTURES WOULD HAVE BEEN
      FORGOTTEN."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ FROM 1974 -- "'MULTICULTURALISM' has been OFFICIAL POLICY
   in Australia, which gave EQUAL RESPECT TO NATIVE CULTURES AND TO
   THE DIFFERENT CULTURES OF THE IMMIGRANTS FROM EUROPE AND ASIA."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The Great Australian Silence" and *Why Weren't We Told?* together answer NCERT's exercise question, and they answer it at two levels.**
> ⭐ **Stanner named the silence** — the point being that the omission was so complete that it had become invisible, and had to be pointed at before anyone could see it. ⭐⭐ **Reynolds named the mechanism: history was written as though it BEGAN with Cook's "discovery" in 1770.** **If your chronology starts at the moment of arrival, then everything before it is prehistory and everybody in it is scenery.** ⭐ **The exclusion was achieved not by lying about the aborigines but by choosing where to start the story.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Note also the title's grammar: *Why Weren't WE Told?* — addressed by white Australians to their own education.** **The recovery of a suppressed history began as a complaint by the beneficiaries about having been deceived**, which is an honest and slightly uncomfortable observation about how such reckonings usually start.

> ⭐⭐ **JUDITH WRIGHT (1915–2000)**, an Australian writer and *"a champion of the rights of the Australian aborigines"*, wrote *"many moving poems about **the loss created by keeping the white people and the natives apart**."* From ***'Two Dreamtimes'***, written for **Oodgeroo Noonuccal**:
> *"Kathy my sister with the torn heart, / I don't know how to thank you / For your dreamtime stories of joy and grief / Written on paperbark. / ⭐ **You were one of the dark children / I wasn't allowed to play with — / Riverbank campers, the wrong colour / (I couldn't turn you white.) / So it was late I met you, / Late I began to know / THEY HADN'T TOLD ME THE LAND I LOVED / WAS TAKEN OUT OF YOUR HANDS.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***Terra nullius* is the most important single concept in this chapter and the one most likely to be examinable, so be precise about what it did.**
> ⭐ **It was not a claim that the land was empty of people — everyone could see it was not. It was a claim that the land BELONGED TO NOBODY**, because its occupants had no agriculture, no permanent towns and no system of ownership a European court would recognise. ⭐⭐ **A legal doctrine converted a different way of relating to land into an absence of any right to it.** **And because nobody owned it, nobody had to be treated with — which is exactly why Australia, alone among these countries, has NO TREATIES.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **That is the sharpest finding in the chapter: the absence of treaties is not an oversight but the LOGICAL CONSEQUENCE of the doctrine.** You do not sign an agreement with people you have defined as having nothing to give. **In the USA, Canada and New Zealand, treaties existed and were repeatedly broken — which was bad but left a legal record to litigate.** ⚠️ **Australia had nothing to break, and therefore nothing to enforce.** *(Which is why Mabo in 1992 had to attack the doctrine itself, and why it recognised title from BEFORE 1770 — it had to establish that there had been something there to take.)*
> ⭐⭐ **And note the modest, honest formula for redress: *"while past acts could not be undone, there should be a PUBLIC APOLOGY."*** **An apology is not compensation and NCERT does not pretend otherwise** — but a National Sorry Day for children taken from the 1820s to the 1970s is a state formally agreeing on the historical record. ⭐ **For GS4, that is a usable point: where restitution is impossible, official acknowledgement is not nothing, because the first injury of such policies is the denial that they happened.**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE VARIANTS OF IMPERIALISM: settler colonies** (America, Australia, NZ, South Africa — the population is replaced); **direct imperial control** (British India — the population is governed and taxed); and **the semi-colony** (China — Britain, France, Germany, Russia, America and Japan **meddled without taking state power**, reducing it to a semi-colony).
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CIVIC vs ETHNIC NATIONALISM.** All nationalisms are **doctrines of popular sovereignty**, which is what makes nationalism **modern**. **Civic** vests sovereignty in **all people regardless of language, ethnicity, religion or gender**, defining nationhood by **citizenship**; **ethnic/religious** builds solidarity around **a language, religion or tradition** and may **limit sovereignty to a chosen people**. **Most western countries are civic; GERMANY is the prominent exception, from the reaction to French occupation in 1806.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **Modernisation rests on TWO developments: the Industrial Revolution, and political revolutions that "transformed SUBJECTS INTO CITIZENS" — the American (1776-81) and French (1789-94).** ⚠️ **There is no single industrial model: Britain began with coal and cotton then railways; RUSSIA began with railways and heavy industry.**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The natives came from ASIA over 30,000 years ago across the BERING land-bridge**; the oldest artefact is an **11,000-year-old arrow-point**. They lived **in bands along river valleys**, hunted **bison** (riding horses **bought from the Spanish** from the 17th century), and ⭐ **"only killed as many animals as they needed."**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **No surplus → no kingdoms.** *"They did not produce a surplus, [so] they did not develop kingdoms and empires as in Central and South America."* **Control of land was not an issue; goods came as GIFTS, not purchases**; **wampum belts** sealed treaties; and ⭐ **"they could READ THE LAND the way literate people read written texts."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **Europeans defined "civilised" by LITERACY, ORGANISED RELIGION and URBANISM** — by which test the natives failed, though they possessed a complete body of transmitted environmental knowledge.
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Four verdicts: ROUSSEAU** (the "noble savage", admired) and **WORDSWORTH** (limited in imagination) — ⚠️ **neither had met one**; **WASHINGTON IRVING**, who had, found them **reserved only around distrusted strangers and "great mimics" among themselves**; and **JEFFERSON**, who wrote that they **"have justified extermination."**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GIFT vs COMMODITY.** To the natives the exchange was **friendship**; to Europeans, **furs and fish were commodities for profit**. **Prices swung with a European market the natives could not see**, and they were **saddened by the slaughter of hundreds of beavers**, fearing the animals' revenge. ⚠️ **Alcohol was new to them; addiction "SUITED the Europeans, because it enabled them to dictate terms of trade."**
9. ⭐⭐⭐ **Settlers followed traders** — religious refugees first, then **younger sons denied inheritance**, **Germans, Swedes and Italians squeezed out by big farmers**, and **Poles drawn to prairies like their steppes.** They introduced **rice and cotton**, hunted **wolves and mountain lions to extinction**, and felt secure only after **barbed wire (1873)**.
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The USA grew by PURCHASE (Louisiana from France 1803, Alaska from Russia) and WAR (southern USA from Mexico)** — and ⭐ **"it did not occur to anyone that the consent of natives living in these areas should have been asked."** The **frontier was a shifting line**, and the natives moved back with it.
11. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE CHEROKEE CASE.** Georgia held them **subject to state law but denied the rights of citizens**, though ⭐ **of all native peoples they had made the most effort to learn English and American ways.** **In 1832 Chief Justice JOHN MARSHALL ruled they were "a distinct community… in which the laws of Georgia had no force."** ⚠️ **President ANDREW JACKSON refused to honour it and sent the army; of 15,000 driven out, OVER A QUARTER died on the TRAIL OF TEARS.**
12. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **The justifications:** the natives **"did not deserve to occupy land which they did not use to the maximum"**, were **lazy** for not producing for the market, would not learn English or dress "correctly", and **"deserved to die out."** ⭐ **Anthropology, introduced from the 1840s, supplied the prophecy that they would "die out" anyway** — *"primitive man will disappear with the primitive animal."*
13. ⭐⭐⭐ **Reservations** were **often land they had no earlier connection with**; grants **"theirs in perpetuity"** were revoked **when lead, gold or oil appeared.** Rebellions were **crushed from 1865 to 1890**, and the **METIS revolted in Canada 1869–85**; *"but after that they gave up."*
14. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CHIEF SEATTLE, 1854:** *"How can you buy or sell the sky…? **If you do not own the freshness of the air and the sparkle of the water, how can one buy them?**… The shining water… **is not just water but the blood of our ancestors.**"*
15. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Gold Rush (California, 1840s) built the railways** — **thousands of CHINESE workers**, **USA complete by 1870, Canada by 1885.** ⭐ **American industry arose NOT from dispossessed peasants as in England but to make RAILWAY EQUIPMENT and FARM MACHINERY.** ⭐⭐ ***"In 1860 the USA had been an undeveloped economy. In 1890 it was the leading industrial power in the world."***
16. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **By 1890 the BISON were almost exterminated, ending the natives' hunting economy; by 1892 the FRONTIER was closed** — and **within a few years the USA took HAWAII and the PHILIPPINES**, becoming an imperial power.
17. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The contradiction:** the USA defined itself by the **"democratic spirit"** and an **inviolable right to property** — ⚠️ **both "ONLY FOR WHITE MEN."** ⭐ **Daniel Paul noted in 2000 that Thomas Paine "used the Indians as models of how society might be organized."** **Spodek:** *"expansion became contraction, democracy became tyranny, prosperity became poverty, and liberty became confinement."*
18. ⭐⭐⭐ **The winds of change in North America:** the **MERIAM survey, *The Problem of Indian Administration* (1928)**; the **INDIAN REORGANISATION ACT (1934)** giving the right to **buy land and take loans**; the **1950s–60s push to "join the mainstream"**, rejected; the **1954 DECLARATION OF INDIAN RIGHTS** accepting citizenship **only if reservations and traditions were untouched**; and **Canada's 1982 CONSTITUTION ACT accepting existing aboriginal and treaty rights** after its **1969 refusal**.
19. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AUSTRALIA: aborigines arrived over 40,000 years ago from New Guinea** — though **in their traditions they had ALWAYS been there, in the DREAMTIME**, where past and present blur. **350–750 communities, each with a language; about 200 survive.** With the **TORRES STRAIT ISLANDERS** (a separate group), **2.4% of the population in 2005.**
20. ⭐⭐⭐ **1606 Dutch sighting · 1642 Tasman · 1770 Cook at BOTANY BAY · 1788 penal colony, SYDNEY founded.** Cook's crew found the natives **friendly**; the reversal came when **Cook was killed IN HAWAII** — ⭐ *"a single incident… used by colonisers to justify subsequent acts of violence towards other people."*
21. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Nearly 90 PER CENT of the aborigines died** — from **germs, loss of land and resources, and battles with settlers.** Most early settlers were **CONVICTS deported from England**, free at the end of their terms **on condition they never returned to Britain**.
22. ⭐⭐⭐ **Australia's economy was narrower than America's — sheep, mining, then vineyards and wheat.** Federation **1901** (six states); **Canberra** as capital **1911**, from ***kamberra*, "meeting place"** — a suggested alternative being ***Woolwheatgold***. **Chinese labour was imported and then banned**, and a policy to keep **"non-white" people out lasted till 1974**.
23. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **W.E.H. STANNER's 1968 lecture "THE GREAT AUSTRALIAN SILENCE"** named the omission; **HENRY REYNOLDS's *WHY WEREN'T WE TOLD?*** condemned **writing Australian history as though it began with Cook's "discovery."** **From 1974 MULTICULTURALISM became official policy.**
24. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***TERRA NULLIUS* — "belonging to nobody" — is why Australia, ALONE among the USA, Canada and New Zealand, had NO TREATIES with the natives.** ⭐ **The MABO case (1992) declared it legally invalid and recognised claims from BEFORE 1770.** **The 1995 National Enquiry** examined the removal of children, and **26 MAY 1999 was "A NATIONAL SORRY DAY"** for the children lost **from the 1820s to the 1970s** — *"while past acts could not be undone, there should be a public apology."*

4. North American natives did not develop kingdoms and empires because — ***they did not produce an agricultural surplus***, *unlike the peoples of Central and South America.*

5. Wampum belts were — ***belts of coloured shells sewn together, exchanged by native tribes after a treaty was agreed to.***

6. Of Rousseau, Wordsworth and Washington Irving, the one who had actually met native Americans was — ***Washington Irving***, *who found them reserved only in the company of distrusted white men, and "great mimics" among themselves.*

7. Alcohol was significant in the fur trade because — ***native addiction to it enabled the Europeans to dictate terms of trade.***

8. In 1832, US Chief Justice John Marshall ruled that the Cherokees were — ***"a distinct community, occupying its own territory in which the laws of Georgia had no force"***; *President **Andrew Jackson refused to honour the judgment**.*

9. Of the 15,000 Cherokees driven along the Trail of Tears — ***over a quarter died.***

10. Consider: (i) the Louisiana territory was bought from France; (ii) Alaska was bought from Russia; (iii) much of southern USA was won by war from Mexico. — ***All three.***

11. Barbed wire, which finally made settlers feel secure on the plains, was invented in — ***1873.***

12. The bison were almost exterminated by — ***1890***, *and the American frontier was declared closed in **1892**, after which the USA took **Hawaii and the Philippines**.*

13. In 1860 the USA was an undeveloped economy; by 1890 it was — ***the leading industrial power in the world.***

14. In the nineteenth-century USA, democratic rights and the right to property were available to — ***white men only.***

15. The Indian Reorganisation Act of 1934 gave natives in reservations — ***the right to buy land and take loans***, *following the **1928 Meriam survey, The Problem of Indian Administration**.*

16. In the 1954 Declaration of Indian Rights, native peoples accepted US citizenship on condition that — ***their reservations would not be taken away and their traditions would not be interfered with.***

17. Canada accepted existing aboriginal and treaty rights in — ***the Constitution Act of 1982***, *having announced in **1969** that it would **not** recognise aboriginal rights.*

18. Australian aborigines are believed to have arrived — ***over 40,000 years ago, from New Guinea across a land-bridge***; *in their own traditions they **had always been there**, in the **Dreamtime**.*

19. Captain Cook was killed — ***in Hawaii***, *though the incident was used to justify violence against natives **in Australia**.*

20. Consider: (i) Cook reached Botany Bay in 1770; (ii) the British penal colony and Sydney were founded in 1788; (iii) Tasman landed on Tasmania in 1642. — ***All three.***

21. The doctrine that Australian land belonged to nobody was — ***terra nullius***, *which is why Australia, unlike the USA, Canada and New Zealand, **had no treaties with the natives**.*

22. The Mabo case, which declared terra nullius legally invalid, was decided in — ***1992***, *recognising native claims to land **from before 1770**.*

23. "A National Sorry Day", apologising for the children removed from the 1820s to the 1970s, was held on — ***26 May 1999***; *the **National Enquiry** into their separation was **1995**, and the **"White Australia" policy ended in 1974**.*
