## 1️⃣ What changed, and who named it ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE OPENING SUMMARY -- five changes, and they structure
      the whole chapter
   "FROM THE FOURTEENTH TO THE END OF THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY"

   1. TOWNS GREW, and "a distinct ⭐ URBAN CULTURE also developed.
      Townspeople began to think of themselves as MORE 'CIVILISED'
      THAN RURAL PEOPLE." FLORENCE, VENICE and ROME became centres
      of art and learning; artists and writers were PATRONISED BY
      THE RICH AND THE ARISTOCRATIC.
                          |
   2. ⭐ PRINTING "made books and prints available to many people,
      INCLUDING THOSE LIVING IN DISTANT TOWNS OR COUNTRIES."
                          |
   3. ⭐⭐ A SENSE OF HISTORY DEVELOPED -- "people CONTRASTED THEIR
      'MODERN' WORLD WITH THE 'ANCIENT' ONE OF THE GREEKS AND
      ROMANS."
                          |
   4. ⭐⭐ RELIGION BECAME A CHOICE -- "Religion came to be seen as
      something WHICH EACH INDIVIDUAL SHOULD CHOOSE FOR HIMSELF."
                          |
   5. ⭐⭐⭐ TWO CENTRES WERE OVERTURNED
      "The church's EARTH-CENTRIC belief was overturned by
       scientists who began to understand the solar system, and
       new geographical knowledge overturned the ⭐ EUROPE-CENTRIC
       view that THE MEDITERRANEAN SEA WAS THE CENTRE OF THE
       WORLD."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Point 5 is the most elegant thing in the chapter and the best possible opening for an essay on this period.** ⭐ **Two centres were displaced in the same century — the EARTH from the centre of the universe, and EUROPE from the centre of the world.** **Astronomy dethroned one and navigation dethroned the other**, and both taught the same lesson: **the place you are standing is not the middle of anything.** ⭐⭐ **That double decentring is what "modern" means in this chapter.**

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Who invented the word "Renaissance" — and this is a historiography question, which UPSC likes:**

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ "FROM THE NINETEENTH CENTURY, historians used the term
   ⭐ 'RENAISSANCE' (LITERALLY, REBIRTH) to describe the cultural
   changes of this period."
   ⚠️ NOTE THE DATE: the people who lived through it did NOT call
      it that. The name was applied 400 YEARS LATER.
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ JACOB BURCKHARDT (1818-97) of the University of BASLE,
   Switzerland -- "the historian who emphasised these most"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ HIS TEACHER: LEOPOLD VON RANKE (1795-1886), who "had
   taught him that THE PRIMARY CONCERN OF THE HISTORIAN WAS TO
   WRITE ABOUT STATES AND POLITICS USING PAPERS AND FILES OF
   GOVERNMENT DEPARTMENTS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE BREAK -- "Burckhardt was DISSATISFIED with these very
   limited goals... To him POLITICS WAS NOT THE BE-ALL AND END-ALL
   in history writing. ⭐ HISTORY WAS AS MUCH CONCERNED WITH
   CULTURE AS WITH POLITICS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ 1860 -- THE CIVILISATION OF THE RENAISSANCE IN ITALY,
   which called readers' attention to LITERATURE, ARCHITECTURE AND
   PAINTING to tell how "a new ⭐ 'HUMANIST' CULTURE had flowered in
   Italian towns from the fourteenth to the seventeenth century."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ HIS THESIS -- learn this sentence
   "This culture was characterised by A NEW BELIEF -- THAT MAN, AS
    AN INDIVIDUAL, WAS CAPABLE OF MAKING HIS OWN DECISIONS AND
    DEVELOPING HIS SKILLS. He was 'MODERN', in contrast to
    'MEDIEVAL' MAN WHOSE THINKING HAD BEEN CONTROLLED BY THE
    CHURCH."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Burckhardt matters twice over, and both are examinable.**
> ⭐ **(1) As a METHOD.** Ranke had defined history as **states and politics, written from government files** — the archival, official-document school. **Burckhardt insisted that CULTURE was equally history**, and wrote a masterpiece out of paintings and buildings. ⭐⭐ **Everything you have studied about print culture, novels, cities, clothing and leisure descends from that argument.** *(Class 10 Ch.5, and the whole supplement, are Burckhardt's children.)*
> ⭐ **(2) As a THESIS to be tested.** He claimed a sharp break: modern individual against medieval man controlled by the Church. ⚠️ **NCERT spends its FINAL SECTION dismantling that claim** — which is unusual and deliberate. **A textbook that ends by questioning its own chapter title is teaching you that periodisation is an argument, not a fact.**

## 2️⃣ The Revival of Italian Cities ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **NCERT sets this as an exercise — *"Why were Italian towns the first to experience the ideas of humanism?"* — so build the answer as you read.**

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE STARTING CONDITION -- WEAKNESS
   "After the fall of the western Roman Empire, many of the towns
    that had been political and cultural centres in Italy FELL INTO
    RUIN. There was ⭐ NO UNIFIED GOVERNMENT, and the POPE in Rome,
    who was sovereign in his own state, WAS NOT A STRONG POLITICAL
    FIGURE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND THE PARADOX NCERT DRAWS -- this IS the answer
   "While WESTERN EUROPE was being reshaped by FEUDAL BONDS and
    unified under the LATIN CHURCH, and EASTERN EUROPE under the
    BYZANTINE EMPIRE, and ISLAM was creating a common civilisation
    further west, ⭐ ITALY WAS WEAK AND FRAGMENTED.
    ⭐⭐ HOWEVER, IT WAS THESE VERY DEVELOPMENTS THAT HELPED IN THE
    REVIVAL OF ITALIAN CULTURE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ HOW TRADE REVIVED THE PORTS
   "With the expansion of trade between THE BYZANTINE EMPIRE AND
    THE ISLAMIC COUNTRIES, the ports on the Italian coast revived.
    ⭐ FROM THE TWELFTH CENTURY, as THE MONGOLS OPENED UP TRADE
    WITH CHINA VIA THE SILK ROUTE, and as trade with western
    European countries also increased, ITALIAN TOWNS PLAYED A
    CENTRAL ROLE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE POLITICAL FORM THAT RESULTED
   "They no longer saw themselves as part of a powerful empire, but
    as ⭐ INDEPENDENT CITY-STATES."
      - FLORENCE and VENICE were ⭐ REPUBLICS
      - "many others were COURT-CITIES, RULED BY PRINCES"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ WHY THIS MATTERED -- VENICE and GENOA
   "They were DIFFERENT FROM OTHER PARTS OF EUROPE --
      ⭐ THE CLERGY WERE NOT POLITICALLY DOMINANT HERE,
      ⭐ NOR WERE THERE POWERFUL FEUDAL LORDS.
    RICH MERCHANTS AND BANKERS ACTIVELY PARTICIPATED IN GOVERNING
    THE CITY, and ⭐⭐ THIS HELPED THE IDEA OF CITIZENSHIP TO STRIKE
    ROOT. Even when these towns were ruled by MILITARY DESPOTS, THE
    PRIDE FELT BY THE TOWNSPEOPLE IN BEING CITIZENS DID NOT
    WEAKEN."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the answer to NCERT's question, and it is a genuinely counter-intuitive one worth stating boldly: ITALY GOT HUMANISM BECAUSE IT WAS POLITICALLY WEAK.**
> ⭐ **Everywhere else in Europe, one of two powers filled the space — the FEUDAL LORD or the CHURCH.** In the Italian city-states **neither did**: no unified monarchy, a politically feeble papacy, no dominant clergy, no powerful feudal nobility. ⭐⭐ **Into that vacuum stepped MERCHANTS AND BANKERS, who governed the cities themselves — and men who govern themselves need an education fit for public life, not for the cloister.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **That is why the humanist curriculum is what it is** (§3): **grammar, rhetoric, poetry, history and moral philosophy** — *"skills developed by individuals through discussion and debate"*. **These are the skills of a citizen arguing in a council, not a monk copying a manuscript.** ⭐ **The syllabus follows the constitution.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐ **And note the trade dependency: Italian ports revived on Byzantine and Islamic trade, and on the Mongol opening of the Silk Route to China.** **The Renaissance was financed by Asian commerce** — which is exactly the point NCERT makes in its final section (§10), and it is worth planting the seed here.

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **But do not romanticise "citizenship" — NCERT prints the evidence against it.** **Cardinal Gasparo Contarini (1483–1542)**, in ***The Commonwealth and Government of Venice* (1534)**, describes the *"democratic government"* of his city-state: *"the whole authority of the city… is in that council, into which **ALL THE GENTLEMEN of the City being once past the age of 25 years are admitted**…"* — and then explains, at length, ⚠️ *"how and with what wisdom it was ordained by our ancestors, that **THE COMMON PEOPLE SHOULD NOT BE ADMITTED INTO THIS COMPANY OF CITIZENS**… **Because many troubles and popular tumults arise in those cities, whose government is swayed by the common people.**"* The rule was to go **"by the NOBILITY OF LINEAGE, rather than by the estimation of WEALTH"**, though extended to *"every other citizen whosoever **not ignobly born**: so that all which were **noble by birth, or ENNOBLED BY VIRTUE**, did… obtain this right of government."*
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read that carefully and the famous Venetian "republic" turns out to be an oligarchy of adult noble males over 25, defending the exclusion of the common people in writing.** ⭐ **"Citizenship" in Renaissance Italy meant participation by a propertied, well-born minority — exactly what "liberalism" meant in Class 10 Ch.1, three centuries later.** ⭐⭐ **The idea of the citizen is genuinely born here; the idea that everyone is one is not.** *(NCERT's exercise asks you to compare the Venetian idea of good government with contemporary France — the point being that Venice is more participatory than absolutist France AND still deliberately exclusive.)*

## 3️⃣ Universities and Humanism ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE EARLIEST UNIVERSITIES in Europe were in ITALIAN TOWNS.
   ⭐ PADUA and BOLOGNA had been centres of LEGAL STUDIES FROM THE
     ELEVENTH CENTURY.
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AND WHY LAW -- commerce
   "Commerce being the chief activity in the city, there was AN
    INCREASING DEMAND FOR LAWYERS AND NOTARIES (⭐ a combination of
    SOLICITOR AND RECORD-KEEPER) to write and interpret RULES AND
    WRITTEN AGREEMENTS, WITHOUT WHICH TRADE ON A LARGE SCALE WAS
    NOT POSSIBLE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE SHIFT -- "Law was therefore a popular subject of
   study, BUT THERE WAS NOW A SHIFT IN EMPHASIS. ⭐ IT WAS STUDIED
   IN THE CONTEXT OF EARLIER ROMAN CULTURE."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ FRANCESCO PETRARCH (1304-78) "REPRESENTED THIS CHANGE."
   "To Petrarch, ⭐ ANTIQUITY WAS A DISTINCTIVE CIVILISATION WHICH
    COULD BE BEST UNDERSTOOD THROUGH THE ACTUAL WORDS OF THE
    ANCIENT GREEKS AND ROMANS. He therefore stressed THE
    IMPORTANCE OF A CLOSE READING OF ANCIENT AUTHORS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND WHAT THAT IMPLIED -- the birth of humanism
   "This educational programme implied that ⭐ THERE WAS MUCH TO BE
    LEARNT WHICH RELIGIOUS TEACHING ALONE COULD NOT GIVE. This was
    the culture which historians in the nineteenth century were to
    label 'HUMANISM'."
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The definition — reproduce it precisely, this is a guaranteed factual item:**

> *"By the **early fifteenth century**, the term **'HUMANIST' was used for masters who taught ⭐ GRAMMAR, RHETORIC, POETRY, HISTORY and MORAL PHILOSOPHY.** The Latin word ⭐⭐ ***HUMANITAS*, from which 'HUMANITIES' was derived, had been used many centuries ago by the Roman lawyer and essayist CICERO (106–43 BCE), a contemporary of JULIUS CAESAR, TO MEAN CULTURE.** ⭐⭐⭐ **These subjects were NOT DRAWN FROM OR CONNECTED WITH RELIGION, and emphasised SKILLS DEVELOPED BY INDIVIDUALS THROUGH DISCUSSION AND DEBATE.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **On debate — Giovanni Pico della Mirandola (1463–94), a humanist of Florence, in *On the Dignity of Man* (1486):**
> *"For [**Plato and Aristotle**] it was certain that, for the attainment of the knowledge of truth… **nothing is better than to attend as often as possible THE EXERCISE OF DEBATE.** ⭐ **For just as BODILY ENERGY IS STRENGTHENED BY GYMNASTIC EXERCISE, so beyond doubt in this WRESTLING-PLACE OF LETTERS, as it were, ENERGY OF MIND BECOMES FAR STRONGER AND MORE VIGOROUS.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note precisely what humanism was NOT: it was not atheism.** ⭐ **NCERT's formulation is that these subjects were *"not drawn from or connected with religion"* — i.e. humanism carved out a SPHERE OF KNOWLEDGE INDEPENDENT OF THEOLOGY, without denying theology.** *(§7 will say explicitly that the Italians *"were not necessarily irreligious"*.)* ⭐⭐ **The revolutionary claim is not "there is no God" but "there is much to be learnt which religious teaching alone could not give."** **A boundary was drawn, and everything modern followed from the drawing of it.**
> ⭐ **And Pico's image of the "wrestling-place of letters" tells you the pedagogy: knowledge produced by ARGUMENT between people, not received from authority.** 🔗 *(Set it against Theme 7's Chinese examination system, where candidates wrote an "eight-legged essay" in a prescribed form — a system that tested conformity to a template. Two ideas of what an educated man is.)*

> ⭐⭐ **Florence, and the "Renaissance Man":** the city *"till the end of the thirteenth century… **had not made a mark as a centre of trade or of learning**, but things changed dramatically in the fifteenth century."* ⭐ *"A city is known by its **great citizens** as much as by its wealth"* — and Florence was known for **DANTE ALIGHIERI (1265–1321)**, *"a **LAYMAN who wrote on religious themes**"*, and **GIOTTO (1267–1337)**, *"an artist who painted **LIFELIKE PORTRAITS, very different from the STIFF FIGURES done by earlier artists**."* ⭐⭐ **The term "RENAISSANCE MAN" describes *"a person with MANY INTERESTS AND SKILLS"* — many of these figures were *"SCHOLAR-DIPLOMAT-THEOLOGIAN-ARTIST combined in one."***
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **A LAYMAN writing on religious themes is the whole shift in three words.** **Religion had stopped being the monopoly of the clergy** — which is the same claim Luther makes politically in §8, two centuries later.

## 4️⃣ The Humanist View of History ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT THE HUMANISTS BELIEVED ABOUT THEMSELVES
   "Humanists thought that THEY WERE RESTORING 'TRUE CIVILISATION'
    AFTER CENTURIES OF DARKNESS, for they believed that a ⭐ 'DARK
    AGE' HAD SET IN AFTER THE COLLAPSE OF THE ROMAN EMPIRE."
   ⚠️ "Following them, later scholars UNQUESTIONINGLY ASSUMED that
      a 'NEW AGE' had begun in Europe FROM THE FOURTEENTH CENTURY."
                          |
   ⭐⭐ "The term 'MIDDLE AGES'/'MEDIEVAL PERIOD' was used for THE
   MILLENNIUM after the fall of Rome. In the 'Middle Ages', they
   argued, ⭐ THE CHURCH HAD HAD SUCH COMPLETE CONTROL OVER MEN'S
   MINDS THAT ALL THE LEARNING OF THE GREEKS AND ROMANS HAD BEEN
   BLOTTED OUT. The humanists used the word 'MODERN' for the period
   from the FIFTEENTH CENTURY."
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The periodisation — a clean Prelims table:**

| Period | Label |
|---|---|
| **5th–14th century** | **The MIDDLE AGES** |
| **5th–9th century** | **The DARK AGES** |
| **9th–11th century** | **The EARLY Middle Ages** |
| **11th–14th century** | **The LATE Middle Ages** |
| ⭐ **15th century onwards** | **The MODERN AGE** |

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's own correction:** *"**Recently, historians have QUESTIONED this division.** With more research being done… scholars are **increasingly RELUCTANT to make sharp divisions between centuries in terms of being culturally vibrant or otherwise.** ⭐ **IT SEEMS UNFAIR TO LABEL ANY PERIOD AS THE 'DARK AGES'.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This section is really about how historical periods get their names, and the lesson generalises to Indian history — which is where UPSC will actually test you.**
> ⭐ **The "Dark Ages" was not a discovery. It was a VERDICT passed by people who had an interest in the contrast** — humanists who wanted to present themselves as the restorers of civilisation needed a darkness to have restored it from. ⭐⭐ **A period is defined by whoever benefits from the definition.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Now make the Indian link, because it is the same operation:** **James Mill's division of Indian history into HINDU, MUSLIM and BRITISH periods** *(Class 8 Ch.1)* — a periodisation that built a communal reading of the past into the very chapter headings, and that presented British rule as the arrival of light. ⭐ **NCERT teaches you to distrust "medieval" and "dark" in Europe precisely so that you distrust Mill's labels in India.** **That connection, stated in one sentence, is worth real marks.**

## 5️⃣ Science and Philosophy — THE ARABS' CONTRIBUTION ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **This short section is the most under-used passage in the book, and it is exactly the kind of thing that makes an Essay distinctive.**

```
   ⭐⭐ THE STARTING POINT -- "Much of the writings of the Greeks and
   Romans HAD BEEN FAMILIAR TO MONKS AND CLERGYMEN through the
   'Middle Ages', ⚠️ BUT THEY HAD NOT MADE THESE WIDELY KNOWN."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE DEBT, STATED PLAINLY
   "In the fourteenth century, many scholars began to read
    TRANSLATED WORKS OF GREEK WRITERS LIKE PLATO AND ARISTOTLE.
    ⭐⭐ FOR THIS THEY WERE INDEBTED NOT TO THEIR OWN SCHOLARS BUT
    TO ARAB TRANSLATORS WHO HAD CAREFULLY PRESERVED AND TRANSLATED
    ANCIENT MANUSCRIPTS
    (⭐ PLATO WAS 'AFLATUN', AND ARISTOTLE 'ARISTU', IN ARABIC)."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE TRAFFIC RAN BOTH WAYS
   "While some European scholars READ GREEK IN ARABIC TRANSLATION,
    THE GREEKS TRANSLATED WORKS OF ARABIC AND PERSIAN SCHOLARS for
    further transmission to other Europeans. These were works on
    ⭐ NATURAL SCIENCE, MATHEMATICS, ASTRONOMY, MEDICINE and
    CHEMISTRY."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE FINGERPRINT IN THE NAME
   "PTOLEMY'S ALMAGEST (a work on ASTRONOMY, written in GREEK
    BEFORE 140 CE and later TRANSLATED INTO ARABIC) carries
    ⭐ THE ARABIC DEFINITE ARTICLE 'AL', WHICH BRINGS OUT THE
    ARABIC CONNECTION."
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three scholars NCERT names — learn both name-forms, since Prelims may use either:**

| Arabic name | Latin name | Who |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **IBN SINA** | **"AVICENNA"** | **980–1037** — *"an **Arab PHYSICIAN AND PHILOSOPHER of BUKHARA in Central Asia**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **AL-RAZI** | **"RHAZES"** | *"author of a **MEDICAL ENCYCLOPAEDIA**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **IBN RUSHD** | **"AVERROES"** | **1126–98** — *"an **Arab philosopher of SPAIN**"*, who *"tried to **RESOLVE THE TENSION BETWEEN PHILOSOPHICAL KNOWLEDGE (*faylasuf*) AND RELIGIOUS BELIEFS.** ⭐⭐ **HIS METHOD WAS ADOPTED BY CHRISTIAN THINKERS.**"* |

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's marginal note, and it is the sharpest sentence in the chapter:** *"**The EUROPEAN SPELLING of these individuals' names MADE LATER GENERATIONS THINK THEY WERE EUROPEANS!**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the chapter's best single insight and you should be able to deploy it in thirty seconds.**
> ⭐ **Europe did not "rediscover" its own classical heritage by opening a cupboard. It received Greek philosophy BACK, in Arabic translation, from the Islamic world** — Plato as *Aflatun*, Aristotle as *Aristu* — **and it received, alongside them, original Arab and Persian work in medicine, mathematics, astronomy and chemistry.**
> ⭐⭐ **And then it forgot.** **Ibn Sina became "Avicenna" and Ibn Rushd became "Averroes", and the Latinised names quietly naturalised them as Europeans.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The transmission was erased by ORTHOGRAPHY — not by argument, not by suppression, but by respelling.** **That is how intellectual debts usually disappear.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Now connect it to §10, where NCERT makes the general claim: *"the Europeans learned not just from the Greeks and Romans, but from INDIA, from ARABIA, from IRAN, from CENTRAL ASIA and CHINA. ⭐ THESE DEBTS WERE NOT ACKNOWLEDGED FOR A LONG TIME BECAUSE… HISTORIANS SAW IT FROM A EUROPE-CENTRED VIEWPOINT."*** ⭐⭐ **The "European miracle" is partly an artefact of who wrote the history.** *(This is also the strongest argument for restoring the deleted theme on **THE CENTRAL ISLAMIC LANDS** — see the [Index](00_Index.md). Without it, the Islamic world appears in this book only as a translator and a trade route.)*
>
> ⚠️ **One line NCERT adds without comment, which you should notice:** *"**Schools at this time were only for boys.**"* **Hold it for §7.**

## 6️⃣ Artists, Realism, Architecture and Print ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 🎨 6.1 Art becomes realistic — because it becomes scientific

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE METHOD -- "Artists were inspired by STUDYING WORKS OF
   THE PAST. The MATERIAL REMAINS of Roman culture were sought with

                          |

   CONTINUED TILL THE NINETEENTH CENTURY."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **ALBRECHT DÜRER (1471–1528)** stated the programme himself: *"**'ART' IS EMBEDDED IN NATURE; HE WHO CAN EXTRACT IT, HAS IT**… Moreover, you may **demonstrate much of your work BY GEOMETRY.** ⭐ **The more closely your work abides by LIFE in its form, so much the better will it appear… NO MAN SHALL EVER BE ABLE TO MAKE A BEAUTIFUL FIGURE OUT OF HIS OWN IMAGINATION UNLESS HE HAS WELL STORED HIS MIND BY MUCH COPYING FROM LIFE.**"* *(His brush drawing **'Praying Hands' is dated 1508** — NCERT notes it captures an age *"deeply religious, but also… confident in man's ability to achieve near-perfection."*)*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **LEONARDO DA VINCI (1452–1519)** — *"an amazing range of interests from **BOTANY and ANATOMY to MATHEMATICS and ART**"*; painted the ⭐ ***MONA LISA*** and ***THE LAST SUPPER***; *"**One of his dreams was to be able to FLY. He spent years OBSERVING BIRDS IN FLIGHT, and DESIGNED A FLYING MACHINE.**"* ⭐⭐ **He signed his name *"LEONARDO DA VINCI, DISCIPLE OF EXPERIMENT."***

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Disciple of experiment" is the signature of the whole chapter, and the art section is really a section about METHOD.**
> ⭐ **Artists went to dissection rooms; Vesalius, a professor of MEDICINE, opened the body and founded modern physiology; geometry produced perspective; observation of light produced depth.** ⭐⭐ **Renaissance art did not become lifelike because artists tried harder — it became lifelike because they adopted the procedures of science: dissect, measure, observe, test.** **Realism is an epistemology before it is a style.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐ **And note the Asian debt again — Chinese and Persian influence in the colours and costumes, carried by the Mongols.** **Even the paintings that define "European civilisation" have Asia in their pigment.**

> ⭐ **And note the firearms–compass–abacus parenthesis: four technologies, all reaching Europe from or via Asia.** **The navigation that decentred Europe's map, and the guns that let it conquer, were both borrowed.**

## 7️⃣ A New Concept of Human Beings — and the exclusion of women ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE CHANGE -- "One of the features of humanist culture was
   ⭐ A SLACKENING OF THE CONTROL OF RELIGION OVER HUMAN LIFE."
   ⚠️ BUT NOTE THE QUALIFICATION: "Italians were strongly attracted
      to MATERIAL WEALTH, POWER AND GLORY, ⭐ BUT THEY WERE NOT
      NECESSARILY IRRELIGIOUS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ TWO WRITERS WHO MADE THE CASE
     FRANCESCO BARBARO (1390-1454), a humanist from VENICE, "wrote
       a pamphlet ⭐ DEFENDING ACQUISITION OF WEALTH AS A VIRTUE"
     LORENZO VALLA (1406-1457), in ⭐ ON PLEASURE -- he "believed
       that THE STUDY OF HISTORY LEADS MAN TO STRIVE FOR A LIFE OF
       PERFECTION", and ⭐ "CRITICISED THE CHRISTIAN INJUNCTION
       AGAINST PLEASURE"
                          |
   ⭐⭐ ALSO A NEW CONCERN WITH GOOD MANNERS -- "how one should
   SPEAK POLITELY and DRESS CORRECTLY, what skills A PERSON OF
   CULTURE should learn"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND THE CLAIM THAT BREAKS THE MEDIEVAL ORDER
   "Humanism also implied that INDIVIDUALS WERE CAPABLE OF SHAPING
    THEIR OWN LIVES through means other than the mere pursuit of
    power and money. This ideal was closely tied with the belief
    that ⭐⭐ HUMAN NATURE WAS MANY-SIDED, WHICH WENT AGAINST THE
    THREE SEPARATE ORDERS THAT FEUDAL SOCIETY BELIEVED IN."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That last sentence is the hinge between Theme 4 and Theme 5, and it is worth learning verbatim.** ⭐ **Feudal society sorted everyone into THREE ORDERS — those who pray, those who fight, those who work** *(Theme 4)* — **and a person's nature was exhausted by his order.** ⭐⭐ **Humanism's claim that "human nature was MANY-SIDED" is not a compliment; it is a structural attack.** **If one man can be scholar, diplomat, theologian and artist at once — the "Renaissance Man" of §3 — then the three orders are not a description of reality but a filing system.**
> ⭐ **And notice Barbaro defending WEALTH as a virtue and Valla attacking the injunction against PLEASURE.** **A society organised around renunciation is being argued out of existence by merchants' intellectuals.** 🔗 *(Class 10 Ch.5's Menocchio — a miller who read his way to his own theology and was executed for it — is what happened when this reached below the educated classes.)*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **MACHIAVELLI on human nature — *The Prince* (1513), chapter 15.** He begins by insisting on realism: *"**leaving aside IMAGINARY THINGS, and referring only to those WHICH TRULY EXIST**, I say that whenever men are discussed (**and especially princes, who are more exposed to view**), they are noted for various qualities which earn them either praise or condemnation. Some are held to be **generous**, and others **miserly**… one man **faithless**, another **faithful**… one **guileless**, another **crafty**…"*
> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's summary:** *"Machiavelli believed that **'all men are bad and ever ready to display their vicious nature partly because of the fact that HUMAN DESIRES ARE INSATIABLE'.** ⭐⭐ **The most powerful motive Machiavelli saw as the incentive for every human action is SELF-INTEREST.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Machiavelli is the dark twin of humanism and belongs in any answer that treats it, because he shows the method turned on politics.** ⭐ **The humanists said: study the ancients as they actually wrote, observe from life, be a "disciple of experiment."** **Machiavelli applies exactly that to statecraft — "leaving aside imaginary things, and referring only to those which truly exist"** — and arrives at **self-interest as the engine of all human action.** ⭐⭐ **Realism in painting and realism in politics are the same intellectual move, and one produces the *Mona Lisa* while the other produces *The Prince*.** *(For GS4 this is a usable contrast: an ethics grounded in what people ARE against one grounded in what they OUGHT to be.)*

### 👩 7.1 The Aspirations of Women ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE VERDICT, STATED FLATLY
   "THE NEW IDEAL OF INDIVIDUALITY AND CITIZENSHIP EXCLUDED WOMEN."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ IN ARISTOCRATIC FAMILIES
     - "MEN from aristocratic families DOMINATED PUBLIC LIFE and
        were THE DECISION-MAKERS in their families"
     - they "educated THEIR SONS to take their place in FAMILY
        BUSINESSES OR IN PUBLIC LIFE, at times sending their
        YOUNGER SONS TO JOIN THE CHURCH"
     - ⚠️⭐⭐ "ALTHOUGH THEIR DOWRIES WERE INVESTED IN THE FAMILY
        BUSINESSES, WOMEN GENERALLY HAD NO SAY IN HOW THEIR
        HUSBANDS SHOULD RUN THEIR BUSINESS"
     - "Often, MARRIAGES WERE INTENDED TO STRENGTHEN BUSINESS
        ALLIANCES"
     - ⚠️ "IF AN ADEQUATE DOWRY COULD NOT BE ARRANGED, DAUGHTERS
        WERE SENT TO CONVENTS TO LIVE THE LIFE OF A NUN"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ BUT IN MERCHANT FAMILIES IT WAS DIFFERENT
   "SHOPKEEPERS were very often ASSISTED BY THEIR WIVES in running
    the shop. In families of MERCHANTS AND BANKERS, ⭐ WIVES LOOKED
    AFTER THE BUSINESSES WHEN THE MALE MEMBERS WERE AWAY ON WORK.
    ⭐⭐ THE EARLY DEATH OF A MERCHANT COMPELLED HIS WIDOW TO PERFORM
    A LARGER PUBLIC ROLE than was the case in aristocratic
    families."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CASSANDRA FEDELE (1465–1558), Venetian** — *"one of a handful of women **who questioned the idea that women were incapable of achieving the qualities of a humanist scholar**"*, known for **proficiency in Greek and Latin**, and ⭐ **invited to give ORATIONS at the University of Padua**:
> *"**Even though the study of letters PROMISES AND OFFERS NO REWARD FOR WOMEN AND NO DIGNITY, EVERY WOMAN OUGHT TO SEEK AND EMBRACE THESE STUDIES.**"*
> ⭐⭐ She was *"one of many **Venetian women writers who criticised the republic 'for creating A HIGHLY LIMITED DEFINITION OF FREEDOM THAT FAVOURED THE DESIRES OF MEN OVER THOSE OF WOMEN'**."*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **ISABELLA D'ESTE (1474–1539), Marchesa of MANTUA** — *"**She RULED THE STATE while her husband was absent**, and the court of Mantua, a small state, was **famed for its INTELLECTUAL BRILLIANCE.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's summary of what these women wanted:** *"Women's writings revealed their conviction that they should have ⭐ **ECONOMIC POWER, PROPERTY AND EDUCATION to achieve an identity in a world dominated by men.**"*

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **And the counter-text — BALTHASAR CASTIGLIONE, *The Courtier* (1528):** *"I hold that **a woman should IN NO WAY RESEMBLE A MAN as regards her ways, manners, words, gestures and bearing.** Thus just as it is very fitting that a man should display a certain **robust and sturdy manliness**, so it is well for a woman to have a certain **soft and delicate tenderness**, with **an air of feminine sweetness in her every movement**… ⭐ **For I consider that MANY VIRTUES OF THE MIND ARE AS NECESSARY TO A WOMAN AS TO A MAN**; as it is to be of good family; to shun affectation; to be **naturally graceful**; to be **well mannered, clever and prudent**… to perform well and gracefully **the sports suitable for women**."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT sets an activity comparing Fedele's aspirations with Castiglione's prescription, and asks a pointed follow-up — *"Did they have only women of a particular CLASS in mind?"* Answer both halves.**
> ⭐ **Fedele's line is remarkable precisely because it CONCEDES the injustice and refuses to be governed by it: study "promises and offers NO REWARD for women and NO DIGNITY" — and every woman should study anyway.** ⭐⭐ **She is claiming education as an intrinsic good rather than as a route to a career that was closed to her.**
> ⭐ **Castiglione is subtler than a simple misogynist, and that is what makes him useful: he grants that "many virtues of the mind are as necessary to a woman as to a man" — and then spends the rest of the passage insisting she must never RESEMBLE a man in manner, bearing or sport.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Equality of mind, difference of conduct — which in practice keeps her out of the council chamber, since public life required exactly the "robust and sturdy" bearing she is forbidden.**
> ⚠️ **And the class answer: BOTH are talking about elite women.** Fedele addresses those who might learn Greek and Latin; Castiglione addresses courtiers. **The washerwoman appears in neither.**
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Now the most interesting finding in this section, and it recurs across your whole syllabus: women's real power tracked ECONOMIC FUNCTION, not ideology.** ⭐ **Aristocratic women had dowries invested in businesses they had no say in; MERCHANT wives ran the shop when the men were away, and merchant WIDOWS took a larger public role than any noblewoman.** **Where a woman's labour was needed, her authority followed** — exactly Class 10 supplement Part B's finding that waged work gave working-class women "some control over their lives", and exactly Theme 7's Japanese factories staffed by women. ⚠️ **And recall §5's flat line: *"Schools at this time were only for boys."***

| Grievance | Detail |

| ⭐⭐⭐ **1. Greed and extortion** | **THOMAS MORE (1478–1535)** in England and **ERASMUS (1466–1536)** in Holland felt the Church *"had become **an institution marked by GREED, EXTORTING MONEY AT WILL from ordinary people**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **2. INDULGENCES** | *"One of the favourite methods of the clergy was to sell **'INDULGENCES', documents which APPARENTLY FREED THE BUYER FROM THE BURDEN OF THE SINS HE HAD COMMITTED.** ⭐ **Christians came to realise FROM PRINTED TRANSLATIONS OF THE BIBLE IN LOCAL LANGUAGES that their religion DID NOT PERMIT SUCH PRACTICES.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **3. Church taxes** | *"In **almost every part of Europe, PEASANTS BEGAN TO REBEL AGAINST THE TAXES IMPOSED BY THE CHURCH**"*, while **princes found the Church's interference in the work of the state irritating** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **4. The DONATION OF CONSTANTINE** | The clergy's claim to **judicial and fiscal powers** rested on a document *"supposed to have been issued by **CONSTANTINE, the first Christian Roman Emperor**"* — ⭐⭐ **"Humanist scholars were able to point out that this was NOT GENUINE, AND HAD BEEN FORGED LATER."** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Donation of Constantine deserves a paragraph of its own, because it shows humanist METHOD doing political work.** ⭐ **Petrarch's programme was a "close reading of ancient authors" — comparing texts, testing language, dating documents.** ⭐⭐ **Turned on the Church's own title deeds, that method exposed the basis of its judicial and fiscal power as a FORGERY.** **Philology became a political weapon.** ⭐ **And note who benefited: the PRINCES, who were already irritated by clerical interference in the state.** **The scholar's finding and the ruler's interest arrived at the same conclusion, which is why the Reformation succeeded where earlier heresies had failed.**
> ⭐⭐ **On indulgences, notice the mechanism NCERT specifies: people discovered the practice was illegitimate FROM PRINTED TRANSLATIONS OF THE BIBLE IN LOCAL LANGUAGES.** **The Church's authority rested partly on being the only party who could read the text.** 🔗 *(Class 10 Ch.5 makes this explicit — Luther's New Testament sold 5,000 copies in weeks, and the Church could not recall what had already been distributed.)*


> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Luther calling on the princes to crush the peasants is the most important thing in this section and the one most often left out of answers.**
> ⭐ **Luther's theology was genuinely revolutionary: no priest is needed between the believer and God.** **The Anabaptists simply carried the logic one step further — if God made all people equal, why taxes, and why priests chosen by others?** ⭐⭐ **Luther refused that step and sided with the rulers.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **The lesson is general and very usable: a religious reform that attacks ECCLESIASTICAL hierarchy need not attack SOCIAL hierarchy, and its leaders will often stop it from doing so.** **The Reformation broke the Church's power and left the lord's power intact** — which is exactly why the princes backed it. 🔗 *(Compare Class 10 supplement Part C: the reforming novel that criticised caste practices while remaining upper-caste in authorship and outlook.)*
> ⭐ **And note the geography NCERT supplies: reform won in TOWNS, backed by MERCHANTS; the Catholic Church held the COUNTRYSIDE.** **The Reformation was, among other things, an urban commercial movement** — the same class that made the Italian city-states.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **WILLIAM TYNDALE (1494–1536), an English Lutheran who translated the Bible into English**, defended Protestantism in terms that name the whole issue:
> *"In this they be all agreed, **TO DRIVE YOU FROM THE KNOWLEDGE OF THE SCRIPTURE, and that ye shall NOT HAVE THE TEXT THEREOF IN THE MOTHER-TONGUE, and TO KEEP THE WORLD STILL IN DARKNESS**, to the intent they might **sit in the consciences of the people, through vain superstition and false doctrine**… Which thing only moved me to translate the New Testament. **Because I had perceived by experience, how that IT WAS IMPOSSIBLE TO ESTABLISH THE LAY-PEOPLE IN ANY TRUTH, EXCEPT THE SCRIPTURE WERE PLAINLY LAID BEFORE THEIR EYES IN THEIR MOTHER-TONGUE.**"*
> ⚠️⭐⭐ **A DATE ERROR IN THE PRINTED BOOK:** NCERT says Tyndale *"translated the Bible into English in **1506**"* — **but it also gives his dates as 1494–1536, which would make him TWELVE.** ⭐ **Tyndale's New Testament was printed in 1526.** **Treat the 1506 in this textbook as a misprint.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **Tyndale's argument is that ACCESS is the whole battle** — not doctrine, but whether the text exists in the language people actually speak. 🔗 **That is precisely the vernacular argument of Class 10 supplement Part C and the Bengali *meyeli* debate, and precisely why the Indian reform movements fought over translation.** **Whoever controls the language of the sacred text controls the religion.**

**⭐⭐⭐ Where it ended up, and the Counter-Reformation:**

> - ⭐⭐ *"radicalism survived, and **merged with the resistance of PROTESTANTS IN FRANCE**, who, persecuted by the Catholic rulers, started claiming ⭐⭐ **THE RIGHT OF A PEOPLE TO REMOVE AN OPPRESSIVE RULER AND TO CHOOSE SOMEONE OF THEIR OWN LIKING.**"*
> - *"Eventually, in **France**, as in many other parts of Europe, the Catholic Church **allowed Protestants to worship as they chose.**"*
> - ⭐⭐ *"In **ENGLAND, the rulers ENDED THE CONNECTION WITH THE POPE. THE KING/QUEEN WAS FROM THEN ONWARDS THE HEAD OF THE CHURCH.**"*
> - ⭐⭐⭐ **The Catholic Church reformed itself from within:** *"In **SPAIN and ITALY, churchmen emphasised the need for A SIMPLE LIFE AND SERVICE TO THE POOR.** In Spain, ⭐ **IGNATIUS LOYOLA**, in an attempt to **combat Protestantism, set up the SOCIETY OF JESUS in 1540.** His followers were called ⭐ **JESUITS**, *whose mission was to **SERVE THE POOR and to WIDEN THEIR KNOWLEDGE OF OTHER CULTURES**."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The French Protestant claim — *"the right of a people to REMOVE AN OPPRESSIVE RULER and to CHOOSE someone of their own liking"* — is the most consequential sentence in the whole chapter.** ⭐ **That is popular sovereignty, arrived at from a religious grievance, in the sixteenth century.** ⭐⭐ **Two hundred years later it is the French Revolution.** 🔗 **And Theme 6's section introduction defines all nationalism as a doctrine of popular sovereignty — so the line runs: persecuted Protestants → the right to depose a ruler → 1789 → nationalism.** **State that chain and you have connected three chapters.**
> ⭐ **On the Jesuits, note the second half of the mission — *"to widen their knowledge of OTHER CULTURES."*** 🔗 **Those are the Jesuits who reached Goa with the first printing press in India** *(Class 10 Ch.5)*, **and the Jesuit Matteo Ricci who introduced Western astronomy and mathematics to China** *(Theme 7)*. ⭐⭐ **The Counter-Reformation is why Europe's first sustained intellectual contact with Asia happened when it did.**

## 9️⃣ The Copernican Revolution and the Scientific Revolution ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE BELIEF BEING ATTACKED
   "Christians had believed that ⭐ THE EARTH WAS A SINFUL PLACE AND
    THE HEAVY BURDEN OF SIN MADE IT IMMOBILE. The earth stood at
    THE CENTRE OF THE UNIVERSE, around which moved the CELESTIAL
    PLANETS."
   ⭐ NOTE: this was a MORAL cosmology -- the earth did not move
     BECAUSE it was sinful.
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ COPERNICUS (1473-1543), "A CONTEMPORARY OF MARTIN
   LUTHER", "asserted that ⭐ THE PLANETS, INCLUDING THE EARTH,
   ROTATE AROUND THE SUN."
                          |
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ AND HE WAS AFRAID
   "A DEVOUT CHRISTIAN, Copernicus WAS AFRAID OF THE POSSIBLE
    REACTION TO HIS THEORY BY TRADITIONALIST CLERGYMEN. For this
    reason, HE DID NOT WANT HIS MANUSCRIPT, DE REVOLUTIONIBUS
    (THE ROTATION), TO BE PRINTED.
    ⭐ ON HIS DEATHBED, HE GAVE IT TO HIS FOLLOWER, JOACHIM
    RHETICUS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ "It took time for people to accept this idea" -- MORE THAN
   HALF A CENTURY later the difference between 'HEAVEN' and EARTH
   was bridged by:
     ⭐ JOHANNES KEPLER (1571-1630) -- his COSMOGRAPHICAL MYSTERY
       "demonstrated that THE PLANETS MOVE AROUND THE SUN NOT IN
        CIRCLES BUT IN ELLIPSES"
     ⭐ GALILEO GALILEI (1564-1642) -- "confirmed the notion of THE
       DYNAMIC WORLD in his work THE MOTION"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ "This revolution in science REACHED ITS CLIMAX WITH ISAAC
   NEWTON'S THEORY OF GRAVITATION."
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ "Reading the Universe" — the epistemological turn, and this is the examinable idea:**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Galileo's remark:** *"**the Bible that lights the road TO HEAVEN does not say much on HOW THE HEAVENS WORK.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's formulation:** *"The work of these thinkers showed that ⭐ **KNOWLEDGE, AS DISTINCT FROM BELIEF, WAS BASED ON OBSERVATION AND EXPERIMENTS.** Once these scientists had shown the way, experiments and investigations into what came to be called **physics, chemistry and biology expanded rapidly.** Historians were to label this new approach **the SCIENTIFIC REVOLUTION.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **And the theological consequence:** *"in the minds of **sceptics and non-believers, GOD BEGAN TO BE REPLACED BY NATURE AS THE SOURCE OF CREATION.** ⭐ Even those who **retained their faith in God started talking about A DISTANT GOD WHO DOES NOT DIRECTLY REGULATE THE ACT OF LIVING IN THE MATERIAL WORLD.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **How it became public:** *"Such ideas were popularised through **SCIENTIFIC SOCIETIES that established a new scientific culture IN THE PUBLIC DOMAIN.** ⭐ **THE PARIS ACADEMY, established in 1670**, and **THE ROYAL SOCIETY IN LONDON for the promotion of natural knowledge, formed in 1662**, held ⭐⭐ **LECTURES AND CONDUCTED EXPERIMENTS FOR PUBLIC VIEWING.**"*
> ⚠️ *(The chapter's own timeline gives **1673** for the "Academy of Sciences set up in Paris" against **1670** in the body text — a minor internal inconsistency. **The Royal Society's 1662 is consistent.**)*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Galileo's line is the cleanest statement of the separation of domains you will find, and it is the intellectual settlement the modern world runs on.** ⭐ **He is not saying the Bible is false. He is saying it answers a DIFFERENT QUESTION — the road to heaven, not the mechanics of the heavens.** ⭐⭐ **That is the same move humanism made in §3** — carving out a sphere of knowledge that religion does not govern — **now applied to nature instead of to grammar and history.** **The chapter has one argument and makes it three times: in education, in art, and in science.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **And the definition to memorise: *"KNOWLEDGE, AS DISTINCT FROM BELIEF, WAS BASED ON OBSERVATION AND EXPERIMENTS."*** **The distinction between knowledge and belief — with a test that separates them — is what "Scientific Revolution" names.**
> ⭐⭐ **Do not miss the public-viewing detail.** The Royal Society (**1662**) and the Paris Academy (**1670**) **held lectures and conducted experiments in front of audiences.** ⭐ **Science became authoritative partly because it was PERFORMED IN PUBLIC and could be checked** — the opposite of a doctrine transmitted by an institution. 🔗 *(And it needed print to spread: Newton's *Principia* is in the timeline at 1687.)*
> ⚠️ **Finally, keep Copernicus's fear in the answer.** **A devout Christian withheld his own book and released it only on his deathbed.** ⭐ **The Scientific Revolution was not a confident march; its opening move was made by a frightened man** — which is worth remembering against any triumphalist account of it.

## 🔟 Was there a European "Renaissance" at all? ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **NCERT ends by attacking its own chapter title. This closing section is the most sophisticated writing in the book and is highly examinable — treat it as the chapter's real conclusion.**

```
   ⭐⭐⭐ THE CHALLENGE -- PETER BURKE of England "suggested that
   BURCKHARDT WAS EXAGGERATING THE SHARP DIFFERENCE between this
   period and the one that preceded it"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ TWO CLAIMS BUILT INTO THE WORD 'RENAISSANCE', BOTH
      EXAGGERATED
     1. that "THE GREEK AND ROMAN CIVILISATIONS WERE REBORN AT THIS
        TIME"
        ⚠️ BUT: "SCHOLARS IN EARLIER CENTURIES HAD BEEN FAMILIAR
           WITH GREEK AND ROMAN CULTURES"
     2. that "scholars and artists of this period SUBSTITUTED THE
        PRE-CHRISTIAN WORLD-VIEW FOR THE CHRISTIAN ONE"
        ⚠️ BUT: "RELIGION CONTINUED TO BE A VERY IMPORTANT PART OF
           PEOPLE'S LIVES"
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE PERIODISATION ITSELF
   "To contrast the Renaissance as a period of DYNAMISM AND
    ARTISTIC CREATIVITY, and the Middle Ages as a period of GLOOM
    AND LACK OF DEVELOPMENT, ⭐ IS AN OVER-SIMPLIFICATION."
     - "MANY ELEMENTS ASSOCIATED WITH THE RENAISSANCE IN ITALY CAN
        BE TRACED BACK TO THE TWELFTH AND THIRTEENTH CENTURIES"
     - ⭐ "in the NINTH CENTURY IN FRANCE, there had been SIMILAR
        LITERARY AND ARTISTIC BLOSSOMING"
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ And the second, larger correction — the Eurocentrism charge:**

> *"The cultural changes in Europe at this time were **NOT SHAPED ONLY BY THE 'CLASSICAL' CIVILISATION OF ROME AND GREECE.** The archaeological and literary recovery of Roman culture did create a great admiration of that civilisation. ⭐⭐ **BUT TECHNOLOGIES AND SKILLS IN ASIA HAD MOVED FAR AHEAD OF WHAT THE GREEKS AND ROMANS HAD KNOWN.** Much more of the world had become connected, and **the new techniques of NAVIGATION enabled people to sail much further than had been possible earlier. THE EXPANSION OF ISLAM AND THE MONGOL CONQUESTS HAD LINKED ASIA AND NORTH AFRICA WITH EUROPE — NOT POLITICALLY BUT IN TERMS OF TRADE AND OF LEARNING SKILLS.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ **THE EUROPEANS LEARNED NOT JUST FROM THE GREEKS AND ROMANS, BUT FROM INDIA, FROM ARABIA, FROM IRAN, FROM CENTRAL ASIA AND CHINA. ⚠️ THESE DEBTS WERE NOT ACKNOWLEDGED FOR A LONG TIME BECAUSE WHEN THE HISTORY OF THIS PERIOD STARTED TO BE WRITTEN, HISTORIANS SAW IT FROM A EUROPE-CENTRED VIEWPOINT.**"*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ What DID change — the two lasting transformations. Learn both:**

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ONE -- THE SEPARATION OF PUBLIC AND PRIVATE
   "gradually the 'PRIVATE' AND THE 'PUBLIC' SPHERES OF LIFE BEGAN
    TO BECOME SEPARATE:
      THE 'PUBLIC' SPHERE meant THE AREA OF GOVERNMENT AND OF
        FORMAL RELIGION
      THE 'PRIVATE' SPHERE included THE FAMILY AND PERSONAL
        RELIGION"
   ⭐⭐ "The individual had A PRIVATE AS WELL AS A PUBLIC ROLE.
      ⭐ HE WAS NOT SIMPLY A MEMBER OF ONE OF THE 'THREE ORDERS';
      HE WAS ALSO A PERSON IN HIS OWN RIGHT. An artist was not just
      a member of a guild, HE WAS KNOWN FOR HIMSELF."
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND THE PAYOFF: "IN THE EIGHTEENTH CENTURY, THIS SENSE OF
      THE INDIVIDUAL WOULD BE EXPRESSED IN A POLITICAL FORM, IN THE
      BELIEF THAT ALL INDIVIDUALS HAD EQUAL POLITICAL RIGHTS."
                          |
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ TWO -- IDENTITY BASED ON LANGUAGE
   "the different regions of Europe started to have their SEPARATE
    SENSE OF IDENTITY, BASED ON LANGUAGE.
    ⭐⭐ EUROPE, EARLIER UNITED PARTLY BY THE ROMAN EMPIRE AND LATER
    BY LATIN AND CHRISTIANITY, WAS NOW DISSOLVING INTO STATES,
    EACH UNITED BY A COMMON LANGUAGE."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This closing section is where the chapter earns its place in your syllabus, because both "lasting transformations" are the direct ancestors of things you study elsewhere.**
> ⭐ **The first — the individual with a private self and a public role, no longer exhausted by his order — becomes, in the eighteenth century, THE CLAIM THAT ALL INDIVIDUALS HAVE EQUAL POLITICAL RIGHTS.** 🔗 **That is Class 9 Ch.1: the Declaration of the Rights of Man.** ⭐⭐ **The Renaissance did not produce democracy; it produced the KIND OF PERSON to whom rights could later be attached.**
> ⭐ **The second — Europe "dissolving into states, each united by a common language" — is the seed of nationalism.** 🔗 **Class 10 Ch.1 is the harvest**, and Theme 6's section introduction supplies the theory (**civic vs ethnic nationalism**). ⭐⭐ **Note the loss implied: Latin and Christianity had made Europe ONE learned community; vernaculars made it many nations.** **The same print revolution that spread humanism across the Alps also broke the shared language that had made such spreading possible.** *(A genuinely elegant irony, and worth an Essay sentence.)*
> ⭐⭐⭐ **And on the title question, give the balanced answer NCERT wants:** ⭐ **"Renaissance" overstates the break** — there was no total darkness before, Greek and Roman learning was never lost to the clergy, religion remained central, and comparable flowerings had occurred in ninth-century France and the twelfth–thirteenth centuries. ⭐⭐ **But something real did happen: the individual separated from the order, knowledge separated from belief, and identity re-formed around language.** **The period is real; the NAME is a nineteenth-century argument.**

### 🟢 Mains angles

- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Why were Italian towns the first to experience the ideas of humanism?"** *(NCERT's own — the flagship question here.)* Answer with the **paradox of weakness**: no unified government, a politically feeble papacy, **clergy not politically dominant**, **no powerful feudal lords** — so **merchants and bankers governed the cities themselves and "the idea of citizenship struck root."** Add the **material base** (Byzantine and Islamic trade, the Mongol opening of the Silk Route) and the **institutional base** (Padua and Bologna teaching law from the eleventh century for a commercial society). ⭐ **Conclude that the humanist curriculum — grammar, rhetoric, poetry, history, moral philosophy, taught through debate — is the education a self-governing merchant citizen needs.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What were the features of humanist thought?"** *(NCERT's own.)* — **(1)** a sphere of knowledge **independent of religion** (*"much to be learnt which religious teaching alone could not give"*); **(2)** the **five subjects**, learnt through **discussion and debate**; **(3)** **close reading of ancient authors** in their own words (Petrarch); **(4)** the individual as **capable of making his own decisions and developing his skills** (Burckhardt); **(5)** **human nature as MANY-SIDED**, against the three orders; **(6)** ⭐ **not irreligious** — Barbaro on wealth, Valla on pleasure.
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Which elements of Greek and Roman culture were revived in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries?"** *(NCERT's own.)* — **Roman LAW** studied in its cultural context; **the texts of Plato and Aristotle** (via **Arabic translations**); **the classical architectural style**, revived from **excavated ruins**; **the sculptural ideal of the perfectly proportioned body**; **Cicero's *humanitas***; and **the historical sense** that made "ancient" and "modern" thinkable. ⚠️ **Add the qualification from §10: earlier scholars had never entirely lost this material.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Write a careful account of how the world appeared different to seventeenth-century Europeans."** *(NCERT's own — an excellent synthesis question.)* Structure it on **the two decentrings**: **the EARTH displaced from the centre of the universe** (Copernicus → Kepler's ellipses → Galileo → Newton) and **EUROPE displaced from the centre of the world** (navigation, Mercator's 1569 map, the voyages). Then **knowledge distinguished from belief** by observation and experiment; **a distant God** who does not regulate daily life; **religion as an individual choice** after the Reformation; **the individual with a private and a public role**; and **Europe dissolving into language-based states**.
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What were the issues on which the Protestants criticised the Catholic Church?"** *(NCERT's own — the four grievances in §8: greed and extortion, indulgences, Church taxes, and the forged Donation of Constantine. Add Luther's positive claim — no priest needed between believer and God — and Tyndale on scripture in the mother-tongue.)*
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Was there a European Renaissance in the fourteenth century?"** *(NCERT's own closing section.)* Give the **balanced verdict**: the word smuggles in **two exaggerations** (rebirth of a lost antiquity; substitution of a pre-Christian world-view), **both false**; comparable flowerings occurred in **ninth-century France** and the **twelfth–thirteenth centuries**; and **the changes owed as much to ASIA as to antiquity**. ⭐ **But concede what did change — the individual, the separation of public and private, and language-based identity.**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Essay / GS1 — "What Europe owed to others."** ⭐ **Greek philosophy returned through ARAB translators** (Plato as *Aflatun*); **Ibn Sina, al-Razi and Ibn Rushd**, whose **Latinised names disguised their origin**; **printing, firearms, the compass and the abacus from CHINA via the MONGOLS**; **Chinese and Persian influence in Renaissance costume and colour**; and NCERT's own verdict — *"**these debts were not acknowledged for a long time because… historians saw it from a EUROPE-CENTRED VIEWPOINT.**"*
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Essay / GS4 — "Knowledge and belief."** **Galileo:** *"the Bible that lights the road to heaven does not say much on how the heavens work."* ⭐ **Knowledge distinguished from belief by observation and experiment**; **Vesalius dissecting**; **Dürer's "copying from life"**; **Leonardo, "disciple of experiment"**; **public experiments at the Royal Society (1662) and Paris Academy (1670)**. ⚠️ **Balance it with Copernicus's fear and the deathbed manuscript.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **GS1 Society — "The individual before individualism."** The **artist signed by name rather than as a guild member**; the **layman writing on religion**; the **"Renaissance Man" of many parts against the three orders**; the **separation of public and private** — and ⭐ **its political payoff in the eighteenth century, "in the belief that ALL INDIVIDUALS HAD EQUAL POLITICAL RIGHTS."** ⚠️ **Then the exclusions: women, and the ordinary man who "could not read or write."**

## 🔁 60-Second Recap

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **From the 14th to the end of the 17th century:** towns grew and a distinct **urban culture** appeared (**Florence, Venice, Rome**); **printing** spread books; a **sense of history** made "ancient" and "modern" thinkable; **religion became an individual choice**; and ⭐ **two centres fell — the EARTH from the centre of the universe, and EUROPE (the Mediterranean) from the centre of the world.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"RENAISSANCE" is a NINETEENTH-CENTURY word.** **JACOB BURCKHARDT (1818–97)** of **Basle**, a student of **LEOPOLD VON RANKE**, broke with his teacher's rule that history meant **states and politics from government files** — *"history was as much concerned with CULTURE as with politics"* — and wrote ***The Civilisation of the Renaissance in Italy* (1860)**, arguing that **man as an individual was now capable of making his own decisions.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Italy got humanism BECAUSE IT WAS WEAK.** No unified government, a feeble papacy, ⭐ **"the clergy were not politically dominant… nor were there powerful feudal lords"** — so **rich merchants and bankers governed the cities and "the idea of citizenship struck root."** **Florence and Venice were REPUBLICS**; others were **court-cities under princes**. ⚠️ **But Contarini (1534) records that the common people were deliberately excluded from the Venetian council.**
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **The material base:** ports revived on **Byzantine and Islamic trade**, and **from the twelfth century on the Mongol opening of the Silk Route to China.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The universities: PADUA and BOLOGNA taught LAW from the eleventh century**, because commerce needed **lawyers and NOTARIES**. **PETRARCH (1304–78)** insisted antiquity be understood **through the actual words of the ancients**, implying ⭐ **"there was much to be learnt which religious teaching alone could not give."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HUMANISM = masters teaching GRAMMAR, RHETORIC, POETRY, HISTORY and MORAL PHILOSOPHY** — subjects **"not drawn from or connected with religion"**, taught **through discussion and debate**. ***Humanitas* is CICERO's word (106–43 BCE) for CULTURE.** **Pico della Mirandola** called debate **"this wrestling-place of letters."**
7. ⭐⭐⭐ **Florence** rose in the fifteenth century, known for **DANTE (a LAYMAN writing on religious themes)** and **GIOTTO (lifelike portraits, against earlier stiff figures)**. The **"RENAISSANCE MAN"** was **scholar-diplomat-theologian-artist in one**.
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The humanists invented the "DARK AGES"** to have something to have rescued civilisation from — **Middle Ages 5th–14th c., Dark Ages 5th–9th, Modern from the 15th.** ⚠️ **NCERT rejects this: "it seems unfair to label any period as the 'Dark Ages'."**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ARAB DEBT.** Europeans read **Plato (*Aflatun*) and Aristotle (*Aristu*) in ARABIC translation**; **Ptolemy's *ALMAGEST* still carries the Arabic "al"**. **IBN SINA ("Avicenna", 980–1037) of Bukhara**, **AL-RAZI ("Rhazes")**, and **IBN RUSHD ("Averroes", 1126–98) of Spain**, whose method of reconciling philosophy and faith **"was adopted by Christian thinkers."** ⚠️ ⭐ **"The European spelling of these individuals' names made later generations think they were Europeans!"**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Art became realistic by becoming scientific.** **DONATELLO's lifelike statues (1416)**; artists went to **medical laboratories**; **VESALIUS (1514–64) of PADUA was the first to dissect the human body — "the beginning of modern physiology"**; **geometry gave perspective**, **light gave three dimensions**, **oil gave colour**; ⭐ **and Chinese and Persian influence, via the Mongols, shows in costumes.** **DÜRER:** *"no man… unless he has well stored his mind by much copying from life."* **LEONARDO signed himself "DISCIPLE OF EXPERIMENT."**
11. ⭐⭐⭐ **Architecture:** popes stronger **from 1417** (the two-rival-popes split since **1378** having ended); **ruins excavated by archaeologists — a NEW skill**; the "new" style was **a revival of imperial Roman, now called "CLASSICAL."** **MICHELANGELO** (Sistine ceiling, the Pieta, St Peter's dome), **BRUNELLESCHI** (the Duomo, and a sculptor first), **ALBERTI** (art theory). ⭐ **And artists were now known INDIVIDUALLY, by name, not as guild members.**
12. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PRINT.** Europe owed **the technology to the CHINESE** and the contact **to the MONGOLS** — *"as with **FIREARMS, THE COMPASS and THE ABACUS**."* **Gutenberg (1400–58) printed 150 Bibles in 1455**, in the time a monk needed for **one**. ⭐ *"The chief reason that the humanist culture of Italy spread more rapidly across the Alps… is that **PRINTED BOOKS WERE CIRCULATING** — this also explains why **earlier intellectual movements had been limited to particular regions.**"*
13. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The new human being:** religion's grip slackened, though Italians were **"not necessarily irreligious"** — **BARBARO defended WEALTH as a virtue**, **VALLA's *On Pleasure* attacked the injunction against pleasure**. ⭐⭐ **The belief that "HUMAN NATURE WAS MANY-SIDED… WENT AGAINST THE THREE SEPARATE ORDERS THAT FEUDAL SOCIETY BELIEVED IN."** **MACHIAVELLI (*The Prince*, 1513)** found the motive of every human action in ⭐ **SELF-INTEREST**.
14. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The new ideal of individuality and citizenship EXCLUDED WOMEN."** Schools were **only for boys**; **dowries were invested in businesses women had no say in**; dowerless daughters **went to convents**. ⭐ **But MERCHANT wives ran shops and businesses, and merchant WIDOWS took a larger public role than noblewomen.** **CASSANDRA FEDELE** (orations at Padua): *"even though the study of letters promises… **no reward for women and no dignity, every woman ought to seek and embrace these studies**."* **ISABELLA D'ESTE ruled Mantua.** Women wanted ⭐ **economic power, property and education.** ⚠️ **CASTIGLIONE's *The Courtier* (1528)** granted equal virtues of mind while forbidding any resemblance to a man in bearing.
15. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Reformation's four grievances: greed and extortion; INDULGENCES; Church taxes; and the DONATION OF CONSTANTINE, which humanist scholars proved a FORGERY.** ⭐ **People learnt indulgences were illegitimate from PRINTED BIBLES IN LOCAL LANGUAGES.**
16. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1517: MARTIN LUTHER (1483–1546)** argued **no priest was needed between believer and God** and that **faith alone** sufficed. **ZWINGLI** and later **CALVIN** spread it in Switzerland; reform won **in TOWNS, backed by MERCHANTS**, while **the countryside stayed Catholic**. ⭐ **The ANABAPTISTS went further — all people equal, therefore no taxes and the right to choose priests** — ⚠️ **and LUTHER CALLED ON THE GERMAN RULERS TO SUPPRESS THE PEASANTS, WHICH THEY DID IN 1525.**
17. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Radicalism survived among French Protestants, who claimed ⭐ "THE RIGHT OF A PEOPLE TO REMOVE AN OPPRESSIVE RULER AND TO CHOOSE SOMEONE OF THEIR OWN LIKING."** **In England the rulers ended the connection with the Pope and the monarch became head of the Church.** **The Catholic Church reformed from within: IGNATIUS LOYOLA founded the SOCIETY OF JESUS (JESUITS) in 1540**, whose mission was **to serve the poor and widen knowledge of other cultures**.
18. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE COPERNICAN REVOLUTION.** Christians had held the earth **immobile because sinful**, at the centre. **COPERNICUS (1473–1543)**, a contemporary of Luther and **a devout Christian who feared the clergy**, withheld ***De revolutionibus*** and gave it to **JOACHIM RHETICUS on his deathbed.** Half a century later **KEPLER** showed the orbits were **ELLIPSES**, **GALILEO** confirmed a dynamic world, and the revolution **climaxed with NEWTON's gravitation.**
19. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Galileo: *"the Bible that lights the road to heaven does not say much on how the heavens work."*** ⭐ **"KNOWLEDGE, AS DISTINCT FROM BELIEF, WAS BASED ON OBSERVATION AND EXPERIMENTS"** — the **SCIENTIFIC REVOLUTION**. God began to be **replaced by Nature**, or became **a DISTANT God**. The **ROYAL SOCIETY (1662)** and **PARIS ACADEMY (1670)** held **experiments for public viewing**.
20. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WAS THERE A RENAISSANCE?** **PETER BURKE** says Burckhardt exaggerated. **Earlier scholars knew Greek and Roman culture; religion remained central; similar flowerings occurred in ninth-century France and the 12th–13th centuries.** ⭐⭐ **And the changes owed as much to ASIA: *"the Europeans learned not just from the Greeks and Romans, but from INDIA, ARABIA, IRAN, CENTRAL ASIA and CHINA — these debts were NOT ACKNOWLEDGED because historians saw it from a EUROPE-CENTRED VIEWPOINT."***
21. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **What DID change — two things.** ⭐ **(1) PUBLIC and PRIVATE separated**: the individual **"was not simply a member of one of the 'three orders'; he was also a person in his own right"** — and **in the eighteenth century this became the belief that ALL INDIVIDUALS HAD EQUAL POLITICAL RIGHTS.** ⭐ **(2) IDENTITY BASED ON LANGUAGE**: Europe, **once united by the Roman Empire and later by Latin and Christianity, was "dissolving into states, each united by a common language."**
