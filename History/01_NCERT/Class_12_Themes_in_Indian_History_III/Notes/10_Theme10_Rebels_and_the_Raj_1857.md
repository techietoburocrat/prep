# Theme 10 — Rebels and the Raj
## *The Revolt of 1857 and Its Representations*

> **The one-line point:** The chapter is in **two unequal halves, and the second half is what makes it Class 12.** Sections 1–4 reconstruct **what happened and what the rebels wanted** — from a handful of proclamations, a few petitions, and the records of the people who crushed them. Section 5 then turns round and studies **the pictures the British painted of it all**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's real subject is stated plainly in § 3: *"The repression of the rebels also meant SILENCING OF THEIR VOICE."*** So the chapter is not asking *what happened in 1857* — it is asking **how you write the history of the losing side when the winners kept the archive, the paintbrushes and the printing presses.**

## 🎬 The Framing — read this before anything else

NCERT does not open with causes. **It opens with a scene, at a stated hour**, and the choice is deliberate:

> *"**Late in the afternoon of 10 May 1857, the sepoys in the cantonment of Meerut broke out in mutiny.** It began in the **lines of the native infantry**, spread very swiftly to the **cavalry** and then to **the city**. **The ordinary people of the town and surrounding villages joined the sepoys.**"*

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Four clauses, and the fourth is the whole chapter.** Infantry → cavalry → city → **the ordinary people of the town and the surrounding villages**. **The event changes category in a single sentence** — and NCERT gives you the vocabulary for that change on the very next page.

```
   THE ARCHITECTURE OF THIS CHAPTER

   SECTIONS 1-4  :  WHAT HAPPENED, AND WHAT THE REBELS WANTED
        |
        |  1. PATTERN of the uprising -- how mutinies began,
        |     how they were coordinated, who led, and the
        |     RUMOURS that moved people
        |  2. AWADH -- one kingdom examined in depth, because
        |     it shows the chain: PRINCE -> TALUQDAR ->
        |     PEASANT -> SEPOY
        |  3. WHAT THE REBELS WANTED -- built from a handful
        |     of proclamations and petitions
        |  4. REPRESSION -- martial law, and reconquest
        |     village by village
        |
   SECTION 5     :  ** IMAGES OF THE REVOLT **
        |
        |  5.1 celebrating the saviours      (Barker)
        |  5.2 English women and honour      (Paton, Miss Wheeler)
        |  5.3 vengeance and retribution     (Punch)
        |  5.4 ** the performance of terror ** (ILN executions)
        |  5.5 no time for clemency          (Punch on Canning)
        |  5.6 ** nationalist imageries **    (the Rani)
        |
        v
   ** THE HINGE BETWEEN THE HALVES, in NCERT's own words:
      "As victors, the British recorded their own trials and
       tribulations as well as their heroism. They dismissed
       the rebels as a bunch of ungrateful and barbaric
       people. THE REPRESSION OF THE REBELS ALSO MEANT
       SILENCING OF THEIR VOICE." **
```

### 🗝️ Two words NCERT defines, and the exam expects you to keep apart ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **MUTINY** — *"a **collective disobedience of rules and regulations WITHIN THE ARMED FORCES**"*
> **REVOLT** — *"a **rebellion of PEOPLE against established authority and power**"*
>
> And NCERT's own application: *"In the context of the revolt of 1857 the term **revolt** refers primarily to the uprising of **the CIVILIAN population (peasants, zamindars, rajas, jagirdars)** while the **mutiny** was of **the SEPOYS**."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is a guaranteed Prelims distinction and a Mains framing device.** NCERT tells you the moment of transition in one sentence: *"**The mutiny in the sepoy ranks quickly became a rebellion. There was a general defiance of all kinds of authority and hierarchy.**"* ⭐ **The tell that it had happened is a change of TARGET** — once ordinary people joined, *"**moneylenders and the rich also became the objects of rebel wrath**"*, because *"peasants not only saw them as **oppressors** but also as **allies of the British**."*
>
> 💡 **So when a question asks whether 1857 was "a sepoy mutiny" or "a popular revolt", you do not have to choose.** **It was a mutiny that became a revolt**, and NCERT hands you the criterion for the transition. *(This is also the answer to the old colonial line that it was "merely a sepoy mutiny" — a line that Section 4's evidence, three-quarters of Awadh's adult males in rebellion, demolishes.)*

### 🏛️ The analogy that fits this chapter: **a memorial hall built by the winners**

Not a courtroom (that was Theme 11's) and not a ledger (Theme 9's). **This chapter is a building you walk through.**

Imagine a grand memorial hall raised after a war by the side that won. Every painting on the wall was commissioned by them; every plaque was written by them; the gift shop sells prints of the heroes. **You are the historian who has been let in with a notebook.**

```

   heroes lit from above, the                to believe in 1859: that the
   dead in the foreground                    trouble was over and they had won

   children huddled, waiting                 The painting works by making you
                                             IMAGINE him. That is a technique,
                                             not a record

   "JUSTICE" -- an armed female            that revenge had to be dressed as
   figure trampling sepoys                   JUSTICE before it could be sold

   hanging in a row, cannons around          OPEN, before assembled troops,
                                             because ** terror that nobody
                                             watches does not work **

   THE CARTOON MOCKING CANNING              that mercy was a MINORITY position
   for offering mercy                        and was publicly ridiculed

   where the rebels' own account               and the rumours people believed.
   should hang                                 THAT IS THE ENTIRE ARCHIVE OF
                                               THE OTHER SIDE. **
```

> 🏛️ **Where the analogy is exact:** a memorial hall is **not a lie**. Campbell really did relieve Lucknow; people really did die in the Residency. ⭐ **Everything on the walls can be true and the hall can still be a distorted account** — because the distortion is in **what was commissioned, what was hung, and what was never painted.** That is precisely how NCERT teaches you to read Section 5.
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And where it becomes uncomfortable:** § 5.6 shows you that **the nationalists built a memorial hall too.** The Rani of Jhansi *"represented as a masculine figure chasing the enemy, slaying British soldiers"*, the revolt *"celebrated as the **First War of Independence** in which **all sections of the people of India came together**"* — that is also a curated wall, built for a purpose. **NCERT applies the same method to both galleries, and expects you to as well.**
>
> ⚠️ **Where the analogy stops:** a museum is static; **rumour is not.** Sections 1.4–1.5 deal with something a building cannot hold — beliefs moving from village to village faster than any official could contradict them. **The analogy organises the evidence problem; the facts, dates, names and quotations still have to be learned.**

## 1️⃣ Pattern of the Uprising

### 🔥 Meerut, 10 May 1857 — the sequence, in NCERT's order ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   MEERUT, LATE AFTERNOON, 10 MAY 1857

   mutiny begins in the NATIVE INFANTRY lines
        -> spreads "very swiftly" to the CAVALRY
        -> then to THE CITY
        -> ** "The ordinary people of the town and surrounding
              villages JOINED the sepoys." **
                |
   they seize ** THE BELL OF ARMS ** -- "a storeroom in which
   weapons are kept" -- where arms and ammunition were kept
                |
   they attack white people, and "ransack and burn their
   bungalows and property"
                |
   GOVERNMENT BUILDINGS destroyed and plundered:
   ** the RECORD OFFICE, the JAIL, the COURT, the POST OFFICE,
      the TREASURY **
                |
   ** THE TELEGRAPH LINE TO DELHI IS CUT **
                |
   "As darkness descended, a group of sepoys rode off
   towards DELHI."
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Look hard at the list of buildings, because it is an argument and not a list.** Record office, jail, court, post office, treasury. **Not one of them is a military target.** ⭐ **They are the instruments by which a colonial state knows, holds, judges, communicates with and taxes a population.** The sepoys destroyed **the apparatus of rule**, and § 1.1 confirms the intent — they attacked government buildings *"**burning all records**."*
>
> 💡 **And note the telegraph.** *(Beyond NCERT: the electric telegraph was barely five years old in India and was the one technology that let the British move information faster than rebels could march. Cutting it was not vandalism; it was the single most effective military decision of the first night.)* ⭐ **Compare Theme 9's Shah Mal, who "destroyed the bridge over the river, and dug up metalled roads."** *Rebels across this book attack communications first — and NCERT tells you they did it "partly to prevent government forces from coming into the area, and partly because bridges and roads were seen as SYMBOLS OF BRITISH RULE."* **Both reasons at once: practical and symbolic.**

### 🕌 Delhi, 11 May — Bahadur Shah, at dawn, in Ramzan ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"The sepoys arrived at the gates of the Red Fort **early in the morning on 11 May**. It was **the month of Ramzan**, the Muslim holy month of prayer and fasting. The old Mughal emperor, **Bahadur Shah**, had just **finished his prayers and meal before the sun rose and the fast began**. He heard the commotion at the gates."*

**And then the sentence the sepoys shouted up at his window — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ learn it word for word, it is the rebels speaking in their own voice, which is rare in this chapter:**

> *"**We have come from Meerut after killing all the Englishmen there, because they asked us to bite bullets that were coated with THE FAT OF COWS AND PIGS with our teeth. This has corrupted THE FAITH OF HINDUS AND MUSLIMS ALIKE.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two things are being done in that one utterance, and both matter.**
> **(1) The grievance is stated as religious, not economic** — and it is stated **jointly**: cow fat offends Hindus, pig fat offends Muslims, and *"the faith of Hindus and Muslims **alike**"* has been corrupted. ⭐ **The very object of complaint was one that could only be shared.** *(This is the ground floor of § 3.1's "vision of unity" — the unity was not manufactured by proclamations later; it is present in the first sentence spoken by the first rebels.)*
> **(2) They are reporting a completed act and requesting a blessing.** They have already killed; what they need now is **legitimacy**.

**What followed in the city, and the reluctant blessing:**

> *"Another group of sepoys also entered Delhi, and **the ordinary people of the city joined them**. Europeans were killed in large numbers; **the rich of Delhi were attacked and looted**. It was clear that Delhi had gone out of British control. Some sepoys **rode into the Red Fort, WITHOUT OBSERVING THE ELABORATE COURT ETIQUETTE expected of them**. They **demanded** that the emperor give them his blessings. **Surrounded by the sepoys, Bahadur Shah had NO OTHER OPTION but to comply.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The revolt thus acquired A KIND OF LEGITIMACY because it could now be carried on IN THE NAME OF THE MUGHAL EMPEROR.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read the phrase "without observing the elaborate court etiquette" carefully — NCERT repeats it in § 1.3, so it is doing real work.** Riding into the Mughal court is itself an act of insubordination. ⭐ **The same men who wanted the emperor's authority were simultaneously demonstrating that they no longer feared it.** **They were not restoring the old order; they were conscripting its symbol.** *That distinction is worth a full sentence in any Mains answer on the rebels' aims.*
>
> 💡 **And note "the rich of Delhi were attacked and looted" — on day one.** ⭐ **The social dimension is present from the very beginning, not added later.**

**Then the pause, and the chain reaction:**

> *"**Through 12 and 13 May, North India remained QUIET.** Once word spread that **Delhi had fallen** to the rebels and **Bahadur Shah had blessed the rebellion**, events moved swiftly. **Cantonment after cantonment in the Gangetic valley and some to the west of Delhi rose in mutiny.**"*

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Those two quiet days are the most eloquent detail in Section 1.** North India was **waiting to hear**. **The rising did not spread by contagion — it spread by NEWS**, and specifically by news of **legitimacy**. *(Which is exactly why § 1.2 on communication is the next sub-section.)*

### 🥁 1.1 How the mutinies began — the standard script ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"If one were to place the dates of these mutinies in **chronological order**, it would appear that **as the news of the mutiny in one town travelled to the next the sepoys there took up arms**. **The sequence of events in every cantonment followed A SIMILAR PATTERN.**"*

```
   THE SCRIPT A CANTONMENT FOLLOWED -- learn the ORDER

   1. THE SIGNAL
      "in many places it was ** THE FIRING OF THE EVENING GUN
       or THE SOUNDING OF THE BUGLE **"

   2. SEIZE THE MEANS OF FIGHTING
      "They first seized ** THE BELL OF ARMS **"

   3. SEIZE THE MEANS OF PAYING
      "and ** PLUNDERED THE TREASURY **"

   4. DESTROY THE MEANS OF RULING
      "They then attacked government buildings -- ** the JAIL,
       TREASURY, TELEGRAPH OFFICE, RECORD ROOM, BUNGALOWS --
       BURNING ALL RECORDS. **"

   5. AND THE GENERAL RULE
      ** "EVERYTHING AND EVERYBODY CONNECTED WITH THE WHITE
         MAN BECAME A TARGET." **

   6. THEN THE APPEAL TO THE PUBLIC
      "PROCLAMATIONS in ** HINDI, URDU AND PERSIAN ** were put
       up in the cities calling upon the population,
       ** BOTH HINDUS AND MUSLIMS, to UNITE, RISE and
       EXTERMINATE THE FIRANGIS. **"
```

> 🔑 **NCERT's gloss on the key word:** ***"FIRANGI**, a term of **Persian origin**, possibly derived from **Frank** (from which France gets its name), is used in **Urdu and Hindi**, often in a **derogatory sense**, to designate **foreigners**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Learn the etymology — it is exactly the kind of box-item Prelims lifts.** And note that the rebels' own word for their enemy was **not "British" or "English" but "foreigner"** — which tells you how they framed the war.
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Step 6 is the most important and the most overlooked.** The proclamations were in **THREE languages** and addressed **both communities**. ⭐ **A movement that prints in Hindi, Urdu and Persian and appeals explicitly to Hindus and Muslims together is not an accident of anger — it is a considered political address.** *(Hold this for § 3.1 and NCERT Exercise 4.)*

**And then the widening — the moment mutiny becomes revolt:**

> *"When **ordinary people began joining the revolt, the TARGETS OF ATTACK WIDENED**. In major towns like **Lucknow, Kanpur and Bareilly, MONEYLENDERS AND THE RICH also became the objects of rebel wrath.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Peasants not only saw them as OPPRESSORS but also as ALLIES OF THE BRITISH.** In most places their houses were **looted and destroyed**. **The mutiny in the sepoy ranks quickly became a REBELLION. There was A GENERAL DEFIANCE OF ALL KINDS OF AUTHORITY AND HIERARCHY.**"*

> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Oppressors AND allies of the British" is the single most transferable sentence in Section 1**, because it explains the whole social geometry of colonial India: **the peasant did not meet the Raj directly.** He met **a moneylender and a landlord** whose power the Raj's courts and contracts had enlarged. **So an anti-colonial revolt looked, at village level, like an attack on the man next door.** ⭐ **This is precisely [Theme 9](09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md)'s Deccan in advance** — and in 1875 those ryots would burn account books exactly as these ones did. *(NCERT names the towns: **Lucknow, Kanpur, Bareilly**. Learn the three.)*

**And the British collapse:**

> *"In the months of **May and June**, the British **had no answer** to the actions of the rebels. Individual Britons tried to save their own lives and the lives of their families. **British rule, as one British officer noted, 'collapsed like A HOUSE MADE OF CARDS'.**"*

#### 📰 Source 1 — *Ordinary life in extraordinary times* ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**From the *Delhi Urdu Akhbar*, 14 June 1857** — and note what NCERT chose to print: **not a battle, but the vegetable market.**

> *"The same thing is true for **vegetables and saag (spinach)**. People have been found to complain that **even kaddu (pumpkin) and baingan (brinjal) cannot be found in the bazaars**. Potatoes and arvi (yam) when available are of **stale and rotten variety**, stored from before by farsighted **kunjras (vegetable growers)**. From the gardens inside the city some produce does reach a few places but **the poor and the middle class can only LICK THEIR LIPS AND WATCH THEM** (as they are earmarked for the select)."*
>
> *"…**the WATER-CARRIERS have stopped filling water. Poor Shurfas (gentility) are seen carrying water in pails on their shoulders**… **The halalkhors (righteous) have become haramkhors (corrupt)**, many mohallas have not been able to earn for several days and if this situation continues then **decay, death and disease will combine together to spoil the city's air and an EPIDEMIC will spread all over the city**…"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why NCERT prints a report about pumpkins in a chapter about a rebellion.** Because **this is what a revolution feels like from inside a household.** Not proclamations — **no brinjal, no water-carrier, and the fear of an epidemic.** ⭐ **When a Mains question asks about the impact of 1857, an answer that includes the collapse of ordinary urban services is doing history rather than reciting battles.**
>
> 💬 **NCERT's question — *"Remember that newspaper reports often express the prejudices of the reporter. How did Delhi Urdu Akhbar view the actions of the people?"*** → ✅ **Look at the vocabulary and the standpoint.** ⭐ The paper writes from the position of **the *shurfa*, the respectable urban gentry** — its distress is that *"poor **Shurfas** are seen **carrying water in pails on their shoulders**"*, i.e. that **people of standing are having to do menial work.** ⭐ Its judgement on the labouring poor is a pun and an accusation: ***"the halalkhors (righteous) have become haramkhors (corrupt)"*** — the sweepers and water-carriers who stopped working are **morally condemned**, not understood as people who had also lost their livelihoods. ⭐ And the *kunjras* are *"farsighted"* — i.e. **hoarders**. ✅ **The verdict:** the paper is **not pro-British**, but it is decidedly **anti-disorder and elite in sympathy**. It experiences the revolt as **a breakdown of the social order that placed it comfortably**.
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The general lesson, and it is the lesson of the whole book: "Indian" is not the same as "sympathetic to the rebels."** *(Exactly as in [Theme 9](09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md), where the Indian-owned *Native Opinion* sided with the moneylenders against the ryots.)*

### 📡 1.2 Lines of communication — the evidence for planning ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**This sub-section is NCERT Exercise 2 in its entirety. There are THREE pieces of evidence — learn all three.**

**① The letter between regiments:**

> *"**After the 7th Awadh Irregular Cavalry had REFUSED TO ACCEPT THE NEW CARTRIDGES in early May, they wrote to the 48th Native Infantry that 'they had ACTED FOR THE FAITH and AWAITED THE 48th's ORDERS'.**"* And *"**sepoys or their emissaries moved from one station to another. People were thus planning and talking about the rebellion.**"*

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The phrase *"awaited the 48th's orders"* is decisive.** A regiment that **waits for another regiment's instructions** is not rioting — **it is operating inside a chain of consultation.** *(And note the date: **early May**, before Meerut.)*

**② Source 2 — *Sisten and the tahsildar*, the best anecdote in the chapter:**

> *"**François Sisten**, a **native Christian police inspector in Sitapur**… had gone to **Saharanpur** to pay his respects to the magistrate. **Sisten was dressed in Indian clothes and sitting cross-legged.** A **Muslim tahsildar from Bijnor** entered the room; upon learning that Sisten was from **Awadh**, he enquired: **'What news from Awadh? HOW DOES THE WORK PROGRESS, BROTHER?'** Playing safe, Sisten replied, **'If we have work in Awadh, your highness will know it.'** The tahsildar said: **'DEPEND UPON IT, WE WILL SUCCEED THIS TIME. THE DIRECTION OF THE BUSINESS IS IN ABLE HANDS.'** **The tahsildar was later identified as THE PRINCIPAL REBEL LEADER OF BIJNOR.**"*

> 💬 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT asks two questions and both have real answers.**
>
> **(1) *What does this conversation suggest about the ways in which plans were communicated and discussed?*** → ✅ **That a shared vocabulary existed which required no explanation.** ⭐ Neither man names a plot. They say ***"the work"***, ***"the business"***, ***"this time"*** — **euphemisms that only work if both parties already know the referent.** ✅ It also shows the network ran **through the Company's own officials** — a **tahsildar** is a *government revenue officer* — and **across provinces**, since a man from **Bijnor** was casually enquiring about **Awadh** while standing in **Saharanpur**. ✅ And *"we will succeed **this time**"* implies **an awareness of previous failure**, i.e. a continuing project rather than a sudden impulse.
>
> **(2) *Why did the tahsildar regard Sisten as a potential rebel?*** → ✅ ⭐ **He read the signs and got them wrong.** Sisten was **dressed in Indian clothes**, **sitting cross-legged**, and **came from Awadh** — the most disaffected province in India. **On appearance and origin he was a natural rebel.** ✅ **What the tahsildar could not see was that Sisten was a NATIVE CHRISTIAN and a POLICE INSPECTOR** — the two things that made him the opposite. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The episode is therefore doubly instructive: it proves a network existed, and it proves that even its members could not reliably tell who was in it.** *(And spare a thought for the historical irony — this evidence of rebel coordination survives only because a policeman reported the conversation.)*

**③ The panchayats — and the chapter's boldest claim:**

> *"Captain **Hearsey** of the Awadh Military Police had been **given protection by his Indian subordinates** during the mutiny. **The 41st Native Infantry**, stationed in the same place, insisted that since **they had killed all their white officers**, the Military Police should also **kill Hearsey or deliver him as prisoner**. The Military Police **refused to do either**, and it was decided that **the matter would be settled by A PANCHAYAT composed of native officers drawn from each regiment.**"*
>
> *"**Charles Ball**, who wrote **one of the earliest histories of the uprising**, noted that **PANCHAYATS WERE A NIGHTLY OCCURRENCE in the Kanpur sepoy lines.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"What this suggests is that **SOME OF THE DECISIONS WERE TAKEN COLLECTIVELY**. Given the fact that the sepoys **lived in lines and shared a common lifestyle** and that **many of them came from the same caste**, it is not difficult to imagine them **sitting together to decide their own future**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SEPOYS WERE THE MAKERS OF THEIR OWN REBELLION.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That final sentence is the thesis of Section 1 and it is arguing against two opponents at once.** Against **the British view** that the sepoys were dupes stirred up by conspirators or by superstition. And against **a certain nationalist view** that the revolt was directed from above by princes. ⭐ **NCERT's position: the initiative came from the ranks — the leaders were mostly recruited afterwards, and often unwillingly.**
>
> 💡 **And notice the Hearsey episode is EVIDENCE OF RESTRAINT, not just of organisation.** The Military Police **refused to kill their officer**, refused to hand him over, and **referred the dispute to a council rather than settling it by force.** ⭐ **A rebellion capable of convening a tribunal to adjudicate the fate of one enemy officer is not the "bunch of ungrateful and barbaric people" of § 3.** *That contrast is worth making explicitly when you answer on rebel organisation.*

### 👑 1.3 Leaders and followers ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**This is NCERT Exercise 1, and the answer has a twist: the rebels needed leaders, and the leaders did not want the job.**

> *"To fight the British, **leadership and organisation were required**. For these the rebels sometimes turned to **those who had been leaders BEFORE the British conquest**."*

| Place | Leader the rebels turned to | ⭐ How they came to lead — **note the verbs** |
|---|---|---|
| **Delhi** | **Bahadur Shah**, the Mughal emperor | *"Bahadur Shah's **first reaction was one of HORROR AND REJECTION**. It was only when some sepoys had **moved into the Mughal court within the Red Fort, in defiance of normal court etiquette**, that the old emperor, **realising he had VERY FEW OPTIONS**, agreed to be **the NOMINAL leader**"* |
| **Kanpur** | **Nana Sahib**, *"the successor to Peshwa Baji Rao II"* | *"the sepoys and the people of the town **gave Nana Sahib NO CHOICE SAVE to join the revolt as their leader**"* |
| **Jhansi** | **The Rani** (Lakshmi Bai) | *"the rani was **FORCED BY THE POPULAR PRESSURE around her** to assume the leadership"* |
| **Arrah, Bihar** | **Kunwar Singh**, *"a local zamindar"* | *"**So was Kunwar Singh**"* — i.e. likewise forced |
| **Lucknow / Awadh** | **Birjis Qadr**, *"the young son of the Nawab"* | *"the populace in Lucknow **celebrated the fall of British rule by HAILING Birjis Qadr… as their leader**"* — the people proclaimed him |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Every single verb is passive or coercive: "no other option", "horror and rejection", "no choice save", "forced by popular pressure", "hailed".** ⭐ **This is NCERT's most quietly subversive point.** The standard picture of 1857 — a revolt led by dispossessed princes defending their thrones — is **inverted**. **The princes were conscripted by their own people.** *(And it is the exact complement of § 1.2's conclusion: "the sepoys were the makers of their own rebellion.")*
>
> 🎯 **So the answer to Exercise 1 — *why did the sepoys turn to erstwhile rulers?* — has three parts:** ⭐ **(a) Legitimacy** — a rebellion in the emperor's name is a restoration, not a crime; NCERT says so directly of Delhi. **(b) Organisation** — *"leadership and organisation were required"*, and only the old courts had experience of administration, revenue and command. **(c) A shared grievance and a shared past** — these were people the British had displaced, and their courts were *"a symbol of all that had been lost"* (§ 3.3). ⭐ **Then add the twist: the initiative ran UPWARD, not downward.**

**And crucially, NCERT insists the leadership was not confined to the courts:**

> *"**NOT EVERYWHERE were the leaders people of the court — ranis, rajas, nawabs and taluqdars.** Often the message of rebellion was carried by **ORDINARY MEN AND WOMEN** and in places by **RELIGIOUS MEN** too. From **Meerut**, there were reports that **a FAKIR had appeared riding on an ELEPHANT** and that the sepoys were **visiting him frequently**. In **Lucknow**, after the annexation of Awadh, there were **many religious leaders and SELF-STYLED PROPHETS who preached the destruction of British rule.**"*

> *"Elsewhere, **local leaders emerged**, urging **peasants, zamindars and tribals** to revolt. **SHAH MAL** mobilised the villagers of **pargana Barout in Uttar Pradesh**; **GONOO**, a **tribal cultivator of Singhbhum in Chotanagpur**, became a rebel leader of **the KOL tribals** of the region."*

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Learn Gonoo — one line in the textbook, and exactly the kind of name a Prelims setter likes: *a tribal cultivator of Singhbhum in Chotanagpur, leader of the Kol tribals*.**

#### 🗡️ The box: **Two rebels of 1857** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**This box is set apart from the main text and is very heavily examinable. Learn both men properly.**

```
          THEY HAD LOST" **

```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Shah Mal is the most useful single example in the chapter, and here is why.** He shows the revolt was **not only** about religion or dynasty. ⭐ **His villages were PROSPEROUS and they rebelled anyway** — over **a high, inflexible revenue demand** and **land passing to traders and moneylenders**. **That is [Theme 9](09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md)'s argument appearing inside Theme 10.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the detail to quote is the English officer's bungalow turned into "a hall of justice."** *A symbol of alien authority converted into an instrument of local justice, by a man his neighbours called Raja.* **The rebels were not only destroying — they were substituting.** *(That connects directly to § 3.3, "the search for alternative power", and to Theme 11's **Satara *prati sarkar*** of 1943 — a parallel government in a district, eighty-six years later.)*

**② MAULVI AHMADULLAH SHAH — the preacher**

```

   * "EDUCATED IN ** HYDERABAD **", became a preacher when young
   * ** IN 1856 ** -- "moving from village to village preaching
     ** JEHAD (religious war) ** against the British and urging
     people to rebel"
       ** i.e. A YEAR BEFORE THE MUTINY. **
   * travelled "in a ** PALANQUIN, with DRUMBEATERS IN FRONT **

       -> hence ** "DANKA SHAH -- the maulvi with the drum
          (danka)" **
   * "British officials ** PANICKED ** as THOUSANDS began
     following the maulvi and many Muslims began seeing him
     as ** AN INSPIRED PROPHET **"
   * ** 1856 ** -- reaching LUCKNOW, "he was ** STOPPED BY THE
     POLICE ** from preaching in the city"
   * ** 1857 ** -- "he was ** JAILED IN FAIZABAD **"
   * ON RELEASE -- ** "he was ELECTED by the mutinous
     22nd NATIVE INFANTRY as their leader" **
   * fought in ** THE FAMOUS BATTLE OF CHINHAT **, "in which
     the British forces under ** HENRY LAWRENCE ** were
     defeated"
   * "Many people in fact believed that he was ** INVINCIBLE,
     had MAGICAL POWERS, and COULD NOT BE KILLED by the
     British. ** ** IT WAS THIS BELIEF THAT PARTLY FORMED THE
     BASIS OF HIS AUTHORITY. ** "
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three examinable points in this box.**
> **(1) The chronology.** He was preaching **jehad in 1856**, stopped by police in Lucknow **in 1856**, and jailed in **Faizabad in 1857** — ⭐ **all BEFORE the Meerut mutiny.** **The ground was being prepared, and the British knew enough to jail him and not enough to prevent anything.**
> **(2) He was ELECTED.** *"He was **elected** by the mutinous 22nd Native Infantry as their leader."* ⭐ **Put this beside the panchayats of § 1.2 and beside the princes who were "forced" and "given no choice" — the sepoys chose their own leaders, and sometimes by vote.**
> **(3) The last sentence is the chapter's method in miniature:** *"Many people believed he was invincible… **It was this belief that PARTLY FORMED THE BASIS OF HIS AUTHORITY.**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT is not asking whether he was invincible. It is telling you that the BELIEF was itself a political fact that produced real power.** *That is precisely the rule it is about to state for rumours in § 1.5 — and it is the single most portable idea in the chapter.*
>
> 💬 **NCERT's Discuss box here — *"explain the similarities and differences you notice in the ways in which leaders emerged during the revolt. For any two leaders, discuss why ordinary people were drawn to them."*** → ✅ **Similarity:** in almost every case **the initiative came from below** — Bahadur Shah surrounded, Nana Sahib given no choice, the Rani forced by popular pressure, Birjis Qadr hailed, the Maulvi elected. ✅ **Difference:** ⭐ **the SOURCE of authority differs completely.** The court leaders had **inherited legitimacy** and had to be pushed into using it; **Shah Mal** built authority from **kinship (a clan across 84 villages), local grievance and demonstrated capability** — an intelligence network, a hall of justice; **Ahmadullah Shah** built it from **religious charisma and a reputation for invulnerability**. ✅ **Why people were drawn to them (pick two):** **Shah Mal** — because he addressed **the injury they actually felt** (revenue, and land lost to outsiders) and because he **delivered something**: dispossessed proprietors got their land back and disputes were settled in his hall. **Ahmadullah Shah** — because he offered **a framework of meaning** (the war as a defence of faith), **visible spectacle** (the palanquin and the drum), and **the belief that he could not be killed**, which made following him feel less like suicide.

### 🗣️ 1.4 Rumours and prophecies ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**The four things circulating in North India in early 1857 — learn all four:**

```
   1. ** THE GREASED CARTRIDGES **
      Bullets "coated with THE FAT OF COWS AND PIGS", which
      the sepoys had to BITE -- "biting those bullets would
      CORRUPT THEIR CASTE AND RELIGION".
      The cartridges "of the ENFIELD RIFLES which had just
      been given to them".
      ** "The British tried to explain to the sepoys that this
         was not the case BUT THE RUMOUR ... SPREAD LIKE
         WILDFIRE across the sepoy lines of North India." **

   2. ** THE BONE DUST IN THE ATTA **
      "the British government had hatched ** A GIGANTIC
      CONSPIRACY to destroy the caste and religion of Hindus
      and Muslims **" -- by mixing ** THE BONE DUST OF COWS
      AND PIGS INTO THE FLOUR ** sold in the market.
      "In towns and cantonments, sepoys and the common people
       ** REFUSED TO TOUCH THE ATTA. ** "

   3. ** THE FEAR OF CONVERSION **
      "There was fear and suspicion that ** THE BRITISH WANTED
      TO CONVERT INDIANS TO CHRISTIANITY. ** Panic spread fast.
      British officers tried to allay their fears, ** BUT IN
      VAIN. ** "

   4. ** THE PROPHECY OF THE PLASSEY CENTENARY **
      "the prophecy that ** BRITISH RULE WOULD COME TO AN END
      ON THE CENTENARY OF THE BATTLE OF PLASSEY, ON
      23 JUNE 1857. ** "

   AND, SEPARATELY, NOT A RUMOUR AT ALL:
   ** THE CHAPATTIS **
```

#### 🔍 The one rumour whose origin can be traced — the Dum Dum incident ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"**Captain Wright, commandant of the Rifle Instruction Depot**, reported that in **the third week of January 1857** a **'low-caste' khalasi who worked in the magazine at DUM DUM** had asked **a Brahmin sepoy** for **a drink of water from his lota**. The sepoy had **refused**, saying that the **'lower caste's' touch would defile the lota**. The khalasi had reportedly retorted: **'YOU WILL SOON LOSE YOUR CASTE, AS ERE LONG YOU WILL HAVE TO BITE CARTRIDGES COVERED WITH THE FAT OF COWS AND PIGS.'**"*
>
> ⭐ *"**We do not know the veracity of the report**, but once this rumour started **no amount of assurances from British officers could stop its circulation and the fear it spread.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is a superb little story and there are three separate things to notice.**
> **(1) It begins with a caste insult INSIDE Indian society, not with the British.** A Brahmin refuses water to a low-caste worker; the worker's revenge is a prophecy of defilement. ⭐ **The most powerful anti-colonial rumour of the century was born out of an argument about a water pot.**
> **(2) NCERT flags its own evidence.** *"We do not know the veracity of the report"* — because the only source is **Captain Wright**, a British officer, reporting what he had heard. ⭐ **Even the origin story of the rumour reaches us through the colonial archive.**
> **(3) The decisive sentence is about what happened AFTER.** *"No amount of assurances from British officers could stop its circulation."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Denial did not work — and § 1.5 explains why.**

#### 🫓 The chapattis — and the honesty of admitting ignorance ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"Reports came from various parts of North India that **CHAPATTIS were being distributed FROM VILLAGE TO VILLAGE. A person would come AT NIGHT and give a chapatti to THE WATCHMAN of the village and ask him to MAKE FIVE MORE and distribute to the next village, and so on.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**The meaning and purpose of the distribution of the chapattis was NOT CLEAR and IS NOT CLEAR EVEN TODAY. But there is NO DOUBT that people read it as AN OMEN OF AN UPHEAVAL.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Notice what NCERT does here, because it is a model for your own answer-writing: it refuses to explain.** A century and a half of scholarship, and the honest position is still *"not clear even today."* ⭐ **And then it states what CAN be established: whatever the chapattis meant, people read them as an omen — and that reading is itself the historical fact.**
>
> 💡 **Write it this way in an exam:** *"The chapatti circulation of 1857 has never been satisfactorily explained. Its historical significance lies not in its intended meaning, which is unrecoverable, but in the fact that people across North India interpreted it as a sign of imminent upheaval — evidence of a population already expecting one."* ⭐ **Admitting the limit of the evidence, and then extracting what survives it, is exactly what an examiner is looking for.**
>
> ⚠️ **Do not invent a theory about the chapattis.** Plenty circulate; NCERT deliberately declines all of them, and so should you.

### 🧠 1.5 Why did people believe in the rumours? ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**The rule NCERT states here is the most portable idea in the chapter. Learn it verbatim.**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**We CANNOT understand the power of rumours and prophecies in history BY CHECKING WHETHER THEY ARE FACTUALLY CORRECT OR NOT. We need to see WHAT THEY REFLECT ABOUT THE MINDS OF PEOPLE WHO BELIEVED THEM — their fears and apprehensions, their faiths and convictions. RUMOURS CIRCULATE ONLY WHEN THEY RESONATE WITH THE DEEPER FEARS AND SUSPICIONS OF PEOPLE.**"*

> 🎯 **Why this is a first-class analytical tool.** Ask "was the cartridge really greased with cow and pig fat?" and you get a fact-check. **Ask instead "why was this instantly believed by tens of thousands of men who had served loyally for decades?" and you get a history of the previous thirty years.** ⭐ **NCERT then supplies exactly that history.**

**The context that made the rumours credible — three strands ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐:**

```
   WHY THE RUMOURS RANG TRUE
   -- "the policies the British pursued FROM THE LATE 1820s"

   STRAND 1 -- ** "REFORMING" INDIAN SOCIETY **
     under ** LORD WILLIAM BENTINCK **, policies "aimed at
     'REFORMING' Indian society by introducing ** WESTERN
     EDUCATION, WESTERN IDEAS AND WESTERN INSTITUTIONS **"
       * English-medium ** SCHOOLS, COLLEGES AND UNIVERSITIES **
         teaching "Western sciences and the liberal arts"
       * laws to ** ABOLISH SATI (1829) **
       * laws to ** PERMIT THE REMARRIAGE OF HINDU WIDOWS **

   STRAND 2 -- ** ANNEXATION **
     "On a variety of pleas, like ** MISGOVERNMENT ** and
     ** THE REFUSAL TO RECOGNISE ADOPTION **, the British
     annexed not only ** AWADH **, but many other kingdoms
     and principalities like ** JHANSI AND SATARA **."
     And then imposed "their own system of administration,
     their own laws and their own methods of ** LAND
     SETTLEMENT AND LAND REVENUE COLLECTION **".

   STRAND 3 -- ** THE MISSIONARIES **
     "This perception was ** AGGRAVATED BY THE ACTIVITIES OF
     CHRISTIAN MISSIONARIES. ** "

   THE CUMULATIVE EFFECT -- and this is the sentence to quote:
   ** "It seemed to the people that ALL THAT THEY CHERISHED AND
      HELD SACRED -- FROM KINGS AND SOCIO-RELIGIOUS CUSTOMS TO
      PATTERNS OF LANDHOLDING AND REVENUE PAYMENT -- WAS BEING
      DESTROYED AND REPLACED BY A SYSTEM THAT WAS MORE
      IMPERSONAL, ALIEN AND OPPRESSIVE."
      "In such a situation of uncertainty, RUMOURS SPREAD WITH
       REMARKABLE SWIFTNESS." **
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The three adjectives — *impersonal, alien and oppressive* — are worth memorising as a set**, because they answer *why* rather than *what*. ⭐ **And note that "impersonal" comes first.** *(§ 2.3 makes this concrete: the taluqdar had been an oppressor **and** a *"generous father figure"* who was *"often considerate in times of need"*; the British revenue officer was neither. **A system can be resented for being fairer and colder at the same time.**)*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Here is the crucial connection to make in an answer.** A government that had **abolished sati, legalised widow remarriage, funded missionary-adjacent education, and annexed kingdom after kingdom** had already shown, over thirty years, that **it was willing to reach into religion and into inheritance.** ⭐ **So when someone said "they have put cow and pig fat on the cartridges", it did not sound outlandish — it sounded consistent.** **The rumour was believed because it fitted the pattern of everything else that had happened.** *That single sentence answers NCERT Exercise 3 ("the extent to which religious beliefs shaped the events of 1857") better than a list of religious grievances.*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐ **And keep NCERT's balance.** It does **not** say the reforms were bad, nor that the rebels were right about the cartridges. It says the reforms **created a plausibility structure** in which such a rumour became instantly credible. **Describing that mechanism without endorsing either side is the mature answer.**

## 2️⃣ Awadh in Revolt

> **Why NCERT devotes a whole section to one kingdom:** *"To explore the basis of the revolt of 1857 in some detail, let us look at **AWADH — one of the major centres where the drama of 1857 unfolded**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the payoff is § 2.3's opening line, which is the answer to NCERT Exercise 6: *"A CHAIN OF GRIEVANCES IN AWADH LINKED PRINCE, TALUQDAR, PEASANT AND SEPOY."***

### 🍒 2.1 "A cherry that will drop into our mouth one day" ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"In **1851** Governor General **Lord Dalhousie** described the kingdom of Awadh as **'a cherry that will drop into our mouth one day'**. **Five years later, in 1856, the kingdom was formally annexed** to the British Empire."*

**The conquest in stages — and the Subsidiary Alliance is the first stage:**

> *"**The Subsidiary Alliance had been imposed on Awadh in 1801.** By the terms of this alliance the Nawab had to **(a) DISBAND HIS MILITARY FORCE**, **(b) ALLOW THE BRITISH TO POSITION THEIR TROOPS within the kingdom**, and **(c) ACT IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE ADVICE OF THE BRITISH RESIDENT** who was now to be attached to the court."*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**DEPRIVED OF HIS ARMED FORCES, the Nawab became INCREASINGLY DEPENDENT ON THE BRITISH to maintain law and order within the kingdom. He could no longer assert control over the rebellious chiefs and taluqdars.**"*

> 🔑 **NCERT's box — THE SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE — learn all four terms, this is standard Prelims:**
>
> *"A system **devised by Lord Wellesley in 1798**. All those who entered into such an alliance with the British had to accept certain terms and conditions:*
> **(a)** *The British would be responsible for **protecting their ally from EXTERNAL AND INTERNAL threats** to their power.*
> **(b)** *In the territory of the ally, **a British armed contingent would be stationed**.*
> **(c)** *The ally would have to **provide the resources for maintaining this contingent**.*
> **(d)** *The ally could enter into **agreements with other rulers or engage in warfare ONLY WITH THE PERMISSION of the British**."*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note the two dates and do not confuse them: the SYSTEM was devised by Wellesley in 1798; it was IMPOSED ON AWADH in 1801.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read clause (a) again — it is the trap that is the whole point.** The British undertook to protect the ally **from INTERNAL threats too.** ⭐ **That is the clause that destroys a state**: once the Nawab's own army is disbanded and the British handle internal order, **he can no longer discipline his own taluqdars**, so his authority hollows out — and then, in 1856, **he can be deposed on the ground that the kingdom is "misgoverned."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The alliance manufactured the misgovernment that justified the annexation.** *Say that in an answer and you have understood the Subsidiary Alliance rather than merely listed it.*
>
> 🔑 **And NCERT's gloss on RESIDENT:** *"the designation of **a representative of the Governor General who lived in a state which was NOT under direct British rule**."*

**Why the British wanted Awadh specifically — three reasons, all economic or strategic:**

| Reason | NCERT's words |
|---|---|
| **Soil** | *"they felt that the soil there was **good for producing INDIGO AND COTTON**"* |
| **Position** | *"the region was **ideally located to be developed into THE PRINCIPAL MARKET OF UPPER INDIA**"* |
| **Completion of the map** | *"by the early 1850s… **all the major areas of India had been conquered: the MARATHA LANDS, the DOAB, the CARNATIC, the PUNJAB and BENGAL.** The takeover of Awadh in 1856 was expected to **complete a process of territorial annexation that had begun with the conquest of Bengal almost a century earlier**"* |

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Learn the list of five prior conquests — Maratha lands, Doab, Carnatic, Punjab, Bengal — it is a compact revision of the whole conquest of India, and "almost a century earlier" points back to Plassey (1757), which is also the prophecy's anchor in § 1.4.**

### 💔 2.2 "The life was gone out of the body" ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"Lord Dalhousie's annexations created disaffection in all the areas… **but NOWHERE MORE SO than in the kingdom of Awadh in the heart of North India.** Here, **Nawab WAJID ALI SHAH was DETHRONED AND EXILED TO CALCUTTA on the plea that the region was BEING MISGOVERNED.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**The British government also WRONGLY ASSUMED that Wajid Ali Shah was AN UNPOPULAR RULER. ON THE CONTRARY, HE WAS WIDELY LOVED**, and when he left his beloved Lucknow, **there were many who FOLLOWED HIM ALL THE WAY TO KANPUR singing songs of lament.**"*

**The contemporary witness, and the folk song:**

> *"**'The life was gone out of the body, and the body of this town had been left lifeless… there was NO STREET OR MARKET AND HOUSE WHICH DID NOT WAIL OUT THE CRY OF AGONY IN SEPARATION OF JAN-I-ALAM.'**"* And one folk song bemoaned that *"**the honourable English came and took the country**"* — ***"Angrez Bahadur ain, mulk lai linho."***

**Source 3 — *The Nawab has left*:**

> *"**Noble and peasant all wept together / and all the world wept and wailed. / Alas! The chief has bidden adieu to / his country and gone abroad.**"*

> 💬 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's question — *"Read the entire section and discuss why people mourned the departure of Wajid Ali Shah."*** → **The answer has TWO layers, and you must give both.**
>
> ✅ **(1) Emotional / cultural.** He was **widely loved**, not the misgoverning idler the British claimed; people followed him to Kanpur **singing songs of lament**; the songs speak of **"the cry of agony in separation"** and record that ***"noble and peasant ALL WEPT TOGETHER"*** — ⭐ **a phrase that matters, because it shows the grief crossed class lines.**
>
> ✅ **(2) Material — and NCERT insists on this.** *"This emotional upheaval was **AGGRAVATED BY IMMEDIATE MATERIAL LOSSES. The removal of the Nawab led to THE DISSOLUTION OF THE COURT AND ITS CULTURE.** Thus a whole range of people — **MUSICIANS, DANCERS, POETS, ARTISANS, COOKS, RETAINERS, ADMINISTRATIVE OFFICIALS** and so on — **LOST THEIR LIVELIHOOD.**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Learn that list of seven occupations.** **A court was not a household; it was an economy.** *(This is the strongest possible answer to anyone who thinks 1857 in Awadh was mere dynastic sentiment: **removing one man put thousands of people out of work.**)*

#### 🏰 The taluqdars — dispossessed twice ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"The countryside of Awadh was **dotted with the ESTATES AND FORTS of taluqdars** who for **many generations had controlled land and power** in the countryside. Before the coming of the British, taluqdars **maintained ARMED RETAINERS, built FORTS, and enjoyed A DEGREE OF AUTONOMY**, as long as they **accepted the suzerainty of the Nawab and paid the revenue of their taluqs**. ⭐ **Some of the bigger taluqdars had as many as 12,000 FOOT-SOLDIERS and even the smaller ones had about 200.**"*
>
> *"The British were **unwilling to tolerate the power of the taluqdars**. **Immediately after the annexation, the taluqdars were DISARMED and their FORTS DESTROYED.**"*

**And then the second blow — the Summary Settlement of 1856:**

> *"the first British revenue settlement, known as **the SUMMARY SETTLEMENT OF 1856**, was based on the assumption that **the taluqdars were INTERLOPERS WITH NO PERMANENT STAKES IN LAND: they had established their hold over land THROUGH FORCE AND FRAUD.** The Summary Settlement proceeded to **remove the taluqdars wherever possible.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**Figures show that in pre-British times, taluqdars had held 67 PER CENT of the total number of villages in Awadh; by the Summary Settlement this number had come down to 38 PER CENT. The taluqdars of SOUTHERN AWADH were the hardest hit and some LOST MORE THAN HALF of the total number of villages they had previously held.**"*

> 🔴 **Memorise: 12,000 / 200 foot-soldiers · 67% → 38% · southern Awadh worst · 30–70% revenue increase.** ⭐ **These four figures will carry a whole answer on Awadh.**

#### 🌾 And the peasant did not benefit — the paragraph that makes the section ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"British land revenue officers believed that **by removing taluqdars they would be able to settle the land with THE ACTUAL OWNERS OF THE SOIL** and thus **REDUCE THE LEVEL OF EXPLOITATION OF PEASANTS while INCREASING REVENUE RETURNS for the state**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BUT THIS DID NOT HAPPEN IN PRACTICE: REVENUE FLOWS FOR THE STATE INCREASED BUT THE BURDEN OF DEMAND ON THE PEASANTS DID NOT DECLINE.** Officials soon found that **large areas of Awadh were actually HEAVILY OVERASSESSED: the increase of revenue demand in some places was FROM 30 TO 70 PER CENT.** **Thus NEITHER TALUQDARS NOR PEASANTS had any reasons to be happy with the annexation.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the analytical heart of Section 2 and it is exactly the same finding as [Theme 9](09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) § 3.3.** A reform justified as **anti-exploitation** delivered **more revenue to the state and no relief to the cultivator.** ⭐ **The intermediary was removed and the burden stayed.** *(Compare the Bombay Deccan, where ryotwari removed the zamindar and delivered the peasant to the sahukar instead.)*

**And then the deepest point — what was lost when the taluqdar fell ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐:**

> *"**The dispossession of taluqdars meant THE BREAKDOWN OF AN ENTIRE SOCIAL ORDER. The ties of LOYALTY AND PATRONAGE that had bound the peasant to the taluqdar were DISRUPTED.** In pre-British times, **the taluqdars were OPPRESSORS but many of them also appeared to be GENEROUS FATHER FIGURES: they exacted a variety of dues from the peasant BUT WERE OFTEN CONSIDERATE IN TIMES OF NEED.** Now, under the British, the peasant was **directly exposed to OVERASSESSMENT of revenue and INFLEXIBLE METHODS of collection.** ⭐ **There was NO LONGER ANY GUARANTEE that in times of hardship or crop failure the revenue demand would be REDUCED OR COLLECTION POSTPONED; or that in times of festivities the peasant would get THE LOAN AND SUPPORT that the taluqdar had earlier provided.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Oppressors BUT ALSO generous father figures" is one of the most sophisticated sentences in Class 12, and it is easy to mis-handle.** NCERT is **not** romanticising the taluqdar — it says plainly that he was an oppressor. ⭐ **The point is that his was an oppression WITH A CUSHION: worse in ordinary years, survivable in bad ones.** **The British system was more uniform, more legal, and had no give in it at all.**
>
> 💡 **This is the "moral economy" argument again — the same one that explains the Deccan riots.** *(There the grievance was that moneylenders had abandoned the customary norm that **interest could not exceed the principal**. Here it is that the state abandoned the customary norm that **a demand bends in a bad harvest**.)* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In both chapters the trigger for revolt is not hardship as such but THE WITHDRAWAL OF CUSTOMARY FLEXIBILITY.** *If you can state that as a general principle and support it from both chapters, you are writing at the level this book is aiming at.*

**Who actually did the fighting in Awadh:**

> *"In areas like Awadh where resistance during 1857 was **intense and long lasting**, the fighting was carried out by **TALUQDARS AND THEIR PEASANTS**. Many of these taluqdars were **loyal to the Nawab of Awadh**, and they joined **BEGUM HAZRAT MAHAL (the wife of the Nawab) in Lucknow** to fight the British; **some even REMAINED WITH HER IN DEFEAT.**"*

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Some even remained with her in defeat" is doing quiet work — it is evidence that this was loyalty, not opportunism.**

#### 📜 Source 4 — *What taluqdars thought* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Hanwant Singh, the Raja of Kalakankar, near Rae Bareli — who had sheltered a British officer and conveyed him to safety, speaking as he took leave of him:**

> *"**Sahib, your countrymen came into this country and DROVE OUT OUR KING. You sent your officers round the districts to EXAMINE THE TITLES TO THE ESTATES. AT ONE BLOW YOU TOOK FROM ME LANDS WHICH FROM TIME IMMEMORIAL HAD BEEN IN MY FAMILY. I SUBMITTED. Suddenly misfortune fell upon you. THE PEOPLE OF THE LAND ROSE AGAINST YOU. You came to me WHOM YOU HAD DESPOILED. I HAVE SAVED YOU. BUT NOW — now I march at the head of my retainers to LUCKNOW to try and DRIVE YOU FROM THE COUNTRY.**"*

> 💬 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT asks three questions. This Source repays close reading — it may be the finest single document in the chapter.**
>
> **(1) *What does this excerpt tell you about the attitude of the taluqdars?*** → ✅ **That the grievance was specific, legal and personal — and entirely compatible with honour toward an individual enemy.** ⭐ Note the precise sequence he recites: **the king was driven out** → **officers came to "examine the titles to the estates"** *(that is the Summary Settlement)* → **"at one blow you took from me lands which from time immemorial had been in my family."** ✅ And note ***"I SUBMITTED"*** — **he did not rebel when it happened.** He rebelled only when the British were already losing. ⭐ **That is not fanaticism; it is a calculation of opportunity layered on a real injury.**
>
> **(2) *Who did Hanwant Singh mean by the people of the land?*** → ✅ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the important one.** He says *"THE PEOPLE of the land rose against you"* — **not "I rose", not "the taluqdars rose."** He is describing **a general, popular rising** of which he is a late joiner, and he distinguishes it from himself. ✅ **In context it means the ordinary inhabitants of Awadh — peasants, villagers, sepoys drawn from those villages — i.e. the "three-fourths of the adult male population" that Forsyth would later estimate (§ 4).** ⭐ **A taluqdar's own testimony that the rising was popular is far stronger evidence than a nationalist's assertion of it.**
>
> **(3) *What reason does Hanwant Singh give for the anger of the people?*** → ✅ **Dispossession by legal process.** Not religion, not the cartridge — **"you sent your officers… to examine the titles"** and **"at one blow you took from me lands."** ⭐ **The instrument of grievance is a revenue survey.** *(Set this beside **Source 6**, the sepoys' *arzi*, which gives an almost purely religious account of the same war — NCERT explicitly asks you to compare them. **Two rebels, two completely different explanations of why they were fighting. Both are true, and neither is the whole.**)*

#### 🎖️ The sepoys — and what changed between the 1820s and the 1840s ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"The grievances of the peasants were **carried over into the sepoy lines** since **a vast majority of the sepoys were recruited from the villages of Awadh.** For decades the sepoys had complained of **LOW LEVELS OF PAY and THE DIFFICULTY OF GETTING LEAVE.** By the 1850s there were **other reasons** for their discontent."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That last line closes a loop opened in § 1.4, and it is the best answer to "why couldn't the British just explain about the cartridges?"** ⭐ **Because an explanation only works if the explainer is trusted — and by the 1840s he was not.** In the 1820s an officer who wrestled with his men and spoke fluent Hindustani could have killed the rumour in an afternoon. **By 1857 the same words came from a man who had been abusing them for a decade.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The cartridge crisis was not a failure of communication; it was a failure of relationship, and the rumour merely found the crack.**
>
> 💡 **Note also the phrase *"disciplinarian and father figure rolled into one"* — it is the SAME formula NCERT used for the taluqdar (*"oppressors but… generous father figures"*).** ⭐ **The same authority-with-a-cushion was dissolving simultaneously in the village AND in the regiment, and the same men experienced both.** *That parallel is worth pointing out; very few candidates will.*

**And the two-way traffic between barracks and village ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐:**

> *"**Close links existed between the sepoys and the RURAL WORLD of North India.** The large majority of the sepoys of the **Bengal Army** were recruited from **the villages of AWADH and EASTERN UTTAR PRADESH.** **Many of them were BRAHMINS or from the 'UPPER' CASTES.** ⭐ **Awadh was, in fact, called 'THE NURSERY OF THE BENGAL ARMY'.**"*
>
> *"**The changes that the families of the sepoys saw around them and the threats they perceived were quickly transmitted to the sepoy lines. IN TURN, the fears of the sepoys about the new cartridge, their grievances about leave, their grouse about the increasing misbehaviour and racial abuse on the part of their white officers were COMMUNICATED BACK TO THE VILLAGES.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**When the sepoys defied their superior officers and took up arms they were joined VERY SWIFTLY by their brethren in the villages. Everywhere, PEASANTS POURED INTO TOWNS and joined the soldiers and the ordinary people of the towns in COLLECTIVE ACTS OF REBELLION.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The nursery of the Bengal Army" is the phrase that makes the whole of Section 2 cohere, and it is a certain exam item.** The British recruited disproportionately from **exactly the province they had most recently dispossessed** — and those recruits were **Brahmins and upper castes**, i.e. precisely the men most exposed to the caste-pollution fear of the cartridge. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Awadh's peasants wore the uniform of the army that held Awadh down. The annexation of 1856 armed its own opposition.**
>
> 🎯 **Use the two-way channel explicitly when answering Exercise 6:** *"the link ran in both directions — village grievance travelled into the lines, and the sepoys' fear of the cartridge travelled back out into the villages — so that when the mutiny came, the countryside was already primed and joined 'very swiftly'."*

## 3️⃣ What the Rebels Wanted

### ⚠️ The source problem, stated before any answer is attempted ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**NCERT opens this section with a warning rather than a claim, and the warning is the most quoted passage in the chapter:**

> *"**As VICTORS, the British recorded their own trials and tribulations as well as their heroism. ### 🤝 3.1 The vision of unity ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**This is NCERT Exercise 4 — *"What were the measures taken to ensure unity among the rebels?"***

> *"The rebel proclamations in 1857 **repeatedly appealed to ALL SECTIONS OF THE POPULATION, IRRESPECTIVE OF THEIR CASTE AND CREED.** Many of the proclamations were **issued by MUSLIM PRINCES or in their names but EVEN THESE TOOK CARE TO ADDRESS THE SENTIMENTS OF HINDUS.** **The rebellion was seen as a war in which BOTH HINDUS AND MUSLIMS HAD EQUALLY TO LOSE OR GAIN.** The **ishtahars HARKED BACK TO THE PRE-BRITISH HINDU-MUSLIM PAST and GLORIFIED THE COEXISTENCE OF DIFFERENT COMMUNITIES UNDER THE MUGHAL EMPIRE.**"*

**The two pieces of hard evidence — memorise both:**

| Evidence | Detail |
|---|---|
| **① The proclamation in Bahadur Shah's name** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"appealed to the people to **join the fight under THE STANDARDS OF BOTH MUHAMMAD AND MAHAVIR**"* |
| **② The Bareilly episode** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"It was remarkable that during the uprising **religious divisions between Hindus and Muslims were HARDLY NOTICEABLE despite BRITISH ATTEMPTS TO CREATE SUCH DIVISIONS.** In **BAREILLY** in western Uttar Pradesh, in **DECEMBER 1857**, the British **spent Rs 50,000 to INCITE THE HINDU POPULATION AGAINST THE MUSLIMS. THE ATTEMPT FAILED.**"* |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Bareilly fact is the single best piece of evidence in the whole NCERT series for Hindu–Muslim unity in 1857, and here is why it is better than any assertion of unity could be.** ⭐ **It is a NEGATIVE proof, and it comes from the British side.** The colonial government **spent a specific sum (Rs 50,000) in a specific place (Bareilly) at a specific time (December 1857) to break the alliance — and could not.** **Money was budgeted, an operation was run, and it failed.** *An adversary's failed attempt to divide people is far stronger evidence of their unity than the people's own claims to be united.* **Deploy it exactly that way.**
>
> 💡 **And note the date: DECEMBER 1857 — seven months in.** The unity had already survived the whole first phase of the war before the British tried to buy it apart.
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Under the standards of both Muhammad and Mahavir"** is also worth a second look. *Mahavir* here does the work of a shared Indic symbol alongside the Prophet. **A proclamation issued in a Mughal emperor's name deliberately reaching for a non-Muslim standard is a designed act of coalition-building, not an accident of rhetoric.**

### 📜 Source 5 — The Azamgarh Proclamation, 25 August 1857 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **NCERT's own framing:** *"**This is ONE OF THE MAIN SOURCES OF OUR KNOWLEDGE ABOUT WHAT THE REBELS WANTED.**"*

**This is the most important document in the chapter. It is organised into FIVE SECTIONS BY SOCIAL GROUP — and that structure is itself the answer to NCERT Exercise 7.**

**The preamble sets the terms:**

> *"It is well known to all, that in this age **the people of Hindostan, BOTH HINDOOS AND MOHAMMEDANS, are being ruined under the tyranny and the oppression of the infidel and treacherous English**… Several of the **Hindoo and Mussalman Chiefs**, who have long since quitted their homes **for the preservation of their religion**… have presented themselves to me, and taken part in **the reigning Indian crusade**… and be it known to all, that **the ancient works, both of the Hindoos and Mohammedans, the writings of miracle workers, and the calculation of the astrologers, pundits, … ALL AGREE IN ASSERTING THAT THE ENGLISH WILL NO LONGER HAVE ANY FOOTING IN INDIA**… otherwise **if this golden opportunity slips away, they will have to repent for their folly**."*

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note two devices already at work: the grievance is stated jointly for both communities in the first line, and the promise of victory is underwritten by BOTH Hindu and Muslim prophecy — "the ancient works, both of the Hindoos and Mohammedans."** *(Which is § 1.4's prophecy of the Plassey centenary, deployed as a recruiting argument.)*

Notice the LANGUAGE in which the proclamation is formulated and the VARIETY OF SENTIMENTS it appeals to."*** → **Here is the full answer, and it has three layers.**
>
> ✅ **(1) The issues, by group:** ⭐ **land revenue and legal humiliation** (zamindars); ⭐ **commercial monopoly and the reduction of Indians to "the trade of trifles"** (merchants); ⭐ **a racial bar on promotion** (public servants); ⭐ **deindustrialisation by imported manufactures** (artisans); ⭐ **threat to both religions** (pundits and fakirs).
>
> ✅ **(2) The technique — and this is the mark-winning observation.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The proclamation does not offer one grievance to everybody. It offers EACH GROUP ITS OWN GRIEVANCE AND ITS OWN REWARD** — light *jumas* and absolute rule to the zamindar, open trade to the merchant, Rs 200–300 a month to the clerk, guaranteed employment to the artisan, a holy war to the priest. **It is a coalition document, assembled section by section.** *(Note how modern that is: it is structured exactly like a party manifesto with a chapter per constituency.)*
>
> ✅ **(3) The language.** Religious and moral throughout — *"infidel and treacherous English"*, *"the reigning Indian crusade"*, *"holy war"* — **but the CONTENT of every section except the fifth is material and secular**: revenue rates, court fees, stamp duty, tolls, salaries, employment. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Religion is the IDIOM; land, trade, jobs and craft are the SUBSTANCE.** *That single distinction is the best possible answer to NCERT Exercise 3 on how far religious belief shaped 1857.*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And now the critical reading NCERT invites in § 3.2 — do not miss it.** Look at **who is NOT in the list.** There is no section addressed to **the peasant, the ryot, the landless labourer.** ⭐ **Worse: Section I promises the zamindar "ABSOLUTE RULE IN HIS OWN ZEMINDARY", and the only mention of a ryot in the whole document is as "a common ryot, a maid servant, or a slave" whose lawsuit disgraces a respectable man.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The proclamation's programme is the restoration of a hierarchy — and yet the people actually rebelling in the villages were burning the account books of that hierarchy's creditors.** *That gap between the leadership's manifesto and the rank and file's actions is § 3.2's whole argument, and it is the single most sophisticated point available on "what the rebels wanted."*

### 📜 Source 6 — *What the sepoys thought* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**An *arzi* (petition) of rebel sepoys — one of the very few surviving:**

> *"**A century ago the British arrived in Hindostan** and gradually entertained troops in their service, and became masters of every state. **Our forefathers have always served them, and we also entered their service** … By the mercy of God and with our assistance **the British also conquered every place they liked, in which THOUSANDS OF US, HINDOSTANI MEN, WERE SACRIFICED, BUT WE NEVER MADE ANY EXCUSES OR PRETENCES NOR REVOLTED** …*
>
> *But in the year eighteen fifty seven the British issued an order that **new cartridges and muskets** … in the former of which **the fats of cows and pigs were mixed**; and also that **attah of wheat MIXED WITH POWDERED BONES was to be eaten** …*
>
> *They gave these cartridges to **the sowars (mounted soldiers) of the 3rd Light Cavalry**, and **ordered them to bite them**; the troopers **objected**… upon this the British officers **paraded the men of the 3 Regiments** and having prepared **1,400 English soldiers**, and other Battalions of European troops and Horse Artillery, **surrounded them**, and **placing SIX GUNS before each of the infantry regiments, loaded the guns with GRAPE** and made **84 NEW TROOPERS PRISONERS, and put them in jail WITH IRONS ON THEM** … **The reason that the sowars… were put into jail was that WE SHOULD BE FRIGHTENED INTO BITING THE NEW CARTRIDGES.***
>
> *On this account **we and all our country-men HAVING UNITED TOGETHER, have fought the British FOR THE PRESERVATION OF OUR FAITH** … **we have fought for two years in order that our faith and religion may not be polluted. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ IF THE RELIGION OF A HINDOO OR MUSSALMAN IS LOST, WHAT REMAINS IN THE WORLD?**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Four things to take from this document, which is the closest thing in the chapter to the sepoys' own voice.**
>
> **(1) It opens with a claim of LOYALTY, not of hatred.** *"Our forefathers have always served them… thousands of us, Hindostani men, were sacrificed, **but we never made any excuses or pretences nor revolted**."* ⭐ **The rhetorical structure is that of a betrayed servant, not a natural enemy** — *we built your empire, and this is what you did to us.* **That framing is far more damaging to the British case than mere hostility would be.**
>
> **(2) It contains a precise military grievance with numbers** — the 3rd Light Cavalry, **1,400 English soldiers**, **six guns loaded with grape** before each regiment, **84 troopers in irons**. ⭐ **And it names the purpose plainly: *"that we should be FRIGHTENED into biting the new cartridges."*** **The sepoys are describing coercion, and the arithmetic of the coercion.**
>
> **(3) The stated cause is religion, and the closing line is one of the great sentences of the period:** *"**If the religion of a Hindoo or Mussalman is lost, what remains in the world?**"* ⭐ **Note that it says Hindu OR Muslim — the joint framing again, from the ranks this time rather than from a prince's proclamation.**
>
> **(4) *"We have fought for TWO YEARS"* dates the document to 1858–59** — this is a petition written **after** the war was effectively lost, which is itself remarkable evidence of persistence.
>
> 💬 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's question — *"Compare the reasons for the mutiny as stated in the arzi with those mentioned by the taluqdar (Source 3)."*** *(NCERT says "Source 3"; the taluqdar's statement is **Hanwant Singh in Source 4** — check the numbering, and answer on Hanwant Singh.)*
>
> ✅ **The comparison is the point of the exercise, and the two accounts barely overlap:**
>
> | | **The sepoys' *arzi* (Source 6)** | **Hanwant Singh, taluqdar (Source 4)** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Cause given** | ⭐ **RELIGION** — greased cartridges, bone dust in the atta, *"that our faith and religion may not be polluted"* | ⭐ **PROPERTY** — *"you sent your officers round the districts to **examine the titles to the estates**… at one blow you took from me lands"* |
> | **Prior relationship** | **Long loyal service** — *"our forefathers have always served them"* | **Sullen submission** — *"I submitted"* |
> | **Trigger to revolt** | **A specific act of coercion** — the parade, the guns, the 84 men in irons | ⭐ **OPPORTUNITY** — *"Suddenly misfortune fell upon you. The people of the land rose against you"* |
> | **Register** | Collective — *"we and all our country-men having united together"* | Intensely personal — *"lands which from time immemorial had been in **my family**"* |
> | **Attitude to the enemy** | Betrayal by a master once served | ⭐ **Honour toward an individual, war against the power** — *"I have saved you. But now… I march"* |
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The conclusion to write:** *"Two rebels in the same province in the same year give almost entirely different accounts of why they were fighting. Neither is lying. 1857 was not one grievance shared by many people but many grievances that briefly pointed in the same direction — which is also why it fragmented so quickly once the British counter-attacked."*

### ⚔️ 3.2 Against the symbols of oppression ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"The proclamations **completely rejected everything associated with British rule or FIRANGI RAJ** as they called it. They **condemned the British for THE ANNEXATIONS they had carried out and THE TREATIES THEY HAD BROKEN.** ⭐ **The British, the rebel leaders said, COULD NOT BE TRUSTED.**"*
>
> *"What **enraged the people** was how **British LAND REVENUE SETTLEMENTS had DISPOSSESSED LANDHOLDERS, both big and small**, and **FOREIGN COMMERCE had driven ARTISANS AND WEAVERS TO RUIN.** Every aspect of British rule was attacked and the firangi accused of **DESTROYING A WAY OF LIFE THAT WAS FAMILIAR AND CHERISHED. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE REBELS WANTED TO RESTORE THAT WORLD.**"*

**And the fear that ran under everything:**

> *"The proclamations expressed the widespread fear that **the British were BENT ON DESTROYING THE CASTE AND RELIGIONS of Hindus and Muslims and CONVERTING THEM TO CHRISTIANITY** — a fear that led people to believe many of the rumours… People were urged to come together and fight **to save THEIR LIVELIHOOD, THEIR FAITH, THEIR HONOUR, THEIR IDENTITY — a fight which was for THE 'GREATER PUBLIC GOOD'.**"*

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Learn that four-item list — *livelihood, faith, honour, identity* — it is a ready-made structure for any answer on rebel motivation, and it deliberately puts LIVELIHOOD first.**

#### 🔀 And then the contradiction NCERT insists on — the most sophisticated paragraph in the chapter ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"As noted earlier, in many places **the rebellion against the British WIDENED into an attack on ALL THOSE WHO WERE SEEN AS ALLIES OF THE BRITISH OR LOCAL OPPRESSORS.** ⭐ **Often the rebels DELIBERATELY SOUGHT TO HUMILIATE THE ELITES OF A CITY. In the villages they BURNT ACCOUNT BOOKS and RANSACKED MONEYLENDERS' HOUSES.** This reflected an attempt to **OVERTURN TRADITIONAL HIERARCHIES, rebel against ALL oppressors.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It presents a glimpse of AN ALTERNATIVE VISION, PERHAPS OF A MORE EGALITARIAN SOCIETY. SUCH VISIONS WERE NOT ARTICULATED IN THE PROCLAMATIONS, WHICH SOUGHT TO UNIFY ALL SOCIAL GROUPS IN THE FIGHT AGAINST FIRANGI RAJ.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the highest-value insight in Theme 10 and almost nobody uses it. Here is how to deploy it.**
>
> **The question "What did the rebels want?" has no single answer, because the rebels were not one group** — and NCERT proves it with evidence rather than assertion. ⭐ **The LEADERS wanted a restoration** (the Azamgarh Proclamation's five sections, § 3.3's return to the eighteenth-century Mughal world). ⭐ **The RANK AND FILE, in their actions, attacked the local hierarchy itself** — burning the bonds of moneylenders who were **also** Indian, also part of the world being "restored." ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note the evidentiary asymmetry: the restorationist vision is in the WRITTEN sources because the literate led; the egalitarian one is visible only in what people BURNED.**
>
> 💡 **Note NCERT's own caution too — *"PERHAPS of a more egalitarian society", "a GLIMPSE".*** ⭐ **It refuses to overclaim, and so should you.** *Burning a moneylender's ledger is not a programme; it is an act with a programme implied in it, and the difference is exactly why the chapter says "glimpse."*
>
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the direct cross-link: burning account books is what the Deccan ryots did in 1875 ([Theme 9](09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) § 3.1), and Shah Mal's villagers did it in 1857 ("plundered the houses of moneylenders and traders", "displaced proprietors took possession of the lands they had lost").** **Three separate episodes, one target: the paper that made the debt enforceable.**

### 🏛️ 3.3 The search for alternative power ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"Once British rule had collapsed, the rebels in places like **DELHI, LUCKNOW and KANPUR** tried to establish **some kind of STRUCTURE OF AUTHORITY AND ADMINISTRATION.** This was, of course, **short-lived** but the attempts show that **the rebel leadership wanted to RESTORE THE PRE-BRITISH WORLD OF THE EIGHTEENTH CENTURY.**"*

```
   WHAT THE REBELS ACTUALLY SET UP

   "The leaders went back to ** THE CULTURE OF THE COURT **"
     * ** APPOINTMENTS were made to various posts **
     * ** arrangements made for THE COLLECTION OF LAND REVENUE **
     * ** and THE PAYMENT OF TROOPS **
     * ** ORDERS ISSUED TO STOP LOOT AND PLUNDER **
     * plans made to fight battles against the British
     * ** CHAINS OF COMMAND were laid down in the army **

   "In all this the rebels ** HARKED BACK TO THE
    EIGHTEENTH-CENTURY MUGHAL WORLD -- A WORLD THAT BECAME
    A SYMBOL OF ALL THAT HAD BEEN LOST. ** "

   THE LIMIT:
   "The administrative structures established by the rebels
    were ** PRIMARILY AIMED AT MEETING THE DEMANDS OF WAR **.
    However, in most cases these structures ** COULD NOT
    SURVIVE THE BRITISH ONSLAUGHT **."

   THE EXCEPTION -- and it is Awadh again:
   ** "But in AWADH, where resistance to the British LASTED
      LONGEST, PLANS OF COUNTER-ATTACK were being drawn up by
      the LUCKNOW COURT and HIERARCHIES OF COMMAND were in
      place AS LATE AS THE LAST MONTHS OF 1857 AND THE EARLY
      PART OF 1858." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The detail that repays attention is *"orders issued to STOP LOOT AND PLUNDER."*** ⭐ **A rebel authority that polices its own supporters is behaving like a state.** It also quietly confirms § 3.2's tension: **the leadership was trying to shut down exactly the popular attacks on property that were the movement's most radical edge.** *(Put the two facts side by side and you can argue that the rebel leadership's restorationism was not only a vision but a constraint — it had to suppress part of its own base.)*
>
> 💡 **And note what the structure was NOT.** It was **not a new political order**. NCERT is explicit: they *"harked back to the eighteenth-century Mughal world."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the honest answer to whether 1857 was a "war of independence" in the modern sense: the rebels wanted the firangi gone, but the future they imagined was a RESTORED PAST, not a new nation.** *(Compare Theme 11: Gandhi's movement produced a Congress, a programme, a constitution-making project. **The difference between 1857 and 1919 is not courage but political imagination — and Section 5.6 shows the twentieth century retrospectively supplying 1857 with the imagination it lacked.**)*

## 4️⃣ Repression

### ⚖️ The law came BEFORE the army — and that is the examinable point ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**NCERT's opening sentence for this section is a concession, and you should quote it:** *"It is clear from all accounts that we have of 1857 that **the British did not have an easy time in putting down the rebellion**."*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SEQUENCE IS THE ANSWER TO EXERCISE 5 ("What steps did the British take to quell the uprising?"). Most candidates write only "military force". NCERT gives FOUR instruments, and the law comes first.**

```
   HOW THE BRITISH PUT DOWN THE REVOLT -- four instruments,
   in NCERT's own order

   INSTRUMENT 1 -- THE LAW, PASSED BEFORE THE TROOPS MOVED
   ------------------------------------------------------
   ** "BEFORE sending out troops to reconquer North India,
      the British passed a series of laws to help them
      quell the insurgency." **

     * a number of Acts, passed in ** MAY AND JUNE 1857 **
     * ** THE WHOLE OF NORTH INDIA PUT UNDER MARTIAL LAW **
     * ** military officers -- AND EVEN ORDINARY BRITONS --
        given the power to TRY AND PUNISH Indians
        suspected of rebellion **
     * "the ordinary processes of ** LAW AND TRIAL WERE
        SUSPENDED ** "
     * ** "it was put out that REBELLION WOULD HAVE ONLY
        ONE PUNISHMENT -- DEATH." **

   INSTRUMENT 2 -- MILITARY FORCE, ON A "GIGANTIC SCALE"
   ----------------------------------------------------
     * reinforcements brought in ** from Britain **
     * ** A TWO-PRONGED ATTACK ON DELHI ** -- because the
       British "like the rebels, ** RECOGNISED THE SYMBOLIC
       VALUE OF DELHI ** "
         one force from ** CALCUTTA ** into North India
         one force from ** THE PUNJAB -- "WHICH WAS LARGELY
           PEACEFUL" **
     * Delhi: attempt began ** EARLY JUNE 1857 **

       -- "the fighting and losses on both sides were heavy",
          because ** rebels from all over North India had come
          to Delhi TO DEFEND THE CAPITAL **

   INSTRUMENT 3 -- VILLAGE-BY-VILLAGE RECONQUEST
   ---------------------------------------------
     * in the Gangetic plain "the progress of British
       reconquest was ** SLOW ** "
     * ** "The forces had to reconquer the area VILLAGE BY
        VILLAGE. The countryside and the people around were
        ENTIRELY HOSTILE." **
     * Awadh brought under control only in ** MARCH 1858 **,
       "after protracted fighting"

   INSTRUMENT 4 -- ** BREAKING THE ALLIANCE ** (the clever one)
   -----------------------------------------------------------
     * where "big landholders and peasants had offered
       ** UNITED RESISTANCE ** ", the British
       ** "tried to BREAK UP THE UNITY by promising to give
          back to the big landholders THEIR ESTATES." **
     * ** "Rebel landholders were DISPOSSESSED and the loyal
        REWARDED." **
     * many landholders "died fighting the British or they
       ** escaped into NEPAL where they died of illness or
       starvation ** "
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Instrument 4 is the one to carry into every answer, because it is the hinge between this theme and Theme 9.** The revolt was dangerous *because* taluqdar and peasant fought together (§2). So the British did not merely defeat that alliance — **they bought one half of it.** Restoring estates to landholders who came over was a political act, not a military one, and it worked. ⭐ **Note the direction of the lesson the British learned: after 1857 they stopped experimenting with dispossessing the old landed elite and started underwriting it.** That is the origin of the loyalist-landlord order that Congress spends the next ninety years working around — and it is exactly what Theme 9's Permanent Settlement story would have predicted.

> 💡 **The two "peaceful" words are worth a sticky note.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Punjab is described as "largely peaceful"** — one of the two prongs came from there. Prelims can ask this as a negative: *which region did NOT rise?* The other structural fact is that **the three colonial cities — Calcutta, Bombay, Madras — were untouched** (Exercise 10 makes you plot exactly this). **The revolt was a NORTH-INDIAN, INLAND, AGRARIAN event, and it stopped at the edge of the areas longest under British rule.**

### 📜 Source 7 — *Villagers as rebels* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**An officer reporting from rural Awadh (spelt *Oude*):**

> *"**The Oude people are gradually pressing down on the line of communication from the North** … the Oude people are **villagers** … **these villagers are nearly intangible to Europeans, melting away before them and collecting again.** The Civil Authorities report these villagers to amount to **a very large number of men, with a number of guns**."*

> 🔴 **NCERT's Discuss box:** *"What, according to this account, were the problems faced by the British in dealing with these villagers?"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The answer, and it is a gift of a phrase: *"nearly INTANGIBLE to Europeans, MELTING AWAY before them and COLLECTING AGAIN."*** The British problem was not that the enemy was strong but that **there was no enemy to find** — no army to defeat, no capital to take, no line to break. A villager who fights and then goes back to being a villager cannot be beaten in a battle. ⭐ **This is a description of guerrilla resistance written by a man who had no word for it**, and it explains the "village by village" slog and the fourteen months.
>
> 💡 **Note what else the source concedes:** they were attacking **"the line of communication"** — i.e. this was *strategic*, not blind fury — and they had **"a number of guns"**. **Both details cut against the official story that the rebels were an undisciplined rabble.** Deploy this alongside Forsyth's estimate below.

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SINGLE MOST QUOTABLE STATISTIC IN THE CHAPTER — memorise the name.** *"As soon as they began their counter-insurgency operations, the British realised that **they were not dealing with a MERE MUTINY but an UPRISING THAT HAD HUGE POPULAR SUPPORT**. In Awadh, for example, a British official called **FORSYTH** estimated that **THREE-FOURTHS OF THE ADULT MALE POPULATION WAS IN REBELLION**."*
>
> **Why it is worth more than its length:** it is the **victors' own admission**, in their own records, that the "sepoy mutiny" label is false. ⭐ *When you need one piece of evidence that 1857 was a popular revolt and not a barracks affair, this is it — and it is unassailable precisely because it is hostile testimony.*

### 📷 Felice Beato — the photographs are evidence of the REPRESSION, not of the revolt ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Two captions in this section are doing historical work, and NCERT means you to notice:

| Figure | What it shows | What NCERT says it MEANS |
|---|---|---|
| **Fig. 10.8** — *A mosque on the Delhi Ridge*, Beato, 1857–58 | ruins | *"After 1857, British photographers recorded **innumerable images of desolation and ruin**."* |

Here the British kill 2,000 men **inside his pleasure garden** and photograph the bones. *Awadh's grievance and Awadh's punishment happen on the same piece of ground.*

## 5️⃣ Images of the Revolt

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS SECTION IS THE REASON THE CHAPTER IS CALLED "REBELS AND THE RAJ **AND ITS REPRESENTATIONS**", AND IT IS THE HALF THAT DISTINGUISHES A GOOD ANSWER FROM AN AVERAGE ONE.** Everybody can narrate 1857. Almost nobody writes about **how it was pictured, by whom, for which audience, and to what effect.** Exercise 8 asks for exactly this and it is a 250–300-word essay. **Do not treat §5 as decoration.**

```
   "These tell us about ** THE FEARS AND ANXIETIES OF OFFICIALS

```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The methodological sentence to steal: a source that is useless for the question you first asked may be excellent for a different question.** British records tell you little about what rebels wanted — but they are **first-class evidence of colonial fear**. ⭐ *A historian's skill is not finding unbiased sources; it is asking each biased source the question it can actually answer.* **This single idea transfers to Theme 9 (the Fifth Report), Theme 11 (police reports on Gandhi) and Theme 12 (the CAD) — it is the spine of Part III.**

### 🖼️ 5.1 Celebrating the saviours

**The image: *"Relief of Lucknow"*, painted by THOMAS JONES BARKER, 1859 (Fig. 10.10).**

**The events it depicts — learn this chain, it is Prelims-shaped:**

```
   THE SIEGE OF LUCKNOW -- four names, in order

   1. ** HENRY LAWRENCE **, Commissioner of Lucknow
      -- when rebel forces besieged Lucknow, he
         "collected the Christian population and took
          refuge in the heavily fortified ** RESIDENCY ** "
      -- ** LAWRENCE WAS KILLED **

   2. ** COLONEL INGLIS **
      -- the Residency "continued to be defended under
         his command"

   3. ** JAMES OUTRAM and HENRY HAVELOCK **
      -- arrived ** 25 SEPTEMBER **, "cut through the rebel
         forces, and reinforced the British garrisons"

   4. ** COLIN CAMPBELL **, "appointed as the NEW COMMANDER
      of British forces in India"
      -- came ** TWENTY DAYS LATER ** and "rescued the
         besieged British garrison"

   ** "In British accounts the siege of Lucknow became a story
      of SURVIVAL, HEROIC RESISTANCE and THE ULTIMATE TRIUMPH

```

**Now the part that earns marks — NCERT reads the painting's *composition* as an argument:**

| Element of the picture | What it is doing |
|---|---|
| **Campbell, Outram and Havelock at the CENTRE of the canvas** | the heroes are literally the focal point |
| **"the GESTURES OF THE HANDS of those around lead the spectator's eyes towards the centre"** | ⭐ the crowd is arranged as an arrow pointing at the heroes |
| **the heroes stand on ground that is WELL LIT**, with **shadows in the foreground** | light = triumph; darkness = the ordeal being left behind |
| the **damaged Residency in the background** | the cost, safely in the past |
| **the dead and injured in the foreground** | *"testimony to the suffering during the siege"* |
| the **triumphant figures of HORSES in the middle ground** | *"emphasise the fact that **British power and control had been RE-ESTABLISHED**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's conclusion is the sentence to reproduce:** *"To the British public such paintings were **REASSURING**. They created a sense that **the time of trouble was past and the rebellion was over; the British were the victors**."*
>
> 🎯 **How to use this in an answer:** the painting is dated **1859** — *after* it was all over. **It is not a record of the relief of Lucknow; it is a piece of reassurance manufactured for a frightened British public two years later.** ⭐ *Ask of every image the two questions NCERT is modelling here: **WHEN was it made relative to the event, and WHO was meant to look at it?** Those two questions alone will carry you through Exercise 8.*

### 👩 5.2 English women and the honour of Britain ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**NCERT begins with a claim about media, not about art:** *"**Newspaper reports have a power over public imagination; they shape feelings and attitudes to events.**"* Inflamed *"particularly by tales of violence against women and children"*, there were **public demands in Britain for revenge and retribution** — the government *"was asked to protect the honour of innocent women and ensure the safety of helpless children"*. ⭐ **And then the key formulation: artists *"EXPRESSED AS WELL AS SHAPED these sentiments"*.**

**Two opposite female figures, and the contrast is the examinable structure:**

```
   THE SAME PROPAGANDA GOAL, TWO OPPOSITE IMAGES OF WOMEN

   ------------------------------------------------------
   IMAGE A -- THE VICTIM
   "IN MEMORIAM", by ** JOSEPH NOEL PATON, 1859 **
   (Fig. 10.11)  -- painted ** TWO YEARS AFTER ** the mutiny

     * English women and children ** HUDDLED IN A CIRCLE **
     * "looking ** HELPLESS AND INNOCENT **, seemingly
        waiting for the inevitable -- ** DISHONOUR, VIOLENCE
        AND DEATH ** "
     * ** IT SHOWS NO GORY VIOLENCE -- "it only SUGGESTS it" **
     * "It ** STIRS UP THE SPECTATOR'S IMAGINATION **, and
        seeks to ** PROVOKE ANGER AND FURY ** "
     * ** "It represents the rebels as VIOLENT AND BRUTISH,
        EVEN THOUGH THEY REMAIN INVISIBLE IN THE PICTURE." **
     * in the background: ** the British rescue forces
       arriving AS SAVIOURS **

   ------------------------------------------------------
   IMAGE B -- THE HEROINE
   ** MISS WHEELER ** defending herself against sepoys
   in Kanpur (Fig. 10.12)

     * she "stands ** FIRMLY AT THE CENTRE **, defending her
       honour, ** SINGLE-HANDEDLY KILLING the attacking
       rebels ** "
     * ** "As in all such British representations, THE REBELS
        ARE DEMONISED." **
     * ** FOUR BURLY MALES with swords and guns shown
       attacking ONE WOMAN **
     * ** THE RELIGIOUS TURN: her struggle "is represented as
       having a DEEPER RELIGIOUS CONNOTATION: it is A BATTLE
       TO SAVE THE HONOUR OF CHRISTIANITY." **
     * ** THE PROOF IS AN OBJECT: "THE BOOK LYING ON THE FLOOR
       IS THE BIBLE." **
   ------------------------------------------------------
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The most sophisticated single observation in the chapter, and it is one line: *"It represents the rebels as violent and brutish, EVEN THOUGH THEY REMAIN INVISIBLE IN THE PICTURE."*** **An absence can be a portrayal.** By painting only the terrified victims and leaving the attackers outside the frame, Paton lets every viewer supply a rebel out of his own fear — which is more inflammatory than any painted figure could be. ⭐ *This is worth a sentence in any answer on colonial representation: **the most effective demonisation showed nothing at all.***
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the Bible on the floor is how you demonstrate "close reading of a visual source" in three words.** A book is not decoration. Placed on the floor beneath a struggle, it converts an assault into a *desecration*, and the woman's self-defence into **a defence of Christianity** — which in turn licenses a religious war of revenge. **One prop changes the category of the event.**

> 💡 **Do not miss the gender argument underneath.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Both images make **the bodies of Englishwomen stand for the honour of Britain itself** — which is why Fig. 10.13's caption speaks of *"the challenge to British honour"* and not to British power. **Empire is being defended as chastity.** *(Deployable in Mains GS1 "role of women" and in essays on gender and nationalism — and note the mirror image in Theme 11 and in Partition, where women's bodies again become the territory on which community honour is fought out.)*

### ⚔️ 5.3 Vengeance and retribution

*"As waves of anger and shock spread in Britain, demands for retribution grew louder."*

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's causal sentence — this is the one to quote, because it makes images into CAUSES rather than reflections:** *"**Visual representations and news about the revolt CREATED A MILIEU IN WHICH VIOLENT REPRESSION AND VENGEANCE WERE SEEN AS BOTH NECESSARY AND JUST.**"*

*"It was as if **justice demanded** that the challenge to British honour and power be met ruthlessly. **Threatened by the rebellion, the British felt that they had to demonstrate their INVINCIBILITY.**"*

**Fig. 10.13 — *"Justice"*, *Punch*, 12 September 1857:**

```
   READING THE ALLEGORY

   ** an allegorical FEMALE FIGURE OF JUSTICE **
     - ** A SWORD in one hand, A SHIELD in the other **
     - ** her posture is AGGRESSIVE **
     - ** her face expresses RAGE AND THE DESIRE FOR REVENGE **
     - ** she is TRAMPLING SEPOYS UNDER HER FEET **
     - ** a mass of INDIAN WOMEN WITH CHILDREN COWER WITH FEAR **

   ** THE CAPTION: "The news of the terrible massacre at
      Cawnpore (Kanpur) produced an outburst of fiery
      indignation and wild desire for revenge throughout
      the whole of England." **

   THE TELL: ** classical Justice is BLINDFOLDED and holds
   SCALES. This one has neither. She has a SWORD and a FACE
   FULL OF RAGE, and she is standing ON somebody. **
   ** The figure of impartial law has been redrawn as the
      figure of revenge -- and still labelled "Justice". **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is the whole point of the cartoon and it is a gift for an answer: the British were not admitting to revenge, they were RENAMING revenge as justice.** ⭐ **And the cowering Indian women and children in the same frame do a second job** — they concede that the vengeance will fall on non-combatants, and ask the viewer to be satisfied by that rather than troubled by it.

**Fig. 10.14 — *"The British Lion's Vengeance on the Bengal Tiger"*, *Punch*, 1857.** NCERT sets a Discuss box on it: *"What idea is the picture projecting? What is being expressed through the images of the lion and the tiger? What do the figures of the woman and the child depict?"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A usable answer:** the **lion** is Britain — the national emblem, and in the visual grammar of the period the king of beasts, i.e. **rightful, noble, sovereign**. The **tiger** is Bengal/India — dangerous, beautiful, *treacherous*, and crucially **an animal**, not a people. ⭐ **Casting the conflict as lion-versus-tiger removes politics from it entirely**: beasts do not have grievances, so the revolt needs no explanation and no redress, only mastery. The **woman and child beneath** are the victim-cause that makes the lion's attack defensive rather than punitive — **the same rhetorical move as §5.2, in animal costume.**

### 💀 5.4 The performance of terror ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

*"The urge for vengeance and retribution was expressed in **the brutal way in which the rebels were executed. They were BLOWN FROM GUNS, or HANGED FROM THE GALLOWS. Images of these executions were WIDELY CIRCULATED through popular journals.**"*

| Figure | Source & date | What NCERT tells you to see |
|---|---|---|
| **Fig. 10.15** — *Execution of mutineers in Peshawar: **blowing from the guns*** | ***Illustrated London News*, 3 October 1857** | ⭐ *"The scene of execution here appears to be **A STAGE WHERE A DRAMA IS BEING PERFORMED — AN ENACTMENT OF BRUTAL POWER**. Mounted soldiers and sepoys in uniform dominate the scene. **They have to WATCH the execution of their fellow sepoys, and experience the chilling consequences of rebellion.**"* |
| **Fig. 10.16** — *Execution of mutinous sepoys in Peshawar* | ***Illustrated London News*, 3 October 1857** | **"12 rebels hang in a row, with cannons all around them."** |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SENTENCE. Learn it verbatim — it is the single most transferable line in the chapter:**
>
> > *"**What you see is NOT ROUTINE PUNISHMENT: IT IS THE PERFORMANCE OF TERROR. For it to instil fear among people, punishment could not be discreetly meted out in ENCLOSED SPACES. IT HAD TO BE THEATRICALLY PERFORMED IN THE OPEN.**"*

```
   WHY "PERFORMANCE" IS THE RIGHT WORD -- unpack it in an answer

   A PUNISHMENT                    A PERFORMANCE OF TERROR
   ------------                    -----------------------
   aimed at THE GUILTY             aimed at ** THE AUDIENCE **
   done in an enclosed space       ** done IN THE OPEN **
   the point is the sentence       ** the point is THE WATCHING **
   ends when it ends               ** circulated afterwards in
                                      ILLUSTRATED JOURNALS,
                                      so the audience becomes
                                      BRITAIN TOO **

   ** NOTE WHO IS MADE TO WATCH: "mounted soldiers and SEPOYS
      IN UNIFORM" -- i.e. THE MEN WHO DID NOT REBEL. **
   ** The execution is addressed to the LOYAL sepoy, not to
      the dead one. **

   AND NOTE THE TWO AUDIENCES OF THE SAME IMAGE:
     in INDIA  -> the parade ground: ** obey **
     in BRITAIN -> the ILLUSTRATED LONDON NEWS:
                   ** we are answering Cawnpore; be satisfied **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This paragraph is why "Representations" is in the chapter's subtitle.** ⭐ **The repression and its images are not two things — the image IS part of the repression**, because terror that nobody sees does no work. *(Compare Fig. 10.9's arranged skeletons in §4: same logic, different medium.)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐ **Cross-link worth building: "blowing from the guns" was chosen partly because it scatters the body and denies the funerary rites of both Hindus and Muslims.** NCERT does not say this — **do not attribute it to NCERT** — but it fits the chapter's own §1 argument that the British repeatedly struck at religious observance, and it explains why this particular method was felt as more than death.

### 🕊️ 5.5 No time for clemency

*"At a time when the clamour was for vengeance, **pleas for moderation were RIDICULED**."*

**Fig. 10.17 — *"The Clemency of Canning"*, *Punch*, 24 October 1857.**

```
   WHAT HAPPENED
   ** Governor General CANNING declared that "A GESTURE OF
      LENIENCY AND A SHOW OF MERCY WOULD HELP IN WINNING BACK
      THE LOYALTY OF THE SEPOYS." **
   ** -> "he was MOCKED IN THE BRITISH PRESS." **

   THE CARTOON
     * Canning drawn as ** "A LOOMING FATHER FIGURE" **
     * ** "with his PROTECTIVE HAND OVER THE HEAD OF A SEPOY" **
     * the sepoy ** "still holds an UNSHEATHED SWORD in one
       hand and a DAGGER in the other, BOTH DRIPPING WITH
       BLOOD" **
     * NCERT: this is ** "an imagery that RECURS in a number
       of British pictures of the time" **

   THE CAPTION -- note the baby-talk, it is the insult:
   ** "Governor General: 'Well, then they shan't blow him from
      nasty guns; but he must promise to be A GOOD LITTLE
      SEPOY.'" **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **What the cartoon is actually arguing, and it is subtler than "Canning was soft":** by drawing the sepoy **still armed and still bleeding**, *Punch* asserts that **the rebellion is not over and the rebel is not repentant** — so mercy is not generous, it is *stupid*, and it endangers the women of §5.2. ⭐ **The caption's nursery voice ("a good little sepoy") does the second half of the work: it makes Canning ridiculous rather than wicked**, and ridicule is the more effective weapon against a policy.
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Use this section to make a point most candidates miss: THE BRITISH WERE NOT UNANIMOUS.** The Governor General himself argued for leniency and was **mocked by his own countrymen's press**. *(He earned the nickname "Clemency Canning" — sneered then, honourable now.)* **This matters twice over:** it shows the pressure for atrocity coming substantially **from public opinion at home**, not only from officials in India; and it is a caution against writing "the British" as a single mind. ⭐ *Canning is also the man who then presides over the 1858 transfer to the Crown and the Queen's Proclamation — so the argument he lost in the press he partly won in policy.*

### 🇮🇳 5.6 Nationalist imageries ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

*"**The national movement in the twentieth century drew its inspiration from the events of 1857.** A whole world of nationalist imagination was woven around the revolt. **It was celebrated as the FIRST WAR OF INDEPENDENCE in which ALL SECTIONS of the people of India came together to fight against imperial rule.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's own framing sentence for the whole section:** *"**Art and literature, AS MUCH AS THE WRITING OF HISTORY, have helped in keeping alive the memory of 1857.**"*

```
   HOW 1857 WAS RE-IMAGINED AFTER THE FACT

   * leaders "presented as ** HEROIC FIGURES leading the
     country into battle **, rousing the people to
     ** RIGHTEOUS INDIGNATION ** against oppressive
     imperial rule"
   * ** HEROIC POEMS ** written about "the valour of the queen
     who, ** with a sword in one hand and the reins of her
     horse in the other **, fought for the freedom of her
     ** MOTHERLAND ** "
   * ** RANI OF JHANSI "was represented as A MASCULINE FIGURE
     chasing the enemy, slaying British soldiers and
     valiantly fighting till her last." **
   * ** SUBHADRA KUMARI CHAUHAN ** -- the lines Indian children
     grow up reading:
        ** "Khoob lari mardani woh to Jhansi wali rani thi" **
        ** ("LIKE A MAN SHE FOUGHT, she was the Rani of
           Jhansi") **
   * in popular prints she is "portrayed in ** BATTLE ARMOUR,
     with a SWORD in hand and RIDING A HORSE ** -- a symbol of
     ** THE DETERMINATION TO RESIST INJUSTICE AND ALIEN
     RULE ** "
   * ** Fig. 10.18: "FILMS AND POSTERS have helped create the
     image of Rani Lakshmi Bai as A MASCULINE WARRIOR." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The comparison that makes this section worth real marks — put §5.1–5.5 and §5.6 in ONE table and the chapter's argument completes itself:**

| | **British imagery (1857–59)** | **Nationalist imagery (20th c.)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Made by** | British artists, *Punch*, *Illustrated London News* | Indian poets, printmakers, later **films and posters** |
| **Made when** | during and immediately after | ⭐ **decades later**, during the freedom struggle |
| **For whom** | a frightened, vengeful British public | a nation being asked to imagine itself |
| **Its hero** | Campbell, Outram, Havelock — **the saviours** | **Rani Lakshmi Bai** — the martyr-warrior |
| **Its claim** | *the rebellion was barbarism; we restored order* | *it was the **First War of Independence**, and **all sections came together*** |
| **What it must leave out** | Forsyth's three-fourths; the taluqdar–peasant alliance; Canning's case | ⭐ the **restorationism** of §3 (the rebels wanted the **Mughal past** back, not a nation); the regions that stayed quiet; the loyal sepoys |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE HONEST POSITION TO TAKE IN AN ANSWER — and examiners reward it.** NCERT never says the nationalist reading is *false*; it says a *"whole world of nationalist imagination was WOVEN around the revolt"*. ⭐ **Both bodies of images are constructions made for a purpose. The difference is the purpose, not the honesty.** Write: *"1857 was neither the barbaric mutiny of British prints nor, in the participants' own understanding, a war for a modern nation — it was a popular anti-colonial uprising whose leaders imagined restoration, which the twentieth century then adopted as its founding sacrifice."* **That sentence uses §3 and §5.6 against each other, which is exactly what the chapter is built for.**

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The gender point returns, inverted, and it is a lovely thing to notice.** In §5.2 the Englishwoman is honoured for being **helpless** (Paton) or for defending **her chastity** (Miss Wheeler). In §5.6 the Rani is honoured for being **"a MASCULINE figure"**, "*mardani*". ⭐ **Both traditions can only admire a woman by measuring her against a male norm** — one by making her the thing men protect, the other by saying she fought *like a man*. *(A ready-made paragraph for essays on women in the national movement, and a good way to show you read the caption rather than the picture.)*

> 🔴 **NCERT's Discuss box for the section:** *"**Examine the elements in each of the visuals in this section and discuss how they allow you to identify the perspective of the artist.**"* — i.e. **the method is the syllabus here, not the pictures.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The chapter's closing claim — images do not only REFLECT, they SHAPE

> *"These images **did not only reflect** the emotions and feelings of the times in which they were produced. **THEY ALSO SHAPED SENSIBILITIES.** Fed by the images that circulated in Britain, **the public SANCTIONED the most brutal forms of repression of the rebels**. On the other hand, **nationalist imageries of the revolt helped SHAPE THE NATIONALIST IMAGINATION**."*

```
   THE TWO-WAY CIRCUIT -- draw this in an answer, it is the
   chapter's thesis in one shape

        events  ---------->  images  ---------->  events
          |                    |                    |
     Cawnpore, the         "In Memoriam",      ** the public
     siege of Lucknow       "Justice",            SANCTIONS
                            the Lion               brutal
                                                repression **

        1857  ------------>  poems, prints,  --->  ** the
      the revolt             films of the          NATIONALIST
                             Rani                  IMAGINATION **

   ** So a representation is never downstream of history.
      It is one of the things history is MADE OF. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And this is where Theme 10 hands over to the rest of Part III.** ⭐ **Theme 10 teaches you that the record of an event is itself an actor in the event.** Theme 9 showed a report written by the accused; Theme 11 will show four kinds of evidence disagreeing about one march; Theme 12 will show a debate preserved *with its losing arguments intact*. **Once you can write "who made this record, when, for whom, and what did making it accomplish?", you have the skill Part III exists to teach.**

```
   1857
   10 May     ** MUTINY STARTS IN MEERUT **
   11-12 May  ** DELHI garrisons revolt; BAHADUR SHAH accepts

   20-27 May  Sepoys mutiny in ** ALIGARH, ETAWAH, MAINPURI,
              ETAH **
   30 May     ** RISING IN LUCKNOW **
   May-June   ** MUTINY TURNS INTO A GENERAL REVOLT OF THE
              PEOPLE **
   30 June    ** British suffer defeat in the BATTLE OF
              CHINHAT **
   25 Sept    British forces under ** HAVELOCK and OUTRAM **
              enter the ** RESIDENCY in LUCKNOW **

   July 1858  ** SHAH MAL KILLED IN BATTLE **

```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The timeline is an argument, and NCERT built it that way — notice the top three entries.** Before a single sepoy appears, you get **1801 Subsidiary Alliance → 1856 annexation → 1856-57 summary settlements.** ⭐ **NCERT is telling you that the chronology of 1857 begins fifty-six years earlier, in a treaty.** *(Use this against any question that treats greased cartridges as the cause: the cartridge is where the timeline gets loud, not where it starts.)*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two dates that get confused — keep them apart.** **30 May 1857 = the rising in Lucknow. 30 June 1857 = the British DEFEAT at Chinhat.** And **25 September** is Outram–Havelock reaching the Residency (§5.1), with **Campbell arriving twenty days later** — the painting celebrates *Campbell's* entry, not the September one.
>
> ⚠️ **Note the ordering quirk in the printed timeline:** the 1858 block prints **July (Shah Mal)** above **June (Rani Jhansi)**. Both are 1858; **the Rani dies in June, Shah Mal in July.**

### 🔴 Prelims traps — the claim vs the truth

| ❌ The plausible-sounding claim | ✅ What the chapter actually says |
|---|---|
| The British met the revolt with military force | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The LAWS CAME FIRST** — Acts of **May–June 1857** put North India under **martial law** *before* troops were sent, suspended ordinary trial, and made **death the only punishment for rebellion** |
| Only military officers could try suspected rebels | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"military officers AND EVEN ORDINARY BRITONS"** were given the power to try and punish |
| Delhi fell quickly | Attempt began **early June 1857**; the city was taken only in **late September 1857** |
| Awadh was subdued along with Delhi | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Awadh was controlled only in **MARCH 1858**, "after protracted fighting" — **the longest resistance** |
| The Punjab rose with the rest of North India | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Punjab was **"largely peaceful"** — one of the two British prongs came *from* it |
| The revolt reached the Presidency cities | **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras were centres of British power, not of revolt** (Exercise 10 asks you to plot the distance) |
| The British broke the revolt purely by arms | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ They also **split the taluqdar–peasant alliance by promising landholders their ESTATES BACK**; **rebel landholders dispossessed, loyal ones rewarded** |
| A British official estimated most of Awadh's men were loyal | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FORSYTH estimated THREE-FOURTHS OF THE ADULT MALE POPULATION was in rebellion** |
| Felice Beato photographed the fighting | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ He photographed **the aftermath** — *"innumerable images of desolation and ruin"*; the **Secundrah Bagh** photo (**1858**) shows **skeletons "meant to be a cold warning of the futility of rebellion"** |
| "Relief of Lucknow" was painted during the siege | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thomas Jones Barker, 1859** — two years after |
| "In Memoriam" depicts the violence graphically | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Joseph Noel Paton, 1859** — it shows **NO gory violence**, "it only **suggests** it", and **the rebels are INVISIBLE in the picture** |
| Miss Wheeler's picture is about military valour | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ It is given **"a deeper RELIGIOUS connotation… a battle to save the honour of CHRISTIANITY"** — **the book on the floor is the BIBLE** |
| *Punch* supported Canning's leniency | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It **mocked** him — *"The Clemency of Canning"*, **24 October 1857**, "a good little sepoy" |
| The executions were ordinary punishments | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"NOT routine punishment: it is the PERFORMANCE OF TERROR… it had to be THEATRICALLY PERFORMED IN THE OPEN"** — **blown from guns / hanged**, at **Peshawar**, in the ***Illustrated London News*, 3 October 1857** |
| Lawrence commanded the Residency through the siege | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Henry Lawrence was KILLED**; the defence continued under **Colonel Inglis** |
| Campbell relieved Lucknow on 25 September | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Outram and Havelock** arrived 25 September; **Campbell came TWENTY DAYS LATER** as the **new Commander** |
| "First War of Independence" is NCERT's verdict | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ NCERT reports it as **20th-century nationalist framing** — *"a whole world of nationalist imagination was WOVEN around the revolt"* — while §3 records that the rebels *"harked back to the eighteenth-century Mughal world"* |
| The Subhadra Kumari Chauhan line praises her femininity | ⭐⭐⭐ ***"Khoob lari MARDANI"*** — *"like a MAN she fought"*; NCERT notes she was represented as **"a MASCULINE figure"** |

### 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | How to build it |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What do visual representations tell us about the revolt of 1857?"** *(NCERT Exercise 8 — and a live GS1 shape)* | Structure: **(i)** they are evidence of the **producers**, not the events; **(ii)** four British modes — *celebration* (Barker), *victimhood* (Paton, Miss Wheeler), *sanctioned vengeance* (**Justice**, **the Lion**), *terror as theatre* (Peshawar); **(iii)** the counter-tradition of **nationalist imagery**; **(iv)** the thesis — **images "did not only reflect… they also SHAPED sensibilities"**; the British public **"sanctioned the most brutal forms of repression"**. Close on **who is absent from each frame** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Was 1857 a "war of independence"?** | Yes in **scale and popular base** (Forsyth's three-fourths; "not a mere mutiny but an uprising that had huge popular support"; taluqdar–peasant unity; the intangible villagers of Source 7). No in **political vision** (§3: they *"harked back to the eighteenth-century Mughal world"*; leaders were **erstwhile rulers**; the structures were "primarily aimed at meeting the demands of war"). Resolve, don't fence-sit: **anti-colonial in fact, restorationist in imagination, nationalist in retrospect** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **How did the British suppress the revolt?** *(Exercise 5)* | The four instruments in order — **law → force → village-by-village reconquest → splitting the alliance**. Most answers give only the second. The **fourth** is the one with consequences after 1858 |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The problem of reconstructing the rebels' viewpoint** | The asymmetry table in §3; §5.0's *"historians till now have continued to discuss rebel actions primarily through accounts written by the British"*; and the method — **British records are excellent evidence of British fear**. Pair with Theme 9's Fifth Report |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gender and empire** | §5.2's two female types (helpless victim / chaste defender), **Englishwomen's honour standing in for Britain's**, versus §5.6's **Rani as "masculine warrior"**. Argue that both traditions measure women against a male standard |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Public opinion as a driver of colonial violence** | Newspapers *"inflamed public feelings and provoked demands of retribution"*; images "created a milieu in which violent repression… **were seen as both necessary and just**"; **Canning was ridiculed for proposing mercy.** A rare, well-evidenced case of **metropolitan opinion shaping colonial policy** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1857 and the memory of the nation** | §5.6 as a case study in how a **founding sacrifice** is constructed — art, poetry, prints, then films and posters. Links to Theme 11 (Gandhi's image-making) and to any essay on nationalism and memory |

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Martial law** | Suspension of ordinary law and courts, with military authority substituted; imposed on **all of North India, May–June 1857** |
| **Counter-insurgency** | Operations against an armed movement with **popular support** — NCERT uses the word deliberately, and Source 7 describes its classic difficulty |
| **Mutiny records** | The **collected volumes** of British memos, notes, assessments and reports on 1857 — the bulk of the surviving archive |
| **Allegorical figure** | A person in a picture who **stands for an abstract idea** (here, *Justice*) — reading one means asking what the idea has been made to look like |
| **Etching / bazaar print** | Cheap reproducible images — why representations reached **mass** audiences on both sides |
| ⭐ **The performance of terror** | Punishment staged **in the open, before a compelled audience**, so that its effect is on the watchers rather than the condemned |
| **Clemency** | Mercy shown by an authority; **"Clemency Canning"** began as a sneer in the British press |
| ***Mardani*** | "Like a man" — the word in **Subhadra Kumari Chauhan's** line on the Rani of Jhansi |
| **Residency** | The fortified British compound at Lucknow where the Christian population took refuge under **Henry Lawrence** |
| **Firangi** | Foreigner/European — the rebels' word, from §1 |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in eight lines

1. **The revolt began as mutiny and became "a general revolt of the people"** — NCERT's own timeline says so at *May–June 1857*.
2. **Awadh is the chapter's laboratory**: annexation (1856) + summary settlements dispossessed **taluqdar and peasant together**, which is why resistance there lasted longest — to **March 1858**.
3. **What the rebels wanted was recorded almost only by the people who defeated them** — a few proclamations, *ishtahars* and letters against a mountain of mutiny records.
4. **Their vision was restorationist**: the *firangi* gone, the **eighteenth-century Mughal world** back. They built administrations, but "primarily aimed at meeting the demands of war".
5. **The British answer began with LAW, not troops** — martial law, suspended trials, death as the only sentence — then gigantic force, then village-by-village reconquest, then **buying back the landholders**.
6. **Their own officials conceded the scale**: not "a mere mutiny" but an uprising with huge popular support; **Forsyth's three-fourths of Awadh's adult men**.
7. **The images are a second front.** British art celebrated saviours, dramatised threatened Englishwomen, allegorised vengeance as *Justice*, and staged executions as **theatre** — and **"sanctioned the most brutal forms of repression"**.
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Images do not only reflect; they shape.** The same mechanism later produced the **nationalist** 1857 — the First War of Independence, the Rani in armour — which is how a restorationist revolt became a nation's founding story.
