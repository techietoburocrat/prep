### 🧭 The analogy that fits this chapter: **a monument's plaque, not its accounts**

> ⚠️ *An analogy builds the model; the facts still have to be learned. Do not put the analogy in an answer.*

**An inscription is the plaque on a building, not the building's accounts.**

```
   ** WHAT A PLAQUE TELLS YOU ** -- reliably, permanently:
      who commissioned it · the date · the honours claimed ·
      what the commissioner wished remembered

   ** WHAT IT NEVER TELLS YOU: **
      what it cost · who actually did the work · whether it
      worked · what the neighbours thought · what happened
      the following year

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND NCERT SAYS EXACTLY THIS, in §8:
   ** "routine agricultural practices and THE JOYS AND SORROWS
      OF DAILY EXISTENCE FIND NO MENTION in inscriptions,
      which focus, more often than not, on
      GRAND, UNIQUE EVENTS." **
   ** "the content of inscriptions ALMOST INVARIABLY PROJECTS
      THE PERSPECTIVE OF THE PERSON(S) WHO COMMISSIONED
      THEM." **

   ** THE STRUCTURAL BIAS TO REMEMBER: **
   ** an inscription records the DONOR, the VICTOR and the
      EXCEPTIONAL. It is silent on the ordinary, the routine,
      the defeated and the poor -- not by accident, but
      BECAUSE OF WHAT IT IS FOR. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE HABIT TO BUILD: for every inscription in this chapter, ask WHO PAID FOR THE STONE.** *Asoka's edicts — **the king**. The Prayaga Prashasti — **Samudragupta's court poet**. The Sudarshana lake inscription — **Rudradaman**, recording that he repaired it *"using his own resources, without imposing any tax"*. Prabhavati Gupta's grant — **the donor of the land**. ⭐ **In every case the text survives because a powerful person wanted it to.** *That is not a reason to reject it; **it is a reason to read it as a CLAIM rather than a REPORT.***

## 🌍 The opening — what the 1,500 years after Harappa contained

> **NCERT sets the scene before it starts, and these facts are examinable in their own right:**

```
   ** BETWEEN THE HARAPPANS AND c.600 BCE: **

   * ** "This was also the period during which THE RIGVEDA was
     composed by people living ALONG THE INDUS AND ITS
     TRIBUTARIES." **
   * ** "AGRICULTURAL SETTLEMENTS emerged in many parts of the
     subcontinent, including NORTH INDIA, THE DECCAN PLATEAU,
     and PARTS OF KARNATAKA." **
   * ** "there is evidence of PASTORAL POPULATIONS in the
     DECCAN AND FURTHER SOUTH." **
   * ⭐ ** MEGALITHS: "New modes of disposal of the dead,
     including THE MAKING OF ELABORATE STONE STRUCTURES KNOWN
     AS MEGALITHS, emerged in CENTRAL AND SOUTH INDIA FROM
     THE FIRST MILLENNIUM BCE. In many cases, THE DEAD WERE
     BURIED WITH A RICH RANGE OF IRON TOOLS AND WEAPONS." **

   ** THEN, FROM c.SIXTH CENTURY BCE -- three simultaneous
      changes, and NCERT insists they are connected: **
     1. ** "the emergence of EARLY STATES, EMPIRES AND
        KINGDOMS" **
     2. ** "changes… in the ways in which AGRICULTURAL
        PRODUCTION WAS ORGANISED" **
     3. ** "SIMULTANEOUSLY, NEW TOWNS APPEARED ALMOST
        THROUGHOUT THE SUBCONTINENT." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE WARNING, IN THE OPENING PARAGRAPH: **
   ** "Historians attempt to understand these developments by
      drawing on a range of sources -- INSCRIPTIONS, TEXTS,
      COINS AND VISUAL MATERIAL. As we will see, THIS IS A
      COMPLEX PROCESS. You will also notice that
      THESE SOURCES DO NOT TELL THE ENTIRE STORY." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The megalith detail is worth keeping for two reasons.** ⭐ *(i) It is a **named Prelims item** — elaborate stone burial structures, central and south India, first millennium BCE, **with iron tools and weapons**. (ii) It is the **evidential contrast with Theme 1**: the Harappans *"did not believe in burying precious things with the dead"*; **these people did.** *A change in burial practice is a change in belief — and it is the kind of inference Theme 1 trained you to make carefully.*

## 1️⃣ Prinsep and Piyadassi ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT opens the chapter with a DISCOVERY rather than with a king, and the choice is deliberate — the chapter's subject is how this history became knowable at all.**

```
   ** THE EVENT: "Some of the most MOMENTOUS developments in
      Indian epigraphy took place IN THE 1830s." **

   ** JAMES PRINSEP ** -- ⭐ ** "an OFFICER IN THE MINT OF THE
   EAST INDIA COMPANY" ** -- ** "DECIPHERED BRAHMI AND
   KHAROSTHI, TWO SCRIPTS USED IN THE EARLIEST INSCRIPTIONS
   AND COINS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHAT HE FOUND, AND THE PUZZLE IT CREATED: **
   ** "He found that MOST OF THESE MENTIONED A KING REFERRED
      TO AS PIYADASSI -- MEANING 'PLEASANT TO BEHOLD'; there
      were A FEW INSCRIPTIONS WHICH ALSO REFERRED TO THE KING
      AS ASOKA, ONE OF THE MOST FAMOUS RULERS KNOWN FROM
      BUDDHIST TEXTS." **

   ** WHAT FOLLOWED: **
   * ** "This gave A NEW DIRECTION to investigations into early
     Indian political history" ** -- scholars used inscriptions
     and texts ** "to reconstruct THE LINEAGES OF MAJOR
     DYNASTIES" **
   * ** "As a result, THE BROAD CONTOURS OF POLITICAL HISTORY
     WERE IN PLACE BY THE EARLY DECADES OF THE TWENTIETH
     CENTURY." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THEN THE SHIFT THAT DEFINES MODERN WORK: **
   ** "SUBSEQUENTLY, scholars began to SHIFT THEIR FOCUS TO
      THE CONTEXT of political history, investigating WHETHER
      THERE WERE CONNECTIONS BETWEEN POLITICAL CHANGES AND
      ECONOMIC AND SOCIAL DEVELOPMENTS. It was soon realised
      that WHILE THERE WERE LINKS, THESE WERE NOT ALWAYS
      SIMPLE OR DIRECT." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE PIYADASSI/ASOKA PROBLEM IS THE PERFECT OPENING FOR THIS CHAPTER AND YOU SHOULD BE ABLE TO EXPLAIN IT IN THREE SENTENCES.** ⭐ **The inscriptions almost never name Asoka. They name a title — *Piyadassi*, "pleasant to behold" — alongside *devanampiya*, "beloved of the gods".** *The identification rests on **a few inscriptions that use BOTH the titles and the name**, plus the fact that Asoka was already known from **Buddhist texts**.* **So the best-documented king of ancient India had to be identified by cross-referencing an epigraphic title against a religious literature.** *§7.3 spells out the logic — epigraphists concluded the inscriptions were one ruler's because they *"match in terms of CONTENT, STYLE, LANGUAGE AND PALAEOGRAPHY."***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note what the last paragraph announces — the whole modern research programme.** *Nineteenth-century epigraphy produced **dynastic lineages**. Twentieth-century history asked **what connected politics to economy and society** — which is exactly why this chapter has a *"Changing Countryside"* and a *"Towns and Trade"* section rather than a king-list. ⭐ **And NCERT's caution is worth stealing: the links exist but are *"not always simple or direct."*** *(§8 closes the loop by describing a THIRD shift — *"recent decades have seen a much greater preoccupation with histories of MARGINALISED GROUPS."*)*

### 📦 The "Inscriptions" box — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ pure Prelims material

```
   ** WHAT AN INSCRIPTION IS: **
   ** "writings ENGRAVED ON HARD SURFACES such as STONE, METAL
      OR POTTERY." **

   ** WHAT THEY RECORD: **
   ** "the ACHIEVEMENTS, ACTIVITIES OR IDEAS OF THOSE WHO
      COMMISSIONED THEM… the EXPLOITS OF KINGS, or DONATIONS
      MADE BY WOMEN AND MEN TO RELIGIOUS INSTITUTIONS." **
   ⭐ Note: even here, the definition already contains §8's
     warning -- an inscription is about ITS COMMISSIONER.

   ** "Inscriptions are VIRTUALLY PERMANENT RECORDS, SOME OF
      WHICH CARRY DATES." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** PALAEOGRAPHY -- how the undated ones are dated: **
   ** "Others are dated ON THE BASIS OF PALAEOGRAPHY OR STYLES
      OF WRITING, with A FAIR AMOUNT OF PRECISION.
      For instance, IN c.250 BCE the letter 'a' was written
      [one way]. BY c.500 CE, it was written [another way]." **

   ** THE LANGUAGES: **
   * ⭐ ** "THE EARLIEST INSCRIPTIONS WERE IN PRAKRIT, A NAME
     FOR LANGUAGES USED BY ORDINARY PEOPLE." **
   * ** Names such as AJATASATTU and ASOKA are given in their
     PRAKRIT forms in this chapter **
   * ** Also used: PALI, TAMIL and SANSKRIT **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "It is possible that PEOPLE SPOKE IN OTHER
     LANGUAGES AS WELL, EVEN THOUGH THESE WERE NOT USED FOR
     WRITING." **

   ** EPIGRAPHY ** = ** "the study of inscriptions" **
   ** JANAPADA ** = ⭐ ** "the land where A JANA (a people,
      clan or tribe) SETS ITS FOOT OR SETTLES" ** -- a word
      used in ** both Prakrit and Sanskrit **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PALAEOGRAPHY IS THE ANSWER TO "HOW DO YOU DATE AN UNDATED INSCRIPTION?" AND IT IS WORTH ONE PRECISE SENTENCE.** ⭐ **Letter forms change over time in traceable ways, so the SHAPE of the writing dates the text even when the text gives no date** — NCERT's own example is the letter "a" changing between **c.250 BCE and c.500 CE**. *(It is also the technique used in Theme 1's stratigraphy chain and, later, in dating manuscripts — **a style is a clock**.)*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LAST LINE OF THE LANGUAGE NOTE IS A §8 POINT PLANTED EARLY, AND ALMOST NOBODY PICKS IT UP: *"people may have spoken in other languages… even though these were not used for writing."*** ⭐ **The linguistic map recoverable from inscriptions is the map of WRITTEN languages — i.e. of languages with institutional backing.** *Whole spoken languages could vanish leaving no epigraphic trace at all. **That is survival bias operating on language rather than on objects, and it is exactly parallel to Theme 1's "organic materials decompose."***

## 2️⃣ The Earliest States

### 2.1 The sixteen mahajanapadas ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"**The sixth century BCE is often regarded as A MAJOR TURNING POINT in early Indian history.** It is an era associated with **EARLY STATES, CITIES, THE GROWING USE OF IRON, THE DEVELOPMENT OF COINAGE**, etc. It also witnessed **the growth of DIVERSE SYSTEMS OF THOUGHT, including BUDDHISM AND JAINISM.**"*

```
   ** THE SIXTEEN -- and note NCERT's careful hedge: **
   ** "Early BUDDHIST AND JAINA TEXTS mention, amongst other
      things, SIXTEEN STATES KNOWN AS MAHAJANAPADAS.
      ⭐ ALTHOUGH THE LISTS VARY, some names occur
      FREQUENTLY: " **

     ** VAJJI · MAGADHA · KOSHALA · KURU · PANCHALA ·
        GANDHARA · AVANTI **

   ** "Clearly, these were amongst THE MOST IMPORTANT
      mahajanapadas." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** TWO KINDS OF STATE -- this is the examinable
      distinction **
   ================================================================

   ** MONARCHIES ** -- "most mahajanapadas were RULED BY KINGS"

   ** GANAS or SANGHAS ** -- ⭐ ** OLIGARCHIES, "where POWER
   WAS SHARED BY A NUMBER OF MEN, OFTEN COLLECTIVELY CALLED
   RAJAS." **
     * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "BOTH MAHAVIRA AND THE BUDDHA BELONGED TO
       SUCH GANAS." **
     * ** "In some instances, as in the case of THE VAJJI
       SANGHA, THE RAJAS PROBABLY CONTROLLED RESOURCES SUCH AS
       LAND COLLECTIVELY." **
     * ⭐ ** "Although their histories are OFTEN DIFFICULT TO
       RECONSTRUCT DUE TO THE LACK OF SOURCES, SOME OF THESE
       STATES LASTED FOR NEARLY A THOUSAND YEARS." **

   ** OLIGARCHY ** (margin) = ** "a form of government where
   POWER IS EXERCISED BY A GROUP OF MEN. ⭐ THE ROMAN
   REPUBLIC… WAS AN OLIGARCHY IN SPITE OF ITS NAME." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE *GANA*/*SANGHA* POINT IS THE MOST IMPORTANT THING IN §2.1 AND IT CARRIES A METHODOLOGICAL STING.** *Note what NCERT says: their histories are hard to reconstruct **"due to the LACK OF SOURCES"** — **and yet some lasted nearly a thousand years.*** ⭐ **A state can endure for a millennium and leave almost no record, because it had no single ruler commissioning inscriptions about himself.** *That is §8's bias made visible: **monarchy generates epigraphy; collective rule does not.** **The historical record is skewed towards kings not because kings mattered more, but because kings COMMISSIONED MORE STONE.***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the Buddha-and-Mahavira detail is a superb cross-link to [Theme 4](04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md).** *Both founders of the great heterodox traditions came **from oligarchies, not monarchies.** ⭐ *(The **sangha** — the Buddhist monastic order — even takes the name of the political form; Theme 4 notes its collective decision-making. **A political vocabulary became a religious one.**)*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two Prelims cautions.** *(i) **"Although the lists vary"** — there is no single canonical sixteen; NCERT gives the **frequently occurring** names, and those seven are what to learn. (ii) **The Roman Republic comparison is NCERT's own** and is aimed at Class 11 Theme 2 — an oligarchy *"in spite of its name."*

### 💰 Where the resources came from — the point that drives the whole chapter

```
   ** THE PROBLEM: "Each mahajanapada had A CAPITAL CITY,
      WHICH WAS OFTEN FORTIFIED. MAINTAINING THESE FORTIFIED
      CITIES AS WELL AS PROVIDING FOR INCIPIENT ARMIES AND
      BUREAUCRACIES REQUIRED RESOURCES." **

   ** THE ANSWER: THE DHARMASUTRAS **
   ** "From c.sixth century BCE onwards, BRAHMANAS BEGAN
      COMPOSING SANSKRIT TEXTS KNOWN AS THE DHARMASUTRAS.
      These LAID DOWN NORMS FOR RULERS (as well as for other
      social categories), WHO WERE IDEALLY EXPECTED TO BE
      KSHATRIYAS." **

   ** "Rulers were advised TO COLLECT TAXES AND TRIBUTE FROM
      CULTIVATORS, TRADERS AND ARTISANS." **

   ⭐ ** AND THE HONEST GAP: "Were resources ALSO PROCURED FROM
      PASTORALISTS AND FOREST PEOPLES? WE DO NOT REALLY
      KNOW." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE OTHER SOURCE OF INCOME, STATED WITHOUT
      EUPHEMISM: **
   ** "What we DO know is that RAIDS ON NEIGHBOURING STATES
      WERE RECOGNISED AS A LEGITIMATE MEANS OF ACQUIRING
      WEALTH." **

   ** THE MILITARY CONSEQUENCE: **
   ** "Gradually, SOME STATES ACQUIRED STANDING ARMIES AND
      MAINTAINED REGULAR BUREAUCRACIES. OTHERS CONTINUED TO
      DEPEND ON MILITIA, RECRUITED, MORE OFTEN THAN NOT,
      FROM THE PEASANTRY." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"RAIDS… WERE RECOGNISED AS A LEGITIMATE MEANS OF ACQUIRING WEALTH" IS A SENTENCE WORTH QUOTING, BECAUSE IT REFRAMES ANCIENT WARFARE.** ⭐ **War was not only politics — it was a REVENUE STRATEGY, and an approved one.** *Set it beside §5.1's *Gandatindu Jataka*, where subjects are *"attacked by robbers at night and by TAX COLLECTORS during the day"*, and you have both of the state's extraction methods described from opposite ends. **The standing-army-vs-militia distinction is the structural point: an army you must feed year-round forces you to build a revenue system; a militia you can send home does not.***

### 2.2 First amongst the sixteen: Magadha ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE SECTION UPSC ASKS ABOUT MOST OFTEN FROM §2, AND NCERT DELIBERATELY GIVES YOU TWO COMPETING KINDS OF EXPLANATION. A good answer gives both.**

```
   ** "Between THE SIXTH AND THE FOURTH CENTURIES BCE, MAGADHA
      (in present-day BIHAR) became THE MOST POWERFUL
      MAHAJANAPADA." **

   ================================================================
   ** EXPLANATION A -- MODERN HISTORIANS: MATERIAL ADVANTAGES **
   ================================================================
   1. ⭐ ** "Magadha was a region where AGRICULTURE WAS
      ESPECIALLY PRODUCTIVE." **
   2. ⭐ ** "IRON MINES (in present-day JHARKHAND) were
      ACCESSIBLE and provided RESOURCES FOR TOOLS AND
      WEAPONS." **
   3. ⭐ ** "ELEPHANTS, AN IMPORTANT COMPONENT OF THE ARMY,
      were found in FORESTS in the region." **
   4. ⭐ ** "the GANGA AND ITS TRIBUTARIES provided A MEANS OF
      CHEAP AND CONVENIENT COMMUNICATION." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** EXPLANATION B -- EARLY BUDDHIST AND JAINA
      WRITERS: INDIVIDUALS **
   ================================================================
   ** "early Buddhist and Jaina writers who wrote about Magadha
      ATTRIBUTED ITS POWER TO THE POLICIES OF INDIVIDUALS:
      RUTHLESSLY AMBITIOUS KINGS of whom BIMBISARA, AJATASATTU
      and MAHAPADMA NANDA are the best known, AND THEIR
      MINISTERS, WHO HELPED IMPLEMENT THEIR POLICIES." **

   ================================================================
   ** THE CAPITALS -- a two-stage move, both examinable **
   ================================================================
   ** RAJAGAHA ** (the Prakrit name for present-day ** RAJGIR,
   BIHAR ** ) was the initial capital
     ⭐ ** "Interestingly, THE OLD NAME MEANS 'HOUSE OF THE
       KING'." **
     ** "Rajagaha was A FORTIFIED SETTLEMENT, LOCATED AMONGST
        HILLS." **
   ** THEN, IN THE FOURTH CENTURY BCE, the capital SHIFTED to
      PATALIPUTRA (present-day PATNA), ⭐ "COMMANDING ROUTES
      OF COMMUNICATION ALONG THE GANGA." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT'S OWN DISCUSS BOX IS THE EXAM QUESTION: *"What are the DIFFERENT EXPLANATIONS offered by EARLY WRITERS and PRESENT-DAY HISTORIANS for the growth of Magadhan power?"*** — and the shape of the answer is the point.
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The contrast is between STRUCTURE and AGENCY, and naming it that way is worth a mark on its own.** ⭐ **Modern historians explain Magadha by its GEOGRAPHY AND RESOURCES — soil, iron, elephants, rivers.** *Buddhist and Jaina writers explained it by **the character of individual kings** — *"ruthlessly ambitious"* men and their ministers.* **Neither is simply wrong.** *The material advantages explain **why Magadha COULD dominate**; the individuals explain **why it DID, and when.*** *(And note the source-critical point lurking underneath: **the early writers were monks writing about kings who were their patrons and contemporaries** — of course they wrote about personalities. **A text's genre shapes its explanations.**)*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The capital shift is a favourite MCQ, so keep the two straight: RAJAGAHA/RAJGIR = fortified, among HILLS, "house of the king". PATALIPUTRA/PATNA = fourth century BCE, chosen for COMMANDING RIVER ROUTES.** ⭐ *The move is itself an argument — **from a defensive site to a commercial-strategic one** — and it tells you the state had shifted from surviving to projecting power. (Fig. 2.2 shows the **fortification walls at Rajgir**, with NCERT asking *"Why were these walls built?"*)*

## 3️⃣ An Early Empire — the Mauryas ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** "The growth of Magadha CULMINATED IN THE EMERGENCE OF
      THE MAURYAN EMPIRE." **

   ** CHANDRAGUPTA MAURYA ** -- founded the empire
   ** c.321 BCE ** -- ⭐ ** "extended control AS FAR NORTHWEST

### 3.1 Finding out about the Mauryas — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the source list

| Source | What it is | NCERT's caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Archaeological finds** | *"especially **SCULPTURE**"* | — |
| ⭐ **MEGASTHENES** | *"a **GREEK AMBASSADOR to the court of CHANDRAGUPTA MAURYA**"* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"which **SURVIVES IN FRAGMENTS**"* |
| ⭐ **The *ARTHASHASTRA*** | *"**parts of which were PROBABLY composed by KAUTILYA or CHANAKYA**, traditionally believed to be **the minister of Chandragupta**"* | note the double hedge — ***"parts of"*** and ***"probably"*** |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ASOKA'S FIRST — AND IT IS A GENUINE FIRST, WORTH STATING PRECISELY:**
>
> > *"**Asoka was THE FIRST RULER WHO INSCRIBED HIS MESSAGES TO HIS SUBJECTS AND OFFICIALS ON STONE SURFACES — NATURAL ROCKS AS WELL AS POLISHED PILLARS.**"*
>
> ⭐ **Not the first ruler to leave inscriptions — the first to use them to ADDRESS HIS SUBJECTS.** *That is a different thing from a donation record or a royal boast, and it is why Prinsep found *Piyadassi* everywhere.*

**☸️ *DHAMMA* — what Asoka actually put on the stones:**

> *"He used the inscriptions to proclaim what he understood to be ***DHAMMA***. This included:*
> - ⭐ ***RESPECT TOWARDS ELDERS***
> - ***GENEROSITY TOWARDS BRAHMANAS and THOSE WHO RENOUNCED WORLDLY LIFE***
> - ***TREATING SLAVES AND SERVANTS KINDLY***
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***RESPECT FOR RELIGIONS AND TRADITIONS OTHER THAN ONE'S OWN***"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE CRUCIAL OBSERVATION ABOUT *DHAMMA*, AND IT IS THE ANSWER TO WHY IT WORKED AS AN IMPERIAL POLICY: NOTHING IN THAT LIST IS SECTARIAN.** ⭐ **There is no doctrine, no deity, no ritual, no exclusive claim.** *NCERT says so explicitly in §3.2 — the principles *"were **SIMPLE AND VIRTUALLY UNIVERSALLY APPLICABLE**."* **That is precisely what an empire stretching from Afghanistan to Karnataka needed: a moral code no community had to abandon its own tradition to accept.** *(And note the last item — **respect for other traditions** — which is the direct ancestor of the *sulh-i kul* material in [Class 7 Ch.4](../../Class_07_Our_Pasts_II/Notes/04_The_Mughals.md) and Class 11 Supplement Part B. **It is one of the most reusable single facts in ancient Indian history.**)*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Do NOT write that *dhamma* was Buddhism.** *NCERT never says that. It says *dhamma* was **"what HE UNDERSTOOD to be dhamma"** — a formulation that carefully attributes the content to Asoka rather than to any religion. **The edicts as summarised here contain no Buddhist doctrine at all.** *(Asoka's personal Buddhism is attested elsewhere; the *dhamma* of the edicts is a public ethic. **Keep the two separate and you will not lose marks.**)*

### 3.2 Administering the empire ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE FIVE MAJOR POLITICAL CENTRES -- memorise all five
      and WHY each mattered: **

   ** 1. PATALIPUTRA ** -- the capital
   ** 2. TAXILA **      -- ⭐ "situated on IMPORTANT
                          LONG-DISTANCE TRADE ROUTES"
   ** 3. UJJAYINI **    -- ⭐ likewise on major trade routes
   ** 4. TOSALI **      -- (in the Kalinga region)
   ** 5. SUVARNAGIRI ** -- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ "LITERALLY, THE GOLDEN
                          MOUNTAIN… POSSIBLY IMPORTANT FOR
                          TAPPING THE GOLD MINES OF KARNATAKA"

   ** "all mentioned in ASOKAN INSCRIPTIONS." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE UNIFORMITY PROBLEM -- NCERT'S ARGUMENT **
   ================================================================
   ** THE OBSERVATION: "If we examine the content of these
      inscriptions, we find VIRTUALLY THE SAME MESSAGE
      ENGRAVED EVERYWHERE -- from the present-day NORTH WEST
      FRONTIER PROVINCES OF PAKISTAN, to ANDHRA PRADESH,
      ORISSA and UTTARAKHAND in India." **

   ** THE QUESTION: "COULD THIS VAST EMPIRE HAVE HAD A UNIFORM
      ADMINISTRATIVE SYSTEM?" **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE ANSWER: "Historians have INCREASINGLY COME
      TO REALISE THAT THIS IS UNLIKELY. THE REGIONS INCLUDED
      WITHIN THE EMPIRE WERE JUST TOO DIVERSE. Imagine the
      contrast between THE HILLY TERRAIN OF AFGHANISTAN AND
      THE COAST OF ORISSA." **

   ** THE REVISED PICTURE: "It is likely that ADMINISTRATIVE
      CONTROL WAS STRONGEST IN AREAS AROUND THE CAPITAL AND
      THE PROVINCIAL CENTRES." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE SINGLE BEST PIECE OF REASONING IN THE CHAPTER AND IT MUST BE IN YOUR MAURYAN ANSWER. WATCH WHAT NCERT DOES:** ⭐ **the same message engraved everywhere looks like PROOF of uniform administration — and NCERT argues it is NOT.** *A standard text can be circulated and copied without any standard administration existing behind it.* **The uniformity is a uniformity of PROCLAMATION, not of GOVERNMENT.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Compare with Theme 1 and the contrast is instructive.** *There, the **4:2:1 brick ratio from Jammu to Gujarat** was treated as evidence FOR central coordination. **Here, an identical message from Afghanistan to Orissa is treated as NOT evidence for uniform administration.*** ⭐ **Why the difference? Because a brick ratio has to be enforced at every kiln in daily practice, while a proclamation only has to be issued once and carried.** *That distinction — **between a standard that must be continuously enacted and a message that need only be transmitted** — is genuinely sophisticated, and it is exactly the kind of point that separates a top answer.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The centres were chosen for ECONOMIC reasons, and that is the pattern to state: Taxila and Ujjayini on TRADE ROUTES, Suvarnagiri for GOLD.** *The empire's administrative geography maps onto its **revenue geography**. **Control was thickest where the money was**, thinning with distance — which is precisely the *"control was not uniform"* picture.*

### 📖 SOURCE 1 — *What the king's officials did* (Megasthenes) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"**Of the great officers of state, some … SUPERINTEND THE RIVERS, MEASURE THE LAND, AS IS DONE IN EGYPT, and INSPECT THE SLUICES by which water is let out from the main canals into their branches, SO THAT EVERY ONE MAY HAVE AN EQUAL SUPPLY OF IT.** The same persons have charge also of **the HUNTSMEN**, and are entrusted with **the power of REWARDING OR PUNISHING them according to their deserts. THEY COLLECT THE TAXES, and superintend the occupations connected with land; as those of the WOODCUTTERS, the CARPENTERS, the BLACKSMITHS, and the MINERS.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"Why were officials appointed to SUPERVISE THESE OCCUPATIONAL GROUPS?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The answer is one word — REVENUE — but the good version explains the pattern.** ⭐ **Look at the list: rivers, land measurement, sluices, huntsmen, woodcutters, carpenters, blacksmiths, miners.** *Every one of these is either **a taxable activity** or **a strategic resource** (timber, iron, and the water on which the harvest depends). **The state supervised the points at which value entered the economy**, because those are the points at which it can be taxed and the points at which the army must be supplied.*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BUT APPLY §8 TO THIS SOURCE, BECAUSE NCERT'S OWN DISCUSS BOX ASKS YOU TO:** *"Read the excerpts from **Megasthenes and the Arthashastra (Sources 1 and 2). TO WHAT EXTENT DO YOU THINK THESE TEXTS ARE USEFUL IN RECONSTRUCTING A HISTORY OF MAURYAN ADMINISTRATION?**"*
> - ⭐ **Megasthenes survives only IN FRAGMENTS** (§3.1), quoted by later Greek authors — **we do not have his book.**
> - ⭐ ***"as is done in Egypt"* is the tell** — he is describing India **through a comparison with another place he knew**, exactly the analogy problem of Theme 1's *"curry stones"* and the *"priest-king"*.
> - ⭐ **The *Arthashastra* is PRESCRIPTIVE** — it says what a ruler *should* do. **A manual is evidence of intention, not of practice.**
> - ⭐ **And NCERT flags the numbers directly:** *"According to **Greek sources**, the Mauryan ruler had a standing army of **600,000 foot-soldiers, 30,000 cavalry and 9,000 elephants. SOME HISTORIANS CONSIDER THESE ACCOUNTS TO BE EXAGGERATED.**"*
> - ✅ **The balanced verdict:** *these texts are **indispensable and insufficient** — they give the **only detailed picture** of Mauryan administration available, but one is fragmentary and foreign, the other prescriptive and undated, **and both must be checked against the inscriptions and the archaeology.**

### 📖 SOURCE 2 — *Capturing elephants for the army* (the *Arthashastra*) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"**Guards of elephant forests, ASSISTED BY those who REAR elephants, those who ENCHAIN THE LEGS of elephants, those who GUARD THE BOUNDARIES, those who LIVE IN FORESTS, as well as by those who NURSE elephants, shall, WITH THE HELP OF FIVE OR SEVEN FEMALE ELEPHANTS to help in tethering wild ones, TRACE THE WHEREABOUTS OF HERDS OF ELEPHANTS BY FOLLOWING THE COURSE OF URINE AND DUNG left by elephants.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's question is the good one: *"If the Greek accounts were true, WHAT KINDS OF RESOURCES do you think the Mauryan ruler would have required to maintain SUCH A LARGE ARMY?"*** ⭐ **9,000 elephants is the figure to think with.** *An elephant eats on the order of a hundred and fifty kilograms of fodder a day. **Multiply by 9,000 and the army's logistics become an agrarian problem** — which is precisely why §5 exists, why Megasthenes' officials supervise woodcutters and sluices, and why the second military subcommittee spent its time *"procuring FOOD for soldiers and FODDER for animals."*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And notice what the passage really shows: SPECIALISED OCCUPATIONAL CATEGORIES.** *Six different kinds of people are named for one task — **forest guards, rearers, leg-chainers, boundary guards, forest-dwellers and nurses.** ⭐ **That degree of occupational subdivision, written into a manual, is itself evidence of an organised state** — whether or not the specific procedure was ever followed. *(And **"those who live in forests"** is a rare glimpse of exactly the group §5.4 says was *"often beyond the reach of officials"* — here they appear as **indispensable specialists**, not as outsiders.)*

>

## 4️⃣ New Notions of Kingship ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 4.1 Chiefs and kings in the south

> *"The new kingdoms that emerged in **the Deccan and further south**, including **the CHIEFDOMS OF THE CHOLAS, CHERAS AND PANDYAS in TAMILAKAM** *(the name of the ancient Tamil country, which included parts of **present-day Andhra Pradesh and Kerala**, in addition to Tamil Nadu)*, **proved to be STABLE AND PROSPEROUS.**"*

**📦 CHIEFS AND CHIEFDOMS — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the definition box, and the chief/king distinction is a standard exam item:**

| | **A CHIEF** | **A KING** *(by implication)* |
|---|---|---|
| **Position** | *"a powerful man whose position **MAY OR MAY NOT BE HEREDITARY**"* | hereditary |
| **Support base** | ⭐ *"He derives support from **HIS KINFOLK**"* | an administration |
| **Functions** | *"performing **SPECIAL RITUALS**, **LEADERSHIP IN WARFARE**, and **ARBITRATING DISPUTES**"* | governing |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Income** | *"He receives **GIFTS from his subordinates** (**unlike kings who usually collect TAXES**)"* | **taxes** |
| ⭐ **What he does with it** | *"often **DISTRIBUTES these amongst his supporters**"* | accumulates / funds the state |
| **Apparatus** | *"Generally, there are **NO REGULAR ARMIES AND OFFICIALS** in chiefdoms"* | standing army, bureaucracy |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GIFT/TAX DISTINCTION IS THE HEART OF IT, AND IT IS WORTH UNDERSTANDING RATHER THAN MEMORISING.** ⭐ **A gift is voluntary, personal and must be RECIPROCATED — which is why the chief redistributes it.** *A tax is compulsory, impersonal and is **retained**.* **So a chiefdom's wealth CIRCULATES and a kingdom's wealth ACCUMULATES** — and accumulation is what pays for standing armies, bureaucracies and fortified capitals. *That single difference explains why §2's mahajanapadas needed the Dharmasutras' advice on taxation, and it is the mechanism by which chiefdoms become states.*

**📖 SOURCE 3 — *The Pandya chief Senguttuvan visits the forest* (the *Silappadikaram*) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"(When he visited the forest) **people came down the mountain, SINGING AND DANCING** … **just as the defeated show respect to the victorious king, so did they BRING GIFTS** — **IVORY, FRAGRANT WOOD, FANS MADE OF THE HAIR OF DEER, HONEY, SANDALWOOD, RED OCHRE, ANTIMONY, TURMERIC, CARDAMOM, PEPPER**, etc. … they brought **COCONUTS, MANGOES, MEDICINAL PLANTS, FRUITS, ONIONS, SUGARCANE, FLOWERS, ARECA NUT, BANANAS, BABY TIGERS, LIONS, ELEPHANTS, MONKEYS, BEAR, DEER, MUSK DEER, FOX, PEACOCKS, MUSK CAT, WILD COCKS, SPEAKING PARROTS**, etc. …"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's questions: *"WHY did people bring these gifts? WHAT would the chief have used these for?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A strong answer, and it uses the box above:**
> - **Why:** ⭐ *the text itself says it — **"just as the defeated show respect to the victorious king."*** **The gift is an act of SUBMISSION and of relationship**, not a payment. It acknowledges the chief's authority **without any machinery of assessment or collection** — exactly as the box describes.
> - **What for:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REDISTRIBUTION** — *"often distributes these amongst his supporters"* — which is how a chief converts tribute into loyalty. **And several items are TRADE goods**: *pepper, cardamom, turmeric, ivory, sandalwood* are precisely what §6.3 and **Source 9's *Periplus*** show being exported to Rome. **So the forest gifts entered long-distance commerce**, which is how these chiefdoms became *"stable and prosperous"*.
> - ⭐ **Note also what the list reveals about the ECONOMY:** it is overwhelmingly **forest produce and animals** — the resources of exactly the *"pastoralists and forest peoples"* §2.1 admitted we know little about. **Here they are, in a poem, paying tribute.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is also the source to compare with **Source 8 (Danguna)** — NCERT's own Exercise 4 asks for exactly that.** *⭐ **Source 3 = a CHIEF receiving GIFTS of forest produce; Source 8 = a QUEEN granting a VILLAGE with its taxes and dues.** **Gifts vs revenue; forest vs settled agriculture; personal homage vs a written charter.** **The two sources are the chief/king distinction in living form.***

**⚔️ The Satavahanas and the Shakas — kingship from trade:**

> *"Many chiefs and kings, including **the SATAVAHANAS who ruled over parts of WESTERN AND CENTRAL INDIA (c. second century BCE – second century CE)** and **the SHAKAS, A PEOPLE OF CENTRAL ASIAN ORIGIN who established kingdoms in the NORTH-WESTERN AND WESTERN parts of the subcontinent, DERIVED REVENUES FROM LONG-DISTANCE TRADE.** ⭐ **Their social origins were often OBSCURE, but… ONCE THEY ACQUIRED POWER THEY ATTEMPTED TO CLAIM SOCIAL STATUS IN A VARIETY OF WAYS.**"*

> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That last sentence is a direct hand-off to [Theme 3](03_Theme3_Kinship_Caste_and_Class.md), and NCERT says so.** *Rulers whose origins were **obscure** — i.e. **not Kshatriya**, and in the Shakas' case **not even from the subcontinent** — nonetheless had to be fitted into a social order that said kings should be Kshatriyas. ⭐ **Theme 3 shows exactly how the Satavahanas managed it.** *Keep the link: **§2.1's Dharmasutras said rulers "were IDEALLY expected to be Kshatriyas"; §4.1 shows that in practice they frequently were not.** **The gap between prescription and practice is Theme 3's entire subject.***

**📖 SOURCE 4 — *In praise of Samudragupta* (the Prayaga Prashasti) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"**He was WITHOUT AN ANTAGONIST ON EARTH**; he, by the overflowing of the multitude of (his) many good qualities … **has WIPED OFF THE FAME OF OTHER KINGS WITH THE SOLES OF (HIS) FEET**; **(he is) PURUSHA (the Supreme Being)**, being the cause of the prosperity of the good and the destruction of the bad; (he is) **INCOMPREHENSIBLE**; (he is) one whose tender heart **can be captured only by DEVOTION AND HUMILITY**; (he is) possessed of **compassion**; (he is) **the giver of many hundred-thousands of cows**; (his) mind has received **ceremonial initiation for the uplift of the MISERABLE, THE POOR, THE FORLORN AND THE SUFFERING**; (he is) **resplendent and EMBODIED KINDNESS TO MANKIND**; ⭐ **(he is) EQUAL TO (the gods) KUBERA (the god of wealth), VARUNA (the god of the ocean), INDRA (the god of rains) and YAMA (the god of death)**…"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's Discuss box: *"WHY DO YOU THINK KINGS CLAIMED DIVINE STATUS?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The best answer connects §4.2 back to §4.1 and to the *samantas*, and it is genuinely counter-intuitive:**
> - ⭐ **Because divinity is a claim that cannot be audited.** *A king who claims to have won battles can be contradicted; **a king who is *"incomprehensible"* and *"equal to Kubera, Varuna, Indra and Yama"* cannot be.***
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Because these rulers often had NO other basis for the claim.** *§4.1 said the Satavahanas' and Shakas' *"social origins were often OBSCURE"*; the **Kushanas were Central Asian**; and the **Dharmasutras said rulers should be Kshatriyas.* **Divinity is a way of claiming legitimacy that BYPASSES birth entirely** — you cannot be out-ranked by a god.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And — the sharpest version — because they depended on *samantas*.** *A king whose military power rests on **semi-independent lords who "offered homage"** and who **could themselves become kings** has a structurally weak position. **§5.4 makes this argument explicit about land grants: kings *"tried to project themselves as SUPERMEN… BECAUSE THEY WERE LOSING CONTROL: they wanted to present at least a FAÇADE of power."*** ⭐ **The louder the claim to divinity, the more it may indicate weakness rather than strength.** *That is a first-class point and it is NCERT's own.*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **How to use a prashasti as a historian — NCERT's instruction is the exam-safe formula:** *treasured *"as works of POETRY rather than as accounts that were LITERALLY TRUE."* ⭐ **So: a prashasti is excellent evidence of HOW A KING WISHED TO BE SEEN, and poor evidence of what he did.** *"Without an antagonist on earth" is a **claim**, not a military record. **Compare Fig. 2.4's question about the Kanishka coin — *"How has the king been PORTRAYED?"* — and Fig. 2.5's — *"What are the ELEMENTS IN THE SCULPTURE that SUGGEST that this is an image of a king?"*** **NCERT keeps asking about portrayal, never about truth. Follow its lead.**

### 5.1 Popular perceptions of kings — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the view from below

> **NCERT opens with the problem, and it is §8's problem stated early:** *"**What did SUBJECTS think about their rulers? OBVIOUSLY, INSCRIPTIONS DO NOT PROVIDE ALL THE ANSWERS. In fact, ORDINARY PEOPLE RARELY LEFT ACCOUNTS OF THEIR THOUGHTS AND EXPERIENCES.**"*

   ** THE WORKAROUND -- and this is Exercise 3's answer
      ("How do historians reconstruct the lives of ordinary

   ** "historians have tried to solve this problem by
      EXAMINING STORIES CONTAINED IN ANTHOLOGIES SUCH AS
      THE JATAKAS AND THE PANCHATANTRA." **

   ⭐ ** WHY THESE WORK: "Many of these stories PROBABLY
      ORIGINATED AS POPULAR ORAL TALES THAT WERE LATER
      COMMITTED TO WRITING." **
   ** "The JATAKAS were written IN PALI around THE MIDDLE OF
      THE FIRST MILLENNIUM CE." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE GANDATINDU JATAKA -- learn this story, it is
      the single most useful piece of evidence in §5 **
   ================================================================
   ** It "describes THE PLIGHT OF THE SUBJECTS OF A WICKED
      KING; these included ELDERLY WOMEN AND MEN, CULTIVATORS,
      HERDERS, VILLAGE BOYS AND EVEN ANIMALS." **

   ** "WHEN THE KING WENT IN DISGUISE TO FIND OUT WHAT HIS
      SUBJECTS THOUGHT ABOUT HIM, EACH ONE OF THEM CURSED HIM

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE COMPLAINT -- memorise this phrasing: **
   ** "complaining that they were ATTACKED BY ROBBERS AT NIGHT

   ** THE RESPONSE: "To escape from this situation,
      PEOPLE ABANDONED THEIR VILLAGE AND WENT TO LIVE IN

```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"ATTACKED BY ROBBERS AT NIGHT AND BY TAX COLLECTORS DURING THE DAY" IS THE BEST SINGLE LINE IN THE CHAPTER FOR SHOWING THE STATE FROM BELOW — AND THE JOKE IS THE ARGUMENT.** ⭐ **The two are put in the same grammatical slot on purpose: from the cultivator's side, the difference between a robber and a revenue official is the hour of the day.** *Set this against **Source 4's Samudragupta**, *"embodied kindness to mankind"*, whose *"mind has received ceremonial initiation for the uplift of the miserable, the poor, the forlorn and the suffering"*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The prashasti and the Jataka describe the same institution. One was paid for by the king; the other was told by the people paying for him.** ***That juxtaposition is the whole methodology of this chapter in two sources, and it belongs in any answer on kingship or on sources.***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOTE WHAT THE PEOPLE DO — THEY LEAVE.** *"People **ABANDONED THEIR VILLAGE and went to live in THE FOREST**."* ⭐ **Flight was the peasant's real weapon**, and it was effective precisely because **the state's revenue depended on settled cultivation**. *NCERT draws the conclusion itself: *"the relationship between a king and his subjects, especially the rural population, **COULD OFTEN BE STRAINED** — kings frequently tried to **FILL THEIR COFFERS BY DEMANDING HIGH TAXES**, and peasants particularly found such demands **OPPRESSIVE**. **ESCAPING INTO THE FOREST REMAINED AN OPTION**."* **And that pressure is what produces §5.2: *"other strategies aimed at INCREASING PRODUCTION to meet growing demand for taxes also came to be adopted."*** *The chapter's logic is tight — **you cannot squeeze harder if the peasants can walk away, so you must raise output instead.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A caution worth stating in an answer:** *the *Jatakas* are **didactic Buddhist stories**, not reportage, and they were written down **centuries after the events this chapter covers**. ⭐ **They are evidence of a WIDELY-HELD PERCEPTION of royal extraction — which is exactly the thing inscriptions cannot give you.** *Say what the source is good for, and you will not be caught overclaiming.*

### 5.2 Strategies for increasing production ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** STRATEGY 1 -- PLOUGH AGRICULTURE, with an IRON TIP **
   ================================================================
   ** "spread in FERTILE ALLUVIAL RIVER VALLEYS such as those
      of THE GANGA AND THE KAVERI from c.SIXTH CENTURY BCE." **
   ** "The IRON-TIPPED PLOUGHSHARE was used TO TURN THE
      ALLUVIAL SOIL in areas which had HIGH RAINFALL." **

   ================================================================
   ** STRATEGY 2 -- TRANSPLANTATION of paddy **
   ================================================================
   ** "in some parts of THE GANGA VALLEY, PRODUCTION OF PADDY
      WAS DRAMATICALLY INCREASED BY THE INTRODUCTION OF
      TRANSPLANTATION" **
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "ALTHOUGH THIS MEANT BACK-BREAKING WORK FOR THE
      PRODUCER." **

   ** TRANSPLANTATION ** (margin) = ** "used for PADDY
   cultivation in areas where WATER IS PLENTIFUL. Here,
   SEEDS ARE FIRST BROADCAST; WHEN THE SAPLINGS HAVE GROWN
   THEY ARE TRANSPLANTED IN WATERLOGGED FIELDS. This ensures
   A HIGHER RATIO OF SURVIVAL OF SAPLINGS AND HIGHER
   YIELDS." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE LIMITS -- WHERE THE PLOUGH WAS *NOT* USED **
   ================================================================
   ** "While the iron ploughshare led to a growth in
      agricultural productivity, ITS USE WAS RESTRICTED TO
      CERTAIN PARTS OF THE SUBCONTINENT: " **

   * ⭐ ** SEMI-ARID AREAS -- "cultivators in areas which were
     SEMI-ARID, such as PARTS OF PUNJAB AND RAJASTHAN,
     DID NOT ADOPT IT TILL THE TWENTIETH CENTURY." **
   * ⭐ ** HILLY TRACTS -- "those living in HILLY TRACTS IN THE
     NORTH-EASTERN AND CENTRAL parts of the subcontinent
     PRACTISED HOE AGRICULTURE, WHICH WAS MUCH BETTER SUITED
     TO THE TERRAIN." **

   ================================================================
   ** STRATEGY 3 -- IRRIGATION **
   ================================================================
   ** "through WELLS AND TANKS, and LESS COMMONLY, CANALS." **
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "COMMUNITIES AS WELL AS INDIVIDUALS ORGANISED
      THE CONSTRUCTION OF IRRIGATION WORKS. THE LATTER,
      USUALLY POWERFUL MEN INCLUDING KINGS, OFTEN RECORDED
      SUCH ACTIVITIES IN INSCRIPTIONS." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT LAST SENTENCE IS A §8 BOMB HIDDEN IN §5, AND IT IS THE SINGLE SHARPEST ILLUSTRATION OF THE CHAPTER'S THESIS. READ IT AGAIN.** ⭐ **Both COMMUNITIES and INDIVIDUALS built irrigation works. Only the individuals — "usually powerful men including kings" — RECORDED IT IN INSCRIPTIONS.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the epigraphic record shows kings building irrigation and is SILENT on communities doing the same thing.** *A historian reading only inscriptions would conclude that irrigation was a royal achievement — **and would be wrong, not because the inscriptions lie, but because of who commissions inscriptions.*** **This is the cleanest possible demonstration of §8's *"the content of inscriptions almost invariably projects the perspective of the person(s) who commissioned them."*** *If you can deploy this example, you can answer Exercise 7 (the D.C. Sircar question) in a single paragraph.*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The "did not adopt it till the twentieth century" fact is a favourite trap, and it also carries a lesson.** *Punjab and Rajasthan — semi-arid — **did not take the iron ploughshare for two and a half millennia**, and north-eastern and central hill peoples used **hoe agriculture** because it *"was MUCH BETTER SUITED TO THE TERRAIN."* ⭐ **NCERT is explicit that this was FITNESS, not backwardness.** *Never write that some regions "lagged behind" in agricultural technology — **write that the technology's advantage was specific to high-rainfall alluvial soils, and elsewhere other methods were better.** That is both correct and the kind of sentence examiners notice.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And keep *"back-breaking work for the producer"* — NCERT's own phrase.** ⭐ *Transplantation raises yields **by raising labour input enormously**. **The higher output the state taxed was purchased with somebody's back.** *That is the honest way to write about agrarian "progress", and it links straight to §5.3's landless labourers.*

**📖 SOURCE 5 — *The Sudarshana lake in Gujarat* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

```
   ** THE SITE: GIRNAR (find it on Map 2 -- it is also an

      ARTIFICIAL RESERVOIR." **

   ** THE EVIDENCE: "a ROCK INSCRIPTION (c. second century CE)

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE THREE-STAGE STORY ON ONE ROCK: **

   2. ** "However, A TERRIBLE STORM BROKE THE EMBANKMENTS and

      ruling in the area, CLAIMED TO HAVE GOT THE LAKE
      REPAIRED USING HIS OWN RESOURCES, WITHOUT IMPOSING ANY
      TAX ON HIS SUBJECTS." **
   3. ** "ANOTHER INSCRIPTION ON THE SAME ROCK (c.fifth
      century) mentions how ONE OF THE RULERS OF THE GUPTA
      DYNASTY GOT THE LAKE REPAIRED ONCE AGAIN." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SUDARSHANA LAKE IS THE BEST SINGLE EXAMPLE IN THIS CHAPTER AND IT ANSWERS THREE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS AT ONCE — learn it as a multi-purpose case study.**
> - ⭐ **For §5.2 (irrigation):** an **artificial reservoir with embankments and water channels**, maintained across **seven centuries** and three dynasties. **NCERT's own question is *"WHY DID RULERS MAKE ARRANGEMENTS FOR IRRIGATION?"*** — and the answer is §5.1: *irrigated land yields more, and more yield is more revenue.* **Public works are revenue investments.**
> - ⭐ **For §4.2 (kingship):** notice the boast — *"using **HIS OWN RESOURCES, WITHOUT IMPOSING ANY TAX** on his subjects."* **Rudradaman is a SHAKA, i.e. of Central Asian origin, writing in SANSKRIT, claiming to be the sort of ruler who repairs public works at his own cost.** *This is §4.1's *"once they acquired power they attempted to claim social status in a variety of ways"* caught in the act.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **For §8 (the limits of epigraphy):** *the inscription tells us **who CLAIMED credit for the repair.** It does not tell us **who dug**, what it cost, or whether the no-tax claim is true. **And the fact that the SAME ROCK carries a second repair inscription three centuries later tells you something the texts never say: a monument is a place where rulers queue up to be recorded.***

   ** "It is likely that these differences were based on
      DIFFERENTIAL ACCESS TO LAND, LABOUR AND SOME OF THE NEW

   ================================================================
   ** GAHAPATI ** (the definition box) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
   ================================================================
   ** "the OWNER, MASTER OR HEAD OF A HOUSEHOLD, who exercised

```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE *GAHAPATI* DEFINITION IS DOING MORE WORK THAN IT LOOKS AND IT IS WORTH UNPACKING IN AN ANSWER.** ⭐ **Note that the same word covers control over PEOPLE (women, children, slaves, workers) and over THINGS (land, animals, resources) — they are listed together, in one sentence, as one kind of ownership.** *That is not sloppy drafting; **it is how the household was conceived.*** *(⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the exact conceptual ancestor of Part III Supplement A's *zan* and *zamin*** — women and land as the two possessions defining a man's honour. **The same fusion of persons and property, two thousand years apart.** Make that link and you have connected the first and last books of Class 12.)*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the last line of the box is the sleeper: *gahapati* was also used for **the URBAN elite, including wealthy merchants**.** ⭐ *So a term rooted in **agrarian household headship** became **a status marker in towns** — which tells you the urban rich were being described in the vocabulary of rural mastery. **§6's cities and §5's countryside share a social language, which is exactly the connection the chapter's title asserts.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Tamil triad is worth memorising precisely because it is INDEPENDENT corroboration: *VELLALAR* (large landowners) / *UZHAVAR* (ploughmen) / *ADIMAI* (slaves).** ⭐ **Two unrelated literatures, in two languages, from two regions, describe the same three-tier agrarian structure.** *When two independent sources agree, the finding is strong — and saying so demonstrates exactly the source-handling §7–§8 is teaching.*

**📖 SOURCE 6 — *The importance of boundaries* (the *Manusmrti*) ⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> **The text:** *"one of the **best-known LEGAL TEXTS of early India, written in SANSKRIT and compiled between c.SECOND CENTURY BCE and c.SECOND CENTURY CE.**"*
>
> **Its advice to the king:** *"**Seeing that in the world CONTROVERSIES CONSTANTLY ARISE DUE TO THE IGNORANCE OF BOUNDARIES**, he should … have … **CONCEALED BOUNDARY MARKERS BURIED — STONES, BONES, COW'S HAIR, CHAFF, ASHES, POTSHERDS, DRIED COW DUNG, BRICKS, COAL, PEBBLES AND SAND. He should also have other similar substances THAT WOULD NOT DECAY IN THE SOIL buried as HIDDEN MARKERS at the intersection of boundaries.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"Would these boundary markers have been ADEQUATE to resolve disputes?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A good answer works both sides:**
> - ✅ **In their favour:** they were chosen for **DURABILITY** — *"substances that would not decay in the soil"* — and being **CONCEALED**, they could not be casually moved by a neighbour. **A hidden marker is tamper-evident in the same way as Theme 1's seal.**
> - ❌ **Against:** ⭐ **a hidden marker is only useful if someone remembers where it is.** *It requires **institutional memory** — someone who knows the burial spot — which means the marker does not settle disputes by itself; **it settles them only alongside testimony and authority.** *And several items (chaff, cow's hair, ashes, dried cow dung) are **not obviously permanent** whatever the text claims.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The historian's point is the one that matters most:** *the fact that a major legal text devotes attention to **burying secret markers at field boundaries** is itself the evidence. **Land had become valuable enough, and disputed enough, to require this.** **Source 6 is §5.3's conclusion — *"questions of control over land must have become crucial"* — demonstrated from the legal side.***

### 5.4 Land grants and new rural elites ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE PRACTICE: "From THE EARLY CENTURIES OF THE COMMON
      ERA, we find GRANTS OF LAND being made, many of which
      were RECORDED IN INSCRIPTIONS." **

   ** THE MEDIUM: "Some of these inscriptions were ON STONE,
      but MOST WERE ON COPPER PLATES, which were PROBABLY
      GIVEN AS A RECORD OF THE TRANSACTION TO THOSE WHO
      RECEIVED THE LAND." **
   ⭐ i.e. ** a copper plate is a TITLE DEED. It is portable
     because the recipient had to be able to produce it. **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHO RECEIVED THEM -- and this is the bias: **
   ** "THE RECORDS THAT HAVE SURVIVED ARE GENERALLY ABOUT
      GRANTS TO RELIGIOUS INSTITUTIONS OR TO BRAHMANAS." **

   ** THE LANGUAGE: "MOST INSCRIPTIONS WERE IN SANSKRIT. In
      some cases, and ESPECIALLY FROM THE SEVENTH CENTURY
      ONWARDS, PART of the inscription was IN SANSKRIT, WHILE
      THE REST WAS IN A LOCAL LANGUAGE SUCH AS TAMIL OR
      TELUGU." **

   ** AGRAHARA ** (margin) = ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "land GRANTED TO A
   BRAHMANA, who was USUALLY EXEMPTED FROM PAYING LAND REVENUE
   AND OTHER DUES TO THE KING, and was OFTEN GIVEN THE RIGHT
   TO COLLECT THESE DUES FROM THE LOCAL PEOPLE." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ THE *AGRAHARA* DEFINITION TWICE, BECAUSE IT CONTAINS THE WHOLE SIGNIFICANCE OF LAND GRANTS IN TWO CLAUSES.** ⭐ **The donee (i) STOPS PAYING revenue to the king, and (ii) STARTS COLLECTING it from the local people.** *That is not a gift of land — **it is a transfer of a piece of the state.** **The king has alienated a revenue stream AND the authority to collect it.** *Once you see that, §5.4's historians' debate answers itself.*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ PRABHAVATI GUPTA — the worked example, and a rare woman in the record**

```
   ** WHO SHE WAS: "the DAUGHTER of one of the most important
      rulers in early Indian history, CHANDRAGUPTA II
      (c.375-415 CE). She was MARRIED INTO another important
      ruling family, THAT OF THE VAKATAKAS, who were powerful
      IN THE DECCAN." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE PROBLEM SHE POSES: **
   ** "According to SANSKRIT LEGAL TEXTS, WOMEN WERE NOT
      SUPPOSED TO HAVE INDEPENDENT ACCESS TO RESOURCES SUCH AS
      LAND. HOWEVER, THE INSCRIPTION INDICATES THAT PRABHAVATI
      HAD ACCESS TO LAND, WHICH SHE THEN GRANTED." **

   ** THE TWO EXPLANATIONS NCERT OFFERS -- give BOTH: **
     (a) ** "This MAY have been because SHE WAS A QUEEN (ONE
         OF THE FEW KNOWN FROM EARLY INDIAN HISTORY), and her
         situation was therefore EXCEPTIONAL." **
     (b) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "IT IS ALSO POSSIBLE THAT THE PROVISIONS
         OF LEGAL TEXTS WERE NOT UNIFORMLY IMPLEMENTED." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXPLANATION (b) IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS, AND IT IS THE HINGE INTO [THEME 3](03_Theme3_Kinship_Caste_and_Class.md).** ⭐ **A legal text says women could not hold land. An inscription shows a woman holding and granting land.** *The two do not both describe reality — **because one of them was never describing reality in the first place.*** **The Dharmasutras and the *Manusmrti* are PRESCRIPTIVE: they say what OUGHT to happen.** *Theme 3's entire subject is the gap between that prescription and observable practice, and **Prabhavati Gupta is the gap made visible in a single copper plate.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note NCERT's parenthesis — *"one of the few [queens] known from early Indian history"*.** ⭐ *Not one of the few who **existed** — one of the few we **know about**. **§8's silence again: the record preserves the exceptional and the powerful, and even among the powerful it preserves women only rarely.***

**📖 SOURCE 8 — *Prabhavati Gupta and the village of Danguna* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"**Prabhavati Gupta … COMMANDS the GRAMAKUTUMBINAS (householders/peasants living in the village), BRAHMANAS and others living in the village of DANGUNA** …*
>
> *'Be it known to you that on the **twelfth (lunar day) of the bright (fortnight) of KARTTIKA**, we have, **IN ORDER TO INCREASE OUR RELIGIOUS MERIT**, donated this village **WITH THE POURING OUT OF WATER**, to the **ACHARYA (teacher) CHANALASVAMIN** … **YOU SHOULD OBEY ALL (HIS) COMMANDS** …*
>
> *⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ We confer on (him) **the following EXEMPTIONS TYPICAL OF AN AGRAHARA**: (this village is) **NOT TO BE ENTERED BY SOLDIERS AND POLICEMEN**; (it is) **exempt from (the obligation to provide) GRASS, (ANIMAL) HIDES AS SEATS, and CHARCOAL (to touring royal officers)**; **exempt from (the royal prerogative of) PURCHASING FERMENTING LIQUORS and DIGGING (SALT)**; **exempt from (the right to) MINES and KHADIRA TREES**; **exempt from (the obligation to supply) FLOWERS and MILK**; (it is donated) **together with (the right to) HIDDEN TREASURES AND DEPOSITS (and) together with MAJOR AND MINOR TAXES** …'*
>
> *This charter **has been written in the thirteenth (regnal) year**. (It has been) **ENGRAVED BY CHAKRADASA.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"What were the things PRODUCED in the village?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The answer has to be read out of the EXEMPTIONS, which is the clever part — and it is why this source is so good.** ⭐ **The list of things the village no longer has to supply is a list of what the village HAD.** *So Danguna produced or held: **grass (fodder), animal hides, charcoal, fermenting liquors, salt, minerals from mines, khadira trees, flowers and milk** — plus, by implication, **the agricultural produce behind the "major and minor taxes"**, and even **hidden treasures and deposits**.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *This is a beautiful piece of method to state explicitly: **an exemption clause is an inventory in negative.** **You learn what a place produced by reading what it was excused from handing over.***
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE THREE SENTENCES THAT MATTER MOST FOR AN ANSWER ON LAND GRANTS:**
> 1. ⭐ ***"NOT TO BE ENTERED BY SOLDIERS AND POLICEMEN."*** **The king's coercive power is excluded from the village.** *This is the transfer of sovereignty, not merely of income.*
> 2. ⭐ ***"together with MAJOR AND MINOR TAXES."*** **The whole revenue, of every kind, now goes to the donee.**
> 3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"YOU SHOULD OBEY ALL (HIS) COMMANDS."*** **The peasants' obedience is reassigned along with the land.** *NCERT states this plainly: the rural population *"would have to **OBEY THE NEW LORD OF THE VILLAGE**, and perhaps **PAY HIM ALL THESE DUES**."*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two smaller details worth having.** ⭐ **The motive is stated: *"IN ORDER TO INCREASE OUR RELIGIOUS MERIT"*** — grants were **acts of piety** as well as politics, which is why the surviving ones go to Brahmanas and religious institutions. **And the charter names its ENGRAVER, *Chakradasa*** — a rare appearance of the craftsman who actually made the record. *(Compare Theme 1's anonymous seal-cutters. **When a name survives, notice it.**)*

   ** "Some feel that land grants were PART OF A STRATEGY
      ADOPTED BY RULING LINEAGES TO EXTEND AGRICULTURE TO NEW
      AREAS." **
   ⭐ Supported by the phrase above: grants included ** "VAST
     STRETCHES OF UNCULTIVATED LAND" ** -- you cannot be
     giving away revenue if the land yields none yet.

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** VIEW B -- GRANTS AS DECLINE (a sign of WEAKNESS) **
   ================================================================
   ** "Others suggest that land grants were INDICATIVE OF
      WEAKENING POLITICAL POWER: AS KINGS WERE LOSING CONTROL
      OVER THEIR SAMANTAS, THEY TRIED TO WIN ALLIES BY MAKING
      GRANTS OF LAND." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE KILLER COROLLARY, WHICH TIES §5.4 BACK
      TO §4.2: **
   ** "They also feel that KINGS TRIED TO PROJECT THEMSELVES AS
      SUPERMEN (as we saw in the previous section) BECAUSE
      THEY WERE LOSING CONTROL: THEY WANTED TO PRESENT AT
      LEAST A FAÇADE OF POWER." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT INTERPRETIVE PASSAGE IN THE CHAPTER, AND THE COROLLARY IS WHAT MAKES IT BRILLIANT.** ⭐ **The same evidence — grandiose royal titles, divine claims, the Prayaga Prashasti's *"without an antagonist on earth"* — reads as STRENGTH on View A and as COMPENSATION on View B.** *A king who is genuinely in command need not insist he is a god.*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HOW TO WRITE IT: do not pick a side and stop. Write that the two readings are testable against DIFFERENT EVIDENCE.** ⭐ *Grants of **uncultivated land in frontier zones** support **expansion**; grants of **already-settled, revenue-yielding villages with full fiscal and judicial exemptions** — like **Danguna, with its "major and minor taxes" and its exclusion of soldiers and policemen** — support **erosion of central authority**. **And NCERT's own note that sizes and rights VARIED REGIONALLY is the resolution: both processes were happening, in different places.** *That is a genuinely strong Mains paragraph.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Do not miss NCERT's closing observation, which is §8 arriving again:** *land grants *"provide some insight into the relationship between cultivators and the state. **HOWEVER, THERE WERE PEOPLE WHO WERE OFTEN BEYOND THE REACH OF OFFICIALS OR SAMANTAS: PASTORALISTS, FISHERFOLK AND HUNTER-GATHERERS, MOBILE OR SEMI-SEDENTARY ARTISANS AND SHIFTING CULTIVATORS. GENERALLY, SUCH GROUPS DID NOT KEEP DETAILED RECORDS OF THEIR LIVES AND TRANSACTIONS.***"* ⭐ **The people hardest for the state to tax are the people hardest for the historian to see — and for the same reason.** *§2.1 asked *"Were resources also procured from pastoralists and forest peoples? **We do not really know**"*, and here is why we don't.*

**📖 SOURCE 7 — *Life in a small village* (the *Harshacharita*) ⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> **The text:** *"a **BIOGRAPHY of HARSHAVARDHANA, the ruler of KANAUJ**, composed **in SANSKRIT by his court poet, BANABHATTA (c. seventh century CE)**"* — and NCERT calls the excerpt ⭐ *"**an EXTREMELY RARE representation of life in a settlement ON THE OUTSKIRTS OF A FOREST IN THE VINDHYAS**"*.
>
> *"**The outskirts being for the most part FOREST, many parcels of RICE-LAND, THRESHING GROUND and ARABLE LAND were being apportioned by SMALL FARMERS … IT WAS MAINLY SPADE CULTURE … OWING TO THE DIFFICULTY OF PLOUGHING the sparsely scattered fields covered with grass, with their few clear spaces, THEIR BLACK SOIL STIFF AS BLACK IRON** …*
>
> *There were people moving along with **BUNDLES OF BARK** … countless sacks of **PLUCKED FLOWERS**, … loads of **FLAX AND HEMP** bundles, quantities of **HONEY, PEACOCKS' TAIL FEATHERS, WREATHS OF WAX, LOGS, and GRASS. VILLAGE WIVES hastened en route for neighbouring villages, all intent on thoughts of SALE and bearing on their heads BASKETS FILLED WITH VARIOUS GATHERED FOREST FRUITS.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"How would you CLASSIFY the people described in the text in terms of their OCCUPATIONS?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The answer, and note that it deliberately cuts across §5.3's neat categories:** **small farmers practising SPADE (hoe) cultivation** · **gatherers of forest produce** (bark, flowers, honey, wax, feathers, logs, grass) · **collectors of fibre crops** (flax, hemp) · and ⭐ **women traders — *"village wives … all intent on thoughts of SALE"*, carrying goods to neighbouring villages.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WHY THIS SOURCE IS SO VALUABLE — three reasons, and they should all appear in your answer:**
> 1. ⭐ **It confirms §5.2's limits from the inside.** *"**It was mainly SPADE CULTURE … owing to the DIFFICULTY OF PLOUGHING** … their **black soil stiff as black iron**."* **Here is a concrete reason the plough was not universal — the soil resisted it.** *Not backwardness; **terrain**.
> 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It shows the forest and the field as ONE economy, not two.** *The same settlement farms rice **and** gathers bark, honey and wax **and** sells them onward. **§5.4's "people beyond the reach of officials" are not a separate world — they are the same villagers, in a different season.**
> 3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WOMEN ARE THE TRADERS.** *"Village wives hastened en route for neighbouring villages, all intent on thoughts of **sale**."* **Compare the legal texts of §5.4 that said women should have no independent access to resources — and compare Prabhavati Gupta.** *Prescription says one thing; **a court poet describing a scene incidentally records another.** ⭐ **Banabhatta had no argument to make about women's economic activity, which is exactly why his testimony is good evidence.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note NCERT's own framing — *"an EXTREMELY RARE representation"*.** *It survives **only because Harsha's court poet needed to set a scene.** **A glimpse of ordinary rural life reaches us as a by-product of royal biography** — §8's problem in one sentence.

**📖 SOURCE 9 — *The Malabar coast* (the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> **The text:** *"composed by **an ANONYMOUS GREEK SAILOR (c. first century CE)**"*. ⭐ **NCERT explains the title:** *"***PERIPLUS*** is a Greek word meaning **SAILING AROUND**, and ***ERYTHRAEAN*** was the Greek name for **THE RED SEA**."*
>
> *"They (i.e. traders from abroad) **send LARGE SHIPS to these market-towns on account of the GREAT QUANTITY AND BULK OF PEPPER and MALABATHRUM** (possibly cinnamon, produced in these regions).*
>
> ***THERE ARE IMPORTED here, in the first place, A GREAT QUANTITY OF COIN; TOPAZ … ANTIMONY** (a mineral used as a colouring substance), **CORAL, CRUDE GLASS, COPPER, TIN, LEAD** …*
>
> ***THERE IS EXPORTED PEPPER**, which is produced in quantity **in only one region near these markets** … Besides this there are exported great quantities of **FINE PEARLS, IVORY, SILK CLOTH, … TRANSPARENT STONES OF ALL KINDS, DIAMONDS AND SAPPHIRES, and TORTOISE SHELL.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"WHY DID THE AUTHOR COMPILE THIS LIST?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The answer is the whole point of the source, and it is a source-criticism answer, not a trade answer.** ⭐ **He was a SAILOR, and the *Periplus* is a COMMERCIAL HANDBOOK — a practical guide for traders telling them what to bring and what to buy at each port.** *It is not a description of India for its own sake.* **So it is superb evidence of what was SOLD AT PORTS TO FOREIGNERS, and no evidence at all of what Indians produced, ate or used inland.** *Compare Theme 1's Mesopotamian texts listing Meluhha's products — **the same kind of source, three thousand years earlier, with the same bias: foreigners record what they can buy.***
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT ITEM IN THE LIST IS THE FIRST IMPORT: *"IN THE FIRST PLACE, A GREAT QUANTITY OF COIN."*** ⭐ **Rome paid for Indian goods largely in BULLION** — which is exactly why §6.4 says *"hoards of ROMAN COINS have been found from archaeological sites in SOUTH INDIA"* even though **"south India was NOT part of the Roman Empire."** *Two independent kinds of evidence — a Greek handbook and coin hoards in Tamil country — confirming one another. **That is corroboration, and naming it as such is what §7–§8 is training you to do.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And NCERT adds the archaeological match:** ⭐ *"Archaeological evidence of **A BEAD-MAKING INDUSTRY, USING PRECIOUS AND SEMI-PRECIOUS STONES**, has been found in **KODUMANAL (TAMIL NADU)**. It is likely that **local traders brought the stones mentioned in the *Periplus* from sites such as these to the coastal ports.**"* **The *Periplus* names the goods at the port; the excavation finds the workshop inland. Text and spade, meeting.**

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE KUSHANA WEIGHT-STANDARD FACT IS THE BEST IN §6.4 AND ALMOST NOBODY USES IT.** ⭐ **Kushana gold coins were *"virtually identical in weight"* with **Roman** and **Parthian** issues.** *A common weight standard across three empires means **coins could pass between currency zones without being re-weighed or re-assayed** — which is an enormous reduction in the friction of long-distance trade. *(Compare Theme 1: **Dilmun adopting the Harappan weight standard.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two chapters, one principle: a shared standard is the deepest evidence of an integrated trading world.**)*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE PUNCH-MARKED COIN POINT IS THE ONE FOR A MAINS ANSWER ON EARLY STATES: WE ARE NOT SURE WHO ISSUED THEM.** *NCERT allows **kings**, but also **"merchants, bankers and townspeople."*** ⭐ **Currency in the earliest phase may not have been a state monopoly at all.** *That matters, because minting is usually treated as a defining attribute of sovereignty — **and here the chapter declines to assume it.** *(Note also the symbols were **punched**, not struck with a die — which is **why they carry no ruler's name or portrait**, and why the Indo-Greeks' named-and-portrayed coins are a genuine innovation.)*

**⚖️ THE POST-600 CE DEBATE — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ a small, perfect historiographical set piece**

```
   ** THE OBSERVATION: "From c.SIXTH CENTURY CE ONWARDS, FINDS
      OF GOLD COINS TAPER OFF." **
   ** THE QUESTION: "DOES THIS INDICATE THAT THERE WAS SOME
      KIND OF AN ECONOMIC CRISIS? HISTORIANS ARE DIVIDED ON
      THIS ISSUE." **

   ** VIEW A -- YES, DECLINE: **
   ** "Some suggest that WITH THE COLLAPSE OF THE WESTERN
      ROMAN EMPIRE LONG-DISTANCE TRADE DECLINED, and this
      AFFECTED THE PROSPERITY OF THE STATES, COMMUNITIES AND
      REGIONS THAT HAD BENEFITED FROM IT." **

   ** VIEW B -- NO, TRANSFORMATION: **
   ** "Others argue that NEW TOWNS AND NETWORKS OF TRADE
      STARTED EMERGING AROUND THIS TIME. They also point out
      that THOUGH FINDS OF COINS OF THAT TIME ARE FEWER, COINS
      CONTINUE TO BE MENTIONED IN INSCRIPTIONS AND TEXTS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND NCERT'S OWN SUGGESTION, WHICH IS THE
      CLEVEREST LINE IN THE SECTION: **
   ** "COULD IT BE THAT THERE ARE FEWER FINDS BECAUSE COINS
      WERE IN CIRCULATION RATHER THAN BEING HOARDED?" **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT LAST QUESTION IS A DIRECT APPLICATION OF THEME 1 §11's DISCARD BIAS, AND SPOTTING THAT IS A REAL MARK OF UNDERSTANDING.** ⭐ **Archaeologists find HOARDS — coins somebody buried and never recovered. A coin in active circulation gets spent, worn, re-melted and eventually lost to the record.** *So **fewer coin finds may indicate MORE monetisation, not less** — a healthier economy in which money moved instead of being buried. **Theme 1 §4.2 made exactly this point about Harappan gold: *"all the gold jewellery found at Harappan sites was recovered from HOARDS."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The same methodological trap, two thousand years apart, and NCERT expects you to recognise it.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Also note View B's textual argument, which is a model of cross-checking source types:** *"though **finds** of coins are fewer, **coins continue to be MENTIONED IN INSCRIPTIONS AND TEXTS.**" ⭐ **When the archaeology and the documents disagree, that disagreement is itself data** — and here it favours the "coins existed but weren't hoarded" reading.*

> **NCERT's framing:** *"So far, we have been studying **excerpts from inscriptions** … But **HOW DO HISTORIANS FIND OUT WHAT IS WRITTEN ON THEM?**"*

### 7.1 Deciphering Brahmi ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHY BRAHMI MATTERS BEYOND THIS CHAPTER: **
   ** "MOST SCRIPTS USED TO WRITE MODERN INDIAN LANGUAGES ARE
      DERIVED FROM BRAHMI, THE SCRIPT USED IN MOST ASOKAN
      INSCRIPTIONS." **

   ** THE METHOD -- WORKING BACKWARDS: **
   ** "From THE LATE EIGHTEENTH CENTURY, EUROPEAN SCHOLARS
      AIDED BY INDIAN PANDITS WORKED BACKWARDS FROM
      CONTEMPORARY BENGALI AND DEVANAGARI (the script used to
      write Hindi) MANUSCRIPTS, COMPARING THEIR LETTERS WITH
      OLDER SPECIMENS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE MISTAKE THAT DELAYED EVERYTHING: **
   ** "Scholars who studied early inscriptions SOMETIMES
      ASSUMED THESE WERE IN SANSKRIT, ALTHOUGH THE EARLIEST
      INSCRIPTIONS WERE, IN FACT, IN PRAKRIT." **

   ** THE RESULT: "It was ONLY AFTER DECADES OF PAINSTAKING
      INVESTIGATIONS BY SEVERAL EPIGRAPHISTS that
      ⭐ JAMES PRINSEP WAS ABLE TO DECIPHER ASOKAN BRAHMI
      IN 1838." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SANSKRIT ASSUMPTION IS THIS CHAPTER'S VERSION OF CUNNINGHAM'S BLIND SPOT, AND THE PARALLEL IS EXACT — MAKE IT.** ⭐ **Scholars assumed the earliest inscriptions were in SANSKRIT, the prestige language of learning, when they were actually in PRAKRIT, *"a name for languages used by ORDINARY PEOPLE."*** *The decipherment was delayed **by decades** by an assumption about what an ancient Indian text ought to be.* **Cunningham could not see Harappa because he expected Indian history to begin in the Ganga valley; the epigraphists could not read Brahmi because they expected it to be Sanskrit.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***In both cases the obstacle was not missing evidence but a wrong expectation.*** *(And Fig. 2.11 makes the point visually — **Asokan Brahmi shown with Devanagari equivalents**, with NCERT asking *"Do some Devanagari letters appear similar to Brahmi?"*)*

### 7.2 How Kharosthi was read — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ a completely different story

```
   ** "The story of the decipherment of KHAROSTHI, THE SCRIPT
      USED IN INSCRIPTIONS IN THE NORTHWEST, IS DIFFERENT." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE KEY: BILINGUAL COINS **
   ** "finds of COINS OF INDO-GREEK KINGS who ruled over the
      area (c. second-first centuries BCE) HAVE FACILITATED
      MATTERS. THESE COINS CONTAIN THE NAMES OF KINGS WRITTEN
      IN GREEK AND KHAROSTHI SCRIPTS." **

   ** THE PROCEDURE: "EUROPEAN SCHOLARS WHO COULD READ THE
      FORMER COMPARED THE LETTERS. For instance, ⭐ THE SYMBOL
      FOR 'A' COULD BE FOUND IN BOTH SCRIPTS FOR WRITING NAMES
      SUCH AS APOLLODOTUS." **

   ** THE FINISH: "With PRINSEP IDENTIFYING THE LANGUAGE OF
      THE KHAROSTHI INSCRIPTIONS AS PRAKRIT, IT BECAME
      POSSIBLE TO READ LONGER INSCRIPTIONS AS WELL." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TWO DECIPHERMENTS USED OPPOSITE METHODS, AND THE CONTRAST IS EXAM-READY:**

| | **BRAHMI** | **KHAROSTHI** |
|---|---|---|
| **Method** | ⭐ **working BACKWARDS in time** — comparing modern **Bengali and Devanagari** manuscripts with progressively older specimens | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BILINGUAL comparison** — Indo-Greek coins bearing the same royal name in **GREEK and KHAROSTHI** |
| **The key resource** | **descendant scripts still in use** | **a script the scholars already knew (Greek)** |
| **Difficulty** | *"decades of painstaking investigations"*, delayed by the **Sanskrit assumption** | ⭐ *"finds of coins … have **FACILITATED matters**"* |
| **The worked example** | the letter **"a"**, c.250 BCE vs c.500 CE *(palaeography)* | the symbol for **"a"** in **APOLLODOTUS** |
| **Who finished it** | ⭐ **James Prinsep, 1838** | Prinsep again — by **identifying the language as PRAKRIT** |

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Kharosthi story is the ROSETTA STONE principle — a known language beside an unknown one.** ⭐ *And it is exactly what **Theme 1 §11 said would settle the Harappan script**: *"IF A BILINGUAL INSCRIPTION IS FOUND, questions about the languages spoken by the Harappans could perhaps be put to rest."* **Kharosthi had its bilingual; Harappan has not found one. That single difference of luck is why one script is read and the other is not.*** *(⭐ And note **the crucial second step even here**: knowing the letters was not enough — Prinsep still had to **identify the LANGUAGE as Prakrit** before longer texts could be read. **A script and a language are two separate problems.**)*

### 7.3 Historical evidence from inscriptions ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**📖 SOURCE 10 — *The orders of the king* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"**Thus speaks king DEVANAMPIYA PIYADASSI:** In the past, **THERE WERE NO ARRANGEMENTS FOR DISPOSING AFFAIRS, NOR FOR RECEIVING REGULAR REPORTS.** But I have made the following (arrangement). ⭐ ***PATIVEDAKAS* SHOULD REPORT TO ME ABOUT THE AFFAIRS OF THE PEOPLE AT ALL TIMES, ANYWHERE, WHETHER I AM EATING, IN THE INNER APARTMENT, IN THE BEDROOM, IN THE COW PEN, BEING CARRIED (possibly in a palanquin), OR IN THE GARDEN. AND I WILL DISPOSE OF THE AFFAIRS OF THE PEOPLE EVERYWHERE.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT USES THIS SOURCE TO TEACH FOUR SEPARATE POINTS OF EPIGRAPHIC METHOD. Learn all four — they are the answer to Exercise 5 and half of Exercise 6.**
>
> **(1) ⭐ THE NAME IS ABSENT.** *"the name of the ruler, **ASOKA, IS NOT MENTIONED** in the inscription. What is used instead are **TITLES**: ***DEVANAMPIYA***, *"beloved of the gods"*, and ***PIYADASSI***, *"pleasant to behold"*."*
>
> **(2) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ HOW THE ATTRIBUTION IS MADE.** *"The name Asoka **is mentioned in SOME OTHER INSCRIPTIONS, WHICH ALSO CONTAIN THESE TITLES.** After examining all these inscriptions, and finding that **they MATCH in terms of CONTENT, STYLE, LANGUAGE AND PALAEOGRAPHY**, epigraphists have concluded that **they were issued by the same ruler.**"* **Four criteria — memorise them.**
>
> **(3) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE KING'S CLAIM MUST BE TESTED.** *"You may also have noticed that **Asoka CLAIMS that EARLIER RULERS HAD NO ARRANGEMENTS TO RECEIVE REPORTS. IF YOU CONSIDER THE POLITICAL HISTORY OF THE SUBCONTINENT PRIOR TO ASOKA, DO YOU THINK THIS STATEMENT IS TRUE?**"* ⭐ **NCERT's instruction is the exam-usable one: *"HISTORIANS HAVE TO CONSTANTLY ASSESS STATEMENTS MADE IN INSCRIPTIONS TO JUDGE WHETHER THEY ARE TRUE, PLAUSIBLE OR EXAGGERATIONS."*** *(And on the evidence of §2 and §3 — Magadha's bureaucracy, Megasthenes' committees, the *Arthashastra*'s minute administrative detail — **the claim is almost certainly an exaggeration.** ⭐ **Denigrating one's predecessors is a standard royal move, and the inscription is a self-portrait.**)*
>
> **(4) ⭐ THE BRACKETS ARE THE EDITOR'S.** *"Did you notice that **there are WORDS WITHIN BRACKETS? EPIGRAPHISTS SOMETIMES ADD THESE TO MAKE THE MEANING OF SENTENCES CLEAR. THIS HAS TO BE DONE CAREFULLY, TO ENSURE THAT THE INTENDED MEANING OF THE AUTHOR IS NOT CHANGED.**"* **What you read as "the source" already contains a modern scholar's interpretive choices.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's question on the *pativedaka*: *"Epigraphists have TRANSLATED the term pativedaka as REPORTER. In what ways would the functions of the pativedaka have been DIFFERENT from those we generally associate with reporters today?"*** ⭐ **A good answer: a *pativedaka* was **a state official reporting TO the ruler, privately**; a modern reporter works **for the public, and often AGAINST the government's preference.** *One carries information UP a hierarchy; the other carries it OUTWARD. **A translation can carry a modern institution's assumptions into an ancient one** — which is the same analogy trap as Theme 1's "curry stones" and the "priest-king", now operating inside a single word.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And do not miss what the edict actually reveals about kingship — the list is extraordinary.** *Reports are to reach him **"whether I am EATING, IN THE INNER APARTMENT, IN THE BEDROOM, IN THE COW PEN, BEING CARRIED, OR IN THE GARDEN."*** ⭐ **A king advertising that he has abolished his own privacy is making a claim about accessibility and diligence** — it is the exact opposite of the Kushana god-king in his shrine or Samudragupta *"incomprehensible"* in his prashasti. **Three models of royal self-presentation in one chapter: the accessible administrator, the god, and the poet's hero.**

**📖 SOURCE 11 — *The anguish of the king* (the Kalinga edict) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"When the king **DEVANAMPIYA PIYADASSI had been ruling for EIGHT YEARS**, the (country of the) **KALINGAS** (present-day coastal Orissa) **was conquered by (him). ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY THOUSAND MEN WERE DEPORTED, A HUNDRED THOUSAND WERE KILLED, AND MANY MORE DIED.**
>
> *After that, now that (the country of) the Kalingas has been taken, **Devanampiya (is devoted) to AN INTENSE STUDY OF DHAMMA, TO THE LOVE OF DHAMMA, AND TO INSTRUCTING (THE PEOPLE) IN DHAMMA.**
>
> ***THIS IS THE REPENTANCE OF DEVANAMPIYA ON ACCOUNT OF HIS CONQUEST OF THE (COUNTRY OF THE) KALINGAS. For this is considered VERY PAINFUL AND DEPLORABLE by Devanampiya that, WHILE ONE IS CONQUERING AN UNCONQUERED (COUNTRY), SLAUGHTER, DEATH AND DEPORTATION OF PEOPLE (TAKE PLACE) THERE …***"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOW THE MOST IMPORTANT OBSERVATION IN THE ENTIRE CHAPTER — NCERT'S OWN, AND IT IS DEVASTATING:**
>
> > *"While Asokan inscriptions **HAVE BEEN FOUND IN PRESENT-DAY ORISSA, THE ONE DEPICTING HIS ANGUISH IS MISSING. IN OTHER WORDS, THE INSCRIPTION HAS NOT BEEN FOUND IN THE REGION THAT WAS CONQUERED.** What are we to make of that? **IS IT THAT THE ANGUISH OF THE RECENT CONQUEST WAS TOO PAINFUL IN THE REGION, AND THEREFORE THE RULER WAS UNABLE TO ADDRESS THE ISSUE?**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THINK ABOUT WHAT THIS MEANS, BECAUSE IT IS THE CHAPTER'S FINEST PIECE OF SOURCE CRITICISM.** ⭐ **The famous expression of remorse for Kalinga was published EVERYWHERE EXCEPT KALINGA.** *The people who were deported and killed — **the hundred and fifty thousand and the hundred thousand — were the one audience not shown the apology.*** **So the edict's remorse was addressed to the empire at large, not to the injured.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HOW TO USE THIS, AND IT IS WORTH REAL MARKS:** *NCERT introduces Source 11 as *"often interpreted as reflecting the anguish of the ruler, as well as marking a change in his attitude towards warfare"* — **and then says the situation *"becomes more complex once we move beyond reading the inscription AT FACE VALUE."*** **Note the careful position: NCERT does NOT say the remorse was insincere. It says the DISTRIBUTION of the text complicates a simple reading.** *Write it that way. **A one-line version worth having: "Asoka's remorse survives as a public document, and where a public document was NOT displayed is as informative as where it was."***
>
> 🔴 **NCERT's Discuss box asks exactly this: *"Look at Map 2 and discuss THE LOCATION of Asokan inscriptions. DO YOU NOTICE ANY PATTERNS?"*** ⭐ **Patterns to state:** *(i) they cluster around **the five political centres** and along **routes of communication** — Taxila, Ujjayini, Pataliputra; (ii) they mark the **frontiers** — Kandahar, Mansehra and Shahbazgarhi in the north-west, and the southern group (Maski, Brahmagiri, Siddapur, Jatinga Rameshwar) near the **Cholas, Keralaputras and Pandyas**; (iii) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **the southern inscriptions sit at the EDGE of, and address, peoples NOT inside the empire** — which is why NCERT's caption asks *"**Could rulers have engraved inscriptions in areas that were NOT INCLUDED within their empire?**"*; and (iv) **the Kalinga anomaly above.**

## 8️⃣ The Limitations of Inscriptional Evidence ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⛔⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE HIGHEST-VALUE SECTION IN THE CHAPTER. It is Exercise 5 in full, it is the frame for Exercise 7 (the D.C. Sircar question), and its argument transfers to every source type in the entire syllabus. Learn it as a structured list.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** PROBLEM 1 -- TECHNICAL **
   ================================================================
   * ** "letters are VERY FAINTLY ENGRAVED, and thus
     RECONSTRUCTIONS ARE UNCERTAIN" **
   * ** "inscriptions may be DAMAGED OR LETTERS MISSING" **
   * ⭐ ** "it is NOT ALWAYS EASY TO BE SURE ABOUT THE EXACT
     MEANING OF THE WORDS used in inscriptions, SOME OF WHICH
     MAY BE SPECIFIC TO A PARTICULAR PLACE OR TIME" **
   * ** "scholars are CONSTANTLY DEBATING AND DISCUSSING
     ALTERNATIVE WAYS OF READING INSCRIPTIONS" **

   ================================================================
   ** PROBLEM 2 -- THE SAMPLE IS A FRACTION **
   ================================================================
   * ** "Although SEVERAL THOUSAND inscriptions have been
     discovered, NOT ALL HAVE BEEN DECIPHERED, PUBLISHED AND
     TRANSLATED." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "Besides, MANY MORE INSCRIPTIONS MUST HAVE
     EXISTED, WHICH HAVE NOT SURVIVED THE RAVAGES OF TIME.
     SO WHAT IS AVAILABLE AT PRESENT IS PROBABLY ONLY A
     FRACTION OF WHAT WAS INSCRIBED." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** PROBLEM 3 -- "PERHAPS MORE FUNDAMENTAL":
      WHAT WAS NEVER INSCRIBED AT ALL **
   ================================================================
   ** "NOT EVERYTHING THAT WE MAY CONSIDER POLITICALLY OR
      ECONOMICALLY SIGNIFICANT WAS NECESSARILY RECORDED IN
      INSCRIPTIONS." **

   ⭐ ** "For instance, ROUTINE AGRICULTURAL PRACTICES AND THE
      JOYS AND SORROWS OF DAILY EXISTENCE FIND NO MENTION IN
      INSCRIPTIONS, WHICH FOCUS, MORE OFTEN THAN NOT, ON
      GRAND, UNIQUE EVENTS." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** PROBLEM 4 -- THE BUILT-IN PERSPECTIVE **
   ================================================================
   ** "the content of inscriptions ALMOST INVARIABLY PROJECTS
      THE PERSPECTIVE OF THE PERSON(S) WHO COMMISSIONED THEM.
      AS SUCH, THEY NEED TO BE JUXTAPOSED WITH OTHER
      PERSPECTIVES SO AS TO ARRIVE AT A BETTER UNDERSTANDING
      OF THE PAST." **

   ** THE CONCLUSION: "THUS EPIGRAPHY ALONE DOES NOT PROVIDE A
      FULL UNDERSTANDING OF POLITICAL AND ECONOMIC
      HISTORY." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PROBLEM 3 IS THE DEEP ONE AND IT IS WORTH STATING AS A PRINCIPLE: AN INSCRIPTION IS A RECORD OF THE EXCEPTIONAL, BY DEFINITION.** ⭐ **Nobody carves a stone to record that the harvest was ordinary.** *The medium is expensive, permanent and public — so it is reserved for **the grand and the unique**, which means **the routine is invisible by construction, not by accident.***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOTE HOW PERFECTLY THE CHAPTER HAS ALREADY DEMONSTRATED PROBLEM 4, THREE TIMES. Use these as your examples — they are far better than restating the principle:**
> 1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **§5.2 — irrigation.** *"**Communities AS WELL AS individuals** organised the construction of irrigation works. **The LATTER, usually POWERFUL MEN including kings, often RECORDED such activities in inscriptions.**" **Both built; only one recorded.**
> 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **§7.3 — the Kalinga edict.** *The remorse appears everywhere **except in Kalinga**.*
> 3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **§2.1 — the *ganas*.** *States that lasted **nearly a thousand years** are hard to reconstruct **"due to the lack of sources"** — because collective rule commissions no royal inscriptions.*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE ANSWER TO EXERCISE 7, WHICH IS THE BEST QUESTION IN THE CHAPTER.** *D.C. Sircar: **"There is NO ASPECT of life, culture and activities of the Indians that is NOT REFLECTED in inscriptions."*** ⭐ **You should DISAGREE, respectfully and with evidence** — and note the delicious irony that **Sircar is himself the epigraphist listed in Timeline 2** for *Indian Epigraphy* and the *Indian Epigraphical Glossary*.
> - ✅ **Concede the strength first:** inscriptions cover an astonishing range — **royal proclamations (Asoka), land transactions (Danguna), public works (Sudarshana lake), donations by a goldsmith's wife (Nagapiya), guilds, occupations, and even a queen's grant.** **§6.2's votive inscriptions genuinely do name washermen, weavers, potters and scribes.**
> - ❌ **Then the case against, using §8's four problems:** *what survives is **"only a fraction"**; the routine and the everyday are **absent by construction**; and the perspective is **always the commissioner's**.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the decisive examples:** ***the ganas* who lasted a thousand years and left almost nothing; the COMMUNITIES who built irrigation and were never recorded; the *"pastoralists, fisherfolk and hunter-gatherers, mobile or semi-sedentary artisans and shifting cultivators"* of §5.4 who *"did not keep detailed records"*; and the peasants of the *Gandatindu Jataka*, whose view of taxation survives ONLY because a Buddhist storyteller wrote it down.*
> - ✅ **Conclude with NCERT's own resolution, which is not rejection but juxtaposition:** *inscriptions *"need to be **JUXTAPOSED WITH OTHER PERSPECTIVES**"* — texts, coins, archaeology and stories. **Sircar's claim describes epigraphy's RANGE; it cannot survive as a claim about epigraphy's SUFFICIENCY.**

**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ AND THE CHAPTER'S FINAL PARAGRAPH — the shifting agenda of history itself:**

> *"Also, **historians often QUESTION BOTH OLD AND NEW EVIDENCE.** Scholars in **the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries were PRIMARILY INTERESTED IN THE HISTORIES OF KINGS.** From **the mid-twentieth century onwards, issues such as ECONOMIC CHANGE, and THE WAYS IN WHICH DIFFERENT SOCIAL GROUPS EMERGED have assumed far more importance.** ⭐ **Recent decades have seen A MUCH GREATER PREOCCUPATION WITH HISTORIES OF MARGINALISED GROUPS. This will probably lead to FRESH INVESTIGATIONS OF OLD SOURCES, and THE DEVELOPMENT OF NEW STRATEGIES OF ANALYSIS.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS CLOSES THE CIRCLE OPENED IN §1, AND SAYING SO IS THE MARK OF HAVING READ THE CHAPTER AS A WHOLE.** *§1 described the first shift — **from dynastic lineages to the CONTEXT of political history**. §8 describes the second and third — **to economic and social change, and then to MARGINALISED GROUPS.*** ⭐ **The evidence has not changed; the questions have.** *And NCERT's last phrase is the promise: **"fresh investigations of OLD sources"** — the same inscriptions, asked new questions, will yield new history. *(Exactly as **Theme 1 §11** ended by naming **gender** as *"a whole new area for future work."* **Both chapters of Part I close by pointing at what has not yet been asked.**)*

| The *Arthashastra* was written entirely by Kautilya | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"PARTS of which were PROBABLY composed by Kautilya or Chanakya"*** |
| The Greek army figures are accepted | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **600,000 foot, 30,000 cavalry, 9,000 elephants** — *"**SOME HISTORIANS CONSIDER THESE ACCOUNTS TO BE EXAGGERATED**"* |
| A chief collects taxes | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A chief receives **GIFTS** *(unlike kings, who collect **taxes**)* and **redistributes** them; chiefdoms generally have **no regular armies or officials** |
| The Kushanas were of Indian origin and used no titles | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ They ruled **from Central Asia to northwest India**, installed **colossal statues in shrines at MAT near MATHURA and in Afghanistan**, and adopted ***DEVAPUTRA*, "son of god"** — possibly inspired by **Chinese** rulers |
| The Prayaga Prashasti is a factual record | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Composed **in SANSKRIT by HARISHENA, court poet of SAMUDRAGUPTA**; treasured *"as works of **POETRY** rather than as accounts that were **literally true**"*. Also called the **Allahabad Pillar Inscription** |
| *Samantas* were royal officials | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Men who maintained themselves through LOCAL RESOURCES including control over land**, offering **homage and military support**. ⭐ **Powerful *samantas* could become kings; weak kings could be reduced to subordination** |
| The iron ploughshare spread across the subcontinent | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Parts of PUNJAB and RAJASTHAN (semi-arid) did not adopt it till the TWENTIETH CENTURY**; hilly north-eastern and central areas used **HOE agriculture**, *"much better suited to the terrain"* |
| Transplantation reduced labour | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It raised yields but *"meant **BACK-BREAKING WORK** for the producer"* |
| The Sudarshana lake was built by Rudradaman | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Built by **a LOCAL GOVERNOR during MAURYAN rule**; **Rudradaman (a SHAKA) REPAIRED it** *"using his own resources, without imposing any tax"*; **repaired AGAIN by a GUPTA ruler**, recorded on the **same rock at GIRNAR** |
| *Gahapati* means a farmer | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The owner/master/head of a HOUSEHOLD**, controlling **women, children, slaves and workers** *and* **land, animals and resources**; also used as a **status marker for the urban elite, including wealthy merchants** |
| The Tamil terms mean the same as the Pali ones | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Tamil: *VELLALAR* (large landowners), *UZHAVAR* (ploughmen), *ADIMAI* (slaves).** ***GAHAPATI*** in Pali covered **small peasants and large landholders** |
| Most land grants were on stone | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MOST were on COPPER PLATES**, given to the recipient as a record; **most were in SANSKRIT**, with local languages appearing **especially from the seventh century** |
| An *agrahara* holder paid revenue to the king | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Usually EXEMPTED from paying**, and **often given the RIGHT TO COLLECT dues from the local people** |
| Women could hold land in early India | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Sanskrit legal texts said they could NOT** — yet **PRABHAVATI GUPTA**, daughter of **Chandragupta II**, married into the **VAKATAKAS**, granted a village. Either she was **exceptional as a queen**, or *"**the provisions of legal texts were not uniformly implemented**"* |
| Historians agree on what land grants signify | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"A subject of **HEATED DEBATE**"* — **extension of agriculture to new areas** vs **weakening political power and winning allies**, with kings projecting themselves as *"supermen… because they were losing control"* |
| NBPW was everyday pottery | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Northern Black Polished Ware** — *"fine pottery bowls and dishes, with a **glossy finish**, **probably used by RICH people**"* |
| *Shrenis* were caste groups | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GUILDS** — *"organisations of **craft producers and merchants**"* which **procured raw materials, regulated production and marketed the finished product** |
| The first coins with rulers' names were Mauryan | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The INDO-GREEKS** issued *"the first coins to bear the **NAMES AND IMAGES OF RULERS**"* (c. second century BCE) |
| Punch-marked coins were certainly royal | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"It is **ALSO LIKELY that MERCHANTS, BANKERS AND TOWNSPEOPLE issued some** of these coins"* |
| The Guptas issued the largest gold hoards | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The KUSHANAS** issued *"the **largest hoards of gold coins**"*, **matched in weight to ROMAN and PARTHIAN issues**. Gupta coins were *"the most **spectacular**"* and **remarkable for their PURITY** |
| Fewer gold coins after 600 CE proves economic crisis | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Historians are DIVIDED**; NCERT asks — *"could it be that there are **fewer finds because coins were IN CIRCULATION rather than being HOARDED**?"* |
| Roman coins in south India mean Roman rule | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**South India was NOT part of the Roman Empire**, but there were close connections **through trade**"* |
| Kharosthi was deciphered the same way as Brahmi | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Kharosthi via BILINGUAL INDO-GREEK COINS (Greek + Kharosthi)**, e.g. the "a" in **APOLLODOTUS**; **Brahmi by working BACKWARDS from Bengali and Devanagari manuscripts** |
| Asoka's Kalinga edict was displayed in Kalinga | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"The inscription has NOT been found in the region that was CONQUERED"*** — the anguish edict is missing from present-day Orissa |
| Inscriptions reflect every aspect of Indian life | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **D.C. Sircar's claim, which Exercise 7 asks you to weigh.** §8: what survives is *"only a **FRACTION**"*; the routine and everyday go unrecorded; and content *"almost invariably projects **the perspective of the person(s) who commissioned them**"* |

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Epigraphy** | ⭐ **The study of inscriptions** |
| **Palaeography** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Dating by **styles of writing** — letter forms change traceably over time |
| **Numismatics** | **The study of coins** — visual elements, **metallurgical analysis**, and **find contexts** |
| ***Janapada*** | *"The land where a ***jana*** (a people, clan or tribe) **sets its foot or settles**"* |
| ***Gana* / *sangha*** | ⭐ An **oligarchy** — power shared among men called ***rajas***; Mahavira and the Buddha came from such states |
| **Oligarchy** | *"A form of government where power is exercised by **a group of men**"* |
| **Dharmasutras** | **Sanskrit texts by Brahmanas from c.6th century BCE**, laying down **norms for rulers and other social categories** |
| ***Dhamma*** *(Asoka's)* | **Respect for elders · generosity to Brahmanas and renouncers · kindness to slaves and servants · respect for other religions and traditions** |
| ***Dhamma mahamatta*** | **Special officers appointed to spread the message of *dhamma*** |
| ***Devanampiya* / *piyadassi*** | ⭐ *"Beloved of the gods"* / *"pleasant to behold"* — **Asoka's titles in the inscriptions** |
| ***Pativedaka*** | **Reporter** — an official required to report to Asoka *"at all times, anywhere"* |
| **Chief** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Position **may or may not be hereditary**; supported by **kinfolk**; receives **GIFTS** (not taxes) and **redistributes** them; no regular army or officials |
| ***Devaputra*** | **"Son of god"** — a **Kushana** royal title, possibly inspired by Chinese rulers |
| ***Prashasti*** | ⭐ A composition **in praise of kings and patrons, by poets** — e.g. the **Prayaga Prashasti** by **Harishena** |
| ***Samanta*** | **A man maintaining himself through LOCAL RESOURCES including land**, who **offered homage and military support**; could rise to kingship |
| **Transplantation** | Paddy technique — **seeds broadcast first, saplings then moved to waterlogged fields**; higher survival and yields, **back-breaking labour** |
| ***Gahapati*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Owner/master/head of a household**, controlling **its people AND its resources**; also a **status marker for the urban elite** |
| ***Vellalar* / *uzhavar* / *adimai*** | **Large landowners / ploughmen / slaves** — the Tamil (Sangam) categories |
| ***Agrahara*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Land granted to a Brahmana**, usually **exempt from revenue** and often carrying **the right to collect dues from local people** |
| ***Gramakutumbina*** | **Householders/peasants living in a village** *(from the Danguna charter)* |
| **Votive inscription** | ⭐ **A record of a gift made to a religious institution** — often naming the **donor and their occupation** |
| ***Shreni*** | **A guild** — an organisation of **craft producers and merchants** |
| ***Masattuvan* / *setthi* / *satthavaha*** | **Successful merchants** — Tamil / Prakrit terms |
| **NBPW** | **Northern Black Polished Ware** — fine glossy pottery, **probably used by rich people** |
| ***Periplus*** | Greek, **"sailing around"** — a **commercial handbook**; *"Erythraean"* = the **Red Sea** |
| **Punch-marked coins** | ⭐ Earliest Indian coins — **silver and copper, symbols PUNCHED onto the surface**, from c. sixth century BCE |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in ten lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The subject became knowable in 1838**, when **James Prinsep** deciphered **Brahmi and Kharosthi** and found a king called ***Piyadassi*** — identifiable as **Asoka** only by cross-checking a few inscriptions against Buddhist texts.
2. **The sixth century BCE is the turning point** — early states, cities, iron, coinage, Buddhism and Jainism. Of the **sixteen mahajanapadas**, some were **monarchies** and some ***ganas*/*sanghas*** whose thousand-year histories are nearly invisible **because they commissioned no royal inscriptions**.
3. **Magadha rose** for **material reasons** (agriculture, Jharkhand iron, elephants, the Ganga) — or, for Buddhist and Jaina writers, because of **ruthlessly ambitious kings**. The capital moved from **Rajagaha** to **Pataliputra**.
4. **The Mauryas** ruled from **five centres chosen for trade and gold**, and Asoka was **the first ruler to address his subjects on stone**. ⭐ **But identical edicts prove uniform PROCLAMATION, not uniform ADMINISTRATION** — and the empire lasted only about **150 years**.
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Nineteenth-century historians over-weighted the Mauryas** because they were writing under an empire of their own — Thread 5 of this book in action.
6. **Post-Mauryan kingship was claimed, not inherited**: southern **chiefs took gifts and redistributed them**; the **Kushanas installed themselves in shrines and called themselves *devaputra***; the **Guptas hired poets**. ⭐ **And the louder the claim, the weaker the position may have been.**
7. **The countryside was pressed for revenue** — *"robbers at night and tax collectors during the day"* — and **peasants could flee to the forest**, which is why the state turned to **raising output**: iron ploughshare, transplantation, irrigation. **None of it was universal**, and none of it was equally shared.
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Land grants transferred not just income but authority** — *"not to be entered by soldiers and policemen"*, *"together with major and minor taxes"*, *"you should obey all his commands."* **Whether they show expansion or decay is still debated.**
9. **Towns grew on routes**, filled with **named craftspeople, guilds and merchants**, tied by coinage into a world where **Kushana gold matched Roman weights** and **Roman coins reached a south India Rome never ruled**.
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the chapter ends by disarming itself**: inscriptions are **permanent, datable and precise — and they record only the grand, the unique and the commissioner's own view.** *"Epigraphy alone does not provide a full understanding."*
