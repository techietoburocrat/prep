# Theme 1 — Bricks, Beads and Bones
## *The Harappan Civilisation*

> **The one-line point:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This chapter is not really about the Harappans. It is about what you can and cannot know from THINGS ALONE.** The script is undeciphered, so **every single statement in this chapter is an inference from an object** — a bone, a bead, a brick, a drain, a burial. NCERT's real subject is **the method**, and it says so in its own opening: *"Let us see **what we know** about the Harappan civilisation, **AND HOW WE KNOW** about it."*

## 🎬 The Framing — read this before anything else

### 🧩 The analogy that fits this chapter: **a vast archive with the index destroyed**

> ⚠️ *An analogy builds the model; the facts still have to be learned. Do not put the analogy in an answer.*

**Imagine inheriting a warehouse containing a whole civilisation's physical output — every pot, tool, ornament, building and drain — but with every label written in a lost alphabet.**

```
   WHAT YOU HAVE                    WHAT YOU DO NOT HAVE
   -------------                    --------------------
   ** the objects themselves **     ** any NAME -- of a king,
   -- millions of them                 a city, a god, a people **
   ** where each object lay **      ** any DATE stated by the
      (context, stratigraphy)          people themselves **
   ** what each is MADE of **       ** any statement of INTENT,
      (chemistry, sourcing)            BELIEF or LAW **
   ** what was thrown away **       ** the SCRIPT -- 375-400 signs,
      (waste = the best clue)          undeciphered **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ SO EVERY CONCLUSION IN THIS CHAPTER HAS THE SHAPE:
   ** "we found THIS, therefore they probably DID that." **

   NCERT flags the inference EVERY time, with words like
   ** "probably", "perhaps", "it seems", "archaeologists
   extrapolate", "we are not sure", "we do not know". **

   ⭐ COPY THAT HABIT. An answer that says "the Great Bath was
     used for ritual bathing" is WORSE than one that says
     "the Great Bath has been INTERPRETED as a ritual
     structure, on the basis of its uniqueness and its
     CONTEXT on the Citadel."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT HABIT THIS CHAPTER TEACHES: distinguish THE FIND from THE INFERENCE.** *The find is that a large watertight tank sits on the Citadel with steps, a well, a drain, and eight bathrooms across a lane. **The inference is that it was ritual.** The find is that bricks everywhere have a 4:2:1 ratio. **The inference is a central authority.*** **Examiners reward the distinction; textbooks-in-a-hurry collapse it.**

## 1️⃣ Beginnings

> *"There were **several archaeological cultures in the region PRIOR TO the Mature Harappan.** These cultures were associated with **distinctive pottery, evidence of agriculture and pastoralism, and some crafts. Settlements were generally SMALL, and there were VIRTUALLY NO LARGE BUILDINGS.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The key claim, and it is the current edition's emphasis — note it carefully:** *"The recent archaeological evidences also indicate **the emergence of Harappan culture FROM EARLY FARMING COMMUNITIES dated around 7000 BCE.** The urban phase (Mature Harappan) was **a consequent result of the GRADUAL TRANSFORMATION AND INTERNAL DEVELOPMENT of the civilisation during the early Harappan phase.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE WORDS TO UNDERLINE ARE "GRADUAL TRANSFORMATION AND INTERNAL DEVELOPMENT".** ⭐ **NCERT is asserting that the cities grew OUT OF the local farming cultures — they were not brought, imposed or imported.** *This is a deliberate position, and it is reinforced by §9's archaeogenetics box. **When you write about Harappan origins, use NCERT's own formulation; do not import an external migration narrative that this textbook does not make.***

## 2️⃣ Subsistence Strategies ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"The Mature Harappan culture developed in **some of the areas occupied by the Early Harappan cultures**. These cultures also **shared certain common elements including subsistence strategies.**"*

```
   ** WHAT THEY ATE -- "a wide range of plant and animal
      products, INCLUDING FISH" **

   ⭐ ** HOW WE KNOW -- name the specialists, it is a cheap
      Prelims fact and a free line in a Mains answer: **

     ** ARCHAEO-BOTANISTS ** -- "specialists in ANCIENT PLANT
        REMAINS"; dietary practice reconstructed from
        ** CHARRED GRAINS AND SEEDS **
     ** ARCHAEO-ZOOLOGISTS ** (or ZOO-ARCHAEOLOGISTS) --
        study the ** ANIMAL BONES **

   ** GRAINS found at Harappan sites: **
     ** WHEAT · BARLEY · LENTIL · CHICKPEA · SESAME **
     ** MILLETS -- found from sites in GUJARAT **
     ⭐ ** "Finds of RICE are RELATIVELY RARE." **

   ** ANIMAL BONES found: **
     ** CATTLE · SHEEP · GOAT · BUFFALO · PIG **
     -- archaeo-zoologists indicate ** THESE WERE
        DOMESTICATED **
     ** WILD species also found: BOAR · DEER · GHARIAL **
     ⭐ ** "We DO NOT KNOW whether the Harappans HUNTED these
        animals themselves or OBTAINED MEAT FROM OTHER
        HUNTING COMMUNITIES." **
     ** Bones of FISH and FOWL are also found. **
```

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two Prelims traps live in this list.** ⭐ **(i) RICE IS RARE** — the Harappan staples are **wheat and barley**; an option claiming rice was a principal crop is wrong. ⭐ **(ii) MILLETS ARE SPECIFICALLY FROM GUJARAT**, not general. *And note the honest gap NCERT leaves on wild animals — **finding a deer bone does not prove hunting; it proves venison.** That distinction is the chapter in miniature.*

### 2.1 Agricultural technologies ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"**While the prevalence of agriculture is indicated by finds of grain, IT IS MUCH MORE DIFFICULT TO RECONSTRUCT ACTUAL AGRICULTURAL PRACTICES.**"*

```
   ** THE CHAIN OF INFERENCE ABOUT THE PLOUGH -- watch each
      step, because NCERT is modelling the method **

   FIND:      ** representations on SEALS and TERRACOTTA
              SCULPTURE indicate THE BULL WAS KNOWN **
   INFERENCE: ** "archaeologists EXTRAPOLATE from this that
              OXEN WERE USED FOR PLOUGHING" **
                              |
   BETTER EVIDENCE:           v
   ** TERRACOTTA MODELS OF THE PLOUGH found at sites in
      CHOLISTAN and at BANAWALI (HARYANA) **
                              |
   BEST EVIDENCE:             v
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AN ACTUAL PLOUGHED FIELD at KALIBANGAN
      (RAJASTHAN), associated with EARLY HARAPPAN levels **
      ** "The field had TWO SETS OF FURROWS AT RIGHT ANGLES
         TO EACH OTHER, suggesting that TWO DIFFERENT CROPS
         WERE GROWN TOGETHER." **

   ** STILL UNANSWERED, and NCERT asks rather than asserts: **
     - ** "Were seeds BROADCAST (scattered) on ploughed
       lands?" **
     - ** "Did the Harappans use STONE BLADES SET IN WOODEN
       HANDLES or did they use METAL TOOLS?" **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE KALIBANGAN PLOUGHED FIELD IS THE HIGHEST-VALUE SINGLE FACT IN §2 — memorise the site, the state, and the two-crop inference.** ⭐ *It is also a lovely illustration of an evidential hierarchy: **a picture of a bull → a model of a plough → the ACTUAL FURROWS.** Each step is better evidence than the last, and the last one is direct.*

**💧 IRRIGATION — and the honest uncertainty:**

> *"**Most Harappan sites are located in SEMI-ARID LANDS, where irrigation was PROBABLY REQUIRED** for agriculture. **Traces of CANALS have been found at the Harappan site of SHORTUGHAI IN AFGHANISTAN, BUT NOT IN PUNJAB OR SIND.** It is possible that **ancient canals SILTED UP long ago.** It is also likely that **water drawn from WELLS was used for irrigation.** Besides, ⭐ **WATER RESERVOIRS found in DHOLAVIRA (GUJARAT) may have been used to store water for agriculture.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DHOLAVIRA'S RESERVOIRS ARE THE MOST EXAM-USEFUL ITEM HERE, AND THEY REACH BEYOND HISTORY.** ⭐ **Dholavira's water management is regularly cited in GS3 answers on ancient/traditional water conservation**, alongside step-wells and tanks. *Fig. 1.5's caption asks you to "note the masonry work" — **these were engineered stone structures, not ponds.** (Dholavira is also a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**, inscribed 2021 — beyond NCERT, but true and worth a line.)*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐ **Note the asymmetry to avoid a trap: canals at SHORTUGHAI (Afghanistan), reservoirs at DHOLAVIRA (Gujarat), and NO canal traces in Punjab or Sind.** *NCERT explicitly offers silting as the possible reason — **absence of evidence is being distinguished from evidence of absence**, which is exactly the habit §11 will formalise.*

### 📖 SOURCE 1 — *How artefacts are identified* (Ernest Mackay, *Further Excavations at Mohenjodaro*, 1937) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **On SADDLE QUERNS:** *"Saddle querns … are found in **considerable numbers** … and they seem to have been **the only means in use for grinding cereals**. As a rule, they were **roughly made of hard, gritty, igneous rock or sandstone** and mostly **show signs of hard usage**. As **their bases are usually convex, they must have been SET IN THE EARTH OR IN MUD to prevent their rocking.** ⭐ **TWO MAIN TYPES have been found: those on which another smaller stone was PUSHED OR ROLLED to and fro, and others with which a second stone was used as a POUNDER, eventually making a large cavity in the nether stone.** Querns of the former type were **probably used solely for grain**; the second type **possibly only for pounding herbs and spices for making curries.** In fact, stones of this latter type are **dubbed 'CURRY STONES' by our workmen** and **our cook asked for the loan of one from the museum for use in the kitchen.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's Discuss box:** *"Archaeologists use **PRESENT-DAY ANALOGIES** to try and understand what ancient artefacts were used for. Mackay was **comparing present-day querns with what he found. IS THIS A USEFUL STRATEGY?**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ANSWER — and it is a two-sided answer, which is the whole point:**
> - ✅ **YES, and this source proves it in the most charming way possible: THE COOK ASKED TO BORROW ONE AND USE IT.** ⭐ **An object four and a half thousand years old was still fit for its original purpose** — which is about as strong a confirmation of function as archaeology can get. The analogy also explains the **convex base** (must be set in earth) and distinguishes **rolling** from **pounding** by the wear.
> - ⚠️ **BUT the analogy quietly smuggles in a conclusion.** *"Curry stones" assumes the pounded material was **herbs and spices for curries** — Mackay himself writes *"possibly"*, and NCERT preserves the hedge. ⭐ **The stone proves pounding; it does not prove curry.** *§11.2 will make this the central problem: **moving from the known present to the unknown past is safe for querns and pots, and "becomes more speculative when we extend it to 'religious' symbols."***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Use this source as your worked example whenever a question asks how archaeologists determine function.** *It shows the method working AND shows exactly where it starts to over-reach — in one paragraph, with a cook in it.*

## 3️⃣ Mohenjodaro — A Planned Urban Centre ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"**Perhaps the most unique feature of the Harappan civilisation was the development of URBAN CENTRES.**"*

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A trap NCERT plants in its own first paragraph and that Prelims loves:** *"Although **MOHENJODARO is the MOST WELL-KNOWN site, THE FIRST SITE TO BE DISCOVERED WAS HARAPPA.**"* **Most famous ≠ first found. And the civilisation is named after the first one found, not the most famous.**

### 🏛️ The two-part city

```
   ** "The settlement is divided into TWO SECTIONS: **
        ** one SMALLER but HIGHER   -> THE CITADEL **
        ** one much LARGER but LOWER -> THE LOWER TOWN **

   ** THE CITADEL **
     * ⭐ "owes its height to the fact that ** BUILDINGS WERE
       CONSTRUCTED ON MUD BRICK PLATFORMS ** "
       -- i.e. the height is ** ARTIFICIAL and DELIBERATE **,
          not a natural hill
     * ** IT WAS WALLED **, "which meant that it was
       PHYSICALLY SEPARATED from the Lower Town"

   ** THE LOWER TOWN **
     * ⭐ ** "The Lower Town was ALSO WALLED." **
     * ** "Several buildings were built ON PLATFORMS, which
       served as FOUNDATIONS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE LABOUR CALCULATION -- learn this number: **
   ** "It has been calculated that IF ONE LABOURER MOVED
      ROUGHLY A CUBIC METRE OF EARTH DAILY, JUST TO PUT THE
      FOUNDATIONS IN PLACE IT WOULD HAVE REQUIRED
      FOUR MILLION PERSON-DAYS, in other words,
      MOBILISING LABOUR ON A VERY LARGE SCALE." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FOUR-MILLION-PERSON-DAYS FIGURE IS THE STRONGEST SINGLE ARGUMENT IN THE CHAPTER FOR THE EXISTENCE OF AN AUTHORITY — and it belongs in your §8 answer, not just here.** ⭐ **Four million person-days of earth-moving, for FOUNDATIONS ALONE, before a single building went up.** *Somebody decided this would happen, somebody assembled the labour, and somebody fed it. **You cannot get that by accident or by consensus of households.** (Note the careful phrase NCERT ends on: *"mobilising labour on a very large scale"* — it asserts the mobilisation without naming the mobiliser. §8 explains why.)*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE PROOF OF PLANNING — NCERT's argument, and it is genuinely elegant:**

```
   ARGUMENT 1 -- ** THE PLATFORMS CAME FIRST **
   ** "Once the platforms were in place, ALL BUILDING ACTIVITY
      WITHIN THE CITY WAS RESTRICTED TO A FIXED AREA ON THE
      PLATFORMS. So it seems that ** THE SETTLEMENT WAS FIRST
      PLANNED AND THEN IMPLEMENTED ACCORDINGLY. ** "
   ⭐ THE LOGIC: you cannot build the platform after deciding
     where the house goes. ** The order of construction proves
     the order of decision. **

   ARGUMENT 2 -- ** THE STANDARDISED BRICK **
   ** "bricks, whether SUN-DRIED OR BAKED, were of a
      STANDARDISED RATIO, where THE LENGTH AND BREADTH WERE
      FOUR TIMES AND TWICE THE HEIGHT respectively." **
      ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** i.e. the ratio 4 : 2 : 1 **
   ** "SUCH BRICKS WERE USED AT ALL HARAPPAN SETTLEMENTS." **

   ARGUMENT 3 -- ** THE DRAINS CAME BEFORE THE HOUSES ** (§3.1)
```

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BRICK RATIO IS 4:2:1 — LENGTH : BREADTH : HEIGHT. Memorise it as a ratio, not as measurements**, because NCERT never gives absolute sizes. ⭐ **And the killer detail for §8 is that this ratio held from JAMMU TO GUJARAT** *(stated in §8)* — **across a territory larger than most ancient empires, in bricks "obviously not produced in any single centre."**

### 3.1 Laying out drains ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"**One of the most distinctive features of Harappan cities was THE CAREFULLY PLANNED DRAINAGE SYSTEM.** … roads and streets were laid out along an approximate **'GRID' PATTERN, INTERSECTING AT RIGHT ANGLES.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SENTENCE THAT MAKES THIS CHAPTER'S CASE FOR PLANNING, AND IT IS THE ONE TO QUOTE:**
>
> > *"**It seems that STREETS WITH DRAINS WERE LAID OUT FIRST AND THEN HOUSES BUILT ALONG THEM. If domestic waste water had to flow into the street drains, EVERY HOUSE NEEDED TO HAVE AT LEAST ONE WALL ALONG A STREET.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read that twice, because it is a piece of reasoning, not a description.** ⭐ **The drain network is not a service added to a city — it is the SKELETON the city was hung on.** *And the consequence is a constraint on every private house: **you may not build where you like; you must touch a street.*** **That is zoning, four and a half thousand years ago — and it is a rule that must have been ENFORCED, which is another arrow pointing at §8.**

### 📖 SOURCE 2 — *The most ancient system yet discovered* (Ernest Mackay, *Early Indus Civilisation*, 1948) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> Mackay: *"**It is certainly THE MOST COMPLETE ANCIENT SYSTEM AS YET DISCOVERED.**"*

```
   ** THE ENGINEERING, in NCERT's summary of Mackay: **

   * ** "EVERY HOUSE was connected to the street drains." **
   * ** main channels "made of BRICKS SET IN MORTAR" **
   * ⭐ ** "covered with LOOSE BRICKS THAT COULD BE REMOVED FOR
     CLEANING" ** -- designed for MAINTENANCE
   * ** "In some cases, LIMESTONE was used for the covers." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "House drains first emptied into A SUMP OR
     CESSPIT into which SOLID MATTER SETTLED while WASTE
     WATER FLOWED OUT into the street drains." **
     -- ** a settling tank. This is sewage TREATMENT. **
   * ** "Very long drainage channels were provided AT
     INTERVALS WITH SUMPS FOR CLEANING." **

   ⭐ ** AND THE HUMAN DETAIL Mackay could not resist: **
   ** "little heaps of material, mostly sand, have frequently
      been found LYING ALONGSIDE DRAINAGE CHANNELS, which
      shows … that THE DEBRIS WAS NOT ALWAYS CARTED AWAY WHEN
      THE DRAIN WAS CLEARED." **

   ** AND IT WAS NOT ONLY A BIG-CITY LUXURY: **
   ** "Drainage systems were NOT UNIQUE TO THE LARGER CITIES,
      but were found IN SMALLER SETTLEMENTS as well. AT LOTHAL
      for example, while HOUSES WERE BUILT OF MUD BRICKS,
      DRAINS WERE MADE OF BURNT BRICKS." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LOTHAL DETAIL IS THE BEST ONE IN THE WHOLE SECTION AND IT IS WORTH A SENTENCE OF ITS OWN: houses of MUD brick, drains of BURNT brick.** ⭐ **Burnt brick is far more expensive — it costs fuel.** **So a settlement that economised on its own houses did NOT economise on its sewers.** *That is a statement about priorities, recovered entirely from building materials, and it is exactly the kind of inference this chapter is teaching.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The little heaps of sand beside the drains are worth keeping too, for a different reason.** *They are evidence of **imperfect maintenance** — somebody cleaned the drain and left the spoil. ⭐ **It is the one moment in the chapter where you can see a person cutting a corner**, and it is a useful corrective to the picture of a flawless machine-civilisation.*

### 🏠 3.2 Domestic architecture ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE STANDARD HOUSE at Mohenjodaro's Lower Town **

   * ** "Many were CENTRED ON A COURTYARD, with rooms on all
     sides." **
   * ** the courtyard "was PROBABLY THE CENTRE OF ACTIVITIES
     SUCH AS COOKING AND WEAVING, particularly during hot and
     dry weather" **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AN APPARENT CONCERN FOR PRIVACY -- two pieces of
      evidence, and both are architectural: **
     1. ** "there are NO WINDOWS IN THE WALLS ALONG THE GROUND
        LEVEL" **
     2. ** "the MAIN ENTRANCE DOES NOT GIVE A DIRECT VIEW OF
        THE INTERIOR OR THE COURTYARD" **

   ** OTHER FEATURES: **
   * ** "EVERY HOUSE HAD ITS OWN BATHROOM PAVED WITH BRICKS,
     with drains connected THROUGH THE WALL to the street
     drains." **
   * ** "Some houses have remains of STAIRCASES to reach A
     SECOND STOREY OR THE ROOF." **
   * ⭐ ** "Many houses had WELLS, often IN A ROOM THAT COULD
     BE REACHED FROM THE OUTSIDE and PERHAPS USED BY
     PASSERS-BY." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "Scholars have estimated that THE TOTAL NUMBER
     OF WELLS IN MOHENJODARO WAS ABOUT 700." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE PRIVACY ARGUMENT IS A LOVELY PIECE OF METHOD AND IS WORTH LEARNING AS A MODEL.** ⭐ *You cannot excavate a value. **But you can excavate a doorway that has been deliberately offset so that a passer-by cannot see in** — and a blank ground-floor wall — and from two such features infer a preference.* **NCERT's hedge is "an APPARENT concern for privacy". Keep the hedge; the inference is good, not certain.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE ~700 WELLS FIGURE IS THE ONE TO REMEMBER FROM §3.2 — because of what it implies.** *Water was **not centralised**; it was distributed to households. **Combine that with "every house had its own bathroom" and "every house was connected to the street drains", and you get a civilisation that delivered water and sanitation AT THE LEVEL OF THE INDIVIDUAL HOUSEHOLD.*** ⭐ **That is the comparison to make with Ur, which had no street drains at all** *(Class 11 Theme 1)* — and, honestly, with a great many places since.
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The wells reachable from OUTSIDE are a small marvel — note the implication.** *A private well with a public door is **a household resource made available to strangers.** NCERT says "perhaps used by passers-by" and leaves it there; **it is a legitimate small piece of evidence about civic norms**, and it is the kind of detail that makes an answer feel observed rather than recited.*

### ⚠️ The two boxes in §3 that are pure Prelims material

> **"THE PLIGHT OF HARAPPA" ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — a favourite exam item:**
>
> *"Although **Harappa was the first site to be discovered, it was BADLY DESTROYED BY BRICK ROBBERS.** As early as **1875, ALEXANDER CUNNINGHAM, the first Director-General of the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), often called THE FATHER OF INDIAN ARCHAEOLOGY**, noted that **the amount of brick taken from the ancient site was enough to lay bricks for 'ABOUT 100 MILES' of the RAILWAY LINE BETWEEN LAHORE AND MULTAN.** Thus, many of the ancient structures at the site were damaged. **In contrast, MOHENJODARO WAS FAR BETTER PRESERVED.**"*
>
> ⭐ **This is why Mohenjodaro dominates the chapter** — not because it was more important in antiquity, **but because its bricks were not carried off to build a railway.** *A superb small example of §11's point: **what survives is not a fair sample of what existed.***

> **"CITADELS" — the variations, and NCERT explicitly warns against over-generalising:**
>
> *"**While MOST Harappan settlements have a small high WESTERN part and a larger lower EASTERN section, THERE ARE VARIATIONS.** At sites such as **DHOLAVIRA and LOTHAL (Gujarat), THE ENTIRE SETTLEMENT WAS FORTIFIED, and sections within the town were also separated by walls. THE CITADEL WITHIN LOTHAL WAS NOT WALLED OFF, BUT WAS BUILT AT A HEIGHT.**"*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A classic Prelims trap: "all Harappan cities had a walled citadel to the west and an unwalled lower town to the east" is FALSE on three counts** — *the Lower Town at Mohenjodaro **was** walled; **Dholavira and Lothal were fortified as a whole**; and **Lothal's citadel was not walled off at all**, merely raised.

### 🛁 3.3 The Citadel — the warehouse and the Great Bath ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"It is on the Citadel that we find evidence of **structures that were PROBABLY used for SPECIAL PUBLIC PURPOSES.**"*

```
   ** THE WAREHOUSE **
   ** "a MASSIVE STRUCTURE of which THE LOWER BRICK PORTIONS
      REMAIN, while THE UPPER PORTIONS, PROBABLY OF WOOD,
      DECAYED LONG AGO" **
   ⭐ Note the reasoning: the wooden upper storey is an
     INFERENCE FROM ABSENCE -- §11 will explain that organic
     materials decompose.

   ** THE GREAT BATH -- learn every feature, it is
      Prelims-shaped **

   * ** "a LARGE RECTANGULAR TANK in a COURTYARD SURROUNDED BY
     A CORRIDOR ON ALL FOUR SIDES" **
   * ** "TWO FLIGHTS OF STEPS on the NORTH AND SOUTH leading
     into the tank" **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** MADE WATERTIGHT "by SETTING BRICKS ON EDGE and
     using A MORTAR OF GYPSUM" **
   * ** "There were ROOMS ON THREE SIDES, in one of which was
     A LARGE WELL." **
   * ** "Water from the tank FLOWED INTO A HUGE DRAIN." **
   * ⭐ ** "Across a lane TO THE NORTH lay A SMALLER BUILDING
     WITH EIGHT BATHROOMS, FOUR ON EACH SIDE OF A CORRIDOR,
     with drains from each bathroom connecting to a drain

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE INTERPRETATION -- note how carefully

It writes that **its uniqueness** and **its context** have **led scholars to suggest** a ritual purpose.* ⭐ **Two grounds, one attribution, one hedge.** *Compare it with §11.2, where NCERT lists the Great Bath among the structures that "have been ASSIGNED ritual significance" — **the same caution, forty pages apart.** In an answer, write: **"the Great Bath has been interpreted as a ritual structure on the grounds of its uniqueness and its position on the Citadel, though Harappan ritual practice remains poorly understood."*** **That sentence is worth more than three describing the gypsum.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The engineering is worth its own note: bricks SET ON EDGE with GYPSUM MORTAR to make a tank watertight, fed by its own well and emptied by its own drain.** ⭐ *This is a **purpose-built, self-contained hydraulic structure** — and the eight bathrooms across the lane suggest a **procedure**, not a swim. *(Do not assert what the procedure was.)*

## 4️⃣ Tracking Social Differences ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"**Archaeologists generally use certain STRATEGIES to find out whether there were SOCIAL OR ECONOMIC DIFFERENCES amongst people living within a particular culture.**"*

### ⚰️ 4.1 Burials

> **The comparison NCERT sets up first — and it is the key to the whole section:** *"You are probably familiar with **THE MASSIVE PYRAMIDS OF EGYPT, SOME OF WHICH WERE CONTEMPORANEOUS WITH THE HARAPPAN CIVILISATION. Many of these pyramids were ROYAL BURIALS, where ENORMOUS QUANTITIES OF WEALTH WAS BURIED.**"*

```
   ** WHAT HARAPPAN BURIALS ACTUALLY LOOK LIKE **

   * ** "the dead were generally LAID IN PITS" **
   * ** "Sometimes, there were differences in the way the
     burial pit was made -- in some instances, the
     HOLLOWED-OUT SPACES WERE LINED WITH BRICKS." **
     ⭐ NCERT's response to its own observation:
     ** "Could these variations be an indication of social
        differences? WE ARE NOT SURE." **
   * ** "Some graves contain POTTERY AND ORNAMENTS, PERHAPS
     indicating a belief that these could be USED IN THE
     AFTERLIFE." **
   * ** "JEWELLERY has been found in burials of BOTH MEN AND
     WOMEN." **
   * ⭐ ** THE HARAPPA CEMETERY FIND (mid-1980s): "an ornament
     consisting of THREE SHELL RINGS, A JASPER (a kind of
     semi-precious stone) BEAD AND HUNDREDS OF MICRO BEADS
     was found NEAR THE SKULL OF A MALE." **
   * ** "In some instances the dead were buried with COPPER
     MIRRORS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE CONCLUSION: **
   ** "But ON THE WHOLE, it appears that THE HARAPPANS DID NOT
      BELIEVE IN BURYING PRECIOUS THINGS WITH THE DEAD." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE EGYPT COMPARISON IS DOING REAL ANALYTICAL WORK, SO USE IT.** ⭐ **Two civilisations of the same era: one buried enormous wealth with its rulers, the other did not.** *This is why Harappan social hierarchy is so hard to read — **the standard archaeological tool for finding elites is the rich grave, and the Harappans did not make rich graves.*** **NCERT returns to exactly this in §11: *"Why do Harappan cemeteries show LITTLE SOCIAL DIFFERENTIATION?"* — it is listed among the chapter's UNANSWERED questions.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Do not over-read the absence.** *NCERT is careful: it says the Harappans **did not BELIEVE IN burying precious things** — an inference about practice. **It does NOT say there were no elites.** A society can be steeply unequal in life and modest in burial. **The correct answer is that burial evidence is INCONCLUSIVE for Harappan hierarchy**, which is a stronger and more accurate claim than either extreme.*

### 💎 4.2 Looking for "luxuries" ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE CLASSIFICATION -- and its difficulties **

   ** UTILITARIAN ** = "objects of DAILY USE made fairly
   easily out of ORDINARY MATERIALS such as STONE OR CLAY"
     e.g. ** QUERNS, POTTERY, NEEDLES, FLESH-RUBBERS (body
        scrubbers) **
     ** "usually found DISTRIBUTED THROUGHOUT SETTLEMENTS" **

   ** LUXURIES ** = archaeologists assume objects are luxuries
   ** "if they are RARE or made from COSTLY, NON-LOCAL
      MATERIALS or with COMPLICATED TECHNOLOGIES" **
     ⭐ e.g. ** little pots of FAIENCE ** -- "a material made
        of GROUND SAND OR SILICA MIXED WITH COLOUR AND A GUM
        AND THEN FIRED" -- "probably considered precious
        because they were DIFFICULT TO MAKE"

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THEN NCERT BREAKS ITS OWN CATEGORY: **
   ** "The situation becomes MORE COMPLICATED when we find
      what seem to be ARTICLES OF DAILY USE, such as SPINDLE
      WHORLS MADE OF RARE MATERIALS SUCH AS FAIENCE.
      DO WE CLASSIFY THESE AS UTILITARIAN OR LUXURIES?" **

   ** THE DISTRIBUTION TEST -- this is the useful part: **
   ** "rare objects made of valuable materials are generally
      CONCENTRATED IN LARGE SETTLEMENTS like MOHENJODARO and
      HARAPPA and are RARELY FOUND IN THE SMALLER
      SETTLEMENTS." **
     * ** MINIATURE POTS OF FAIENCE, "perhaps used as PERFUME
       BOTTLES", found MOSTLY IN MOHENJODARO AND HARAPPA,
       and ** NONE FROM SMALL SETTLEMENTS LIKE KALIBANGAN **
     * ⭐ ** "GOLD too was RARE, and as at present, probably
       precious -- ALL THE GOLD JEWELLERY FOUND AT HARAPPAN
       SITES WAS RECOVERED FROM HOARDS." **

   ** HOARDS ** (margin definition, and it matters for §11):
   ** "objects KEPT CAREFULLY by people, often INSIDE
      CONTAINERS SUCH AS POTS… saved for REUSE by
      metalworkers. IF FOR SOME REASON THE ORIGINAL OWNERS DO
      NOT RETRIEVE THEM, they remain where they are left till
      some archaeologist finds them." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SPINDLE-WHORL PROBLEM IS THE BEST THING IN §4 AND IT IS THE ANSWER TO NCERT'S OWN DISCUSS BOX.** *A spindle whorl is a **tool** — you spin thread with it. Made of **faience**, it is also a **luxury object**. ⭐ **So the two categories are not properties of the object; they are properties of the archaeologist's scheme.*** *(The modern parallel is obvious and you may safely think it without writing it: a wristwatch tells the time and signals wealth, and no classification of "utilitarian vs luxury" survives contact with one.)*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The DISTRIBUTION test is what rescues the section, and it is the transferable method.** ⭐ *You cannot decide from the object alone whether it was precious. **But you CAN observe that faience perfume pots occur at Mohenjodaro and Harappa and NOT at Kalibangan** — and that pattern is evidence, independent of any guess about what the Harappans valued.* **A find's DISTRIBUTION can answer questions its FORM cannot.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"All the gold jewellery found at Harappan sites was recovered from HOARDS" is a sentence with a sting in it, and §11 supplies the sting.** *Hoards are, by definition, **things somebody hid and never came back for.*** ⭐ **So our entire sample of Harappan gold consists of accidents — wealth that failed to be retrieved.** *NCERT makes this explicit in §11: **"valuable artefacts that are found intact were either LOST in the past or HOARDED AND NEVER RETRIEVED… such finds are ACCIDENTAL RATHER THAN TYPICAL."*** *That is a genuinely deep point about sampling, and it is worth a line in any answer about the limits of archaeology.*

## 5️⃣ Finding Out About Craft Production ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**⚙️ CHANHUDARO — the specialised workshop town:**

> *"This is a **TINY SETTLEMENT (LESS THAN 7 HECTARES)** as compared to **MOHENJODARO (125 HECTARES)**, **ALMOST EXCLUSIVELY DEVOTED TO CRAFT PRODUCTION**, including **BEAD-MAKING, SHELL-CUTTING, METAL-WORKING, SEAL-MAKING and WEIGHT-MAKING.**"*

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The two areas — 7 hectares vs 125 hectares — are exactly the sort of paired figure Prelims likes.** *And the concept matters more than the numbers: **Chanhudaro is a settlement that made things and apparently did little else** — evidence of **regional specialisation**, i.e. an economy in which one place can produce for others because exchange is reliable.*

```
   ** BEADS -- the materials list, and it is worth learning
      because it is long, specific and checkable **

   ** STONES: CARNELIAN ** ("of a beautiful red colour"),
     ** JASPER · CRYSTAL · QUARTZ · STEATITE **
   ** METALS: COPPER · BRONZE · GOLD **
   ** ALSO: SHELL · FAIENCE · TERRACOTTA (burnt clay) **

   ⭐ ** "Some beads were made of TWO OR MORE STONES, CEMENTED
      TOGETHER, some of STONE WITH GOLD CAPS." **

   ** SHAPES: DISC-SHAPED · CYLINDRICAL · SPHERICAL ·
      BARREL-SHAPED · SEGMENTED **
   ** DECORATION: "some were decorated by INCISING OR
      PAINTING, and some had DESIGNS ETCHED onto them" **

   ** STEATITE -- "a VERY SOFT stone, was EASILY WORKED" **
   ⭐ ** "Some beads were MOULDED OUT OF A PASTE MADE WITH
      STEATITE POWDER. This permitted making A VARIETY OF
      SHAPES, UNLIKE THE GEOMETRICAL FORMS MADE OUT OF HARDER
      STONES." **
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE UNSOLVED PUZZLE: "HOW THE STEATITE MICRO
      BEAD WAS MADE REMAINS A PUZZLE FOR ARCHAEOLOGISTS
      studying ancient technology." **

   ** CARNELIAN -- the process, reconstructed EXPERIMENTALLY: **
   ** "Archaeologists' EXPERIMENTS have revealed that THE RED
      COLOUR OF CARNELIAN WAS OBTAINED BY FIRING THE YELLOWISH
      RAW MATERIAL and beads AT VARIOUS STAGES OF
      PRODUCTION." **
   ** THE SEQUENCE: "NODULES WERE CHIPPED INTO ROUGH SHAPES,
      and then FINELY FLAKED INTO THE FINAL FORM. GRINDING,
      POLISHING AND DRILLING COMPLETED THE PROCESS." **
   ⭐ ** "SPECIALISED DRILLS have been found at CHANHUDARO,
      LOTHAL and more recently at DHOLAVIRA." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOTICE THE THIRD METHOD APPEARING HERE — EXPERIMENTAL ARCHAEOLOGY.** *§2 used **analogy** (Mackay's querns). §4 used **distribution**. ⭐ **Here archaeologists RE-CREATED the process to discover that carnelian's red comes from firing a yellowish raw material.*** **You cannot dig up a technique — so you reproduce it.** *Add this to your list of methods; a question on "how do archaeologists reconstruct the past?" (**Exercise 8**) should name analogy, context, distribution, experiment and stratigraphy.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And keep the steatite micro bead as your example of an honest unknown.** *NCERT states plainly that **nobody knows how it was made.** ⭐ *A textbook admitting an unsolved technical problem is a gift — **it is concrete proof that Harappan craft skill exceeded what modern reconstruction has managed**, which is a far more striking way to praise their technology than any adjective.*

**🐚 THE COASTAL SHELL CENTRES:**

> *"**NAGESHWAR and BALAKOT** … both settlements are **NEAR THE COAST. These were SPECIALISED CENTRES FOR MAKING SHELL OBJECTS — including BANGLES, LADLES and INLAY — which were TAKEN TO OTHER SETTLEMENTS.**"*
>
> *"Similarly, it is likely that **finished products (such as beads) from CHANHUDARO and LOTHAL were taken to the large urban centres such as MOHENJODARO and HARAPPA.**"*

### 🔍 5.1 Identifying centres of production — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE ANSWER IS WASTE

```
   ** "In order to IDENTIFY CENTRES OF CRAFT PRODUCTION,
      archaeologists usually look for the following: " **

     ** RAW MATERIAL ** -- stone nodules, whole shells,
                          copper ore
     ** TOOLS **
     ** UNFINISHED OBJECTS **
     ** REJECTS **
     ** WASTE MATERIAL **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "In fact, WASTE IS ONE OF THE BEST INDICATORS OF
      CRAFT WORK." **

   ** THE LOGIC: "if shell or stone is CUT to make objects,
      then PIECES OF THESE MATERIALS WILL BE DISCARDED AS
      WASTE AT THE PLACE OF PRODUCTION." **

   ⭐ ** AND THE REFINEMENT THAT MAKES IT WORK: **
   ** "Sometimes, LARGER WASTE PIECES WERE USED UP to make
      smaller objects, BUT MINUSCULE BITS WERE USUALLY LEFT
      IN THE WORK AREA." **

   ** THE FINDING: "These traces suggest that APART FROM
      SMALL, SPECIALISED CENTRES, CRAFT PRODUCTION WAS ALSO
      UNDERTAKEN IN LARGE CITIES SUCH AS MOHENJODARO AND
      HARAPPA." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"WASTE IS ONE OF THE BEST INDICATORS OF CRAFT WORK" IS THE MOST QUOTABLE METHODOLOGICAL LINE IN THE CHAPTER, AND IT IS BEAUTIFULLY COUNTER-INTUITIVE.** ⭐ **The finished bead travels — it is traded away, and turns up a thousand kilometres from where it was made. THE CHIPS DO NOT TRAVEL.** *So the rubbish, not the product, tells you where the work happened.* **And the refinement is what makes it reliable: big offcuts get recycled into smaller objects, but the tiny fragments are swept nowhere and stay put — so *"minuscule bits"* are the truly diagnostic find.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is a genuinely general principle and it recurs across the whole discipline: to locate an activity, look for what the activity DISCARDED, not what it produced.** *(It is also a quiet rebuke to treasure-hunting archaeology — **Cunningham "tended to recover artefacts that he thought had CULTURAL VALUE" (§10.1), which is precisely the habit that makes a site's waste invisible.** §5.1 and §10.1 are answering each other.)*

### 6.2 Contact with distant lands ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE OMAN COPPER ARGUMENT -- a beautiful piece of
      scientific reasoning; learn the chain **

   STEP 1  ** "Recent archaeological finds suggest that COPPER
           was also probably brought from OMAN, on the
           SOUTH-EASTERN TIP OF THE ARABIAN PENINSULA." **

   STEP 2  ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "CHEMICAL ANALYSES have shown that BOTH
           THE OMANI COPPER AND HARAPPAN ARTEFACTS HAVE TRACES
           OF NICKEL, suggesting A COMMON ORIGIN." **

   STEP 3  ** THE RETURN CARGO: "A DISTINCTIVE TYPE OF VESSEL,
           A LARGE HARAPPAN JAR COATED WITH A THICK LAYER OF
           BLACK CLAY has been found at OMANI SITES. SUCH
           THICK COATINGS PREVENT THE PERCOLATION OF
           LIQUIDS." **
           ⭐ ** "WE DO NOT KNOW WHAT WAS CARRIED IN THESE
           VESSELS, but it is possible that the Harappans
           EXCHANGED THE CONTENTS OF THESE VESSELS FOR OMANI
           COPPER." **

   STEP 4  ** AND THE TEXTUAL CONFIRMATION FROM A THIRD
           CIVILISATION: **
           ** "MESOPOTAMIAN TEXTS datable to THE THIRD
           MILLENNIUM BCE refer to COPPER COMING FROM A REGION
           CALLED MAGAN, PERHAPS A NAME FOR OMAN, and
           interestingly enough COPPER FOUND AT MESOPOTAMIAN
           SITES ALSO CONTAINS TRACES OF NICKEL." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE NICKEL ARGUMENT IS THE BEST SINGLE EXAMPLE IN THIS BOOK OF SCIENCE SETTLING A HISTORICAL QUESTION, AND IT IS WORTH SPELLING OUT IN AN ANSWER.** ⭐ **A trace impurity in a metal acts as a FINGERPRINT of the ore body it came from.** *Nickel in Omani copper + nickel in Harappan artefacts = **common origin**. Then nickel in **Mesopotamian** copper, plus Mesopotamian texts naming **Magan** as their copper source, **closes the triangle from a completely independent direction — chemistry and cuneiform agreeing.*** **When a question asks how archaeologists reconstruct trade, this is the example to give.**

## 7️⃣ Seals, Script, Weights ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 7.1 Seals and sealings — how they actually worked

```
   ** THE MECHANISM -- NCERT explains it as a procedure, and
      it is worth reproducing exactly: **

   1. ** "a bag of goods being sent from one place to
      another" **
   2. ** "Its mouth was TIED WITH ROPE" **
   3. ** "on the knot was affixed SOME WET CLAY" **
   4. ** "on which ONE OR MORE SEALS WERE PRESSED, leaving AN
      IMPRESSION" **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE TWO FUNCTIONS: **
     (a) ** "If the bag reached WITH ITS SEALING INTACT, IT
         MEANT THAT IT HAD NOT BEEN TAMPERED WITH." **
     (b) ** "The sealing ALSO CONVEYED THE IDENTITY OF THE
         SENDER." **

   ⚠️ ** NOTE THE VOCABULARY DISTINCTION -- Prelims-shaped: **
     ** A SEAL ** = the carved object (usually STEATITE)
     ** A SEALING ** = the CLAY IMPRESSION it leaves
     (Fig. 1.22 is "A SEALING FROM ROPAR".)
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The seal is a TAMPER-EVIDENT SEAL and an IDENTITY TOKEN at once — and that combination tells you a great deal about the economy.** ⭐ **You only need a device proving a package was not opened en route if goods travel BEYOND THE SENDER'S SUPERVISION, between parties who need not trust each other personally.** *Seals are therefore evidence of **impersonal, long-distance, institutionalised trade** — which is why §7 groups seals, script and weights together. **All three are machinery for exchange between strangers.***

### 7.2 An enigmatic script ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** WHAT THE SCRIPT IS ON: **
   ** "Harappan seals usually have A LINE OF WRITING,
      PROBABLY CONTAINING THE NAME AND TITLE OF THE OWNER." **
   ⭐ ** "Scholars have also suggested that THE MOTIF
      (generally AN ANIMAL) CONVEYED A MEANING TO THOSE WHO
      COULD NOT READ." **

   ** THE FACTS ABOUT THE SCRIPT -- every one is examinable: **

   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "MOST INSCRIPTIONS ARE SHORT, THE LONGEST
     CONTAINING ABOUT 26 SIGNS." **
   * ** "the script REMAINS UNDECIPHERED TO DATE" **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "IT WAS EVIDENTLY NOT ALPHABETICAL (where each
     sign stands for a vowel or a consonant) AS IT HAS JUST
     TOO MANY SIGNS -- SOMEWHERE BETWEEN 375 AND 400." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WRITTEN RIGHT TO LEFT -- and note HOW we know:
     "as SOME SEALS SHOW A WIDER SPACING ON THE RIGHT AND
     CRAMPING ON THE LEFT, AS IF THE ENGRAVER BEGAN WORKING
     FROM THE RIGHT AND THEN RAN OUT OF SPACE." **

   ** WHAT IT WAS WRITTEN ON -- the list matters (see below): **
   ** SEALS · COPPER TOOLS · RIMS OF JARS · COPPER AND
      TERRACOTTA TABLETS · JEWELLERY · BONE RODS ·
      ⭐ "EVEN AN ANCIENT SIGNBOARD!" **
      (Fig. 1.21 -- the signboard is from ** DHOLAVIRA ** )

   ⭐ ** AND THE QUESTION NCERT LEAVES OPEN: **
   ** "Remember, THERE MAY HAVE BEEN WRITING ON PERISHABLE
      MATERIALS TOO. COULD THIS MEAN THAT LITERACY WAS
      WIDESPREAD?" **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE RIGHT-TO-LEFT ARGUMENT IS A PERFECT MINIATURE OF THIS CHAPTER'S METHOD AND YOU SHOULD BE ABLE TO REPRODUCE IT IN ONE SENTENCE.** *We cannot read a single word — **yet we know the direction of writing, because the engraver ran out of room at the LEFT end.** ⭐ **A craftsman's mistake, repeated often enough, became evidence.*** *Nothing else in the chapter shows so neatly that **you can extract real knowledge from an unreadable source if you attend to how it was MADE rather than what it SAYS.***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The 375–400 signs figure is doing logical work, not just being a number — explain the logic, don't just quote it.** ⭐ **An alphabet needs a few dozen signs. 375–400 is far too many, so the script CANNOT be alphabetic** — it is likely **logo-syllabic** (signs for words and/or syllables). *NCERT does not use that term, so do not attribute it — **but do reproduce the reasoning: the SIGN COUNT rules out a whole class of writing system.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The signboard from Dholavira is worth a mention of its own.** *Everything else with writing on it is **portable and personal** — a seal, a tool, a jar rim, a piece of jewellery. ⭐ **A signboard is PUBLIC and FIXED**: it addresses whoever passes. *That is the closest thing in the entire corpus to writing meant for a general reader, and it is why NCERT's question about widespread literacy is a serious one rather than a rhetorical flourish.*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Be careful how you answer the literacy question — it is a trap for the over-confident.** *The honest position: **the variety of objects bearing writing is suggestive, and perishable materials would not survive — but "most inscriptions are short" and the longest is only 26 signs.*** **There is no Harappan equivalent of a Mesopotamian tablet archive.** *NCERT asks; it does not answer. **Neither should you.***

### ⚖️ 7.3 Weights ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** "Exchanges were REGULATED BY A PRECISE SYSTEM OF
      WEIGHTS" **

   * ⭐ ** MADE OF A STONE CALLED CHERT **
   * ⭐ ** "GENERALLY CUBICAL, WITH NO MARKINGS" **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE TWO-PART SYSTEM -- this is the fact: **
       ** LOWER denominations were BINARY:
          1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 **
       ** HIGHER denominations followed the DECIMAL system:
          160, 200, 320, 640 **
   * ** "The SMALLER weights were probably used for WEIGHING
     JEWELLERY AND BEADS." **
   * ** "METAL SCALE-PANS have also been found." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"BINARY BELOW, DECIMAL ABOVE" IS THE DETAIL TO CARRY, AND IT IS A COMMON MCQ.** *Note the elegance: **doubling (1-2-4-8-16-32) is the easiest possible system for balance-scale work with small precious goods** — you halve and halve again. **Decimal is easier for counting bulk.** ⭐ **So the system is not arbitrary; it is fitted to two different kinds of transaction.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND "WITH NO MARKINGS" IS THE SLEEPER FACT — it is worth a sentence in any answer on Harappan authority.** *The weights carry **no numerals, no owner's mark, no authority's stamp**. ⭐ **They were trusted BY THEIR PHYSICAL FORM ALONE** — you knew a cube of chert of that size was a standard weight because the standard was universal and stable. **A system like that requires either enforcement or extraordinarily deep convention**, and it worked well enough that **Dilmun adopted it (§6.2)**.

### 8.1 Palaces and kings — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the three rival positions

```
   ** FIRST, THE PROBLEM: **
   ** "If we look for A CENTRE OF POWER or for DEPICTIONS OF
      PEOPLE IN POWER, ARCHAEOLOGICAL RECORDS PROVIDE NO
      IMMEDIATE ANSWERS." **

   ** THE TWO CANDIDATES, AND WHY BOTH FAIL: **

   ** THE "PALACE" ** -- "A large building found at
   MOHENJODARO was LABELLED AS A PALACE by archaeologists
   ** BUT NO SPECTACULAR FINDS WERE ASSOCIATED WITH IT. ** "

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE "PRIEST-KING" ** -- and read this carefully,
   because it is a lesson in how a label becomes a fact:
   ** "A stone statue was LABELLED AND CONTINUES TO BE KNOWN
      AS THE 'PRIEST-KING'. THIS IS BECAUSE ARCHAEOLOGISTS
      WERE FAMILIAR WITH MESOPOTAMIAN HISTORY AND ITS
      'PRIEST-KINGS' AND HAVE FOUND PARALLELS IN THE INDUS
      REGION." **
   ** "But… THE RITUAL PRACTICES OF THE HARAPPAN CIVILISATION
      ARE NOT WELL UNDERSTOOD YET, NOR ARE THERE ANY MEANS OF
      KNOWING WHETHER THOSE WHO PERFORMED THEM ALSO HELD
      POLITICAL POWER." **

   ================================================================
   ** THE THREE POSITIONS ON HARAPPAN AUTHORITY **
   ================================================================

   ** POSITION 1 -- NO RULERS AT ALL **
   ** "Some archaeologists are of the opinion that HARAPPAN
      SOCIETY HAD NO RULERS, AND THAT EVERYBODY ENJOYED EQUAL
      STATUS." **

   ** POSITION 2 -- SEVERAL RULERS, ONE PER CITY **
   ** "Others feel there was NO SINGLE RULER BUT SEVERAL, that
      MOHENJODARO HAD A SEPARATE RULER, HARAPPA ANOTHER, AND
      SO FORTH." **

   ** POSITION 3 -- A SINGLE STATE **   <-- most evidence
   ** "Yet others argue that THERE WAS A SINGLE STATE, given: **
      ** (a) THE SIMILARITY IN ARTEFACTS **
      ** (b) THE EVIDENCE FOR PLANNED SETTLEMENTS **
      ** (c) THE STANDARDISED RATIO OF BRICK SIZE **
      ** (d) THE ESTABLISHMENT OF SETTLEMENTS NEAR SOURCES OF
          RAW MATERIAL **

   ⭐ ** NCERT'S OWN CLOSING REMARK in this edition: **
   ** "It is likely that THE HARAPPANS PRACTICED SOME SORT OF
      DEMOCRATIC SYSTEM. Some of the structures with their
      given features were created APPARENTLY FOR UTILITARIAN
      PURPOSES." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "PRIEST-KING" IS THE MOST IMPORTANT CAUTIONARY TALE IN THE CHAPTER AND IT MUST BE IN YOUR ANSWER ON HARAPPAN AUTHORITY.** ⭐ **The statue is called a priest-king FOR NO REASON ARISING FROM THE STATUE.** *It is called that because **the excavators knew Mesopotamia**, where priest-kings existed, **and reached for the nearest familiar category.*** **The name then hardened — NCERT says it *"continues to be known as"* that — and every subsequent reader inherits an assumption dressed up as a label.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And notice NCERT's demolition, which is a double one:** *(i) **Harappan ritual practice is not understood**, so we cannot say he is a priest; and (ii) ⭐ **even if he were a priest, "nor are there any means of knowing whether those who performed [rituals] ALSO HELD POLITICAL POWER"** — so the *"king"* half fails independently. **Both halves of the name are unsupported.** *This is the same lesson as **Cunningham** (§10.1) and the same lesson as **"curry stones"** (Source 1): **the interpreter's existing frame supplies the answer before the evidence gets a vote.***
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HOW TO WRITE THE §8 ANSWER WELL — the structure that earns marks:**
> 1. **State the evidence for ORGANISATION** — uniformity of pottery, seals, weights and **bricks of one ratio from Jammu to Gujarat**, though "obviously not produced in any single centre"; **planned settlements**; **four million person-days** of labour mobilised for foundations alone; **standard weights adopted as far as Dilmun**.
> 2. **State the ABSENCE** — no palace with royal finds, no royal burials *(§4.1: unlike Egypt's pyramids)*, no depictions of rulers, no readable inscriptions, and **the one "priest-king" is a mislabel**.
> 3. **Give the three positions** with the evidence behind each.
> 4. ⭐ **Conclude honestly**: *"the evidence establishes SUSTAINED CENTRAL COORDINATION but does not identify its FORM. Harappan authority is visible in what it DID and invisible in who it WAS."* **That sentence is the answer.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A note on NCERT's "some sort of democratic system" line.** *It is a **suggestion**, offered without supporting evidence in the text, and it sits alongside the three positions rather than replacing them. ⭐ **Report it as NCERT's remark; do not present it as an established finding**, and do not let it displace the three-position structure, which is what the question is actually testing.*

## 9️⃣ The End of the Civilisation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** WHAT HAPPENED, AND WHEN **

   ** "There is evidence that BY c.1800 BCE MOST OF THE MATURE
      HARAPPAN SITES IN REGIONS SUCH AS CHOLISTAN HAD BEEN
      ABANDONED. SIMULTANEOUSLY, there was AN EXPANSION OF
      POPULATION INTO NEW SETTLEMENTS IN GUJARAT, HARYANA AND
      WESTERN UTTAR PRADESH." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHAT DISAPPEARED after 1900 BCE -- learn this
      list; it is the evidence for the argument below: **

     ** "a TRANSFORMATION OF MATERIAL CULTURE, marked by THE
        DISAPPEARANCE OF THE DISTINCTIVE ARTEFACTS of the
        civilisation -- WEIGHTS, SEALS, SPECIAL BEADS." **
     ** "WRITING, LONG-DISTANCE TRADE, and CRAFT
        SPECIALISATION also disappeared." **
     ** "In general, FAR FEWER MATERIALS WERE USED TO MAKE FAR
        FEWER THINGS." **
     ** "HOUSE CONSTRUCTION TECHNIQUES DETERIORATED and LARGE
        PUBLIC STRUCTURES WERE NO LONGER PRODUCED." **

   ** "Overall, artefacts and settlements indicate A RURAL WAY
      OF LIFE in what are called 'LATE HARAPPAN' or
      'SUCCESSOR CULTURES'." **
```

**🌪️ THE PROPOSED CAUSES — and why NCERT rejects all of them as sufficient:**

> *"Several explanations have been put forward. These range from **CLIMATIC CHANGE, DEFORESTATION, EXCESSIVE FLOODS, THE SHIFTING AND/OR DRYING UP OF RIVERS, to OVERUSE OF THE LANDSCAPE.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**Some of these 'causes' MAY HOLD FOR CERTAIN SETTLEMENTS, BUT THEY DO NOT EXPLAIN THE COLLAPSE OF THE ENTIRE CIVILISATION.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT'S OWN PREFERRED EXPLANATION — and it is a structural one, not an environmental one. This is the paragraph to learn:**
>
> > *"**It appears that A STRONG UNIFYING ELEMENT, PERHAPS THE HARAPPAN STATE, CAME TO AN END.** This is evidenced by **the disappearance of SEALS, THE SCRIPT, DISTINCTIVE BEADS AND POTTERY; the shift FROM A STANDARDISED WEIGHT SYSTEM TO THE USE OF LOCAL WEIGHTS; and the decline and abandonment of cities.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Notice how the argument works, because it is genuinely clever and it ties the whole chapter together.** ⭐ **Every item on the "disappeared" list is an item from §7 and §8 — the machinery of a CONNECTED, STANDARDISED, LONG-DISTANCE system.** *Seals (trust between strangers), the script (recording), standard weights (a common measure), distinctive beads and long-distance trade (the network itself).* **A flood drowns a city; it does not abolish a weight standard across a region.** *So the pattern of loss points at **the failure of an integrating institution, not a local disaster** — and the single most telling detail is ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **the shift from a STANDARDISED weight system to LOCAL weights.** **That is a network dissolving into its parts.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note the closing sentence, which is a bridge to Theme 2:** *"**The subcontinent would have to wait for OVER A MILLENNIUM for new cities to develop in A COMPLETELY DIFFERENT REGION.**"* ⭐ **This is why Theme 2 opens where it does — the SECOND urbanisation, in the GANGA valley, from c. 600 BCE.** *The two are separate events, not a continuous story, and Cunningham's error in §10.1 was precisely to assume Indian history began with the later one.*

### 📖 SOURCE 3 — *Evidence of an "invasion"* — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the chapter's showpiece in source criticism

> **This source is a THREE-STAGE ARGUMENT ACROSS THREE SCHOLARS AND FIFTY YEARS, and NCERT prints it precisely so you can watch an interpretation be built and then dismantled. Learn it as a sequence.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** STAGE 1 -- THE FINDS (John Marshall, 1931) **
   ================================================================
   ** "DEADMAN LANE is a NARROW ALLEY, varying from 3 TO 6
      FEET in width… At the point where the lane turns
      westward, PART OF A SKULL AND THE BONES OF THE THORAX
      AND UPPER ARM OF AN ADULT were discovered, all in VERY
      FRIABLE CONDITION, at a depth of 4 FT 2 IN. THE BODY LAY
      ON ITS BACK DIAGONALLY ACROSS THE LANE. Fifteen inches
      to the west were A FEW FRAGMENTS OF A TINY SKULL. It is
      to these remains that THE LANE OWES ITS NAME." **
   ** Also: "SIXTEEN SKELETONS of people WITH THE ORNAMENTS
      THAT THEY WERE WEARING WHEN THEY DIED were found from
      THE SAME PART OF MOHENJODARO IN 1925." **

   ================================================================
   ** STAGE 2 -- THE INTERPRETATION (R.E.M. Wheeler, 1947) **
   ================================================================
   ⭐ Note the DATE -- 1947, the year of Partition, and the
     year after Wheeler took over the ASI.

   ** "The RIGVEDA mentions PUR, meaning RAMPART, FORT OR
      STRONGHOLD. INDRA, THE ARYAN WAR-GOD, IS CALLED
      PURAMDARA, THE FORT-DESTROYER." **
   ** "Where are -- or were -- these citadels? IT HAS IN THE
      PAST BEEN SUPPOSED THAT THEY WERE MYTHICAL… The recent
      excavation of Harappa MAY BE THOUGHT TO HAVE CHANGED THE
      PICTURE. Here we have A HIGHLY EVOLVED CIVILISATION OF
      ESSENTIALLY NON-ARYAN TYPE, NOW KNOWN TO HAVE EMPLOYED
      MASSIVE FORTIFICATIONS… " **
   ** "What destroyed this firmly settled civilisation?
      CLIMATIC, ECONOMIC OR POLITICAL DETERIORATION MAY HAVE
      WEAKENED IT, but ITS ULTIMATE EXTINCTION IS MORE LIKELY
      TO HAVE BEEN COMPLETED BY DELIBERATE AND LARGE-SCALE
      DESTRUCTION. IT MAY BE NO MERE CHANCE THAT AT A LATE
      PERIOD OF MOHENJODARO MEN, WOMEN, AND CHILDREN, APPEAR
      TO HAVE BEEN MASSACRED THERE." **
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "ON CIRCUMSTANTIAL EVIDENCE, INDRA STANDS
      ACCUSED." **

   ================================================================
   ** STAGE 3 -- THE REFUTATION (George Dales, 1964) **
   ================================================================
   ** "He demonstrated that THE SKELETONS FOUND AT THE SITE
      DID NOT BELONG TO THE SAME PERIOD: " **

   ** "Whereas A COUPLE OF THEM DEFINITELY SEEM TO INDICATE A
      SLAUGHTER, … THE BULK OF THE BONES WERE FOUND IN
      CONTEXTS SUGGESTING BURIALS OF THE SLOPPIEST AND MOST
      IRREVERENT NATURE." **
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE FOUR ABSENCES -- this is the demolition: **
     ** "THERE IS NO DESTRUCTION LEVEL covering the latest
        period of the city" **
     ** "NO SIGN OF EXTENSIVE BURNING" **
     ** "NO BODIES OF WARRIORS CLAD IN ARMOUR AND SURROUNDED
        BY THE WEAPONS OF WAR" **
     ** "THE CITADEL, THE ONLY FORTIFIED PART OF THE CITY,
        YIELDED NO EVIDENCE OF A FINAL DEFENCE." **
   ** (The paper's title says it: "THE MYTHICAL MASSACRE AT
      MOHENJODARO", Expedition, 1964.) **

   ⭐ ** NCERT'S OWN MORAL: **
   ** "As you can see, A CAREFUL RE-EXAMINATION OF THE DATA
      CAN SOMETIMES LEAD TO A REVERSAL OF EARLIER
      INTERPRETATIONS." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WHY WHEELER'S ARGUMENT FAILED — and you should be able to name the fault, not merely the conclusion.** ⭐ **He STARTED FROM A TEXT AND WENT LOOKING FOR THE ARCHAEOLOGY TO MATCH IT.** *The Rigveda calls Indra *puramdara*, "fort-destroyer"; Harappa has forts; Mohenjodaro has some skeletons — **therefore Indra.*** **The skeletons were made to fit a story that existed before they were examined.** *Dales did the elementary thing Wheeler had not: **he checked whether the bodies were even CONTEMPORARY with each other.** They were not.*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DALES'S METHOD IS THE MOST TRANSFERABLE THING IN THE SECTION: HE ARGUED FROM WHAT WAS *NOT* THERE.** ⭐ *A city sacked in war leaves **a destruction layer, burning, dead defenders, weapons, and signs of a last stand at its fortification.*** **Mohenjodaro's Citadel — the only fortified part — shows none of it.** *In an answer, write: **"Dales showed that the massacre hypothesis failed not because the skeletons were absent but because EVERY OTHER EXPECTED CORRELATE OF A SACK WAS ABSENT, and because the bones were not contemporaneous."***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Do not miss the phrase *"burials of the sloppiest and most irreverent nature."*** *Dales is not saying nothing bad happened — **he explicitly concedes "a couple of them definitely seem to indicate a slaughter."*** ⭐ **He is saying the BULK of the evidence is careless burial, not battle.** *Reproduce that nuance; it is what makes his refutation credible rather than merely contrarian.*

     OF GENES IN SMALL QUANTITY." **
   * ⭐ ** "The continuity WITHOUT ANY BREAK in genetic history
     as well as cultural history RULES OUT LARGE SCALE
     IMMIGRATION OF THE SO-CALLED ARYANS." **
   * ** "people coming from bordering areas and distant regions
     WERE ABSORBED IN THE INDIAN SOCIETY. AT NO STAGE, THE
     GENETIC HISTORY OF THE INDIANS WAS EITHER DISCONTINUED OR
     BROKEN." **
   * ** "As the Harappans started MOVING TOWARDS IRAN AND
     CENTRAL ASIA, their genes also GRADUALLY SPREAD in these
     regions." **
   * ⭐ ** 3D FACIAL RECONSTRUCTION: "The reconstructed facial
     features of the Harappans, male and female, show
     REMARKABLE SIMILARITY WITH THE MODERN POPULATION OF
     HARYANA. Thus, this study has indicated UNBROKEN
     CONTINUITY FOR 5000 YEARS in this region." **

   ** ARCHAEOGENETICS ** (margin definition):
   ** "the STUDY OF DNA OF ANCIENT POPULATION. It involves
      various methods of MOLECULAR GENETICS to understand
      POPULATION HISTORY." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Where this box sits in the chapter's argument is itself worth noticing, and it is a good observation to make.** ⭐ **NCERT places the archaeogenetics immediately after the Wheeler/Dales exchange** — i.e. **right after demolishing an invasion hypothesis built from a text.** *The sequence reads: **Wheeler inferred invasion from the Rigveda → Dales refuted it from the archaeology → and now a third, independent technique (ancient DNA) is brought to bear on the same question.*** **Three methods, one question, across ninety years.** *That is the §10–§11 lesson arriving early: **a discipline advances by applying new techniques to old claims.***
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **How to handle this in an exam — be precise and be safe.** *For **Prelims**, learn it as stated: **Rakhigarhi, Hisar district, Haryana, 550 ha, biggest Harappan city; Deccan College + CCMB + Harvard; Shinde et al., *Cell*, 2019; genetic roots to 10,000 BCE.*** ⭐ *For **Mains**, **attribute rather than assert** — write **"the Rakhigarhi archaeogenetic study (Shinde et al., 2019) concluded that…"** rather than stating it flatly as settled fact. **That is how NCERT itself frames it (as a cited study), it is how professional history is written, and it keeps you safe on a question where scholarly discussion is live.***

## 🔟 Discovering the Harappan Civilisation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS SECTION IS THE CHAPTER'S REAL PAYLOAD FOR MAINS. It is the history of the historians — and its lesson generalises to every other chapter in Class 12.**

> **NCERT's framing:** *"So far, we have examined facets of the Harappan civilisation in the context of **how archaeologists have used evidence from material remains to piece together parts of a fascinating history. However, THERE IS ANOTHER STORY AS WELL — ABOUT HOW ARCHAEOLOGISTS 'DISCOVERED' THE CIVILISATION.**"*
>
> ⭐ *"When Harappan cities fell into ruin, **PEOPLE GRADUALLY FORGOT ALL ABOUT THEM.** When men and women began living in the area millennia later, **THEY DID NOT KNOW WHAT TO MAKE OF THE STRANGE ARTEFACTS that occasionally surfaced** — washed by floods or exposed by soil erosion, or turned up while ploughing a field, or digging for treasure."*

### 10.1 Cunningham's confusion — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the most important story in the book

```
   ** WHAT CUNNINGHAM DID **

   * ** First Director-General of the ASI ** ; began
     excavations ** in the mid-nineteenth century **
   * ** "archaeologists preferred to use THE WRITTEN WORD
     (TEXTS AND INSCRIPTIONS) AS A GUIDE TO INVESTIGATIONS." **
   * ⭐ ** "Cunningham's MAIN INTEREST was in the archaeology of

   ================================================================

      converging reasons, and all three are the SAME reason **
   ================================================================

   1. ⭐ ** "A site like HARAPPA, WHICH WAS NOT PART OF THE

   3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE SEAL: "A Harappan seal was given to

      UNSUCCESSFULLY TRIED TO PLACE IT WITHIN THE TIME-FRAME
      WITH WHICH HE WAS FAMILIAR. THIS WAS BECAUSE HE, LIKE
      MANY OTHERS, THOUGHT THAT INDIAN HISTORY BEGAN WITH THE

   ** "Given HIS SPECIFIC FOCUS, IT IS NOT SURPRISING THAT HE

   (Fig. 1.24 is ** "Cunningham's sketch of the first-known

```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE STORY TO TELL WHENEVER A QUESTION TOUCHES ON HOW HISTORICAL KNOWLEDGE IS PRODUCED — IN THIS CHAPTER OR ANY OTHER.** ⭐ **The father of Indian archaeology HELD A HARAPPAN SEAL IN HIS HAND, SKETCHED IT, AND COULD NOT SEE IT.** *Not through carelessness — **through method.** He was doing exactly what his discipline recommended: **use texts to guide the spade.** *And texts were the problem: **there are no texts for the Harappans**, so a text-led archaeology was structurally blind to them.*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GENERALISABLE LESSON, AND IT IS ONE SENTENCE: A FRAMEWORK OF INVESTIGATION DETERMINES WHAT COUNTS AS EVIDENCE, AND THEREFORE WHAT CAN BE SEEN AT ALL.** ⭐ *Cunningham believed **Indian history began with the cities of the Ganga valley**; an object two thousand years older than that had **nowhere to go** in his scheme, so it became an unclassifiable curiosity rather than a discovery.*
>
> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THIS IS THE SAME LESSON AS THE REST OF CLASS 12 — say so, and the whole course locks together.** *The **"priest-king"** was named from Mesopotamian expectations (§8.1). **Mackay's "curry stones"** were named from a modern kitchen (Source 1). **Wheeler found Indra** because he began from the Rigveda (Source 3). **Theme 10's British painters** showed the rebels of 1857 as they needed them to be. **Theme 9's Fifth Report** was written by the accused. **Supplement A's Thoa Khalsa ritual** remembers martyrdom and forgets coercion.* **Six chapters, one point: the observer's frame selects the evidence.**

### 10.2 A new old civilisation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE DISCOVERY SEQUENCE -- names and roles, all
      Prelims-shaped **

   ** DAYA RAM SAHNI ** -- discovered seals ** at HARAPPA ** ,
   "in the EARLY DECADES OF THE TWENTIETH CENTURY", ⭐ ** "in
   LAYERS THAT WERE DEFINITELY MUCH OLDER THAN EARLY HISTORIC
   LEVELS. IT WAS THEN THAT THEIR SIGNIFICANCE BEGAN TO BE
   REALISED." **

   ** RAKHAL DAS BANERJI ** -- ** "found SIMILAR SEALS at
   MOHENJODARO, leading to the conjecture that THESE SITES
   WERE PART OF A SINGLE ARCHAEOLOGICAL CULTURE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** JOHN MARSHALL, Director-General of the ASI --
   "Based on these finds, IN 1924, John Marshall ANNOUNCED THE
   DISCOVERY OF A NEW CIVILISATION IN THE INDUS VALLEY TO THE
   WORLD." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE FAMOUS LINE, and it is worth quoting exactly.
      S.N. Roy, in THE STORY OF INDIAN ARCHAEOLOGY: **
   ** "MARSHALL LEFT INDIA THREE THOUSAND YEARS OLDER THAN HE
      HAD FOUND HER." **

   ** WHY THE ANNOUNCEMENT MATTERED SO MUCH: **
   ** "This was because SIMILAR, TILL-THEN-UNIDENTIFIED SEALS
      WERE FOUND AT EXCAVATIONS AT MESOPOTAMIAN SITES. IT WAS
      THEN THAT THE WORLD KNEW NOT ONLY OF A NEW CIVILISATION,
      BUT ALSO OF ONE CONTEMPORANEOUS WITH MESOPOTAMIA." **
```

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note the crucial methodological difference between Sahni and Cunningham, because it is the entire point of §10.2.** *Cunningham also had a seal. **Sahni had a seal IN A STRATIGRAPHIC LAYER** — *"in layers that were definitely much older than Early Historic levels."* ⭐ **The object was the same; the CONTEXT was the discovery.** *Which is precisely why §10.3's argument about stratigraphy matters so much.*

**⚖️ MARSHALL — the great advance, and the great mistake:**

| ✅ What Marshall got right | ❌ What Marshall got wrong |
|---|---|
| ⭐ *"the **FIRST PROFESSIONAL ARCHAEOLOGIST** to work in India"*, bringing *"his experience of working in **GREECE AND CRETE**"* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Marshall tended to **EXCAVATE ALONG REGULAR HORIZONTAL UNITS, MEASURED UNIFORMLY THROUGHOUT THE MOUND, IGNORING THE STRATIGRAPHY OF THE SITE**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"though like Cunningham he too was interested in **spectacular finds**, he was **EQUALLY KEEN TO LOOK FOR PATTERNS OF EVERYDAY LIFE**"* | *"This meant that **ALL THE ARTEFACTS RECOVERED FROM THE SAME UNIT WERE GROUPED TOGETHER, EVEN IF THEY WERE FOUND AT DIFFERENT STRATIGRAPHIC LAYERS**"* |
| *"his stint… **marked A MAJOR CHANGE IN INDIAN ARCHAEOLOGY**"* | ⭐ *"As a result, **VALUABLE INFORMATION ABOUT THE CONTEXT OF THESE FINDS WAS IRRETRIEVABLY LOST.**"* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"PATTERNS OF EVERYDAY LIFE" IS THE PHRASE THAT MARKS THE TURN IN INDIAN ARCHAEOLOGY, AND IT IS WORTH A SENTENCE.** *Cunningham recovered *"artefacts that he thought had CULTURAL VALUE"* — i.e. the impressive ones. ⭐ **Marshall was interested in the ordinary.** *And it is the ordinary — the querns, the drains, the waste chips, the bones — that this entire chapter is built from. **§5.1's "waste is one of the best indicators of craft work" is only possible in a discipline that has stopped hunting treasure.***
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The word "IRRETRIEVABLY" is doing heavy work and you should stress it in an answer.** ⭐ **Excavation destroys its own evidence: you can only dig a layer once.** *An object recorded without its context has permanently lost the information its context carried — **the damage cannot be repaired by later scholarship.*** *This is why §10.3's correction mattered, and why archaeological method is not a technicality.*

### 📏 The "Sites, mounds, layers" box — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ learn this, it is pure Prelims

```
   ** HOW A SITE FORMS **
   ** "Archaeological sites are FORMED THROUGH THE PRODUCTION,
      USE AND DISCARDING OF MATERIALS AND STRUCTURES." **

   ** MOUND ** -- ⭐ ** "When people CONTINUE TO LIVE IN THE
   SAME PLACE, their CONSTANT USE AND REUSE OF THE LANDSCAPE
   RESULTS IN THE BUILD UP OF OCCUPATIONAL DEBRIS, CALLED A
   MOUND." **

   ** "BRIEF OR PERMANENT ABANDONMENT results in ALTERATION OF
      THE LANDSCAPE BY WIND OR WATER ACTIVITY AND EROSION." **

   ** LAYERS ** -- ** "Occupations are detected by TRACES OF
   ANCIENT MATERIALS FOUND IN LAYERS, WHICH DIFFER FROM ONE
   ANOTHER IN COLOUR, TEXTURE AND THE ARTEFACTS THAT ARE FOUND
   IN THEM." **

   ⭐ ** "STERILE LAYERS" ** -- ** "Abandonment or desertions…
   can be identified by THE ABSENCE OF SUCH TRACES." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** STRATIGRAPHY ** -- ** "GENERALLY, THE LOWEST
   LAYERS ARE THE OLDEST AND THE HIGHEST ARE THE MOST RECENT.
   THE STUDY OF THESE LAYERS IS CALLED STRATIGRAPHY.
   ARTEFACTS FOUND IN LAYERS CAN BE ASSIGNED TO SPECIFIC
   CULTURAL PERIODS AND CAN THUS PROVIDE THE CULTURAL SEQUENCE
   FOR A SITE." **

   ⚠️ ** AND THE CAPTION TO FIG. 1.25 IS THE WHOLE POINT: **
   ** "NOTICE THAT THE LAYERS ARE NOT EXACTLY HORIZONTAL." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FIG. 1.25's CAPTION EXPLAINS MARSHALL'S ERROR IN SIX WORDS, AND CONNECTING THE TWO IS EXACTLY THE KIND OF READING THAT EARNS MARKS.** ⭐ **Layers are NOT exactly horizontal — they dip, tilt and interleave.** *So digging in **uniform horizontal slices measured across the whole mound** will slice **through** several layers at once, mixing objects of different ages into one "unit". **That is precisely how the context was "irretrievably lost".*** **Marshall's method assumed a geometry the ground does not have.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Sterile layers" is a lovely small concept worth keeping: ABSENCE is itself readable.** *A layer with no cultural traces records **abandonment** — ⭐ **a period of nothing is a historical fact, and it is datable.** *(Compare §2.1's canals: NCERT is careful there too, offering silting rather than concluding "no irrigation." **This chapter is consistently rigorous about what absence does and does not prove.**)*

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Problems of Piecing Together the Past ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⛔⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS SECTION IS THE CHAPTER'S CONCLUSION AND ITS MOST EXAMINABLE PART FOR MAINS. It is the honest accounting of what archaeology cannot do — and it is the direct answer to Exercise 8 ("Discuss how archaeologists reconstruct the past").**

> **NCERT's opening restates the premise of the whole chapter:** *"**It is NOT the Harappan script that helps in understanding the ancient civilisation. Rather, IT IS MATERIAL EVIDENCE that allows archaeologists to better reconstruct Harappan life** — pottery, tools, ornaments, household objects."*

```
   ** PROBLEM 1 -- ** SURVIVAL BIAS ** (what the ground keeps)

   ** "ORGANIC MATERIALS SUCH AS CLOTH, LEATHER, WOOD AND REEDS
      GENERALLY DECOMPOSE, ESPECIALLY IN TROPICAL REGIONS.
      WHAT SURVIVE ARE STONE, BURNT CLAY (OR TERRACOTTA),
      METAL, ETC." **

   ⭐ THE CONSEQUENCE: an entire material world -- clothing,
     furniture, baskets, ropes, boats, wooden architecture --
     is simply MISSING. (Recall §3.3: the warehouse's upper
     storey was "PROBABLY OF WOOD" and "DECAYED LONG AGO".)

   ** PROBLEM 2 -- ** DISCARD BIAS ** (what people threw away)

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "It is also important to remember that ONLY
      BROKEN OR USELESS OBJECTS WOULD HAVE BEEN THROWN AWAY.
      OTHER THINGS WOULD PROBABLY HAVE BEEN RECYCLED." **
   ** "Consequently, VALUABLE ARTEFACTS THAT ARE FOUND INTACT
      WERE EITHER LOST IN THE PAST OR HOARDED AND NEVER
      RETRIEVED. IN OTHER WORDS, SUCH FINDS ARE ACCIDENTAL
      RATHER THAN TYPICAL." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PROBLEM 2 IS THE DEEPEST IDEA IN THE CHAPTER AND IT IS WORTH REAL THOUGHT.** *The archaeological record is **not a random sample** of a past society. It is **overwhelmingly composed of two categories: RUBBISH, and ACCIDENTS.*** ⭐ **What was valuable was kept, repaired, reused, inherited and eventually melted down or worn out — so it is NOT in the ground.** *What is in the ground intact is there **because somebody lost it or hid it and died.*** **Hence §4.2: *all* the Harappan gold jewellery came from **hoards**.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Say this once in an answer and it will lift the whole thing: *"the archaeological record consists disproportionately of what was DISCARDED and what was ACCIDENTALLY LOST — it is therefore systematically unrepresentative of what a society VALUED."*** *And note the neat tension with §5.1: **rubbish is a distortion when you want to know what people treasured, and it is the BEST evidence when you want to know where they WORKED.** Same material, opposite value, depending on the question. **That is what "different sources answer different questions" means in practice.**

### 11.1 Classifying finds ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE TWO PRINCIPLES OF CLASSIFICATION **

   ** PRINCIPLE 1 -- BY MATERIAL ** ("one SIMPLE principle")
      ** stone · clay · metal · bone · ivory **

   ** PRINCIPLE 2 -- BY FUNCTION ** ("the second, and MORE
   COMPLICATED")
      ⭐ ** "archaeologists have to DECIDE whether, for
      instance, AN ARTEFACT IS A TOOL OR AN ORNAMENT, OR BOTH,
      OR SOMETHING MEANT FOR RITUAL USE." **

   ** THE THREE WAYS FUNCTION IS DETERMINED: **

   (a) ** RESEMBLANCE TO THE PRESENT ** -- "An understanding of
       the function of an artefact is often SHAPED BY ITS
       RESEMBLANCE WITH PRESENT-DAY THINGS -- BEADS, QUERNS,
       STONE BLADES AND POTS are obvious examples."

   (b) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** CONTEXT ** -- "Archaeologists also try to
       identify the function of an artefact by INVESTIGATING
       THE CONTEXT IN WHICH IT WAS FOUND: ** WAS IT FOUND IN A
       HOUSE, IN A DRAIN, IN A GRAVE, IN A KILN? ** "

   (c) ** INDIRECT EVIDENCE ** -- "Sometimes, archaeologists
       have to take recourse to indirect evidence. For
       instance, ⭐ ** though there are TRACES OF COTTON at
       some Harappan sites, TO FIND OUT ABOUT CLOTHING WE HAVE
       TO DEPEND ON INDIRECT EVIDENCE INCLUDING DEPICTIONS IN
       SCULPTURE. ** "

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE FOURTH REQUIREMENT -- A FRAME OF
      REFERENCE: **
   ** "Archaeologists have to DEVELOP FRAMES OF REFERENCE. We
      have seen that THE FIRST HARAPPAN SEAL THAT WAS FOUND
      COULD NOT BE UNDERSTOOD TILL ARCHAEOLOGISTS HAD A
      CONTEXT IN WHICH TO PLACE IT -- both in terms of THE
      CULTURAL SEQUENCE in which it was found, and in terms of
      A COMPARISON WITH FINDS IN MESOPOTAMIA." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE CHAPTER CLOSES ITS OWN LOOP HERE, AND SPOTTING IT IS A GENUINE MARK OF HAVING READ THE CHAPTER.** ⭐ **Cunningham's seal (§10.1) failed not because he lacked the object but because he lacked A FRAME OF REFERENCE.** *It became intelligible only once there was **(i) a stratigraphic sequence** — Sahni's older-than-Early-Historic layers — **and (ii) a comparison** — the same seals turning up in Mesopotamia. **The object never changed. The frame did.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The cotton example is a small gem — use it if a question asks about indirect evidence.** *We have **traces of cotton**, which proves the material existed. **We have no cloth**, because it rotted. ⭐ **So everything we say about Harappan CLOTHING comes from SCULPTURE — an image of a garment, standing in for the garment.*** **Two removes from the thing itself, and NCERT tells you so plainly.**

### 11.2 Problems of interpretation — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ RELIGION, and the *"proto-Shiva"* problem

> *"**The problems of archaeological interpretation are PERHAPS MOST EVIDENT IN ATTEMPTS TO RECONSTRUCT RELIGIOUS PRACTICES.**"*

```
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE ORIGINAL SIN, STATED BY NCERT ITSELF: **
   ** "EARLY ARCHAEOLOGISTS THOUGHT THAT CERTAIN OBJECTS WHICH
      SEEMED UNUSUAL OR UNFAMILIAR MAY HAVE HAD A RELIGIOUS
      SIGNIFICANCE." **

   ⭐ Read that again. The criterion was ** "unusual or
     unfamiliar" ** -- i.e. ** "we cannot think what this is
     for, therefore it is religious." **

   ** WHAT GOT CLASSIFIED AS RELIGIOUS ON THAT BASIS: **

   * ** "TERRACOTTA FIGURINES OF WOMEN, HEAVILY JEWELLED, some
     with ELABORATE HEAD-DRESSES. THESE WERE REGARDED AS
     MOTHER GODDESSES." **   (Fig. 1.26: ⭐ NCERT's own caption
     is a question -- ** "WAS THIS A MOTHER GODDESS?" ** )
   * ** "RARE STONE STATUARY OF MEN IN AN ALMOST STANDARDISED
     POSTURE, SEATED WITH ONE HAND ON THE KNEE -- SUCH AS THE
     'PRIEST-KING'" **
   * ** STRUCTURES: "THE GREAT BATH and FIRE ALTARS FOUND AT
     KALIBANGAN AND LOTHAL" **
   * ** SEALS: "some of which SEEM TO DEPICT RITUAL SCENES.
     Others, with PLANT MOTIFS, are thought to indicate NATURE
     WORSHIP." **
   * ** "Some animals -- such as THE ONE-HORNED ANIMAL, OFTEN
     CALLED THE 'UNICORN' -- depicted on seals SEEM TO BE
     MYTHICAL, COMPOSITE CREATURES." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "In some seals, A FIGURE SHOWN SEATED
     CROSS-LEGGED IN A 'YOGIC' POSTURE, SOMETIMES SURROUNDED
     BY ANIMALS, HAS BEEN REGARDED AS A DEPICTION OF
     'PROTO-SHIVA'." **
   * ** "CONICAL STONE OBJECTS have been classified as
     LINGAS." **
     (margin: ** A LINGA is "a polished stone that is
      worshipped as A SYMBOL OF SHIVA" ** )

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE METHODOLOGICAL WARNING -- learn this
      sentence: **
   ================================================================
   ** "Many reconstructions of Harappan religion are made ON
      THE ASSUMPTION THAT LATER TRADITIONS PROVIDE PARALLELS
      WITH EARLIER ONES. This is because ARCHAEOLOGISTS OFTEN
      MOVE FROM THE KNOWN TO THE UNKNOWN, THAT IS, FROM THE
      PRESENT TO THE PAST. WHILE THIS IS PLAUSIBLE IN THE CASE
      OF STONE QUERNS AND POTS, IT BECOMES MORE SPECULATIVE
      WHEN WE EXTEND IT TO 'RELIGIOUS' SYMBOLS." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "PROTO-SHIVA" DEMOLITION — this is the chapter's finest single argument and it must be reproducible in your own words. NCERT dismantles the identification IN THREE STEPS:**
>
> ```
>    STEP 1 -- ** THE CLAIM: the seated cross-legged figure
>             surrounded by animals is "PROTO-SHIVA", i.e.
>             "AN EARLY FORM OF ONE OF THE MAJOR DEITIES OF
>             HINDUISM." **
>
>    STEP 2 -- ** THE TEXTUAL CHECK: "The earliest religious
>             text, THE RIGVEDA (compiled c.1500-1000 BCE)
>             mentions A GOD NAMED RUDRA, WHICH IS A NAME USED
>             FOR SHIVA IN LATER PURANIC TRADITIONS (in the
>             first millennium CE)." **
>
>    STEP 3 -- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE REFUTATION: "HOWEVER, UNLIKE
>             SHIVA, RUDRA IN THE RIGVEDA IS NEITHER DEPICTED
>             AS PASHUPATI (LORD OF ANIMALS IN GENERAL AND
>             CATTLE IN PARTICULAR), NOR AS A YOGI. IN OTHER
>             WORDS, THIS DEPICTION DOES NOT MATCH THE
>             DESCRIPTION OF RUDRA IN THE RIGVEDA." **
>
>    ⭐ ** AND THE ALTERNATIVE OFFERED: "IS THIS, THEN,
>       POSSIBLY A SHAMAN AS SOME SCHOLARS HAVE SUGGESTED?" **
>       (margin: ** SHAMANS are "men and women who claim
>       MAGICAL AND HEALING POWERS, as well as AN ABILITY TO
>       COMMUNICATE WITH THE OTHER WORLD" ** )
> ```
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WHY THE REFUTATION IS SO ELEGANT — spell this out, because it is the point.** *The "proto-Shiva" reading works by looking **BACKWARDS from PURANIC Shiva** (first millennium **CE**) to a seal of **2600–1900 BCE** — a gap of **two to three thousand years**. ⭐ **NCERT tests the claim at the INTERMEDIATE point — the Rigveda — and the chain breaks there:** *Rigvedic Rudra is **neither Pashupati nor a yogi**.* **The two features the seal is being identified BY are exactly the two features the earliest text does NOT have.** *The resemblance is to the LATE form only, which means the identification is reading the present into the past. **That is the "known to unknown" fallacy caught in the act.***
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HOW TO WRITE ABOUT HARAPPAN RELIGION SAFELY AND WELL — a formula worth memorising:** *"Certain objects — terracotta female figurines, the seated figure on some seals, conical stones, the Great Bath, the fire altars at Kalibangan and Lothal — **have been interpreted** as religious. **These identifications rest on analogy with later traditions, and NCERT itself cautions that such analogy is plausible for querns and pots but speculative for religious symbols.** The 'proto-Shiva' identification, for instance, does not survive comparison with Rigvedic Rudra, who is neither Pashupati nor a yogi."* ⭐ **Never write "the Harappans worshipped Shiva" or "the mother goddess cult" as fact. The chapter exists largely to stop you doing that.**

### 🎲 The Mackay box — *Gamesmen or lingas?* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Mackay, *Early Indus Civilisation*, 1948, on the conical stones:** *"**Various small cones made of LAPIS LAZULI, JASPER, CHALCEDONY, and other stones, MOST BEAUTIFULLY CUT AND FINISHED, AND LESS THAN TWO INCHES IN HEIGHT, are also thought to be LINGAS … ON THE OTHER HAND, IT IS JUST AS POSSIBLE THAT THEY WERE USED IN THE BOARD-GAMES.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BEST SHORT ILLUSTRATION IN THE ENTIRE BOOK OF THE INTERPRETIVE PROBLEM, AND IT IS FUNNY, WHICH MAKES IT MEMORABLE.** ⭐ **The same small polished cone is either A SACRED OBJECT or A GAME PIECE, and the material evidence cannot decide between them.** *One reading makes Harappan religion continuous with later Hinduism; the other makes it a set of counters for a board game.* **The distance between those two conclusions, drawn from one object, is the measure of how carefully you must write about Harappan belief.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note the two details Mackay gives, because they cut both ways and that is why the case is undecidable.** *"**Most beautifully cut and finished**" and made of **lapis lazuli, jasper, chalcedony** — that is a lot of craftsmanship and costly material for a game piece. ⭐ **But "less than two inches in height" is very small for a cult object.** *Neither observation settles it. **NCERT prints the box precisely so you can feel an interpretation fail to close.***

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ What has been achieved, and what remains unknown — NCERT's own audit

```
   ** WHAT WE KNOW: **
   ** "We have A FAIRLY GOOD IDEA OF THE HARAPPAN ECONOMY.
      We have been able to TEASE OUT SOCIAL DIFFERENCES and we
      have SOME IDEA OF HOW THE CIVILISATION FUNCTIONED." **

   ⭐ ** AND A STRIKING ADMISSION ABOUT THE SCRIPT: **
   ** "IT IS REALLY NOT CLEAR HOW MUCH MORE WE WOULD KNOW IF
      THE SCRIPT WERE TO BE DECIPHERED. IF A BILINGUAL
      INSCRIPTION IS FOUND, QUESTIONS ABOUT THE LANGUAGES
      SPOKEN BY THE HARAPPANS COULD PERHAPS BE PUT TO REST." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE LIST OF OPEN QUESTIONS -- NCERT names them,
      and naming them in an answer is a mark of quality: **

     * ** "WAS THE GREAT BATH A RITUAL STRUCTURE?" **
     * ** "HOW WIDESPREAD WAS LITERACY?" **
     * ** "WHY DO HARAPPAN CEMETERIES SHOW LITTLE SOCIAL
       DIFFERENTIATION?" **
     * ⭐ ** GENDER: "did women MAKE POTTERY or did they ONLY
       PAINT POTS (AS AT PRESENT)? What about other
       craftspersons? WHAT WERE THE TERRACOTTA FEMALE
       FIGURINES USED FOR?" **
       ** "VERY FEW SCHOLARS HAVE INVESTIGATED ISSUES OF GENDER
          IN THE CONTEXT OF THE HARAPPAN CIVILISATION AND THIS
          IS A WHOLE NEW AREA FOR FUTURE WORK." **
     * ** "More research is also required on THE RELATIONSHIP
       BETWEEN THE HARAPPANS AND THE VEDIC PEOPLE, as some
       scholars have argued that THE AUTHORS OF THE HARAPPAN
       CIVILISATION AND THE VEDIC PEOPLE WERE THE SAME." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BILINGUAL-INSCRIPTION REMARK IS THE MOST SOPHISTICATED THING IN §11 AND ALMOST NOBODY NOTICES IT.** ⭐ **NCERT says it is "really not clear how much more we would know" if the script were read** — *because the inscriptions are **short** (longest ~26 signs) and are on **seals**, i.e. probably names and titles. **A thousand name-tags do not make a literature.*** *And the specific hope offered is precise: **a BILINGUAL inscription** — the Rosetta-stone principle, a known language beside the unknown one — *which would settle **the LANGUAGE question**, not necessarily the history.* **That distinction, between decipherment and understanding, is worth a sentence in any answer about the script.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the gender paragraph is genuinely unusual — a textbook naming a gap in its own discipline and inviting work on it.** ⭐ *Note the built-in caution in its own example: **"did women make pottery or did they only paint pots (AS AT PRESENT)?"*** — **the phrase "as at present" flags the very present-to-past analogy §11.2 warned about**, in the same breath as using it. *That is §11 practising what it preaches, and it is a lovely note to end the chapter on.*

### 🔴 Prelims traps — the claim vs the truth

| ❌ The plausible-sounding claim | ✅ What the chapter actually says |
|---|---|
| Mohenjodaro was the first Harappan site discovered | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HARAPPA was first** — Mohenjodaro is merely **best known**, and better preserved because **Harappa's bricks were robbed for a railway** |
| The Mature Harappan phase is 3000–1500 BCE | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **2600–1900 BCE.** Early Harappan **6000–2600**; Late Harappan **1900–1300**; total span **6000–1300 BCE** |
| Rice and millets were principal Harappan crops | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Finds of RICE are relatively RARE**; **MILLETS are from GUJARAT sites**. Staples: **wheat, barley, lentil, chickpea, sesame** |
| Most Harappan sites lie in the Indus basin | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Nearly TWO-THIRDS of these settlements are in the SARASWATI basin"**; **over 2000 sites** known |
| The five major cities include Lothal and Kalibangan | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The five are **RAKHIGARHI, MOHENJODARO, HARAPPA, DHOLAVIRA, GANWERIWALA.** Lothal is a **port**; other categories are regional centres, agricultural villages, manufacturing centres |
| Mohenjodaro is the largest Harappan city | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **RAKHIGARHI (550 ha, Hisar district, Haryana)** is *"the biggest Harappan city"*; Mohenjodaro is **125 ha**; Chanhudaro **under 7 ha** |
| The Citadel was naturally elevated | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ It *"owes its height to the fact that **buildings were constructed on MUD BRICK PLATFORMS**"* |
| Only the Citadel was walled | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The Lower Town was ALSO walled."** At **Dholavira and Lothal the ENTIRE settlement was fortified**; **Lothal's Citadel was NOT walled off**, only raised |
| The brick ratio was 1:2:4 height:breadth:length in a few cities | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Length : breadth : height = 4 : 2 : 1**, used at **ALL** Harappan settlements, **"from Jammu to Gujarat"** |
| Houses were built first, drains added later | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**Streets with drains were laid out FIRST and then houses built along them**"*; every house needed **at least one wall along a street** |
| Lothal's drains were of mud brick like its houses | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Houses of MUD brick; DRAINS of BURNT brick** |
| Mohenjodaro had a few public wells | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **About 700 wells**; **every house had its own bathroom**; many wells were in rooms **reachable from outside** |
| The Great Bath was made watertight with bitumen | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Bricks SET ON EDGE with a MORTAR OF GYPSUM**; **two flights of steps (north and south)**; a **well** in one of the rooms; **eight bathrooms** in a building across the lane to the north |
| Harappan elites were buried with great wealth | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"On the whole… the Harappans **did NOT believe in burying precious things with the dead**"* — **unlike the contemporaneous Egyptian pyramids** |
| All Harappan gold was found in graves | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***All* the gold jewellery was recovered from HOARDS** |
| Faience is a natural stone | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"a material made of **GROUND SAND OR SILICA MIXED WITH COLOUR AND A GUM AND THEN FIRED**"* |
| Archaeologists locate workshops by finished products | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **By WASTE** — *"waste is one of the best indicators of craft work"*; also raw material, tools, unfinished objects, rejects. **Minuscule bits stay put** |
| Shortughai was a source of copper | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **LAPIS LAZULI** (Afghanistan). **Copper came from KHETRI (Rajasthan)** and probably **Oman**; **gold from south India** |
| The Ganeshwar-Jodhpura culture was Harappan | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It had **"distinctive NON-Harappan pottery"** and an *"unusual wealth of copper objects"* — probably **suppliers** of copper to the Harappans |
| Meluhha is Bahrain | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DILMUN ≈ Bahrain · MAGAN ≈ Oman · MELUHHA ≈ the Harappan region.** Meluhha's products: **carnelian, lapis lazuli, copper, gold, varieties of wood** |
| Harappan influence on Mesopotamia is unproven | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Local *Dilmun* weights followed the HARAPPAN standard**, and a **Mesopotamian cylinder seal carries a humped-bull motif "derived from the Indus region"** |
| The Harappan script is alphabetic | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOT alphabetical — 375 to 400 signs**, far too many; **longest inscription about 26 signs**; **written RIGHT TO LEFT** |
| We know the writing direction from texts | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ From **cramping**: *"wider spacing on the right and cramping on the left, as if the engraver began from the right and RAN OUT OF SPACE"* |
| Harappan weights were marked with numerals | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Made of **CHERT**, **generally CUBICAL**, **with NO MARKINGS**. **Lower denominations BINARY (1,2,4,8,16,32); higher DECIMAL (160,200,320,640)** |
| The "priest-king" statue proves priestly rule | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The label came from **archaeologists' familiarity with MESOPOTAMIAN priest-kings**; Harappan ritual is **not well understood**, and there is **no means of knowing whether ritual performers held political power** |
| Archaeologists agree the Harappans had a single state | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE positions**: no rulers at all · several rulers, one per city · a single state. NCERT also remarks it is *"likely… some sort of democratic system"* |
| The civilisation ended because of an Aryan invasion | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Wheeler's "Indra stands accused" (1947) was refuted by GEORGE DALES (1964)** — the skeletons were **not contemporaneous**; **no destruction level, no burning, no armoured warriors, no final defence of the Citadel** |
| One cause explains the collapse | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Some of these 'causes' may hold for certain settlements, but they **DO NOT EXPLAIN THE COLLAPSE OF THE ENTIRE CIVILISATION**."* NCERT points to the end of *"a strong unifying element, perhaps the Harappan state"* |
| Cunningham never saw a Harappan object | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A Harappan seal was given to him and he SKETCHED it** — he could not place it, because he thought **Indian history began with the cities of the Ganga valley** and used **Chinese Buddhist pilgrims' accounts** as his guide |
| Marshall's excavation method was sound | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ He dug in **uniform horizontal units, IGNORING STRATIGRAPHY**, so context was **"irretrievably lost"**. **WHEELER (DG from 1944)** corrected it |
| Stratigraphy means the highest layers are oldest | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The LOWEST layers are the oldest**; **"sterile layers"** indicate abandonment |
| The proto-Shiva seal confirms Harappan Shiva worship | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Rigvedic RUDRA is "neither Pashupati… nor a yogi"**, so the depiction **does not match** the earliest text; NCERT asks whether it may be **a SHAMAN** |
| The conical stones are certainly lingas | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Mackay: *"it is just as possible that they were used in the BOARD-GAMES"*** |
| The Rakhigarhi DNA study was purely Indian | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Deccan College (Pune) + CCMB (Hyderabad) + Harvard Medical College**; Shinde et al., ***Cell*, 2019**; archaeogenetic work begun **2013** |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in ten lines

1. **Three phases** — Early (6000–2600 BCE), ⭐ **Mature/urban (2600–1900 BCE)**, Late (1900–1300 BCE); **over 2000 sites**, two-thirds in the **Saraswati basin**; **five major cities**, the largest being **Rakhigarhi**.
2. **Subsistence is reconstructed from charred grains and bones** by archaeo-botanists and archaeo-zoologists — **wheat and barley, not rice**; and the **ploughed field at Kalibangan** with furrows at right angles is the best direct evidence for agriculture.
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Mohenjodaro proves PLANNING three times over**: platforms built before buildings, **4:2:1 bricks from Jammu to Gujarat**, and **drains laid before houses** — plus **four million person-days** for the foundations alone.
4. **Water and sanitation reached the individual household** — a bathroom in every house, **~700 wells**, sumps that settled solids, and at **Lothal, mud-brick houses with burnt-brick drains**.
5. **Social difference is hard to read** because, **unlike Egypt, the Harappans did not bury wealth**; the workable test is **distribution**, and the "luxury" category itself breaks on a **faience spindle whorl**.
6. ⭐ **Craft was regionally specialised** — Chanhudaro made things and little else — and **production centres are identified by WASTE, not by products**.
7. **Materials were got two ways**: **settlements at the source** (Shortughai/lapis, Nageshwar/shell, Lothal) or **expeditions** (Khetri/copper, south India/gold). **Nickel traces tie Harappan copper to Oman**, and Mesopotamia knew the region as **Meluhha**.
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Seals, an undeciphered 375–400-sign script written right to left, and unmarked chert weights (binary below, decimal above)** are the machinery of trade between strangers — and **Dilmun adopted the Harappan weight standard**.
9. **Authority is visible in what it did and invisible in who it was.** Three positions exist; the **"priest-king" is a label borrowed from Mesopotamia**, not a finding.
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the chapter's real subject is method**: Cunningham held a seal and could not see it; Marshall lost context by ignoring stratigraphy; Wheeler blamed Indra and Dales refuted him; and the *"proto-Shiva"* fails because **Rigvedic Rudra is neither Pashupati nor a yogi**. **The frame decides what counts as evidence.**
