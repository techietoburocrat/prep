# Theme 5 — Through the Eyes of Travellers
## *Perceptions of Society (c. tenth to seventeenth century)*

> **The one-line point:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This chapter gives you three men looking at India from outside — and its real subject is HOW THE PLACE YOU COME FROM DECIDES WHAT YOU CAN SEE.** *Al-Biruni came from a scholarly Islamic world and read India through **Sanskrit texts**; Ibn Battuta came looking for **wonders** and found them; Bernier came from France determined to prove a **political point about property**. **Same subcontinent, three incompatible pictures.**

[Index](00_Index.md) | [Next: Theme 6 (Bhakti–Sufi Traditions) →](06_Theme6_Bhakti_Sufi_Traditions.md)

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT STATES THE CHAPTER'S VALUE IN ITS OPENING, AND IT IS A GENUINELY GOOD ARGUMENT FOR READING FOREIGNERS AT ALL:** *"As these authors came from **vastly different social and cultural environments**, they were often **MORE ATTENTIVE TO EVERYDAY ACTIVITIES AND PRACTICES WHICH WERE TAKEN FOR GRANTED BY INDIGENOUS WRITERS, FOR WHOM THESE WERE ROUTINE MATTERS, NOT WORTHY OF BEING RECORDED. IT IS THIS DIFFERENCE IN PERSPECTIVE THAT MAKES THE ACCOUNTS OF TRAVELLERS INTERESTING.**"*
>
> ⭐ **The outsider notices what the insider never thinks to write down.** *Ibn Battuta describes **a coconut** and **paan** at length precisely because no Indian would bother. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is the exact complement to [Part I Theme 2 §8's problem](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/02_Theme2_Kings_Farmers_and_Towns.md)** — *"routine agricultural practices and the joys and sorrows of daily existence find NO MENTION in inscriptions."* **Inscriptions miss the ordinary; travellers are fascinated by it.**

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And NCERT's second opening sentence is a silence worth naming:** *"**Unfortunately, WE HAVE PRACTICALLY NO ACCOUNTS OF TRAVEL LEFT BY WOMEN, THOUGH WE KNOW THAT THEY TRAVELLED.**"* ⭐ **Every account in this chapter is by a man**, and §7 shows what that costs — *"why do you think the lives of ordinary women workers did not attract the attention of travellers such as Ibn Battuta and Bernier?"*

### 🧭 The three travellers at a glance — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ learn this table; it answers Exercise 2 by itself

| | ⭐ **AL-BIRUNI** | ⭐ **IBN BATTUTA** | ⭐ **FRANÇOIS BERNIER** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dates** | **973–1048** | **1304–77** | **1620–88** |
| **From** | **Khwarizm, present-day UZBEKISTAN** | **Tangier, MOROCCO** *(north-west Africa)* | **FRANCE** |
| **In India** | from **1017 onwards** *(brought to Ghazni as a hostage)* | **from 1333** | **1656–1668, twelve years** |
| **How he came** | ⭐ **AS A HOSTAGE** — Sultan Mahmud took scholars and poets back to Ghazni | ⭐ **BY CHOICE** — lured by Muhammad bin Tughlaq's *"reputation as a generous patron"* | ⭐ *"in search of **OPPORTUNITIES**"* |
| **What he did here** | scholar; **learned Sanskrit** from Brahmana priests | ⭐ **QAZI (judge) of DELHI**, later of the **Maldives**; then **envoy to China** | ⭐ **physician to Prince DARA SHUKOH**; later with **DANISHMAND KHAN**, an Armenian noble |
| **The book** | ⭐ ***KITAB-UL-HIND*, in ARABIC** | ⭐ ***RIHLA*, in ARABIC** | ⭐ ***TRAVELS IN THE MUGHAL EMPIRE*** |
| **Written for** | *"peoples living along **the frontiers of the subcontinent**"* | ⭐ **dictated on the local ruler's orders** — for *"entertainment to the mind"* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **dedicated to LOUIS XIV**; aimed at **policy-makers and the intelligentsia** |
| **Method** | ⭐ **question → Sanskritic description → COMPARISON with other cultures** | ⭐ **describe the UNFAMILIAR by comparison with the familiar** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BINARY OPPOSITION — India as the inverse of Europe, and RANKED BELOW it** |
| **Governing attitude** | ⭐ **careful, self-critical, comparative** | ⭐ **delighted** | ⭐ **critical, and looking for a warning to send home** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE IS THE AUDIENCE, AND IT EXPLAINS EVERYTHING ELSE.** ⭐ *Al-Biruni wrote for **scholars who might argue with Hindus** — hence precision. Ibn Battuta **dictated on a ruler's instruction** for material that would give *"entertainment to the mind and delight to the ears and eyes"* — hence wonders. **Bernier DEDICATED HIS BOOK TO THE KING OF FRANCE and wrote his other work as letters to ministers — hence a political argument.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Ask "who was this written for?" and each account's distortions become predictable.**

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND ONE STRUCTURAL FACT THAT MATTERS ENORMOUSLY, GIVEN IN §3.1 — CIRCULATION.** *Bernier's works were *"published in France in **1670-71** and translated into **English, Dutch, German and Italian within the next five years**. Between 1670 and 1725 his account was **reprinted EIGHT TIMES in French**, and by 1684 **three times in English**."* ⭐ *"**This was in marked contrast to the accounts in ARABIC AND PERSIAN, WHICH CIRCULATED AS MANUSCRIPTS AND WERE GENERALLY NOT PUBLISHED BEFORE 1800.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PRINT IS WHY BERNIER'S PICTURE WON.** *Al-Biruni was careful and comparative; **few people in India read him before 1500.** Bernier was tendentious; **he was reprinted eleven times in fifty years and translated into four languages.** **The account that shaped Europe's idea of India was the one with a printing press behind it, not the one with the best method.** *(⭐ NCERT makes the point directly in its box on *"the creation and circulation of ideas about India"*: European travellers' writings *"**helped PRODUCE AN IMAGE OF INDIA for Europeans through the PRINTING AND CIRCULATION of their books**"* — **and that later Indians in Europe, like Shaikh Itisamuddin and Mirza Abu Talib, "confronted this image… and tried to influence it by producing their own version of matters."**)*

## 1️⃣ Al-Biruni and the *Kitab-ul-Hind* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 1.1 From Khwarizm to the Punjab

```
   ** BORN 973, in KHWARIZM, present-day UZBEKISTAN --
      "an important CENTRE OF LEARNING" **

   ⭐ ** HIS LANGUAGES: "well versed in several languages:
      SYRIAC, ARABIC, PERSIAN, HEBREW and SANSKRIT." **
   ⭐ ** "ALTHOUGH HE DID NOT KNOW GREEK, he was familiar with
      the works of PLATO and other Greek philosophers, HAVING
      READ THEM IN ARABIC TRANSLATIONS." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** HOW HE CAME TO INDIA -- NOT as a traveller **
   ================================================================
   ** "In 1017, when SULTAN MAHMUD INVADED KHWARIZM, HE TOOK
      SEVERAL SCHOLARS AND POETS BACK TO HIS CAPITAL, GHAZNI;
      AL-BIRUNI WAS ONE OF THEM." **
   ⭐ ** "HE ARRIVED IN GHAZNI AS A HOSTAGE, but GRADUALLY
      DEVELOPED A LIKING FOR THE CITY, where he spent the rest
      of his life until his death at the age of 70." **

   ** WHY HIS INTEREST WAS NOT UNUSUAL: "SANSKRIT WORKS ON
      ASTRONOMY, MATHEMATICS AND MEDICINE HAD BEEN TRANSLATED
      INTO ARABIC FROM THE EIGHTH CENTURY ONWARDS." **
   ** "When the PUNJAB became a part of the GHAZNAVID EMPIRE,
      contacts with the local population helped create AN
      ENVIRONMENT OF MUTUAL TRUST AND UNDERSTANDING." **
   ⭐ ** "Al-Biruni spent YEARS IN THE COMPANY OF BRAHMANA
      PRIESTS AND SCHOLARS, LEARNING SANSKRIT, and studying
      religious and philosophical texts." **

   ⚠️ ** "While HIS ITINERARY IS NOT CLEAR, it is LIKELY that
      he travelled widely in the Punjab and parts of northern
      India." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"HE ARRIVED IN GHAZNI AS A HOSTAGE" IS THE FACT THAT MOST STUDENTS MISS, AND IT REFRAMES HIM ENTIRELY.** ⭐ **Al-Biruni did not choose to come.** *He was **carried off by an invader** as part of the plunder — **scholars and poets taken along with the treasure** — and the man who wrote the most sympathetic and careful account of India in the medieval world **arrived in that world as a captive of Mahmud of Ghazni.** *(⭐ **And note the honest caveat: *"his itinerary is NOT CLEAR."*** **We do not actually know where in India he went.** **He is the most cited of the three and the least documented as a traveller.**)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TRANSLATION BOX IS THE BEST SMALL DETAIL IN §1 — and it is a two-way street.** *"Al-Biruni's expertise in several languages allowed him to **compare languages and translate texts**. ⭐ **He translated several SANSKRIT works, including PATANJALI'S WORK ON GRAMMAR, into ARABIC. FOR HIS BRAHMANA FRIENDS, HE TRANSLATED THE WORKS OF EUCLID (a Greek mathematician) INTO SANSKRIT.**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He carried Panini's tradition out and Euclid in.** *(⭐ **This is [Class 11 Supplement Part B](../../Class_11_Themes_in_World_History/Notes/08_Supplement_Industrial_Revolution_Islamic_Lands_and_Confrontation.md)'s *Bayt al-Hikma* world in one man** — the Abbasid translation movement, in which Greek, Syriac and Indian works crossed into Arabic. **And note the phrase "for his BRAHMANA FRIENDS."**)*

### 1.2 The *Kitab-ul-Hind* — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Exercise 1 in one box

```
   ** LANGUAGE: ARABIC. "simple and lucid." **
   ** SIZE: "A VOLUMINOUS TEXT, DIVIDED INTO 80 CHAPTERS." **

   ⭐ ** THE SUBJECTS -- learn the list: **
      ** RELIGION AND PHILOSOPHY · FESTIVALS · ASTRONOMY ·
         ALCHEMY · MANNERS AND CUSTOMS · SOCIAL LIFE ·
         WEIGHTS AND MEASURES · ICONOGRAPHY · LAWS ·
         METROLOGY **
      (⭐ METROLOGY = "the science of measurement")

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE STRUCTURE OF EACH CHAPTER -- three steps,
      and this is the most examinable thing in §1 **
   ================================================================
   ** "Generally (though not always), Al-Biruni adopted A
      DISTINCTIVE STRUCTURE IN EACH CHAPTER: " **
      1. ** BEGINNING WITH A QUESTION **
      2. ** FOLLOWING THIS UP WITH A DESCRIPTION BASED ON
         SANSKRITIC TRADITIONS **
      3. ⭐ ** AND CONCLUDING WITH A COMPARISON WITH OTHER
         CULTURES **

   ⭐ ** "Some present-day scholars have argued that this
      ALMOST GEOMETRIC STRUCTURE, REMARKABLE FOR ITS PRECISION
      AND PREDICTABILITY, OWED MUCH TO HIS MATHEMATICAL
      ORIENTATION." **

   ** THE AUDIENCE: "he PROBABLY INTENDED HIS WORK FOR PEOPLES
      LIVING ALONG THE FRONTIERS OF THE SUBCONTINENT." **
   ⭐ ** AND HE WAS IMPROVING ON PREDECESSORS: "he was also
      CRITICAL about the ways in which these texts were
      written, and CLEARLY WANTED TO IMPROVE ON THEM." **
```

**📖 SOURCE 1 — *Al-Biruni's objectives*, in his own words ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *He described his work as: ⭐ **"a HELP TO THOSE WHO WANT TO DISCUSS RELIGIOUS QUESTIONS WITH THEM (the Hindus), and as A REPERTORY OF INFORMATION TO THOSE WHO WANT TO ASSOCIATE WITH THEM."**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BOTH STATED PURPOSES ARE PRACTICAL, NOT ANTIQUARIAN, AND THAT IS THE POINT.** ⭐ **He is writing a HANDBOOK — for people who will DEBATE with Hindus, and for people who will LIVE AND WORK among them.** *That explains the **80 chapters covering everything from festivals to weights and measures**, and it explains the **precision**: a manual that is wrong is worse than useless. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It also explains the CONCLUDING COMPARISON in every chapter — his reader needs to know not just what Hindus believe but how it maps onto what he already knows.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The three-step structure is worth admiring as METHOD, and it is the answer to why al-Biruni is the most reliable of the three.** ⭐ *He **poses a question** (so his subject is explicit), **answers from the tradition's own texts** (so he is not guessing), and **then compares** (so his reader can locate it). *(⭐ **And §4 shows him doing something rarer still — identifying and NAMING the obstacles to his own understanding before he begins.** **No other traveller in this chapter does that.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "HINDU" BOX — a near-certain Prelims item, and an important corrective:** *"The term **'HINDU' was derived from AN OLD PERSIAN WORD, used c. SIXTH–FIFTH CENTURIES BCE, to refer to THE REGION EAST OF THE RIVER SINDHU (INDUS).** ⭐ **The Arabs continued the Persian usage and called this region 'AL-HIND' and its people 'HINDI'.** Later **the Turks referred to the people east of the Indus as 'HINDU', their land as 'HINDUSTAN', and their language as 'HINDAVI'.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NONE OF THESE EXPRESSIONS INDICATED THE RELIGIOUS IDENTITY OF THE PEOPLE. IT WAS MUCH LATER THAT THE TERM DEVELOPED RELIGIOUS CONNOTATIONS.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The word began as a GEOGRAPHICAL term — "the people who live east of the Indus" — and acquired a religious meaning only much later.** *That is a **datable, sourced, checkable fact**, and it is exactly the kind of thing an examiner rewards you for knowing precisely.*

## 2️⃣ Ibn Battuta's *Rihla* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 2.1 An early globe-trotter

```
   ** THE BOOK: "Ibn Battuta's book of travels, called RIHLA,
      WRITTEN IN ARABIC, provides EXTREMELY RICH AND
      INTERESTING DETAILS about the social and cultural life in
      the subcontinent in the FOURTEENTH CENTURY." **

   ** THE MAN: "born in TANGIER into ONE OF THE MOST
      RESPECTABLE AND EDUCATED FAMILIES known for their
      expertise in ISLAMIC RELIGIOUS LAW or SHARI`A." **
   ** "True to the tradition of his family, Ibn Battuta received
      LITERARY AND SCHOLASTIC EDUCATION when he was quite
      young." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE SENTENCE THAT DEFINES HIM: **
   ** "UNLIKE MOST OTHER MEMBERS OF HIS CLASS, IBN BATTUTA
      CONSIDERED EXPERIENCE GAINED THROUGH TRAVELS TO BE A MORE
      IMPORTANT SOURCE OF KNOWLEDGE THAN BOOKS." **
   ** "HE JUST LOVED TRAVELLING, and went to far-off places,
      EXPLORING NEW WORLDS AND PEOPLES." **

   ================================================================
   ** THE ITINERARY -- learn the shape, not every stop **
   ================================================================
   ** BEFORE INDIA (before 1332-33): pilgrimage trips to MECCA;
      travelled extensively in SYRIA, IRAQ, PERSIA, YEMEN,
      OMAN and TRADING PORTS ON THE COAST OF EAST AFRICA. **

   ** "Travelling OVERLAND THROUGH CENTRAL ASIA, Ibn Battuta
      REACHED SIND IN 1333." **
   ⭐ ** WHY DELHI: "he had heard about MUHAMMAD BIN TUGHLAQ,
      the Sultan of Delhi, and LURED BY HIS REPUTATION AS A
      GENEROUS PATRON OF ARTS AND LETTERS, set off for Delhi,
      passing through MULTAN and UCH." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** HIS CAREER IN INDIA -- a full arc: **
   ** "The Sultan was IMPRESSED BY HIS SCHOLARSHIP, and
      APPOINTED HIM THE QAZI OR JUDGE OF DELHI. He remained in
      that position for several years, UNTIL HE FELL OUT OF
      FAVOUR AND WAS THROWN INTO PRISON. Once the
      misunderstanding… was cleared, he was RESTORED TO
      IMPERIAL SERVICE, and was ORDERED IN 1342 TO PROCEED TO
      CHINA AS THE SULTAN'S ENVOY TO THE MONGOL RULER." **

   ** THEN: MALABAR COAST (through central India) -> THE
      MALDIVES ("stayed EIGHTEEN MONTHS as the QAZI") ->
      SRI LANKA -> back to MALABAR and the MALDIVES ->
      BENGAL and ASSAM -> by ship to SUMATRA -> another ship
      to the Chinese port of ZAYTUN (now QUANZHOU) ->
      travelled in China ** "going as far as BEIJING" ** ->
      ⭐ ** "deciding to RETURN HOME IN 1347." **

   ⭐ ** "His account is often COMPARED WITH THAT OF MARCO POLO,
      who visited China (and also India) from his home base in
      VENICE in the LATE THIRTEENTH CENTURY." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE KEY TO IBN BATTUTA IS ONE SENTENCE: HE VALUED *EXPERIENCE* OVER *BOOKS* — WHICH IS THE EXACT OPPOSITE OF AL-BIRUNI.** ⭐ **Al-Biruni learned Sanskrit and read the Vedas, the Puranas, the *Bhagavad Gita*, Patanjali and the *Manusmriti* (§4.1).** *Ibn Battuta **went and looked.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *That single difference in epistemology explains everything downstream: **al-Biruni gives you the NORMATIVE system (and inherits its distortions); Ibn Battuta gives you the STREET (and misses the structure).** **Neither is better; they fail in opposite directions** — and saying so is the core of Exercise 2.
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The distances are worth keeping, because they make the fourteenth century concrete:** ⭐ **Multan → Delhi: FORTY days. Sind → Delhi: about FIFTY days. Daulatabad → Delhi: FORTY days. Gwalior → Delhi: TEN days.** *(⭐ **Hold on to "fifty days from Sind to Delhi" — §5.3's postal system did it in FIVE.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 2 — *The bird leaves its nest* ⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"My departure from **TANGIER, my birthplace**, took place on Thursday … **I set out ALONE, having neither fellow-traveller… nor caravan whose party I might join, but SWAYED BY AN OVERMASTERING IMPULSE WITHIN ME and a desire long-cherished in my bosom to visit these illustrious sanctuaries.** ⭐ **So I braced my resolution to QUIT ALL MY DEAR ONES, FEMALE AND MALE, AND FORSOOK MY HOME AS BIRDS FORSAKE THEIR NESTS… MY AGE AT THAT TIME WAS TWENTY-TWO YEARS.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And NCERT's flat closing note:** *"**Ibn Battuta returned home in 1354, ABOUT 30 YEARS AFTER HE HAD SET OUT.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"AS BIRDS FORSAKE THEIR NESTS" IS A LOVELY IMAGE AND A REVEALING ONE — a bird leaving the nest does not come back.** ⭐ *He left **at twenty-two, alone, without a caravan**, and returned **thirty years later.** *(⭐ **And note the danger this understates.** Travel was *"much more ARDUOUS AND HAZARDOUS"*: *"Ibn Battuta was **attacked by bands of robbers several times**"*, and while travelling from Multan to Delhi *"**his caravan was attacked and MANY OF HIS FELLOW TRAVELLERS LOST THEIR LIVES**; those who survived, including Ibn Battuta, were **severely wounded**."*)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "LONELY TRAVELLER" BOX IS THE MOST HUMAN PASSAGE IN THE CHAPTER — quote it if a question allows.** *"**I was attacked by the FEVER, and I actually TIED MYSELF ON THE SADDLE WITH A TURBAN-CLOTH IN CASE I SHOULD FALL OFF by reason of my weakness…** So at last we reached **TUNIS**, and the townsfolk came out to welcome the shaikh… **On all sides they came forward with greetings and questions to one another, BUT NOT A SOUL SAID A WORD OF GREETING TO ME, since there was none of them I knew. ⭐ I FELT SO SAD AT HEART ON ACCOUNT OF MY LONELINESS THAT I COULD NOT RESTRAIN THE TEARS THAT STARTED TO MY EYES, AND WEPT BITTERLY. But one of the pilgrims, REALISING THE CAUSE OF MY DISTRESS, CAME UP TO ME WITH A GREETING…**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A man tying himself to his saddle so fever will not throw him off, and weeping in a crowded street because nobody knows him. Travel accounts are usually about places; this is about what travelling COSTS.**

### 2.2 The "enjoyment of curiosities" ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"When he returned, **THE LOCAL RULER ISSUED INSTRUCTIONS THAT HIS STORIES BE RECORDED.**"*

**📖 SOURCE 3 — *Education and entertainment* (IBN JUZAYY, who wrote what Ibn Battuta dictated) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"**A gracious direction was transmitted (by the ruler) that he should DICTATE AN ACCOUNT OF THE CITIES WHICH HE HAD SEEN in his travel, and of THE INTERESTING EVENTS WHICH HAD CLUNG TO HIS MEMORY, and that he should speak of THOSE WHOM HE HAD MET OF THE RULERS OF COUNTRIES, OF THEIR DISTINGUISHED MEN OF LEARNING, AND THEIR PIOUS SAINTS.***
>
> *⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Accordingly, he dictated upon these subjects **a narrative WHICH GAVE ENTERTAINMENT TO THE MIND AND DELIGHT TO THE EARS AND EYES, WITH A VARIETY OF CURIOUS PARTICULARS by the exposition of which HE GAVE EDIFICATION and OF MARVELLOUS THINGS, by referring to which HE AROUSED INTEREST.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS SOURCE IS THE KEY TO READING THE *RIHLA*, AND IT SHOULD BE IN YOUR EXERCISE 2 ANSWER.** ⭐ **The book was DICTATED, on a ruler's ORDER, by a SCRIBE (Ibn Juzayy), and the stated purpose was *"entertainment to the mind and delight to the ears and eyes."*** *So when Ibn Battuta lingers on **a coconut, paan, a market of singers, a foot-post relay** — **he is doing exactly what he was asked to do: produce *"curious particulars"* and *"marvellous things."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **His delight is not naïveté; it is a COMMISSION.** *And note the honest consequence: **the strange gets recorded and the ordinary does not**, which is the mirror-image of the bias in official records.
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note there are TWO layers of authorship: Ibn Battuta remembered and spoke; IBN JUZAYY wrote.** *We are reading **a scribe's rendering of a thirty-year memory, produced for a court.** *(⭐ Compare **[Part I Theme 4's Buddhist texts](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md)** — taught orally, compiled after death, written later. **Dictation is a source-type with its own distortions.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"IN THE FOOTSTEPS OF IBN BATTUTA" — the box worth knowing, because it shows the tradition continuing and NOT all in one voice:** *"In the centuries between **1400 and 1800** visitors to India wrote a number of **travelogues in PERSIAN.** At the same time, **INDIAN VISITORS to Central Asia, Iran and the Ottoman empire also sometimes wrote about their experiences.**"*
> - ⭐ **ABDUR RAZZAQ SAMARQANDI** — *visited south India in the **1440s*** *(and see [Theme 7](07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) — he gives one of the most important descriptions of **Vijayanagara**)*
> - ⭐ **MAHMUD WALI BALKHI** — *travelled widely in the **1620s**; **"even became a sort of SANYASI for a time"***
> - ⭐ **SHAIKH ALI HAZIN** — *came to north India in the **1740s**, and was **"DISAPPOINTED AND EVEN DISGUSTED with India, where they expected to receive a red carpet treatment"***
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's summary: *"MOST OF THEM SAW INDIA AS A LAND OF WONDERS."*** *(⭐ **But not all — Hazin is the reminder that even within one tradition of travel writing, the verdicts differ, and that a traveller's DISAPPOINTMENT may say more about his expectations than about the place.**)*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BERNIER'S POSITION IS THE THING TO NOTICE: HE WAS INSIDE THE COURT, NOT OUTSIDE IT.** ⭐ **Physician to the heir apparent, then attached to a Mughal noble, for twelve years.** *He had **better access than either of the others** — and produced **the least reliable account.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Proximity is not objectivity.** *(⭐ **And Tavernier is worth remembering as the contrast: a jeweller, interested in trade, who compared India to Iran and the Ottoman empire — i.e. to OTHER ASIAN STATES rather than to Europe.** **Bernier's binary was a choice, not a necessity.** *Note also **Fig. 5.6 shows Bernier in EUROPEAN clothes and Fig. 5.7 shows Tavernier in INDIAN clothes** — NCERT has placed them facing each other.)*

### 3.1 Comparing "East" and "West" ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** "He DEDICATED HIS MAJOR WRITING TO LOUIS XIV, THE KING OF
      FRANCE, and many of his other works were WRITTEN IN THE
      FORM OF LETTERS TO INFLUENTIAL OFFICIALS AND
      MINISTERS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE VERDICT, and NCERT's correction in the same
      breath: **
   ** "IN VIRTUALLY EVERY INSTANCE BERNIER DESCRIBED WHAT HE
      SAW IN INDIA AS A BLEAK SITUATION IN COMPARISON TO
      DEVELOPMENTS IN EUROPE. ⭐ AS WE WILL SEE, THIS ASSESSMENT
      WAS NOT ALWAYS ACCURATE. HOWEVER, WHEN HIS WORKS WERE
      PUBLISHED, BERNIER'S WRITINGS BECAME EXTREMELY
      POPULAR." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** HIS METHOD, named precisely by NCERT (§6): **
   ================================================================
   ** "His representation of India works on THE MODEL OF BINARY
      OPPOSITION, WHERE INDIA IS PRESENTED AS THE INVERSE OF
      EUROPE." **
   ** "He also ORDERED THE PERCEIVED DIFFERENCES
      HIERARCHICALLY, SO THAT INDIA APPEARED TO BE INFERIOR TO
      THE WESTERN WORLD." **
   ⭐ ** "His account contains discussions trying to PLACE THE
      HISTORY OF THE MUGHALS WITHIN SOME SORT OF A UNIVERSAL
      FRAMEWORK." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"BINARY OPPOSITION" PLUS "ORDERED HIERARCHICALLY" IS THE TWO-STEP THAT MAKES BERNIER DANGEROUS, AND NAMING BOTH STEPS IS WORTH REAL MARKS.** ⭐ **Step one: India is defined as the OPPOSITE of Europe — whatever Europe has, India lacks.** *Step two: **the opposition is RANKED**, so difference becomes inferiority.* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Once you set the model up that way, no evidence can disturb it — anything Indian is either absent-in-Europe (therefore backward) or present-in-Europe-but-missing (therefore lacking).** *(⭐ **This is [Part I Theme 4 §11.1's Greek yardstick](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md) with a political purpose attached** — *"devising yardsticks derived from the familiar to make sense of the unfamiliar."* **Bernier's yardstick was France, and he was not merely measuring; he was arguing.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 4 — *Travelling with the Mughal army* ⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"I am expected to keep **TWO GOOD TURKOMAN HORSES**, and I also take with me **a powerful PERSIAN CAMEL and driver, a GROOM for my horses, a COOK and a SERVANT to go before my horse with a flask of water in his hand, ACCORDING TO THE CUSTOM OF THE COUNTRY.** I am also provided with… **a TENT of moderate size, a CARPET, a PORTABLE BED made of four very strong but light canes, a PILLOW, a MATTRESS, round LEATHER TABLE-CLOTHS…, some few NAPKINS of dyed cloth, three small bags with CULINARY UTENSILS**… ⭐ I have taken care to lay in **a stock of EXCELLENT RICE for five or six days' consumption, of SWEET BISCUITS flavoured with ANISE, of LIMES and SUGAR. Nor have I forgotten A LINEN BAG WITH ITS SMALL IRON HOOK FOR THE PURPOSE OF SUSPENDING AND DRAINING DAHI OR CURDS; NOTHING BEING CONSIDERED SO REFRESHING IN THIS COUNTRY AS LEMONADE AND DAHI.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This source undercuts Bernier's own thesis, which is why it is worth noticing.** ⭐ **The man who described India as a land of *"beggars and barbarians"* travelled with two Turkoman horses, a Persian camel, a groom, a cook, a servant, a tent, a bed, a mattress and a bag for draining curd.** *He was **comfortable, well-supplied and well-served**, and he **adopted local practice** — the water-carrier *"according to the custom of the country"*, and **lemonade and *dahi*** because *"nothing [is] considered so refreshing in this country."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A traveller's DESCRIPTION of misery and his own EXPERIENCE of the place can diverge sharply, and Source 4 is the evidence.**

## 4️⃣ Making Sense of an Alien World — Al-Biruni and the Sanskritic Tradition ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 4.1 Overcoming barriers to understanding ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AL-BIRUNI'S THREE BARRIERS -- memorise all three,
      and note that he identified them HIMSELF **
   ================================================================
   ** "Al-Biruni… WAS AWARE OF THE PROBLEMS INHERENT IN THE
      TASK HE HAD SET HIMSELF. He discussed several 'BARRIERS'
      that he felt OBSTRUCTED UNDERSTANDING." **

   ** BARRIER 1 -- LANGUAGE **
   ** "According to him, SANSKRIT WAS SO DIFFERENT FROM ARABIC
      AND PERSIAN THAT IDEAS AND CONCEPTS COULD NOT BE EASILY
      TRANSLATED FROM ONE LANGUAGE INTO ANOTHER." **

   ** BARRIER 2 -- RELIGION **
   ** "the DIFFERENCE IN RELIGIOUS BELIEFS AND PRACTICES." **

   ** BARRIER 3 -- INSULARITY **
   ** "THE SELF-ABSORPTION AND CONSEQUENT INSULARITY OF THE
      LOCAL POPULATION, according to him, constituted the third
      barrier." **

   ================================================================
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND NCERT'S CRITICISM -- do not omit this **
   ================================================================
   ** "What is interesting is that EVEN THOUGH HE WAS AWARE OF
      THESE PROBLEMS, AL-BIRUNI DEPENDED ALMOST EXCLUSIVELY ON
      THE WORKS OF BRAHMANAS, often citing passages from
      THE VEDAS, THE PURANAS, THE BHAGAVAD GITA, THE WORKS OF
      PATANJALI, THE MANUSMRITI, etc., to provide an
      understanding of Indian society." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE SHARPEST CRITICISM IN THE CHAPTER AND IT IS AIMED AT THE MOST CAREFUL TRAVELLER — which is what makes it worth making.** ⭐ **Al-Biruni identified three barriers to understanding, and then read India almost entirely through ONE social group's texts.** *He was **aware of the problem in principle and did not escape it in practice.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ And note **which** texts — the **Vedas, Puranas, Bhagavad Gita, Patanjali, Manusmriti**. **[Part I Theme 3](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/03_Theme3_Kinship_Caste_and_Class.md) spent an entire chapter showing that these are PRESCRIPTIVE texts whose norms were "by no means all-pervasive."** **Al-Biruni read the rulebook and reported it as the game.**)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The "language with an enormous range" box shows him wrestling honestly with Barrier 1 — and it is charming:** *"**If you want to conquer this difficulty (i.e. to learn Sanskrit), YOU WILL NOT FIND IT EASY, because THE LANGUAGE IS OF AN ENORMOUS RANGE, BOTH IN WORDS AND INFLECTIONS**… **CALLING ONE AND THE SAME THING BY VARIOUS NAMES**, both original and derivative, and **USING ONE AND THE SAME WORD FOR A VARIETY OF SUBJECTS**, which, in order to be properly understood, **must be distinguished from each other by various qualifying epithets. ⭐ GOD KNOWS BEST!**"*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note his scepticism elsewhere, which shows he did NOT swallow everything he was told:** *faced with the story of a wooden idol *"that supposedly lasted for **216,432 years**"*, he asks: *"**HOW, THEN, COULD WOOD HAVE LASTED SUCH A LENGTH OF TIME, AND PARTICULARLY IN A PLACE WHERE THE AIR AND THE SOIL ARE RATHER WET? GOD KNOWS BEST!**"* **He applied a physical test to a religious claim and reported that it failed. That is a scholar, not a credulous visitor.**

### 4.2 Al-Biruni's description of the caste system — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Exercise 6

```
   ================================================================
   ** MOVE 1 -- HE LOOKED FOR PARALLELS ELSEWHERE **
   ================================================================
   ** "He noted that IN ANCIENT PERSIA, FOUR SOCIAL CATEGORIES
      WERE RECOGNISED: " **
      ** KNIGHTS AND PRINCES **
      ** MONKS, FIRE-PRIESTS AND LAWYERS **
      ** PHYSICIANS, ASTRONOMERS AND OTHER SCIENTISTS **
      ** PEASANTS AND ARTISANS **
   ⭐ ** "In other words, HE ATTEMPTED TO SUGGEST THAT SOCIAL
      DIVISIONS WERE NOT UNIQUE TO INDIA." **

   ================================================================
   ** MOVE 2 -- BUT HE NOTED A DIFFERENCE **
   ================================================================
   ** "At the same time HE POINTED OUT THAT WITHIN ISLAM ALL
      MEN WERE CONSIDERED EQUAL, DIFFERING ONLY IN THEIR
      OBSERVANCE OF PIETY." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** MOVE 3 -- HE ACCEPTED THE STRUCTURE BUT REJECTED
      POLLUTION, AND HIS ARGUMENT IS SCIENTIFIC **
   ================================================================
   ** "In spite of his acceptance of the Brahmanical description
      of the caste system, AL-BIRUNI DISAPPROVED OF THE NOTION
      OF POLLUTION." **
   ** "He remarked that EVERYTHING WHICH FALLS INTO A STATE OF
      IMPURITY STRIVES AND SUCCEEDS IN REGAINING ITS ORIGINAL
      CONDITION OF PURITY. ⭐ THE SUN CLEANSES THE AIR, AND THE
      SALT IN THE SEA PREVENTS THE WATER FROM BECOMING POLLUTED.
      IF IT WERE NOT SO, INSISTED AL-BIRUNI, LIFE ON EARTH WOULD
      HAVE BEEN IMPOSSIBLE." **
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "THE CONCEPTION OF SOCIAL POLLUTION, INTRINSIC TO
      THE CASTE SYSTEM, WAS ACCORDING TO HIM, CONTRARY TO THE
      LAWS OF NATURE." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"CONTRARY TO THE LAWS OF NATURE" IS THE LINE TO QUOTE IN EXERCISE 6, AND THE ARGUMENT BEHIND IT IS GENUINELY ELEGANT.** ⭐ **He does not appeal to scripture or to Islamic doctrine. He appeals to OBSERVATION: nature is SELF-PURIFYING.** *The sun cleans the air; salt keeps the sea from spoiling; **impurity everywhere in nature is temporary and self-correcting.** **A doctrine of PERMANENT, HEREDITARY pollution therefore contradicts how the world visibly works.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A scientist's refutation of a social doctrine, in the eleventh century.** *(⭐ **And it is exactly the shape of [Punna's argument in Part I Theme 4](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md)** — *"in that case all the frogs and turtles would go to heaven"*. **Test a ritual claim against an obvious natural consequence.** **Two critics, four centuries apart, same method.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 5 — *The system of varnas*, in al-Biruni's words ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"**The highest caste are the BRAHMANA, of whom the books of the Hindus tell us that THEY WERE CREATED FROM THE HEAD OF BRAHMAN.** And as the Brahman is only another name for **the force called nature**, and the head is the highest part of the… body, **the Brahmana are the choice part of the whole genus**…*
>
> *The next caste are the **KSHATRIYA, who were created… FROM THE SHOULDERS AND HANDS of Brahman. Their degree is NOT MUCH BELOW that of the Brahmana.** After them follow the **VAISHYA, who were created FROM THE THIGH.** The **SHUDRA, who were created FROM HIS FEET**…*
>
> *⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Between the latter two classes THERE IS NO VERY GREAT DISTANCE. MUCH, HOWEVER, AS THESE CLASSES DIFFER FROM EACH OTHER, THEY LIVE TOGETHER IN THE SAME TOWNS AND VILLAGES, MIXED TOGETHER IN THE SAME HOUSES AND LODGINGS.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"Compare what Al-Biruni wrote with Source 6, Chapter 3. Do you notice SIMILARITIES AND DIFFERENCES? Do you think Al-Biruni depended ONLY on Sanskrit texts for his information and understanding?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ANSWER TURNS ON THE LAST SENTENCE, AND IT IS THE BEST THING IN §4.**
> - ⭐ **SIMILARITY:** *the **Purusha sukta scheme** — head, shoulders and hands, thigh, feet — **is exactly [Part I Theme 3's Source 13](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/03_Theme3_Kinship_Caste_and_Class.md)**, where the Brahmanas claim to be *"sons of Brahma, BORN OF HIS MOUTH."* **He is reproducing the normative text faithfully.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DIFFERENCE — and this is the crucial one:** *"**they LIVE TOGETHER in the same towns and villages, MIXED TOGETHER IN THE SAME HOUSES AND LODGINGS**"* — **that is NOT in the texts. That is OBSERVATION.** *And it flatly contradicts what the *Manusmriti* prescribes for *chandalas*, who *"had to live OUTSIDE the village."*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SO THE ANSWER TO THE SECOND QUESTION IS NO — he did not depend only on Sanskrit texts.** *He also **rationalises** *(Brahman = *"the force called nature"*)* and **observes** *(people living mixed together)*. **Where he observes, he contradicts his texts.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NCERT'S OWN CORRECTION COMPLETES EXERCISE 6 — do not stop at praising him:** *"Al-Biruni's description… was **DEEPLY INFLUENCED BY HIS STUDY OF NORMATIVE SANSKRIT TEXTS which laid down the rules… FROM THE POINT OF VIEW OF THE BRAHMANAS. HOWEVER, IN REAL LIFE THE SYSTEM WAS NOT QUITE AS RIGID.**"* ⭐ **The worked example NCERT gives is superb:** *"the categories defined as ***ANTYAJA* (literally, BORN OUTSIDE THE SYSTEM) were often expected to PROVIDE INEXPENSIVE LABOUR to both peasants and zamindars… ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In other words, WHILE THEY WERE OFTEN SUBJECTED TO SOCIAL OPPRESSION, THEY WERE INCLUDED WITHIN ECONOMIC NETWORKS.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT SENTENCE IS THE WHOLE CORRECTION AND IT IS EXAM-GOLD: SOCIALLY EXCLUDED, ECONOMICALLY INDISPENSABLE.** *A normative text can describe someone as outside the system; **the economy cannot afford to leave them out.** **Al-Biruni, reading only the texts, could see the exclusion and not the inclusion.**

## 5️⃣ Ibn Battuta and the Excitement of the Unfamiliar ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's framing of the fourteenth-century world is worth quoting, because it corrects the idea of a closed medieval India:** *"By the time Ibn Battuta arrived in Delhi… **THE SUBCONTINENT WAS PART OF A GLOBAL NETWORK OF COMMUNICATION THAT STRETCHED FROM CHINA IN THE EAST TO NORTH-WEST AFRICA AND EUROPE IN THE WEST.**"*
>
> ⭐ *He travelled through these lands *"visiting **sacred shrines**, spending time with **learned men and rulers**, often **officiating as qazi**, and **enjoying the COSMOPOLITAN CULTURE of urban centres where people who spoke ARABIC, PERSIAN, TURKISH and other languages, SHARED IDEAS, INFORMATION AND ANECDOTES.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the working rule of the whole genre, stated outright:** *"**ANYTHING THAT WAS UNFAMILIAR WAS PARTICULARLY HIGHLIGHTED IN ORDER TO ENSURE THAT THE LISTENER OR THE READER WAS SUITABLY IMPRESSED BY ACCOUNTS OF DISTANT YET ACCESSIBLE WORLDS.**"*

### 5.1 The coconut and the paan ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> *"Some of the best examples of Ibn Battuta's **STRATEGIES OF REPRESENTATION** are evident in the ways in which he described **THE COCONUT and THE PAAN, two kinds of plant produce that were COMPLETELY UNFAMILIAR TO HIS AUDIENCE.**"*

**📖 SOURCE 6 — *Nuts like a man's head*: the coconut**

> *"These trees are among the **MOST PECULIAR TREES IN KIND AND MOST ASTONISHING IN HABIT. THEY LOOK EXACTLY LIKE DATE-PALMS**, without any difference between them except that the one produces nuts as its fruits and the other produces dates. ⭐ **THE NUT OF A COCONUT TREE RESEMBLES A MAN'S HEAD, for in it are WHAT LOOK LIKE TWO EYES AND A MOUTH, and THE INSIDE OF IT WHEN IT IS GREEN LOOKS LIKE THE BRAIN, and attached to it is A FIBRE WHICH LOOKS LIKE HAIR.** ⭐ They make from this **CORDS WITH WHICH THEY SEW UP SHIPS INSTEAD OF (USING) IRON NAILS**, and they also make from it **CABLES FOR VESSELS.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's questions: *"What are the COMPARISONS that Ibn Battuta makes to give his readers an idea about what coconuts looked like? Do you think these are APPROPRIATE? How does he convey a sense that this fruit is UNUSUAL? How ACCURATE is his description?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE COMPARISONS, AND THE METHOD BEHIND THEM — this is the best single illustration of how a traveller describes the undescribable:**
> - ⭐ **THE TREE → A DATE-PALM.** *His audience in Morocco and West Asia knew date-palms intimately. **He anchors the unknown to the most familiar tree they had**, then states the single difference: nuts instead of dates. **Efficient and accurate.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE NUT → A HUMAN HEAD.** *Two eyes, a mouth, a **brain** inside when green, **hair** on the outside. **Anatomically sustained, and genuinely a good description of a coconut's three germination pores and its husk.**
> - ⭐ **IS IT APPROPRIATE?** *Yes for **communication**, and it is **strikingly accurate** — anyone who has seen a coconut recognises the face. ⭐ **But note what it does: it makes the fruit MACABRE.** *Comparing a food to a severed head with a brain in it is a choice, and it serves §5's purpose — *"anything unfamiliar was particularly highlighted."***
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE PRACTICAL DETAIL IS THE HISTORICALLY VALUABLE ONE:** ***coir cords used to SEW SHIPS TOGETHER "INSTEAD OF IRON NAILS."*** **That is real technological information about Indian Ocean shipbuilding, dropped in almost as an aside.** *The wonder gets the attention; the economics come free with it.*

**📖 SOURCE 7 — *The paan***

> *"**The betel is A TREE WHICH IS CULTIVATED IN THE SAME MANNER AS THE GRAPE-VINE**;… **THE BETEL HAS NO FRUIT AND IS GROWN ONLY FOR THE SAKE OF ITS LEAVES**… The manner of its use is that **before eating it one takes ARECA NUT; this is LIKE A NUTMEG but is broken up until it is reduced to SMALL PELLETS**, and one places these in his mouth and chews them. Then he takes **the leaves of betel, puts A LITTLE CHALK on them, and masticates them along with the betel.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Notice the same technique, applied twice: BETEL → THE GRAPE-VINE (a familiar climbing cultivated plant), ARECA NUT → NUTMEG (a familiar hard aromatic nut).** ⭐ **Every unfamiliar thing is pinned to a Mediterranean or West Asian equivalent.** *And what genuinely puzzles him is the **uselessness** of it by his standards — *"**has NO FRUIT and is grown ONLY for the sake of its LEAVES**"* — followed by a **precise procedural account** of preparation. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He cannot explain WHY people do it, so he describes exactly HOW.** *(⭐ **NCERT's Discuss box for §5 asks precisely this: *"How did Ibn Battuta handle the problem of DESCRIBING THINGS OR SITUATIONS TO PEOPLE WHO HAD NOT SEEN OR EXPERIENCED THEM?"* The answer is the two-step: ANCHOR to something familiar, then SPECIFY the difference.**)*

### 5.2 Ibn Battuta and Indian cities ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐ ** THE VERDICT: "Ibn Battuta found cities in the
      subcontinent FULL OF EXCITING OPPORTUNITIES FOR THOSE WHO
      HAD THE NECESSARY DRIVE, RESOURCES AND SKILLS. They were
      DENSELY POPULATED AND PROSPEROUS, except for the
      occasional disruptions caused by WARS AND INVASIONS." **

   ** "most cities had CROWDED STREETS and BRIGHT AND COLOURFUL
      MARKETS that were STACKED WITH A WIDE VARIETY OF
      GOODS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE TWO CITIES HE NAMES: **
   ** DELHI -- "A VAST CITY, WITH A GREAT POPULATION, THE
      LARGEST IN INDIA." **
   ** DAULATABAD (Maharashtra) -- "was NO LESS, and EASILY
      RIVALLED DELHI IN SIZE." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE BAZAAR -- and this is the point to carry **
   ================================================================
   ** "The bazaars were NOT ONLY PLACES OF ECONOMIC
      TRANSACTIONS, BUT ALSO THE HUB OF SOCIAL AND CULTURAL
      ACTIVITIES. MOST BAZAARS HAD A MOSQUE AND A TEMPLE, and
      in some of them at least, SPACES WERE MARKED FOR PUBLIC
      PERFORMANCES BY DANCERS, MUSICIANS AND SINGERS." **
```

**📖 SOURCE 8 — *Dehli*, in Ibn Battuta's words ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"The city of Dehli **covers a wide area and has a large population**… ⭐ **THE RAMPART ROUND THE CITY IS WITHOUT PARALLEL. THE BREADTH OF ITS WALL IS ELEVEN CUBITS; and INSIDE IT are HOUSES FOR THE NIGHT SENTRY AND GATE-KEEPERS.** Inside the ramparts, there are **STORE-HOUSES for storing EDIBLES, MAGAZINES, AMMUNITION, BALLISTAS AND SIEGE MACHINES. The grains that are stored can LAST FOR A LONG TIME, WITHOUT ROTTING**…*
>
> *⭐ **In the interior of the rampart, HORSEMEN AS WELL AS INFANTRYMEN MOVE FROM ONE END OF THE CITY TO ANOTHER. The rampart is PIERCED THROUGH BY WINDOWS which open on the side of the city, and it is through these windows that LIGHT ENTERS INSIDE. THE LOWER PART OF THE RAMPART IS BUILT OF STONE; THE UPPER PART OF BRICKS. It has MANY TOWERS close to one another.***
>
> *⭐ **There are TWENTY-EIGHT GATES of this city which are called DARWAZA**, and of these, **the BUDAUN DARWAZA is the greatest**; inside the **MANDWI DARWAZA there is a GRAIN MARKET**; adjacent to the **GUL DARWAZA there is an ORCHARD**…*
>
> *⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It has **A FINE CEMETERY in which GRAVES HAVE DOMES over them, and those that do not have a dome, HAVE AN ARCH, for sure. In the cemetery they SOW FLOWERS such as TUBEROSE, JASMINE, WILD ROSE, etc.; and FLOWERS BLOSSOM THERE IN ALL SEASONS.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"What were the ARCHITECTURAL FEATURES that Ibn Battuta noted?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The features, and notice that the rampart is described as INHABITED ARCHITECTURE, not just a wall:** ⭐ **a wall ELEVEN CUBITS broad, containing HOUSES for sentries and gatekeepers, STOREHOUSES for food and weapons, and PASSAGES wide enough for horsemen and infantry to move through** · **WINDOWS piercing it to let in light** · ⭐ **STONE below, BRICK above** · **many TOWERS** · **TWENTY-EIGHT named gates (*darwaza*)** · and **DOMED and ARCHED graves in the cemetery.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE OBSERVATION THAT LIFTS AN ANSWER: Ibn Battuta describes Delhi's wall as A BUILDING, not a boundary.** *You can **live in it, store grain in it, garrison it, and march an army through its thickness.** **That is a city fortified against siege, and the food store *"without rotting"* is the tell.** *(⭐ **And note what he chooses to end on: FLOWERS IN THE CEMETERY, BLOOMING IN ALL SEASONS.** He begins with siege engines and closes with jasmine. **That range — military engineering and tuberoses in the same paragraph — is exactly the "delight to the ears and eyes" his commission asked for.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 9 — *Music in the market*: Daulatabad's Tarababad ⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"In **DAULATABAD** there is **a market place for MALE AND FEMALE SINGERS, which is known as TARABABAD. It is ONE OF THE GREATEST AND MOST BEAUTIFUL BAZAARS.** It has **numerous shops** and **every shop has a door which leads into the house of the owner**… The shops are **DECORATED WITH CARPETS** and at the centre of a shop there is **A SWING on which sits the female singer**… ⭐ In the middle of the market place there stands **a LARGE CUPOLA, which is carpeted and decorated**, in which **the CHIEF OF THE MUSICIANS takes his place EVERY THURSDAY after the dawn prayers**… **The female singers come in successive crowds, SING BEFORE HIM AND DANCE UNTIL DUSK.** ⭐ In this bazaar there are **MOSQUES for offering prayers**… **One of the HINDU RULERS… ALIGHTED AT THE CUPOLA every time he passed by this market place, and the female singers would sing before him. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ EVEN SOME MUSLIM RULERS DID THE SAME.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"Why do you think Ibn Battuta HIGHLIGHTED THESE ACTIVITIES in his description?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Because it is exactly the kind of *"curious particular"* Source 3 commissioned — but the historically valuable content is underneath the novelty.** ⭐ **A market DEDICATED to performers, with an organised structure (a chief of the musicians, a fixed weekly assembly, a central cupola), MOSQUES inside it, and BOTH Hindu and Muslim rulers stopping to listen.** *That single passage gives you **occupational specialisation, guild-like organisation, religious infrastructure inside a commercial space, and shared elite patronage across religious lines** — which is precisely §5.2's claim that bazaars were *"the hub of SOCIAL AND CULTURAL activities."*

**💰 WHY THE TOWNS WERE RICH — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ and note that this is HISTORIANS' inference, not Ibn Battuta's**

```
   ⭐ ** "While Ibn Battuta was NOT PARTICULARLY CONCERNED WITH
      EXPLAINING THE PROSPERITY OF TOWNS, HISTORIANS HAVE USED
      HIS ACCOUNT TO SUGGEST THAT TOWNS DERIVED A SIGNIFICANT
      PORTION OF THEIR WEALTH THROUGH THE APPROPRIATION OF
      SURPLUS FROM VILLAGES." **

   ** WHAT HE DID NOTICE: **
   * ⭐ ** "Indian agriculture VERY PRODUCTIVE because of THE
     FERTILITY OF THE SOIL, WHICH ALLOWED FARMERS TO CULTIVATE
     TWO CROPS A YEAR." **
   * ** "the subcontinent was WELL INTEGRATED WITH INTER-ASIAN
     NETWORKS OF TRADE AND COMMERCE, with INDIAN MANUFACTURES
     BEING IN GREAT DEMAND IN BOTH WEST ASIA AND SOUTHEAST
     ASIA, FETCHING HUGE PROFITS FOR ARTISANS AND
     MERCHANTS." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE TEXTILES: "INDIAN TEXTILES, PARTICULARLY
     COTTON CLOTH, FINE MUSLINS, SILKS, BROCADE AND SATIN, were
     in great demand. Certain varieties of FINE MUSLIN WERE SO
     EXPENSIVE THAT THEY COULD BE WORN ONLY BY THE NOBLES AND

```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FIRST SENTENCE IS A METHOD LESSON WORTH STATING: the historian's conclusion is NOT the traveller's observation.** ⭐ *Ibn Battuta **did not ask** where urban wealth came from. **Historians read his descriptions of rich towns and productive agriculture side by side and inferred the transfer of rural surplus.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A source can support a conclusion its author never reached — which is exactly how [Part I Theme 1's waste-as-evidence](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/01_Theme1_Bricks_Beads_and_Bones_Harappan.md) works.**

### 5.3 A unique system of communication ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** "The state evidently TOOK SPECIAL MEASURES TO ENCOURAGE
      MERCHANTS. Almost all trade routes were WELL SUPPLIED
      WITH INNS AND GUEST HOUSES." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHAT THE POSTAL SYSTEM COULD DO: **
   ** "Ibn Battuta was AMAZED by the EFFICIENCY of the postal
      system which allowed merchants NOT ONLY TO SEND
      INFORMATION AND REMIT CREDIT ACROSS LONG DISTANCES, BUT
      ALSO TO DISPATCH GOODS REQUIRED AT SHORT NOTICE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE HEADLINE FACT -- learn the two numbers: **
   ** "while IT TOOK FIFTY DAYS TO REACH DELHI FROM SIND, THE
      NEWS REPORTS OF SPIES WOULD REACH THE SULTAN THROUGH THE
      POSTAL SYSTEM IN JUST FIVE DAYS." **
```

**📖 SOURCE 10 — *On horse and on foot*: the two postal systems ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"In India **the postal system is OF TWO KINDS.***
>
> *⭐ **THE HORSE-POST, called ULUQ, is run by ROYAL HORSES stationed at a distance of EVERY FOUR MILES.***
>
> *⭐ **THE FOOT-POST has THREE STATIONS PER MILE; it is called DAWA, that is ONE-THIRD OF A MILE**… Now, at every third of a mile there is **a well-populated village, outside which are THREE PAVILIONS in which sit men with GIRDED LOINS READY TO START. Each of them carries A ROD, TWO CUBITS IN LENGTH, WITH COPPER BELLS AT THE TOP.***
>
> *⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **When the courier starts from the city he holds THE LETTER IN ONE HAND AND THE ROD WITH ITS BELLS IN THE OTHER; and he RUNS AS FAST AS HE CAN. WHEN THE MEN IN THE PAVILION HEAR THE RINGING OF THE BELL THEY GET READY. As soon as the courier reaches them, ONE OF THEM TAKES THE LETTER FROM HIS HAND AND RUNS AT TOP SPEED, shaking the rod all the while until he reaches the next DAWA.** And the same process continues till the letter reaches its destination.*
>
> *⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS FOOT-POST IS QUICKER THAN THE HORSE-POST; and often it is used to TRANSPORT THE FRUITS OF KHURASAN WHICH ARE MUCH DESIRED IN INDIA.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BEST SINGLE SOURCE IN THE CHAPTER AND IT REPAYS CLOSE READING — the counter-intuitive fact is that THE RUNNERS BEAT THE HORSES.**
> - ⭐ **The reason is in the spacing: the horse-post changes mount every FOUR MILES; the foot-post changes runner every THIRD OF A MILE.** *Twelve handovers to the horse's one. **Nobody tires, so nobody slows down.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the bell is the mechanism that makes it work.** *The next runner **hears the courier coming and is on his feet before he arrives** — so the handover costs almost nothing. **That is a relay optimised to eliminate idle time at the exchange**, and it is why fifty days becomes five.
> - ⭐ **The detail that makes it real: the system was used to carry FRUIT from Khurasan.** *A network built for **intelligence and administration** was good enough to move **perishables** — which tells you both its speed and its reliability.
>
> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"Do you think the foot-post system could have operated THROUGHOUT THE SUBCONTINENT?"*** ⭐ **A good answer says probably not, and gives the reason from the source itself:** *the system required **a well-populated village every third of a mile**, three staffed pavilions at each, and continuous state maintenance. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It could only exist along DENSELY SETTLED, STATE-CONTROLLED TRUNK ROUTES** — not in forest, hill or thinly-populated country. **The postal system maps the reach of the state, not the extent of the subcontinent.**

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"A STRANGE NATION?" — the box on ABDUR RAZZAQ, and it is a lovely corrective to any single traveller's verdict.** *His travelogue of the **1440s** is *"an interesting **MIXTURE OF EMOTIONS AND PERCEPTIONS**."* ⭐ **At CALICUT (present-day Kozhikode) he was unimpressed** — the people were *"a people the likes of whom I had never imagined"*, whom he called **"a strange nation."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then, near MANGALORE, having crossed the Western Ghats, he saw a temple that "FILLED HIM WITH ADMIRATION":** *"an idol-house **the likes of which is not to be found in all the world**… **square, approximately ten yards a side, five yards in height, ALL COVERED WITH CAST BRONZE, with four porticos.** In the entrance portico was **a statue in the likeness of a human being, full stature, MADE OF GOLD. It had TWO RED RUBIES FOR EYES, SO CUNNINGLY MADE THAT YOU WOULD SAY IT COULD SEE. WHAT CRAFT AND ARTISANSHIP!**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The same traveller, within one journey, calls the people *"a strange nation"* and their craftsmanship the finest in the world.** ⭐ **A traveller's verdict is not a single settled judgement — it changes with what he happens to be looking at, and with his mood.** *Quote this whenever an answer risks treating any one account as "the foreign view of India."*

## 6️⃣ Bernier and the "Degenerate" East ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's contrast between him and Ibn Battuta is the spine of Exercise 2 — quote it:** *"**If IBN BATTUTA chose to describe EVERYTHING THAT IMPRESSED AND EXCITED HIM BECAUSE OF ITS NOVELTY, FRANÇOIS BERNIER BELONGED TO A DIFFERENT INTELLECTUAL TRADITION. He was far more preoccupied with COMPARING AND CONTRASTING what he saw in India with the situation in Europe in general and France in particular, FOCUSING ON SITUATIONS WHICH HE CONSIDERED DEPRESSING.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the motive, stated plainly:** *"**HIS IDEA SEEMS TO HAVE BEEN TO INFLUENCE POLICY-MAKERS AND THE INTELLIGENTSIA TO ENSURE THAT THEY MADE WHAT HE CONSIDERED TO BE THE 'RIGHT' DECISIONS.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT SENTENCE IS THE KEY TO EVERYTHING IN §6.** ⭐ **Bernier was not primarily reporting on India. He was arguing with FRANCE, using India as evidence.** *And the case he was making was **about private property** — which is why the "no private property in land" thesis is the hinge of his whole account.

### 6.1 The question of landownership — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the chapter's central error, and its consequences

```
   ================================================================
   ** BERNIER'S THESIS **
   ================================================================
   ** "one of THE FUNDAMENTAL DIFFERENCES between Mughal India
      and Europe was THE LACK OF PRIVATE PROPERTY IN LAND in
      the former." **
   ⭐ ** "He was A FIRM BELIEVER IN THE VIRTUES OF PRIVATE
      PROPERTY, and saw CROWN OWNERSHIP OF LAND AS BEING
      HARMFUL FOR BOTH THE STATE AND ITS PEOPLE." **
   ** "He thought that IN THE MUGHAL EMPIRE THE EMPEROR OWNED
      ALL THE LAND AND DISTRIBUTED IT AMONG HIS NOBLES." **
   ⚠️ ** "THIS PERCEPTION WAS NOT UNIQUE TO BERNIER, but is
      found in MOST TRAVELLERS' ACCOUNTS OF THE SIXTEENTH AND
      SEVENTEENTH CENTURIES." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE CHAIN OF CONSEQUENCES HE DEDUCED **
   ================================================================
   ** crown ownership **
        -> ** "landholders COULD NOT PASS ON THEIR LAND TO
           THEIR CHILDREN" **
        -> ** "So they were AVERSE TO ANY LONG-TERM INVESTMENT
           in the sustenance and expansion of production" **
        -> ⭐ ** "The absence of private property in land had,
           therefore, PREVENTED THE EMERGENCE OF THE CLASS OF
           'IMPROVING' LANDLORDS (AS IN WESTERN EUROPE) with a
           concern to maintain or improve the land" **
        -> ** "It had led to THE UNIFORM RUINATION OF
           AGRICULTURE, EXCESSIVE OPPRESSION OF THE PEASANTRY
           and A CONTINUOUS DECLINE IN THE LIVING STANDARDS OF
           ALL SECTIONS OF SOCIETY, EXCEPT THE RULING
           ARISTOCRACY." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE SOCIAL PICTURE THAT FOLLOWED: **
   ** "Indian society [consisted] of UNDIFFERENTIATED MASSES OF
      IMPOVERISHED PEOPLE, SUBJUGATED BY A SMALL MINORITY OF A
      VERY RICH AND POWERFUL RULING CLASS. Between the poorest
      of the poor and the richest of the rich, THERE WAS NO
      SOCIAL GROUP OR CLASS WORTH THE NAME." **
   ⭐ ** Bernier confidently asserted: "THERE IS NO MIDDLE STATE
      IN INDIA." **

   ** THE SUMMARY IMAGE: "its king was the king of 'BEGGARS AND
      BARBARIANS'; its cities and towns were RUINED AND
      CONTAMINATED WITH 'ILL AIR'; and its fields, 'OVERSPREAD
      WITH BUSHES' and full of 'PESTILENTIAL MARISHES'.
      ⭐ AND, ALL THIS WAS BECAUSE OF ONE REASON: CROWN
      OWNERSHIP OF LAND." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT'S REFUTATION IS THE MOST IMPORTANT PASSAGE IN THE CHAPTER. IT HAS THREE PARTS AND YOU SHOULD GIVE ALL THREE.**
>
> ```
>    ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** REFUTATION 1 -- THE MUGHAL RECORDS SAY
>       OTHERWISE **
>    ** "CURIOUSLY, NONE OF THE MUGHAL OFFICIAL DOCUMENTS
>       SUGGEST THAT THE STATE WAS THE SOLE OWNER OF LAND." **
>    ⭐ ** "For instance, ABU'L FAZL, the sixteenth-century
>       official chronicler of Akbar's reign, DESCRIBES THE LAND
>       REVENUE AS 'REMUNERATIONS OF SOVEREIGNTY', A CLAIM MADE
>       BY THE RULER ON HIS SUBJECTS FOR THE PROTECTION HE
>       PROVIDED, RATHER THAN AS RENT ON LAND THAT HE OWNED." **
>
>    ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** REFUTATION 2 -- WHY THE TRAVELLERS GOT IT WRONG **
>    ** "IT IS POSSIBLE THAT EUROPEAN TRAVELLERS REGARDED SUCH
>       CLAIMS AS RENT BECAUSE LAND REVENUE DEMANDS WERE OFTEN
>       VERY HIGH. However, THIS WAS ACTUALLY NOT A RENT OR EVEN
>       A LAND TAX, BUT A TAX ON THE CROP." **
>
>    ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** REFUTATION 3 -- RURAL SOCIETY WAS HIGHLY
>       DIFFERENTIATED **
>    ** "this picture of rural society WAS FAR FROM TRUE. In
>       fact, during the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries,
>       RURAL SOCIETY WAS CHARACTERISED BY CONSIDERABLE SOCIAL
>       AND ECONOMIC DIFFERENTIATION: " **
>      * ** at one end, THE BIG ZAMINDARS, "who enjoyed SUPERIOR
>        RIGHTS IN LAND" **
>      * ** at the other, THE "UNTOUCHABLE" LANDLESS LABOURERS **
>      * ⭐ ** in between, THE BIG PEASANT, "who USED HIRED
>        LABOUR and ENGAGED IN COMMODITY PRODUCTION" **
>      * ** and THE SMALLER PEASANT, "who could BARELY PRODUCE
>        FOR HIS SUBSISTENCE" **
> ```
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REFUTATION 2 IS THE INTELLECTUALLY SATISFYING ONE, SO LEAD WITH IT IN AN ANSWER: BERNIER MISTOOK A HIGH TAX FOR A RENT.** ⭐ **A landlord collects RENT because he OWNS the land; a state collects TAX because it CLAIMS SOVEREIGNTY.** *When the tax is very high, the two feel identical from below — **and Bernier, who believed in private property and was looking for its absence, read the amount as evidence of the ownership.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He inferred a property regime from a rate of extraction.** *(⭐ **And note that Abu'l Fazl's own phrase — *"REMUNERATIONS OF SOVEREIGNTY… for the PROTECTION he provided"* — is a CLAIM by the state's own chronicler, not neutral testimony either.** **[Theme 8](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) and the [deleted Theme 9](00_Index.md) are both about exactly that source.**)*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE AFTERLIFE OF THE ERROR IS THE HIGHEST-VALUE MATERIAL IN THE CHAPTER — a wrong idea becoming two centuries of theory. Learn this chain:**
>
> ```
>    ** BERNIER (1670s) **
>       "the emperor owned all the land"
>              |
>    ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** MONTESQUIEU ** -- "the French philosopher…
>       USED THIS ACCOUNT TO DEVELOP THE IDEA OF
>       ** ORIENTAL DESPOTISM ** , according to which RULERS IN
>       ASIA (THE ORIENT OR THE EAST) ENJOYED ABSOLUTE AUTHORITY
>       OVER THEIR SUBJECTS, WHO WERE KEPT IN CONDITIONS OF
>       SUBJUGATION AND POVERTY, arguing that ALL LAND BELONGED
>       TO THE KING AND THAT PRIVATE PROPERTY WAS NON-EXISTENT."
>              |
>    ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** KARL MARX (nineteenth century) ** -- "developed
>       [this] as the concept of the ** ASIATIC MODE OF
>       PRODUCTION ** . He argued that in India (and other Asian
>       countries), BEFORE COLONIALISM, ** SURPLUS WAS
>       APPROPRIATED BY THE STATE. ** This led to the emergence
>       of a society composed of ** A LARGE NUMBER OF AUTONOMOUS
>       AND (INTERNALLY) EGALITARIAN VILLAGE COMMUNITIES. THE
>       IMPERIAL COURT PRESIDED OVER THESE… RESPECTING THEIR
>       AUTONOMY AS LONG AS THE FLOW OF SURPLUS WAS UNIMPEDED.
>       ⭐ THIS WAS REGARDED AS A STAGNANT SYSTEM. ** "
> ```
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE POINT TO MAKE IN A MAINS ANSWER: A TRAVELLER'S MISREADING BECAME A PHILOSOPHICAL DOCTRINE, AND THEN A THEORY OF WORLD HISTORY.** ⭐ **"Oriental despotism" and "the Asiatic mode of production" both rest on a claim about Indian land that the Mughal records themselves contradict.** *(⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the consequence reaches [Part III](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md).** The idea that India had **no private property in land and a stagnant, unchanging village order** is precisely the assumption behind the **Permanent Settlement** — an attempt to manufacture the *"improving landlords"* Bernier said India lacked. **Bernier's error did not stay an error. It became policy.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 11 — *The poor peasant* ⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"Of the vast tracts of country constituting the empire of Hindustan, **many are little more than SAND, OR BARREN MOUNTAINS, BADLY CULTIVATED, AND THINLY POPULATED. Even a considerable portion of the good land REMAINS UNTILLED FOR WANT OF LABOURERS**; many of whom **perish in consequence of the BAD TREATMENT they experience from GOVERNORS. The poor people, when they become incapable of discharging the demands of their RAPACIOUS LORDS, are not only often DEPRIVED OF THE MEANS OF SUBSISTENCE**, but are also made to **LOSE THEIR CHILDREN, WHO ARE CARRIED AWAY AS SLAVES.** ⭐ Thus, it happens that **THE PEASANTRY, DRIVEN TO DESPAIR BY SO EXCESSIVE A TYRANNY, ABANDON THE COUNTRY.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And NCERT's framing of it:** *"In this instance, Bernier was **PARTICIPATING IN CONTEMPORARY DEBATES IN EUROPE concerning the nature of state and society**, and **INTENDED THAT HIS DESCRIPTION OF MUGHAL INDIA WOULD SERVE AS A WARNING TO THOSE WHO DID NOT RECOGNISE THE 'MERITS' OF PRIVATE PROPERTY.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"What, according to Bernier, were the PROBLEMS FACED BY PEASANTS? Do you think his description would have SERVED TO STRENGTHEN HIS CASE?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The problems: bad land, labour shortage, ill-treatment by governors, impossible revenue demands, loss of subsistence, children taken, and finally FLIGHT — *"abandon the country."*** ⭐ **And yes, emphatically, it served his case — that is why it is written this way.** *NCERT tells you the passage is **an intervention in a European argument**, not a report. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The peasants are evidence in a French debate about property rights.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A fair note, though: the last element is independently attested.** *Peasant **flight** in the face of excessive demands is exactly what **[Part I Theme 2's *Gandatindu Jataka*](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/02_Theme2_Kings_Farmers_and_Towns.md)** describes a thousand years earlier — *"people abandoned their village and went to live in the forest."* ⭐ **Bernier's FRAMEWORK is wrong; not every OBSERVATION in it is.** *(⭐ And the box on **PELSAERT**, the Dutch traveller of the early seventeenth century, shows he was not alone: he too was *"shocked to see the widespread poverty"* and held the state responsible — *"**so much is wrung from the peasants that even dry bread is scarcely left to fill their stomachs.**")*

**📖 SOURCE 12 — *A warning for Europe* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> **Bernier warned that if European kings followed the Mughal model, their kingdoms** *"would be **VERY FAR FROM BEING WELL-CULTIVATED AND PEOPLED, SO WELL BUILT, SO RICH, SO POLITE AND FLOURISHING as we see them**… **They would soon be KINGS OF DESERTS AND SOLITUDES, OF BEGGARS AND BARBARIANS**… We should find **the great Cities and the great Burroughs RENDERED UNINHABITABLE BECAUSE OF ILL AIR, and to fall to ruine without ANY BODIES TAKING CARE OF REPAIRING THEM; the hillocks ABANDON'D, and the fields OVERSPREAD WITH BUSHES, or FILL'D WITH PESTILENTIAL MARISHES.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's question — *"How does Bernier depict A SCENARIO OF DOOM?"* — and the answer is that the passage is not about India at all.** ⭐ **Every clause is in the CONDITIONAL: this is what would happen TO FRANCE if French kings copied the Mughals.** *India appears only as **the finished product of a policy he is warning against.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Source 12 is the clearest proof in the chapter that Bernier's India is an ARGUMENT, not a description** — and NCERT's instruction is exactly right: *"**once you have read Chapters 8 and 9, RETURN TO THIS DESCRIPTION AND ANALYSE IT AGAIN.**"*

### 6.2 A more complex social reality — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ where Bernier contradicts himself

> ⭐ *"While Bernier's preoccupation with projecting the Mughal state as tyrannical is obvious, **HIS DESCRIPTIONS OCCASIONALLY HINT AT A MORE COMPLEX SOCIAL REALITY.**"*

```
   ** THE CONTRADICTION, in NCERT's own summary: **
   * ** he felt ARTISANS HAD NO INCENTIVE TO IMPROVE the
     quality of their manufactures, "since PROFITS WERE
     APPROPRIATED BY THE STATE. Manufactures were,
     consequently, EVERYWHERE IN DECLINE." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "AT THE SAME TIME, HE CONCEDED THAT VAST
     QUANTITIES OF THE WORLD'S PRECIOUS METALS FLOWED INTO
     INDIA, AS MANUFACTURES WERE EXPORTED IN EXCHANGE FOR GOLD
     AND SILVER." **
   * ** "He also NOTICED THE EXISTENCE OF A PROSPEROUS MERCHANT
     COMMUNITY, ENGAGED IN LONG-DISTANCE EXCHANGE." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PUT THOSE TWO STATEMENTS SIDE BY SIDE AND THE THESIS COLLAPSES — this is the sharpest thing you can say about Bernier.** ⭐ **Manufactures are *"everywhere in decline"* AND *"vast quantities of the world's precious metals flowed into India"* in exchange for those same manufactures.** *A country whose manufacturing is collapsing does not run a bullion surplus with the world. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Bernier reports the fact and keeps the theory.** *(⭐ **And he says it himself in Source 13:** *"**gold and silver, after circulating in every other quarter of the globe, come at length to be SWALLOWED UP, LOST IN SOME MEASURE, IN HINDUSTAN.**")*

**📖 SOURCE 13 — *A different socio-economic scenario* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"**of this vast tract of country, A LARGE PORTION IS EXTREMELY FERTILE; the large kingdom of BENGALE, for instance, SURPASSING EGYPT ITSELF, not only in the production of RICE, CORN, and other necessaries of life, but of INNUMERABLE ARTICLES OF COMMERCE which are not cultivated in Egypt; such as SILKS, COTTON, and INDIGO.***
>
> *⭐ **There are also many parts of the Indies, where THE POPULATION IS SUFFICIENTLY ABUNDANT, AND THE LAND PRETTY WELL TILLED; and where the artisan… is yet compelled by necessity or otherwise to EMPLOY HIMSELF IN MANUFACTURING CARPETS, BROCADES, EMBROIDERIES, GOLD AND SILVER CLOTHS, and the various sorts of SILK AND COTTON GOODS, which are used in the country or EXPORTED ABROAD.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"In what ways is the description in this excerpt DIFFERENT from that in Source 11?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **They contradict each other point for point, and setting them out as a table is the answer:**

| ⭐ **SOURCE 11** | ⭐ **SOURCE 13** |
|---|---|
| *"little more than **sand, or barren mountains, badly cultivated**"* | *"a large portion is **EXTREMELY FERTILE**"*; **Bengal surpassing EGYPT** |
| *"**thinly populated**"* | *"the population is **sufficiently abundant**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SAME AUTHOR, SAME BOOK, OPPOSITE COUNTRIES.** *The difference is **what each passage is FOR**: Source 11 is **an argument about property**, Source 13 is **an observation about commerce.** ⭐ **When Bernier stops arguing, he describes a rich country.** *(⭐ **And note the small concession he cannot resist even in Source 13 — the artisan is *"naturally indolent"* and works only *"compelled by necessity."*** **The prejudice survives inside the praise.**)*

**🏙️ BERNIER ON TOWNS — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Exercise 3**

```
   ** BERNIER'S CLAIM: "he described Mughal cities as
      ** 'CAMP TOWNS' ** , by which he meant TOWNS THAT OWED
      THEIR EXISTENCE, AND DEPENDED FOR THEIR SURVIVAL, ON THE
      IMPERIAL CAMP." **
   ** "He believed that these CAME INTO EXISTENCE WHEN THE
      IMPERIAL COURT MOVED IN AND RAPIDLY DECLINED WHEN IT
      MOVED OUT… they DID NOT HAVE VIABLE SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC
      FOUNDATIONS but were DEPENDENT ON IMPERIAL PATRONAGE." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** NCERT'S REFUTATION -- three parts **
   ================================================================
   1. ⭐ ** THE NUMBERS: "during the seventeenth century ABOUT
      15 PER CENT OF THE POPULATION LIVED IN TOWNS. THIS WAS,
      ON AVERAGE, HIGHER THAN THE PROPORTION OF URBAN
      POPULATION IN WESTERN EUROPE IN THE SAME PERIOD." **

   2. ** THE VARIETY: "There were ALL KINDS of towns:
      ** MANUFACTURING TOWNS, TRADING TOWNS, PORT-TOWNS, SACRED

      INDEX OF THE PROSPERITY OF MERCHANT COMMUNITIES AND
      PROFESSIONAL CLASSES." **

   3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE INSTITUTIONS -- merchants were ORGANISED: **
      ** "Merchants often had STRONG COMMUNITY OR KIN TIES, and
      were ORGANISED INTO THEIR OWN CASTE-CUM-OCCUPATIONAL
      BODIES. In WESTERN INDIA these groups were called
      ** MAHAJANS ** , and their chief, the ** SHETH ** . In
      urban centres such as ** AHMEDABAD ** the mahajans were
      COLLECTIVELY REPRESENTED BY THE CHIEF OF THE MERCHANT
      COMMUNITY WHO WAS CALLED THE ** NAGARSHETH ** ." **

   ** AND THE PROFESSIONAL CLASSES: **
   ** "PHYSICIANS (HAKIM or VAID), TEACHERS (PUNDIT or MULLA),

   ⭐ ** "While SOME depended on imperial patronage, MANY MADE

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE 15 PER CENT FIGURE IS THE KILLER FACT FOR EXERCISE 3 — MUGHAL INDIA WAS MORE URBANISED THAN WESTERN EUROPE IN THE SAME CENTURY.** ⭐ *A man who came from Europe and called Indian cities parasitic *"camp towns"* was describing **a more urban society than the one he had left.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE ***MAHAJAN*/*SHETH*/*NAGARSHETH*** MATERIAL IS THE STRUCTURAL REFUTATION — these are PERMANENT INSTITUTIONS.** *A **caste-cum-occupational merchant body**, with **an elected or recognised chief**, and in Ahmedabad **a chief of chiefs** — **that is a self-governing commercial community with continuity independent of any court.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A "camp town" cannot have a *nagarsheth*.** *(⭐ **And the professional list dismantles *"there is no middle state in India"* directly** — physicians, teachers, lawyers, painters, architects, musicians, calligraphers, **many of them serving *"ordinary people in crowded markets or bazaars"*, not the court at all.** **That IS the middle stratum Bernier said did not exist.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 14 — *The imperial karkhanas* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **NCERT's own note:** *"**Bernier is perhaps THE ONLY HISTORIAN WHO PROVIDES A DETAILED ACCOUNT OF THE WORKING OF THE IMPERIAL KARKHANAS or WORKSHOPS.**"*
>
> *"**Large halls are seen at many places, called KARKHANAS or WORKSHOPS for the artisans. In one hall, EMBROIDERERS are busily employed, SUPERINTENDED BY A MASTER. In another, you see the GOLDSMITHS; in a third, PAINTERS; in a fourth, VARNISHERS IN LACQUER-WORK; in a fifth, JOINERS, TURNERS, TAILORS AND SHOE-MAKERS; in a sixth, MANUFACTURERS OF SILK, BROCADE AND FINE MUSLINS**…*
>
> *⭐ **The artisans COME EVERY MORNING to their karkhanas where they REMAIN EMPLOYED THE WHOLE DAY; and IN THE EVENING RETURN TO THEIR HOMES.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In this QUIET REGULAR MANNER, THEIR TIME GLIDES AWAY; NO ONE ASPIRING FOR ANY IMPROVEMENT IN THE CONDITION OF LIFE WHEREIN HE HAPPENS TO BE BORN.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's question: *"How does Bernier convey a sense that ALTHOUGH THERE WAS A GREAT DEAL OF ACTIVITY, THERE WAS LITTLE PROGRESS?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ANSWER IS IN THE LAST SENTENCE, AND IT IS A MASTERCLASS IN LOADED WRITING.** ⭐ *The first two-thirds of the passage is **impressive**: six specialised workshops, master craftsmen supervising, an organised working day. **Then the verdict arrives and re-colours everything: *"in this QUIET REGULAR manner, their time GLIDES AWAY; NO ONE ASPIRING for any improvement."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Quiet", "regular" and "glides away" turn ORDER into STAGNATION**, and *"the condition of life wherein he happens to be BORN"* imports his assumption that **birth fixes everything.** *(⭐ **He is describing a functioning industrial organisation and calling it sleep.** *And it is worth noting **Exercise 9**, where Bernier praises Indian craftsmen extravagantly — *"handsome pieces of workmanship made by persons DESTITUTE OF TOOLS"*, imitations of European goods so exact that *"the difference between the original and copy can hardly be discerned"*, **excellent muskets**, gold ornaments whose *"exquisite workmanship"* may exceed any European goldsmith's, and paintings whose *"beauty, softness and delicacy"* he **"often admired."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The same author, on the same artisans. Set Source 14 against Exercise 9 and the contradiction is complete.**)*

> 🔴 **NCERT's Discuss box: *"Why do you think scholars like Bernier chose to COMPARE INDIA WITH EUROPE?"*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *A strong answer: **because the comparison was the point.** *He was **intervening in a European argument about state, society and property** (NCERT says so of Source 11), writing **for the King of France and his ministers**, and needed **a worked example of what happens without private property.** ⭐ **Comparison also gives the reader a handhold** — the same technique as Ibn Battuta's coconut — **but where Ibn Battuta compared to EXPLAIN, Bernier compared to RANK.**

## 7️⃣ Women — Slaves, Sati and Labourers ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT opens with the limitation of its own sources, and this framing should open your answer too:** *"**Travellers who left written accounts were GENERALLY MEN who were interested in and sometimes INTRIGUED by the condition of women in the subcontinent. ⭐ SOMETIMES THEY TOOK SOCIAL INEQUITIES FOR GRANTED AS A 'NATURAL' STATE OF AFFAIRS.**"*

### 📌 Slavery — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Exercise 4, *"analyse the evidence for slavery provided by Ibn Battuta"*

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE EVIDENCE, point by point **
   ================================================================
   1. ⭐ ** SLAVES WERE TRADED OPENLY: "slaves were OPENLY SOLD
      IN MARKETS, LIKE ANY OTHER COMMODITY, and were REGULARLY
      EXCHANGED AS GIFTS." **

   2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** IBN BATTUTA HIMSELF PARTICIPATED -- which is
      the analytically important point: **
      * ** on reaching SIND he PURCHASED "HORSES, CAMELS AND
        SLAVES" as GIFTS for Sultan Muhammad bin Tughlaq **
      * ** at MULTAN he presented the governor with "A SLAVE
        AND HORSE together with RAISINS AND ALMONDS" **

   3. ** THE SCALE OF ROYAL GIFTING: Muhammad bin Tughlaq was
      so pleased with a sermon by a preacher named NASIRUDDIN
      that he gave him ** "A HUNDRED THOUSAND TANKAS (COINS)
      AND TWO HUNDRED SLAVES." **

   4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THERE WAS CONSIDERABLE DIFFERENTIATION AMONG
      SLAVES -- they were not one undifferentiated condition: **
      * ** SOME FEMALE SLAVES IN THE SULTAN'S SERVICE WERE
        EXPERTS IN MUSIC AND DANCE ** -- Ibn Battuta enjoyed
        their performance ** at the wedding of the Sultan's
        sister **
      * ⭐ ** OTHERS WERE EMPLOYED BY THE SULTAN TO KEEP A WATCH
        ON HIS NOBLES ** (see Source 15)
      * ** MOST were used for ** DOMESTIC LABOUR ** , and their
        services were ** "particularly indispensable for
        CARRYING WOMEN AND MEN ON PALANQUINS OR DOLA" **

   5. ⭐ ** THE PRICE: "The price of slaves, PARTICULARLY FEMALE
      SLAVES REQUIRED FOR DOMESTIC LABOUR, WAS VERY LOW, and
      MOST FAMILIES WHO COULD AFFORD TO DO SO KEPT AT LEAST ONE
      OR TWO OF THEM." **
```

**📖 SOURCE 15 — *Slave women*, on surveillance:**

> **Ibn Battuta:** *"It is the habit of the emperor… to **KEEP WITH EVERY NOBLE, GREAT OR SMALL, ONE OF HIS SLAVES WHO SPIES ON THE NOBLES.** He also appoints **FEMALE SCAVENGERS WHO ENTER THE HOUSES UNANNOUNCED; and to them THE SLAVE GIRLS COMMUNICATE ALL THE INFORMATION THEY POSSESS.**"*
>
> ⭐ **NCERT's note:** *"**MOST FEMALE SLAVES WERE CAPTURED IN RAIDS AND EXPEDITIONS.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HOW TO WRITE EXERCISE 4 SO IT SCORES — do not merely list; ANALYSE, which is what the question asks.**
> 1. ⭐ **On the evidence's QUALITY:** *it is **incidental and therefore strong.** Ibn Battuta is not writing about slavery; **he mentions slaves while describing gifts he gave, entertainments he enjoyed, and how he travelled.** **Unselfconscious testimony is better evidence than an argument.**
> 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **On what it shows about the INSTITUTION:** *slaves functioned as **currency in gift-exchange** *(the coupling of *"a slave and horse"* with *"raisins and almonds"* is itself the finding — a person listed among commodities)*, as **skilled performers**, as **an intelligence network**, and as **ordinary domestic labour cheap enough for any moderately prosperous household.**
> 3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **On the SURVEILLANCE point, which almost nobody uses:** *Source 15 describes **an information system built out of enslaved women.** *Slave attendants in every noble's household, and **female scavengers with a pretext to enter any house unannounced**, carrying reports outward. ⭐ **The people with the least power in the household had access to everything said in it — and the state used exactly that.**
> 4. ⚠️ ⭐ **And on the LIMITS of the source:** *he tells us **who owned slaves and what they did**; he tells us almost nothing about **what enslaved people thought, or how they were treated day to day.** *NCERT's note that most female slaves were **captured in raids and expeditions** supplies the origin the *Rihla* passes over.

### 📌 Bernier and sati — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Exercise 5

> *"Contemporary European travellers and writers often highlighted **THE TREATMENT OF WOMEN AS A CRUCIAL MARKER OF DIFFERENCE BETWEEN WESTERN AND EASTERN SOCIETIES. NOT SURPRISINGLY, BERNIER CHOSE THE PRACTICE OF SATI FOR DETAILED DESCRIPTION.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the one substantive observation NCERT draws from him:** *"He noted that **WHILE SOME WOMEN SEEMED TO EMBRACE DEATH CHEERFULLY, OTHERS WERE FORCED TO DIE.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 5 ASKS: *"What were THE ELEMENTS OF THE PRACTICE OF SATI THAT DREW THE ATTENTION OF BERNIER?"* — and the answer has three parts. Note that the question is about HIS ATTENTION, not about the practice.**
> 1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ABSENCE OF CONSENT.** *Bernier's account (**Source 16**, describing a widow at **Lahore** whom he judged **no more than about twelve years old**) turns entirely on **coercion**: she was **unwilling**, she **wept**, she was **restrained and physically forced** toward the pyre by several people, and her **hands and feet were tied so that she could not escape.** ⭐ **Every detail he selects is a detail establishing that she did not choose.**
> 2. ⭐ **THE AGE OF THE WIDOW.** *He stresses that she was **a child**, and calls her *"the poor little creature"* and *"the innocent creature."*
> 3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HIS OWN REACTION, WHICH HE PUTS IN THE ACCOUNT.** *He records that he *"found it difficult to **REPRESS MY FEELINGS** and to prevent their bursting forth into **CLAMOROUS AND UNAVAILING RAGE**."* ⭐ **The observer's emotion is part of the text — which is exactly what makes it a rhetorical document as well as a report.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE ANALYTICAL POINT, WHICH IS WHAT LIFTS THE ANSWER: WHY DID HE CHOOSE THIS SUBJECT AT ALL?** ⭐ *NCERT tells you: European writers used **the treatment of women as *"a crucial marker of difference between Western and Eastern societies."*** **Sati was therefore the ideal subject for a man whose entire method (§3.1, §6) was BINARY OPPOSITION ranked hierarchically.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *That does not make his horror insincere or the practice defensible — **it means the SELECTION was doing argumentative work.** **His own distinction, that some seemed willing and others were forced, is the more historically useful observation, and it is the one he does least with.**

### 📌 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ What ALL of them missed — NCERT's corrective, and the best part of §7

> *"**HOWEVER, WOMEN'S LIVES REVOLVED AROUND MUCH ELSE BESIDES THE PRACTICE OF SATI.***
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**THEIR LABOUR WAS CRUCIAL IN BOTH AGRICULTURAL AND NON-AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION.**"*
> - ⭐ *"**WOMEN FROM MERCHANT FAMILIES PARTICIPATED IN COMMERCIAL ACTIVITIES, SOMETIMES EVEN TAKING MERCANTILE DISPUTES TO THE COURT OF LAW.**"*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**IT THEREFORE SEEMS UNLIKELY THAT WOMEN WERE CONFINED TO THE PRIVATE SPACES OF THEIR HOMES.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's Discuss box: *"Why do you think THE LIVES OF ORDINARY WOMEN WORKERS DID NOT ATTRACT THE ATTENTION of travellers such as Ibn Battuta and Bernier?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE REASONS, AND THE THIRD IS THE BEST:**
> 1. ⭐ **THE TRAVELLERS WERE ALL MEN, AND MOVED IN MALE ELITE SPACES** — courts, mosques, bazaars, the army. *NCERT's opening line of the chapter already told you: *"**we have practically no accounts of travel left by women.**"*
> 2. ⭐ **THEY RECORDED THE STRIKING, NOT THE ORDINARY.** *Ibn Battuta's commission was *"curious particulars"* and *"marvellous things"* (Source 3). **A woman transplanting rice or spinning thread is not a marvel.** ⭐ **A woman being burned alive is.**
> 3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND FOR BERNIER SPECIFICALLY, ORDINARY WORKING WOMEN WERE NO USE TO HIS ARGUMENT.** *He needed **a marker of civilisational difference**. **Women labouring in fields and running commercial disputes in court are exactly what Europe also had — so they prove nothing and get left out.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The binary method REQUIRES him to notice only what is unlike home.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SENTENCE TO WRITE:** *"Travellers recorded sati and slavery because these marked difference, and omitted women's agricultural labour and commercial activity because these did not — **so the accounts preserve the exceptional condition of women and erase their ordinary economic role.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ NCERT's own closing caution — and it is the chapter's conclusion

> *"travellers' accounts provide us with **A TANTALISING GLIMPSE of the lives of men and women during these centuries. ⭐ HOWEVER, THEIR OBSERVATIONS WERE OFTEN SHAPED BY THE CONTEXTS FROM WHICH THEY CAME. AT THE SAME TIME, THERE WERE MANY ASPECTS OF SOCIAL LIFE THAT THESE TRAVELLERS DID NOT NOTICE.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the silence it ends on, which is worth quoting:** *"**Also RELATIVELY UNKNOWN are the experiences and observations of MEN (AND POSSIBLY WOMEN) FROM THE SUBCONTINENT WHO CROSSED SEAS AND MOUNTAINS AND VENTURED INTO LANDS BEYOND THE SUBCONTINENT. WHAT DID THEY SEE AND HEAR? HOW WERE THEIR RELATIONS WITH PEOPLES OF DISTANT LANDS SHAPED? WHAT WERE THE LANGUAGES THEY USED?** These and other questions will hopefully be **systematically addressed by historians in the years to come.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE CHAPTER ENDS BY REVERSING ITS OWN DIRECTION OF GAZE, AND THAT IS A SUPERB CLOSING POINT FOR AN ANSWER.** ⭐ **Three chapters' worth of foreigners looking at India — and NCERT closes by asking what INDIANS saw when they went abroad.** *(⭐ **And it has already given you the beginning of an answer**, in the "creation and circulation of ideas" box: after **1750**, Indians like **SHAIKH ITISAMUDDIN and MIRZA ABU TALIB visited Europe, CONFRONTED the image Europeans had built of their society, and "tried to influence it by PRODUCING THEIR OWN VERSION OF MATTERS."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The travelogue became a two-way instrument — and the moment Indians began writing back is the moment the genre stops being purely about being described and starts being about answering.**)*

| News took fifty days from Sind to Delhi | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Travel took fifty days; the POSTAL SYSTEM did it in FIVE** |
| Bernier was a merchant | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A DOCTOR, political philosopher and historian**; **physician to DARA SHUKOH**, later with **DANISHMAND KHAN**; in India **1656–1668** |
| Bernier's book was addressed to a general readership | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DEDICATED TO LOUIS XIV**; other works written as **letters to influential officials and ministers** |
| Arabic and Persian accounts circulated as widely as Bernier's | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Bernier: 8 French reprints 1670–1725, 3 English by 1684, four translations in five years.** Arabic and Persian accounts **"circulated as MANUSCRIPTS and were generally NOT PUBLISHED BEFORE 1800"** |
| Mughal documents confirm crown ownership of land | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"NONE of the Mughal official documents suggest that the state was the sole owner of land."*** **ABU'L FAZL calls land revenue *"REMUNERATIONS OF SOVEREIGNTY"*** — for protection provided, **not rent** |
| Land revenue was a tax on land | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"this was actually **NOT A RENT OR EVEN A LAND TAX, BUT A TAX ON THE CROP**"* |
| Montesquieu originated the idea of oriental despotism independently | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ He **"USED THIS ACCOUNT"** — i.e. Bernier's — to develop it; **Marx then developed it as the ASIATIC MODE OF PRODUCTION** |
| Mughal India was far less urbanised than Europe | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **About 15 PER CENT of the population lived in towns — "ON AVERAGE, HIGHER THAN… WESTERN EUROPE IN THE SAME PERIOD"** |
| Bernier found no merchant institutions | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ NCERT supplies them: ***MAHAJANS* in western India, their chief the *SHETH*, and in AHMEDABAD the *NAGARSHETH***, chief of the whole merchant community |
| Bernier said manufactures were declining and nothing else | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ He also **conceded that "vast quantities of THE WORLD'S PRECIOUS METALS FLOWED INTO INDIA"** in exchange for manufactures, and noticed **a prosperous long-distance merchant community** |
| Bernier is a minor source on workshops | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"perhaps **THE ONLY HISTORIAN who provides a detailed account of the working of the IMPERIAL KARKHANAS**"* |
| Travellers described women's work in detail | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ They did not — NCERT's corrective is that women's **labour was crucial in agricultural AND non-agricultural production**, and **merchant women even took disputes to court** |
| Slaves in the Delhi Sultanate were an undifferentiated group | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Considerable differentiation** — **skilled musicians and dancers**, **an intelligence network reporting on nobles**, and **cheap domestic labour** |
| We have women's travel accounts from this period | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"We have PRACTICALLY NO ACCOUNTS OF TRAVEL LEFT BY WOMEN, though we know that they travelled."*** |

### 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | How to build it |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Compare and contrast the perspectives of IBN BATTUTA and BERNIER."** *(Exercise 2 — the chapter's central question)* | ⭐ **Use the three-traveller table**: origin, position in India, audience, method, attitude. **Ibn Battuta: experience over books, dictated for ENTERTAINMENT, describes the unfamiliar by ANCHORING it to the familiar, delighted.** **Bernier: a court insider writing for LOUIS XIV, BINARY OPPOSITION ranked hierarchically, intending to influence policy.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Conclude: *"one compared in order to EXPLAIN, the other in order to RANK"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Discuss AL-BIRUNI's understanding of the caste system."** *(Exercise 6)* | **His three barriers** · **the Persian four-fold parallel** *(social divisions not unique to India)* · **the Islamic contrast** *(equality, differing only in piety)* · **Source 5's Purusha-sukta account** · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **his rejection of pollution as *"contrary to the laws of nature"*** · then **NCERT's correction** — reliance on **normative Brahmanical texts**, and the ***ANTYAJA*** point: **socially oppressed, economically included** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"To what extent does BERNIER's account enable historians to reconstruct rural society?"** *(Exercise 8)* | ⭐ **What it gives**: a vivid picture of **high revenue demand, official oppression and peasant flight** — the last independently plausible. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **What it gets wrong**: crown ownership *(refuted by Mughal documents and Abu'l Fazl)*; **rent mistaken for a tax on the crop**; and *"no middle state"* against a **four-tier rural structure** — big zamindars, big peasants using hired labour and producing for the market, small subsistence peasants, landless labourers. **Conclude: usable for TEXTURE, unusable for STRUCTURE** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Discuss the picture of URBAN CENTRES that emerges from Bernier's account."** *(Exercise 3)* | **His "camp towns" thesis** → **NCERT's three refutations**: **15% urban, higher than Western Europe**; **the variety of town types**; and ⭐ **the *mahajan*/*sheth*/*nagarsheth* institutions plus the professional classes** — the very *"middle state"* he denied |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Is IBN BATTUTA useful for understanding contemporary urban life?"** *(Exercise 7)* | ⭐ **YES**: Delhi's fortification and gates, Daulatabad's scale, bazaars as social-cultural hubs with mosque and temple, the Tarababad performers' market, the postal relay, two crops a year, textiles in demand. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BUT**: he *"was not particularly concerned with EXPLAINING prosperity"* — the surplus-transfer argument is **historians' inference**; and his commission was **wonders**, so the ordinary is under-recorded |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"A traveller's account is shaped by where he stands."** *(the transferable essay/GS1 angle)* | ⭐ **Al-Biruni: names his own three barriers — and still reads India through one social group's texts.** **Ibn Battuta: commissioned to produce marvels, so marvels is what survives.** **Bernier: arguing with France, using India as evidence.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Then generalise to **[Part I Theme 4's Greek yardstick](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md)** and **[Part III Theme 10's British paintings of 1857](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/10_Theme10_Rebels_and_the_Raj_1857.md)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **From Bernier to Montesquieu to Marx** | ⭐ **The chain in full**, and the crucial point that **the foundation was factually wrong**. Then the consequence: this picture of **stagnant, propertyless Asia** underpins colonial land policy — **read straight into [Part III Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Women in travellers' accounts** *(Exercises 4 and 5, plus GS1 Society)* | ⭐ **What they recorded** — slavery *(and the surveillance system built from it)*, sati *(and Bernier's own distinction between apparent willingness and force)*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **What they omitted** — women's **agricultural and non-agricultural labour** and **merchant women in court**. **Then the reason: the travellers were men, wrote for effect, and needed markers of DIFFERENCE** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Print, circulation and the making of an image** | **Bernier's reprints and translations against Arabic/Persian manuscripts unpublished before 1800** · the **"creation and circulation of ideas about India"** box · and ⭐ **Shaikh Itisamuddin and Mirza Abu Talib after 1750 "producing their own version of matters"** |

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***Kitab-ul-Hind*** | ⭐ **Al-Biruni's work, in ARABIC, 80 chapters**, each typically **question → Sanskritic description → comparison** |
| ***Rihla*** | ⭐ **Ibn Battuta's book of travels, in ARABIC** — **dictated**, and written down by **Ibn Juzayy** |
| **Metrology** | *"The science of measurement"* — one of the *Kitab-ul-Hind*'s subjects |
| **"Hindu"** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Originally a **GEOGRAPHICAL** term from Old Persian, for the region **east of the Sindhu**; *"none of these expressions indicated the RELIGIOUS identity of the people"* |
| ***Antyaja*** | ⭐ *"Literally, **BORN OUTSIDE THE SYSTEM**"* — expected to provide **inexpensive labour**; **socially oppressed but economically included** |
| ***Qazi*** | **Judge** — Ibn Battuta's office in **Delhi** and in the **Maldives** |
| ***Shari`a*** | **Islamic religious law** — his family's field of expertise |
| ***Uluq* / *dawa*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The horse-post (stations every FOUR MILES) / the foot-post (THREE STATIONS PER MILE)** — and **the foot-post was faster** |
| ***Darwaza*** | **Gate** — Delhi had **twenty-eight**, the greatest being **Budaun** |
| ***Dola*** | **A palanquin** — slaves' services were *"particularly indispensable"* for carrying people on them |
| **"Camp town"** | ⭐ **Bernier's term** for towns he believed existed only while the imperial court was present — **refuted by NCERT** |
| ***Mahajan* / *sheth* / *nagarsheth*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Western Indian merchant bodies / their chief / the chief of the whole merchant community** *(as at Ahmedabad)* |
| ***Karkhana*** | ⭐ **An imperial workshop** — Bernier is *"perhaps the only historian"* to describe their working in detail |
| ***Hakim*/*vaid*, *pundit*/*mulla*, *wakil*** | **Physicians · teachers · lawyers** — the professional classes Bernier's *"no middle state"* claim ignores |
| **Oriental despotism** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MONTESQUIEU's idea**, built on Bernier — Asian rulers with **absolute authority**, subjects in **subjugation and poverty**, **all land the king's and private property non-existent** |
| **Asiatic mode of production** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MARX's development of it** — surplus appropriated by the state, **autonomous internally egalitarian village communities**, an imperial court above them, and **the whole regarded as STAGNANT** |
| **"Remunerations of sovereignty"** | ⭐ **ABU'L FAZL's description of land revenue** — a claim for **PROTECTION PROVIDED**, not rent on land owned |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in ten lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Travellers are valuable because they notice what insiders never bother to record** — *"routine matters, not worthy of being recorded"* by indigenous writers.
2. ⚠️ **And the whole surviving corpus is male:** *"we have practically no accounts of travel left by women, though we know that they travelled."*
3. **AL-BIRUNI (973–1048, Uzbekistan) came as a HOSTAGE**, learned Sanskrit, and wrote the ***Kitab-ul-Hind* in Arabic, 80 chapters**, each moving **question → Sanskritic account → comparison**.
4. ⭐ **He named THREE BARRIERS — language, religion, insularity — and then read India almost entirely through Brahmanical texts.** *But he rejected pollution as **"contrary to the laws of nature."***
5. **IBN BATTUTA (1304–77, Morocco) valued EXPERIENCE OVER BOOKS**, was **qazi of Delhi**, and **dictated the *Rihla* on a ruler's order** for *"entertainment to the mind."*
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He described the unfamiliar by anchoring it to the familiar** — the coconut as a date-palm bearing heads — **and gave us Delhi's twenty-eight gates, the bazaars, and the foot-post that beat the horse-post.**
7. **BERNIER (in India 1656–68) wrote for LOUIS XIV**, using **binary opposition ranked hierarchically**, and made **crown ownership of land** the single cause of everything he disliked.
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He was wrong** — Mughal documents claim no such ownership, **Abu'l Fazl calls revenue *"remunerations of sovereignty"*, and it was a TAX ON THE CROP, not rent.** *He mistook a high rate of extraction for a property regime.*
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the error travelled: Bernier → MONTESQUIEU's oriental despotism → MARX's Asiatic mode of production** — because **print carried him and left the Arabic and Persian accounts in manuscript until 1800.**
10. **NCERT's corrections stand against him**: **15% urban, above Western Europe**; ***mahajans*, *sheths* and *nagarsheths***; **a four-tier rural society**; and **women whose labour was crucial and who took commercial disputes to court** — none of which suited anyone's argument, and so went largely unrecorded.
