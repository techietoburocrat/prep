# Class XII — *Themes in Indian History, Part II*
## Medieval India · Travellers · Devotion · Empire

> **This is the ART & CULTURE and MEDIEVAL SOURCES book, and it has an unusual character: three of its four themes are organised around a KIND OF WITNESS rather than a period.** *Theme 5 is built on **foreign travellers**; Theme 6 on **devotional poetry and hagiography**; Theme 7 on **a ruined city plus travellers' accounts**; Theme 8 on **one official chronicle**. ⭐ **Read it as Part I's method applied to a better-documented age — and to sources that are, for the first time, often written by people who were THERE.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why losing *Kings and Chronicles* hurts more than it looks

**Theme 8 is built on the *Ain-i-Akbari* — and it repeatedly warns that the *Ain* was produced INSIDE the imperial court, for imperial purposes.** ⭐ **Old Theme 9 was where that court, its patronage, and its historians were actually explained.** *Without it, Theme 8's caution about its own source has lost its foundation, and the series loses its only treatment of **Mughal painting, the ideology of kingship, and the illustrated chronicle** — all of which Prelims asks about directly.

> 📌 **A supplement restoring it is included in this folder** *(see the status table below)*, following the precedent set for **Class 7, Class 9, Class 10, Class 11 and Class 12 Part III.**

## ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The organising idea: this book is about WHO IS LOOKING

**Part I named each theme after a KIND OF EVIDENCE. Part II names each after a KIND OF OBSERVER — and the observer's position is the problem every time.**

| Theme | Its subtitle | Who is looking | ⭐ The problem with them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | *Perceptions of Society* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FOREIGN TRAVELLERS** — al-Biruni, Ibn Battuta, Bernier | **Outsiders describing an unfamiliar society** — each measuring India against **home** |
| **6** | *Changes in Religious Beliefs and Devotional Texts* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **POETS, SAINTS AND THEIR BIOGRAPHERS** | **Hagiography, and compositions transmitted orally for centuries before being written** |
| **7** | *New Architecture: Hampi* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ARCHAEOLOGY + travellers' accounts** | **A city in ruins**, read partly through the eyes of **foreign visitors again** |
| **8** | *Agrarian Society and the Mughal Empire* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AN OFFICIAL CHRONICLE — the *AIN-I-AKBARI*** | **A view from the CENTRE, of a countryside it taxed** |
| ⚠️ **9** *(deleted)* | *The Mughal Courts* | ⭐ **COURT HISTORIANS the emperor commissioned** | **A chronicle is both a history AND an instrument of rule** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE THREAD THAT RUNS THROUGH ALL OF IT: EVERY SOURCE IN THIS BOOK WAS PRODUCED BY SOMEONE STANDING SOMEWHERE — a foreigner, a devotee, a courtier — AND THE CHAPTER ALWAYS TELLS YOU WHERE.** *That is the same discipline as Part I, but the sources are richer and the distortions are more interesting: **not "what does the evidence fail to record?" so much as "what did this particular observer's position let him see, and stop him seeing?"***

> 🔗 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE LINK TO PART III IS DIRECT.** *[Part III's Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) opens on **the colonial countryside and the Fifth Report** — an official record of an agrarian order, written by the people running it. **That is Theme 8's problem, two centuries later, in English.** **Read Theme 8 and Part III's Theme 9 as a pair and the whole of Class 12's agrarian history locks together.**

## 🗺️ Suggested reading orders

| If you have… | Read in this order | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Full time (recommended)** | **5 → 6 → 7 → 8 → Supplement** *(print order, supplement last)* | The travellers frame the whole book, and the supplement belongs after Theme 8 because it explains the court that produced the *Ain* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Prelims-first** | **7 → 6 → 8 → 5 → Supplement** | **Theme 7 (Vijayanagara) is the most Prelims-dense** — architectural terms and named structures; **Theme 6** is next for its named saints, orders and terms |
| **Mains / GS1 Art & Culture** | **6 → 7 → Supplement → 5 → 8** | ⭐ **Bhakti-Sufi and Vijayanagara are the two biggest GS1 Art & Culture chapters in Class 12**, and the supplement's Mughal painting completes the set |

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A note on weightage, stated honestly.** *Medieval India sits between Ancient (this book's predecessor) and Modern (Part III) in exam weight — **less than Modern, more than Ancient for Mains**. ⭐ **But Themes 6 and 7 are disproportionately valuable because they carry the ART & CULTURE syllabus**, which is a named GS1 item and which most candidates prepare badly. *Do not treat this book as filler between Harappa and the freedom struggle.*
