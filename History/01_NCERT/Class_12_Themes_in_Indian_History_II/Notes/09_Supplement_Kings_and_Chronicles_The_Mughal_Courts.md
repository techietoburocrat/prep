### 🧭 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ NCERT's own opening — the double character of a chronicle

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE OPENING PARAGRAPH STATES THE CHAPTER'S WHOLE THESIS **
   ================================================================
   ** "The rulers of the Mughal Empire saw themselves as
      ⭐ APPOINTED BY DIVINE WILL TO RULE OVER A LARGE AND
      HETEROGENEOUS POPULACE. Although this grand vision was often
      ⭐ CIRCUMSCRIBED BY ACTUAL POLITICAL CIRCUMSTANCES, IT
      REMAINED IMPORTANT. ONE WAY OF TRANSMITTING THIS VISION WAS
      THROUGH THE WRITING OF DYNASTIC HISTORIES." **

   ** "The Mughal kings COMMISSIONED COURT HISTORIANS to write
      accounts... In addition, their writers COLLECTED VAST AMOUNTS
      OF INFORMATION FROM THE REGIONS OF THE SUBCONTINENT TO HELP
      THE RULERS GOVERN THEIR DOMAIN." **

   ⭐ ** THE NAME: "MODERN HISTORIANS WRITING IN ENGLISH HAVE
      TERMED THIS GENRE OF TEXTS ** CHRONICLES ** , AS THEY PRESENT
      A CONTINUOUS CHRONOLOGICAL RECORD OF EVENTS." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE TWO-LEVEL VERDICT -- THE SINGLE MOST
      IMPORTANT PASSAGE IN THIS CHAPTER **
   ================================================================
   ** "AT ONE LEVEL they were ⭐ A REPOSITORY OF FACTUAL
      INFORMATION ABOUT THE INSTITUTIONS OF THE MUGHAL STATE,
      PAINSTAKINGLY COLLECTED AND CLASSIFIED BY INDIVIDUALS CLOSELY
      CONNECTED WITH THE COURT. " **

   ** "AT THE SAME TIME these texts were ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ INTENDED AS
      CONVEYORS OF MEANINGS THAT THE MUGHAL RULERS SOUGHT TO IMPOSE
      ON THEIR DOMAIN. THEY THEREFORE GIVE US A GLIMPSE INTO HOW
      IMPERIAL IDEOLOGIES WERE CREATED AND DISSEMINATED. " **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT PAIR OF SENTENCES IS WHY THIS CHAPTER MATTERS AND WHY DELETING IT DAMAGES THEME 8.** ⭐ **A chronicle is simultaneously *"a REPOSITORY OF FACTUAL INFORMATION"* and *"a CONVEYOR OF MEANINGS the rulers sought to IMPOSE."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It is not that some parts are true and others propaganda — the SAME text is both at once, because a state that wants to look well-governed has every reason to govern accurately AND to record it flatteringly.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the historian's question is never *"is the *Ain* reliable?"* but *"reliable FOR WHAT?"*** ⭐ **For revenue rates, measured area, the caste of zamindars, the number of *sarkars* — highly. For what the empire MEANT to a peasant in the Doab — worthless, and [Theme 8](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) had to go to petitions in the Rajasthan archives for that.** *(⭐ **Note the elegant symmetry with the rest of Part II: [Theme 5's](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md) traveller is an outsider looking in; the chronicler is an insider looking out — and NCERT applies exactly the same discipline to both.**)*

## 1️⃣ The Mughals and Their Empire ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "MUGHAL" IS NOT THEIR NAME -- a Prelims-perfect
      opening fact **
   ================================================================
   ** "The name MUGHAL DERIVES FROM MONGOL. Though today the term
      evokes the grandeur of an empire, ⭐ IT WAS NOT THE NAME THE
      RULERS OF THE DYNASTY CHOSE FOR THEMSELVES." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "THEY REFERRED TO THEMSELVES AS TIMURIDS, AS
      DESCENDANTS OF THE TURKISH RULER TIMUR ON THE PATERNAL SIDE.
      ⭐ BABUR, the first Mughal ruler, WAS RELATED TO GHENGHIZ KHAN
      FROM HIS MOTHER'S SIDE. HE SPOKE TURKISH AND ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
      REFERRED DERISIVELY TO THE MONGOLS AS BARBARIC HORDES." **

   ** "During the SIXTEENTH CENTURY, ⭐ EUROPEANS USED THE TERM
      MUGHAL to describe the Indian rulers of this branch of the
      family." ** -- and NCERT's charming aside: ** "even THE NAME
      MOWGLI, the young hero of Rudyard Kipling's Jungle Book, IS
      DERIVED FROM IT." **

   ⭐ ** CHAGHTAI TURKS "traced descent from THE ELDEST SON OF
      GHENGIZ KHAN." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE NAMING POINT IS A GENUINE INSIGHT, NOT TRIVIA, AND IT IS THE SORT OF THING THAT LIFTS AN ANSWER.** ⭐ **The dynasty that world history calls "Mughal" **rejected the word**: Babur used "Mongol" as an INSULT.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The name we use for the greatest Indian empire of the medieval period is **a European mistake about an ancestry its rulers were embarrassed by** — they claimed **TIMUR** on the father's side and were quiet about **Genghis Khan** on the mother's.** *(⭐ **And the reason is politics: Timur was a Muslim conqueror in the Persianate world; the Mongols were the pagan destroyers of Baghdad. **Descent is chosen, not merely inherited.** ⭐⭐ Compare **[Class 11 Theme 3](../../Class_11_Themes_in_World_History/Notes/03_Theme3_Nomadic_Empires.md)**, where NCERT notes Babur descends from BOTH — and that the Mongols gave later regimes their pluralist model.**)*

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE DYNASTIC SPINE -- the facts to have cold **
   ================================================================
   ** ZAHIRUDDIN BABUR ** -- "driven from his Central Asian
      homeland, ⭐ FARGHANA, BY THE WARRING UZBEKS. He first
      established himself AT KABUL and then IN 1526 pushed further
      into the Indian subcontinent ⭐ IN SEARCH OF TERRITORIES AND
      RESOURCES TO SATISFY THE NEEDS OF THE MEMBERS OF HIS CLAN."

   ** NASIRUDDIN HUMAYUN (1530-40, 1555-56) ** -- "expanded the
      frontiers... but LOST IT TO THE AFGHAN LEADER SHER SHAH SUR,
      who drove him into exile. ⭐ HUMAYUN TOOK REFUGE IN THE COURT
      OF THE SAFAVID RULER OF IRAN. IN 1555 HUMAYUN DEFEATED THE
      SURS, BUT DIED A YEAR LATER."

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** JALALUDDIN AKBAR (1556-1605) ** -- "MANY CONSIDER
      [him] THE GREATEST of all the Mughal emperors, for he NOT
      ONLY EXPANDED BUT ALSO CONSOLIDATED his empire, making it
      ⭐ THE LARGEST, STRONGEST AND RICHEST KINGDOM OF HIS TIME."
      He "extended the frontiers TO THE HINDUKUSH MOUNTAINS, and
      CHECKED THE EXPANSIONIST DESIGNS OF ⭐ THE UZBEKS OF TURAN
      (Central Asia) AND THE SAFAVIDS OF IRAN."

   ** JAHANGIR (1605-27) · SHAH JAHAN (1628-58) · AURANGZEB
      (1658-1707) ** -- ⭐ "THREE FAIRLY ABLE SUCCESSORS...
      MUCH AS THEIR CHARACTERS VARIED. Under them the territorial
      expansion continued, THOUGH AT A MUCH REDUCED PACE. The three
      rulers MAINTAINED AND CONSOLIDATED THE VARIOUS INSTRUMENTS OF
      GOVERNANCE."

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE COURT AS THE VISIBLE CENTRE -- and the sentence
      that defines the Mughal political method **
   ================================================================
   ** "THE VISIBLE CENTRE OF MUGHAL POWER WAS THE COURT. Here
      ⭐ POLITICAL ALLIANCES AND RELATIONSHIPS WERE FORGED, STATUS
      AND HIERARCHIES DEFINED." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "The political system devised by the Mughals was
      based on A COMBINATION OF MILITARY POWER AND ⭐ CONSCIOUS
      POLICY TO ACCOMMODATE THE DIFFERENT TRADITIONS THEY
      ENCOUNTERED IN THE SUBCONTINENT." **

   ================================================================
   ** AND THE AFTERLIFE -- the sentence that reaches 1857 **
   ================================================================
   ** "After 1707, following the death of Aurangzeb, THE POWER OF
      THE DYNASTY DIMINISHED. In place of the vast apparatus of
      empire controlled from DELHI, AGRA OR LAHORE -- the different
      capital cities -- REGIONAL POWERS ACQUIRED GREATER AUTONOMY.
      ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ YET SYMBOLICALLY THE PRESTIGE OF THE MUGHAL RULER DID
      NOT LOSE ITS AURA. IN 1857 THE LAST SCION OF THIS DYNASTY,
      BAHADUR SHAH ZAFAR II, WAS OVERTHROWN BY THE BRITISH." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"YET SYMBOLICALLY THE PRESTIGE OF THE MUGHAL RULER DID NOT LOSE ITS AURA"* IS THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT SENTENCE IN §1, AND IT IS THE BRIDGE TO 1857.** ⭐ **A century and a half after the empire had ceased to govern anything, its NAME still conferred legitimacy** — which is exactly why, in **[Part III Theme 10](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/10_Theme10_Rebels_and_the_Raj_1857.md)**, **the sepoys who had no shared politics of their own rode to Delhi and compelled a reluctant, elderly Bahadur Shah to lead them.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **They needed a name that everyone already recognised as sovereign — and the ideology this chapter describes is what had put it there.** *(⭐ **That is a superb link to make in a 1857 answer: the rebels' choice of figurehead is the last dividend of two centuries of chronicle-writing and court ritual.**)*

## 2️⃣ The Production of Chronicles ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE THREE PURPOSES -- learn them as a numbered
      list; Exercise 6 needs them **
   ================================================================
   ** "Chronicles commissioned by the Mughal emperors are an
      important source... They were written --" **

   ** 1. ** ⭐ "TO PROJECT A VISION OF AN ENLIGHTENED KINGDOM TO
      ALL THOSE WHO CAME UNDER ITS UMBRELLA "
   ** 2. ** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ "to CONVEY TO THOSE WHO RESISTED THE RULE OF
      THE MUGHALS THAT ALL RESISTANCE WAS DESTINED TO FAIL "
   ** 3. ** "the rulers wanted TO ENSURE THAT THERE WAS AN ACCOUNT
      OF THEIR RULE FOR POSTERITY "

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHO WROTE THEM, AND WHAT THEIR TITLES GIVE AWAY **
   ================================================================
   ** "The authors of Mughal chronicles were ⭐ INVARIABLY
      COURTIERS. The histories they wrote focused on events centred
      on THE RULER, HIS FAMILY, THE COURT AND NOBLES, WARS AND
      ADMINISTRATIVE ARRANGEMENTS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "Their titles, such as the AKBAR NAMA, SHAHJAHAN
      NAMA, ALAMGIR NAMA -- that is, THE STORY OF AKBAR, SHAH JAHAN
      AND ALAMGIR (a title of the Mughal ruler AURANGZEB) --
      ⭐ SUGGEST THAT IN THE EYES OF THEIR AUTHORS THE HISTORY OF
      THE EMPIRE AND THE COURT WAS SYNONYMOUS WITH THAT OF THE
      EMPEROR." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PURPOSE 2 IS THE ONE TO QUOTE, AND IT IS THE EXACT SENTENCE [THEME 8 §1.1](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) ECHOES WHEN IT SAYS RESISTANCE WAS *"PREDESTINED TO FAIL."*** ⭐ **A chronicle is not only a record for posterity; it is **addressed to the empire's opponents**, and its message is that opposition is futile.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is a political instrument disguised as a history book — and it is why Theme 8's peasants, who left no chronicles, had to be recovered from petitions instead.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE TITLES ARGUMENT IS A LOVELY PIECE OF METHOD, BECAUSE IT READS A BOOK'S EVIDENCE OFF ITS COVER.** ⭐ ***Akbar Nama* means "the story of AKBAR", not "the history of India in the sixteenth century."** ⭐ **NCERT's inference is that for these authors *"the history of the empire and the court was SYNONYMOUS WITH THAT OF THE EMPEROR."*** *So the genre's central silence is built into its title page: **there is no room in an* Akbar Nama *for a history in which Akbar is not the agent.** *(⭐ **This is the same argument [Theme 8 §8](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) makes about the *Ain*'s eight columns — the FORM of the source announces its blind spots before you read a word.**)*

### 2.1 From Turkish to Persian ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE SEQUENCE -- three languages, one deliberate policy **
   ================================================================
   ** "Mughal court chronicles were written IN PERSIAN. ⭐ Under
      the SULTANS OF DELHI it flourished as a language of the court
      and of literary writings, ALONGSIDE NORTH INDIAN LANGUAGES,
      ESPECIALLY HINDAVI AND ITS REGIONAL VARIANTS." **

   ** "As the Mughals were CHAGHTAI TURKS by origin, ⭐ TURKISH WAS
      THEIR MOTHER TONGUE. Their first ruler BABUR WROTE POETRY AND
      HIS MEMOIRS IN THIS LANGUAGE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "IT WAS AKBAR WHO CONSCIOUSLY SET OUT TO MAKE PERSIAN
      THE LEADING LANGUAGE OF THE MUGHAL COURT." **

   ** WHY: "CULTURAL AND INTELLECTUAL CONTACTS WITH IRAN, as well
      as ⭐ A REGULAR STREAM OF IRANIAN AND CENTRAL ASIAN MIGRANTS
      SEEKING POSITIONS AT THE MUGHAL COURT, might have motivated
      the emperor to adopt the language." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHAT IT DID -- language as an instrument of power **
   ================================================================
   * ** "Persian was ELEVATED TO A LANGUAGE OF EMPIRE, ⭐ CONFERRING
     POWER AND PRESTIGE ON THOSE WHO HAD A COMMAND OF IT." **
   * ** "It was spoken by THE KING, THE ROYAL HOUSEHOLD AND THE
     ELITE AT COURT." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "Further, it became THE LANGUAGE OF ADMINISTRATION
     AT ALL LEVELS SO THAT ACCOUNTANTS, CLERKS AND OTHER
     FUNCTIONARIES ALSO LEARNT IT." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE TRAFFIC RAN BOTH WAYS -- this is the part
      most students miss **
   ================================================================
   ** "Even when Persian was NOT DIRECTLY USED, ⭐ ITS VOCABULARY
      AND IDIOM HEAVILY INFLUENCED THE LANGUAGE OF OFFICIAL RECORDS
      IN RAJASTHANI AND MARATHI AND EVEN TAMIL." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "Since the people using Persian... CAME FROM MANY
      DIFFERENT REGIONS of the subcontinent and SPOKE OTHER INDIAN
      LANGUAGES, ⭐ PERSIAN TOO BECAME INDIANISED BY ABSORBING LOCAL
      IDIOMS. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A NEW LANGUAGE, URDU, SPRANG FROM THE
      INTERACTION OF PERSIAN WITH HINDAVI." **

   ================================================================
   ** THE TRANSLATION PROGRAMME -- and one title is Prelims-certain **
   ================================================================
   * ** "Babur's memoirs were TRANSLATED FROM THE TURKISH INTO THE
     PERSIAN BABUR NAMA." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "TRANSLATIONS OF SANSKRIT TEXTS SUCH AS THE
     MAHABHARATA AND THE RAMAYANA INTO PERSIAN WERE COMMISSIONED BY
     THE MUGHAL EMPERORS. ⭐ THE MAHABHARATA WAS TRANSLATED AS THE
     RAZMNAMA ( BOOK OF WARS )." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE PERSIAN STORY IS A COMPLETE MAINS ANSWER ON LANGUAGE AND STATE POWER, AND IT HAS FOUR MOVEMENTS. LEARN IT AS A SEQUENCE:**
> - ⭐ **(i) ADOPTION AS POLICY** — Persian was **not the dynasty's mother tongue**; **Akbar CONSCIOUSLY chose it**, for reasons of Iranian contact and migration.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) PENETRATION DOWNWARD** — from the king and the elite to **"accountants, clerks and other functionaries" at ALL LEVELS.** *That is how a court language becomes a career requirement, and how a service class is created.*
> - ⭐ **(iii) DIFFUSION SIDEWAYS** — Persian **vocabulary and idiom entering Rajasthani, Marathi and even Tamil official records**, i.e. **into the paperwork of states the Mughals did not rule.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iv) TRANSFORMATION** — **Persian was Indianised in the process, and URDU was born of Persian meeting Hindavi.**
> - 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The conclusion worth writing: *the Mughals imposed a language and were changed by it.* **They did not Persianise India so much as produce a hybrid** — and the ***RAZMNAMA*, a Persian *Mahabharata* commissioned by a Muslim emperor,** is that hybrid in a single volume. *(⭐ **This is [Theme 6's](06_Theme6_Bhakti_Sufi_Traditions.md) "integration" and [Theme 7's](07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) *"Hindu Suratrana"* in a third register — and note that Persian itself came to India via the Ghaznavids from **[the Central Islamic Lands](../../Class_11_Themes_in_World_History/Notes/08_Supplement_Industrial_Revolution_Islamic_Lands_and_Confrontation.md)**.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "FLIGHT OF THE WRITTEN WORD" BOX — Abu'l Fazl on writing itself, and it is quotable in an Essay paper:** *"**THE WRITTEN WORD MAY EMBODY THE WISDOM OF BYGONE AGES** and may become a means to intellectual progress. **THE SPOKEN WORD GOES TO THE HEART OF THOSE WHO ARE PRESENT TO HEAR IT. THE WRITTEN WORD GIVES WISDOM TO THOSE WHO ARE NEAR AND FAR.** ⭐ **IF IT WAS NOT FOR THE WRITTEN WORD, THE SPOKEN WORD WOULD SOON DIE, AND NO KEEPSAKE WOULD BE LEFT US FROM THOSE WHO ARE PASSED AWAY.** Superficial observers see in the letter a dark figure, but **THE DEEP-SIGHTED SEE IN IT A LAMP OF WISDOM (*CHIRAG-I SHINASAI*)**… A letter (*khat*) is **THE PORTRAIT OF WISDOM; a rough sketch from the realm of ideas; A DARK LIGHT USHERING IN DAY; a black cloud pregnant with knowledge; SPEAKING THOUGH DUMB; STATIONARY YET TRAVELLING**; stretched on the sheet, and yet soaring upwards."*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Notice what the man who wrote the *Akbar Nama* thinks writing DOES: it defeats distance *("those who are near and far")* and it defeats death *("no keepsake would be left us from those who are passed away")*.** ⭐ **Both are exactly what an empire wants** — *reach and permanence* — **and both are the stated purposes of the chronicles in §2.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **And set this against [Theme 6's](06_Theme6_Bhakti_Sufi_Traditions.md) bhakti poetry, transmitted ORALLY for centuries and compiled long after its authors died. **Two traditions, two theories of what survives — and the one with the scriptorium is the one whose version we have in the most detail.**)*

Two of them — **calligraphy and painting** — carried **titles, awards and high social standing**; one calligrapher was named ***ZARRIN QALAM*, "golden pen"**, and **signed his own work across a quarter of the page.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The paper makers and bookbinders *"have remained ANONYMOUS ARTISANS"* — and without paper there is no book at all.**

What kind of wages did they get?"*** ⭐ **A Mughal manuscript and a Vijayanagara temple have the same evidentiary shape: **the designer is named and the labour is not.*** *(⭐ **And the reason is the same in both cases — the crafts that produced VISIBLE INDIVIDUAL STYLE got names; the crafts that produced UNIFORM MATERIAL did not.**)*

## 3️⃣ The Painted Image ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** HOW IMAGE AND TEXT WERE PHYSICALLY COMBINED -- a lovely
      concrete detail **
   ================================================================
   ** "Chronicles narrating the events of a Mughal emperor's reign
      contained, ALONGSIDE THE WRITTEN TEXT, IMAGES THAT DESCRIBED
      AN EVENT IN VISUAL FORM. ⭐ When scenes or themes in a book
      were to be given visual expression, THE SCRIBE LEFT BLANK
      SPACES ON NEARBY PAGES; PAINTINGS, EXECUTED SEPARATELY BY
      ARTISTS, WERE INSERTED to accompany what was described in
      words." **

   ⭐ ** "These paintings were MINIATURES, and could therefore BE
      PASSED AROUND FOR VIEWING AND MOUNTING ON THE PAGES OF
      MANUSCRIPTS." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHY PAINTINGS MATTERED -- NCERT'S CLAIM, AND IT IS
      STRONG **
   ================================================================
   ** "Paintings served NOT ONLY to enhance the beauty of a book,
      but ⭐ WERE BELIEVED TO POSSESS SPECIAL POWERS OF
      COMMUNICATING IDEAS ABOUT THE KINGDOM AND THE POWER OF KINGS
      ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ IN WAYS THAT THE WRITTEN MEDIUM COULD NOT." **

   ** "The historian ABU'L FAZL described painting as ⭐ A 'MAGICAL
      ART': in his view IT HAD THE POWER TO MAKE INANIMATE OBJECTS
      LOOK AS IF THEY POSSESSED LIFE." **

   ================================================================
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THAT IS EXACTLY WHY IT WAS CONTROVERSIAL --
      the ULAMA's objection, stated precisely **
   ================================================================
   ** "The production of paintings portraying the emperor, his
      court and the people who were part of it, was ⭐ A SOURCE OF
      CONSTANT TENSION BETWEEN RULERS AND REPRESENTATIVES OF THE
      MUSLIM ORTHODOXY, THE ULAMA." **

   ** THE GROUNDS: "The latter did not fail to invoke ⭐ THE ISLAMIC
      PROHIBITION OF THE PORTRAYAL OF HUMAN BEINGS ENSHRINED IN THE
      QUR'AN AS WELL AS THE HADIS, which described an incident from
      the life of the Prophet Muhammad. Here the Prophet is cited
      as having FORBIDDEN THE DEPICTION OF LIVING BEINGS IN A
      NATURALISTIC MANNER ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ AS IT WOULD SUGGEST THAT THE
      ARTIST WAS SEEKING TO APPROPRIATE THE POWER OF CREATION. THIS
      WAS A FUNCTION THAT WAS BELIEVED TO BELONG EXCLUSIVELY TO
      GOD." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOTICE THE COLLISION, BECAUSE IT IS PERFECT AND NCERT SETS IT UP DELIBERATELY: THE ORTHODOX OBJECTION AND ABU'L FAZL'S PRAISE ARE THE *SAME CLAIM*, WITH OPPOSITE SIGNS.** ⭐ **The *ulama* say a naturalistic image implies **the artist is appropriating God's power of creation.** Abu'l Fazl praises painting because *"even inanimate objects look as if they have life."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Both agree that a good painting seems to CREATE LIFE. They disagree only about whether that is blasphemy or genius.**

**📖 SOURCE 1 — *In praise of taswir*: ABU'L FAZL ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"**DRAWING THE LIKENESS OF ANYTHING IS CALLED *TASWIR*.** His Majesty **from his earliest youth, has shown A GREAT PREDILECTION FOR THIS ART, and gives it every encouragement**, as he looks upon it as **A MEANS BOTH OF STUDY AND AMUSEMENT.** ⭐ **A VERY LARGE NUMBER OF PAINTERS HAVE BEEN SET TO WORK. EACH WEEK, SEVERAL SUPERVISORS AND CLERKS OF THE IMPERIAL WORKSHOP SUBMIT BEFORE THE EMPEROR THE WORK DONE BY EACH ARTIST, AND HIS MAJESTY GIVES A REWARD AND INCREASES THE MONTHLY SALARIES OF THE ARTISTS ACCORDING TO THE EXCELLENCE DISPLAYED.**
>
> *… Most excellent painters are now to be found, and **MASTERPIECES, WORTHY OF A BIHZAD, MAY BE PLACED AT THE SIDE OF THE WONDERFUL WORKS OF THE EUROPEAN PAINTERS** who have attained worldwide fame. **THE MINUTENESS IN DETAIL, THE GENERAL FINISH AND THE BOLDNESS OF EXECUTION** now observed in pictures are incomparable; ⭐ **EVEN INANIMATE OBJECTS LOOK AS IF THEY HAVE LIFE. MORE THAN A HUNDRED PAINTERS HAVE BECOME FAMOUS MASTERS OF THE ART.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS ESPECIALLY TRUE OF THE HINDU ARTISTS. THEIR PICTURES SURPASS OUR CONCEPTION OF THINGS. FEW, INDEED, IN THE WHOLE WORLD ARE FOUND EQUAL TO THEM.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT ASKS: *"WHY did Abu'l Fazl consider the art of painting important? HOW DID HE SEEK TO LEGITIMISE THIS ART?"* — AND THE SECOND HALF IS THE EXAM QUESTION. HE LEGITIMISES IT FOUR WAYS, AND EVERY ONE IS A DEFENCE AGAINST THE *ULAMA*:**
> - ⭐ **(i) BY THE EMPEROR'S OWN PRACTICE AND MOTIVE** — Akbar has favoured it *"from his earliest youth"*, and treats it as ***"a means both of STUDY and AMUSEMENT"***. *Framing it as study makes it sober, not idolatrous.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) BY BUREAUCRATISING IT** — *"supervisors and clerks of the imperial workshop"* submit each artist's work **weekly**, and salaries rise **according to excellence.** *This is the language of a government department, not a temptation.*
> - ⭐ **(iii) BY INTERNATIONAL COMPARISON** — the Mughal painters stand beside ***BIHZAD*** *(the great Safavid master)* and beside **the European painters of worldwide fame**. *Prestige by association with the Persianate and Christian worlds alike.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iv) BY PRAISING THE HINDU ARTISTS ABOVE ALL** — *"few, indeed, in the whole world are found equal to them."* ⭐ **That single sentence is *SULH-I KUL* applied to an art workshop, and it is aimed straight at the orthodoxy that objected to the workshop's existence.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND AKBAR'S OWN ANSWER, WHICH IS THE FINEST LINE IN THE CHAPTER — quote it verbatim:** *"**THERE ARE MANY THAT HATE PAINTING, BUT SUCH MEN I DISLIKE. IT APPEARS TO ME THAT AN ARTIST HAS A UNIQUE WAY OF RECOGNISING GOD WHEN HE MUST COME TO FEEL THAT HE CANNOT BESTOW LIFE ON HIS WORK…**"*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read the argument carefully, because it is genuinely clever and it INVERTS the objection rather than dodging it.** ⭐ **The *ulama* say: painting a living being is an attempt to usurp God's power of creation.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Akbar replies: the painter is the ONE man who learns, at his own workbench and against his own will, that **he cannot give life** — so painting is not a rival to God's creation, **it is a daily lesson in the limits of human power, and therefore a route to recognising God.*** *(⭐ **This is theology deployed in defence of state patronage, and it tells you something about how Akbar's court argued: not by ignoring the orthodoxy, but by out-reasoning it on its own ground.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "TRAVELS OF THE *BADSHAH NAMA*" BOX — a small story that is worth a great deal, and it is a ready-made Essay paragraph on heritage:** *"**GIFTING OF PRECIOUS MANUSCRIPTS WAS AN ESTABLISHED DIPLOMATIC CUSTOM UNDER THE MUGHALS.** In emulation of this, **THE NAWAB OF AWADH GIFTED THE ILLUSTRATED *BADSHAH NAMA* TO KING GEORGE III IN 1799.** Since then it has been preserved in **THE ENGLISH ROYAL COLLECTIONS, now at WINDSOR CASTLE.** ⭐ In **1994**, conservation work required the bound manuscript **to be taken apart. This made it possible to EXHIBIT the paintings**, and in **1997**, for the first time, the *Badshah Nama* paintings were shown in exhibitions in **NEW DELHI, LONDON AND WASHINGTON.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three things in that box, and they are worth separating.** ⭐ **(i) A MUGHAL CUSTOM WAS USED AGAINST THE MUGHALS' HEIRS: the Nawab of Awadh gifted the emperor's own official history to a foreign king, *"in emulation of"* the Mughal practice of diplomatic manuscript-gifting.** *In 1799 — with the Company ascendant — the object that certified Shah Jahan's sovereignty had become a diplomatic present to George III.* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) It took an accident of CONSERVATION to make the paintings visible: a bound manuscript can be read by one person at a time, and only when it was UNBOUND in 1994 could it be exhibited.** ⭐ **(iii) And the paintings came to New Delhi in 1997 — as visitors, on loan.**
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Set that beside the last line of §4: *"the text in its entirety STILL AWAITS TRANSLATION."*** ⭐ **The official history of Shah Jahan's reign — one of the two most important Mughal chronicles — has never been fully translated into English.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Which means part of what we "know" about Shah Jahan rests on excerpts chosen by somebody else. *A gap in translation is a gap in the historiography, and it is still open.***

### 5.1 A divine light — ***FARR-I IZADI*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```

   ** THE LEGEND -- and it is a Prelims-shaped story **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND HOW IT WAS PAINTED -- a superb Art & Culture

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TRACE THE HALO'S JOURNEY AND YOU HAVE ONE OF THE BEST SINGLE POINTS IN CLASS 12 ART & CULTURE.** ⭐ **A philosophical idea from **PLATO'S *REPUBLIC*** reaches a **twelfth-century Iranian Sufi**, is taught to **Shaikh Mubarak**, passes to **his son Abu'l Fazl**, becomes **the official theory of Mughal sovereignty** — and is then given a visual form **borrowed from EUROPEAN CHRISTIAN PAINTINGS of Christ and the Virgin Mary**, brought to the court by **Jesuit missions (§9.3)**.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A Greek idea, a Muslim philosopher, a Christian icon, an Indian emperor. That is what "composite culture" looks like when you follow one motif through it.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note the practical point about dating: **the halo appears in Mughal painting FROM THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY ONWARDS**, i.e. Jahangir and after — **which is a usable dating criterion for a miniature**, and exactly the sort of thing a Prelims question about Mughal painting can turn on.**

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FIG. 9.5 — *JAHANGIR HOLDING A PORTRAIT OF AKBAR*, by ABU'L HASAN. NCERT tells you how to read it, and the reading is worth memorising:**
> - ⭐ **Jahangir is *"dressed in RESPLENDENT CLOTHES AND JEWELS, HOLDING UP A PORTRAIT OF HIS FATHER AKBAR."***
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"AKBAR IS DRESSED IN WHITE, ASSOCIATED IN SUFI TRADITIONS WITH THE ENLIGHTENED SOUL. HE PROFFERS A GLOBE, SYMBOLIC OF DYNASTIC AUTHORITY."***
> - ⚠️ **And the political fact NCERT places directly beneath it:** *"In the Mughal empire there was **NO LAW LAYING DOWN WHICH OF THE EMPEROR'S SONS WOULD SUCCEED** to the throne. This meant that **EVERY DYNASTIC CHANGE WAS ACCOMPANIED AND DECIDED BY A FRATRICIDAL WAR. Towards the end of Akbar's reign, PRINCE SALIM REVOLTED AGAINST HIS FATHER, SEIZED POWER AND ASSUMED THE TITLE OF JAHANGIR.**"*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE JUXTAPOSITION IS THE WHOLE LESSON, AND IT IS THE ANSWER TO NCERT'S QUESTION *"How does this painting describe the relationship between father and son?"*** ⭐ **The painting shows a devoted son reverently holding his father's portrait and receiving the globe of dynastic authority from him.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The history is that this son **REVOLTED against that father, seized power, and had his father's historian MURDERED.*** *The painting is not a record of the relationship; **it is a repair of it** — a claim of legitimate succession made in paint, precisely because there was no law of succession and the succession had in fact been violent.
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And NCERT's other two questions have technical answers worth knowing: *"Why do you think Mughal artists frequently portrayed emperors against DARK OR DULL BACKGROUNDS? What are the SOURCES OF LIGHT in this painting?"*** ⭐ **Because the emperor must be the source of light, not merely lit. A dark ground makes the halo and the white robe RADIATE.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The *farr-i izadi* is not stated in the painting; it is arranged as an optical effect.** *(⭐ **The same device is used in **Fig. 9.7**, where the artist *"enveloped the target in a DARK CLOUD."*)*

### 5.2 A unifying force — ***SULH-I KUL*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE DEFINITION -- learn it exactly, INCLUDING THE
      CONDITION, which most students drop **
   ================================================================
   ** "Mughal chronicles present the empire as comprising MANY
      DIFFERENT ETHNIC AND RELIGIOUS COMMUNITIES -- ⭐ HINDUS,
      JAINAS, ZOROASTRIANS AND MUSLIMS. As the source of all peace
      and stability ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE EMPEROR STOOD ABOVE ALL RELIGIOUS
      AND ETHNIC GROUPS, MEDIATED AMONG THEM, AND ENSURED THAT

   ** "Abu'l Fazl describes the ideal of ⭐ SULH-I KUL ( ABSOLUTE
      PEACE ) AS THE CORNERSTONE OF ENLIGHTENED RULE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE CONDITION: "In sulh-i kul ALL RELIGIONS AND
      SCHOOLS OF THOUGHT HAD FREEDOM OF EXPRESSION ⭐ BUT ON

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** HOW IT WAS IMPLEMENTED -- four concrete state

   ================================================================

   ** 2. ABOLITION OF THE PILGRIMAGE TAX -- 1563 **
   ** 3. ABOLITION OF JIZYA -- 1564 **
      ⭐ "as the two were BASED ON RELIGIOUS DISCRIMINATION"

   ** 4. ADMINISTRATIVE INSTRUCTION ** -- ⭐ "INSTRUCTIONS WERE SENT
      TO OFFICERS OF THE EMPIRE TO FOLLOW THE PRECEPT OF SULH-I KUL
      IN ADMINISTRATION."

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE GRANTS -- with NCERT'S OWN HONEST
      QUALIFICATION AT THE END. Do not quote the first half without

   ================================================================
   ** "ALL MUGHAL EMPERORS GAVE GRANTS TO SUPPORT THE BUILDING AND
      MAINTENANCE OF PLACES OF WORSHIP. ⭐ EVEN WHEN TEMPLES WERE

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "HOWEVER, DURING THE REIGN OF THE LATTER,

```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***SULH-I KUL* IS THE HIGHEST-VALUE TERM IN THIS SUPPLEMENT, AND THE COMMONEST MISTAKE IS TO TRANSLATE IT AS "TOLERANCE" AND STOP.** ⭐ **It literally means **"ABSOLUTE PEACE"**, and NCERT's definition contains a CONDITION that is not a footnote: freedom of expression for all religions and schools of thought, *"**BUT ON CONDITION THAT THEY DID NOT UNDERMINE THE AUTHORITY OF THE STATE OR FIGHT AMONG THEMSELVES.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So *sulh-i kul* is a doctrine of SOVEREIGNTY as much as of religion.** ⭐ **The emperor *"stood ABOVE all religious and ethnic groups, MEDIATED among them"* — and a mediator who is above all parties is, by that fact, **more powerful than any of them.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Placing the crown outside every religious community is what makes the crown the only thing every community must deal with.** *That is why the doctrine and the composite nobility arrive together: **a nobility of Iranis, Turanis, Afghans, Rajputs and Deccanis, promoted purely on service and loyalty, cannot combine against the emperor** — a point §8.1 makes explicitly.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND WRITE THE ANSWER HONESTLY, BECAUSE NCERT DOES.** ⭐ **The evidence FOR: two discriminatory taxes abolished in **1563** and **1564**; a composite nobility; instructions to officers; grants to places of worship by **ALL** emperors; **temples repaired at state expense after being destroyed in war, under SHAH JAHAN AND AURANGZEB.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The evidence AGAINST, in the same paragraph: **AURANGZEB RE-IMPOSED THE *JIZYA*.*** *An answer that gives only one side is not being generous — it is being incomplete, and NCERT's own text will contradict it.* *(⭐ **Note also the internal tension: temples were being **destroyed during war** and **repaired by grant** under the same rulers. **The state's behaviour toward religion was neither uniformly protective nor uniformly hostile — it varied with war, politics and reign.**)*

### 5.3 Just sovereignty as a SOCIAL CONTRACT ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE FOUR ESSENCES -- MEMORISE ALL FOUR WITH THEIR
      PERSIAN/ARABIC NAMES. This is the single most quotable
      formulation of Mughal political theory. **
   ================================================================
   ** "ABU'L FAZL DEFINED SOVEREIGNTY AS ⭐ A SOCIAL CONTRACT: THE
      EMPEROR PROTECTS THE FOUR ESSENCES OF HIS SUBJECTS, NAMELY --" **

        ** JAN **     -> ⭐ LIFE
        ** MAL **     -> PROPERTY
        ** NAMUS **   -> HONOUR
        ** DIN **     -> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ FAITH

   ** "-- AND IN RETURN DEMANDS ⭐ OBEDIENCE AND A SHARE OF
      RESOURCES." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "ONLY JUST SOVEREIGNS WERE THOUGHT TO BE ABLE TO
      HONOUR THE CONTRACT WITH POWER AND DIVINE GUIDANCE." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE VISUAL VOCABULARY OF JUSTICE **
   ================================================================
   ** "A number of SYMBOLS WERE CREATED FOR VISUAL REPRESENTATION
      OF THE IDEA OF JUSTICE ⭐ WHICH CAME TO STAND FOR THE HIGHEST
      VIRTUE OF MUGHAL MONARCHY." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE LION AND THE LAMB: "One of the favourite symbols
      used by artists was THE MOTIF OF THE LION AND THE LAMB (OR
      GOAT) PEACEFULLY NESTLING NEXT TO EACH OTHER. This was meant
      to signify ⭐ A REALM WHERE BOTH THE STRONG AND THE WEAK COULD
      EXIST IN HARMONY." **

   ⭐ ** WHERE IT WAS PLACED: "Court scenes from the illustrated
      Badshah Nama place such motifs ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ IN A NICHE DIRECTLY
      BELOW THE EMPEROR'S THRONE." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FOUR ESSENCES ARE THE MOST STRIKING THING IN THIS CHAPTER FOR A MODERN READER, AND YOU SHOULD SAY WHY IN AN ANSWER: A SIXTEENTH-CENTURY COURTIER DEFINED SOVEREIGNTY AS A *CONTRACT WITH RECIPROCAL OBLIGATIONS*.** ⭐ **The emperor owes the protection of **LIFE, PROPERTY, HONOUR and FAITH**; the subject owes **obedience and a share of resources.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is a bargain with terms on both sides — and it is roughly contemporary with, and independent of, the European social-contract tradition that produced Hobbes (1651) and Locke (1689).**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note what the fourth essence is: *DIN*, FAITH.** ⭐ **The emperor's contractual duty includes protecting his subjects' religion — which is *sulh-i kul* restated as an OBLIGATION rather than a policy, and it is why the two ideas belong in the same section.** *(⭐ **A useful modern comparison, and a fair one for an Essay: **life, property, honour and faith** map remarkably closely onto **Articles 21, 300A and 25** of the Indian Constitution. **Do not overstate it — this was a theory of a duty owed by an absolute ruler, not a justiciable right — but the resemblance is real and it makes the point that ideas of what a state OWES its people are not a modern import.**)*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE PLACEMENT OF THE LION-AND-LAMB MOTIF IS THE POINT, NOT THE MOTIF ITSELF.** ⭐ **It sits *"in a NICHE DIRECTLY BELOW THE EMPEROR'S THRONE."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The claim being made architecturally is that **the throne is what holds the strong and the weak in harmony** — remove it and the lion eats the lamb.** *That is a visual argument for absolutism, made in the language of gentleness.*

**📖 SOURCE — *The Chain of Justice*: JAHANGIR, in his own memoirs ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **Fig. 9.7 — *JAHANGIR SHOOTING THE FIGURE OF POVERTY*, by ABU'L HASAN.** *NCERT's caption teaches you a term: *"the artist has **ENVELOPED THE TARGET IN A DARK CLOUD to suggest that THIS IS NOT A REAL PERSON, but a human form used to symbolise AN ABSTRACT QUALITY.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Such a mode of personification in art and literature is termed ALLEGORY.**"* *"**THE CHAIN OF JUSTICE IS SHOWN DESCENDING FROM HEAVEN.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And Jahangir's own description:** *"**AFTER MY ACCESSION, THE FIRST ORDER THAT I GAVE WAS FOR THE FASTENING UP OF THE CHAIN OF JUSTICE**, so that **IF THOSE ENGAGED IN THE ADMINISTRATION OF JUSTICE SHOULD DELAY OR PRACTISE HYPOCRISY** in the matter of those seeking justice, **THE OPPRESSED MIGHT COME TO THIS CHAIN AND SHAKE IT SO THAT ITS NOISE MIGHT ATTRACT ATTENTION.** ⭐ **The chain was made of PURE GOLD, 30 *GAZ* IN LENGTH AND CONTAINING 60 BELLS.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ THE CHAIN OF JUSTICE CAREFULLY, BECAUSE ITS DESIGN CONTAINS ITS POLITICS AND THAT IS WHAT NCERT'S QUESTION *"Summarise the message of this painting"* is after.** ⭐ **The chain does not bypass injustice in general — it bypasses **THE EMPEROR'S OWN OFFICIALS**: it is for those cases where *"those engaged in the administration of justice should **DELAY OR PRACTISE HYPOCRISY**."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It asserts that the subject has **direct access to the sovereign, over the heads of the entire bureaucracy** — which is precisely the claim that the ***JHAROKA DARSHAN*** makes in §6.2, and precisely the claim the hunt makes in [Theme 8 §4.2](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md).**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three details reward attention.** ⭐ **(i) It was *"THE FIRST ORDER"* of the reign — an accession statement, not an administrative measure.** ⭐ **(ii) It was of **PURE GOLD, 30 *gaz* long, with 60 BELLS** — and note the paradox: **an instrument for the poor to summon the emperor, made of gold.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) In the painting, the chain **DESCENDS FROM HEAVEN** — so the emperor's justice is not shown as an administrative arrangement but as *farr-i izadi* made into hardware.**
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And ***ALLEGORY*** is a term to keep — an abstract quality given human form.** *Jahangir is not shooting a man; **he is shooting POVERTY**, and the dark cloud is the painter's signal that you must not read the image literally.* *(⭐ **Which raises the question NCERT wants you to sit with: an emperor who commissions a picture of himself abolishing poverty with an arrow is making a claim, not a report. **Compare [Theme 7's](07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) statue of Krishnadeva Raya against Paes's note that he was fat and pockmarked.**)*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's "Discuss…" box asks *"WHY was justice regarded as such an important virtue of monarchy in the Mughal Empire?"* — and §5.3 has already answered it: because sovereignty was defined as A CONTRACT, and *"ONLY JUST SOVEREIGNS were thought to be able to honour the contract."*** ⭐ **Justice is not one royal virtue among several; **it is the term on which the emperor's side of the bargain is judged**, and therefore the ground of his legitimacy.** *(⭐⭐ **And it connects downward to [Theme 8's](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) petitioners, who regarded the panchayat as *"the court of appeal that would ensure that THE STATE CARRIED OUT ITS MORAL OBLIGATIONS AND GUARANTEED JUSTICE."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The peasants of Rajasthan and the ideologue of the court were, remarkably, using the same theory of what a ruler is for — one in an archive of grievances, the other in a golden chain with sixty bells.**)*

A treaty lapses; a marriage produces **grandchildren**, and the direction of the gift — daughters going TO the Mughals — encodes who is senior.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's conclusion is that this kinship network *"HELPED TO HOLD A VAST EMPIRE TOGETHER"* — so the imperial household was **an instrument of statecraft on the same footing as the *mansab* system**, and §8.1's first Rajput recruit, **Raja Bharmal Kachhwaha of Amber, "to whose daughter Akbar got married,"** is the two systems meeting in one sentence.

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GULBADAN BEGUM IS THE MOST VALUABLE SINGLE FACT IN THIS SUPPLEMENT, AND YOU SHOULD BE ABLE TO SAY WHY IN THREE SENTENCES.** ⭐ **She is **a woman writing history in the Mughal court** — Babur's daughter, Humayun's sister, Akbar's aunt, fluent in Turkish and Persian — **commissioned by the emperor himself as a SOURCE for Abu'l Fazl.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And what she produced was *"NO EULOGY"*: she wrote about **the conflicts and tensions among princes and kings**, and about **the elderly women of the family who mediated them.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SET HER BESIDE ABU'L FAZL AND THE CONTRAST IS THE WHOLE LESSON OF THIS CHAPTER IN ONE PAIR OF WRITERS.** ⭐ **Abu'l Fazl was **commissioned to write** *"the account of the GLORIOUS EVENTS and of our DOMINION-CONQUERING VICTORIES"*, revised his draft five times, and had it **recited in the royal hearing.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gulbadan was asked only to **remember**, and she remembered the quarrels.** *Same court, same commission, same decade — and a completely different history, because **she was inside the family rather than inside the ideology.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And her subject matter is itself evidence: *"the important MEDIATING ROLE elderly women of the family played in resolving some of these conflicts."*** ⭐ **In a dynasty with **no law of succession**, where *"every dynastic change was accompanied and decided by a FRATRICIDAL WAR"* (§5.1), the people who talked princes down were **the senior women of the household** — and **the only reason we know that is that one of them wrote it down.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **This is the exact counterpart of [Theme 8's](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) erased petitioners: there, women appear in the record only as "the mother, sister or wife of" a man; here, a woman writes the record — and immediately the record contains something no chronicle has.**)*
>
> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE EXERCISE 3 SKELETON — *"Assess the role played by women of the imperial household"* — in five points, each with its evidence:**
> - ⭐ **(i) DIPLOMATIC** — marriage as alliance; **the gift of territory accompanied by the gift of a daughter**; the kinship network that *"helped to hold a vast empire together."*
> - ⭐ **(ii) STRUCTURAL** — the *begam*/*agha*/*aghacha* hierarchy, ***mahr***, monthly cash allowances, **and genuine upward mobility through motherhood.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) ECONOMIC** — **after Nur Jahan**, queens and princesses controlled real resources; **Jahanara and Roshanara's income equalled that of high *mansabdars*; Jahanara held the revenues of SURAT.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iv) ARCHITECTURAL AND URBAN** — Jahanara's **double-storeyed caravanserai**, and **CHANDNI CHOWK, designed by her.** *The commercial heart of Shahjahanabad was laid out by a princess.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(v) POLITICAL AND INTELLECTUAL** — **Nur Jahan, *"politically influential"* (§8.1), under whom Iranians gained high office**; the **mediating elders**; and **Gulbadan Begum, historian.**
> - 🎯 ⭐ **Close with the limit, honestly: this was power exercised WITHIN a household defined by polygamy, concubinage and slavery, and it belonged to women of the very highest rank.** *(⭐⭐ **And note the *KHWAJASARA*, who acted as *"AGENTS FOR WOMEN DABBLING IN COMMERCE"* — proof that the household's women were trading, and that they needed intermediaries to do it.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DARA SHUKOH'S WEDDING (1633) — a box worth reading for its FINANCES, which are more revealing than its pageantry:** *The wedding of **DARA SHUKOH and NADIRA, daughter of Prince Parwez**, was **arranged by PRINCESS JAHANARA and SATI UN NISA KHANUM, chief maid of the late empress MUMTAZ MAHAL.** Gifts were exhibited in the ***diwan-i am***; the ***HINABANDI*** (henna) ceremony was performed in the ***diwan-i khas***; **betel leaf (*paan*), cardamom and dry fruit** were distributed. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Total cost Rs 32 LAKH — of which the IMPERIAL TREASURY paid only Rs 6 LAKH, JAHANARA paid Rs 16 LAKH (including a sum earlier set aside by Mumtaz Mahal), and the bride's mother paid the rest.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Do the arithmetic, because it is the point: **the emperor's daughter paid nearly THREE TIMES what the imperial treasury did** for the wedding of the emperor's son.** ⭐ **That is not a princess spending an allowance; that is **an independent financial power inside the household.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note where the ceremonies were held — the *DIWAN-I AM* and the *DIWAN-I KHAS*, the two halls of government from §6.2.** *A family wedding was staged in the rooms where the empire was administered, in the same graded order of publicness.*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 8 — *"What were the DISTINCTIVE FEATURES of the Mughal nobility? How was their RELATIONSHIP WITH THE EMPEROR shaped?"* — AND THE ANSWER HAS A SPINE: EVERY FEATURE OF THIS SYSTEM IS DESIGNED TO PREVENT THE NOBILITY FROM BECOMING AN INDEPENDENT POWER.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DIVERSITY AS POLICY** — *"recruited from diverse ethnic and religious groups. **THIS ENSURED THAT NO FACTION WAS LARGE ENOUGH TO CHALLENGE THE AUTHORITY OF THE STATE.**"* **Turanis, Iranis, Rajputs, Shaikhzadas, Khatris, Marathas — a *GULDASTA* held together only by loyalty to the emperor.** *Note that this is [§5.2's *sulh-i kul*](#) doing administrative work.*
> - ⭐ **RANK GRANTED, NOT INHERITED** — a *mansab* came by ***TAJWIZ***, a petition **routed through an existing noble**, and *"the emperor **PERSONALLY REVIEWED** changes in rank, titles and official postings for all except the lowest-ranked officers."*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **RANK WAS DUAL, AND THAT IS THE CLEVER PART** — ***ZAT*** fixed **status and salary**, ***SAWAR*** fixed **how many horsemen he must maintain.** *The two are separate numbers, so **the state could raise a man's dignity without raising his troops, or demand more troops without more honour** — a control mechanism built into the arithmetic of the rank itself.*
> - ⭐ **VERIFICATION** — horses **branded with the imperial mark (*DAGH*)**, so a commander could not muster borrowed animals at review.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PAYMENT BY TRANSFERABLE ASSIGNMENT** — *"the majority… were paid through assignments of revenue (**JAGIRS**)… **THEY WERE TRANSFERRED PERIODICALLY**"* ([Theme 8 §6](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md)). *No *jagirdar* could put down roots.*
> - ⭐ **SEPARATION OF POWERS AT THE TOP** — the ***MIR BAKHSHI***, ***DIWAN-I ALA*** and ***SADR-US SUDUR*** *"occasionally came together as an advisory body, but were **INDEPENDENT OF EACH OTHER**."* *Nobody but the emperor sees the whole machine.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **RITUAL AND SPIRITUAL BINDING** — nobles at court appeared **TWICE DAILY** to express submission; and **Akbar took a select band as his DISCIPLES (*MURID*)**, converting an employment relationship into a spiritual one.
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The closing judgement to write: *the Mughal nobility was a service élite, not an aristocracy.* **Its members held rank at the emperor's pleasure, were paid in revenue they did not own, were moved before they could settle, were drawn from groups that could not combine, and were watched by three ministers who could not combine either.*** *(⭐ **And then add the honest qualification NCERT itself supplies in §8.3: the chroniclers *"usually portrayed the emperor and his court as controlling the entire administrative apparatus **DOWN TO THE VILLAGE LEVEL** — yet, as you have seen (Chapter 8), **this could hardly have been a process free of tension**."*)*
>
> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE *GULDASTA* IN ACTUAL DETAIL — Chandrabhan Barahman's *CHAR CHAMAN* ("Four Gardens"), written under Shah Jahan, and it is the best single quotation on Mughal cosmopolitanism:** *people from *"**ARABS, IRANIANS, TURKS, TAJIKS, KURDS, TATARS, RUSSIANS, ABYSSINIANS**"* and from *"**TURKEY, EGYPT, SYRIA, IRAQ, ARABIA, IRAN, KHURASAN, TURAN**"*; and from India, ⭐ *"**BUKHARIS AND BHAKKARIS, SAIYYADS of genuine lineage, SHAIKHZADAS with noble ancestry, AFGHAN TRIBES such as the LODIS, ROHILLAS, YUSUFZAI, and CASTES OF RAJPUTS… RATHOR, SISODIA, KACHHWAHA, HADA, GAUR, CHAUHAN, PANWAR, BHADURIYA, SOLANKI, BUNDELA, SHEKHAWAT**"*, plus *"**GHAKKAR, KHOKAR, BALUCHI**"* — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"and *MANSABS* FROM 100 TO 7000 *ZAT***", and landowners *"from the regions of **KARNATAKA, BENGAL, ASSAM, UDAIPUR, SRINAGAR, KUMAON, TIBET AND KISHTWAR**."*
>

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ***AKHBARAT*** ARE THE MOST UNDERRATED SOURCE IN THIS CHAPTER, AND THEY ARE STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT FROM EVERYTHING ELSE IN PART II. NOTICE WHO KEPT THEM.** ⭐ **The ***WAQIA NAWIS*** worked for the state. **But the *AKHBARAT-I DARBAR-I MUALLA* were compiled by the *WAKILS* — the private agents of NOBLES AND REGIONAL RULERS**, recording the court's proceedings **for their own masters**, with **the date and the *PAHAR* (watch of the day).*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is a daily, timed, unofficial record kept by people whose job was to tell their employers what was really going on — which makes it far less flattering than a chronicle, and far more granular.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And look at what got recorded: attendance, grants of office and title, diplomatic missions, presents received — and *"THE ENQUIRIES MADE BY THE EMPEROR ABOUT THE HEALTH OF AN OFFICER."*** ⭐ **A *wakil* reported his master's health being asked after **because at a court where status was proximity, an enquiry was a political signal.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's own conclusion is the historian's prize: this material is *"valuable for writing the history of the PUBLIC AND PRIVATE LIVES of kings and nobles"* — the daily texture that the *Akbar Nama* is far too grand to contain.**
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE INFORMATION LOOP IS A GENUINE ADMINISTRATIVE ACHIEVEMENT WORTH STATING PLAINLY: *round-the-clock relays of FOOT-RUNNERS carrying papers in BAMBOO CONTAINERS brought reports from distant provincial capitals to the emperor WITHIN A FEW DAYS* — in the seventeenth century, over a subcontinent, on foot.** ⭐ **And it ran in both directions and outside the state: **Rajput princes and tributary rulers copied the court news and sent it home**, so the empire's political class was reading the same bulletin.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **Compare **[Theme 5's](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md) Ibn Battuta on the Delhi Sultanate's postal system**, which he found astonishing for exactly the same reason. **The Mughals inherited and extended a fourteenth-century Indian institution — and both chapters make the same point: information speed IS state capacity.**)*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 5 ASKS TWO THINGS — *"the major features of Mughal provincial administration"* AND *"HOW DID THE CENTRE CONTROL THE PROVINCES?"* — AND THE SECOND HALF HAS FOUR ANSWERS:**
> - ⭐ **(i) STRUCTURAL MIRRORING** — the centre's division of functions was **replicated** in every *suba* *(diwan, bakhshi, sadr)*, so **the emperor was looking at the same organisational chart everywhere.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) DIRECT REPORTING** — the ***SUBADAR* reported DIRECTLY TO THE EMPEROR**, not through an intermediate tier.
> - ⭐ **(iii) A SEPARATE MILITARY CHAIN** — ***FAUJDARS* with heavy cavalry and musketeers**, whose jurisdictions *"often OVERLAPPED"* with the *sarkars*. **Deliberately overlapping jurisdictions mean no single officer controls a territory completely.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iv) A COMMON LANGUAGE AND A PAPER TRAIL** — **Persian throughout**, staffed by *"technically qualified officials, functioning in accordance with STANDARDISED RULES AND PROCEDURES, and generating COPIOUS WRITTEN ORDERS AND RECORDS"*, all flowing back through **the *waqia nawis* and the imperial post.**
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BUT THE ANSWER IS INCOMPLETE WITHOUT THE LIMIT, AND NCERT SUPPLIES IT: AT THE ***PARGANA*** LEVEL THE OFFICERS WERE ***SEMI-HEREDITARY***.** ⭐ **The ***QANUNGO*** who kept the revenue records and the ***CHAUDHURI*** who collected the revenue were **local men from families that held those offices**, not transferable imperial servants.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the empire's carefully anti-entrenchment design — transferable *jagirs*, personally reviewed postings, overlapping jurisdictions — **stops exactly one level above the village**, and below that line sits **local, hereditary, permanent knowledge.** *And [Theme 8's](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) headman was chosen by village elders and **ratified by the ZAMINDAR**, not by any imperial officer.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE LANGUAGE FACT IS THE SAME BOUNDARY IN ANOTHER FORM: *"Persian was made the language of administration THROUGHOUT, but LOCAL LANGUAGES WERE USED FOR VILLAGE ACCOUNTS."*** ⭐ **You can trace the empire's real reach by following where Persian stops.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It stops at the village ledger — which means the most basic record in the entire fiscal system, the one that says who owes what, was kept in a language the imperial service did not administer in.** *(⭐ **That is the precise, documentary version of [Theme 8's](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) conclusion that the Mughal state was **DEEP BUT NARROW** — and NCERT's own closing sentence here, that *"the zamindars often succeeded in MOBILISING PEASANT SUPPORT AGAINST THE STATE,"* is Theme 8 §5's finding stated from the court's side of the relationship.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FIG. 9.16 — *JAHANGIR'S DREAM*, by ABU'L HASAN, AND IT IS THE BEST PIECE OF POLITICAL ART IN THE CHAPTER. READ NCERT'S CAPTION CAREFULLY:** *"An inscription records that **JAHANGIR COMMISSIONED ABU'L HASAN TO RENDER IN PAINTING A DREAM THE EMPEROR HAD HAD.** Abu'l Hasan painted **the two rulers — JAHANGIR and the Safavid SHAH ABBAS — IN FRIENDLY EMBRACE.** Both kings are depicted in their traditional costumes. ⭐ **The figure of the Shah is based upon PORTRAITS MADE BY BISHANDAS, WHO ACCOMPANIED THE MUGHAL EMBASSY TO IRAN IN 1613.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS GAVE A SENSE OF AUTHENTICITY TO A SCENE WHICH IS FICTIONAL, AS THE TWO RULERS HAD NEVER MET.**"*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOW PUT THE PAINTING NEXT TO THE DATES AND IT BECOMES EXTRAORDINARY.** ⭐ **The 1613 embassy to Shah Abbas — the one that took the painter **Bishandas** to Iran — was the mission **sent to plead for Qandahar, and it FAILED.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the emperor commissioned a picture of himself **embracing** the rival who had just refused him, and had the rival's face painted **accurately, from life studies made on the failed mission**, to lend authenticity to **a meeting that never happened.***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is the single sharpest illustration in Part II of what an official image is FOR. Three layers, and you should name all three:** ⭐ **(i) FACTUAL ACCURACY in the details — a real portrait, real costumes, a documented embassy.** ⭐ **(ii) A FICTIONAL EVENT — the two men never met.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) A POLITICAL CLAIM — that the Mughal emperor and the Safavid shah stand in a relationship of amity, on Jahangir's terms.** *And NCERT's own questions push you further: *"COMPARE THEIR PHYSIQUE AND POSTURES. What do the ANIMALS stand for? What does THE MAP suggest?"* — **the relative size of the two figures, and who stands on what part of the globe, are the argument.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **Keep this beside [Theme 7's](07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) Krishnadeva Raya statue and Paes's smallpox. **Both chapters end up at the same place: the image records what the ruler wanted to be true.** But *Jahangir's Dream* is subtler, because it uses **genuine documentary evidence** to authenticate **an event that never occurred** — which is a far harder thing for a historian to catch.**)*

```
   ================================================================

   ================================================================

      marked by ⭐ THE CONCERN TO ENSURE ** FREE MOVEMENT FOR

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE MECHANISM: "The Mughal emperor usually
      ** COMBINED RELIGION AND COMMERCE ** BY ⭐ EXPORTING VALUABLE
      MERCHANDISE TO ** ADEN AND MOKHA, BOTH RED SEA PORTS ** , AND
      ** DISTRIBUTING THE PROCEEDS OF THE SALES IN CHARITY TO THE

   ** "HOWEVER, WHEN AURANGZEB DISCOVERED ** CASES OF

```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE MECHANISM IS WORTH UNDERSTANDING, BECAUSE IT IS CLEVERER THAN "the emperor gave charity."** ⭐ **The Mughals **shipped valuable goods to Aden and Mocha, SOLD them, and gave away the PROCEEDS** to shrine-keepers and religious men in the Hijaz.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So a single operation performed **three functions at once**: it **conducted profitable Red Sea trade**, it **discharged the emperor's religious duty**, and it **bought goodwill from the Ottoman authorities who controlled the pilgrimage route.** *Piety financed by exports, and diplomacy delivered as alms.*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND AURANGZEB'S REMARK — INDIA *"WAS AS MUCH A HOUSE OF GOD AS MECCA"* — IS A GENUINELY SURPRISING QUOTATION AND WORTH KEEPING, BECAUSE IT CUTS ACROSS THE STOCK PICTURE OF HIM.** ⭐ **The trigger was administrative — **misappropriation of funds sent to Arabia** — but the justification he reached for was theological, and it **relocated the sacred to the subcontinent.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In an answer, use it exactly as NCERT does: as evidence that Mughal policy toward religion was **shaped by practical governance**, and that the emperor most often cast as an orthodox literalist argued that charity was better spent at home.** *(⭐ **Hold it beside §5.2's honest pairing — **Aurangzeb issued grants to REPAIR temples AND re-imposed the *jizya*.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The lesson NCERT keeps teaching is that these emperors do not fit single-word descriptions, and an answer that gives them one is doing worse history than the textbook.**)*

## 🔟 Questioning Formal Religion ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE JESUITS' MISREADING -- and Monserrate's
      devastating one-liner **
   ================================================================
   ** "THE HIGH RESPECT SHOWN BY AKBAR TOWARDS THE MEMBERS OF THE
      JESUIT MISSION IMPRESSED THEM DEEPLY. ⭐ THEY INTERPRETED THE
      EMPEROR'S OPEN INTEREST IN THE DOCTRINES OF CHRISTIANITY
      ** AS A SIGN OF HIS ACCEPTANCE OF THEIR FAITH. ** This can be
      understood in the light of ⭐ THE PREVAILING CLIMATE OF
      RELIGIOUS INTOLERANCE IN WESTERN EUROPE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** MONSERRATE'S REMARK: "THE KING CARED LITTLE THAT
      ** IN ALLOWING EVERYONE TO FOLLOW HIS RELIGION HE WAS IN
      REALITY VIOLATING ALL. ** " **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE IBADAT KHANA -- a Prelims certainty **
   ================================================================
   ** "AKBAR'S QUEST FOR RELIGIOUS KNOWLEDGE LED TO ⭐ INTERFAITH
      DEBATES IN ** THE IBADAT KHANA AT FATEHPUR SIKRI ** BETWEEN
      LEARNED ** MUSLIMS, HINDUS, JAINAS, PARSIS AND CHRISTIANS. ** " **

   ** "Akbar's religious views MATURED AS HE QUERIED SCHOLARS OF
      DIFFERENT RELIGIONS AND SECTS AND GATHERED KNOWLEDGE ABOUT
      THEIR DOCTRINES. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ INCREASINGLY, HE MOVED AWAY FROM THE
      ORTHODOX ISLAMIC WAYS OF UNDERSTANDING RELIGIONS TOWARDS
      ** A SELF-CONCEIVED ECLECTIC FORM OF DIVINE WORSHIP FOCUSED ON
      LIGHT AND THE SUN. ** " **

   ⭐ ** "We have seen that AKBAR AND ABU'L FAZL CREATED
      ** A PHILOSOPHY OF LIGHT ** AND USED IT TO SHAPE THE IMAGE OF
      THE KING AND THE IDEOLOGY OF THE STATE. In this, ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
      A DIVINELY INSPIRED INDIVIDUAL HAS ** SUPREME SOVEREIGNTY OVER
      HIS PEOPLE AND COMPLETE CONTROL OVER HIS ENEMIES. ** " **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MONSERRATE'S SENTENCE IS THE MOST QUOTABLE LINE IN THE WHOLE SUPPLEMENT, AND YOU SHOULD USE IT PRECISELY: *"THE KING CARED LITTLE THAT IN ALLOWING EVERYONE TO FOLLOW HIS RELIGION HE WAS IN REALITY VIOLATING ALL."*** ⭐ **It is a hostile witness making an acute observation.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **From inside any exclusive faith — and Monserrate came from a Europe then tearing itself apart over precisely this — **a ruler who treats all religions as equally permissible is not honouring any of them; he is subordinating all of them to the state.*** *That is *sulh-i kul*'s critics' case, stated in one line by a man who was there.
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOTE HOW NCERT FRAMES THE JESUITS' MISTAKE — it is a lesson in reading foreign observers.** ⭐ **They took Akbar's curiosity for conversion, *"in the light of THE PREVAILING CLIMATE OF RELIGIOUS INTOLERANCE IN WESTERN EUROPE."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Coming from a world where serious interest in another faith meant you were leaving your own, they could not conceive of a ruler who debated all of them and joined none.** *(⭐ **[Theme 5's](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md) rule exactly: the traveller measures the new place against home — and here NCERT names the "home" that produced the error.**)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE CLOSING SENTENCE OF §10 IS WHERE THE CHAPTER SHOWS ITS HAND. Read it slowly: the philosophy of light produces a state in which *"A DIVINELY INSPIRED INDIVIDUAL HAS SUPREME SOVEREIGNTY OVER HIS PEOPLE AND COMPLETE CONTROL OVER HIS ENEMIES."*** ⭐ **That is not a description of tolerance; **it is a description of ABSOLUTISM legitimised by tolerance.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The emperor who stands above all religions is, for that very reason, answerable to none of them — and NCERT lets you draw the conclusion yourself.**

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE *"HOM IN THE HARAM"* BOX — ABDUL QADIR BADAUNI's *MUNTAKHAB-UT TAWARIKH*, and it is the most important single source in this chapter for one reason: IT IS A DISSENTING CHRONICLE.**
>
> ⭐ **NCERT's own framing:** *"**A THEOLOGIAN AND A COURTIER, BADAUNI WAS CRITICAL OF HIS EMPLOYER'S POLICIES AND DID NOT WISH TO MAKE THE CONTENTS OF HIS BOOK PUBLIC.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**FROM EARLY YOUTH, IN COMPLIMENT TO HIS WIVES, THE DAUGHTERS OF RAJAS OF HIND, HIS MAJESTY HAD BEEN PERFORMING *HOM* IN THE *HARAM*, WHICH IS A CEREMONY DERIVED FROM FIRE-WORSHIP ( *ATISH-PARASTI* ).** But on the New Year of **THE TWENTY-FIFTH REGNAL YEAR (1578) HE PROSTRATED PUBLICLY BEFORE THE SUN AND THE FIRE.** In the evening **THE WHOLE COURT HAD TO RISE UP RESPECTFULLY WHEN THE LAMPS AND CANDLES WERE LIGHTED.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BADAUNI IS THE ANSWER TO ANYONE WHO THINKS MUGHAL CHRONICLES WERE UNIFORMLY FLATTERING, AND HE IS THE PERFECT COUNTERWEIGHT TO ABU'L FAZL. NOTICE THE THREE THINGS THAT MAKE HIM VALUABLE:** ⭐ **(i) He was **a courtier and a theologian — an insider**, not an outside critic.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) He was **hostile to the policies he was recording**, which means his account is not shaped to please.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) He *"DID NOT WISH TO MAKE THE CONTENTS OF HIS BOOK PUBLIC"* — **a secret chronicle, written without the constraint that Abu'l Fazl worked under, whose draft was recited in the royal hearing.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And look at what the same facts become in the two men's hands.** ⭐ **For Abu'l Fazl, the philosophy of light is ***FARR-I IZADI***, descending from God through a hierarchy to the king.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **For Badauni, it is ***ATISH-PARASTI*** — **fire-worship** — and the emperor **prostrating publicly before the sun and the fire in 1578**, with the whole court made to stand for the lighting of the lamps.** *Same practices, same court, same decade — **one man calls it divine illumination and the other calls it Zoroastrianism.*** ⭐ *(⭐ **Note Badauni's explanation of the origin too, and it links straight to §7: Akbar performed *hom* *"IN COMPLIMENT TO HIS WIVES, THE DAUGHTERS OF RAJAS OF HIND."* **The marriage alliances of the imperial household brought their own rituals into the palace.** Whether you read that as syncretism or as apostasy is exactly what these two chroniclers disagreed about.**)*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TRANSFERABLE LESSON, AND IT IS THE ONE TO CARRY OUT OF THIS SUPPLEMENT: *NCERT prints Abu'l Fazl and Badauni in the same chapter so that you cannot treat "the Mughal chronicles" as a single voice.* **The court contained its own opposition, and it wrote too.***

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE CHAPTER'S LAST PARAGRAPH -- and it explains the
      dynasty's afterlife **
   ================================================================
   ** "These ideas were IN HARMONY WITH THE PERSPECTIVE OF THE
      COURT CHRONICLERS who give us a sense of the processes by
      which ⭐ THE MUGHAL RULERS COULD EFFECTIVELY ** ASSIMILATE
      SUCH A HETEROGENEOUS POPULACE WITHIN AN IMPERIAL EDIFICE. ** " **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "THE NAME OF THE DYNASTY ** CONTINUED TO ENJOY

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT FINAL SENTENCE IS THE PAYOFF OF THE ENTIRE CHAPTER, AND IT IS THE ANSWER TO "DID ANY OF THIS IDEOLOGY ACTUALLY WORK?"** ⭐ **Chronicles, paintings, halos, *jharoka darshan*, the Chain of Justice, *sulh-i kul*, titles, robes and rituals — **all of it was manufactured legitimacy**, and the test of manufactured legitimacy is **whether it outlives the power that made it.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It did, by **a century and a half.** *(⭐ **And that is why, in **[Part III Theme 10](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/10_Theme10_Rebels_and_the_Raj_1857.md)**, sepoys from Meerut rode to Delhi in May 1857 and pressed an unwilling octogenarian to accept a sovereignty his family had not exercised for generations. **They needed a name that everyone recognised as legitimate — and Abu'l Fazl, Abdul Hamid Lahori and a workshop of painters had spent two centuries making one.**)*

## 📅 Timeline — Some Major Mughal Chronicles and Memoirs ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** NCERT'S OWN TIMELINE. It is short, dated, and exactly the
      shape of a match-the-pairs question. **
   ================================================================

   ** c. 1530 **   ⭐ MANUSCRIPT OF BABUR'S MEMOIRS IN TURKISH --
                   ** SAVED FROM A STORM ** -- BECOMES PART OF THE
                   FAMILY COLLECTION OF THE TIMURIDS

   ** c. 1587 **   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ GULBADAN BEGUM BEGINS TO WRITE THE
                   ** HUMAYUN NAMA **

   ** 1589 **      BABUR'S MEMOIRS ** TRANSLATED INTO PERSIAN ** AS
                   THE ** BABUR NAMA **

   ** 1589-1602 ** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ABU'L FAZL WORKS ON THE ** AKBAR NAMA **
                   (thirteen years; he is murdered in 1602)

   ** 1605-22 **   JAHANGIR WRITES HIS MEMOIRS, THE
                   ** JAHANGIR NAMA **

   ** 1639-47 **   ⭐ LAHORI COMPOSES THE ** FIRST TWO DAFTARS ** OF
                   THE ** BADSHAH NAMA **

   ** c. 1650 **   MUHAMMAD WARIS BEGINS TO CHRONICLE ** THE THIRD
                   DECADE ** OF SHAH JAHAN'S REIGN

   ** 1668 **      ⭐ ** ALAMGIR NAMA ** , a history of ** THE FIRST
                   TEN YEARS OF AURANGZEB'S REIGN ** , COMPILED BY
                   ** MUHAMMAD KAZIM **
```

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE THINGS IN THIS TIMELINE ARE WORTH NOTICING, AND THEY ARE NOT OBVIOUS.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(i) *GULBADAN BEGUM (c.1587) BEGAN BEFORE ABU'L FAZL (1589).*** *The woman's memoir is the EARLIER project — and it was commissioned as **raw material** for the man's.*
> - ⭐ **(ii) *The first entry is about a book SURVIVING A STORM.*** **Babur's Turkish memoirs are in the record because a manuscript was physically rescued** — a reminder that the sources we have are the ones that happened to last. *(⭐⭐ Compare **[Theme 6's](06_Theme6_Bhakti_Sufi_Traditions.md) bhakti verses transmitted orally for centuries**, and **[Theme 7's](07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) vanished wooden Vijayanagara.**)*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) *The tradition is CONTINUOUS across four reigns* — Babur's memoirs → Gulbadan → Abu'l Fazl → Jahangir's own *Jahangir Nama* → Lahori → Waris → Muhammad Kazim's *Alamgir Nama*.** **Every emperor from Akbar to Aurangzeb had his reign written down**, and Jahangir wrote his own. *That is a sustained institutional commitment to being remembered, and it is why we know the Mughals in a detail no earlier Indian dynasty allows.*

| Babur invaded India to spread Islam | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Driven from **FARGHANA by the UZBEKS**, he established himself at **KABUL**, then in **1526** pushed on *"**IN SEARCH OF TERRITORIES AND RESOURCES TO SATISFY THE NEEDS OF THE MEMBERS OF HIS CLAN**"* |
| Humayun took refuge with the Ottomans | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In the court of the SAFAVID ruler of IRAN**, after defeat by **SHER SHAH SUR**; he **defeated the Surs in 1555 and died a year later** |

| Persian remained a court language only | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It became **the language of administration AT ALL LEVELS**, influenced official records in **RAJASTHANI, MARATHI AND EVEN TAMIL**, was itself **INDIANISED**, and **URDU sprang from Persian meeting HINDAVI** |
| The *Mahabharata* was translated as the *Razm Nama* under Shah Jahan | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ NCERT attributes the Sanskrit translation programme to **"the Mughal emperors"** in the context of **Akbar's language policy**; the ***MAHABHARATA* became the *RAZMNAMA*, "BOOK OF WARS"** |
| A *kitabkhana* was a library | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Although *kitabkhana* can be translated as library, **IT WAS A SCRIPTORIUM**"* — a place where manuscripts were **kept AND PRODUCED** |
| All those who made a manuscript were honoured | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CALLIGRAPHERS AND PAINTERS held high social standing; PAPER MAKERS and BOOKBINDERS *"HAVE REMAINED ANONYMOUS ARTISANS"*** |
| Akbar's favourite calligraphic style was *naskh* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***NASTALIQ*, "A FLUID STYLE WITH LONG HORIZONTAL STROKES"**, written with a trimmed reed *(**QALAM**, tip **5–10 mm**, nib split to absorb ink)* dipped in carbon ink *(**SIYAHI**)* |
| *Zarrin qalam* was a kind of pen | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"GOLDEN PEN" — a TITLE**, awarded to **MUHAMMAD HUSAYN OF KASHMIR** for *"the perfectly proportioned curvature of his letters"* |
| Paintings were made on the same page as the text | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**THE SCRIBE LEFT BLANK SPACES ON NEARBY PAGES; PAINTINGS, EXECUTED SEPARATELY BY ARTISTS, WERE INSERTED**"* — they were **MINIATURES**, passed around for viewing |
| The *ulama* objected to painting because it was expensive | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ They invoked **the Qur'an and the HADIS** — a naturalistic depiction of living beings would suggest *"**THE ARTIST WAS SEEKING TO APPROPRIATE THE POWER OF CREATION**"*, which belongs **exclusively to God** |
| Akbar dismissed the objection to painting | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ He **inverted it**: *"an artist has **A UNIQUE WAY OF RECOGNISING GOD** when he must come to feel that **HE CANNOT BESTOW LIFE ON HIS WORK**"* |
| Abu'l Fazl praised only the Iranian painters | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**THIS IS ESPECIALLY TRUE OF THE HINDU ARTISTS. THEIR PICTURES SURPASS OUR CONCEPTION OF THINGS. FEW, INDEED, IN THE WHOLE WORLD ARE FOUND EQUAL TO THEM**"* |
| Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad came with Akbar | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ They *"**WERE MADE TO ACCOMPANY EMPEROR HUMAYUN TO DELHI**"* |
| The *Akbar Nama* has two books | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE — the first two are chronicles, and THE THIRD IS THE *AIN-I AKBARI*** |
| Volume 1 of the *Akbar Nama* begins with Babur | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"THE HISTORY OF MANKIND FROM ADAM to ONE CELESTIAL CYCLE OF AKBAR'S LIFE (30 YEARS)"**; Volume 2 closes in the **FORTY-SIXTH REGNAL YEAR (1601)** |
| Abu'l Fazl worked on the *Akbar Nama* for three years | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIRTEEN — beginning in 1589, "REPEATEDLY REVISING THE DRAFT"** |
| Abu'l Fazl died of natural causes | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MURDERED in 1602 — "A CONSPIRACY HATCHED BY PRINCE SALIM", executed by BIR SINGH BUNDELA** |
| A synchronic account traces change over time | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REVERSED. DIACHRONIC = over time; SYNCHRONIC = one or several situations at ONE POINT of time.** The *Ain* is the **synchronic** part, and NCERT calls it *"the MORE NOVEL sense"* |

| The *Badshah Nama* has three volumes all by Lahori | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three *daftars* of TEN LUNAR YEARS each. Lahori wrote the FIRST TWO (1627–47), later REVISED BY SADULLAH KHAN (Shah Jahan's *wazir*); the THIRD decade was chronicled by WARIS** |
| The *Badshah Nama* has been fully translated | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**ONLY EXCERPTS… THE TEXT IN ITS ENTIRETY STILL AWAITS TRANSLATION**"*. *(The **AKBAR NAMA** was translated by **HENRY BEVERIDGE** in the early twentieth century)* |

| The illustrated *Badshah Nama* is in the National Museum | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gifted by the NAWAB OF AWADH to KING GEORGE III in 1799**; in the **English Royal Collections at WINDSOR CASTLE**; unbound for conservation in **1994**, exhibited in **1997** |

| *Sulh-i kul* means religious tolerance | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It means **"ABSOLUTE PEACE"**, and freedom of expression came **"ON CONDITION THAT THEY DID NOT UNDERMINE THE AUTHORITY OF THE STATE OR FIGHT AMONG THEMSELVES"** |

| No Mughal emperor after Akbar supported non-Muslim worship | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**ALL** Mughal emperors gave grants… **EVEN WHEN TEMPLES WERE DESTROYED DURING WAR, GRANTS WERE LATER ISSUED FOR THEIR REPAIR — as we know from the reigns of SHAH JAHAN AND AURANGZEB**"* — **though Aurangzeb also RE-IMPOSED the *jizya*** |
| Abu'l Fazl defined three essences the emperor protects | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FOUR — *JAN* (life), *MAL* (property), *NAMUS* (honour), *DIN* (faith)** — in return for **obedience and a share of resources** |

| All Mughal wives held equal rank | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***BEGAMS* (from royal families, married after huge *MAHR*) > *AGHAS* (not of noble birth) > *AGHACHA* (concubines)** — but *"the agha and the aghacha **COULD RISE** to the position of a begam"*, subject to the **four-wife limit** |

| Mughal princesses had no independent income | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **JAHANARA and ROSHANARA had annual incomes "OFTEN EQUAL TO THAT OF HIGH IMPERIAL *MANSABDARS*"; JAHANARA received the revenues of SURAT** |

| Todar Mal was a Rajput | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Akbar's finance minister, of the KHATRI caste** — an example of *"members of Hindu castes inclined towards **EDUCATION AND ACCOUNTANCY**"* |

## 🔁 Recap — the supplement in ten lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This chapter was DELETED, and it is the one that explains [Theme 8](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md)'s warning about its own source.** *Three dangling *"see Chapter 9"* references, one old-numbering reference, and **a running header — "KINGS AND CHRONICLES" — still printed on page 224 of the current book** are the traces it left.
2. ⭐ **A chronicle is TWO things at once** — *"a repository of factual information"* **and** *"a conveyor of meanings the rulers sought to impose."* *Its three purposes were to project an enlightened kingdom, **to tell resisters that resistance was destined to fail**, and to secure an account for posterity.
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The dynasty called itself TIMURID, not Mughal** — Babur used "Mongol" as an insult — and **AKBAR deliberately made PERSIAN the language of empire**, which reached the clerks, coloured Rajasthani, Marathi and Tamil records, was Indianised in return, and **produced URDU**.
4. ⭐ **A manuscript took five crafts**, made in the ***KITABKHANA* — a SCRIPTORIUM, not a library** — and **only the calligraphers and painters got names.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Painting was contested and defended brilliantly.** *The *ulama* said a lifelike image usurps God's power of creation; **Akbar replied that the painter is the one man who learns he CANNOT bestow life, and so recognises God** — and Abu'l Fazl praised **the Hindu artists above all others.**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Kingship rested on THREE PILLARS** — ***FARR-I IZADI*** *(Plato → Suhrawardi → Shaikh Mubarak → Abu'l Fazl, then a halo copied from European paintings of Christ)*, ***SULH-I KUL*** *("absolute peace", **conditional on not undermining the state**)*, and **SOVEREIGNTY AS A SOCIAL CONTRACT — *jan, mal, namus, din*** in return for obedience and a share of resources.
7. ⭐ **The court was a machine for making hierarchy visible**: **status WAS spatial proximity**, nobody moved once the emperor sat, salutations were graded by depth — and **Thomas Roe demanded a chair.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And every morning the emperor showed himself at the *JHAROKA*, so that imperial authority could become *"part of POPULAR FAITH."***
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The nobility was engineered so that it could not combine** — deliberately mixed, ranked by two separate numbers *(**ZAT** and **SAWAR**)*, paid in **transferable *jagirs***, watched by **three mutually independent ministers**, and bound by twice-daily attendance and, for some, **discipleship**.
9. ⭐ **The empire ran on paper and on foot** — ***waqia nawis*** for the state, ***AKHBARAT*** kept by nobles' own agents, and **relays of runners with bamboo tubes** bringing news from distant capitals **within days.** *But **Persian stopped at the village ledger**, and the *pargana* officers were **semi-hereditary locals.***
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the whole apparatus WORKED, by its own test:** *"the name of the dynasty **CONTINUED TO ENJOY LEGITIMACY IN THE SUBCONTINENT FOR A CENTURY AND A HALF, EVEN AFTER ITS GEOGRAPHICAL EXTENT AND THE POLITICAL CONTROL IT EXERCISED HAD DIMINISHED CONSIDERABLY**"* — which is why, in 1857, **the rebels went to Delhi.** ⭐ *And the court also produced its own dissenters: **GULBADAN BEGUM, who wrote no eulogy, and BADAUNI, who did not want his book made public.**

Prepare a report **comparing the present-day system of government with the Mughal court and administration**, focusing on **ideals of rulership, court rituals, and means of recruitment into the imperial service**. *(⭐⭐ **A rich comparison: *TAJWIZ* recommendation vs a competitive examination; *ZAT* and *SAWAR* vs pay grades and cadre strength; *jagir* transfers vs the transferable civil service; the *jharoka* vs the modern public appearance; and **the four essences vs Articles 21, 300A and 25.**)*

### ⭐ Beyond NCERT — UPSC-shaped

13. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"At one level a repository of factual information; at the same time conveyors of meanings the rulers sought to impose."* Discuss **the Mughal chronicle as a historical source**.
14. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"An artist has a unique way of recognising God when he must come to feel that he cannot bestow life on his work."* Examine **the controversy over painting at the Mughal court**, and how it was resolved.
15. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"In allowing everyone to follow his religion he was in reality violating all."* Assess **Monserrate's criticism of *sulh-i kul***.
16. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"A social contract: life, property, honour and faith."* Discuss **Abu'l Fazl's theory of sovereignty**, and compare it with **later European contract theory**.
17. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Status was determined by spatial proximity to the king."* Examine **court ritual as an instrument of government**.
18. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"A bouquet of flowers held together by loyalty to the emperor."* Analyse **the composition and control of the Mughal nobility**.
19. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"The scene is fictional, as the two rulers had never met."* Using ***Jahangir's Dream***, discuss **how accurate detail can be used to authenticate a false claim**.
20. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"What Gulbadan wrote was no eulogy."* Compare **GULBADAN BEGUM and ABU'L FAZL** as historians of the same court.
21. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"He did not wish to make the contents of his book public."* Discuss **BADAUNI as evidence that the Mughal court contained its own opposition**.
22. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Persian became the language of administration at all levels — but local languages were used for village accounts."* Examine **language as a measure of a state's reach**.
23. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"The name of the dynasty continued to enjoy legitimacy for a century and a half."* Discuss **manufactured legitimacy and its afterlife**, ending at **1857**.
24. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"A view from the top."* Compare **this chapter's account of how chronicles were made** with **[Theme 8](08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md)'s account of what the *Ain* leaves out**. *(⭐ **The question the deletion of this chapter makes it hard to answer — which is exactly why the supplement exists.**)*
