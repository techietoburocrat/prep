### 🧭 The three zones — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the single most important structure to fix

```
   ================================================================
   ** VIJAYANAGARA HAD THREE MAJOR ZONES, PLUS FARMLAND **
   ================================================================

   ** 1. THE SACRED CENTRE ** -- the rocky NORTHERN end, on the
        banks of the TUNGABHADRA
        -> Virupaksha and Vitthala temples; the hills of
           Pampadevi and (by tradition) Vali and Sugriva

   ** 2. THE URBAN CORE ** -- ordinary residences, markets,
        the Muslim residential quarter, small shrines

   ** 3. THE ROYAL CENTRE ** -- the SOUTH-WESTERN part;
        ⭐ "although designated as a royal centre, IT INCLUDED
           OVER 60 TEMPLES" and about 30 palace complexes

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND BETWEEN THE SACRED CENTRE AND THE URBAN CORE:
      AN AGRICULTURAL TRACT ** -- "the cultivated valley",
      irrigated by the HIRIYA CANAL, and ** ENCLOSED WITHIN THE
      FORTIFICATIONS. **

   ** THE WALLS: ** ⭐ ABDUR RAZZAQ "mentioned SEVEN LINES OF
      FORTS" ** ; NCERT describes three principal circuits --
      the OUTERMOST (round city AND fields), a SECOND round the
      urban core, and a THIRD round the royal centre, "within
      which EACH SET OF MAJOR BUILDINGS WAS SURROUNDED BY ITS
      OWN HIGH WALLS."
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FACT THAT WILL EARN YOU MARKS IN ANY ANSWER ON THIS CHAPTER: VIJAYANAGARA FORTIFIED ITS FARMLAND.** ⭐ *NCERT spells out why, and the reasoning is superb: *"the objective of **MEDIEVAL SIEGES** was to **STARVE THE DEFENDERS INTO SUBMISSION**. These sieges could last **for several months and sometimes even years.** **Normally rulers tried to be prepared… by building LARGE GRANARIES within fortified areas.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE RULERS OF VIJAYANAGARA ADOPTED A MORE EXPENSIVE AND ELABORATE STRATEGY OF PROTECTING THE AGRICULTURAL BELT ITSELF.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Say it in one line: *"most cities stored food against a siege; Vijayanagara enclosed the fields that GREW it."*** *A granary can be exhausted; **a besieged farm keeps producing.**

## 1️⃣ The Discovery of Hampi ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** 1800 -- "The ruins at Hampi were brought to light… by AN
      ENGINEER AND ANTIQUARIAN NAMED COLONEL COLIN MACKENZIE.
      An employee of THE ENGLISH EAST INDIA COMPANY, he
      PREPARED THE FIRST SURVEY MAP OF THE SITE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHERE HIS INFORMATION CAME FROM -- and this is
      the point most students miss: **
   ** "MUCH OF THE INITIAL INFORMATION HE RECEIVED WAS BASED ON
      THE MEMORIES OF PRIESTS OF THE VIRUPAKSHA TEMPLE AND THE
      SHRINE OF PAMPADEVI." **

   ** THEN, IN SEQUENCE: **
   * ⭐ ** "Subsequently, FROM 1856, PHOTOGRAPHERS BEGAN TO
     RECORD THE MONUMENTS which enabled scholars to study
     them." **
   * ⭐ ** "AS EARLY AS 1836 EPIGRAPHISTS BEGAN COLLECTING
     SEVERAL DOZEN INSCRIPTIONS found at this and other temples
     at Hampi." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "In an effort to reconstruct the history…
     historians COLLATED INFORMATION FROM THESE SOURCES WITH
     ACCOUNTS OF FOREIGN TRAVELLERS AND OTHER LITERATURE
     WRITTEN IN TELUGU, KANNADA, TAMIL AND SANSKRIT." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 1 ASKS FOR *"the METHODS used to study the ruins of Hampi over the last two centuries"* AND — crucially — *"IN WHAT WAY DO YOU THINK THEY WOULD HAVE COMPLEMENTED ONE ANOTHER."* THE SECOND HALF IS WHERE THE MARKS ARE. Build it as a list, then explain the complementarity:**
>
> | ⭐ **Method** | **What it gives** | ⭐ **What it CANNOT give** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **ORAL TRADITION** *(the Virupaksha and Pampadevi priests)* | ⭐ **the name Hampi, the identity of the site, living ritual continuity** | dates, political history |
> | **SURVEY AND MAPPING** *(Mackenzie 1800; the 1980s project)* | **layout, zones, roads, extent** | function, meaning |
> | **PHOTOGRAPHY** *(from 1856; Greenlaw)* | ⭐ **a fixed record of monuments as they then stood** | anything now lost |
> | **EPIGRAPHY** *(from 1836; Fleet from 1876)* | ⭐ **dates, donations, names of rulers and nayakas, events** | the lives of the unrecorded |
> | **TRAVELLERS' ACCOUNTS** *(Abdur Razzaq, Paes, Nuniz, Barbosa, Nicolo de Conti, Nikitin)* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **the LIVING city — bazaars, crowds, festivals, and the WOODEN and CLOTH structures that have vanished** | accuracy; each is an outsider *(see [Theme 5](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md))* |
> | **LITERATURE in Telugu, Kannada, Tamil and Sanskrit** | ⭐ **ideology, statecraft** *(e.g. the *Amuktamalyada*)* | material detail |
> | **ARCHAEOLOGY / EXCAVATION** | **pavements, pillar bases, porcelain shards, canal traces** | names, dates, intentions |
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE COMPLEMENTARITY, IN ONE SENTENCE:** *"**Archaeology supplies the STONE, inscriptions supply the DATES AND NAMES, travellers supply the LIFE AND THE VANISHED WOODEN CITY, and oral tradition supplied the IDENTITY OF THE SITE ITSELF — no single method could have recovered Vijayanagara.**"* *(⭐ **And the strongest single illustration is Fritz, Michell and Nagaraja Rao's remark in §6: we must *"IMAGINE A WHOLE SERIES OF VANISHED WOODEN ELEMENTS."* **Only the travellers can tell you what those looked like.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 1 — *Colin Mackenzie* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"Born in **1754**, Colin Mackenzie became famous as an **ENGINEER, SURVEYOR AND CARTOGRAPHER. In 1815 he was appointed THE FIRST SURVEYOR GENERAL OF INDIA**, a post he held **till his death in 1821.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **His stated motive:** *"He embarked on **collecting LOCAL HISTORIES and SURVEYING HISTORIC SITES in order to BETTER UNDERSTAND INDIA'S PAST AND MAKE GOVERNANCE OF THE COLONY EASIER.**"*
>
> ⭐ **His view of the pre-British past:** *"it **'struggled long under the miseries of BAD MANAGEMENT… before the South came under the BENIGN INFLUENCE of the British government'.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And what he expected to gain:** *by studying Vijayanagara, the East India Company could gain *"**much USEFUL INFORMATION on many of these INSTITUTIONS, LAWS AND CUSTOMS whose influence STILL PREVAILS among the various Tribes of Natives forming the general mass of the population to this day**."*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS SOURCE IS THE CHAPTER'S BEST PIECE OF SOURCE CRITICISM, AND IT SHOULD APPEAR IN ANY ANSWER ON HOW HAMPI WAS STUDIED.** ⭐ **Mackenzie was not an antiquarian with a hobby. He was the FIRST SURVEYOR GENERAL OF INDIA, and he says outright that the purpose was TO MAKE GOVERNANCE EASIER.** *Knowing *"institutions, laws and customs whose influence STILL PREVAILS"* is **administratively useful information about the people you are ruling.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND HIS FRAMING OF THE PAST IS THE COLONIAL ARGUMENT IN MINIATURE: *"bad management"* before, *"the BENIGN INFLUENCE of the British government"* after.** ⭐ *(⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is [Theme 5's Bernier](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md) arriving as POLICY.** *Bernier's picture of a stagnant, misgoverned Orient becomes, two centuries later, **the justification a Company surveyor writes into his own project proposal.** **Make that link — it connects two chapters and shows you understand what colonial knowledge was FOR.**)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND FIG. 7.2 IS WORTH READING AS CAREFULLY AS THE TEXT.** *A copy of a Thomas Hickey portrait, **c.1825**, showing Mackenzie with **his peon KISTNAJI holding a telescope**, and **two Brahmana assistants — a JAINA PANDIT and the Telugu Brahmana CAUVELLERY VENTAK LETCHMIAH.** ⭐ NCERT asks: *"**How has the artist PORTRAYED Mackenzie and his indigenous informers? What ideas about him and his informants are sought to be IMPRESSED UPON THE VIEWERS?**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The answer: Mackenzie is central and the informants are arranged around him — yet the painting also RECORDS THEIR NAMES, which is unusual and valuable.** *The "discovery" was made by a European **using Indian scholars and Indian priests' memories**, and the picture concedes as much even while composing him as the discoverer.*

## 2️⃣ Rayas, Nayakas and Sultans ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE FOUNDING: "According to TRADITION AND EPIGRAPHIC
      EVIDENCE two brothers, ** HARIHARA AND BUKKA ** , founded
      the Vijayanagara Empire in ** 1336 ** ." **
   ⭐ ** "This empire included WITHIN ITS FLUCTUATING FRONTIERS
      PEOPLES WHO SPOKE DIFFERENT LANGUAGES AND FOLLOWED
      DIFFERENT RELIGIOUS TRADITIONS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE CONTEMPORARY NAME -- a lovely Prelims item: **
   ** "While historians use the term VIJAYANAGARA EMPIRE,
      CONTEMPORARIES DESCRIBED IT AS THE
      ** KARNATAKA SAMRAJYAMU ** ." **

   ================================================================
   ** THE RIVALS, AND THE THREE ANIMAL TITLES **
   ================================================================
   ** "On their northern frontier, the Vijayanagara kings
      COMPETED with THE SULTANS OF THE DECCAN and THE GAJAPATI
      RULERS OF ORISSA -- for control of THE FERTILE RIVER
      VALLEYS and THE RESOURCES GENERATED BY LUCRATIVE OVERSEAS
      TRADE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE TITLES BOX -- learn all three: **
   ** GAJAPATI ** = "LORD OF ELEPHANTS" -- ⭐ "the name of a
      ruling lineage that was very powerful in ORISSA in the
      FIFTEENTH CENTURY"
   ** ASHVAPATI ** = "LORD OF HORSES" -- ⭐ what the popular
      traditions of Vijayanagara called ** THE DECCAN
      SULTANS **
   ** NARAPATI ** = "LORD OF MEN" -- ⭐ what ** THE RAYAS **
      were called

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE CRUCIAL QUALIFICATION ABOUT RIVALRY: **
   ** "At the same time, INTERACTION BETWEEN THESE STATES LED TO
      SHARING OF IDEAS, ESPECIALLY IN THE FIELD OF
      ARCHITECTURE. THE RULERS OF VIJAYANAGARA BORROWED CONCEPTS
      AND BUILDING TECHNIQUES WHICH THEY THEN DEVELOPED
      FURTHER." **

   ** THE INHERITED TRADITION: "Some of the areas… had witnessed
      the development of powerful states such as THE CHOLAS in
      TAMIL NADU and THE HOYSALAS in KARNATAKA. Ruling elites…
      had extended patronage to elaborate temples such as
      ** THE BRIHADISHVARA TEMPLE AT THANJAVUR ** and
      ** THE CHENNAKESHAVA TEMPLE AT BELUR ** . The rulers of
      Vijayanagara, who called themselves ** RAYAS ** , built on
      these traditions and CARRIED THEM… LITERALLY TO NEW
      HEIGHTS." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE THREE ANIMAL TITLES ARE A GIFT — *GAJAPATI* (elephants, Orissa) · *ASHVAPATI* (horses, the Deccan Sultans) · *NARAPATI* (men, the rayas) — and they are not decorative.** ⭐ *They describe **what each power's army rested on**, and they explain §2.1: **the Sultans were "lords of horses" because they controlled the horse trade**, which is precisely why Vijayanagara had to compete for it. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **And *"CARRIED THEM LITERALLY TO NEW HEIGHTS"* is NCERT's own pun — §5.2's *raya gopurams* "often DWARFED the towers on the central shrines."**)*

### 2.1 Kings and traders — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ why horses decided politics

```
   ** "As WARFARE during these times DEPENDED UPON EFFECTIVE
      CAVALRY, THE IMPORT OF HORSES FROM ARABIA AND CENTRAL
      ASIA WAS VERY IMPORTANT FOR RIVAL KINGDOMS." **
   * ⭐ ** "This trade was INITIALLY CONTROLLED BY ARAB
     TRADERS." **
   * ⭐ ** "Local communities of merchants known as
     ** KUDIRAI CHETTIS ** or HORSE MERCHANTS also
     participated." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "FROM 1498 other actors appeared on the scene.
     These were ** THE PORTUGUESE ** , who arrived on the west
     coast… and attempted to establish TRADING AND MILITARY
     STATIONS. ⭐ THEIR SUPERIOR MILITARY TECHNOLOGY, ESPECIALLY
     THE USE OF MUSKETS, ENABLED THEM TO BECOME IMPORTANT
     PLAYERS IN THE TANGLED POLITICS OF THE PERIOD." **

   ** AND THE CITY'S OWN COMMERCE: "Vijayanagara was also noted
      for its MARKETS DEALING IN SPICES, TEXTILES AND PRECIOUS
      STONES. ⭐ TRADE WAS OFTEN REGARDED AS A STATUS SYMBOL for
      such cities, which boasted of A WEALTHY POPULATION THAT
      DEMANDED HIGH-VALUE EXOTIC GOODS… THE REVENUE DERIVED FROM
      TRADE IN TURN CONTRIBUTED SIGNIFICANTLY TO THE PROSPERITY
      OF THE STATE." **
```

**📖 SOURCE 2 — *Kings and traders*: KRISHNADEVA RAYA's *AMUKTAMALYADA* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> **NCERT's framing:** *"**KRISHNADEVA RAYA (ruled 1509–29), THE MOST FAMOUS RULER OF VIJAYANAGARA, composed A WORK ON STATECRAFT IN TELUGU known as the *AMUKTAMALYADA*.**"* On traders he wrote:
>
> *"**A king should IMPROVE THE HARBOURS of his country and so encourage its commerce that HORSES, ELEPHANTS, PRECIOUS GEMS, SANDALWOOD, PEARLS and other articles are FREELY IMPORTED**… ⭐ He should arrange that **THE FOREIGN SAILORS WHO LAND IN HIS COUNTRY ON ACCOUNT OF STORMS, ILLNESS AND EXHAUSTION ARE LOOKED AFTER IN A SUITABLE MANNER**…*
>
> *⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Make THE MERCHANTS OF DISTANT FOREIGN COUNTRIES WHO IMPORT ELEPHANTS AND GOOD HORSES BE ATTACHED TO YOURSELF by providing them with DAILY AUDIENCE, PRESENTS and ALLOWING DECENT PROFITS. THEN THOSE ARTICLES WILL NEVER GO TO YOUR ENEMIES.**"*

> 🔴 **NCERT's questions: *"Why do you think the king was interested in ENCOURAGING TRADE? Which groups of people would have BENEFITED from these transactions?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LAST SENTENCE GIVES THE GAME AWAY, AND IT IS THE BEST LINE IN §2: *"THEN THOSE ARTICLES WILL NEVER GO TO YOUR ENEMIES."*** ⭐ **This is not commercial policy; it is MILITARY PROCUREMENT policy.** *Horses and elephants are **weapons systems**, and the supply is **finite and imported.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Krishnadeva Raya's advice — daily audience, presents, decent profits, and care for shipwrecked sailors — is a strategy for MONOPOLISING THE ARMS SUPPLY BY BEING THE BEST CUSTOMER.**
> - ⭐ **WHO BENEFITED:** *foreign merchants *(daily audience, presents, "decent profits")* · **the *kudirai chettis*** and local traders · **the state**, through customs revenue and military advantage · **the wealthy urban population** who bought the *"high-value exotic goods"* · **and the ports themselves**, which the king was to "improve."
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note the humane clause on shipwrecked sailors is ALSO strategic** — *"looked after in a suitable manner"* because **a reputation for good treatment brings ships back.** ⭐ *A king writing a manual in **Telugu** *(not Sanskrit)*, giving practical commercial advice, is itself worth a sentence: **the *Amuktamalyada* is a rare thing — statecraft written by a ruling emperor in a regional language.**

### 2.2 The apogee and decline ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE FOUR DYNASTIES -- a certain Prelims item **
   ================================================================
   ** 1. SANGAMA ** -- the first dynasty, "exercised control
        ** TILL 1485 ** "
   ** 2. SALUVAS ** -- ⭐ ** MILITARY COMMANDERS ** , "who
        remained in power ** TILL 1503 ** "
   ** 3. TULUVAS ** -- ⭐ ** KRISHNADEVA RAYA BELONGED TO THIS
        DYNASTY **
   ** 4. ARAVIDU ** -- from ** 1542 ** , "which remained in
        power ** TILL THE END OF THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY ** "

   ⭐ NOTE THE PATTERN: "claimants to power included MEMBERS OF
     THE RULING LINEAGE ** AS WELL AS MILITARY COMMANDERS ** " --
     and the Saluvas WERE military commanders. ** The throne
     changed hands by force, twice. **

   ================================================================
   ** KRISHNADEVA RAYA (1509-29) -- "EXPANSION AND
      CONSOLIDATION" **
   ================================================================
   * ⭐ ** 1512 -- the RAICHUR DOAB (between the Tungabhadra and
     Krishna) acquired **
   * ⭐ ** 1514 -- the rulers of ORISSA subdued **
   * ⭐ ** 1520 -- "severe defeats… inflicted on THE SULTAN OF
     BIJAPUR" **
   ** "Although the kingdom remained in A CONSTANT STATE OF
      MILITARY PREPAREDNESS, IT FLOURISHED UNDER CONDITIONS OF
      UNPARALLELED PEACE AND PROSPERITY." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHAT HE BUILT: "credited with building SOME FINE
      TEMPLES and ADDING IMPRESSIVE GOPURAMS TO MANY IMPORTANT
      SOUTH INDIAN TEMPLES. He also FOUNDED A SUBURBAN TOWNSHIP
      NEAR VIJAYANAGARA CALLED ** NAGALAPURAM AFTER HIS
      MOTHER ** ." **
   ⭐ ** "SOME OF THE MOST DETAILED DESCRIPTIONS OF VIJAYANAGARA
      COME FROM HIS TIME OR JUST AFTER." **

   ================================================================
   ** THE DECLINE, AND THE END **
   ================================================================
   ** "STRAIN BEGAN TO SHOW WITHIN THE IMPERIAL STRUCTURE
      FOLLOWING KRISHNADEVA RAYA'S DEATH IN 1529. His successors
      were TROUBLED BY REBELLIOUS NAYAKAS OR MILITARY CHIEFS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** 1565 -- THE BATTLE: "an ALLIANCE OF THE SULTANATES
      against Vijayanagara. In 1565 ** RAMA RAYA, THE CHIEF
      MINISTER ** of Vijayanagara, led the army into battle at
      ** RAKSHASI-TANGADI (also known as TALIKOTA) ** , where
      his forces were ROUTED BY THE COMBINED ARMIES OF
      ** BIJAPUR, AHMADNAGAR AND GOLCONDA ** ." **
   ⭐ ** "The victorious armies SACKED THE CITY of Vijayanagara.
      THE CITY WAS TOTALLY ABANDONED WITHIN A FEW YEARS." **

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOW THE MOST IMPORTANT PARAGRAPH IN §2, WHICH IS ALSO THE ONE MOST OFTEN GOT WRONG. NCERT IS EXPLICIT THAT RAYA–SULTAN RELATIONS WERE NOT INEVITABLY HOSTILE:**
>
> > *"**Although the armies of the Sultans were responsible for the destruction of the city, RELATIONS BETWEEN THE SULTANS AND THE RAYAS WERE NOT ALWAYS OR INEVITABLY HOSTILE, IN SPITE OF RELIGIOUS DIFFERENCES.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE THREE PIECES OF EVIDENCE — learn all three:**
> 1. ⭐ **KRISHNADEVA RAYA *"SUPPORTED SOME CLAIMANTS TO POWER IN THE SULTANATES"*** — i.e. he intervened **on behalf of** Sultanate factions.
> 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He *"TOOK PRIDE IN THE TITLE **'ESTABLISHER OF THE YAVANA KINGDOM'**."*** *(**Yavana** = *"a Sanskrit word used for the Greeks and other peoples who entered the subcontinent from the north west."*) **A Hindu emperor boasting of having ESTABLISHED a Muslim kingdom.**
> 3. ⭐ ***"the SULTAN OF BIJAPUR INTERVENED TO RESOLVE SUCCESSION DISPUTES IN VIJAYANAGARA following the death of Krishnadeva Raya."***
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's conclusion is unambiguous:** *"**In fact the Vijayanagara kings were KEEN TO ENSURE THE STABILITY OF THE SULTANATES AND VICE VERSA. IT WAS THE ADVENTUROUS POLICY OF RAMA RAYA WHO TRIED TO PLAY OFF ONE SULTAN AGAINST ANOTHER THAT LED THE SULTANS TO COMBINE TOGETHER AND DECISIVELY DEFEAT HIM.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT INTERPRETIVE POINT IN THE CHAPTER, AND IT MUST BE IN ANY ANSWER THAT TOUCHES 1565.** ⭐ **Talikota was not the culmination of an inevitable religious conflict. It was the result of a SPECIFIC DIPLOMATIC MISCALCULATION by ONE MAN.** *For two centuries these states **fought, allied, intervened in each other's successions, and borrowed each other's architecture.** **Rama Raya broke a working equilibrium by playing the Sultans off against one another, and they responded by combining.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **And the architectural evidence backs the political point: §3.2's arches and domes are *"typical features of the architecture introduced by the TURKISH SULTANS"*, and NCERT asks outright: *"WHY DO YOU THINK THE RULERS OF VIJAYANAGARA ADOPTED ELEMENTS OF INDO-ISLAMIC ARCHITECTURE?"* **You cannot borrow a rival's building techniques while being permanently at war with him.**)*

### 2.3 The rayas and the nayakas — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the *AMARA-NAYAKA* system

```
   ** WHO THE NAYAKAS WERE: "MILITARY CHIEFS who usually
      CONTROLLED FORTS and had ARMED SUPPORTERS. These chiefs
      OFTEN MOVED FROM ONE AREA TO ANOTHER, and in many cases
      were ACCOMPANIED BY PEASANTS LOOKING FOR FERTILE LAND ON
      WHICH TO SETTLE. ⭐ These chiefs… USUALLY SPOKE TELUGU OR
      KANNADA. Many submitted to the authority of the kings…
      BUT THEY OFTEN REBELLED AND HAD TO BE SUBDUED BY MILITARY
      ACTION." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE AMARA-NAYAKA SYSTEM -- "A MAJOR POLITICAL
      INNOVATION OF THE VIJAYANAGARA EMPIRE" **
   ================================================================
   ⭐ ** "It is likely that MANY FEATURES OF THIS SYSTEM WERE
      DERIVED FROM THE IQTA SYSTEM OF THE DELHI SULTANATE." **

   ** AMARA ** (margin) = "believed to be derived from the
      Sanskrit ** SAMARA, meaning BATTLE OR WAR ** . ⭐ It also
      resembles the Persian term ** AMIR, meaning A HIGH
      NOBLE ** ."

   ** HOW IT WORKED -- five steps: **
   1. ** "The amara-nayakas were MILITARY COMMANDERS WHO WERE
      GIVEN TERRITORIES TO GOVERN BY THE RAYA." **
   2. ** "They COLLECTED TAXES AND OTHER DUES FROM PEASANTS,
      CRAFTSPERSONS AND TRADERS in the area." **
   3. ⭐ ** "They RETAINED PART OF THE REVENUE FOR PERSONAL USE
      AND FOR MAINTAINING A STIPULATED CONTINGENT OF HORSES AND
      ELEPHANTS. These contingents PROVIDED THE VIJAYANAGARA
      KINGS WITH AN EFFECTIVE FIGHTING FORCE with which they
      BROUGHT THE ENTIRE SOUTHERN PENINSULA UNDER THEIR
      CONTROL." **
   4. ⭐ ** "SOME OF THE REVENUE WAS ALSO USED FOR THE
      MAINTENANCE OF TEMPLES AND IRRIGATION WORKS." **
   5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE CHECKS: "The amara-nayakas SENT TRIBUTE TO
      THE KING ANNUALLY and PERSONALLY APPEARED IN THE ROYAL
      COURT WITH GIFTS TO EXPRESS THEIR LOYALTY. ⭐ KINGS
      OCCASIONALLY ASSERTED THEIR CONTROL OVER THEM BY
      TRANSFERRING THEM FROM ONE PLACE TO ANOTHER." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE FAILURE: "HOWEVER, DURING THE COURSE OF
      THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY, MANY OF THESE NAYAKAS ESTABLISHED

      CENTRAL IMPERIAL STRUCTURE." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE *AMARA-NAYAKA* SYSTEM IS THE MOST EXAMINABLE ADMINISTRATIVE TOPIC IN THIS CHAPTER, AND ITS LOGIC IS WORTH UNDERSTANDING RATHER THAN MEMORISING.**
> - ⭐ **THE PROBLEM IT SOLVED:** *a king cannot maintain cavalry across the whole peninsula from a central treasury. **So he devolves the REVENUE and requires the CONTINGENT.** *The nayaka collects locally and pays in **horses and elephants**, not cash.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TWO SAFEGUARDS — and both are about PERSONAL BONDS, not institutions:** *annual **tribute**, and **personal appearance at court with gifts.** ⭐ **Plus the crucial one: TRANSFER.** *Moving a nayaka from one place to another **prevents him putting down roots** — the same device every state uses against entrenchment.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND WHY IT FAILED:** *the safeguards depended on **the centre being strong enough to enforce them.** *Once Krishnadeva Raya died (1529) and *"strain began to show"*, **the transfers stopped working, the nayakas stopped appearing — and by the seventeenth century they were kings.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ***IQTA*** LINK IS A HIGH-VALUE CROSS-BOOK CONNECTION AND YOU SHOULD MAKE IT.** *NCERT says *"many features… were **DERIVED FROM THE IQTA SYSTEM OF THE DELHI SULTANATE**"* — and **[Class 11 Supplement Part B](../../Class_11_Themes_in_World_History/Notes/08_Supplement_Industrial_Revolution_Islamic_Lands_and_Confrontation.md)** traces the ***iqta*** back to the **Central Islamic Lands**, where it arrived in India *"fully formed."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So a revenue-assignment system from the Abbasid world reached Delhi, and Vijayanagara — a rival power — adopted it.** *That is §2's *"sharing of ideas"* operating in administration as well as architecture, and it is exactly the kind of point that lifts an answer. *(⭐ **And the word itself carries the story: *AMARA* from Sanskrit *samara* (war), yet *"it also RESEMBLES the Persian term AMIR."*** **The etymology is as hybrid as the institution.**)*

**📖 SOURCE — *A House of Victory?*: PAES on the audience hall and the *mahanavami dibba* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **Paes called the two platforms TOGETHER "the House of Victory":**
>
> *"**These buildings have TWO PLATFORMS ONE ABOVE THE OTHER, BEAUTIFULLY SCULPTED** … On the upper platform … in this House of Victory **THE KING HAS A ROOM MADE OF CLOTH** … where **THE IDOL HAS A SHRINE** … and in the other **IN THE MIDDLE IS PLACED A DAIS ON WHICH STANDS A THRONE OF STATE, (THE CROWN AND THE ROYAL ANKLET)** …"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 4 — *"What do you think was the significance of the rituals associated with the mahanavami dibba?"* — AND THE ANSWER IS THAT THE FESTIVAL WAS §2.3's AMARA-NAYAKA SYSTEM PERFORMED IN PUBLIC, ONCE A YEAR.** ⭐ **Go back and check the two safeguards NCERT listed in §2.3: *"The amara-nayakas SENT TRIBUTE TO THE KING ANNUALLY AND PERSONALLY APPEARED IN THE ROYAL COURT WITH GIFTS TO EXPRESS THEIR LOYALTY."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Mahanavami is WHEN that happened.** *The obligation was constitutional; the festival was the occasion; the platform was the stage.*
>
> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the ritual is doing FOUR jobs at once, and a good answer says all four:**
> - **RELIGIOUS** — worship of the image, of the state horse, animal sacrifice, on the great ninth day of the goddess's festival.
> - ⭐ **MILITARY** — a **review of the entire army, the king's and the nayakas' together**, in an open field. *An inspection is also a demonstration: each nayaka sees exactly how large the others' contingents are, and how large the king's is.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FISCAL** — **the stipulated tribute is physically handed over.** *The empire's revenue settlement is completed as a religious act.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **POLITICAL AND SYMBOLIC** — **subordination is publicly re-enacted.** *The nayakas and subordinate kings make "ritual presentations BEFORE THE KING AND HIS GUESTS." **The audience is the point.** A loyalty that is renewed in front of witnesses is far harder to withdraw quietly.*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The one-line version worth memorising: *the Mahanavami festival was the amara-nayaka system's annual audit — tribute, troops and obedience all delivered in person, in public, on the highest point in the city.*** *(⭐ **And its collapse is a dated fact: once *"strain began to show"* after 1529 and the nayakas stopped coming, the ceremony had nothing left to display.**)*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOW THE HONEST ENDING, AND NCERT PUTS IT IN AS A QUESTION AGAINST ITS OWN RECONSTRUCTION:**
>
> ⭐ *"**Was the 'mahanavami dibba' THAT STANDS TODAY the centre of this elaborate ritual? Scholars have pointed out that THE SPACE SURROUNDING THE STRUCTURE DOES NOT SEEM TO HAVE BEEN ADEQUATE FOR ELABORATE PROCESSIONS OF ARMED MEN, WOMEN, AND LARGE NUMBERS OF ANIMALS. Like some of the other structures in the royal centre, IT REMAINS AN ENIGMA.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE CHAPTER'S METHOD IN MINIATURE AND YOU SHOULD QUOTE IT.** ⭐ **The festival is described from travellers' accounts. The platform is known from archaeology. The two are joined by an ASSUMPTION — that this platform is where that festival happened — and the assumption fails a simple physical test: there is not enough room around it for the crowd the sources describe.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two independent kinds of evidence, each solid, and the join between them is the weak point.** *That is a transferable examination point, and it is the same discipline as **[Part I Theme 1's](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/01_Theme1_Bricks_Beads_and_Bones_Harappan.md)** "Great Bath" and "granary" — **a structure named for a function, and the name then treated as a finding.***

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 7 IS SITTING RIGHT HERE — *"What does the architecture of buildings like the LOTUS MAHAL and ELEPHANT STABLES tell us about the rulers who commissioned them?"* — and the honest answer has TWO halves, one of which most students miss.**
>
> ⭐ **HALF ONE — what the buildings DO tell us:**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ARCHITECTURAL OPENNESS.** *The **arches and domes are Indo-Islamic**, adopted from the rulers these kings fought. **§2 already gave the reason** — *"interaction between these states led to **SHARING OF IDEAS, ESPECIALLY IN THE FIELD OF ARCHITECTURE**"* — and §3.2 showed the same borrowing in **the fort gateways.*** *A ruler who calls himself **"Hindu Suratrana"** and builds with arches is not being inconsistent; **he is doing the same thing in a title and in a doorway.***
> - **SCALE AND RESOURCE COMMAND.** *A structure large enough to be mistaken for elephant stables, and a nine-towered pavilion, are statements about what the treasury could pay for.*
> - ⭐ **DELIBERATE DISTINCTION BETWEEN SACRED AND SECULAR IDIOMS.** *Temples in this city are built in the southern stone tradition; **the secular buildings of the royal centre are the ones that carry the arches.** The rulers borrowed **where borrowing carried no ritual cost.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HALF TWO — and this is the half that earns marks: what they DON'T tell us.** *We do not know what the Lotus Mahal was **for**; its name is a **nineteenth-century British invention**; the only guess on record comes from **Mackenzie's map**, not from any Vijayanagara source; and NCERT itself asks whether the "elephant stables" were stables at all. ⭐ **So the buildings tell us a great deal about the rulers' TASTE, TECHNOLOGY AND RESOURCES, and almost nothing about their INTENTIONS — because the people who named these buildings for us arrived three centuries after the people who used them.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE NAYAKA CONTINUATION ANSWERS NCERT'S "DISCUSS…" BOX — *"Why did the nayakas continue with the building traditions of the rulers of Vijayanagara?"*** ⭐ **Because the nayakas were the successors of exactly the men §2.3 described: *"during the course of the seventeenth century, MANY OF THESE NAYAKAS ESTABLISHED INDEPENDENT KINGDOMS."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A new king with no ancient lineage needs to look like the old one.** *Building in the imperial style is the cheapest available claim to be the empire's heir — and the **audience hall at Madurai** and the **nayaka gopurams** are that claim in stone. *(⭐ **Note the neatness of it: they inherited the empire's ARCHITECTURE and its LEGITIMACY at the same time, and the second is what they actually wanted.**)*

>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Say this in an answer and it will land: *the Vijayanagara state borrowed its most effective military technology, its revenue institution, its gateway architecture AND its very word for "king" from the Sultanates it fought — and none of that made it less Hindu, because it borrowed INSTRUMENTS, not devotions.*** *(⭐ And remember the mirror-image fact from §3.3: **the mosques of the Muslim quarter were built in the form of Hampi's *mandapas*.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The borrowing ran BOTH ways, inside one city.**)*
>
> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The two innovations are a compact Mains point on RITUAL AND STATECRAFT.** ⭐ **Royal portrait sculpture INSIDE a temple puts the king permanently in the god's presence** *(⭐ and §7's Krishnadeva Raya statue **on the gopuram at Chidambaram** is exactly this — *"obviously the way in which the ruler wanted to project himself"*)*. **And converting the king's temple visit into a state occasion attended by the nayakas does for the sacred centre what Mahanavami did for the royal centre — it makes attendance on the king a public, witnessed obligation.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE VIRUPAKSHA TEMPLE IS THE BEST SINGLE ILLUSTRATION IN THE CHAPTER OF WHAT "DRAWING ON EARLIER TRADITIONS AND TRANSFORMING THEM" ACTUALLY MEANS — AND EXERCISE 8 ASKS EXACTLY THAT.** ⭐ **A shrine of the ninth–tenth centuries, still standing, is not demolished; it is *"substantially enlarged."* Krishnadeva Raya marks his accession by adding a HALL in front of it and a GOPURAM at its east.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the result, in NCERT's own dry sentence, is that *"the central shrine came to occupy a RELATIVELY SMALL PART of the complex."***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That sentence is worth an entire paragraph in an essay. The old sanctum was never touched, never diminished, never contradicted — and it ended up small.** *The empire did not overwrite the tradition it inherited; **it grew around it until the inheritance was a detail inside the imperial frame.*** *(⭐ **That is a far more sophisticated point than "the rulers were pious," and it is defensible straight from the text.**)*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **VITTHALA IS THE OTHER HALF OF THE SAME ARGUMENT AND IT IS A CLEAN PRELIMS FACT: *VITTHALA IS A FORM OF VISHNU GENERALLY WORSHIPPED IN MAHARASHTRA, AND THE VIJAYANAGARA RULERS BROUGHT HIS WORSHIP INTO KARNATAKA.*** ⭐ **The temple's principal deity is an IMPORT.** *(⭐⭐ **And a deeply resonant one for the rest of your syllabus — Vitthala of Pandharpur is the deity of the Marathi *varkari* tradition, of Namdev and later Tukaram. **[Theme 6](06_Theme6_Bhakti_Sufi_Traditions.md)'s devotional map and Theme 7's imperial map touch here.**)*
>
> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SO GATHER THE FOUR TRADITIONS VIJAYANAGARA DREW ON — this is the skeleton of the Exercise 8 answer:**
> - ⭐ **The DRAVIDIAN temple tradition of the CHOLAS, PALLAVAS, CHALUKYAS and HOYSALAS** *(named by NCERT; the Brihadishvara at Thanjavur and the Chennakeshava at Belur are its examples)* — **taken and *"carried literally to NEW HEIGHTS"* in the *raya gopuram*.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **INDO-ISLAMIC building technique** — **arches and domes** on the fort gateways and the Lotus Mahal.
> - ⭐ **LOCAL cults already on the site** — **Pampadevi and Virupaksha**, plus **pre-Vijayanagara JAINA temples** in the same hills.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND A DEITY FROM ANOTHER REGION ENTIRELY** — **Vitthala, from Maharashtra.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then say what the TRANSFORMATION was, because the question asks for it: they changed the SCALE (immense *raya gopurams* that dwarf the sanctum), the FUNCTION (halls for music, drama, divine weddings and swings; special images distinct from the sanctum image), the SETTING (royal portrait sculpture inside the temple, and the king's visit as a state occasion), and the ECONOMY (chariot streets, paved and lined with merchants' pavilions).** ⭐ **A Vijayanagara temple is a theatre, a market, a court and a shrine at once — and only the last of those it inherited.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The chariot street detail deserves its own line because it connects §5 to §6.** *A **straight, stone-paved street running from the gopuram, lined with pillared shops** — that is why §6's surveyors could find bazaars at all: **all that remains of them is "PILLAR BASES AND PLATFORMS," and they are in a straight line pointing at a temple gate.***

   ⭐ ** THE RESULT: "these detailed surveys have been EXTREMELY
      PAINSTAKING, and have RECOVERED AND DOCUMENTED TRACES OF
      THOUSANDS OF STRUCTURES -- FROM TINY SHRINES AND RESIDENCES
      TO ELABORATE TEMPLES. They have also led to THE RECOVERY OF

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND HOW A BAZAAR IS FOUND AT ALL: "THE LATTER HAVE
      BEEN LOCATED THROUGH FINDS OF PILLAR BASES AND PLATFORMS --

```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GRID IS WORTH UNDERSTANDING PROPERLY, BECAUSE IT IS WHY A FIND CAN BE TRUSTED.** ⭐ **A hierarchical grid — 25 lettered squares, each subdivided, each of those subdivided again — gives every single pillar base a unique address (square N → square NM → a unit within it).** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is what converts "we found some stones" into evidence: two surveyors, twenty years apart, can record the same object at the same coordinates, and a pattern of pillar bases can be recognised as A STREET rather than a scatter.**
>
> 💡 ⭐ *(NCERT's margin question — *"WHICH IS THE LETTER OF THE ALPHABET THAT WAS NOT USED?"* — is a look-at-the-printed-map exercise: **the grid has 25 squares and the alphabet has 26 letters, so exactly one letter is dropped**, as survey grids routinely do to avoid a letter being misread as a numeral. **Read it off Fig. 7.27 in the textbook; the arithmetic is the point of the question.**)*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"PILLAR BASES AND PLATFORMS — ALL THAT REMAIN OF THRIVING MARKETS"* IS THE MOST QUOTABLE LINE IN §6, AND IT IS THE PERISHABLE-SUPERSTRUCTURE POINT ARRIVING FOR THE THIRD TIME.** ⭐ **§4 said palaces had perishable superstructures. §5.2 said the chariot streets were *"lined with PILLARED PAVILIONS in which merchants set up their shops."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the pavilions had stone bases and wooden everything-else — and today a market of the richest city Paes had ever seen is a line of stone stumps.**

**📖 SOURCE 5 — *The bazaar*: PAES, and then NUNIZ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **PAES on the great street:** *"Going forward, you have **A BROAD AND BEAUTIFUL STREET** … In this street **LIVE MANY MERCHANTS**, and there you will find **ALL SORTS OF RUBIES, AND DIAMONDS, AND EMERALDS, AND PEARLS, AND SEED-PEARLS, AND CLOTHS, and EVERY OTHER SORT OF THING THERE IS ON EARTH and that you may wish to buy.** Then you have there **EVERY EVENING A FAIR** where they sell **many common HORSES AND NAGS**, and also many **citrons, and limes, and oranges, and grapes, and every other kind of garden stuff, and wood; YOU HAVE ALL IN THIS STREET.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PAES on provisions:** he described the city as ***"THE BEST-PROVIDED CITY IN THE WORLD"***, with the markets *"**stocked with provisions such as RICE, WHEAT, GRAINS, INDIA CORN and a certain amount of BARLEY AND BEANS, MOONG, PULSES AND HORSE-GRAM**"* — all *"**cheaply and abundantly available.**"*
>
> ⭐ **FERNAO NUNIZ on the same markets:** *"**overflowing with abundance of FRUITS, GRAPES AND ORANGES, LIMES, POMEGRANATES, JACKFRUIT AND MANGOES and all very cheap**"* — and on meat, sold in abundance: *"**MUTTON, PORK, VENISON, PARTRIDGES, HARES, DOVES, QUAIL and all kinds of birds, SPARROWS, RATS AND CATS AND LIZARDS**"* — in the market of ***BISNAGA*** *(the Portuguese rendering of Vijayanagara)*.

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE THINGS TO TAKE FROM SOURCE 5, AND THE THIRD IS THE ONE MOST STUDENTS MISS.**
> - ⭐ **(i) IT CORROBORATES §6 EXACTLY.** *Archaeology found **pillar bases in straight lines running from temple gopurams**; Paes describes **a broad street where merchants live and trade**. **Neither source alone would establish the bazaar; together they do** — and that is [Exercise 1's](#) "complementarity" in a single case.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) THE COMMODITY LIST IS AN ECONOMIC PROFILE.** *At one end **rubies, diamonds, emeralds, pearls and cloths** — §2.1's *"high-value exotic goods"* demanded by a wealthy population; at the other **horse-gram, moong, pulses and firewood**. ⭐ **And in the middle of the list, the item that decided the empire's politics: HORSES, sold at a fair EVERY EVENING.** *(⭐⭐ **Tie this straight back to §2.1 — *"the import of horses from Arabia and Central Asia was very important for rival kingdoms"*, the ***kudirai chettis***, the Arab traders, and the Portuguese after 1498. **The bazaar is where that strategic import became a retail transaction.**)*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) NUNIZ'S MEAT LIST IS EVIDENCE ABOUT SOCIETY, NOT ABOUT DIET.** *A market openly selling **pork, venison, hares, and — his words — sparrows, rats, cats and lizards** alongside mutton tells you that **the capital's population was extremely diverse in caste, community and means**, and that the imperial capital of a self-described *"Hindu Suratrana"* regulated none of it. ⭐ **It also tells you something about Nuniz: he lists the unfamiliar and the startling, because that is what a foreign observer notices.** *(⭐ **Exactly [Theme 5's](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md) discipline — read the traveller as well as the market.**)*

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE WARNING THE ARCHAEOLOGISTS THEMSELVES
      ISSUE -- learn this quotation; it is the best single line in
      §6 and it is attributable **
   ================================================================
   ** JOHN M. FRITZ, GEORGE MICHELL and M.S. NAGARAJA RAO, "who
      worked for years at the site": **

   ** "IN OUR STUDY OF THESE MONUMENTS OF VIJAYANAGARA WE HAVE TO
      ⭐ IMAGINE A WHOLE SERIES OF VANISHED WOODEN ELEMENTS --
      COLUMNS, BRACKETS, BEAMS, CEILINGS, OVERHANGING EAVES, AND
      TOWERS -- DECORATED WITH PLASTER AND PAINTED, PERHAPS
      BRIGHTLY. " **

   ** NCERT's conclusion: "ALTHOUGH WOODEN STRUCTURES ARE LOST,
      AND ONLY STONE STRUCTURES SURVIVE, ⭐ THE DESCRIPTIONS LEFT
      BY TRAVELLERS ALLOW US TO RECONSTRUCT SOME ASPECTS OF THE
      VIBRANT LIFE OF THE TIMES." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FRITZ–MICHELL–NAGARAJA RAO SENTENCE IS THE CORRECTIVE THE WHOLE CHAPTER HAS BEEN BUILDING TOWARDS, AND YOU SHOULD CARRY IT INTO ANY ANSWER ON HAMPI.** ⭐ **Hampi today is grey, bare, monumental stone. It was none of those things.** *It had **wooden columns, brackets, beams, ceilings, eaves and towers**, and it was **plastered and painted, *"perhaps brightly."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The austerity that photographs of Hampi convey is not a fact about Vijayanagara — it is a fact about what wood, plaster and paint do over four hundred years.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOTICE THE HONEST VERB: *"we have to IMAGINE."* Three archaeologists who spent years on the site are telling you that the correct response to the evidence includes a controlled act of imagination — because the alternative, taking the ruin at face value, is a bigger error.** *(⭐ **That is a superb line for an Essay paper on ruins, memory or heritage, and it pairs with Paes's *"twenty thousand men… looking like ants"* from §3.1 — the site's LABOUR is as invisible as its PAINT.**)*

## 7️⃣ Questions in Search of Answers ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT ends the chapter not with a summary but with a list of things it cannot tell you. Read this section as the chapter's real conclusion — and as the reason this book is Part II of a course in source criticism.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** FIRST: WHAT BUILDINGS ** DO ** TELL US -- four things, and
      NCERT lists them precisely **
   ================================================================
   * ** "the way SPACES WERE ORGANISED AND USED, HOW THEY WERE
     BUILT, WITH WHAT MATERIALS AND TECHNIQUES " **
   * ⭐ ** "we can assess THE DEFENCE REQUIREMENTS AND MILITARY
     PREPAREDNESS OF A CITY BY STUDYING ITS FORTIFICATIONS " **
   * ** "about THE SPREAD OF IDEAS AND CULTURAL INFLUENCES IF WE
     COMPARE THEM WITH BUILDINGS IN OTHER PLACES " **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "They CONVEY IDEAS WHICH THE BUILDERS OR THEIR
     PATRONS WISHED TO PROJECT. They are often SUFFUSED WITH
     SYMBOLS WHICH ARE A PRODUCT OF THEIR CULTURAL CONTEXT.
     ⭐ THESE WE CAN UNDERSTAND WHEN WE COMBINE INFORMATION FROM
     OTHER SOURCES LIKE LITERATURE, INSCRIPTIONS AND POPULAR
     TRADITIONS. " **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** SECOND: WHAT THEY DO NOT -- and NCERT asks it as
      SEVEN QUESTIONS. This is the moral centre of the chapter. **
   ================================================================
   ** "Investigations of architectural features DO NOT TELL US
      WHAT ⭐ ORDINARY MEN, WOMEN AND CHILDREN, COMPRISING THE
      VAST MAJORITY OF THE PEOPLE WHO LIVED IN THE CITY AND ITS
      OUTSKIRTS, THOUGHT ABOUT THESE IMPRESSIVE BUILDINGS." **

   * ** "WOULD THEY HAVE HAD ACCESS TO ANY OF THE AREAS WITHIN THE
     ROYAL CENTRE OR THE SACRED CENTRE?" **
   * ** "WOULD THEY HURRY PAST THE SCULPTURE, OR WOULD THEY PAUSE
     TO SEE, REFLECT AND TRY AND UNDERSTAND ITS COMPLICATED
     SYMBOLISM?" **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "AND WHAT DID THE PEOPLE WHO WORKED ON THESE
     COLOSSAL CONSTRUCTION PROJECTS THINK OF THE ENTERPRISES TO
     WHICH THEY HAD CONTRIBUTED THEIR LABOUR?" **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THIRD: AND THE QUESTIONS ABOUT THE BUILDERS **
   ================================================================
   ** THE FRAME: "WHILE RULERS TOOK ALL IMPORTANT DECISIONS ABOUT
      THE BUILDINGS TO BE CONSTRUCTED, THE SITE, THE MATERIAL TO
      BE USED AND THE STYLE TO BE FOLLOWED --" **

   * ** "WHO POSSESSED THE SPECIALISED KNOWLEDGE required for such
     enormous enterprises?" **
   * ** "WHO DREW UP THE PLANS for the buildings?" **
   * ⭐ ** "WHERE DID THE MASONS, STONECUTTERS, SCULPTORS who did
     the actual building COME FROM? ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ WERE THEY CAPTURED
     DURING WAR FROM NEIGHBOURING REGIONS?" **
   * ** "WHAT KIND OF WAGES DID THEY GET?" **
   * ** "WHO SUPERVISED THE BUILDING ACTIVITY?" **
   * ** "HOW WAS BUILDING MATERIAL TRANSPORTED AND WHERE DID IT
     COME FROM?" **

   ⭐ ** NCERT'S CLOSING: "THESE ARE SOME OF THE QUESTIONS THAT WE
      CANNOT ANSWER BY MERELY LOOKING AT THE BUILDINGS OR THEIR
      REMAINS. CONTINUING RESEARCH USING OTHER SOURCES MIGHT
      PROVIDE SOME FURTHER CLUES." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SHAPE OF §7 IS THE THING TO NOTICE: EVERY QUESTION IT ASKS IS ABOUT SOMEBODY WHO DID NOT COMMISSION A BUILDING.** ⭐ **The rulers are fully documented — *"rulers took ALL important decisions about the buildings to be constructed, the site, the material… and the style."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The architects, masons, stonecutters, sculptors, supervisors and haulers are not documented at all, and NCERT will not let you leave without noticing.**
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE SHARPEST QUESTION IS DELIBERATELY LEFT OPEN: *"WERE THEY CAPTURED DURING WAR FROM NEIGHBOURING REGIONS?"*** ⭐ **NCERT does not answer it — and the fact that it is a live possibility, unresolved, tells you something about how this city may have been built.** *(⭐ **Set it beside Paes's "fifteen or twenty thousand men, looking like ants" from §3.1. **We have a count of the workforce from a foreign tourist and no word about who they were, whether they were free, or what they were paid.**)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the same silence you have already met three times, and saying so shows you are reading the SERIES:**
> - ⭐ **[Part I Theme 1](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/01_Theme1_Bricks_Beads_and_Bones_Harappan.md)** — *who built and cleaned Harappan drains?*
> - ⭐ **[Part I Theme 4](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md)** — *the donative inscriptions at Sanchi name the givers, not the carvers.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Theme 7 §3.3** — *"there is RELATIVELY LITTLE ARCHAEOLOGICAL EVIDENCE OF THE HOUSES OF ORDINARY PEOPLE."*
> - **And now §7 — nothing at all about what any of them THOUGHT.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LINE THAT WILL SERVE YOU IN AN ESSAY: *the ruins of Vijayanagara record what the state wanted remembered, in the one material that lasts. Everything the state was indifferent to was built in wood.***

**📖 SOURCE — *Krishnadeva Raya*: the closing box, and it is a deliberate lesson in perspective ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **NCERT introduces it explicitly as a recapitulation:** *"**TO RECAPITULATE ABOUT SOME OF THE PROBLEMS OF PERSPECTIVE**, look at this beautiful statue of **KRISHNADEVA RAYA placed on THE GOPURAM OF THE TEMPLE AT CHIDAMBARAM, TAMIL NADU. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THIS IS OBVIOUSLY THE WAY IN WHICH THE RULER WANTED TO PROJECT HIMSELF.**"*
>
> ⭐ **And immediately beside it, PAES on the same king:** *"**Of MEDIUM HEIGHT, and of FAIR COMPLEXION AND GOOD FIGURE, RATHER FAT THAN THIN; he has ON HIS FACE SIGNS OF SMALLPOX.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BEST-CONSTRUCTED PAGE IN THE CHAPTER AND YOU SHOULD BE ABLE TO EXPLAIN IT IN TWO SENTENCES.** ⭐ **On the left, the king as he commissioned himself: a sculpture on a temple gateway, permanent, idealised, in stone, at Chidambaram — hundreds of kilometres from his capital. On the right, the king as a Portuguese visitor actually saw him: middle height, running to fat, and pockmarked.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Same man, two sources, and the difference between them is not error — it is PURPOSE.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NEITHER SOURCE IS "TRUER", AND THAT IS THE EXAM POINT.** ⭐ **The statue is worthless as a physical description and priceless as evidence of self-projection — it is §5.1's *"royal portrait sculpture was now displayed in temples"* in a single object, and it places the king permanently in the god's gateway, in a temple town he did not rule from.** ⭐ **Paes is worthless as evidence of royal ideology and priceless as physical description — and note his smallpox detail, which no court sculptor would ever have carved.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The historian's job is to know which question each source can answer.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **And it closes the loop with [Theme 5](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md) precisely.** *That chapter taught you to ask what a traveller's own position let him see. Here, the traveller's outsider status is exactly what makes him useful: **Paes had no stake in Krishnadeva Raya's dignity, so he wrote down the pockmarks.**)*

| Rayas and Sultans were permanently hostile | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**NOT ALWAYS OR INEVITABLY HOSTILE, IN SPITE OF RELIGIOUS DIFFERENCES**"* — Krishnadeva Raya **supported claimants in the Sultanates** and took the title ***"ESTABLISHER OF THE YAVANA KINGDOM"***; **the Sultan of Bijapur intervened to resolve Vijayanagara's succession disputes** after his death |
| The 1565 defeat was caused by religious antagonism | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ NCERT blames **RAMA RAYA's *"ADVENTUROUS POLICY"* of playing one Sultan off against another**, which *"led the Sultans to combine together and decisively defeat him"* |
| Talikota and Rakshasi-Tangadi were different battles | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The same battle, 1565** — **Rama Raya, the CHIEF MINISTER (not the king), led the army; routed by BIJAPUR, AHMADNAGAR and GOLCONDA** |
| The city was destroyed and the empire ended in 1565 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The CITY was sacked and *"totally abandoned within a few years"*; the EMPIRE continued under the ARAVIDU from PENUKONDA and later CHANDRAGIRI till the end of the seventeenth century** |
| *Amara-nayaka* derives from a Persian word | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***AMARA* is believed to derive from the SANSKRIT *SAMARA*, meaning battle or war — "it ALSO RESEMBLES the Persian term *AMIR*"** |
| The *amara-nayaka* system was borrowed from the Cholas | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"It is likely that many features of this system were **DERIVED FROM THE *IQTA* SYSTEM OF THE DELHI SULTANATE**"* |
| *Amara-nayakas* kept all the revenue they collected | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ They **retained PART** — for personal use and **for maintaining a stipulated contingent of horses and elephants**; some also went to **temples and irrigation works**; and **annual TRIBUTE went to the king** |
| Abdur Razzaq described three lines of fortification | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SEVEN lines of forts**, encircling *"not only the city but also **ITS AGRICULTURAL HINTERLAND AND FORESTS**"* |
| The fort walls were mortared masonry | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**NO MORTAR OR CEMENTING AGENT WAS EMPLOYED ANYWHERE**"* — **WEDGE-SHAPED stone blocks**, an **earth-and-rubble inner core**, a **slight taper**, and **square or rectangular bastions** |
| Vijayanagara stored grain in granaries against a siege | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ That was **the normal practice**; Vijayanagara *"adopted **A MORE EXPENSIVE AND ELABORATE STRATEGY OF PROTECTING THE AGRICULTURAL BELT ITSELF**"* — **fields inside the walls** |
| The Hiriya canal was built by Krishnadeva Raya | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"apparently built by kings of the **SANGAMA DYNASTY**"*; it drew water **from a dam across the Tungabhadra** and irrigated **the valley between the SACRED CENTRE and the URBAN CORE**. The **KAMALAPURAM tank** is of **the early fifteenth century** |
| Ordinary people's houses have been excavated | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"there is **RELATIVELY LITTLE ARCHAEOLOGICAL EVIDENCE OF THE HOUSES OF ORDINARY PEOPLE**"* — the only description is **BARBOSA's**: *"thatched, but nonetheless well built and **ARRANGED ACCORDING TO OCCUPATIONS, IN LONG STREETS**"* |
| Chinese porcelain was found in the royal centre | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ In **the NORTH-EASTERN CORNER OF THE URBAN CORE** — suggesting **rich traders**, and *"this was also **THE MUSLIM RESIDENTIAL QUARTER**"* |
| The mosques of Vijayanagara look Indo-Islamic | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Tombs and mosques located here have distinctive functions, yet **THEIR ARCHITECTURE RESEMBLES THAT OF THE *MANDAPAS* FOUND IN THE TEMPLES OF HAMPI**"* |
| The royal centre was purely secular | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Although designated as a royal centre, **IT INCLUDED OVER 60 TEMPLES**"* — plus **about thirty complexes identified as palaces** |
| Palaces and temples were built alike | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Temples "constructed ENTIRELY OF MASONRY"; the SUPERSTRUCTURE of secular buildings was of PERISHABLE MATERIALS** — which is why palaces survive only as platforms |
| The "king's palace" has been identified as the royal residence | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It is the largest enclosure but *"has NOT YIELDED DEFINITIVE EVIDENCE OF BEING A ROYAL RESIDENCE"*** |
| The *mahanavami dibba* is 40 ft square | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A base of about 11,000 SQ. FT rising to a height of 40 FT**, on **one of the highest points in the city**, and **it supported a WOODEN structure** |
| Mahanavami is a spring festival | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The great ninth day of a TEN-DAY festival in the AUTUMN months of SEPTEMBER AND OCTOBER** — **Dusehra (north), Durga Puja (Bengal), Navaratri/Mahanavami (peninsular India)** |
| The Mahanavami ceremonies were purely religious | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ They included **worship of the image AND OF THE STATE HORSE, animal sacrifice, dances, wrestling, processions — and on the LAST DAY the king INSPECTED HIS ARMY AND THE ARMIES OF THE NAYAKAS, who brought RICH GIFTS AND THE STIPULATED TRIBUTE** |
| The *mahanavami dibba* has been confirmed as the festival's site | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Scholars point out the surrounding space *"does not seem to have been adequate"* for the processions described — *"IT REMAINS AN ENIGMA"*** |
| Paes called the *mahanavami dibba* the "House of Victory" | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ He used that name for **the audience hall AND the *mahanavami dibba* TOGETHER**; on the upper platform he saw ***"a room made of CLOTH"*** and **a dais with a throne of state** |
| The Lotus Mahal is named in Vijayanagara sources | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"So named by BRITISH TRAVELLERS IN THE NINETEENTH CENTURY"; historians are NOT SURE what it was used for.** The one suggestion — **a COUNCIL CHAMBER** — comes **from a map drawn by MACKENZIE** |
| The Lotus Mahal has seven towers | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NINE — a high central one and eight along the sides**; its **arches were probably inspired by INDO-ISLAMIC techniques** |
| The Hazara Rama temple was a public temple | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In the ROYAL CENTRE, *"probably meant to be used ONLY BY THE KING AND HIS FAMILY"*.** **The central-shrine images are MISSING**; what survives is **sculpted panels, including scenes from the RAMAYANA on the INNER walls** |
| Vijayanagara's building traditions died with the city | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**TRADITIONS OF BUILDING PALATIAL STRUCTURES WERE CONTINUED BY THE NAYAKAS**"* — e.g. **the audience hall at MADURAI** — and *"some of the most spectacular **GOPURAMS** were also built by the local nayakas"* |
| The sacred centre is in the south of the city | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The ROCKY NORTHERN END, on the banks of the TUNGABHADRA** *(the ROYAL centre is the SOUTH-WESTERN part)* |
| Only Hindu shrines predate the city on that site | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Also **JAINA TEMPLES OF THE PRE-VIJAYANAGARA PERIOD** — *"this area was associated with SEVERAL sacred traditions"* |
| Virupaksha is a form of Vishnu | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A form of SHIVA**, and **the guardian deity of the kingdom**; **PAMPADEVI**, the local mother goddess, **did penance to marry him — a marriage still celebrated annually in the temple** |
| Royal orders were signed by the king's own name | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **All royal orders were signed *"SHRI VIRUPAKSHA"*, usually in the KANNADA SCRIPT** — the kings *"claimed to rule **ON BEHALF OF** the god Virupaksha"* |
| "Hindu Suratrana" is a Sanskrit title of ancient origin | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***SURATRANA* is a SANSKRITISATION of the ARABIC *SULTAN*** — the title literally means **"HINDU SULTAN"** |
| The *shikhara* over the sanctum was the tallest structure | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The *RAYA GOPURAMS* "OFTEN DWARFED THE TOWERS ON THE CENTRAL SHRINES"** and *"signalled the presence of the temple from a great distance"* |
| The Virupaksha temple was built by the Vijayanagara kings | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Its earliest shrine dates by inscription to the NINTH–TENTH CENTURIES**; Vijayanagara *"substantially enlarged"* it. **Krishnadeva Raya built the hall in front of the main shrine TO MARK HIS ACCESSION, and the EASTERN GOPURAM** — so that *"the central shrine came to occupy A RELATIVELY SMALL PART of the complex"* |
| Vitthala is a local Karnataka deity | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A form of VISHNU GENERALLY WORSHIPPED IN MAHARASHTRA** — his introduction shows how the rulers *"drew on **DIFFERENT TRADITIONS TO CREATE AN IMPERIAL CULTURE**"*. His temple has **a unique shrine designed as a CHARIOT** |
| A *kalyana mandapa* was for royal weddings | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **For the MARRIAGES OF DEITIES.** Other halls hosted **music, dance and drama before the images**, and some were **for the deities to SWING in** — using **SPECIAL IMAGES distinct from the central shrine's** |
| Chariot streets were processional routes only | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **They extended from the gopuram IN A STRAIGHT LINE, were PAVED WITH STONE SLABS, and were LINED WITH PILLARED PAVILIONS IN WHICH MERCHANTS SET UP THEIR SHOPS** |
| Hampi became a World Heritage site in 1976 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1976 = recognised as a site of NATIONAL IMPORTANCE; 1986 = UNESCO WORLD HERITAGE SITE** |
| Conservation began with the ASI in 1861 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Timeline 2: conservation begins under JOHN MARSHALL in 1902** |
| The 1980s survey divided the site into a numbered grid | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **25 SQUARES, EACH DESIGNATED BY A LETTER OF THE ALPHABET** — then subdivided, then subdivided again |
| Bazaars were found by excavation of shops | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Located through finds of PILLAR BASES AND PLATFORMS — *"all that remain of thriving markets"*** |
| What survives at Hampi is what the city looked like | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Fritz, Michell and Nagaraja Rao: we must *"IMAGINE A WHOLE SERIES OF VANISHED WOODEN ELEMENTS — columns, brackets, beams, ceilings, overhanging eaves, and towers — DECORATED WITH PLASTER AND PAINTED, PERHAPS BRIGHTLY"*** |
| The travellers were all Portuguese | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Fifteenth century: NICOLO DE CONTI (Italian trader), ABDUR RAZZAQ (ambassador of the ruler of PERSIA), AFANASII NIKITIN (merchant from RUSSIA). Sixteenth century: BARBOSA, PAES and NUNIZ — those three Portuguese** |
| Paes gave a flattering description of Krishnadeva Raya | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Of MEDIUM HEIGHT… **RATHER FAT THAN THIN; he has on his face SIGNS OF SMALLPOX**"* — set by NCERT against **the idealised statue on the gopuram at CHIDAMBARAM** |

| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What methods have been used to study the ruins of Hampi over the last two centuries, and how do they complement the priests' information?"** *(Exercise 1)* | ⭐ **Run Timeline 2 as the spine** — Mackenzie's survey map (1800) → inscriptions (1836, Fleet 1876) → photographs (Greenlaw 1856) → conservation (Marshall 1902) → protection (1976, UNESCO 1986) → the twenty-year total survey of the 1980s with its **three-level lettered grid**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then answer the "complement" half BOTH ways** — the priests gave **identification, names, deities, continuity and the location of what mattered**; the surveys gave **dates, measurement, extent, and everything memory had no reason to keep** *(bazaars, roads, ordinary houses)*. | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"How were the water requirements of Vijayanagara met?"** *(Exercise 2)* | ⭐ **Start with the constraint, not the works** — *"one of the **MOST ARID ZONES OF THE PENINSULA**"*, a natural basin on the **Tungabhadra**, granite hills, seasonal streams. Then: **embankments across the streams to make reservoirs**; the **KAMALAPURAM TANK** *(early fifteenth century — irrigated fields AND fed the royal centre by channel)*; the **HIRIYA CANAL** *(from a dam across the Tungabhadra, irrigating the valley between the sacred centre and the urban core, built by the SANGAMAS)*; **wells, rainwater tanks and temple tanks** for ordinary dwellers. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Finish with Paes's tank as engineering** — site at the mouth of two hills, **water piped ~15 km**, **carved pillars serving as sluices**, a hill broken down, **15–20,000 men** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Advantages and disadvantages of enclosing agricultural land within the fortified area."** *(Exercise 3)* | ⭐ **ADVANTAGE — NCERT's own reasoning:** medieval sieges aimed **to starve the defenders**, lasted **months or years**, and were normally met by **granaries**; Vijayanagara chose *"a **MORE EXPENSIVE AND ELABORATE STRATEGY** of protecting the agricultural belt itself"* — **a granary empties, a protected field keeps producing.** ⭐ **DISADVANTAGES:** an **enormously longer wall** to build and man *(seven lines, by Abdur Razzaq's count)*; **huge cost** in labour and stone; **the enclosed tract needs its own irrigation**, hence the Hiriya canal; **defenders spread thin**; and **fields, gardens and houses inside the walls** are hard to police in a siege. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Judgement: it converts a military problem into a HYDRAULIC and FISCAL one — which only a very rich state can do** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The significance of the rituals associated with the *mahanavami dibba*."** *(Exercise 4)* | ⭐ **Answer in the four registers — religious, military, fiscal, political** *(see §4.1)*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The core argument: Mahanavami was the *AMARA-NAYAKA* SYSTEM PERFORMED** — §2.3 required annual tribute and personal attendance with gifts, and this is the occasion on which both were delivered, in public, before the king's guests. Add **the last-day review of the king's and the nayakas' armies together**, and **the worship of the STATE HORSE** *(the empire's strategic import, given divine status)*. ⭐ **Close with NCERT's own doubt** — *"it remains an enigma"* — for the marks that honesty earns |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Is 'royal centre' an appropriate description?"** *(Exercise 6)* | ⭐ **FOR:** the most heavily fortified zone *(a third circuit, and **each set of major buildings inside its own high walls**)*; **about thirty palace complexes**; the **audience hall** and the ***mahanavami dibba***; the **Hazara Rama temple reserved for the king and his family**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AGAINST:** it holds **over 60 temples**, so it is not a secular quarter; **not one building has yielded definitive evidence of being a royal residence**; the names *"king's palace"*, *"audience hall"*, *"Lotus Mahal"*, *"elephant stables"* are **modern labels assigned from FORM**; and the *"House of Victory"* is **Paes's phrase**. ⭐ **Verdict: a useful working label for a zone whose function is inferred, and NCERT's own quotation marks are the admission** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What does the architecture of the Lotus Mahal and elephant stables tell us about the rulers?"** *(Exercise 7)* | ⭐ **Two halves** *(see §4.2)*: **what it DOES show** — Indo-Islamic **arches and domes** adopted from rivals, matching **the fort gateways**, the ***iqta*-derived *amara-nayaka* system** and the title ***Hindu Suratrana***; scale and command of resources; and a **deliberate split between sacred and secular idiom**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **What it does NOT** — we do not know the buildings' functions at all, and both names are **nineteenth-century British inventions**. *That combination — confident about the taste, ignorant of the purpose — is the honest position* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What architectural traditions inspired the architects of Vijayanagara, and how did they transform them?"** *(Exercise 8)* | ⭐ **THE FOUR SOURCES:** the **Dravidian temple tradition of the PALLAVAS, CHALUKYAS, HOYSALAS and CHOLAS** *(Brihadishvara at Thanjavur; Chennakeshava at Belur)* · **INDO-ISLAMIC arch-and-dome technique** · **local cults already on the site** *(Virupaksha, Pampadevi, pre-Vijayanagara JAINA temples)* · and **an imported deity, VITTHALA of Maharashtra**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TRANSFORMATIONS:** SCALE *(the raya gopuram dwarfing the central shrine)*; FUNCTION *(mandapas for music, drama, divine marriages and swings; special images)*; SETTING *(royal portrait sculpture in temples; temple visits as state occasions attended by nayakas)*; ECONOMY *(chariot streets paved and lined with merchants' pavilions)*. ⭐ **The one-line thesis: they "carried the tradition LITERALLY TO NEW HEIGHTS" — and moved the temple's visual summit from the deity to the gateway** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What impression of the lives of ORDINARY PEOPLE can you cull from the chapter?"** *(Exercise 9)* | ⭐ **Say first that the question is hard BECAUSE OF THE EVIDENCE, and prove it** — *"relatively little archaeological evidence of the houses of ordinary people"*, secular superstructures in perishable material, bazaars reduced to **pillar bases**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then assemble what there is:** **Barbosa** *(thatched, well built, **arranged BY OCCUPATION in long streets**)* · **Chinese porcelain in the north-eastern urban core = rich traders, and the Muslim quarter** · **numerous shrines and small temples "pointing to the prevalence of a VARIETY OF CULTS, perhaps supported by different communities"** · **wells, rainwater tanks and temple tanks** as their water supply · **Paes's and Nuniz's markets** *(pulses, horse-gram, firewood beside rubies; meat of every kind)* · **the 15–20,000 labourers on the tank** · **merchants in the chariot-street pavilions**. ⭐ **Close with §7: we know what they bought and where they lived, and nothing whatever about what they THOUGHT** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Vijayanagara and the Deccan Sultanates: conflict or coexistence?"** *(a very likely GS1 framing)* | ⭐ **Refuse the binary, as NCERT does** — *"relations… were **NOT ALWAYS OR INEVITABLY HOSTILE, IN SPITE OF RELIGIOUS DIFFERENCES**"*. **Evidence for exchange:** Krishnadeva Raya backing claimants in the Sultanates and titling himself ***"establisher of the Yavana kingdom"***; **Bijapur intervening in Vijayanagara's succession**; *"sharing of ideas, especially in the field of architecture"*; **Indo-Islamic gateways**; the ***iqta*-derived *amara-nayaka*** system; ***Hindu Suratrana***; **a Muslim residential quarter in the capital, with mosques built like *mandapas***. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Evidence for conflict:** competition for **the fertile river valleys, overseas trade revenue and horses**; the **Raichur doab (1512)**; **1565**. **Verdict: rival states in a single Deccan political system, competing for the same resources and borrowing from each other continuously — and NCERT names the trigger of 1565 as RAMA RAYA's diplomacy, not religion** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Temple patronage as statecraft"** *(reusable across the syllabus)* | ⭐ **§5.1 is NCERT's clearest statement anywhere:** *associating the ruler with the divine* · *the deity explicitly or implicitly identified with the king* · *temples as centres of learning* · *land grants and endowments* · *temples as "religious, social, cultural and economic centres"* · and **constructing, repairing AND maintaining as means of "winning support and recognition for their power, wealth and piety."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then Vijayanagara's own version:** ***"Shri Virupaksha"* on every royal order**; **rule claimed ON BEHALF of the god**; **royal portrait sculpture inside temples**; **temple visits as state occasions**; **Krishnadeva Raya's accession marked by a hall and a gopuram**. ⭐ **Link back to [Theme 6](06_Theme6_Bhakti_Sufi_Traditions.md)'s Chola–Nayanar case and forward to [Part III Theme 10](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/10_Theme10_Rebels_and_the_Raj_1857.md)'s use of religious authority in 1857** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"An imperial culture assembled from borrowings"** *(the essay-shaped angle)* | ⭐ **Gather the whole set into one argument:** a **Sanskritised Arabic royal title**; a revenue system **from the Delhi Sultanate's *iqta***; **arches and domes** on gateways and palace; **a Maharashtrian deity** installed in Karnataka; **a Chola–Hoysala temple form pushed to imperial scale**; **a local goddess's hill chosen as the capital site**; **mosques built in the form of Hampi's *mandapas***; and **Persian, Italian, Russian and Portuguese visitors** in the same markets. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thesis: Vijayanagara's "imperial culture" was not a synthesis that happened to it — NCERT says the rulers "DREW ON DIFFERENT TRADITIONS TO CREATE" it. It was policy** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What buildings cannot tell us"** *(§7 — the transferable methodology angle)* | ⭐ **The four things they DO tell** *(spatial organisation, technique, cultural influence, and the ideas patrons wished to project)* — noting that the last is understood only **by combining literature, inscriptions and popular traditions**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the silences: what ordinary people thought; whether they had access; whether the builders were CAPTURED IN WAR; their wages; who drew the plans.** Add **Fritz–Michell–Nagaraja Rao's *"we have to IMAGINE"***, the **grey-stone-versus-painted-plaster correction**, and close on **the Krishnadeva Raya statue against Paes's smallpox** |

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Vijayanagara** | ⭐ **"City of victory"** — the name of **both a city and an empire**; contemporaries called the empire the ***karnataka samrajyamu*** |
| **Hampi** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The name by which the ruins **lived on in popular memory**, derived from **PAMPADEVI**, the local mother goddess |
| ***Raya*** | ⭐ What the rulers of Vijayanagara **called themselves** |
| ***Narapati* / *ashvapati* / *gajapati*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Lord of MEN (the rayas) / lord of HORSES (the Deccan Sultans) / lord of ELEPHANTS (the Orissa lineage)** — from the popular traditions of Vijayanagara |
| ***Kudirai chetti*** | **Horse merchant** — local merchant communities in the horse trade |
| ***Yavana*** | ⭐ A **Sanskrit word used for the Greeks and other peoples who entered the subcontinent from the north-west**; Krishnadeva Raya took the title *"establisher of the Yavana kingdom"* |
| ***Amuktamalyada*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Krishnadeva Raya's work on statecraft, in TELUGU** — the source of the passage on encouraging traders |
| ***Nayaka*** | **Military chiefs** who controlled forts and had armed supporters, **usually Telugu- or Kannada-speaking**, often moving with peasants seeking land |
| ***Amara-nayaka*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Military commanders given territories to govern by the raya** — collecting revenue, keeping part for themselves and for **a stipulated contingent of horses and elephants**, sending **annual tribute**, and **appearing personally at court with gifts**. ***Amara*** from Sanskrit ***samara*** *(war)*, also resembling Persian ***amir*** |
| ***Iqta*** | ⭐ The **Delhi Sultanate's revenue-assignment system**, from which *"many features"* of the *amara-nayaka* system were derived |
| **Doab** | The land **between two rivers** — here the **Raichur doab** *(Krishna–Tungabhadra)*, acquired 1512 |
| **Rakshasi-Tangadi / Talikota** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The battle of 1565** at which **Rama Raya's forces were routed by Bijapur, Ahmadnagar and Golconda**, after which the city was sacked |
| ***Mahanavami dibba*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The **massive platform in the royal centre** — base **c. 11,000 sq. ft**, height **40 ft**, on **one of the highest points in the city**, **supporting a wooden structure**, its base **covered with relief carvings** |
| **Mahanavami** | ⭐ **"The great ninth day"** of the ten-day autumn festival — **Dusehra / Durga Puja / Navaratri** |
| **"House of Victory"** | ⭐ **PAES's name** for the audience hall and the *mahanavami dibba* together |
| **Lotus Mahal** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ A building in the royal centre, **named by NINETEENTH-CENTURY BRITISH TRAVELLERS**, **function unknown**; **nine towers**; **arches probably inspired by Indo-Islamic techniques**; possibly a **council chamber** per Mackenzie's map |
| **Hazara Rama temple** | ⭐ A temple **in the ROYAL centre**, *"probably meant to be used only by the king and his family"*; **central images missing**, **Ramayana panels on the inner walls** |
| **Elevation / plan** | ⭐ **An elevation is a VERTICAL view** of a structure *(showing what a photograph cannot)*; **a plan is a HORIZONTAL view** |
| **Virupaksha** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The guardian deity of the kingdom, a form of SHIVA** — **all royal orders were signed *"Shri Virupaksha"*, usually in Kannada script** |
| **Pampadevi** | ⭐ **The local mother goddess** who *"did penance in these hills in order to marry Virupaksha"* — **the marriage is still celebrated annually** |
| ***Hindu Suratrana*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A royal title — ***suratrana* is a SANSKRITISATION of the Arabic *SULTAN***, so it literally means **"Hindu Sultan"** |
| ***Gopuram* / *raya gopuram*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The temple gateway / the ROYAL gateway** — of *"immense scale"*, a **mark of imperial authority**, which **"often dwarfed the towers on the central shrines"** |
| ***Mandapa*** | ⭐ **A pavilion or hall** in a temple complex — for music, dance and drama before the images, for **divine marriages** *(the **kalyana mandapa**)*, and for **swings** |
| **Chariot street** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ A street **extending from the temple gopuram in a straight line**, **paved with stone slabs** and **lined with pillared pavilions in which merchants set up shops** |
| **Vitthala** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A form of VISHNU generally worshipped in MAHARASHTRA**, whose worship the rayas introduced in Karnataka; his temple has **a shrine designed as a chariot** |
| **Indo-Islamic** | ⭐ The art historians' term for the style of **the arch and the dome** introduced by the Turkish Sultans, which *"grew continually through **INTERACTION WITH LOCAL BUILDING PRACTICES** in different regions"* |
| **Bisnaga** | The **Portuguese rendering of Vijayanagara**, used by Nuniz |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in ten lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The empire's own name died in 1565; the GODDESS's name survived.** *The ruins were remembered as **Hampi**, after **Pampadevi**, and it was **the priests of her shrine and of the Virupaksha temple** who told **Colin Mackenzie** in **1800** what he was looking at.
2. **Founded c. 1336 by HARIHARA and BUKKA** *(a date NCERT's own timeline marks with a question mark)*, it ran through **four dynasties — SANGAMA → SALUVA → TULUVA → ARAVIDU** — and reached its height under **KRISHNADEVA RAYA (1509–29)** of the **Tuluvas**.
3. ⭐ **Warfare depended on cavalry, so the empire's politics depended on IMPORTED HORSES** — Arab traders first, then the ***kudirai chettis***, then **the Portuguese from 1498 with their muskets.**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Rayas and Sultans were not permanently at war** — Krishnadeva Raya backed claimants in the Sultanates and called himself *"establisher of the **Yavana** kingdom"*; **Bijapur intervened in Vijayanagara's succession**. **It was RAMA RAYA's policy of playing one Sultan against another that united them at TALIKOTA in 1565.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The *AMARA-NAYAKA* system — derived from the Delhi Sultanate's *IQTA* — gave the empire its army and eventually destroyed it**, as the nayakas became independent kings in the seventeenth century.
6. ⭐ **The city is in "one of the most arid zones of the peninsula", so it is a HYDRAULIC achievement first** — reservoirs on every stream, the **Kamalapuram tank**, the **Hiriya canal**, and a royal tank fed by **fifteen kilometres of pipes**.
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Its walls used NO MORTAR ANYWHERE** — wedge-shaped blocks, an earth-and-rubble core — and, uniquely, **they enclosed the FARMLAND**, because *"most rulers built granaries; these rulers protected the fields."*
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Temples were built entirely of masonry; palaces were stone platforms under perishable superstructures** — so the sacred centre survives as architecture and the royal centre survives as floors, which is why every building there wears **quotation marks**.
9. ⭐ **MAHANAVAMI was the *amara-nayaka* system performed in public** — worship of the image and **of the state horse**, sacrifices, wrestling, processions, and on the last day **an inspection of the king's and the nayakas' armies, with tribute and gifts handed over.** *(And the platform may not even be the right platform — *"it remains an enigma."*)*
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The chapter ends by listing what stone cannot say** — whether ordinary people could enter these zones, what they thought of them, whether the masons were **captured in war**, what they were paid — and by setting **Krishnadeva Raya's statue on a gopuram** against **Paes's note that he was fat and pockmarked.** ⭐ **Hampi was never grey: it was wood, plaster and bright paint, and the archaeologists tell you that you have to IMAGINE it.**
