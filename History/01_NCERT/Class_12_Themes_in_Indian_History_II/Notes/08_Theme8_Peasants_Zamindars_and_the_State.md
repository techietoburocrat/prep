# Theme 8 — Peasants, Zamindars and the State
## *Agrarian Society and the Mughal Empire* — *(c. sixteenth to seventeenth century)*

> **The one-line point:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the chapter about the 85 per cent — and it is written almost entirely from documents produced by the people who taxed them.** *NCERT states the problem in its second paragraph and never lets you forget it: **"peasants did not write about themselves."** Everything that follows is a reconstruction of rural India from **a court chronicle, a revenue manual and the records of a trading company.*

[Index](00_Index.md) | [← Theme 7 (Vijayanagara)](07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) | [Next: Supplement — Kings and Chronicles →](09_Supplement_Kings_and_Chronicles_The_Mughal_Courts.md)

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT'S OPENING PARAGRAPH IS A COMPLETE THESIS STATEMENT — read it as four moves:** *"During the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries **ABOUT 85 PER CENT OF THE POPULATION OF INDIA LIVED IN ITS VILLAGES.** ⭐ **BOTH PEASANTS AND LANDED ELITES WERE INVOLVED IN AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION AND CLAIMED RIGHTS TO A SHARE OF THE PRODUCE.** This created relationships of **COOPERATION, COMPETITION AND CONFLICT** among them. **THE SUM OF THESE AGRARIAN RELATIONSHIPS MADE UP RURAL SOCIETY.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And then the outsider arrives:** *"At the same time **AGENCIES FROM OUTSIDE ALSO ENTERED INTO THE RURAL WORLD. MOST IMPORTANT AMONG THESE WAS THE MUGHAL STATE, WHICH DERIVED THE BULK OF ITS INCOME FROM AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION.** ⭐ Agents of the state — **REVENUE ASSESSORS, COLLECTORS, RECORD KEEPERS** — sought to control rural society **so as to ensure that CULTIVATION TOOK PLACE and the state got ITS REGULAR SHARE OF TAXES from the produce.** Since **MANY CROPS WERE GROWN FOR SALE, TRADE, MONEY AND MARKETS ENTERED THE VILLAGES** and linked the agricultural areas with the towns."*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three claims are made before the chapter properly begins, and every section below is an elaboration of one of them: (i) the village was NOT a self-contained world — money and markets were already inside it; (ii) the state's interest was NOT merely extractive but PRODUCTIVE, because a peasant who stops cultivating pays nothing; and (iii) rural society IS the set of relationships over who gets what share, not a place.**

## 1️⃣ Peasants and Agricultural Production ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** THE UNIT: "The BASIC UNIT of agricultural society was THE
      VILLAGE, inhabited by peasants who performed THE MANIFOLD
      SEASONAL TASKS that made up agricultural production
      throughout the year -- TILLING THE SOIL, SOWING SEEDS,
      HARVESTING THE CROP WHEN IT WAS RIPE. Further, they
      CONTRIBUTED THEIR LABOUR TO THE PRODUCTION OF AGRO-BASED
      GOODS SUCH AS SUGAR AND OIL." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND IMMEDIATELY, THE CORRECTION THAT SETS UP §4: **
   ** "BUT RURAL INDIA WAS NOT CHARACTERISED BY SETTLED PEASANT
      PRODUCTION ALONE. Several kinds of areas such as ⭐ LARGE
      TRACTS OF DRY LAND OR HILLY REGIONS were not cultivable in
      the same way as the more fertile expanses of land. In
      addition, ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ FOREST AREAS MADE UP A SUBSTANTIAL
      PROPORTION OF TERRITORY. We need to keep this VARIED
      TOPOGRAPHY in mind when discussing agrarian society." **
```

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note that the peasant is already a manufacturer in the first paragraph — *"agro-based goods such as SUGAR AND OIL."*** *That is not a detail; **it is the seed of §1.4's *jins-i kamil* and §2.3's artisans**, and it is why the "subsistence agriculture" question in Exercise 2 has to be answered NO.*

```
      LEARN THIS LIST; IT IS THE SECOND HALF OF EXERCISE 1. **
   ================================================================
   ** NCERT: the Ain "can be SUPPLEMENTED BY DESCRIPTIONS CONTAINED
      IN SOURCES EMANATING FROM REGIONS AWAY FROM THE MUGHAL
      CAPITAL." **

   ** 2. PROVINCIAL REVENUE RECORDS ** -- ⭐ "DETAILED REVENUE
      RECORDS FROM GUJARAT, MAHARASHTRA AND RAJASTHAN dating from

   ** 3. EAST INDIA COMPANY RECORDS ** -- ⭐ "the EXTENSIVE RECORDS

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND WHAT THEY GIVE YOU THAT THE AIN CANNOT: **
   ** "ALL THESE SOURCES RECORD INSTANCES OF CONFLICTS BETWEEN

```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LOGIC OF THAT LAST SENTENCE IS THE BEST METHODOLOGICAL POINT IN THE CHAPTER, AND MOST STUDENTS WALK PAST IT.** ⭐ **The *Ain* is a record of ORDER; the provincial and Company records are records of DISPUTE.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And a dispute is the only place a peasant's own view of fairness ever enters the archive — because a peasant appears in writing at the moment he refuses to pay.** *(⭐ **This is exactly why §2.2's PETITIONS matter so much, and why NCERT devotes a whole paragraph to them.**)*
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 1 — *"What are the problems in using the Ain as a source for reconstructing agrarian history? HOW DO HISTORIANS DEAL WITH THIS SITUATION?"* — HAS TWO HALVES AND THE SECOND HALF IS WHERE THE MARKS ARE.** ⭐ **Do not stop at "it is biased." The answer is: historians deal with it by TRIANGULATION** — reading the *Ain* alongside **Gujarat, Maharashtra and Rajasthan revenue records** and **East India Company records**, i.e. **sources produced AWAY FROM THE CENTRE and, crucially, in CONFLICT rather than in celebration.** ⭐ **Add §8's technical problems too** *(errors in totalling; data not collected uniformly; no caste data on zamindars for **BENGAL AND ORISSA**; prices and wages drawn mainly from **around AGRA**)* — **that combination of a political bias and a statistical skew, plus the named remedy, is a full-mark answer.**

### 1.2 Peasants and their lands ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE VOCABULARY -- straight Prelims material **
   ================================================================
   ** "The term which INDO-PERSIAN SOURCES of the Mughal period
      MOST FREQUENTLY USED to denote a peasant was ⭐ RAIYAT
      (plural, RIAYA) or MUZARIAN. In addition, we also encounter
      the terms KISAN or ASAMI." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE TWO KINDS OF PEASANT -- "sources of the
      SEVENTEENTH CENTURY refer to two kinds" **
   ================================================================

   ** KHUD-KASHTA ** -- ⭐ "RESIDENTS OF THE VILLAGE IN WHICH THEY
      HELD THEIR LANDS"

   ** PAHI-KASHTA ** -- ⭐ "NON-RESIDENT CULTIVATORS WHO BELONGED
      TO SOME OTHER VILLAGE, BUT CULTIVATED LANDS ELSEWHERE ON A
      CONTRACTUAL BASIS"

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE TWO ROUTES INTO IT -- NCERT gives BOTH,
      and an answer that gives only one is half an answer: **
   * ** OUT OF CHOICE ** -- "for example, when TERMS OF REVENUE IN
     A DISTANT VILLAGE WERE MORE FAVOURABLE"
   * ** OUT OF COMPULSION ** -- "for example, FORCED BY ECONOMIC
     DISTRESS AFTER A FAMINE"

   ================================================================
   ** THE SIZE OF HOLDINGS -- three numbers, all examinable **
   ================================================================
   * ** NORTH INDIA: "SELDOM did the average peasant possess MORE
     THAN A PAIR OF BULLOCKS AND TWO PLOUGHS; ⭐ MOST POSSESSED
     EVEN LESS." **
   * ** GUJARAT: peasants possessing ABOUT SIX ACRES "were
     considered to be AFFLUENT" **
   * ⭐ ** BENGAL: "FIVE ACRES was THE UPPER LIMIT OF AN AVERAGE
     PEASANT FARM; TEN ACRES WOULD MAKE ONE A RICH ASAMI." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE PROPERTY PRINCIPLE -- this single sentence
      demolishes the "little republic" myth before §2.4 gets to it **
   ================================================================
   ** "CULTIVATION WAS BASED ON THE PRINCIPLE OF INDIVIDUAL
      OWNERSHIP. ⭐ PEASANT LANDS WERE BOUGHT AND SOLD IN THE SAME
      WAY AS THE LANDS OF OTHER PROPERTY OWNERS." **

   ** AND THE PHYSICAL PROOF -- a nineteenth-century description of
      the Delhi-Agra region which NCERT says "would apply equally
      to the seventeenth century": **
   ** "The cultivating peasants (ASAMIS), who plough up the fields,
      ⭐ MARK THE LIMITS OF EACH FIELD, FOR IDENTIFICATION AND
      DEMARCATION, WITH BORDERS OF (RAISED) EARTH, BRICK AND THORN
      so that THOUSANDS OF SUCH FIELDS MAY BE COUNTED IN A
      VILLAGE." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BOUNDARY MARKERS ARE THE ARGUMENT, NOT A PICTURESQUE DETAIL — AND THIS IS A LOVELY POINT TO MAKE IN AN ESSAY.** ⭐ **Earth, brick and thorn ridges around thousands of separately countable fields is what INDIVIDUAL OWNERSHIP looks like on the ground.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A genuinely communal village would have no need to mark, count or identify anything.** *(⭐ **It is also what makes the revenue system of §6 possible at all — a state can only measure land that is already divided.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 1 — *Peasants on the move*: BABUR, in the *BABUR NAMA* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"**In Hindustan HAMLETS AND VILLAGES, TOWNS INDEED, ARE DEPOPULATED AND SET UP IN A MOMENT!** ⭐ **If the people of a large town, one inhabited for years even, FLEE FROM IT, they do it in such a way that NOT A SIGN OR TRACE OF THEM REMAINS IN A DAY AND A HALF.** On the other hand, if they fix their eyes on a place to settle, **THEY NEED NOT DIG WATER COURSES BECAUSE THEIR CROPS ARE ALL RAIN-GROWN**, and as **THE POPULATION OF HINDUSTAN IS UNLIMITED IT SWARMS IN. They make A TANK OR A WELL; they need not build houses or set up walls … KHAS-GRASS ABOUNDS, WOOD IS UNLIMITED, HUTS ARE MADE, AND STRAIGHTAWAY THERE IS A VILLAGE OR A TOWN!**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BABUR IS DESCRIBING THE PEASANT'S GREATEST WEAPON, AND HE DOES NOT REALISE IT.** ⭐ **He reads this mobility as a curiosity of Hindustan. What he is actually recording is the condition that §2.2 names explicitly: *"peasants took recourse to more drastic forms of resistance, such as **DESERTING THE VILLAGE.** The relatively easy availability of **UNCULTIVATED LAND** and the **COMPETITION OVER LABOUR RESOURCES** made this **AN EFFECTIVE WEAPON IN THE HANDS OF CULTIVATORS.**"*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So read Source 1 forward into §2.2 and the chapter's central bargaining relationship appears.** *Land was abundant; **LABOUR was the scarce factor**. A state or zamindar that pressed too hard **lost the cultivators to a neighbour offering better terms** — which is precisely why a peasant could become ***pahi-kashta* "out of CHOICE."** ⭐ **The three things Babur lists as making resettlement cheap — rain-fed crops, unlimited grass and wood, huts not houses — are the reasons the threat was credible.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A peasantry that can rebuild a village in a day and a half is a peasantry with leverage.**
>
> 💡 ⭐ *(⭐⭐ **This also explains the state's behaviour in §6.** *If your revenue base can walk away, you do not merely tax — you **encourage cultivation, fund canals, and instruct your officials, as Aurangzeb did in 1665, to weigh "the financial interests of the government AND THE WELFARE OF THE PEASANTRY."*** **That clause is self-interest, not sentiment.**)*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE PLOUGH SENTENCE IS THE ONE TO UNDERSTAND, BECAUSE IT REVERSES AN ASSUMPTION EXAMINERS LOVE TO TEST.** ⭐ **A light plough that makes shallow furrows is not primitive technology; NCERT says it *"PRESERVED THE MOISTURE BETTER during the intensely hot months."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Deep ploughing in a hot, semi-arid climate turns wet subsoil up to evaporate. The shallow furrow is an ADAPTATION, not a limitation** — the same logic behind modern minimum-tillage. *(⭐ **Say this in an answer on Indian agricultural technology and you separate yourself immediately from "Indian agriculture was backward."**)*

**📖 SOURCE 2 — *Irrigating trees and fields*: BABUR again, on two devices ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **The observation:** *"**The greater part of Hindustan country is situated ON LEVEL LAND.** Many though its towns and cultivated lands are, **IT NOWHERE HAS RUNNING WATERS** … For … **WATER IS NOT AT ALL A NECESSITY IN CULTIVATING CROPS AND ORCHARDS. AUTUMN CROPS GROW BY THE DOWNPOUR OF THE RAINS THEMSELVES; and strange it is that SPRING CROPS GROW EVEN WHEN NO RAINS FALL.** (However) **to young trees water is made to flow by means of BUCKETS OR WHEELS** …"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DEVICE 1 — THE PERSIAN WHEEL, at *LAHORE and DIPALPUR* (both in present-day Pakistan):** *"people water by means of **A WHEEL**. They make **TWO CIRCLES OF ROPE long enough to suit the depths of the well**, fix **STRIPS OF WOOD** between them, and on these fasten **PITCHERS**. The ropes with the wood and attached pitchers are put over the wheel-well. **At one end of the wheel-axle A SECOND WHEEL IS FIXED, and close to it ANOTHER ON AN UPRIGHT AXLE. THE LAST WHEEL THE BULLOCK TURNS; ITS TEETH CATCH IN THE TEETH OF THE SECOND (WHEEL)**, and thus the wheel with the pitchers is turned. **A TROUGH is set where the water empties from the pitchers and from this the water is conveyed everywhere.**"*
>
> ⭐ **DEVICE 2 — THE BUCKET-AND-BULLOCK, at *AGRA, CHANDWAR and BAYANA* (all in present-day Uttar Pradesh):** *"people water with **A BUCKET** … At the well-edge they set up **A FORK OF WOOD, having A ROLLER adjusted between the forks**, tie a rope to **A LARGE BUCKET**, put the rope over a roller, and **TIE ITS OTHER END TO THE BULLOCK. ONE PERSON MUST DRIVE THE BULLOCK, ANOTHER EMPTY THE BUCKET.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT'S QUESTION ON THIS SOURCE IS EXCELLENT AND IT WANTS A COMPARATIVE ANSWER: *"COMPARE the irrigation devices observed by Babur with what you have learnt about irrigation in VIJAYANAGARA. What kind of RESOURCES would each of these systems require? WHICH SYSTEMS COULD ENSURE THE PARTICIPATION OF PEASANTS in improving agricultural technology?"***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The answer turns on SCALE AND WHO CAN BUILD IT, and it is one of the best cross-chapter points available to you in Part II:**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **[VIJAYANAGARA (Theme 7)](07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) built STATE-SCALE HYDRAULIC WORKS** — the **Kamalapuram tank**, the **Hiriya canal from a dam across the Tungabhadra**, and Paes's royal tank *(a hill broken down, water piped **fifteen kilometres**, **fifteen to twenty thousand men** at work)*. ⭐ **Resources required: an empire's treasury, forced or paid mass labour, engineers, and the political authority to break a hill.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A peasant can neither build nor improve such a thing — he can only receive water from it.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BABUR'S DEVICES ARE HOUSEHOLD TECHNOLOGY** — wood, rope, pitchers, a roller, **one well and one bullock.** ⭐ **Resources required: a bullock, a village carpenter and a potter — all of whom §2.3 tells you live in the village and are paid in grain.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS is the system that permits peasant participation in improving technology**, because the peasant owns it, repairs it, and can copy an improvement he sees at the next well.
> - ⭐ **And note the middle term NCERT supplies: the STATE dug and repaired canals (*nahr*, *nala*, the *shahnahr*), while the PANCHAYAT (§2.2) used its common fund for *"construction of a BUND or DIGGING A CANAL which peasants usually could not afford to do on their own."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So there are THREE tiers of water technology in this book — imperial, communal and household — and the middle one, the panchayat's, is the one most students forget.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **One caution about Babur as a witness, in keeping with the whole of Part II: he is a newcomer from Central Asia, and *"strange it is that spring crops grow even when no rains fall"* is the sentence of a man raised where irrigation was compulsory.** ⭐ **His astonishment is evidence of what HE was used to, not of what India lacked** — *and it sits oddly beside his own claim that Hindustan *"nowhere has running waters,"* which the Ganga, Yamuna and Indus would dispute. *(⭐ **Exactly [Theme 5's](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md) discipline: the traveller measures the new place against home.**)*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 2 — *"To what extent is it possible to characterise agricultural production in the sixteenth–seventeenth centuries as SUBSISTENCE AGRICULTURE?"* — AND THE ANSWER IS *"ONLY PARTLY, AND LESS THAN YOU WOULD EXPECT."* Build it from six pieces:**
> - ⭐ **(i) THE STAPLES WERE DOMINANT** — *"since the primary purpose of agriculture is to feed people, basic staples such as **rice, wheat or millets** were the most frequently cultivated crops."* **Concede this first; the question says "to what extent."**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) BUT ***JINS-I KAMIL*** EXISTED AS A CATEGORY, AND THE STATE PROMOTED IT** — cotton and sugarcane *par excellence*, plus oilseeds and lentils, **"as they brought in more revenue."** *A state that steers cropping toward cash crops is not presiding over subsistence farming.*
> - ⭐ **(iii) THE TWO CROPS WERE GROWN ON THE SAME HOLDING** — NCERT's own words, ***"closely intertwined in an AVERAGE peasant's holding"*** — not a rich-peasant phenomenon.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iv) REVENUE WAS ASSESSED AND COLLECTED IN CASH** *(§2.4, §6)* — **which FORCES market participation.** *A peasant taxed in silver must sell grain for silver whether he wants to or not.*
> - ⭐ **(v) THE VILLAGE WAS FULL OF MONEY** — Tavernier's ***shroff*** in all but the smallest villages; **advances and wages in cash** to weavers and to producers of **cotton, silk and indigo**.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(vi) AND THE PRODUCE REACHED THE WORLD** — §7's **silver arriving from the Americas and Japan to pay for Indian goods**, and §4.2's **gum lac becoming a major overseas export.**
> - 🎯 **VERDICT: production began with subsistence and did not stop there. The right formula is NCERT's — *subsistence and commercial production were intertwined within a single holding* — and the mechanism that made it unavoidable was CASH REVENUE.**

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TOBACCO BOX — a complete little story, and it is dated, which makes it Prelims-shaped:**
> - ⭐ **It *"arrived FIRST IN THE DECCAN"* and spread to **northern India in the EARLY YEARS OF THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TELLING DETAIL: *"THE AIN DOES NOT MENTION TOBACCO IN THE LISTS OF CROPS IN NORTHERN INDIA."*** **Akbar and his nobles came across it for the first time in 1604** — six years after the *Ain* was completed in **1598**.
> - **Smoking (in *hookahs* or *chillums*) *"caught on in a big way"*; JAHANGIR was so concerned about its addiction that HE BANNED IT.**
> - ⭐ **The ban was *"TOTALLY INEFFECTIVE"* — by the end of the seventeenth century tobacco was *"a major article of CONSUMPTION, CULTIVATION AND TRADE ALL OVER INDIA."***
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **USE THE TOBACCO BOX IN THE EXERCISE 1 ANSWER — it is the neatest possible demonstration of the *Ain*'s limits, and almost nobody thinks of it.** ⭐ **The *Ain* is a snapshot dated 1598. Within a decade a major new crop was spreading across northern India, and the *Ain* cannot know it.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A gazetteer is not wrong about the year it describes; it is simply mute about every year after.** *(⭐ **And the failed ban is a second, independent point — an emperor's prohibition against a genuinely popular commodity was unenforceable, which tells you something real about the reach of the Mughal state in the countryside.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE POPULATION BOX — one statistic, and it is the payoff of the whole section:** *"One important outcome of such **VARIED AND FLEXIBLE FORMS OF AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION** was **A SLOW DEMOGRAPHIC GROWTH. Despite periodic disruptions caused by FAMINES AND EPIDEMICS**, India's population increased, according to calculations by economic historians, **by ABOUT 50 MILLION PEOPLE BETWEEN 1600 AND 1800 — AN INCREASE OF ABOUT 33 PER CENT OVER 200 YEARS.**"*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note the word NCERT chooses to explain it: *FLEXIBLE.* Two or three crops a year, 39–50 varieties, staples and cash crops on the same holding, new crops absorbed from three continents — that redundancy is what carried a population through famine and epidemic to a third more people.** ⭐ *(⭐⭐ **And note the honesty of "slow" and "according to calculations by economic historians" — this is an ESTIMATE, not a census. There was no census.**)*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXERCISE 6 — *"To what extent do you think CASTE was a factor in influencing social and economic relations in agrarian society?"* — AND THE WHOLE ART OF THE ANSWER IS IN NCERT'S OWN PHRASE, *"NOT SO MARKED AT INTERMEDIATE LEVELS."***
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The structure to use: caste was DECISIVE at the bottom and NEGOTIABLE in the middle.**
> - ⭐ **AT THE BOTTOM it was almost absolute** — *"despite the abundance of cultivable land"*, certain groups were **assigned menial tasks and relegated to poverty**, formed a **large section** of the village, had **the least resources**, and NCERT explicitly compares them to **the Dalits of modern India**. **And it crossed religious lines** — ***halalkhoran* housed outside the village boundary**, ***mallahzadas* in Bihar comparable to slaves.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **IN THE MIDDLE, ECONOMICS MOVED PEOPLE.** *The **Ahirs, Gujars and Malis rose "BECAUSE OF THE PROFITABILITY of cattle rearing and horticulture"** — **an explicitly economic cause of ritual advancement.** The **Sadgops and Kaivartas** moved from pastoral and fishing occupations into **peasant status** *(and §5 adds that the Sadgops went further still, carving out **powerful zamindaris** in Bengal)*. The **Gauravas near Vrindavan claimed Rajput status.** And in **Marwar, Rajputs and Jats appear as peasants sharing the same space** — **status did not determine occupation at that level.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THEN CLOSE ACROSS THE CHAPTER, because caste appears in five more places:** ***jati panchayats* deciding ritual precedence, marriage validity and land claims (§2.2)** · **the panchayat's duty to prevent "any offence against their caste", enforced by expulsion (§2.2)** · **petitions against *begar* demanded by "SUPERIOR" castes (§2.2)** · **caste as one basis of the zamindars' elevated status, and Abu'l Fazl's "BRAHMANA-RAJPUT COMBINE" (§5)** · ⭐ **and the fact that *"zamindaris were bought and sold quite briskly"*, which "PERMITTED PEOPLE BELONGING TO THE RELATIVELY 'LOWER' CASTES TO ENTER THE RANK OF ZAMINDARS" (§5).**
> - 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **VERDICT: caste set the floor and the ceiling; the market moved the middle. And the mechanism of mobility was always the same — a group that acquired ECONOMIC weight subsequently claimed RITUAL status, not the reverse.**


> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT LAST SENTENCE IS ONE OF THE MOST IMPORTANT IN THE CHAPTER AND IT IS EASY TO READ PAST. IT DEFINES THE MUGHAL STATE'S ACTUAL FOOTPRINT IN THE VILLAGE.** ⭐ **The empire reserved to itself **CRIMINAL JUSTICE** and **REVENUE** — and left **civil disputes, land claims, marriage validity and ritual precedence** to bodies it neither appointed nor controlled.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is not weakness; it is a deliberate division of labour. A state that tried to adjudicate marriage norms in a hundred thousand villages would collapse under the cost, and would gain no revenue by it.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Say it this way in an answer: *the Mughal state was DEEP but NARROW in the countryside — it reached every field with a measuring rod (§6) and almost nothing else.*** *(⭐ **And note the corollary: **caste hierarchy was enforced locally, by the community itself, with the state's endorsement.** That is a far more durable arrangement than imperial decree, and it is why it survived every dynasty that came and went above it.**)*
>
> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "CORRUPT MANDALS" BOX — do not skip it; it is the counterweight to the tidy picture above:** *"The mandals **OFTEN MISUSED THEIR POSITIONS.** They were principally accused of **DEFRAUDING VILLAGE ACCOUNTS IN CONNIVANCE WITH THE *PATWARI***, and for **UNDERASSESSING THE REVENUE THEY OWED FROM THEIR OWN LANDS IN ORDER TO PASS THE ADDITIONAL BURDEN ON TO THE SMALLER CULTIVATOR.**"*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is a precise mechanism, not a general complaint — and it is worth stating precisely.** ⭐ **The headman supervised the accounts, and the accountant worked for him. When a village's total assessment is fixed, every rupee the headman shifts off his own land lands on somebody else's.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A collective assessment turns the man who keeps the register into the man who decides who is poor.** *(⭐ **Hold this thought for [Part III Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) — the same structural problem reappears under the Permanent Settlement, in English, with a jamabandi.**)*

>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE CONTENT OF THE PETITIONS IS A CONCEPT, NOT A COMPLAINT: THE RIGHT TO SUBSISTENCE, SANCTIONED BY CUSTOM.** ⭐ **The petitioners are not arguing that the tax is illegal; they are arguing that it is *"MORALLY ILLEGITIMATE"* — that a demand which **endangers survival in a drought year** breaks an obligation the state owes them.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is a claim about what a ruler is FOR.** *(⭐ **This is the historians' "MORAL ECONOMY" of the peasantry, and it is the single most reusable idea in this chapter — it reappears in [Part III Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) with the Deccan riots and the ryots' petitions, and in [Class 10 Ch.2](../../Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/02_Nationalism_in_India.md) where Awadh's peasants understood *swaraj* as the end of *begar* — **the same word, *begar*, that appears in these seventeenth-century petitions.**)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOTE THE ESCALATION LADDER NCERT SETS OUT, BECAUSE IT SHOWS THE PANCHAYAT'S REAL POSITION: petition → panchayat suggests COMPROMISE → if reconciliation fails, DESERTION.** ⭐ **The panchayat is not the peasants' champion; it is an OLIGARCHY of propertied elders that acts as a broker.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And when brokerage fails, the peasant's final sanction is not violence but ABSENCE — and it works, because land was abundant and labour was scarce.** *(⭐ **Full circle to Babur's Source 1: a people who can raise a village in a day and a half can also abandon one.**)*

```
   ================================================================
   ** HOW ARTISANS WERE PAID -- THREE SYSTEMS **
   ================================================================

   ** 1. A SHARE OF THE HARVEST ** -- "THE MOST COMMON WAY"
   ** 2. AN ALLOTMENT OF LAND ** -- ⭐ "perhaps CULTIVABLE WASTES,
      WHICH WAS LIKELY TO BE DECIDED BY THE PANCHAYAT. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ IN
      MAHARASHTRA SUCH LANDS BECAME THE ARTISANS' MIRAS OR WATAN --
      THEIR HEREDITARY HOLDING."
   ** 3. A NEGOTIATED HOUSEHOLD ARRANGEMENT ** -- "artisans and
      individual peasant households entered into A MUTUALLY
      NEGOTIATED SYSTEM OF REMUNERATION, MOST OF THE TIME GOODS FOR
      SERVICES." ⭐ Eighteenth-century records: ** zamindars in
      BENGAL remunerated blacksmiths, carpenters, even goldsmiths

      PRELIMS TRAP: "THIS LATER CAME TO BE DESCRIBED AS THE
      JAJMANI SYSTEM, ⭐ THOUGH THE TERM WAS NOT IN VOGUE IN THE

```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE *JAJMANI* WARNING IS EXACTLY THE KIND OF THING NCERT PUTS IN AND EXAMINERS TEST.** ⭐ **The arrangement existed; the NAME did not.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"*Jajmani*" is a later label applied backwards onto seventeenth-century evidence — and the danger of a backward-applied label is that it imports a whole theory of a rigid, unchanging, ritually fixed village economy into evidence that actually shows *"MUTUALLY NEGOTIATED"* terms, cash payments, and land grants decided case by case by the panchayat.** *(⭐ **Compare §2.4's *"little republic"* — a second nineteenth-century label imposed on the same evidence. **NCERT is quietly teaching you to distrust names invented by later observers, which is the historiographical thread of all of Part II.**)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***MIRAS* / *WATAN* is worth its own line, because it shows how a service became a property right.** ⭐ **A blacksmith paid in land — often cultivable waste, allotted by the panchayat — held that land HEREDITARILY. So the right to serve the village became an inheritable asset**, which is the same logic by which the panchayat's elders held *"HEREDITARY RIGHTS OVER THEIR PROPERTY."* *(⭐⭐ **Remember the word ***watan***: it comes back in modern Indian history as the *watandar* of the Deccan.**)*

### 2.4 A "little republic"? ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE CLAIM, AND THE REFUTATION. NCERT'S OWN
      QUESTION MARK IN THE HEADING IS THE ANSWER. **
   ================================================================
   ** THE CLAIM: "SOME BRITISH OFFICIALS IN THE NINETEENTH CENTURY
      saw the village as A 'LITTLE REPUBLIC' MADE UP OF FRATERNAL
      PARTNERS SHARING RESOURCES AND LABOUR IN A COLLECTIVE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** REFUTATION 1 -- INEQUALITY: **
   ** "HOWEVER, THIS WAS NOT A SIGN OF RURAL EGALITARIANISM." **
   * ** "There was INDIVIDUAL OWNERSHIP OF ASSETS " **
   * ** "and DEEP INEQUITIES BASED ON CASTE AND GENDER
     DISTINCTIONS " **
   * ⭐ ** "A GROUP OF POWERFUL INDIVIDUALS DECIDED THE AFFAIRS OF
     THE VILLAGE, EXPLOITED THE WEAKER SECTIONS AND HAD THE
     AUTHORITY TO DISPENSE JUSTICE " **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** REFUTATION 2 -- AND NCERT CALLS IT "MORE
      IMPORTANTLY": THE VILLAGE WAS NOT SELF-CONTAINED. **
   ** "MORE IMPORTANTLY, A CASH NEXUS HAD ALREADY DEVELOPED THROUGH
      TRADE BETWEEN VILLAGES AND TOWNS." **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "IN THE MUGHAL HEARTLAND TOO, REVENUE WAS
     ASSESSED AND COLLECTED IN CASH " **
   * ** "ARTISANS PRODUCING FOR THE EXPORT MARKET (for example,
     WEAVERS) RECEIVED THEIR ADVANCES OR WAGES IN CASH " **
   * ** "as did PRODUCERS OF COMMERCIAL PRODUCTS LIKE COTTON, SILK
     OR INDIGO " **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TWO REFUTATIONS ATTACK TWO DIFFERENT WORDS, AND A GOOD ANSWER SAYS WHICH IS WHICH.** ⭐ **"REPUBLIC" fails because the village was an OLIGARCHY of propertied elders that excluded its menial workers, enforced caste, and exploited the weak. "LITTLE" — meaning small, closed, self-sufficient — fails because CASH was everywhere: in the revenue demand, in the weaver's advance, in the indigo grower's wages, and in Tavernier's village *shroff*.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Neither half of the phrase survives.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND ASK WHY THE BRITISH BELIEVED IT — that is the point that turns a good answer into a very good one.** ⭐ **A countryside of timeless, self-sufficient, unchanging little republics is a countryside with **no history of its own**, whose institutions owe nothing to any Indian state, and which can be **reorganised from outside without destroying anything that was working.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The idea was administratively useful before it was ever historically tested.** *(⭐ **This is the direct bridge to [Part III Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) and its Fifth Report — the same officials, the same archive, and the same habit of describing rural India in terms that justified what they intended to do to it.**)*
>
> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TAVERNIER BOX — the single sharpest piece of evidence against "little":** *The seventeenth-century French traveller **JEAN-BAPTISTE TAVERNIER** found it remarkable that in *"**India A VILLAGE MUST BE VERY SMALL INDEED IF IT HAS NOT A MONEY-CHANGER CALLED A SHROFF. (They) ACT AS BANKERS TO MAKE REMITTANCES OF MONEY (and who) ENHANCE THE RUPEE AS THEY PLEASE FOR PAISA AND THE PAISA FOR THESE (COWRIE) SHELLS.**"*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three things in one sentence, and all three matter.** ⭐ **(i) A financial specialist in virtually EVERY village.** ⭐ **(ii) He *"makes REMITTANCES of money"* — so villages were connected to a network that moved value across distances.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) A three-tier currency — RUPEE, PAISA and COWRIE SHELLS — with the *shroff* setting the exchange rate *"as they please."*** *So the poorest transactions ran on cowries, and the man who converted cowries into rupees took a cut at every step. **That is the real texture of "money in the village": ubiquitous, and not neutral.** *(⭐ **Hold the cowrie detail for §7 — India's silver came from the Americas and Japan, and its small change came from the shells of the Maldives.**)*

> - ⭐ **(i) FIELD LABOUR** — *men tilled and ploughed; **women sowed, weeded, threshed and winnowed**.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) THE HOUSEHOLD AS THE PRODUCTIVE UNIT** — nucleated villages and individuated farming meant *"the basis of production was **the labour and resources of THE ENTIRE HOUSEHOLD**"*, so **the home/world segregation "was NOT POSSIBLE."**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) CRAFT AND COMMERCE** — **spinning, clay-kneading, embroidery**; *"the more commercialised the product, the greater the demand on women's labour"*; and women **went to employers' houses and to markets.** ⭐ **Add Figs. 8.8a–b: women crushing stones and carrying loads at Fatehpur Sikri, migrating from neighbouring villages** — *wage labour outside agriculture altogether.*
> - ⭐ **(iv) REPRODUCTION AND ITS CONSEQUENCES** — the child-bearer in a labour-scarce society; high mortality; **bride-price not dowry; remarriage legitimate** — *and, from the same premise, strict control and draconian punishment.*
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(v) PROPERTY AND POWER** — **inheritance among the landed gentry**, **widows selling land in Punjab**, **Hindu and Muslim women inheriting zamindaris**, and **RAJSHAHI, one of the biggest eighteenth-century zamindaris, headed by a woman.**
> - 🎯 **Close with the limit: they petitioned the panchayat in their own right, and *"their names were excluded from the record."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That single closing line shows you have read the section as evidence rather than as a list.**

**📖 SOURCE 3 — *Clearance of forests for agricultural settlements*: the *CHANDIMANGALA* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **A sixteenth-century Bengali poem by MUKUNDARAM CHAKRABARTI. Its hero, KALAKETU, sets up a kingdom by clearing forest:**
>
> *"Hearing the news, **OUTSIDERS CAME FROM VARIOUS LANDS.** Kalaketu then **BOUGHT AND DISTRIBUTED AMONG THEM HEAVY KNIVES, AXES, BATTLE-AXES AND PIKES.** / **From the north came the DAS (people)**, one hundred of them advanced… who distributed **betel-nut** to each of them. / **From the south came THE HARVESTERS, FIVE HUNDRED of them under one organiser.** / ⭐ **From the west came ZAFAR MIAN, together with TWENTY-TWO THOUSAND MEN. SULAIMANI BEADS IN THEIR HANDS, THEY CHANTED THE NAMES OF THEIR *PIR* AND *PAIGHAMBAR* (PROPHET).** / **HAVING CLEARED THE FOREST THEY ESTABLISHED MARKETS.** / **Hundreds and hundreds of foreigners ate and entered the forest.** / ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HEARING THE SOUND OF THE AXE, TIGERS BECAME APPREHENSIVE AND RAN AWAY, ROARING.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT'S QUESTION IS THREE-PART AND THE THIRD PART IS THE SHARP ONE: *"What forms of intrusion into the forest does the text evoke? COMPARE ITS MESSAGE WITH THAT OF THE MINIATURE PAINTING IN FIG. 8.9. **WHO ARE THE PEOPLE IDENTIFIED AS 'FOREIGNERS' FROM THE PERSPECTIVE OF THE FOREST DWELLERS?**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The answer to the third part is the whole point of putting this poem in the book: the "FOREIGNERS" are INDIANS.** ⭐ **The Das from the north, five hundred harvesters from the south, and Zafar Mian's twenty-two thousand Muslim settlers from the west are all migrants from within the subcontinent — but **from the forest dwellers' point of view they are an invasion**, arriving with **axes and pikes**, felling the trees, and **planting markets where the forest was.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Foreigner" is a matter of standpoint, and the poem quietly hands you the forest dwellers'.**
>
> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE COMPARISON WITH FIG. 8.9 IS THE REAL EXAM ANSWER: TWO SOURCES, TWO POSITIONS, ON THE SAME PROCESS.** ⭐ **The painting is the IMPERIAL view — the hunt as justice, harmony inserted in a corner, the emperor relating to all his subjects.** ⭐ **The poem is the FRONTIER view — organised, financed, armed clearance, celebrated as heroism, with the last line given to the animals that fled.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Neither is neutral, and NCERT is teaching you to notice that both are arguments.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two more things in the poem are worth extracting.** ⭐ **(i) Kalaketu *"BOUGHT and distributed"* the tools — **forest clearance required CAPITAL**, which is why §5 says zamindars *"spearheaded the COLONISATION of agricultural land… providing them with the means of cultivation, INCLUDING CASH LOANS."* **The poem is describing a zamindari being founded.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) *"Having cleared the forest THEY ESTABLISHED MARKETS"* — **the market arrives with the plough, not after it.** *Another nail in the "little republic" coffin.*

**📖 SOURCE 4 — *Trade between the hill tribes and the plains, c. 1595*: ABU'L FAZL on the *suba* of AWADH ⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **DOWN from the northern mountains, *"carried on THE BACKS OF MEN, OF STOUT PONIES AND OF GOATS"*:** *"**GOLD, COPPER, LEAD, MUSK, TAILS OF THE *KUTAS* COW (THE YAK), HONEY**, *chuk* (an acid of orange and lemon juice boiled together), **POMEGRANATE SEED, GINGER, LONG PEPPER**, *majith* root (a red dye), **BORAX**, *zedoary*, **WAX, WOOLLEN STUFFS, WOODEN WARE, HAWKS, FALCONS, BLACK FALCONS, MERLINS**, and other articles."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT asks about the MODES OF TRANSPORT and WHY — and the answer is terrain. *"The backs of MEN, of STOUT PONIES and of GOATS"* means there were no roads and no carts; a **goat-load** is the unit of a mountain path.** ⭐ **Then read the two lists against each other: the hills send **RAW MATERIALS, ANIMAL PRODUCTS AND SPECIALITIES** *(metals, musk, yak tails, honey, dyestuffs, spices, live hunting birds)*; the plains send **MANUFACTURES AND NECESSITIES** *(cloth, glass, earthenware, ornaments — and SALT, which no hill economy can make).*
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That asymmetry is the classic shape of hill–plain trade, and it matters for §4.2's argument: forest and hill peoples were NOT isolated. They were already inside a commercial network in 1595 — and it is that network, not conquest, that transformed them.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ***SANAD*** SENTENCE IS THE SUBTLEST POINT IN §5 AND IT IS WORTH A LINE IN ANY ANSWER ON THE MUGHAL STATE.** ⭐ **A zamindar could seize a neighbour's land by force — but *"it is UNLIKELY that the state would have allowed such a show of aggression… UNLESS HE HAD BEEN CONFIRMED BY AN IMPERIAL ORDER (*SANAD*)."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So even conquest ran through paperwork. The empire did not prevent violence in the countryside; it LICENSED it** — and a licensed conquest is a conquest that owes the licensor.
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOTICE THE SADGOPS, BECAUSE THEY CLOSE A LOOP THIS CHAPTER OPENED TWO SECTIONS EARLIER.** ⭐ **In §2.1 the Sadgops were *"intermediate pastoral and fishing castes"* who *"acquired the status of PEASANTS."* By §5 the same group has *"carved out POWERFUL ZAMINDARIS in central and south-western Bengal."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is a single named community moving from herding, to peasant status, to the narrow apex of the rural pyramid — and it is the strongest single piece of evidence in the chapter that caste position followed economic power rather than fixing it.**

```
      CLAIMS WAS, HOWEVER, SOMETIMES THWARTED BY LOCAL
      CONDITIONS." **

   ================================================================
   ** MEASUREMENT -- and its limit **
   ================================================================
   * ** "BOTH CULTIVATED AND CULTIVABLE LANDS WERE MEASURED IN EACH
     PROVINCE. THE AIN COMPILED THE AGGREGATES OF SUCH LANDS DURING
     AKBAR'S RULE." **
   * ⭐ ** "in 1665, AURANGZEB EXPRESSLY INSTRUCTED HIS REVENUE
     OFFICIALS TO PREPARE ANNUAL RECORDS OF THE NUMBER OF
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ***JAMA*** / ***HASIL*** DISTINCTION IS NOT VOCABULARY — IT IS THE WHOLE ARGUMENT ABOUT THE REACH OF THE MUGHAL STATE, AND YOU SHOULD SAY WHY.** ⭐ **The state's ambition was recorded in the *jama*; its actual power was recorded in the *hasil*; and **the gap between them is the measure of everything that resisted it** — drought, desertion, a zamindar's fortress, a headman's crooked register, and forty per cent of the land under forest and therefore unmeasured.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT says it plainly: the state tried *"to MAXIMISE its claims"*, and realising them was *"SOMETIMES THWARTED BY LOCAL CONDITIONS."* **An empire that must distinguish what it demanded from what it received is an empire telling you its own limits.**

**📖 SOURCE 5 — *Classification of lands under Akbar*: from the ***AIN*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The four land categories. This is a certainty for Prelims — learn the fallow period attached to each:**
>
> | Category | ⭐ The *Ain*'s definition |
> |---|---|
> | ***POLAJ*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"land which is **ANNUALLY CULTIVATED FOR EACH CROP IN SUCCESSION** and is **NEVER ALLOWED TO LIE FALLOW**"* |
> | ***PARAUTI*** | *"land **LEFT OUT OF CULTIVATION FOR A TIME THAT IT MAY RECOVER ITS STRENGTH**"* |
> | ***CHACHAR*** | ⭐ *"land that **HAS LAIN FALLOW FOR THREE OR FOUR YEARS**"* |
> | ***BANJAR*** | ⭐ *"land **UNCULTIVATED FOR FIVE YEARS AND MORE**"* |
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the assessment formula, which almost everyone forgets to learn:** *"**Of THE FIRST TWO KINDS OF LAND** [*polaj* and *parauti*], **there are THREE CLASSES, GOOD, MIDDLING, AND BAD.** ⭐ **THEY ADD TOGETHER THE PRODUCE OF EACH SORT, AND THE THIRD OF THIS REPRESENTS THE MEDIUM PRODUCE, ONE-THIRD PART OF WHICH IS EXACTED AS THE ROYAL DUES.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WORK THE FORMULA THROUGH ONCE AND YOU WILL NEVER MISREAD IT: take the produce of GOOD, MIDDLING and BAD land, add the three together, divide by three to get the "MEDIUM PRODUCE" — and the state takes ONE-THIRD OF THAT.** ⭐ **Two separate thirds are involved, and questions are set on exactly that confusion.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOTICE WHAT THE CLASSIFICATION IS REALLY FOR — NCERT asks *"WHAT PRINCIPLES did the Mughal state follow while classifying lands?"* The principle is CONTINUITY OF CULTIVATION, measured in years of fallow.** ⭐ **The categories are a sliding scale from land that never rests to land abandoned for five years or more, and the assessment is graded down accordingly.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It is a system designed to keep land IN cultivation** — a peasant who lets good land slip toward *banjar* pays less, but the state has every incentive to prevent that, which is why §1's "encouragement of cultivation" and §6's taxation are one policy, not two. *(⭐ **Compare **[Part III Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md)**: the Permanent Settlement fixed a CASH sum in perpetuity and stopped caring what the land did. **The Mughal system graded by the land's condition; the colonial one graded by nothing at all.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ***MANSABDARI*** BOX — short, and every word is examinable:** *"The Mughal administrative system had at its apex **A MILITARY-CUM-BUREAUCRATIC APPARATUS ( *MANSABDARI* )** which was responsible for looking after **THE CIVIL AND MILITARY AFFAIRS** of the state. ⭐ **SOME MANSABDARS WERE PAID IN CASH ( *NAQDI* ), WHILE THE MAJORITY OF THEM WERE PAID THROUGH ASSIGNMENTS OF REVENUE ( *JAGIRS* ) IN DIFFERENT REGIONS OF THE EMPIRE. THEY WERE TRANSFERRED PERIODICALLY.** ⚠️ **See also Chapter 9.**"*
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT "See also Chapter 9" IS ONE OF THE THREE DANGLING REFERENCES — the *mansabdari* system, the backbone of Mughal administration, is explained in a chapter that has been DELETED from this book.** *You get four sentences where a chapter used to be. **[The supplement](09_Supplement_Kings_and_Chronicles_The_Mughal_Courts.md) restores it.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Even from these four sentences, extract the two facts that matter: (i) a *JAGIR* is an ASSIGNMENT OF REVENUE, NOT A GRANT OF LAND — the *jagirdar* got the right to collect, not ownership; and (ii) *"THEY WERE TRANSFERRED PERIODICALLY."*** ⭐ **Transfer is the anti-entrenchment device**, and you have now met it three times in two chapters: **[Vijayanagara moved its *amara-nayakas*](07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md)**, **the Delhi Sultanate moved its *iqta* holders**, and **the Mughals moved their *jagirdars*.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Every one of these systems failed the same way — when the centre weakened, the assignees stopped moving and became local rulers.**

**📖 SOURCE 6 — *Cash or kind?*: the ***AIN*** on the four methods of collection in kind ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **Akbar's instruction first:** *"**LET HIM (THE *AMIL-GUZAR*) NOT MAKE IT A PRACTICE OF TAKING ONLY IN CASH BUT ALSO IN KIND.** The latter is effected in several ways."*
>
> | # | Method | ⭐ How it worked |
> |:--:|---|---|
> | **1** | ***KANKUT*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ESTIMATION BEFORE HARVEST.** *"in the Hindi language **KAN signifies GRAIN, and KUT, ESTIMATES**"* — the standing crop is appraised. *"If any doubts arise, **THE CROPS SHOULD BE CUT AND ESTIMATED IN THREE LOTS, THE GOOD, THE MIDDLING, AND THE INFERIOR**, and the hesitation removed."* Often *"the land taken by **APPRAISEMENT** gives a sufficiently accurate return"* |
> | **2** | ***BATAI*** *(also **BHAOLI**)* | ⭐ **CROP-SHARING AFTER HARVEST.** *"the crops are **REAPED AND STACKED AND DIVIDED BY AGREEMENT IN THE PRESENCE OF THE PARTIES**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *But* — *"in this case **SEVERAL INTELLIGENT INSPECTORS ARE REQUIRED; otherwise, THE EVIL-MINDED AND FALSE ARE GIVEN TO DECEPTION**"* |
> | **3** | ***KHET-BATAI*** | **DIVISION OF THE FIELDS THEMSELVES** — *"when they **DIVIDE THE FIELDS AFTER THEY ARE SOWN**"* |
> | **4** | ***LANG BATAI*** | **DIVISION OF THE HEAPS** — *"after cutting the grain, they **FORM IT IN HEAPS AND DIVIDE IT AMONG THEMSELVES, AND EACH TAKES HIS SHARE HOME AND TURNS IT TO PROFIT**"* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT'S QUESTION IS THE GOOD ONE: *"WHAT DIFFERENCE WOULD EACH OF THE SYSTEMS OF ASSESSMENT AND COLLECTION HAVE MADE TO THE CULTIVATOR?"* — AND THE ANSWER TURNS ON **WHO CARRIES THE RISK OF A BAD HARVEST.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***KANKUT* AND CASH PUT THE RISK ON THE PEASANT.** *An estimate made on the standing crop becomes a fixed obligation; if the crop then fails, or the price collapses, **the demand does not move.***
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE THREE *BATAI* METHODS PUT THE RISK ON THE STATE.** *A SHARE of an actual harvest **falls automatically when the harvest falls.** ⭐ **That is why Akbar told his collector not to insist on cash: a share is self-adjusting, and a peasant who is not ruined in a bad year is a peasant still farming in a good one.**
> - ⭐ **BUT SHARING COSTS MORE TO ADMINISTER** — *"several INTELLIGENT INSPECTORS are required"*, and someone must supervise the heaps. **Cash is cheap to collect and hard on the peasant; a share is kind to the peasant and expensive to police.**
> - 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THIS ANSWERS THE "Discuss…" BOX — *"Would you consider the land revenue system of the Mughals as A FLEXIBLE ONE?"* — YES, AND WITH SPECIFIC EVIDENCE:** **four land categories graded by fallow**, **three quality classes within the top two**, **four methods of collection in kind plus cash**, **the collector instructed to keep the kind option open**, **the *jama*/*hasil* distinction admitting that the demand was not always realised**, and **Aurangzeb ordering assessment *"village by village, PEASANT-WISE."*** ⭐ **Then add the honest qualification: flexibility existed IN THE RULES; whether a peasant experienced it depended on the *amil-guzar*, the *amin*, the *patwari* and the *mandal* — and §2.2 showed you what those men did with village accounts.**

**📖 SOURCE 7 — *The jama*: AURANGZEB's order to his revenue official, 1665 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> *"He should direct **THE *AMINS* OF THE *PARGANAS*** that they should **DISCOVER THE ACTUAL CONDITIONS OF CULTIVATION ( *MAUJUDAT* ), VILLAGE BY VILLAGE, PEASANT-WISE ( *ASAMIWAR* )**, and after **MINUTE SCRUTINY, ASSESS THE *JAMA***, keeping in view ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FINANCIAL INTERESTS ( *KIFAYAT* ) OF THE GOVERNMENT, AND THE WELFARE OF THE PEASANTRY.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FINAL CLAUSE IS THE ONE TO QUOTE, AND THE RIGHT WAY TO READ IT IS AS POLICY, NOT BENEVOLENCE.** ⭐ **An emperor instructing his officials to weigh *"the FINANCIAL INTERESTS of the government AND the WELFARE of the peasantry"* in the same sentence is not being sentimental — he is acknowledging §1.2's arithmetic: **a peasantry that can desert, into a forest the state cannot measure, is a peasantry whose welfare is a fiscal variable.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Ruin the *raiyat* and you lose the *hasil* permanently; the field reverts toward *banjar* and pays nothing at all.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐ **NCERT asks *"Why do you think the emperor insisted on A DETAILED SURVEY?"* — and the two words that answer it are ***MAUJUDAT* (the ACTUAL conditions)** and ***ASAMIWAR* (PEASANT-WISE)**.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Assessment village by village lets the headman redistribute the burden downward (the "corrupt mandals" box); assessment PEASANT BY PEASANT takes that discretion away from him.** *An order for a household-level survey in 1665 is an emperor trying to see past his own intermediaries.*

That is the "drain", and Theme 8's §7 is the "before" picture.***

**📖 SOURCE 8 — *How silver came to India*: GIOVANNI CARERI, an Italian traveller who passed through India **c. 1690** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ *(NCERT notes his account is **based on Bernier's** — so a traveller reporting a traveller, and both of them in [Theme 5's](05_Theme5_Through_the_Eyes_of_Travellers.md) tradition.)*
>
> *"That the Reader may form some idea of **THE WEALTH OF THIS (MUGHAL) EMPIRE**, he is to observe that **ALL THE GOLD AND SILVER, WHICH CIRCULATES THROUGHOUT THE WORLD AT LAST CENTRES HERE.** ⭐ It is well known that as much of it **COMES OUT OF AMERICA**, after running through several Kingdoms of Europe, goes partly **INTO TURKY**, for several sorts of Commodities; and part **INTO PERSIA, BY THE WAY OF SMIRNA FOR SILK.** Now the **TURKS NOT BEING ABLE TO ABSTAIN FROM COFFEE, which comes from HYEMAN (Oman), and ARABIA** … nor Persia, Arabia, and the Turks themselves **to go without THE COMMODITIES OF INDIA, SEND VAST QUANTITIES OF MONY TO MOKA (MOCHA) ON THE RED SEA … TO BASSORA (BASRA) AT THE BOTTOM OF THE PERSIAN GULF; … WHICH IS AFTERWARDS SENT OVER IN SHIPS TO INDOSTAN.**
>
> *Besides, the **INDIAN, DUTCH, ENGLISH, AND PORTUGUESE SHIPS**, that every Year carry the Commodities of Indostan to **PEGU, TANASSERRI (Myanmar), SIAM (Thailand), CEYLON (Sri Lanka) … THE MALDIVE ISLANDS, MOZAMBIQUE** and other Places, **MUST OF NECESSITY CONVEY MUCH GOLD AND SILVER THITHER, FROM THOSE COUNTRIES.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ALL THAT THE DUTCH FETCH FROM THE MINES IN JAPAN, SOONER OR LATER, GOES TO INDOSTAN; and the goods carry'd hence into Europe, whether to FRANCE, ENGLAND, OR PORTUGAL, ARE ALL PURCHAS'D FOR READY MONY, WHICH REMAINS THERE.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS SOURCE IS A MAP OF THE SEVENTEENTH-CENTURY WORLD ECONOMY, AND IT IS ALSO EXERCISE 10's MAP-WORK ANSWER. TRACE THE THREE ROUTES:**
> - ⭐ **THE ATLANTIC–LEVANT ROUTE:** **AMERICA → EUROPE → TURKEY and PERSIA** *(via Smyrna, for silk)* **→ MOCHA and BASRA → by ship to INDIA.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE PACIFIC ROUTE:** **JAPAN's mines → the DUTCH → INDIA.**
> - ⭐ **THE INDIAN OCEAN ROUTE:** Indian, Dutch, English and Portuguese ships carrying Indian goods to **Myanmar, Thailand, Sri Lanka, the Maldives and Mozambique**, and bringing bullion back.
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND NOTICE THE MECHANISM CARERI IDENTIFIES, BECAUSE IT IS AN ECONOMIST'S POINT: EUROPE HAD NOTHING INDIA WANTED.** ⭐ **Indian goods carried to France, England and Portugal are *"ALL PURCHAS'D FOR READY MONY, WHICH REMAINS THERE."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A one-way trade balance, settled permanently in bullion — which is precisely why India accumulated silver it did not mine.** *(⭐ **His chain of causation is delightful and worth remembering: **the Turks cannot give up coffee**, so money goes to Arabia; and neither Turks nor Persians nor Arabs can do without Indian goods, so the money continues to India. **A global bullion flow explained through an addiction.** *(⭐ And keep §1.4's tobacco and §2.4's cowries beside it — **the seventeenth-century Indian village was already inside a world of American silver, Maldivian shells, American tobacco, American maize, chillies and potatoes.**)*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ THE EIGHT COLUMNS AS A PORTRAIT OF WHAT THE MUGHAL STATE THOUGHT WORTH KNOWING, BECAUSE THAT IS WHAT A FORM ALWAYS IS.** ⭐ **Four columns are about **revenue** *(area measured, cash assessment, charitable exemptions, and the administrative unit)*. **Three are about the ZAMINDARS — their identity, their CASTE, and their CAVALRY, INFANTRY AND ELEPHANTS.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A state that records, district by district, the caste and the armed strength of the local magnates is a state that regards them as its most important variable — and the *"parallel army"* figures in §5 are simply this column totalled up.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note what is NOT in the eight columns: the peasants.** ⭐ **No column for *raiyat* numbers, holdings, crops grown, wages or prices.** *§1.1's *"view from the top"* is not an attitude in the prose — **it is the structure of the table.** *(⭐ **Which is exactly why Aurangzeb's 1665 order for a survey *"village by village, PEASANT-WISE"* is such an interesting document: it is a later attempt to record precisely what the *Ain*'s form left out.**)*

**📖 SOURCE 9 — *"Moistening the rose garden of fortune"*: ABU'L FAZL ON HIS OWN METHOD ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐**

> ⭐ **The commission:** *"… to Abu'l Fazl, son of Mubarak … **THIS SUBLIME MANDATE WAS GIVEN. 'WRITE WITH THE PEN OF SINCERITY THE ACCOUNT OF THE GLORIOUS EVENTS AND OF OUR DOMINION-CONQUERING VICTORIES.'**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **His sources, in his own words:**
> - *"I spent much labour and research in collecting **THE RECORDS AND NARRATIVES OF HIS MAJESTY'S ACTIONS**"*
> - *"I was a long time **INTERROGATING THE SERVANTS OF THE STATE AND THE OLD MEMBERS OF THE ILLUSTRIOUS FAMILY**"*
> - ⭐ *"I examined **BOTH PRUDENT, TRUTH-SPEAKING OLD MEN AND ACTIVE-MINDED, RIGHT-ACTIONED YOUNG ONES AND REDUCED THEIR STATEMENTS TO WRITING**"* — **oral testimony**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**THE ROYAL COMMANDS WERE ISSUED TO THE PROVINCES, THAT THOSE WHO FROM OLD SERVICE REMEMBERED, WITH CERTAINTY OR WITH ADMINICLE OF DOUBT, THE EVENTS OF THE PAST, SHOULD COPY OUT THE NOTES AND MEMORANDA AND TRANSIT THEM TO THE COURT**"* — **memory collected by imperial order**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"I obtained **THE CHRONICLE OF EVENTS BEGINNING AT THE NINETEENTH YEAR OF THE DIVINE ERA, WHEN THE RECORD OFFICE WAS ESTABLISHED** by the enlightened intellect of His Majesty"* — **a state archive**
> - *"Great pains too, were taken to procure **THE ORIGINALS OR COPIES OF MOST OF THE ORDERS WHICH HAD BEEN ISSUED TO THE PROVINCES FROM THE ACCESSION UP TO THE PRESENT-DAY**"*
> - *"I also took much trouble to incorporate **MANY OF THE REPORTS WHICH MINISTERS AND HIGH OFFICIALS HAD SUBMITTED, ABOUT THE AFFAIRS OF THE EMPIRE AND THE EVENTS OF FOREIGN COUNTRIES**"*
> - *"I also exerted myself energetically to collect **THE ROUGH NOTES AND MEMORANDA OF SAGACIOUS AND WELL-INFORMED MEN**"*
>
> ⭐ **And the review process:** *"a second command shone forth … that **THE MATERIALS WHICH HAD BEEN COLLECTED SHOULD BE … RECITED IN THE ROYAL HEARING**, and whatever might have to be written down afterwards should be introduced into the noble volume **AS A SUPPLEMENT**."*
>
> ⭐ **The closing image, which gives the source its title:** *"By these means, **I CONSTRUCTED A RESERVOIR FOR IRRIGATING AND MOISTENING THE ROSE GARDEN OF FORTUNE** (the *Akbar Nama*)."*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT'S THREE QUESTIONS ON THIS SOURCE ARE A COMPLETE EXAM ANSWER IN THEMSELVES, AND THE THIRD IS THE KILLER. TAKE THEM IN TURN.**
>
> ⭐ **(i) *"LIST ALL THE SOURCES that Abu'l Fazl used."*** **Seven kinds: existing records and narratives · interrogation of state servants and royal family elders · oral testimony from old and young · provincial memoranda gathered by ROYAL COMMAND · the state RECORD OFFICE (from the nineteenth year of the reign) · originals and copies of imperial orders to the provinces · and reports submitted by ministers and high officials.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) *"WHICH OF THESE WOULD HAVE BEEN MOST USEFUL for arriving at an understanding of AGRARIAN RELATIONS?"*** **The IMPERSONAL, DOCUMENTARY ones — the record office, the imperial orders, and the officials' reports** — because those contain **measurements, assessments and administrative fact**, which §8 confirms have real *"quantitative veracity."* ⭐ **The personal testimony is about the emperor's deeds, and it came from courtiers and the royal family — the very people with the strongest interest in how the reign was described.**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(iii) *"TO WHAT EXTENT do you think his work would have been INFLUENCED BY HIS RELATIONSHIP WITH AKBAR?"* — AND THE SOURCE ANSWERS THIS AGAINST ITSELF, THREE TIMES OVER:**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The commission itself specifies the CONCLUSION: *"the account of THE GLORIOUS EVENTS and of OUR DOMINION-CONQUERING VICTORIES."*** *He is told what kind of history to write before he begins.*
> - ⭐ **The evidence was gathered BY IMPERIAL COMMAND** — provincial officers were ordered to send in their recollections. **A memory submitted to the court on the court's instruction is not an independent memory.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The draft was *"RECITED IN THE ROYAL HEARING."*** **The subject of the book vetted the book.**
> - 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And even the metaphor confesses: the *Akbar Nama* is *"THE ROSE GARDEN OF FORTUNE"*, and the historian's labour is *"A RESERVOIR FOR IRRIGATING AND MOISTENING"* it. **His own image of his work is that he WATERS the emperor's glory. That is §1.1's "view from the top" in the author's own words.***
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The balanced verdict, which is what a good answer needs: *"Write with the PEN OF SINCERITY"* was, on the evidence of §8, taken seriously — five revisions, oral testimony CROSS-CHECKED before being admitted as fact, numerals written out in words to prevent copying errors.** ⭐ **Abu'l Fazl was a careful researcher working within a frame he did not choose. The care is real; so is the frame.**

```

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE FINAL VERDICT -- why the Ain matters
      DESPITE all that **
   ================================================================
   ** "THESE LIMITATIONS NOTWITHSTANDING, THE AIN REMAINS AN
      EXTRAORDINARY DOCUMENT OF ITS TIMES." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE BREAKTHROUGH: "Abu'l Fazl ACHIEVED A MAJOR
      BREAKTHROUGH IN THE TRADITION OF MEDIEVAL CHRONICLERS ⭐ WHO
      WROTE MOSTLY ABOUT REMARKABLE POLITICAL EVENTS -- WARS,
      CONQUESTS, POLITICAL MACHINATIONS, AND DYNASTIC TURMOIL.
      INFORMATION ABOUT THE COUNTRY, ITS PEOPLE AND ITS PRODUCTS
      WAS MENTIONED ONLY INCIDENTALLY AND AS EMBELLISHMENTS TO THE
      ESSENTIALLY POLITICAL THRUST OF THE NARRATIVE." **

   ** "THE AIN COMPLETELY DEPARTED FROM THIS TRADITION AS IT
      RECORDED INFORMATION ABOUT THE EMPIRE ** AND THE PEOPLE OF
      INDIA ** , and thus ⭐ CONSTITUTES A BENCHMARK FOR STUDYING
      INDIA AT THE TURN OF THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE LAST SENTENCE OF THE CHAPTER: "THE VALUE OF
      THE AIN'S QUANTITATIVE EVIDENCE IS UNCONTESTED WHERE THE
      STUDY OF AGRARIAN RELATIONS IS CONCERNED. But it is the
      information it contains ON PEOPLE, THEIR PROFESSIONS AND
      TRADES AND ON THE IMPERIAL ESTABLISHMENT AND THE GRANDEES OF
      THE EMPIRE WHICH ENABLES HISTORIANS TO RECONSTRUCT ⭐ THE
      SOCIAL FABRIC OF INDIA AT THAT TIME." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FINAL VERDICT IS MORE INTERESTING THAN IT LOOKS, AND IT IS THE BEST WAY TO CLOSE AN EXERCISE 1 ANSWER: THE *AIN* IS A BREAKTHROUGH PRECISELY IN THE DIMENSION WHERE IT IS ALSO BIASED.** ⭐ **Every earlier medieval chronicle wrote about **wars, conquests, court intrigue and dynastic turmoil**, and mentioned the country and its people *"only INCIDENTALLY and AS EMBELLISHMENTS."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Abu'l Fazl was the first to make the COUNTRY the subject — its provinces, products, prices, castes, professions and revenue.** *And he did it **as an imperial exercise, to help an emperor govern**. **The same impulse that produced the systematic knowledge produced the view from the top.** ⭐ **They are not separable, and that is the honest historiographical point.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **And this is the exact argument [Part III Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) makes about the FIFTH REPORT and the colonial revenue archive — an unprecedented body of information about rural India, produced by an administration, for administration.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read Theme 8 §8 and Part III Theme 9 §1 back to back and you have the single most useful methodological pair in Class 12 History: two empires, two centuries, two languages, and the SAME reason the record exists and the SAME reason it must be read against the grain.**)*

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "TRANSLATING THE AIN" BOX — small, but it is the sort of detail that shows up in a Prelims option:** ⭐ ***HENRY BLOCHMANN* edited it, and **THE ASIATIC SOCIETY OF BENGAL, CALCUTTA** published it in its ***BIBLIOTHECA INDICA*** series. The English translation runs to **THREE VOLUMES**: **Volume 1 by HENRY BLOCHMANN (Calcutta, 1873)**; **Volumes 2 and 3 by H.S. JARRETT (Calcutta, 1891 and 1894).**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(⭐ **Worth noticing who did this work and when: a Persian imperial gazetteer of 1598 became usable by historians because **a colonial-era learned society in Calcutta** edited and translated it in **1873–1894**. **The Asiatic Society is the same institution that made [Part I's](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/00_Index.md) Sanskrit and Prakrit sources accessible**, and its work is inseparable from the colonial project of knowing India in order to govern it — the third time in this one chapter that a body of knowledge and a project of rule turn out to be the same body of work.**)*

### 🔴 Prelims traps — the claim vs the truth

| ❌ The plausible-sounding claim | ✅ What the chapter actually says |
|---|---|
| Around half the population lived in villages | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ABOUT 85 PER CENT** |
| The chief source is peasant testimony and folk memory | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**PEASANTS DID NOT WRITE ABOUT THEMSELVES**"* — the major sources are **CHRONICLES AND DOCUMENTS FROM THE MUGHAL COURT** |
| The *Ain* was written to inform historians | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Its **central purpose** was *"to present a vision of Akbar's empire where **SOCIAL HARMONY WAS PROVIDED BY A STRONG RULING CLASS**"*, in which revolt was ***"PREDESTINED TO FAIL"*** — *"a **VIEW FROM THE TOP**"* |
| The *Ain* is corrected using folk sources | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Using **revenue records from GUJARAT, MAHARASHTRA and RAJASTHAN (17th–18th c.)** and **EAST INDIA COMPANY records for eastern India** — sources that **record CONFLICTS**, hence peasants' *"expectations of fairness"* |
| *Raiyat* was a rare literary term | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ It was **the term Indo-Persian sources used MOST FREQUENTLY** *(plural **riaya**)*, alongside ***muzarian***, ***kisan***, ***asami*** |
| *Khud-kashta* peasants were the migrant cultivators | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REVERSED.** ***KHUD-KASHTA* = RESIDENTS** of the village where they held land; ***PAHI-KASHTA* = NON-RESIDENT** cultivators farming elsewhere **on a contractual basis** |
| Peasants became *pahi-kashta* only out of distress | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BOTH** — *"out of **CHOICE**"* when **revenue terms in a distant village were more favourable**, and *"out of **COMPULSION**"* after **famine** |
| An average north Indian peasant had a substantial holding | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**SELDOM** more than **A PAIR OF BULLOCKS AND TWO PLOUGHS**; most possessed **EVEN LESS**"* |
| Six acres was an average holding in Bengal | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gujarat: ~6 acres = AFFLUENT. Bengal: 5 acres was the UPPER LIMIT of an AVERAGE farm; 10 acres made one A RICH *ASAMI*** |
| Land was held communally by the village | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Cultivation was based on the principle of **INDIVIDUAL OWNERSHIP. PEASANT LANDS WERE BOUGHT AND SOLD** in the same way as the lands of other property owners"* — fields marked with **earth, brick and thorn** |
| Rice was grown wherever irrigation existed | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Areas with **40 INCHES OR MORE of rainfall** were generally rice zones, *"followed by **WHEAT AND MILLETS**, corresponding to **A DESCENDING SCALE OF PRECIPITATION**"* |
| The state took no part in irrigation | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It **dug new canals (*nahr*, *nala*)** and **repaired old ones — the *SHAHNAHR* in the PUNJAB during SHAH JAHAN's reign** |
| The light wooden plough shows technological backwardness | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"It therefore **DID NOT MAKE DEEP FURROWS, WHICH PRESERVED THE MOISTURE BETTER** during the intensely hot months"* — **an adaptation** |
| Seed drills were the standard method of sowing | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ A drill *(pulled by **a pair of giant oxen**)* existed, but ***"BROADCASTING OF SEED WAS THE MOST PREVALENT METHOD"*** |
| Babur described the Persian wheel at Agra | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The WHEEL was at LAHORE and DIPALPUR; the BUCKET-and-bullock method was at AGRA, CHANDWAR and BAYANA** |
| Most regions grew one crop a year | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A minimum of TWO (*DO-FASLA*)** across the ***KHARIF* (autumn)** and ***RABI* (spring)** cycles; **some regions gave THREE** |
| Bengal produced 50 varieties of crops | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **50 VARIETIES OF RICE ALONE.** *(**AGRA: 39 varieties of crops; DELHI: 43** — over the two seasons)* |
| *Jins-i kamil* means "food crops" | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Literally "PERFECT CROPS"** — cash crops **the state ENCOURAGED because they brought in MORE REVENUE**; **COTTON and SUGARCANE** *par excellence*, plus **oilseeds (mustard) and lentils** |
| Bengal was famous for cotton, the Deccan for sugar | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REVERSED — COTTON across CENTRAL INDIA and the DECCAN PLATEAU; BENGAL was famous for its SUGAR** |
| Maize came to India from the Americas directly | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***MAKKA* was introduced VIA AFRICA AND SPAIN**, and by the seventeenth century was **a major crop of WESTERN INDIA** |
| Potatoes and chillies are ancient Indian crops | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TOMATOES, POTATOES and CHILLIES came from the NEW WORLD in this period**, as did **the PINEAPPLE and the PAPAYA** |
| The *Ain* lists tobacco among northern crops | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**THE AIN DOES NOT MENTION TOBACCO**"* — **Akbar and his nobles first encountered it in 1604**, six years after the *Ain* was completed; it **arrived first in the DECCAN** |
| Aurangzeb banned tobacco | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **JAHANGIR did — and the ban was *"TOTALLY INEFFECTIVE"*** |
| India's population fell over the seventeenth century | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ It rose by **about 50 MILLION between 1600 and 1800 — roughly 33% over 200 years** |
| The village community had four constituents | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE — the CULTIVATORS, the PANCHAYAT, and the VILLAGE HEADMAN (*muqaddam* or *mandal*)** |
| The panchayat was a representative democratic body | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**AN OLIGARCHY**"* of elders **with hereditary property rights**; *"the village **MENIAL-CUM-AGRICULTURAL WORKER WAS UNLIKELY TO BE REPRESENTED THERE**"* |
| The headman was appointed by the state | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Chosen by CONSENSUS OF THE VILLAGE ELDERS, RATIFIED BY THE ZAMINDAR**, and **dismissable by the elders** |
| The headman kept the accounts himself | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ His chief function was **to SUPERVISE their preparation**, *"assisted by the accountant or ***PATWARI***"* |
| Panchayat funds came from a state grant | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ From ***"CONTRIBUTIONS MADE BY INDIVIDUALS TO A COMMON FINANCIAL POOL"*** — spent on **entertaining visiting revenue officials**, **calamity relief**, and **bunds and canals** |
| The harshest panchayat punishment was a fine | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EXPULSION FROM THE COMMUNITY** — *"in most cases meted out **FOR A LIMITED PERIOD**"*, making a person **an outcaste who LOST HIS RIGHT TO PRACTISE HIS PROFESSION** |
| The *jati panchayat* and the village panchayat were the same body | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SEPARATE AND PARALLEL** — *"**each caste or jati in the village had ITS OWN**"*, deciding **civil disputes, land claims, marriage norms and ritual precedence** |
| The Mughal state overrode caste bodies | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**IN MOST CASES, EXCEPT IN MATTERS OF CRIMINAL JUSTICE, THE STATE RESPECTED THE DECISIONS OF *JATI PANCHAYATS***"* |
| Peasant petitions came from prosperous cultivators | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"usually made by villagers **FROM THE LOWEST RUNGS OF RURAL SOCIETY**"*, often **collectively**, against ***"MORALLY ILLEGITIMATE DEMANDS"*** and ***BEGAR*** |
| Peasants' final recourse was armed revolt | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"more drastic forms of resistance, such as **DESERTING THE VILLAGE**"* — effective because of **abundant uncultivated land and COMPETITION OVER LABOUR** |
| Artisans were a negligible part of village society | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Sometimes as high as 25 PER CENT of the total households"** *(Marathi documents and early British village surveys)* |
| The *jajmani* system is the contemporary Mughal term | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The term was NOT IN VOGUE in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries"** — a later label |
| Artisans were always paid in grain | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three ways — a SHARE OF THE HARVEST · an ALLOTMENT OF LAND (in Maharashtra, *MIRAS* or *WATAN*, hereditary) · a NEGOTIATED goods-for-services arrangement.** *"CASH remuneration was not entirely unknown either"* |
| The "little republic" describes the village accurately | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A NINETEENTH-CENTURY BRITISH idea.** Refuted twice — **individual ownership + caste and gender inequity + a powerful group that "exploited the weaker sections"**, and **"MORE IMPORTANTLY", the CASH NEXUS** |
| Villages were largely cashless | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TAVERNIER: a village *"must be very small indeed if it has not a money-changer called a SHROFF"***, dealing in **rupee, paisa and COWRIE SHELLS** |
| Men and women worked in separate spheres | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"a gendered segregation between the home and the world **WAS NOT POSSIBLE**"* — **men tilled and ploughed; women SOWED, WEEDED, THRESHED and WINNOWED** |
| Rural marriages required dowry, as elite ones did | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BRIDE-PRICE, paid TO the bride's family** — and **remarriage was legitimate for BOTH divorced and widowed women** |
| These customs indicate greater freedom for rural women | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The SAME premise produced STRICT CONTROL** — *"the fear of losing control over them was great"*, male-headed households, and ***"DRACONIAN PUNISHMENTS"*** on suspicion of infidelity |
| Women's petitions are recorded under their own names | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**THEIR NAMES WERE EXCLUDED FROM THE RECORD**: the petitioner was referred to as **the mother, sister or wife of the male head of the household**"* |
| Women could not own zamindaris | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HINDU AND MUSLIM WOMEN INHERITED ZAMINDARIS, free to sell or mortgage; women zamindars were known in 18th-century Bengal; RAJSHAHI — one of the biggest — HAD A WOMAN AT THE HELM** |
| Forest cover was about 20 per cent | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Informed conjectures suggest AN AVERAGE OF 40 PER CENT** |
| *Jangli* meant uncivilised | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**DID NOT MEAN AN ABSENCE OF 'CIVILISATION'**"* — it described those living by **gathering forest produce, hunting and SHIFTING AGRICULTURE** |
| The Bhils hunted in the monsoon | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SPRING = forest produce · SUMMER = fishing · MONSOON = cultivation · AUTUMN AND WINTER = hunting** |
| *Mawas* was a kind of forest tax | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***MAWAS* = A PLACE OF REFUGE*** — the state saw the forest as **"a SUBVERSIVE place"**; Babur: *"behind which the people of the pargana become **stubbornly rebellious and pay no taxes**"* |
| *Peshkash* was a land tax | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A form of TRIBUTE** collected by the Mughal state — **from forest people it often included A SUPPLY OF ELEPHANTS** |
| Mughal hunting scenes are straightforward records | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The painter *"inserted A SMALL SCENE somewhere in the picture that functioned as A SYMBOL OF A HARMONIOUS REIGN"*** — and the hunt's justification comes *"so **COURT HISTORIANS** tell us"* |
| The *Chandimangala*'s "foreigners" are Europeans | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **They are INDIAN MIGRANTS** — the **Das from the north, five hundred harvesters from the south, and ZAFAR MIAN with twenty-two thousand men from the west** — foreign **from the forest dwellers' perspective** |
| Gum lac was a local forest product only | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It became **"a MAJOR ITEM OF OVERSEAS EXPORT from India in the seventeenth century"** |
| Tribal societies remained stateless | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Many tribal chiefs **HAD BECOME ZAMINDARS, SOME EVEN BECAME KINGS**"* — **Sind: 6,000 cavalry and 7,000 infantry** · **the AHOM *PAIKS*, military service in exchange for land**, with **wild-elephant capture a ROYAL MONOPOLY** · **the KOCH wars of subjugation** |
| Zamindars were cultivators on a large scale | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ They *"**LIVED OFF AGRICULTURE BUT DID NOT PARTICIPATE DIRECTLY IN THE PROCESSES OF AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION**"* |
| *Milkiyat* was land granted by the state | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The zamindars' OWN PERSONAL PROPERTY**, worked by **hired or servile labour**, which they **could sell, bequeath or mortgage AT WILL** |
| Zamindars were all upper caste | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A **BRAHMANA-RAJPUT combine** dominated, but with **"a fairly large representation from the INTERMEDIATE castes"** and **"a liberal sprinkling of MUSLIM zamindaris"**; and **purchase let *"relatively LOWER castes"* enter the rank** |
| Zamindaris were created mainly by conquest | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**MORE IMPORTANT** were the **SLOW PROCESSES** of consolidation"* — **colonisation of new lands, transfer of rights, state order, and PURCHASE** — and even conquest needed confirmation by an imperial order (***SANAD***) |
| Zamindars only extracted from the countryside | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ They **spearheaded COLONISATION of land**, **settled cultivators with means including CASH LOANS**, **sold *milkiyat* produce**, and **established markets (*HAATS*)**; their buying and selling **accelerated MONETISATION** |
| The bhakti saints denounced zamindars | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **They did NOT** — *"nor, interestingly, the moneylender"*. ***"Usually it was THE REVENUE OFFICIAL OF THE STATE who was the object of their ire"*** |
| In seventeenth-century revolts peasants fought zamindars | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"zamindars often received **THE SUPPORT OF THE PEASANTRY in their struggle AGAINST THE STATE**"* |
| *Jama* is the amount collected | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***JAMA* = ASSESSED. *HASIL* = COLLECTED.** |
| Akbar ordered revenue to be taken only in cash | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The *amil-guzar* should **strive for cash**, *"**BUT THE OPTION OF PAYMENT IN KIND WAS ALSO TO BE KEPT OPEN**"* |
| All land in the empire was successfully measured | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**NOT ALL AREAS WERE MEASURED SUCCESSFULLY** … forests … **REMAINED UNMEASURED**"* |
| *Chachar* is land never left fallow | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***POLAJ* = never fallow · *PARAUTI* = rested briefly · *CHACHAR* = fallow THREE OR FOUR years · *BANJAR* = uncultivated FIVE YEARS AND MORE** |
| The state took one-third of the crop directly | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two steps:** the produce of **good, middling and bad** land is **added and divided by three** to give *"the MEDIUM PRODUCE"*, and **one-third OF THAT** is the royal due. **Quality classes apply only to *polaj* and *parauti*** |
| *Kankut* means crop-sharing | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***KANKUT* = ESTIMATION*** *(**kan** = grain, **kut** = estimates)*. The **sharing** methods are ***BATAI*/*BHAOLI*** *(divide the harvested heap)*, ***KHET-BATAI*** *(divide the fields after sowing)* and ***LANG BATAI*** *(divide the heaps after cutting)* |
| A *jagir* was a grant of land | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **An ASSIGNMENT OF REVENUE**, not of land — and *mansabdars* **"were TRANSFERRED PERIODICALLY"**; some were paid in cash (***NAQDI***) |
| Aurangzeb's 1665 order was for a village-level survey only | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**village by village, PEASANT-WISE ( *ASAMIWAR* )**"*, weighing **the government's *KIFAYAT* AND "the WELFARE OF THE PEASANTRY"** |
| India's silver came from its own mines | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**India… DID NOT HAVE NATURAL RESOURCES OF SILVER**"* — bullion arrived from **the Americas** *(via Europe, Turkey, Persia, Mocha and Basra)* and **the mines of JAPAN** *(via the Dutch)* |
| The four great Asian empires included the Qing | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MING (China) · SAFAVID (Iran) · OTTOMAN (Turkey) · MUGHAL (India)** |
| The *Ain* was completed in 1605 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1598, Akbar's FORTY-SECOND REGNAL YEAR, after FIVE REVISIONS** |
| The *Ain* is a separate work from the *Akbar Nama* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It is the *AKBAR NAMA*'s THIRD BOOK** — the first two being **historical narrative** |
| The *Ain* has three *daftars* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FIVE — *MANZIL-ABADI* (imperial household) · *SIPAH-ABADI* (military and civil administration, with sketches of *mansabdars*, learned men, poets and artists) · *MULK-ABADI* (fiscal, with the "Account of the Twelve Provinces") · and books 4–5 (religious, literary and cultural traditions, plus Akbar's "auspicious sayings")** |
| The *sarkar* tables have six columns | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EIGHT — *parganat*/*mahal* · *qila* · measured area (*arazi*, *zamin-i paimuda*) · *NAQDI* · *SUYURGHAL* (grants of revenue in charity) · zamindars · and their CASTES and TROOPS (*sawar*, *piyada*, *fil*)** |
| The *Ain*'s figures are unreliable overall | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Errors in totalling are *"generally **MINOR** and **DO NOT DETRACT FROM THE OVERALL QUANTITATIVE VERACITY**"* — **numerals were written out IN WORDS to prevent transcription errors**, and **oral testimony was cross-checked** |
| The *Ain*'s data cover all provinces equally | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **No caste data on zamindars for BENGAL AND ORISSA**, and the **prices and wages come mainly from in and around AGRA** — *"of limited relevance for the rest of the country"* |
| Medieval chroniclers usually wrote about society and economy | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ They wrote *"mostly about **WARS, CONQUESTS, POLITICAL MACHINATIONS, AND DYNASTIC TURMOIL**"*, mentioning the country and its people *"only **INCIDENTALLY and AS EMBELLISHMENTS**"*. **The *Ain* "COMPLETELY DEPARTED FROM THIS TRADITION"** |
| The *Ain* was translated by the ASI | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Edited by HENRY BLOCHMANN**, published by **THE ASIATIC SOCIETY OF BENGAL** in ***Bibliotheca Indica***; **Vol. 1 Blochmann (1873), Vols. 2–3 H.S. JARRETT (1891, 1894)** |
| The *diwani* of Bengal passed to the Company in 1757 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1765** *(1739 Nadir Shah sacks Delhi; 1761 third battle of Panipat; 1857 Bahadur Shah II exiled to Rangoon)* |

### 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | How to build it |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Problems in using the *Ain* — and how historians deal with them."** *(Exercise 1 — the likeliest question here)* | ⭐ **Problems in two families.** **POLITICAL:** its purpose was **a vision of harmony under a strong ruling class** with revolt *"predestined to fail"*; it is *"a view of the regions **FROM THE CENTRE**, of society **FROM ITS APEX**"*; **the eight-column table records zamindars' castes and troops but has NO column for peasants**; and it is **the emperor's own commission, vetted by recitation in the royal hearing.** **TECHNICAL:** **errors in totalling** *(minor)*; **no zamindar-caste data for BENGAL AND ORISSA**; **prices and wages only from around AGRA**; ⭐ **and a fixed date — 1598 — which is why it has no tobacco.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REMEDIES:** triangulate with **Gujarat/Maharashtra/Rajasthan revenue records** and **EIC records for the east** — sources that record **CONFLICT**, and therefore **peasants' own expectations of fairness**; use **petitions** for the voice the *Ain* omits; and credit what the *Ain* is genuinely good at — **five revisions, cross-checked oral testimony, numerals written out in words, and uncontested quantitative value for agrarian relations** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Was this subsistence agriculture?"** *(Exercise 2)* | ⭐ **Concede the staples, then dismantle:** ***jins-i kamil* promoted BY THE STATE** *(cotton, sugarcane, oilseeds, lentils)* · *"intertwined in an **AVERAGE** peasant's holding"* · **do-fasla and three crops; 39/43/50 varieties** · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CASH REVENUE as the forcing mechanism** · **Tavernier's *shroff*** · **cash advances to weavers and indigo growers** · **§7's world silver** · **§4.2's gum lac exports.** **Verdict in NCERT's own words** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The role of women in agricultural production."** *(Exercise 3)* | ⭐ **Five layers** *(see §3)*: **field tasks** · **the HOUSEHOLD as the productive unit, so no home/world segregation** · **craft, commercialisation and market work, plus Fatehpur Sikri's migrant women** · **reproduction, bride-price, remarriage — AND the control that came from the same premise** · **property: inheritance, Punjab widows selling land, Hindu and Muslim women's zamindaris, RAJSHAHI.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Close on the erased names** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The significance of monetary transactions."** *(Exercise 4)* | ⭐ **Build the chain**: world trade → **American and Japanese silver** → **stability of the silver *rupya*** → **unprecedented minting** → **the state's ability to tax IN CASH.** Then show money INSIDE the village: **revenue assessed and collected in cash in the Mughal heartland** · **Tavernier's *shroff* and the rupee/paisa/cowrie ladder** · **cash advances to weavers, cotton, silk and indigo producers** · **zamindars' CASH LOANS to settlers and their *haats*** · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"the buying and selling of zamindaris ACCELERATED THE PROCESS OF MONETISATION"** · **and cash remuneration even for village artisans.** **Close: money is the single strongest argument against the "little republic"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Evidence that land revenue was important to the Mughal fiscal system."** *(Exercise 5)* | ⭐ *"Revenue from the land was **THE ECONOMIC MAINSTAY**"* and the state *"derived **THE BULK OF ITS INCOME** from agricultural production."* **Then the machinery as evidence:** **the *diwan*'s *daftar*** · **an army of assessors, collectors and record keepers who "PENETRATED the agricultural domain"** · **measurement of cultivated AND cultivable land in every province** · **four land categories and three quality classes** · **five collection methods** · **the *jama*/*hasil* distinction** · **Aurangzeb's 1665 *asamiwar* order** · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **and the clinching structural fact: the *MULK-ABADI*, a whole *daftar* of the *Ain*, is the fiscal book — and *mansabdars* were paid in *JAGIRS*, i.e. assignments of land revenue.** *An empire that pays its officer corps in revenue assignments IS a land-revenue state* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"To what extent was caste a factor in agrarian social and economic relations?"** *(Exercise 6)* | ⭐ **The two-tier structure** — **decisive at the bottom** *(menial tasks despite abundant land; "much like the Dalits of modern India"; *halalkhoran* outside the village; *mallahzadas* comparable to slaves)*; **negotiable in the middle** *(Ahirs/Gujars/Malis rising through **the profitability of cattle rearing and horticulture**; Sadgops and Kaivartas becoming peasants and then ZAMINDARS; Gauravas claiming Rajput status; Rajputs and Jats as peasants together in Marwar)*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the institutions that enforced it** — the panchayat's duty to prevent *"any offence against their caste"*, **expulsion**, ***jati panchayats* on ritual precedence with STATE ENDORSEMENT**, ***begar* demanded by "superior" castes**, and **caste as a basis of zamindari status recorded in the *Ain*'s own column 7**. **Verdict: caste set the floor and the ceiling; the market moved the middle** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"How were the lives of forest dwellers transformed?"** *(Exercise 7)* | ⭐ **Four inroads plus clearance** *(see §4.2)* — **STATE** *(peshkash in elephants, the hunt, the forest as *mawas*)* · **COMMERCE** *(gum lac as overseas export, elephants sold, the Lohanis, Awadh's hill–plain trade)* · **POLITICS** *(chiefs into zamindars and kings; Sind's armies; the Ahom *paiks* and the elephant monopoly; the Koch wars)* · **RELIGION** *(*pirs* in newly colonised tracts)* · **and physical CLEARANCE** *(the *Chandimangala*)*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thesis: the forest was absorbed into a market and a state, and its own chieftains led much of the absorption** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Examine the role played by zamindars."** *(Exercise 8)* | ⭐ **Definition and the THREE PILLARS** *(*milkiyat* · revenue collection for *khidmat* · *qilachas* and private armies)* · **the "parallel army" figures** · **the pyramid's narrow apex** · **the SIX routes of origin, with NCERT's own ranking and the *SANAD* caveat** · **social composition** *(Brahmana-Rajput combine, intermediate castes, Muslim zamindaris, purchase enabling "lower"-caste entry)* · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **the PRODUCTIVE role** *(colonisation, cash loans to settlers, selling *milkiyat* produce, founding *haats*, accelerating monetisation)* · **and the RECIPROCITY evidence — the bhakti saints' silence, and peasant support for zamindars against the state.** ⭐ **Close on abolition after Independence, distinguishing this zamindar from the Permanent Settlement's** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"How did panchayats and headmen regulate rural society?"** *(Exercise 9)* | ⭐ **Composition and its limit** *("AN OLIGARCHY"; the menial worker unrepresented; decisions BINDING)* · **the headman's selection, ratification by the zamindar, dismissal, and account-keeping with the *patwari*** · **the common fund and its three uses** · **caste regulation, marriages before the *mandal*, fines and EXPULSION** · **the parallel *jati panchayats* and the state's deference except in criminal justice** · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **the PETITIONS and the moral-economy claim** · **compromise, and desertion when compromise failed** · ⭐ **and the "corrupt mandals" box as the necessary counterweight** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The moral economy of the peasantry"** *(the highest-value transferable angle)* | ⭐ **The core text:** *"the right to **THE BASIC MINIMUM FOR SURVIVAL WAS SANCTIONED BY CUSTOM**"*, and the panchayat as **"the COURT OF APPEAL that would ensure that THE STATE CARRIED OUT ITS MORAL OBLIGATIONS."** **Show the ladder:** petition → compromise → desertion. **Show the state conceding it:** Aurangzeb's *"welfare of the peasantry"*; Akbar keeping payment in kind open; the flexible land classification. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then carry it forward** — the same claim animates **[Part III Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md)'s Deccan ryots** and **the Awadh peasants of [Class 10 Ch.2](../../Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/02_Nationalism_in_India.md), whose *swaraj* meant the end of *BEGAR* — the very word in these seventeenth-century petitions** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The Mughal state in the village: deep or shallow?"** *(a strong analytical framing)* | ⭐ **DEEP:** every province measured; four land classes; five collection methods; *jama* fixed peasant-wise by 1665; assessors, collectors and record keepers *"a DECISIVE AGENT in shaping agrarian relations"*; revenue taken in CASH. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SHALLOW:** **40% of land unmeasured forest**; ***jati panchayats* left to decide everything but criminal justice**; **headmen chosen locally and ratified by the zamindar, not the state**; **zamindars fielding a larger force than the empire**; **desertion as an effective peasant weapon**; **and Jahangir's tobacco ban simply ignored.** ⭐ **Verdict: DEEP BUT NARROW — it reached every field with a measuring rod and almost nothing else** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"India and the seventeenth-century world economy"** *(GS1/GS3 crossover)* | ⭐ **The four Asian empires and overland stability** · **the New World voyages** · **silver from the Americas via Europe/Turkey/Persia/Mocha/Basra and from JAPAN via the Dutch** · **Careri's one-way balance — European purchases "ALL PURCHAS'D FOR READY MONY, WHICH REMAINS THERE"** · **India's textiles, indigo, sugar, gum lac** · **the New World crops arriving in the other direction.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the reversal: after 1765 the Company buys Indian goods with Indian revenue and the bullion stops coming — [Part III Theme 9](../../Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Two empires, two archives"** *(the pairing that impresses)* | ⭐ **Set the *AIN* (1598) beside the FIFTH REPORT (1813).** **Both:** unprecedented in scope; produced BY an administration FOR administration; quantitatively valuable; and structurally silent about the people at the bottom. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The difference:** the *Ain*'s categories track **the LAND's condition** *(polaj → banjar)* and the **magnates' armed strength**; the Fifth Report's track **DEFAULT and SALE.** *State knowledge always mirrors what the state fears losing* |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in ten lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Eighty-five per cent of India lived in villages, and almost nothing they wrote survives** — so this chapter is built on **a court chronicle, provincial revenue records and East India Company papers**, and NCERT tells you at the outset that the *Ain* is *"a **VIEW FROM THE TOP**."*
2. ⭐ **Peasants were ***raiyat***, resident (***khud-kashta***) or migrant (***pahi-kashta***), and they held land as **INDIVIDUAL PROPERTY** that was **bought and sold** — a pair of bullocks and two ploughs was already above average.
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This was not subsistence farming.** *Two crops a year, 39–50 varieties, and ***JINS-I KAMIL*** — **cotton, sugarcane, oilseeds — promoted BY THE STATE because they paid more revenue**, with subsistence and commerce *"closely intertwined in an AVERAGE peasant's holding."*
4. ⭐ **The village had three parts — cultivators, panchayat, headman** — and the panchayat was **an OLIGARCHY** that **excluded its menial workers**, **policed caste**, and could **expel a man from his trade.** *Each caste also had **its own *jati panchayat***, whose decisions **the state respected except in criminal justice.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The "little republic" was a nineteenth-century British invention.** *There was **individual property, deep caste and gender inequity, a powerful group that exploited the weak — and CASH everywhere**, from the revenue demand to the weaver's advance to **Tavernier's village *shroff*.**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Women worked in the fields and in the crafts** — and *"the more commercialised the product, the greater the demand on women's labour."* *A scarcity of wives produced **bride-price and legitimate remarriage**, and, from the same premise, **strict control and draconian punishment.** **Women inherited zamindaris; RAJSHAHI had a woman at its head; and their names were erased from their own petitions.**
7. ⭐ **Forty per cent of the land was forest**, and ***JANGLI* did not mean uncivilised.** *The state saw the forest as ***mawas***, a refuge for people who *"become stubbornly rebellious and pay no taxes"* — **and it stayed UNMEASURED.**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Forest life was transformed less by conquest than by markets and by its own chiefs** — **gum lac became an overseas export**, and **tribal chieftains became zamindars and kings**, raising armies and, like the Ahoms, **monopolising the elephant trade.**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The zamindars were an exploitative class with private forts and a combined force larger than the emperor's — and the peasantry sided WITH them against the state**, while the bhakti saints, who spared no one, **attacked the revenue official and never the zamindar.**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The state measured, classified and assessed** — ***polaj* to *banjar***, ***jama*** against ***hasil***, cash or four kinds of share — and it could demand **CASH only because the world's silver was flowing to India** to pay for Indian goods. ⭐ **Then the chapter turns round and audits the *Ain* itself: five revisions, cross-checked testimony, numerals written in words — and no column anywhere in its tables for the peasants it is our chief evidence about.**
