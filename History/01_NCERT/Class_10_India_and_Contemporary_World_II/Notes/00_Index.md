# Class X — *India and the Contemporary World – II*

> **What this book actually is:** the **single highest-yield NCERT in the History series for Mains GS1.** Class 9 gave you the ideas; this one gives you **how those ideas built nations, and how the world got wired together.** Three of its chapters — *Nationalism in India*, *The Making of a Global World* and *The Age of Industrialisation* — are questioned, in substance, almost every year.

---

## 🏷️ Why this book outranks Class 9

Class 9 covered *"political philosophies like communism, capitalism, socialism"*. Class 10 covers the rest of the GS1 World History line, and the Indian core as well:

```
   UPSC SYLLABUS PHRASE            ->  CHAPTER

   "industrial revolution"         ->  Ch.4  The Age of Industrialisation
   "redrawal of national
    boundaries"                    ->  Ch.1  Rise of Nationalism in Europe
   "colonization, DECOLONIZATION"  ->  SUPPLEMENT — Indo-China
   "effect on the society"         ->  Ch.3  The Making of a Global World
                                       SUPPLEMENT — Work, Life and Leisure

   AND, from the INDIAN core of GS1
   "The Freedom Struggle — its
    various stages and important
    contributors"                  ->  Ch.2  Nationalism in India
   "modern Indian history from
    about the middle of the
    eighteenth century"            ->  Ch.4, Ch.5
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Ch.2 (*Nationalism in India*) is the most examinable single chapter in the entire NCERT History series.** It is Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, the Salt March, and — crucially — **how different social groups understood swaraj differently.** That last idea is what separates a good Mains answer on the freedom struggle from a chronology.

## ⚠️ What NCERT removed — and why we are putting it back

The current book prints **5 chapters**. The 2018-19 edition had **8**:

| Removed chapter | UPSC value | Our decision |
|---|---|---|
| **The Nationalist Movement in Indo-China** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Decolonization" is named in the GS1 syllabus.** This is the *only* place in the entire NCERT series you meet **Vietnam, French colonialism in Asia, and an anti-colonial war that defeated two superpowers** | ✅ **Restoring** — supplement Part A |
| **Work, Life and Leisure: Cities in the Contemporary World** | ⭐⭐⭐ **London and Bombay** — urbanisation, slums, migration, city planning. Feeds GS1 society *and* the urbanisation topic | ✅ **Restoring** — supplement Part B |
| **Novels, Society and History** | ⭐⭐ Caste, gender and nationalism read through fiction — and ⭐⭐⭐ **the highest density of author–work–year facts in the book**, which is exactly how Prelims asks about literature | ✅ **Restored in full** — supplement Part C |

**This is the third time we have made this call** — Class 7 (*Rulers and Buildings*, *Towns, Traders and Craftspersons*), Class 9 (*Peasants and Farmers*, *Clothing*), and now Class 10. **NCERT's rationalisation is a school-workload decision, not a UPSC-relevance decision**, and on this book it removed the one chapter that covers a syllabus keyword outright.

## 🧭 How this book connects to Class 9

**Read the two books as one argument.** Almost every Class 10 chapter picks up a Class 9 thread:

| Class 9 gave you | Class 10 develops it into |
|---|---|
| The **French Revolution** — the nation belongs to the people | **Ch.1** — how that idea remade the map of Europe, 1789–1871 |
| **Socialism and 1917** | **Ch.2** — why the Congress after 1917 sounds different; and the supplement's Ho Chi Minh |
| **Nazism** and the redrawal of boundaries | **Ch.1 and Ch.3** — nationalism's dark turn, and the interwar economy |
| **Forest Society / Pastoralists** — colonial extraction | **Ch.3 and Ch.4** — the global market those forests and pastures were feeding |
| **Peasants and Farmers** (supplement) — enclosures, opium, the world market | **Ch.3** — *The Making of a Global World* is the same story told whole |
| **Clothing** (supplement) — Swadeshi, khadi, Gandhi's dress | **Ch.2** — the movement that clothing was a weapon in |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐ **The single strongest cross-link:** Class 9's supplement ends with **opium, the triangular trade and Indian de-industrialisation**; Class 10 Ch.4 (*Age of Industrialisation*) begins with **why Indian weavers collapsed**. Read them back to back and you have a complete, quotable account of colonial economic drain — one that most candidates assemble badly from coaching notes.

## 🎯 The things to know cold from this book

1. **Frédéric Sorrieu's 1848 prints** and what the *"utopian vision"* was.
2. **The Congress of Vienna, 1815** — who met, what they restored, what they redrew.
3. **Giuseppe Mazzini, Young Italy, Young Europe** — and why Metternich called him *"the most dangerous enemy of our social order."*
4. **Unification of Germany (Bismarck) and of Italy (Cavour, Garibaldi)** — the two contrasting routes.
5. **Britain as the exception** — a nation-state formed by *"no abrupt upheaval"* but a long process.
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Rowlatt Act → Jallianwala Bagh → Non-Cooperation → Chauri Chaura → Civil Disobedience → Salt March → Round Table → Poona Pact.**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **How each group read swaraj differently** — Awadh peasants, Gudem hill people, Assam plantation workers, industrialists, the merchant class, women, Dalits, Muslims.
8. **Simon Commission, Lahore 1929 (Purna Swaraj), 26 January 1930.**
9. **The three flows of the pre-modern world: trade, migration, capital** — and the **silk routes**.
10. **The Corn Laws, the Bretton Woods twins, the Great Depression's effect on India.**
11. **Proto-industrialisation, the guilds, the "jobber", and why Indian weavers collapsed.**
12. **Gutenberg, the Protestant Reformation, Vernacular Press Act 1878, and the reading revolution.**
13. *(supplement)* **Ho Chi Minh, the Ho Chi Minh trail, the scholars' revolt, the Tonkin Free School, and why the US lost.**
14. *(supplement)* **London's tenements and Bombay's chawls, Haussmann's Paris, the Garden City, the 1863 Underground, the Improvement Trust's 64,000-vs-14,000, and why cities were feared.**
15. *(supplement)* ⭐⭐ **The novel: *Indulekha*, *Pariksha-Guru*, *Godan*, *Anandamath*, *Saraswativijayam*, *Sultana's Dream*** — and **why the same form served both the coloniser and the nationalist.**

## ✍️ Study order

1. ⭐ **Ch.2 first.** It is the most examinable chapter in the series, and you already have its background from Class 8 Ch.8.
2. **Ch.4, then Ch.3** — industrialisation explains the global world, not the other way round.
3. **Ch.1** — pairs with Class 9 Ch.1 and Ch.3.
4. **Ch.5**, then the **supplement**, leading with **Indo-China (Part A)**.
5. ⭐ **Read supplement Part C (Novels) immediately after Ch.5** — NCERT wrote it as Ch.5's sequel and says so in its first line. **Part B (Cities) pairs with Ch.4**, whose mill workers are the people who filled Girangaon.
