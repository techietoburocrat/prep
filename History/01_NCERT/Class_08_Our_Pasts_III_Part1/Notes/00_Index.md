## 🧵 Five Threads

### 1️⃣ Trade → revenue → territory
The Company came to **buy cloth and spices**. To buy, it needed silver. Then it discovered it could **take Bengal's revenue and buy Indian goods with Indian money**. **Chapter 2 and 3 are one continuous argument about that discovery.**

### 2️⃣ Every "reform" had a revenue motive underneath
Permanent Settlement, forest laws, the shift to commercial crops — all presented as improvement, all designed to **secure a predictable flow of revenue**. Learn to ask of every policy: *who paid, and who collected?*

### 3️⃣ Resistance was continuous, not occasional
Not just 1857. Santhal, Kol, Munda, indigo, Deccan riots, faqir and sanyasi rebellions — **the countryside was in revolt for a century before the Congress existed.** NCERT insists on this.

### 4️⃣ The colonial state classified in order to rule
Surveys, censuses, maps, ethnography. **Knowing India was a technique of governing it** — and the categories created (caste counts, "criminal tribes", "martial races") reshaped Indian society permanently.

### 5️⃣ Two nationalisms, and a partition
The movement was never one thing. **Moderates vs Extremists; Congress vs Muslim League; Gandhi's mass politics vs revolutionary action vs Ambedkar's separate demand.** Understanding **why it ended in Partition** requires taking all these strands seriously.
