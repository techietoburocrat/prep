# Chapter 5 — When People Revolt: 1857 and After

## 🎬 Start Here: What Turns a Mutiny into an Uprising

**NCERT gives a definition worth memorising, because it is the analytical spine of the chapter:**

> ⭐ ***MUTINY*** = **"when soldiers as a group disobey their officers in the army."**
>
> ⭐⭐ **But:** *"sometimes such struggles become quite **widespread as a POPULAR RESISTANCE so that THE POWER OF THE STATE BREAKS DOWN**. A very large number of people begin to believe that **they have a COMMON ENEMY** and **rise up against the enemy AT THE SAME TIME**. For such a situation to develop, people have to **ORGANISE, COMMUNICATE, TAKE INITIATIVE and DISPLAY THE CONFIDENCE to turn the situation around.**"*

```
   THE FOUR CONDITIONS:
       ORGANISE  |  COMMUNICATE  |  TAKE INITIATIVE  |  BE CONFIDENT

   ...plus the belief in a COMMON ENEMY, and SIMULTANEITY.
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the answer to "was 1857 a mutiny or a war of independence?"** — and NCERT hands you the framework rather than the slogan. **It began as a mutiny (soldiers disobeying officers) and became a popular uprising (the state's power breaking down over a region).** Use the four conditions to show *how* the transition happened. That is a far better answer than picking a label.

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Grievances — Three Groups

### 👑 Nawabs and rajas lose their power

> *"Since the mid-eighteenth century, nawabs and rajas had seen their power erode. **Residents had been stationed in many courts, the freedom of the rulers reduced, their armed forces disbanded, and their revenues and territories taken away by stages.**"* *(All of Chapter 2, in one sentence.)*

**⭐⭐ The three refusals that created three rebel leaders:**

| Petitioner | The request | The outcome |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **RANI LAKSHMIBAI of JHANSI** | That the Company **recognise her ADOPTED SON as heir** after her husband's death | **Refused** |
| ⭐⭐ **NANA SAHEB**, adopted son of **Peshwa Baji Rao II** | That he be given **his father's PENSION** when the latter died | **Refused** |
| ⭐ **AWADH** | — | **Subsidiary alliance imposed 1801**; **annexed 1856** on Dalhousie's declaration of **"misgovernment"** |

> ⭐ *"However, the Company, **confident of its superiority and military powers, turned down these pleas**."* **Both refusals produced leaders of the revolt.** *(And note the Bithur connection from Class 7 Ch.8 — Baji Rao II was pensioned off to Bithur near Kanpur in 1818; his son leads Kanpur in 1857.)*

**⭐⭐ And the plan to end the Mughal dynasty:**

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **The coins** | *"The **name of the Mughal king was REMOVED from the coins** minted by the Company"* |
| ⭐ **1849, Dalhousie** | After Bahadur Shah Zafar's death, **the family would be shifted OUT OF THE RED FORT** and given another place in Delhi |
| ⭐⭐ **1856, Canning** | **Bahadur Shah Zafar would be the LAST MUGHAL KING**; after his death **none of his descendants would be recognised as kings — they would just be called PRINCES** |

> 🎯 **Removing the emperor's name from the coinage was a deliberate act of desacralisation** — coinage is the classic assertion of sovereignty (Class 7 Ch.8). **The Company was formally erasing the Mughal claim just as it was about to need someone to blame for reviving it.**

### 🌾 The peasants

> *"In the countryside, **peasants and zamindars resented the HIGH TAXES and the RIGID METHODS of revenue collection**. Many **failed to pay back their loans to the moneylenders** and **gradually LOST THE LANDS they had tilled for generations**."* *(All of Chapter 3.)*

### 🎖️ ⭐⭐⭐ The sepoys

| Grievance | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Basic conditions** | *"They were unhappy about their **pay, allowances and conditions of service**."* |
| ⭐⭐ **Religious sensibility** | *"Some of the new rules **violated their religious sensibilities and beliefs**."* Many believed that **crossing the sea meant losing religion and caste** |
| ⭐⭐ **1824** | Sepoys **ordered to go to BURMA by the SEA route** refused — *"though they agreed to go by the **LAND route**"* — and were **severely punished** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1856** | Because the issue did not die down, the Company passed a **new law**: **every new recruit had to agree to SERVE OVERSEAS if required** |
| ⭐⭐ **The link to the countryside** | *"Many of them were **PEASANTS** and had **families living in the villages**. **So the anger of the peasants quickly spread among the sepoys.**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **That last row is the hinge of the whole chapter, and the best single insight to carry into an answer.**
>
> **The sepoy was not a separate professional caste — he was a peasant in uniform.** Every grievance of the countryside travelled straight into the barracks through family letters. **That is why a soldiers' mutiny could become a peasants' rebellion within weeks: they were the same people.**
>
> 🔗 **And recall Chapter 2's warning:** the army was built on **European drill and discipline that ignored caste and community feeling**, with NCERT asking *"could they see themselves ONLY as soldiers and NOT as members of communities?"* **1857 is the answer to that question.**

### ⛪ ⭐⭐ Responses to reform

> *"The British believed that Indian society **had to be reformed**."*

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Laws** | To **stop *sati*** and to **encourage the remarriage of widows** |
| **Education** | ⭐ **English-language education actively promoted** |
| ⭐ **After 1830** | The Company **allowed Christian missionaries to function freely** in its domain, and **even own land and property** |
| ⭐⭐ **1850** | A new law made **conversion to Christianity EASIER** — it allowed **an Indian who had converted to inherit the property of his ancestors** |

> ⭐⭐ **The consequence, in NCERT's own words:** *"**Many Indians began to feel that the British were DESTROYING THEIR RELIGION, THEIR SOCIAL CUSTOMS AND THEIR TRADITIONAL WAY OF LIFE.**"*
>
> ⚠️ **But NCERT immediately balances it:** *"There were of course **other Indians who WANTED to change existing social practices**."* → **Chapter 7.**
>
> 🎯 **Hold both halves.** The reforms were experienced by many as an attack on religion **and** were demanded by Indian reformers themselves. **A good answer refuses to flatten this into either "benevolent reform" or "cultural aggression".**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Through the Eyes of the People — Two Primary Sources

**These are gold, because they let you quote Indians rather than officials.**

### ⭐ Source 1 — Vishnubhatt Godse, *Majha Pravaas*

A **Brahman from a village in Maharashtra**, travelling with his uncle to a *yajna* at Mathura, met sepoys who told him not to proceed because **an upheaval would break out in three days**:

> *"The English were determined to **wipe out the religions of the Hindus and the Muslims**... they had made **a list of EIGHTY-FOUR RULES** and announced these in a gathering of all big kings and princes in Calcutta... the kings **refused to accept** these rules and **warned the English of dire consequences**... **A DATE WAS FIXED FOR THE WAR OF RELIGION** and the secret plan had been **circulated from the cantonment in MEERUT by letters sent to different cantonments.**"*

### ⭐⭐ Source 2 — Subedar Sitaram Pande, *From Sepoy to Subedar*

| About him | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Recruited** | **1812**, into the **Bengal Native Army** |
| **Served** | **48 years**, retiring in **1860** |
| ⭐ **His position** | *"He **helped the British to suppress the uprising** though **his own son was a rebel and was killed by the British in front of his eyes**"* |
| **The memoir** | Written in **1861 in AWADHI**; his commanding officer **Norgate** translated and published it |

> *"It is my humble opinion that **THIS SEIZING OF OUDH filled the minds of the Sepoys with DISTRUST** and led them to plot against the Government... They worked upon the feelings of sepoys, **telling them how treacherously the foreigners had behaved towards their king**..."*
>
> And on the cartridges: *"a report got about... that the cartridges used for these new rifles were **greased with the fat of cows and pigs**. The men from our regiment **wrote to others**... and **there was soon EXCITEMENT IN EVERY REGIMENT**. Some men pointed out that **in forty years' service nothing had ever been done by the Sarkar to insult their religion**, but... **the sepoys' minds had been INFLAMED BY THE SEIZURE OF OUDH.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Sitaram Pande's testimony is exceptionally valuable, and here is why you should use it:**
>
> He was **loyal to the British, lost his own son to them, and still names the annexation of Awadh as the cause.** A hostile witness confirming the grievance is the strongest kind of evidence.
>
> **And his sequence is the correct historical one: the cartridge did not cause the revolt — it detonated a charge that Awadh had already laid.** He says so explicitly: nothing in forty years had insulted their religion, *but* their minds had already been inflamed by the seizure of Oudh.
>
> ⭐ **Note also the communication mechanism** in both sources — **letters circulated between cantonments**, rumour travelling faster than administration. Compare the *chapati* and the Queen Victoria rumour of Chapter 3: **rumour was the network on which mobilisation ran.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ From Meerut to Delhi — the Outbreak

**Learn these dates exactly. They are asked directly.**

```
   *** 8 APRIL 1857 ***
   MANGAL PANDEY hanged to death for attacking his officers
   at BARRACKPORE
                            |
                            v
   *** 9 MAY 1857 ***
   At MEERUT, sepoys had refused to do the drill using the new
   cartridges, "suspected of being coated with the FAT OF COWS
   AND PIGS".
   *** EIGHTY-FIVE SEPOYS were DISMISSED from service and
       SENTENCED TO TEN YEARS IN JAIL for disobeying orders. ***
                            |
                            v
   *** 10 MAY 1857 -- "The response of the other Indian soldiers
       in Meerut was quite EXTRAORDINARY" ***
     - marched to the JAIL and RELEASED the imprisoned sepoys
     - attacked and KILLED BRITISH OFFICERS
     - captured GUNS AND AMMUNITION
     - SET FIRE to British buildings and properties
     - *** DECLARED WAR ON THE FIRANGIS ***
                            |
                            v
   "But WHO WOULD RULE THE LAND INSTEAD? The soldiers had an
    answer -- *** THE MUGHAL EMPEROR BAHADUR SHAH ZAFAR. ***"
                            |
                            v
   *** THE NIGHT OF 10 MAY: the sepoys RODE ALL NIGHT to reach
       DELHI in the early hours of 11 May ***
     - regiments stationed in Delhi ALSO ROSE
     - officers killed, arms seized, buildings burnt
     - troops gathered at the RED FORT walls DEMANDING TO MEET
       the Badshah
     - "The emperor was NOT QUITE WILLING to challenge the mighty
        British power BUT THE SOLDIERS PERSISTED. They FORCED
        THEIR WAY INTO THE PALACE and PROCLAIMED Bahadur Shah
        Zafar as their leader."
```

> ⭐ ***FIRANGIS*** = **foreigners** — *"the term reflects an attitude of contempt."*
>
> 🎯 **Notice the crucial detail: the soldiers had an answer to "who rules instead?"** A mutiny without an alternative is a riot. **The moment they named a sovereign, it became a political project.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Why Bahadur Shah Zafar's acceptance changed everything

> The ageing emperor had to accept. *"He wrote **letters to ALL THE CHIEFS AND RULERS of the country to come forward and organise a CONFEDERACY OF INDIAN STATES to fight the British**. **This single step taken by Bahadur Shah had GREAT IMPLICATIONS.**"*

```
   WHY IT MATTERED:

   "The Mughal dynasty had ruled over a VERY LARGE PART of the
    country. *** MOST SMALLER RULERS AND CHIEFTAINS CONTROLLED
    DIFFERENT TERRITORIES ON BEHALF OF THE MUGHAL RULER. ***
                            |
                            v
    Threatened by the expansion of British rule, many of them felt
    that *** IF THE MUGHAL EMPEROR COULD RULE AGAIN, THEY TOO
    WOULD BE ABLE TO RULE THEIR OWN TERRITORIES ONCE MORE,
    UNDER MUGHAL AUTHORITY. ***"
                            |
                            v
   "The British had NOT EXPECTED this to happen. They thought the
    disturbance caused by the cartridges WOULD DIE DOWN. But
    *** Bahadur Shah Zafar's decision to BLESS THE UPRISING
        CHANGED THE ENTIRE SITUATION DRAMATICALLY. ***
    Often when people SEE AN ALTERNATIVE POSSIBILITY, they feel
    INSPIRED AND ENTHUSED. It gives them the COURAGE, HOPE AND
    CONFIDENCE TO ACT."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the single most important analytical passage in the chapter.**
>
> The Mughal emperor was **politically powerless but constitutionally central** — the fiction under which dozens of Indian rulers held their legitimacy. **Reviving the fiction gave every dispossessed raja a legal theory for his own restoration.** It converted scattered grievances into **a single alternative sovereignty**.
>
> 💻 The Mughal throne was a **shared authority anchor**. Everyone's claim ultimately chained back to it. Reactivating the root **re-validated every claim beneath it at once**.
>
> ⭐ **NCERT's own question — "why did the Mughal emperor agree to support the rebels?" — is worth thinking about honestly.** He was reluctant; he was old; he was about to be the last of his line by Canning's 1856 decision. He had **nothing left to protect and one thing left to be.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Uprising Spreads — Leaders and Centres

> *"After the British were routed from Delhi, **there was no uprising for almost a week. IT TOOK THAT MUCH TIME FOR NEWS TO TRAVEL.** Then, a spurt of mutinies began."*

**Regiment after regiment mutinied and joined troops at **nodal points like DELHI, KANPUR and LUCKNOW**. Then *"the people of the towns and villages also rose up in rebellion and rallied around **local leaders, zamindars and chiefs**."*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The leaders — memorise this table

| Leader | Centre | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Bahadur Shah Zafar** | **DELHI** | Proclaimed leader; wrote to all chiefs to form a **confederacy** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **NANA SAHEB** | **KANPUR** | Adopted son of the late **Peshwa Baji Rao**, living near Kanpur; **gathered forces and expelled the British garrison**; ⭐ **proclaimed himself PESHWA** and **declared he was a GOVERNOR UNDER Emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar** |
| ⭐⭐ **BIRJIS QADR** | **LUCKNOW** | Son of the **deposed Nawab Wajid Ali Shah**; proclaimed the **new Nawab**; ⭐ **also acknowledged the suzerainty of Bahadur Shah Zafar** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **BEGUM HAZRAT MAHAL** | **LUCKNOW** | Birjis Qadr's **mother**; ⭐ **"took an active part in organising the uprising against the British"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **RANI LAKSHMIBAI** | **JHANSI** | **Joined the rebel sepoys** and fought the British |
| ⭐⭐ **TANTIA TOPE** | Kanpur / central India | ⭐ **The general of Nana Saheb** |
| ⭐⭐ **RANI AVANTIBAI LODHI** | **RAMGARH**, **Mandla region of Madhya Pradesh** | ⭐ **Raised and led an army of FOUR THOUSAND** against the British who had taken over her state's administration |
| ⭐⭐ **AHMADULLAH SHAH** | **Faizabad → Lucknow** | ⭐ A **maulvi from FAIZABAD** who **prophesied that British rule would soon end**; *"caught the imagination of the people and raised a huge force"* |
| ⭐⭐ **BAKHT KHAN** | **Bareilly → DELHI** | ⭐ A **soldier from BAREILLY** who took charge of a large force and **became a key military leader of the uprising** |
| ⭐⭐ **KUNWAR SINGH** | **BIHAR** | ⭐ An **OLD ZAMINDAR** who joined the rebel sepoys and **battled the British for many months** |
| **Ghazis** | Delhi | *"A large number of **ghazis or religious warriors** came together"* |

> 🧠 **Memory hook by place: Delhi–Zafar & Bakht Khan · Kanpur–Nana Saheb & Tantia Tope · Lucknow–Birjis Qadr & Begum Hazrat Mahal · Jhansi–Lakshmibai · Ramgarh–Avantibai · Faizabad–Ahmadullah Shah · Bihar–Kunwar Singh.**
>
> ⭐⭐ **Note the pattern of legitimacy:** Nana Saheb called himself a **governor under Bahadur Shah Zafar**; Birjis Qadr **acknowledged his suzerainty**. **The rebels were building a chain of authority, not just fighting.** That is evidence for the "more than a mutiny" argument.
>
> ⭐ **And note three women leading armed forces** — Lakshmibai, Hazrat Mahal, Avantibai. Excellent for a GS1 question on women in the national movement.

### ⭐⭐ Why the revolt spread — and the telegram

> *"The British were **greatly outnumbered** by the rebel forces. They were **defeated in a number of battles**. This **convinced the people that the rule of the British had collapsed for good** and **gave them the confidence to take the plunge and join the uprising**."*

**⭐⭐ The telegram of 6 August 1857**, from **Lieutenant Colonel Tytler** to his Commander-in-Chief:

> *"Our men are **cowed by the numbers opposed to them and the endless fighting**. **EVERY VILLAGE IS HELD AGAINST US, THE ZAMINDARS HAVE RISEN TO OPPOSE US.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Quote this.** It is the British themselves confirming the revolt had passed beyond the army: **every village, and the zamindars.** That single line does more to establish "popular uprising" than any assertion could.
>
> 🎯 **And notice the feedback loop:** British defeats convinced people the Raj had collapsed → which brought more people in → which produced more defeats. **Success recruited.** The same dynamic runs in reverse during the suppression.

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Company Fights Back

> *"**Unnerved by the scale of the upheaval**, the Company decided to **repress the revolt with all its might**. It **brought reinforcements from England**, **passed new laws so that the rebels could be convicted with ease**, and then **moved into the storm centres of the revolt**."*

| Event | Date | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Delhi recaptured** | **September 1857** | *(A **7-mile-long siege train** of cannon and ammunition pulled by elephants arrived on **3 September 1857**)* |
| ⭐⭐ **Bahadur Shah Zafar tried** | — | **Sentenced to LIFE IMPRISONMENT**; he and his wife **Begum Zinat Mahal** sent to prison in ⭐ **RANGOON in October 1858** |
| ⭐⭐ **Zafar died** | ⭐ **November 1862**, in the **Rangoon jail** | |
| ⭐⭐ **Lucknow taken** | ⭐ **March 1858** | |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Rani Lakshmibai** | ⭐ **Defeated and killed in JUNE 1858** | |
| ⭐ **Rani Avantibai** | — | After an **initial victory at Kheri**, she **chose to embrace death** when surrounded on all sides |
| ⭐⭐ **Tantia Tope** | ⭐ **Captured, tried and killed in APRIL 1859** | **Escaped to the jungles of central India** and continued a ⭐ **GUERRILLA WAR with the support of many tribal and peasant leaders** |

> ⭐ **"The recapture of Delhi did NOT mean that the uprising died down."** *"The British had to fight for **TWO YEARS** to suppress the massive forces of popular uprising."*

**⭐⭐ Carrot and stick:**

> *"Just as victories against the British had earlier encouraged uprising, **the defeat of rebel forces encouraged DESERTIONS**. The British also tried their best to **win back the loyalty of the people**. They announced **rewards for loyal landholders**, who would be allowed to **continue to enjoy traditional rights over their lands**. Those who had rebelled were told that **if they submitted, and if they had NOT KILLED ANY WHITE PEOPLE, they would remain safe** and their **rights and claims to land would not be denied**."*
>
> ⚠️ *"**Nevertheless, hundreds of sepoys, rebels, nawabs and rajas were TRIED AND HANGED.**"*

> 🎯 **NCERT's question — "how did the British succeed in securing the submission of the rebel landowners of Awadh?" — is answered exactly here:** by **guaranteeing their land rights** if they submitted. **Land was the currency of both rebellion and reconciliation.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Aftermath — The Six Changes

> *"The British had **regained control of the country by the end of 1859**, but **they could not carry on ruling the land with the same policies any more.**"*

**⭐⭐⭐ Learn all six. This is guaranteed exam content.**

```
   [1] *** THE GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1858 ***
       The British Parliament TRANSFERRED THE POWERS OF THE EAST
       INDIA COMPANY TO THE BRITISH CROWN, "in order to ensure a
       more responsible management of Indian affairs".
         - A member of the British Cabinet was appointed
           *** SECRETARY OF STATE FOR INDIA *** , responsible for
           all matters of Indian governance
         - He was given an advisory council, the *** INDIA COUNCIL ***
         - The Governor-General was given the title *** VICEROY ***,
           "a personal representative of the Crown"
       => "the British government accepted DIRECT RESPONSIBILITY
           for ruling India."

   [2] *** NO MORE ANNEXATION ***
       All ruling chiefs were assured their territory would NEVER BE
       ANNEXED in future, and could PASS ON THEIR KINGDOMS TO THEIR
       HEIRS, *** INCLUDING ADOPTED SONS. ***
       BUT they had to acknowledge the British Queen as their
       *** SOVEREIGN PARAMOUNT *** -- holding their kingdoms as
       SUBORDINATES of the British Crown.

   [3] *** THE ARMY RESTRUCTURED ***
       - the proportion of INDIAN soldiers would be REDUCED
       - the number of EUROPEAN soldiers INCREASED
       - instead of recruiting from AWADH, BIHAR, CENTRAL INDIA and
         SOUTH INDIA, more soldiers would come from the
         *** GURKHAS, SIKHS and PATHANS ***

   [4] *** MUSLIMS TARGETED ***
       "The LAND AND PROPERTY OF MUSLIMS was CONFISCATED ON A LARGE
        SCALE and they were treated with SUSPICION AND HOSTILITY.
        The British believed that they were responsible for the
        uprising in a big way."

   [5] *** RELIGIOUS NON-INTERFERENCE ***
       "The British decided to RESPECT THE CUSTOMARY RELIGIOUS AND
        SOCIAL PRACTICES of the people in India."

   [6] *** LANDLORDS PROTECTED ***
       "Policies were made to PROTECT LANDLORDS AND ZAMINDARS and
        give them SECURITY OF RIGHTS OVER THEIR LANDS."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Read changes [2], [5] and [6] together and the logic is unmistakable.** The British identified their two fatal mistakes — **annexing states** and **appearing to attack religion** — and reversed both. Then they **bought the loyalty of the landed class** with security of tenure.
>
> **The post-1857 Raj was built on an alliance with princes and landlords, and a promise not to interfere in religion.** That is why the later national movement had to be led by **a new English-educated middle class** rather than by the old elites — the old elites had been **bought**. **This is the direct bridge to Chapter 8.**
>
> ⭐ **Change [3] is the origin of the "martial races" doctrine** — recruitment shifted to communities judged loyal in 1857, and away from Awadh and Bihar, which had rebelled. It shaped the Indian Army for a century.
>
> ⭐⭐ **Change [4] is the origin of a long and consequential policy shift.** Muslims were held disproportionately responsible; land was confiscated; and the community was treated with hostility for decades — a fact that matters when you reach **the founding of the Muslim League and the politics of Partition** in Chapter 8. **NCERT states it plainly and so should you, without embroidery.**
>
> 🔗 **Polity crossover:** the **Government of India Act 1858** created the **Secretary of State, the India Council and the Viceroy** — the constitutional architecture that runs to 1947. Cross-reference with your Polity notes on the evolution of the Indian constitution.

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐ Case Study: The Khurda (Paika) Uprising, 1817

**A newer NCERT addition and genuinely current-affairs relevant** — the Paika Rebellion has been the subject of major commemoration, and there has been public debate about calling it a "first war of independence".

### The background

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Where** | **KHURDA**, a small kingdom in the **south-eastern part of ORISSA**, built in the **late 16th century** |
| **Its size** | **105 *garhs*, 60 large and 1,109 small villages** at the start of the 19th century |
| **Earlier losses** | **Raja Birakishore Dev** had been forced to give the **Marathas** four *parganas*, the **superintendence of the JAGANNATH TEMPLE**, and the administration of fourteen *garjats* |
| ⭐ **The betrayal** | His successor **MUKUNDA DEV II**, sensing opportunity in the **Anglo-Maratha conflict**, negotiated with the British to **recover his territories and rights over the Jagannath Temple**. ⭐ **But after the British occupied Orissa in 1803 they showed no inclination to oblige him on either score** |
| **The consequence** | He tried to assert his rights by force, in alliance with other feudatory chiefs and with **secret Maratha support** → **deposed, territories annexed**; left only with **management of the Jagannath Temple** and a grant of **one-tenth of his former revenue**, residing at **Puri** |

> 🔗 **Note the Jagannath Temple again** — Class 7 Ch.7 told you that *"all those who conquered Orissa, including the English East India Company, attempted to gain control over the temple."* **Here is that story from the other side.**

### ⭐⭐ The causes of the 1817 uprising

```
   [1] "The British followed a policy of RESUMING SERVICE TENURES.
        *** It bitterly affected the lives of the EX-MILITIA of the
        state, the PAIKS. ***"

   [2] An UNREASONABLE INCREASE in the revenue demand, and
       OPPRESSIVE ways of collecting it
       -> "LARGE SCALE DESERTION of people from Khurda between
           1805 and 1817"

   [3] A series of SHORT-TERM SETTLEMENTS, "each time INCREASING
       THE DEMANDS, *** NOT RECOGNISING either the PRODUCTIVE
       CAPACITY of the land or the PAYING CAPACITY of the ryots ***"

   [4] "*** NO LENIENCY was shown even in case of NATURAL
        CALAMITIES ***, which Orissa was frequently prone to.
        Rather, LANDS OF DEFAULTERS WERE SOLD OFF to scheming
        revenue officials or SPECULATORS FROM BENGAL."

   PLUS four further grievances:
     (a) the introduction of the *** SICCA RUPEE *** (silver currency)
     (b) the insistence on payment of revenue IN THE NEW CURRENCY
     (c) an unprecedented RISE IN THE PRICES OF FOOD-STUFF AND SALT,
         following the *** SALT MONOPOLY *** which deprived Orissa's
         traditional salt makers of making salt
     (d) the AUCTION OF LOCAL ESTATES IN CALCUTTA, which brought
         *** ABSENTEE LANDLORDS FROM BENGAL *** into Orissa

   ...and "the INSENSITIVE AND CORRUPT POLICE SYSTEM".
```

> ⭐⭐ **This list is a compact catalogue of everything Chapter 3 described** — over-assessment, no relief in calamity, land sold to outsiders, absentee landlords — **plus a currency change and a salt monopoly.**
>
> 🎯 **The salt monopoly is worth flagging:** it deprived **traditional salt makers of Orissa** of their livelihood and raised prices. **The same grievance Gandhi would choose for the Salt March in 1930.**

### ⭐⭐ The leader and the course

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The leader** | **JAGABANDHU BIDYADHAR MAHAPATRA BHRAMARABAR RAI**, popularly ⭐ **BUXI JAGABANDHU** — the **hereditary Military Commander of the deposed king**, himself among the dispossessed landholders |
| ⭐ **His condition** | *"He had **in effect become a beggar**, and for **nearly two years survived on voluntary contributions from the people of Khurda** before deciding to fight for their grievances as well as his own"* |
| ⭐⭐ **The outbreak** | ⭐ **29 MARCH 1817** — the **Paiks attacked the police station and other government establishments at BANPUR**, killing **more than a hundred men** and taking a large amount of government money |
| **The spread** | Ripples spread with **Khurda as the epicentre**; ⭐ **zamindars and ryots alike joined the Paiks**; those who did not **were taken to task**; a ⭐ **"NO-RENT CAMPAIGN"** was started |
| ⭐⭐ **14 April 1817** | **Buxi Jagabandhu, leading five to ten thousand Paiks and men of the KANDH tribe, SEIZED PURI** and **declared the hesitant king Mukunda Dev II as their ruler**. ⭐ **The priests of the Jagannath Temple extended full support** |
| **Suppression** | The British clamped ⭐ **MARTIAL LAW**; the king was captured and imprisoned at **Cuttack**; **Major-General Martindell** was sent in; **hundreds of Paiks were killed**, many fled to the jungles, some returned under an **amnesty**. By **May 1817** the uprising was mostly contained |
| ⭐ **The long tail** | Outside Khurda it was **sustained by Buxi Jagabandhu** with help from the **Raja of Kujung** and the loyalty of the Paiks **until his surrender in MAY 1825** |

### ⭐⭐ The outcome — and why it matters

> Afterwards the British adopted a policy of ⭐ **"leniency, indulgence and forbearance"** towards the people of Khurda:
> - **The price of salt was reduced**
> - **Necessary reforms were made in the police and justice systems**
> - **Corrupt revenue officials were dismissed**
> - ⭐ **Former landholders were restored to their lands**
> - The king's son **Ram Chandra Dev III** was allowed to move to Puri and take charge of the **Jagannath Temple** with a grant of **twenty-four thousand rupees**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's closing judgement, and it is pointed:**
>
> *"In sum, it was the **first such popular anti-British armed uprising in Orissa**, which had **far reaching effect on the future of British administration** in that part of the country. **To merely call it a 'Paik Rebellion' will thus be an UNDERSTATEMENT.**"*
>
> 🎯 **Two exam-relevant points:**
> 1. **It was popular, not merely military** — zamindars, ryots, the Kandh tribe and the temple priests all joined, and there was a **no-rent campaign**. That is the "mutiny → popular uprising" test from the start of this chapter, met **forty years before 1857**
> 2. **It won concessions** — salt prices cut, land restored, officials dismissed. **Resistance produced results**, exactly as with the jhum cultivators and Birsa in Chapter 4
>
> ⭐ **The chapter's placement is deliberate:** it shows that *"resentment against the colonial policies of the British had been **building up since the beginning of the 19th century in different parts of the country**"* — **1857 was not the beginning of resistance, only its largest instance.**

## 🔁 2-MINUTE RECAP

1. **A mutiny is soldiers disobeying officers; a popular uprising is when the state's power breaks down** because many people believe they face **a common enemy** and act **at the same time** — requiring them to **organise, communicate, take initiative and be confident**.
2. **Rulers' grievances:** **Lakshmibai's adopted heir** and **Nana Saheb's pension** both refused; **Awadh annexed in 1856** for "misgovernment"; the emperor's name **removed from the coinage**, and **Canning's 1856 decision** that Zafar would be the **last king**.
3. **Peasants' grievances:** **high taxes, rigid collection, debt, and loss of ancestral land** (Chapter 3).
4. ⭐ **Sepoys' grievances:** pay and conditions; the **1824 refusal to sail to Burma**; the **1856 law compelling overseas service**; and above all that **sepoys were peasants with families in the villages, so rural anger travelled straight into the barracks**.
5. **Reform grievances:** *sati* and widow-remarriage laws, **English education**, **missionaries free to own property after 1830**, and the **1850 law letting converts inherit** — though **other Indians actively wanted reform**.
6. ⭐ **Sitaram Pande**, a loyal 48-year veteran whose own son was killed as a rebel, blamed **the seizure of Oudh**, noting that in forty years nothing had insulted their religion before.
7. ⭐⭐ **The outbreak:** **Mangal Pandey hanged 8 April at Barrackpore**; **9 May, 85 Meerut sepoys jailed for ten years**; **10 May, the Meerut regiments freed them, killed officers and declared war on the *firangis***; they **rode overnight to Delhi** and **forced Bahadur Shah Zafar to lead them**.
8. ⭐⭐⭐ **Zafar's acceptance transformed everything**, because most smaller rulers held their territories **on behalf of the Mughal ruler** — so his restoration promised **theirs**. He wrote to all chiefs to form a **confederacy**.
9. **The leaders:** **Nana Saheb** (Kanpur, "governor under Zafar"), **Birjis Qadr and Begum Hazrat Mahal** (Lucknow), **Lakshmibai** (Jhansi), **Tantia Tope**, **Rani Avantibai** (4,000 men), **Ahmadullah Shah** (Faizabad), **Bakht Khan** (Bareilly), **Kunwar Singh** (Bihar).
10. ⭐ **Tytler's telegram, 6 August 1857:** *"Every village is held against us, the zamindars have risen to oppose us."*
11. **Suppression:** **Delhi September 1857**, **Lucknow March 1858**, **Lakshmibai killed June 1858**, **Zafar exiled to Rangoon October 1858 and died there in 1862**, **Tantia Tope killed April 1859**. **Two years of fighting**; control regained **by the end of 1859**. Rebels who **submitted and had killed no white people** kept their land — **but hundreds were tried and hanged**.
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **The six changes of 1858:** Crown rule via the **Government of India Act** (**Secretary of State**, **India Council**, **Viceroy**); **no further annexation** and **adopted heirs allowed**, with the Queen as **Sovereign Paramount**; **fewer Indian and more European troops**, recruited from **Gurkhas, Sikhs and Pathans**; **Muslim property confiscated** and the community treated with hostility; **religious non-interference**; and **protection of landlords and zamindars**.
13. ⭐⭐ **Khurda, 1817:** the **Paiks** under **Buxi Jagabandhu** rose on **29 March at Banpur**, **seized Puri on 14 April** with **Kandh** support and the **Jagannath priests**, ran a **no-rent campaign**, and were suppressed under **martial law** — but won **cheaper salt, police and judicial reform, and restored lands**. He held out until **May 1825**. NCERT: *"to merely call it a 'Paik Rebellion' will be an understatement."*
