## 🎬 Start Here: The Question That Created a Nation

> *"The above-mentioned developments led the people to ask a crucial question: ⭐⭐ **WHAT IS THIS COUNTRY OF INDIA AND FOR WHOM IS IT MEANT?**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **"The answer that gradually emerged was: INDIA WAS THE PEOPLE OF INDIA — ALL the people irrespective of CLASS, COLOUR, CASTE, CREED, LANGUAGE, or GENDER. And the country, its resources and systems, WERE MEANT FOR ALL OF THEM."**
>
> *"With this answer came the awareness that **the British were exercising control over the resources of India and the lives of its people, and UNTIL THIS CONTROL WAS ENDED, INDIA COULD NOT BE FOR INDIANS.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Memorise this passage. It is the best short definition of Indian nationalism in any textbook, and it makes an ideal opening line for almost any Mains answer on the freedom struggle.**
>
> Note what it does: it defines the nation **not by religion, language or race, but by inclusion** — and it derives the demand for independence **logically** from that definition. **If India belongs to all Indians, foreign control is by definition illegitimate.**

**⭐ And the key concept:** ***SOVEREIGN*** = *"the capacity to act independently without outside interference."* The associations *"worked with the idea that **the people should be SOVEREIGN — a MODERN CONSCIOUSNESS and a KEY FEATURE OF NATIONALISM**... the Indian people should be **empowered to take decisions regarding their affairs.**"*

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Early Political Associations

> *"This consciousness began to be clearly stated by the **political associations formed after 1850**, especially those of the **1870s and 1880s**. **Most of these were led by ENGLISH-EDUCATED PROFESSIONALS such as LAWYERS.**"*

| Association |
|---|
| ⭐ **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** |
| ⭐ **Indian Association** |
| ⭐ **Madras Mahajan Sabha** |
| ⭐ **Bombay Presidency Association** |
| ⭐⭐ **The Indian National Congress** |

> ⭐⭐ **NCERT makes a lovely point about the name:** *"**sarvajanik**" literally means **"of or for ALL THE PEOPLE"** (*sarva* = all + *janik* = of the people).
>
> ⭐ *"Though many of these associations functioned in **specific parts of the country**, their goals were stated as **THE GOALS OF ALL THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, not those of any one region, community or class**."*

> 🔗 **Note the continuity from Chapter 6:** these were **English-educated professionals** — the exact class Macaulay's 1835 Act created. **The instrument designed to produce loyal subjects produced the leadership of the freedom movement.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Three Provocations, 1878–1883

```
   *** 1878 -- THE ARMS ACT ***
   "disallowing Indians from POSSESSING ARMS"

   *** 1878 -- THE VERNACULAR PRESS ACT ***
   "enacted in an effort to SILENCE THOSE WHO WERE CRITICAL OF
    THE GOVERNMENT. The Act allowed the government to CONFISCATE
    THE ASSETS OF NEWSPAPERS INCLUDING THEIR PRINTING PRESSES
    if the newspapers published anything found 'OBJECTIONABLE'."

   *** 1883 -- THE ILBERT BILL ***
   "The bill provided for the TRIAL OF BRITISH OR EUROPEAN PERSONS
    BY INDIANS, and sought EQUALITY BETWEEN BRITISH AND INDIAN
    JUDGES in the country."
                            |
                            v
   *** "But when WHITE OPPOSITION forced the government to
       WITHDRAW the bill, INDIANS WERE ENRAGED. The event
       HIGHLIGHTED THE RACIAL ATTITUDES OF THE BRITISH IN INDIA." ***
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The Ilbert Bill episode is the crucial one and is asked directly.** The lesson Indians drew was devastating: **the British would not accept legal equality even in principle, and organised white agitation could overturn government policy where Indian opinion could not.**
>
> 🎯 **It also taught a tactical lesson:** the Europeans had won by **organising**. *"The need for an all-India organisation of educated Indians had been felt since 1880, but **THE ILBERT BILL CONTROVERSY DEEPENED THIS DESIRE.**"* **The Congress was founded partly in imitation of the pressure group that had just defeated Indian interests.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Indian National Congress, 1885

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Founded** | *"The Indian National Congress was established when ⭐ **72 DELEGATES** from all over the country met at ⭐ **BOMBAY in DECEMBER 1885**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **Early leadership** | ⭐ **Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Badruddin Tyabji, W. C. Bonnerji, Surendranath Banerji, Romesh Chandra Dutt, S. Subramania Iyer** — *"largely from **BOMBAY and CALCUTTA**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **Dadabhai Naoroji** | *"A **businessman and publicist settled in LONDON**, and **for a time a MEMBER OF THE BRITISH PARLIAMENT**, [who] **guided the younger nationalists**"*. ⭐ His book ***Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*** *"offered a **scathing criticism of the ECONOMIC IMPACT of British rule**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **A. O. Hume** | *"A **RETIRED BRITISH OFFICIAL** also played a part in **bringing Indians from the various regions together**"* |

**⭐ Two contemporary voices:**

> *The Indian Mirror*, January 1886: *"The First National Congress at Bombay is **the NUCLEUS OF A FUTURE PARLIAMENT for our country**."*
>
> **Badruddin Tyabji**, as President in **1887**: *"This Congress is composed of the representatives, **not of any ONE class or community of India, but of ALL the different communities of India.**"*

> ⭐ ***Publicist*** = *"someone who publicises an idea by circulating information, writing reports, speaking at meetings."*
>
> 🎯 **NCERT's activity asks why the Congress chose to speak for all Indians from the very beginning** — and the answer is both principled and strategic. Principled, because of the definition of India in the opening passage. Strategic, because **a body claiming to represent only one community could never claim to represent the nation** — and the entire legitimacy of the demand for self-rule rested on that claim. **This is also exactly the claim the Muslim League would later contest.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Moderates, 1885–1905

> *"It has often been said that the Congress in the **first twenty years** was ⭐ **'MODERATE' in its OBJECTIVES AND METHODS**."*

### Their political demands

| Demand |
|---|
| **A greater voice for Indians in the government and administration** |
| ⭐ **Legislative Councils** to be made **more representative**, given **more power**, and **introduced in provinces where none existed** |
| ⭐⭐ **Indians to be placed in HIGH POSITIONS** in government — and for this, ⭐ **civil service examinations to be held IN INDIA as well, not just in London** |
| ⭐ **Separation of the JUDICIARY from the EXECUTIVE** |
| ⭐ **Repeal of the ARMS ACT** |
| ⭐ **Freedom of SPEECH AND EXPRESSION** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Why "Indianisation" mattered — two reasons

```
   [1] AGAINST RACISM
       "most important jobs at the time were MONOPOLISED BY WHITE
        OFFICIALS, and the British generally assumed that
        *** INDIANS COULD NOT BE GIVEN POSITIONS OF RESPONSIBILITY ***"

   [2] AGAINST THE DRAIN OF WEALTH
       "Since British officers were *** SENDING A MAJOR PART OF THEIR
        LARGE SALARIES HOME ***, Indianisation, it was hoped, would
        also *** REDUCE THE DRAIN OF WEALTH TO ENGLAND ***"
```

> ⭐⭐ **The second reason is the one most students miss, and it is the more sophisticated point.** Indianisation was not merely a demand for dignity — **it was an economic argument**. Every British officer's salary remitted to England was capital leaving India. **Employ Indians and the money stays.**
>
> 🔗 **The drain argument traces back to the Diwani of 1765 (Ch.2)** and was systematised by **Dadabhai Naoroji** in *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Their economic critique — and why it matters

> *"The early Congress also raised a number of **ECONOMIC ISSUES**. It declared that **BRITISH RULE HAD LED TO POVERTY AND FAMINES**: **increase in the LAND REVENUE had impoverished peasants and zamindars**, and **EXPORTS OF GRAINS TO EUROPE had created FOOD SHORTAGES**."*

| Their demands | Their resolutions |
|---|---|
| **Reduction of revenue** | On the **SALT TAX** |
| **Cut in military expenditure** | On the **treatment of Indian labourers abroad** |
| **More funds for irrigation** | On the **sufferings of FOREST DWELLERS** — *"caused by an interfering forest administration"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's own conclusion is important and worth quoting:** *"All this shows that **DESPITE BEING A BODY OF THE EDUCATED ELITE, the Congress did NOT talk only on behalf of professional groups, zamindars or industrialists.**"*
>
> 🎯 **This is the corrective to the standard dismissal of the Moderates as a narrow elite club.** They raised the **salt tax, indentured labour abroad, and forest dwellers' grievances** — issues of the poorest. **Note the salt tax especially: the grievance Gandhi would choose forty-five years later was already on the Congress agenda in the 1880s.**

### Their methods

> *"The Moderate leaders wanted to **DEVELOP PUBLIC AWARENESS about the UNJUST NATURE of British rule**. They **published newspapers, wrote articles, and showed how British rule was leading to the ECONOMIC RUIN of the country**. They **criticised British rule in their speeches** and **sent representatives to different parts of the country to MOBILISE PUBLIC OPINION**."*

**⭐⭐ Their underlying assumption:**

> *"They felt that **the British had RESPECT FOR THE IDEALS OF FREEDOM AND JUSTICE**, and so **they would ACCEPT THE JUST DEMANDS of Indians**. What was necessary, therefore, was **to EXPRESS these demands, and MAKE THE GOVERNMENT AWARE of the feelings of Indians.**"*

> ⭐⭐ **That assumption is the whole Moderate strategy, and its whole vulnerability.** It rested on a belief that **the British were persuadable** — that once they understood, they would concede. **The next generation attacked precisely this belief.**
>
> ⚠️ **And NCERT prints a critical voice from inside** — **Dinshaw Wacha** to Naoroji, **1887**: *"Pherozeshah is nowadays too busy with his personal work... **They are already rich enough**... I wonder **how if all remain busy in the pursuit of gold can the progress of the country be advanced?**"* **A frank admission that the early leaders were part-time politicians with lucrative practices.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ "Freedom Is Our Birthright" — the Radicals

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **When** | *"By the **1890s**, many Indians began to raise questions about the political style of the Congress"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Who and where** | In **BENGAL, MAHARASHTRA and PUNJAB** — ⭐ **BEPIN CHANDRA PAL**, ⭐ **BAL GANGADHAR TILAK**, ⭐ **LALA LAJPAT RAI** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Their charge** | *"They **criticised the Moderates for their 'POLITICS OF PRAYERS'**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **Their emphasis** | *"The importance of **SELF-RELIANCE and CONSTRUCTIVE WORK**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Their argument** | *"**People must rely on THEIR OWN STRENGTH, not on the 'GOOD' INTENTIONS of the government**; people must **fight for SWARAJ**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Tilak's slogan** | ⭐ **"FREEDOM IS MY BIRTHRIGHT AND I SHALL HAVE IT!"** |

> 🧠 **"Lal, Bal, Pal" — Lala Lajpat Rai (Punjab), Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Maharashtra), Bipin Chandra Pal (Bengal).** The classic trio, and their regions are asked.
>
> ⭐ **Tilak's newspaper was *KESARI*, in Marathi** — *"one of the strongest critics of British rule."* **Lala Lajpat Rai** was also *"an active member of the **Arya Samaj**"* (Ch.7 link).
>
> 🎯 **The philosophical difference in one line:** the Moderates believed **the British could be persuaded**; the Radicals believed **only Indian strength counted**. That is not a difference of temperament — **it is a different theory of how power works.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Partition of Bengal, 1905, and Swadeshi

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Who and when** | **Viceroy CURZON partitioned Bengal in 1905** |
| **Bengal then** | *"The **BIGGEST PROVINCE** of British India, including **BIHAR and parts of ORISSA**"* |
| **The official reason** | *"Reasons of **ADMINISTRATIVE CONVENIENCE**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **NCERT's questioning of it** | *"But what did 'administrative convenience' mean? **Whose 'convenience' did it represent?** Clearly, it was **closely tied to the interests of BRITISH OFFICIALS AND BUSINESSMEN**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The giveaway** | *"**Even so, INSTEAD OF REMOVING THE NON-BENGALI AREAS from the province, the government SEPARATED EAST BENGAL AND MERGED IT WITH ASSAM.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The real motives** | *"Perhaps the main British motives were to **CURTAIL THE INFLUENCE OF BENGALI POLITICIANS** and to **SPLIT THE BENGALI PEOPLE**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The logical demolition in that "even so" sentence is worth reproducing in an answer.** If the problem were genuinely that Bengal was too big and contained non-Bengali areas, **the obvious remedy was to detach Bihar and Orissa.** Instead the government **split the Bengali-speaking population itself** and attached half of it to Assam. **The stated reason cannot explain the action taken.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The Swadeshi movement

> *"The partition of Bengal **INFURIATED PEOPLE ALL OVER INDIA**. **ALL sections of the Congress — the MODERATES and the RADICALS, as they may be called — OPPOSED IT.** Large **public meetings and demonstrations** were organised and ⭐ **NOVEL METHODS OF MASS PROTEST developed.**"*

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Its name** | The ⭐ **SWADESHI MOVEMENT**, *"strongest in Bengal but with echoes elsewhere too"* |
| ⭐⭐ **In Andhra** | *"In **deltaic ANDHRA** it was known as the ⭐ **VANDEMATARAM MOVEMENT**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **What it sought** | *"To **OPPOSE BRITISH RULE** and encourage the ideas of ⭐ **SELF-HELP, SWADESHI ENTERPRISE, NATIONAL EDUCATION, and USE OF INDIAN LANGUAGES**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The radicals' method** | *"To fight for swaraj, the radicals advocated ⭐ **MASS MOBILISATION and BOYCOTT of British institutions and goods**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **And a new current** | *"**Some individuals also began to suggest that ⭐ 'REVOLUTIONARY VIOLENCE' would be necessary to overthrow British rule**"* |

> ⭐ ***Revolutionary violence*** = *"the use of violence to make a radical change within society."*
>
> 🎯 **Swadeshi is the hinge of the movement's history.** It is where nationalism moves **from petitions to programmes** — not just demanding, but **building** (swadeshi enterprise, national education) and **withholding** (boycott). **Every later mass movement, including Gandhi's, uses this template.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ 1906–1916: League, Split and Pact

```
   *** 1906 -- THE ALL INDIA MUSLIM LEAGUE ***
   "A group of MUSLIM LANDLORDS AND NAWABS formed the All India
    Muslim League at *** DACCA in 1906. ***"
     - "The League *** SUPPORTED the partition of Bengal ***"
     - "It desired *** SEPARATE ELECTORATES for Muslims ***"
                            |
                            v
   *** 1909 -- SEPARATE ELECTORATES CONCEDED ***
   "a demand conceded by the government in 1909. Some seats in the
    councils were now RESERVED FOR MUSLIMS who would be
    *** ELECTED BY MUSLIM VOTERS. ***"
                            |
                            v
   *** THE CONSEQUENCE NCERT FLAGS: ***
   "This *** TEMPTED POLITICIANS TO GATHER A FOLLOWING BY
    DISTRIBUTING FAVOURS TO THEIR OWN RELIGIOUS GROUPS. ***"
                            |
                            v
   *** 1907 -- THE CONGRESS SPLITS ***
   "The Moderates were OPPOSED TO THE USE OF BOYCOTT. They felt
    that it involved the use of force."
   "After the split, the Congress came to be DOMINATED BY THE
    MODERATES with *** TILAK'S FOLLOWERS FUNCTIONING FROM
    OUTSIDE. ***"
                            |
                            v
   *** DECEMBER 1915 -- THE TWO GROUPS REUNITED ***
                            |
                            v
   *** 1916 -- THE LUCKNOW PACT ***
   "the Congress and the Muslim League signed the historic Lucknow
    Pact and decided to *** WORK TOGETHER FOR REPRESENTATIVE
    GOVERNMENT *** in the country."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The 1909 separate electorates deserve careful attention, because NCERT's comment is analytically precise.**
>
> Separate electorates meant a candidate in a reserved seat needed **only Muslim votes** — so there was **no electoral incentive to appeal across communities**, and every incentive to demonstrate loyalty to one's own. NCERT's phrasing — *"tempted politicians to gather a following by distributing favours to their own religious groups"* — describes **an institutional design shaping political behaviour.**
>
> 💻 **The constituency boundaries determined the strategy.** Change who can vote for you and you change who you must please. **Communal politics was not merely a sentiment; it was rewarded by the electoral architecture.**
>
> 🎯 **This is essential background for the Partition section at the end of the chapter, and for any Mains answer on the growth of communalism.**

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Growth of Mass Nationalism, and the First World War

> *"**After 1919**, the struggle against British rule gradually became a ⭐ **MASS MOVEMENT**, involving ⭐ **peasants, tribals, students and women in large numbers and occasionally factory workers as well**. ⭐ **Certain BUSINESS GROUPS too began to actively support the Congress in the 1920s.** Why was this so?"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The impact of the First World War — five effects

```
   [1] *** TAXES AND PRICES ***
       "It led to a HUGE RISE IN THE DEFENCE EXPENDITURE of the
        Government of India. The government in turn INCREASED TAXES
        ON INDIVIDUAL INCOMES AND BUSINESS PROFITS."
       "Increased military expenditure and the demands for war
        supplies led to a *** SHARP RISE IN PRICES *** which created
        GREAT DIFFICULTIES FOR THE COMMON PEOPLE."

   [2] *** BUSINESS PROFITED ***
       "On the other hand, BUSINESS GROUPS REAPED FABULOUS PROFITS
        from the war."
       "The war created a DEMAND FOR INDUSTRIAL GOODS (jute bags,
        cloth, rails) and caused a DECLINE OF IMPORTS from other
        countries. So *** INDIAN INDUSTRIES EXPANDED *** during the
        war, and Indian business groups began to DEMAND GREATER
        OPPORTUNITIES for development."

   [3] *** FORCED RECRUITMENT ***
       "The war led the British to EXPAND THEIR ARMY.
        *** VILLAGES WERE PRESSURISED TO SUPPLY SOLDIERS FOR AN
        ALIEN CAUSE. ***"

   [4] *** RETURNING SOLDIERS ***
       "Many returned after the war with an *** UNDERSTANDING OF THE
        WAYS IN WHICH IMPERIALIST POWERS WERE EXPLOITING THE PEOPLES
        OF ASIA AND AFRICA *** and with a DESIRE TO OPPOSE COLONIAL
        RULE IN INDIA."

   [5] *** THE RUSSIAN REVOLUTION, 1917 ***
       "News about PEASANTS' AND WORKERS' STRUGGLES and *** IDEAS OF
        SOCIALISM *** circulated widely, INSPIRING INDIAN NATIONALISTS."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **This section answers the crucial question of why the movement became a MASS movement after 1919 — and the answer is that the war created grievances and capacities simultaneously.**
>
> **Prices hit the poor; profits enriched Indian business (giving the Congress a funding base); forced recruitment angered villages; returning soldiers brought anti-imperial consciousness; and the Russian Revolution supplied a vocabulary of mass struggle.** **Every social group acquired a reason to join at the same moment.**
>
> 🎯 **Cite all five in a Mains answer on the transformation of the national movement.**

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Advent of Mahatma Gandhi

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Arrival** | *"Gandhiji, **aged 46**, arrived in India in ⭐ **1915 from SOUTH AFRICA**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **His standing** | *"Having **led Indians in that country in NON-VIOLENT MARCHES against RACIST RESTRICTIONS**, he was already a **respected leader, KNOWN INTERNATIONALLY**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **What South Africa gave him** | *"His South African campaigns had brought him **in contact with VARIOUS TYPES OF INDIANS: Hindus, Muslims, Parsis and Christians; Gujaratis, Tamils and north Indians; and upper-class merchants, lawyers and WORKERS**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **His first year** | *"Spent his **first year in India TRAVELLING THROUGHOUT THE COUNTRY, UNDERSTANDING THE PEOPLE, their needs and the overall situation**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **First interventions** | ⭐ **CHAMPARAN, KHEDA and AHMEDABAD** — *"where he came into contact with **RAJENDRA PRASAD and VALLABHBHAI PATEL**"*. ⭐ *"In Ahmedabad, he led a **successful MILLWORKERS' STRIKE in 1918**"* |

> ⭐⭐ **The South Africa point explains Gandhi's later effectiveness better than anything else.** Before he ever led an Indian campaign, he had already organised **across religion, region and class** — the exact coalition Indian nationalism needed and had not achieved. **He arrived with the skill already learned.**
>
> ⭐ **Also note the 1895 Natal Congress**, which he founded with other Indians in Durban **to fight racial discrimination**.
>
> 🔗 **CHAMPARAN is Chapter 3's indigo story** — the planters had moved from Bengal to Bihar, and **a peasant from Bihar persuaded Gandhi to come.** Cross-reference it.

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐ The Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh, 1919

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The Act** | *"The **ROWLATT ACT** that the British had just passed... ⭐ **curbed FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS such as the FREEDOM OF EXPRESSION and STRENGTHENED POLICE POWERS**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **Who objected** | ⭐ *"**Mahatma Gandhi, MOHAMMAD ALI JINNAH and others** felt that the government had **no right to restrict people's basic freedoms**. They criticised the Act as ⭐ **'DEVILISH' and TYRANNICAL**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The call** | *"Gandhiji asked the Indian people to observe ⭐ **6 APRIL 1919** as a day of **non-violent opposition**, as a day of ⭐ **'HUMILIATION AND PRAYER' and HARTAL (strike)**. **SATYAGRAHA SABHAS** were set up"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Its significance** | *"The Rowlatt Satyagraha turned out to be ⭐ **THE FIRST ALL-INDIA STRUGGLE against the British government** — although it was **largely restricted to CITIES**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Jallianwala Bagh** | *"The **JALLIANWALA BAGH atrocities, inflicted by ⭐ GENERAL DYER in AMRITSAR on ⭐ BAISAKHI DAY (13 APRIL)**, were a part of this repression"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Tagore's response** | *"On learning about the massacre, ⭐ **RABINDRANATH TAGORE expressed the pain and anger of the country by RENOUNCING HIS KNIGHTHOOD**"* |

> ⭐ ***Knighthood*** = *"an honour granted by the British Crown for exceptional personal achievement or public service."*
>
> ⭐⭐ **Note that Jinnah opposed the Rowlatt Act alongside Gandhi.** In 1919 they were on the same side — a fact worth carrying into the Partition section, where NCERT notes Jinnah was *"an ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity until 1920."* **The eventual division was not inevitable from the start.**
>
> ⭐⭐ **And note the Hindu-Muslim unity theme:** *"During the Rowlatt Satyagraha, the participants tried to ensure that **Hindus and Muslims were UNITED in the fight**. This was also the call of Mahatma Gandhi, **who always saw India as a land of ALL the people who lived in the country**."*

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Khilafat and Non-Cooperation, 1920–22

### The Khilafat cause

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The issue** | *"In **1920**, the British imposed a **HARSH TREATY on the TURKISH SULTAN or KHALIFA**"* |
| ⭐ **The feeling** | *"People were **furious about this as they had been about the Jallianwala massacre**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **The specific concern** | *"Indian Muslims were keen that the **Khalifa be allowed to RETAIN CONTROL OVER MUSLIM SACRED PLACES in the erstwhile OTTOMAN EMPIRE**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The leaders** | ⭐ **MOHAMMAD ALI and SHAUKAT ALI** — *"now wished to initiate a **full-fledged NON-COOPERATION MOVEMENT**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Gandhi's three demands** | *"Gandhiji **supported their call** and urged the Congress to campaign against ⭐ **(1) 'PUNJAB WRONGS' (the Jallianwala massacre), (2) the KHILAFAT WRONG, and (3) demand SWARAJ**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The three-part demand is asked directly, and the political construction is worth admiring:** Gandhi combined **a Punjabi/Hindu-Sikh grievance, a pan-Islamic grievance, and a universal national demand** into a single platform. **That is how a mass movement across communities was built.**

### ⭐⭐ What Non-Cooperation looked like

| Action |
|---|
| ⭐ *"**Thousands of STUDENTS left government-controlled schools and colleges**"* |
| ⭐⭐ *"Many **LAWYERS** such as **MOTILAL NEHRU, C. R. DAS, C. RAJAGOPALACHARI and ASAF ALI gave up their practices**"* |
| ⭐ *"**British TITLES were surrendered** and **LEGISLATURES BOYCOTTED**"* |
| ⭐⭐ *"People lit **PUBLIC BONFIRES OF FOREIGN CLOTH**. The **imports of foreign cloth FELL DRASTICALLY between 1920 and 1922**"* |

> ⭐ *"But all this was merely **THE TIP OF THE ICEBERG**. **Large parts of the country were on the brink of a FORMIDABLE REVOLT.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ People's initiatives — the crucial section

> *"In many cases, people resisted British rule non-violently. In others, ⭐⭐ **different classes and groups, INTERPRETING GANDHIJI'S CALL IN THEIR OWN MANNER, protested in ways that were NOT IN ACCORDANCE WITH HIS IDEAS.** In either case, ⭐ **people LINKED THEIR MOVEMENTS TO LOCAL GRIEVANCES.**"*

**⭐⭐⭐ The regional forms — a favourite Mains question ("discuss the various forms the Non-Cooperation Movement took in different parts of India"):**

| Region | Form it took |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **KHEDA, Gujarat** | **PATIDAR peasants** organised **non-violent campaigns against the high land revenue demand** |
| ⭐ **Coastal ANDHRA and interior TAMIL NADU** | **LIQUOR SHOPS were PICKETED** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **GUNTUR district, Andhra** | **Tribals and poor peasants staged ⭐ "FOREST SATYAGRAHAS"**, *"sometimes **sending their cattle into forests without paying grazing fee**"* — protesting the colonial restriction of forest use. ⭐ *"They believed that **Gandhiji would get their taxes reduced and have the forest regulations abolished**"*; in many forest villages **peasants proclaimed swaraj** and believed ⭐ **"GANDHI RAJ" was about to be established** |
| ⭐⭐ **SIND** | **Muslim traders and peasants** were **very enthusiastic about the Khilafat call** |
| ⭐⭐ **BENGAL** | *"The **Khilafat–Non-Cooperation alliance gave ENORMOUS COMMUNAL UNITY AND STRENGTH** to the national movement"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **PUNJAB** | The ⭐ **AKALI AGITATION of the Sikhs** sought to **remove corrupt *MAHANTS* — supported by the British — from their GURDWARAS**. *"This movement got **closely identified with the Non-Cooperation Movement**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **ASSAM** | ⭐ **TEA GARDEN LABOURERS**, shouting ⭐ **"GANDHI MAHARAJ KI JAI"**, **demanded a big increase in their wages** and **left the British-owned plantations** declaring they were following Gandhiji's wish. ⭐ *"Interestingly, in the **Assamese VAISHNAVA SONGS of the period, the reference to KRISHNA was substituted by 'GANDHI RAJA'**"* |

> ⭐ ***Mahants*** = *"religious functionaries of Sikh gurdwaras."* ⭐ ***Picket*** = *"people protesting outside a building or shop to prevent others from entering."*

### ⭐⭐⭐ "The people's Mahatma"

> *"Sometimes people thought of Gandhiji as **a kind of MESSIAH, as someone who could help them overcome their misery and poverty**. ⭐⭐ **Gandhiji wished to build CLASS UNITY, NOT CLASS CONFLICT, yet PEASANTS could imagine that he would help them in their fight against ZAMINDARS, and AGRICULTURAL LABOURERS believed he would PROVIDE THEM LAND.**"*
>
> ⭐ *"At times, **ordinary people CREDITED GANDHIJI WITH THEIR OWN ACHIEVEMENTS.**"* — the peasants of **PRATAPGARH** in the United Provinces *"managed to **stop ILLEGAL EVICTION of tenants**; but they **felt it was GANDHIJI who had won this demand for them**."*
>
> ⭐ *"At other times, **USING GANDHIJI'S NAME, tribals and peasants undertook actions that DID NOT CONFORM TO GANDHIAN IDEALS**."*

**⭐⭐⭐ And the CID report from Allahabad, January 1921** — one of the best sources in the book:

> *"**The CURRENCY which Mr. Gandhi's name has acquired even in the remotest villages is ASTONISHING. NO ONE SEEMS TO KNOW QUITE WHO OR WHAT HE IS, but it is an accepted fact that WHAT HE SAYS IS SO, AND WHAT HE ORDERS MUST BE DONE.** He is a Mahatma or sadhu, a Pundit, a Brahmin who lives at Allahabad, **even a DEOTA**... the real power of his name is to be traced back to the idea that **it was he who got *bedakhli* [illegal eviction] stopped in Pratapgarh**... ⭐⭐ **as a general rule, GANDHI IS NOT THOUGHT OF AS BEING ANTAGONISTIC TO GOVERNMENT, BUT ONLY TO THE ZAMINDARS... 'WE ARE FOR GANDHIJI AND THE SARKAR.'**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's activity asks exactly the right question — why did people think Gandhi was opposed to zamindars but not to the government? — and the answer is genuinely illuminating.**
>
> **People fitted Gandhi into the grievance they actually had.** Their oppressor was the **zamindar** they saw every day, not the distant Sarkar. So they read him as **an ally against the landlord**, and there was no contradiction in being *"for Gandhiji and the Sarkar."*
>
> ⭐⭐ **The deeper point for a Mains answer:** a mass movement's leader **cannot control what his name is made to mean.** Gandhi's authority in the villages rested on **a version of Gandhi that the villagers had constructed** — and that gap between the leader's programme and the people's interpretation is what makes mass movements powerful **and uncontrollable**. **Chauri Chaura is that gap becoming visible.**

## 1️⃣2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ 1922–1929

### ⭐⭐⭐ Chauri Chaura

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **When and what** | *"He **abruptly CALLED OFF the Non-Cooperation Movement** when in ⭐ **FEBRUARY 1922**, a **crowd of peasants SET FIRE TO A POLICE STATION at CHAURI CHAURA**. ⭐ **TWENTY-TWO POLICEMEN were killed** on that day"* |
| ⭐⭐ **The provocation** | ⭐ *"The peasants were **provoked because THE POLICE HAD FIRED ON THEIR PEACEFUL DEMONSTRATION**"* |
| **Why he stopped** | *"Mahatma Gandhi was **against violent movements**"* |

> ⭐⭐ **NCERT includes the provocation, and you should too.** The crowd was not spontaneously violent — **the police had fired on a peaceful demonstration first.** Gandhi withdrew the movement anyway, which is precisely what made the decision so controversial among his own colleagues. **State both facts and the decision becomes genuinely debatable rather than simply noble.**

### The two paths after 1922

| Path | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Constructive work** | **Gandhiji's followers** | *"The Congress must undertake **CONSTRUCTIVE WORK IN THE RURAL AREAS**"* — ⭐ *"Through sincere social work in villages in the **mid-1920s**, the Gandhians were able to **EXTEND THEIR SUPPORT BASE. This proved to be VERY USEFUL IN LAUNCHING THE CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE MOVEMENT IN 1930.**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **Council entry** | ⭐ **CHITTA RANJAN DAS and MOTILAL NEHRU** | *"The party should **FIGHT ELECTIONS TO THE COUNCILS and ENTER THEM in order to INFLUENCE GOVERNMENT POLICIES**"* |

> 🎯 **The link NCERT draws is important:** the quiet village work of the mid-1920s **built the base that made 1930 possible.** Movements are not spontaneous; **the interval between them is when the organisation is built.**

### ⭐⭐ Other developments of the mid-1920s

> *"Two important developments of the mid-1920s were the formation of the ⭐ **RASHTRIYA SWAYAMSEVAK SANGH (RSS)**, a Hindu organisation, and the ⭐ **COMMUNIST PARTY OF INDIA**. **These parties have held very different ideas about the kind of country India should be.** ⭐ The revolutionary nationalist **BHAGAT SINGH** too was active in this period."*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The revolutionaries

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Who** | **BHAGAT SINGH, CHANDRA SHEKHAR AZAD, SUKHDEV** and others |
| ⭐⭐ **Their aim** | *"To fight against **the colonial rule AND the rich exploiting classes** through a **REVOLUTION OF WORKERS AND PEASANTS**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **What they founded** | The ⭐ **HINDUSTAN SOCIALIST REPUBLICAN ASSOCIATION (HSRA)** in ⭐ **1928 at FEROZESHAH KOTLA, DELHI** |
| ⭐⭐ **17 December 1928** | **Bhagat Singh, Azad and Rajguru assassinated SAUNDERS**, *"a police officer **involved in the lathi-charge that had caused the death of LALA LAJPAT RAI**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **8 April 1929** | **Bhagat Singh and B. K. DUTT threw a bomb in the CENTRAL LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY.** ⭐ *"The aim, as their leaflet explained, was **NOT TO KILL but 'TO MAKE THE DEAF HEAR'**, and to remind the foreign government of its **callous exploitation**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **23 March 1931** | **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were EXECUTED.** ⭐ *"Bhagat Singh's age at that time was only **23**"* |
| ⭐ **The slogan** | ⭐ **"INQUILAB ZINDABAD!"** |

> ⭐⭐ **Note the two-part aim: against colonial rule AND the rich exploiting classes.** The HSRA was **socialist**, not merely nationalist — the "S" in its name matters. **And the bomb was explicitly designed not to kill** but to force attention. That intent is quoted from their own leaflet and is frequently asked.

### ⭐⭐ Simon and Purna Swaraj

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1927** | *"The British government decided to send a commission headed by ⭐ **LORD SIMON** to decide India's political future. ⭐ **The Commission had NO INDIAN REPRESENTATIVE.** The decision created an **outrage in India. ALL political groups decided to BOYCOTT the Commission.** When it arrived, it was met with demonstrations with banners saying ⭐ **'SIMON GO BACK'**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1929** | *"The decade closed with the Congress resolving to fight for ⭐ **PURNA SWARAJ (complete independence)** in **1929** under the presidentship of ⭐ **JAWAHARLAL NEHRU**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **26 January 1930** | *"Consequently, ⭐ **'INDEPENDENCE DAY' was observed on 26 JANUARY 1930 all over the country**"* |

> 🔗 **26 January was chosen as Republic Day in 1950 precisely to honour this date.** A neat Polity crossover.

## 1️⃣3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The March to Dandi, 1930

> *"**Purna Swaraj would never come on its own. IT HAD TO BE FOUGHT FOR.**"*

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **The law** | *"According to this law, **the state had a MONOPOLY on the MANUFACTURE AND SALE OF SALT**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **The argument** | *"Gandhi and other nationalists reasoned that **it was SINFUL TO TAX SALT since it is such an ESSENTIAL ITEM OF OUR FOOD**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Why salt was chosen** | ⭐ *"The Salt March **RELATED THE GENERAL DESIRE OF FREEDOM TO A SPECIFIC GRIEVANCE SHARED BY EVERYBODY**, and thus ⭐ **DID NOT DIVIDE THE RICH AND THE POOR**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The march** | *"Gandhiji and his followers **marched for over ⭐ 240 MILES from SABARMATI to the coastal town of ⭐ DANDI**, where they **broke the government law by GATHERING NATURAL SALT found on the seashore, and BOILING SEA WATER to produce salt**"* |
| ⭐⭐ **The date** | ⭐ **6 April 1930** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's own question — "why did Gandhiji choose to break the salt law?" — is answered in that one sentence, and it is the best short statement of Gandhi's political genius:**
>
> **Salt was universal.** Every Indian used it; the poorest were hit hardest by the tax; and **no class or community could object to the cause.** A campaign about land revenue would have divided peasant from landlord; a campaign about legislative seats would have excluded the illiterate. **Salt included everyone.**
>
> 🔗 **Recall Class 8 Ch.5's Khurda case study** — the **salt monopoly** deprived Orissa's traditional salt makers and raised prices, and was one of the grievances behind the **1817 uprising.** The salt grievance was a **century old** when Gandhi picked it up. **And the Moderates had passed resolutions on the salt tax in the 1880s.** Gandhi did not invent the issue — **he found the one everybody already agreed on.**

**⭐⭐ The response:**

> *"**Peasants, tribals and women participated in large numbers.** ⭐ **A BUSINESS FEDERATION published a pamphlet on the salt issue.** The government tried to **crush the movement through BRUTAL ACTION against peaceful satyagrahis. THOUSANDS were sent to jail.**"*

## 1️⃣4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Women in the Freedom Struggle

> *"Women from **diverse backgrounds** participated. **Young and old, single and married, they came from RURAL AND URBAN areas, from both CONSERVATIVE AND LIBERAL homes.** ⭐ **Both British officials AND Indian nationalists felt that women's participation gave the national struggle an IMMENSE FORCE.**"*

**⭐⭐ What participation meant for women:**

> *"Participation in the freedom movement ⭐ **BROUGHT WOMEN OUT OF THEIR HOMES. It gave them a place in the PROFESSIONS, in the GOVERNANCE OF INDIA, and it could PAVE THE WAY FOR EQUALITY WITH MEN.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐ Ambabai of Karnataka — the case study

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Her life** | ⭐ **Married at TWELVE**, ⭐ **widowed at SIXTEEN** |
| **Her activity** | ⭐ **Picketed foreign cloth and liquor shops in UDIPI** |
| **The consequences** | ⭐ **Arrested, served a sentence, and was REARRESTED** |
| **Between prison terms** | *"She **made speeches, taught SPINNING, and organised ⭐ PRABHAT PHERIS**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Her own verdict** | *"Ambabai regarded these as ⭐ **THE HAPPIEST DAYS OF HER LIFE because they gave it a NEW PURPOSE AND COMMITMENT**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Ambabai is the single best example in the book for a GS1 answer on women and the national movement, and it must be told with the biography attached.**
>
> A girl **married at twelve and widowed at sixteen** — condemned by the customs of Chapter 7 to a life of invisibility — found in the national movement **a public role, a purpose, and by her own account the happiest days of her life.** **The freedom struggle was, for many women, personal liberation before it was national liberation.**

### ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐ And the resistance they faced from their own side

> *"**Women, however, HAD TO FIGHT FOR THEIR RIGHT TO PARTICIPATE IN THE MOVEMENT.** ⭐⭐ **During the Salt Satyagraha, for instance, EVEN MAHATMA GANDHI WAS INITIALLY OPPOSED TO WOMEN'S PARTICIPATION. ⭐ SAROJINI NAIDU HAD TO PERSUADE HIM to allow women to join the movement.**"*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT states this plainly and so should you.** It is a fact that complicates the standard hagiography and **strengthens** an answer rather than weakening it. **Women's participation was not granted; it was won — and won against the leadership of the movement itself.**
>
> ⭐ **Sarojini Naidu:** active since the early 1920s, *"a significant leader of the Dandi March"*, and ⭐ **the FIRST INDIAN WOMAN to become PRESIDENT OF THE INDIAN NATIONAL CONGRESS (1925).**
>
> 🔗 **This is the same deferral pattern as Chapter 7** — women told to wait, told to concentrate on the national struggle. **Here they simply refused to wait.**

## 1️⃣5️⃣ ⭐⭐ 1935–1939: Provincial Autonomy and the War

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1935** | *"The combined struggles of the Indian people bore fruit when the ⭐ **GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT OF 1935 prescribed PROVINCIAL AUTONOMY**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1937** | *"The government announced **elections to the PROVINCIAL LEGISLATURES in 1937**. ⭐ **The Congress formed governments in 7 OUT OF 11 PROVINCES**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **September 1939** | *"After **two years of Congress rule in the provinces, the SECOND WORLD WAR broke out**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The Congress position** | ⭐ *"**Critical of HITLER, Congress leaders were READY TO SUPPORT THE BRITISH WAR EFFORT. BUT IN RETURN THEY WANTED THAT INDIA BE GRANTED INDEPENDENCE AFTER THE WAR. The British REFUSED to concede the demand. THE CONGRESS MINISTRIES RESIGNED IN PROTEST.**"* |

> ⭐ ***Provincial autonomy*** = *"capacity of the provinces to make relatively independent decisions while remaining within a federation."*
>
> 🎯 **The 1939 offer is important and often forgotten: the Congress was willing to fight Hitler in exchange for a promise of independence.** The British refusal is what produced the resignations, and ultimately Quit India. **It also created the vacuum in which the Muslim League grew** — see below.

## 1️⃣6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Quit India, 1942

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **When and why** | *"Mahatma Gandhi decided to initiate a **new phase of movement against the British IN THE MIDDLE OF THE SECOND WORLD WAR**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The demand** | *"**The British must QUIT INDIA IMMEDIATELY**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The call to the people** | ⭐ **"DO OR DIE" in your effort to fight the British — ⭐ "BUT YOU MUST FIGHT NON-VIOLENTLY"** |
| ⭐⭐ **The leaders** | *"**Gandhiji and other leaders were JAILED AT ONCE — BUT THE MOVEMENT SPREAD**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Who joined** | ⭐ *"It **specially attracted PEASANTS and the YOUTH who GAVE UP THEIR STUDIES to join it**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **What happened** | ⭐ *"**COMMUNICATIONS AND SYMBOLS OF STATE AUTHORITY were ATTACKED all over the country. IN MANY AREAS THE PEOPLE SET UP THEIR OWN GOVERNMENTS.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The repression** | *"The first response of the British was **SEVERE REPRESSION. By the end of 1943, ⭐ OVER 90,000 PEOPLE WERE ARRESTED, and around ⭐ 1,000 KILLED in police firing. ⭐ In many areas, ORDERS WERE GIVEN TO MACHINE-GUN CROWDS FROM AIRPLANES.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The verdict** | ⭐ **"THE REBELLION, HOWEVER, ULTIMATELY BROUGHT THE RAJ TO ITS KNEES."** |

**⭐⭐ A regional voice — Orissa, from Baji Mohammed, President of the Nabrangpur Congress:**

> *"On **25 August 1942**... **nineteen people died on the spot in police firing at PAPARANDI in NABARANGPUR**. Many died thereafter from their wounds. **Over 300 were injured. More than a thousand were jailed in Koraput district.** Several were shot or executed. ⭐ **VEER LAKHAN NAYAK** (a legendary tribal leader who defied the British) **was hanged.**"*
>
> ⭐ *"Nayak was **not worried about being executed, only sad that he would not live to see freedom's dawn.**"* **Baji Mohammad** himself *"mobilised **20,000 people** to join the national struggle... and served long jail terms."*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **"In many areas the people set up their own governments" is the most significant sentence about Quit India.** It was not merely a protest — in places it was **a collapse of British authority and its replacement by parallel government** (Satara, Ballia, Tamluk). **That is why NCERT says it brought the Raj to its knees**, despite being crushed militarily.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Bose and the INA

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Who** | **SUBHAS CHANDRA BOSE** — *"a **RADICAL NATIONALIST, with SOCIALIST LEANINGS**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **His difference with Gandhi** | ⭐ *"Bose **DID NOT SHARE GANDHIJI'S IDEAL OF AHIMSA**, though he **RESPECTED HIM AS THE 'FATHER OF THE NATION'**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **His escape** | *"In ⭐ **JANUARY 1941**, he **secretly left his CALCUTTA home**, went to **SINGAPORE, VIA GERMANY**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **What he raised** | The ⭐ **AZAD HIND FAUJ or INDIAN NATIONAL ARMY (INA)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The campaign** | *"To free India from British control, in ⭐ **1944** the INA **tried to ENTER INDIA through ⭐ IMPHAL and KOHIMA — but the campaign FAILED**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The trials** | *"The INA members were **imprisoned and tried**. ⭐ **People across the country, FROM ALL WALKS OF LIFE, participated in the movement AGAINST THE INA TRIALS.**"* |

> ⭐⭐ **The INA trials matter more than the INA campaign.** The military effort failed at Imphal and Kohima — but **the trials at the Red Fort produced nationwide agitation across every community**, and shook British confidence in the loyalty of the Indian army. **That, not the fighting, is the INA's historical significance.**

## 1️⃣7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Towards Independence and Partition

### ⭐⭐⭐ Why the League moved to a separate demand — five reasons

```
   *** 1940 -- THE MUSLIM LEAGUE RESOLUTION ***
   "the Muslim League had moved a resolution demanding
    *** 'INDEPENDENT STATES' for Muslims in the NORTH-WESTERN and
        EASTERN areas of the country. ***
    *** THE RESOLUTION DID NOT MENTION PARTITION OR PAKISTAN. ***"

   WHY DID THE LEAGUE ASK FOR THIS? -- NCERT's reasons:

   [1] "From the LATE 1930s, the League began viewing the Muslims
        as a *** SEPARATE 'NATION' *** from the Hindus."

   [2] "it may have been influenced by the HISTORY OF TENSION between
        some Hindu and Muslim groups in the *** 1920s and 1930s. ***"

   [3] *** MORE IMPORTANTLY -- THE 1937 ELECTIONS ***
       "the provincial elections of 1937 seemed to have CONVINCED THE
        LEAGUE THAT *** MUSLIMS WERE A MINORITY, and they would ALWAYS
        HAVE TO PLAY SECOND FIDDLE IN ANY DEMOCRATIC STRUCTURE. ***
        It feared that *** MUSLIMS MAY EVEN GO UNREPRESENTED. ***"

   [4] "The Congress's *** REJECTION of the League's desire to form a
        JOINT CONGRESS-LEAGUE GOVERNMENT IN THE UNITED PROVINCES
        in 1937 *** also ANNOYED the League."

   [5] *** "The Congress's FAILURE TO MOBILISE THE MUSLIM MASSES in
       the 1930s ALLOWED THE LEAGUE TO WIDEN ITS SOCIAL SUPPORT." ***
       "It sought to enlarge its support in the early 1940s
        *** WHEN MOST CONGRESS LEADERS WERE IN JAIL. ***"
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **This is NCERT's answer to "discuss those developments of the 1937–47 period that led to the creation of Pakistan", and it is notable for how much responsibility it assigns to Congress decisions.**
>
> **Reasons [4] and [5] are Congress failures, stated plainly:** refusing a coalition in the UP, and failing to mobilise Muslim masses. **Reason [3] is structural** — under a democratic franchise a permanent minority fears permanent subordination.
>
> ⭐ **And note [5]'s timing:** the League grew **precisely when Congress leaders were in jail after Quit India.** The 1942 movement, which brought the Raj to its knees, also **left the field open** to the League for three critical years. **That is a genuinely difficult and important point, and it makes a strong Mains answer.**

### The final sequence

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **End of 1945** | *"The British **opened negotiations between the Congress, the League and themselves** for the independence of India. ⭐ **The talks FAILED because the LEAGUE SAW ITSELF AS THE SOLE SPOKESPERSON OF INDIA'S MUSLIMS. The Congress could not accept this claim since A LARGE NUMBER OF MUSLIMS STILL SUPPORTED IT.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1946 elections** | *"The **Congress did well in the 'GENERAL' constituencies** but ⭐ **the League's success in the SEATS RESERVED FOR MUSLIMS was SPECTACULAR. It persisted with its demand for 'Pakistan'.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **March 1946** | *"The British cabinet sent a ⭐ **THREE-MEMBER MISSION to DELHI** to examine this demand... ⭐ **This mission suggested that INDIA SHOULD REMAIN UNITED and constitute itself as a LOOSE CONFEDERATION with SOME AUTONOMY FOR MUSLIM-MAJORITY AREAS. But it COULD NOT GET THE CONGRESS AND THE MUSLIM LEAGUE TO AGREE to specific details.** ⭐ **PARTITION NOW BECAME MORE OR LESS INEVITABLE.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **16 August 1946** | *"After the failure of the Cabinet Mission, the Muslim League decided on **mass agitation** for winning its Pakistan demand. It announced ⭐ **16 AUGUST 1946 as 'DIRECT ACTION DAY'. On this day RIOTS BROKE OUT IN CALCUTTA, lasting several days and resulting in the death of THOUSANDS of people.**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **March 1947** | *"**Violence spread to different parts of NORTHERN INDIA.**"* |

> ⭐ ***"General" constituencies*** = *"election districts with **no reservations** for any religious or other community."*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The human cost

> *"**Many HUNDRED THOUSAND people were KILLED and NUMEROUS WOMEN had to face UNTOLD BRUTALITIES during the Partition. MILLIONS of people were FORCED TO FLEE THEIR HOMES. Torn asunder from their homelands, they were REDUCED TO BEING REFUGEES IN ALIEN LANDS.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ *"Partition also meant that **India changed, many of its cities changed, and a new country — Pakistan — was born.** ⭐⭐ **SO, THE JOY OF OUR COUNTRY'S INDEPENDENCE FROM BRITISH RULE CAME MIXED WITH THE PAIN AND VIOLENCE OF PARTITION.**"*

### ⭐⭐ The leaders NCERT profiles — know these

| Leader | Key detail |
|---|---|

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Two of these profiles carry real analytical weight.**
>
> **Jinnah "an ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity until 1920"** — the architect of Pakistan began as the architect of the **Lucknow Pact**. NCERT is refusing a caricature, and so should you. **Positions changed; they were not fixed from the start.**
>
> **Badshah Khan opposed Partition and criticised his own Congress colleagues for accepting it** — a Muslim Pashtun leader of a **non-violent** mass movement who felt abandoned in 1947. **He is the single best counter-example to any claim that the demand for Pakistan represented all Indian Muslims.**

## 🔁 3-MINUTE RECAP

1. ⭐ **The founding idea:** *"India was the people of India — **all** the people irrespective of class, colour, caste, creed, language or gender"* — and therefore British control was illegitimate. The associations worked for **popular sovereignty**, *"a modern consciousness and a key feature of nationalism."*
2. **Three provocations:** the **Arms Act (1878)**, the **Vernacular Press Act (1878)**, and the **Ilbert Bill (1883)** — withdrawn under **white opposition**, exposing British racial attitudes and **deepening the desire for an all-India body**.
3. ⭐ **The Congress**, **Bombay, December 1885, 72 delegates**; **Naoroji, Tyabji, Bonnerji, Surendranath Banerji, Pherozeshah Mehta, R. C. Dutt, Subramania Iyer**, with **A. O. Hume**'s help.
4. ⭐ **The Moderates** demanded representative councils, **civil service exams in India**, separation of judiciary and executive, repeal of the Arms Act — and raised **the salt tax, indentured labour and forest dwellers**. **Indianisation** would fight racism **and reduce the drain of wealth**. Their method was **persuasion**, on the belief that the British **would accept just demands**.
5. ⭐ **The Radicals** — **Lal, Bal, Pal** — attacked the **"politics of prayers"**, demanded **self-reliance and swaraj**: *"Freedom is my birthright and I shall have it."*
6. ⭐⭐ **Curzon partitioned Bengal in 1905** — "administrative convenience" that **split the Bengali people** and merged East Bengal with **Assam**. The ⭐ **Swadeshi movement** followed: self-help, swadeshi enterprise, **national education**, **boycott**, and in Andhra the **Vandemataram Movement**.
7. **1906** the **Muslim League** at **Dacca** (supporting the partition, seeking **separate electorates**, conceded **1909** — which **tempted politicians to court their own religious groups**); **1907** the **Congress split**; **December 1915** reunion; **1916 the Lucknow Pact**.
8. ⭐⭐ **The First World War** raised taxes and prices, enriched **Indian business**, forced **village recruitment**, returned soldiers with anti-imperial ideas, and — with the **1917 Russian Revolution** — inspired mass politics. **After 1919 the movement became a mass movement.**
9. ⭐⭐ **Gandhi arrived in 1915, aged 46**, already experienced at organising **across religion, region and class** in South Africa; spent a year travelling; then **Champaran, Kheda and Ahmedabad** (**millworkers' strike, 1918**).
10. ⭐⭐ **Rowlatt Satyagraha:** hartal on **6 April 1919**, **the first all-India struggle**, largely urban. ⭐ **Jallianwala Bagh — General Dyer, Amritsar, Baisakhi, 13 April 1919** — and **Tagore renounced his knighthood**.
11. ⭐⭐ **Khilafat + Non-Cooperation:** led by **Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali**; Gandhi's three demands were **Punjab wrongs, the Khilafat wrong, and swaraj**. Students left colleges, lawyers gave up practice, titles were surrendered, **foreign cloth burnt**.
12. ⭐⭐⭐ **It took different forms everywhere:** Kheda's **Patidars**, liquor picketing in Andhra and Tamil Nadu, **forest satyagrahas in Guntur** where people proclaimed **"Gandhi Raj"**, enthusiasm in **Sind**, communal unity in **Bengal**, the **Akali agitation** in Punjab, and **Assam tea labourers** shouting **"Gandhi Maharaj ki Jai"** — with **Krishna replaced by "Gandhi Raja"** in Vaishnava songs.
13. ⭐⭐ **The people's Mahatma:** the CID reported that *"no one seems to know quite who or what he is, but... what he says is so"*, and that villagers thought him **against zamindars but not the Sarkar** — *"We are for Gandhiji and the Sarkar."*
14. ⭐⭐ **Chauri Chaura, February 1922** — **22 policemen killed** after the police **fired on a peaceful demonstration** — and Gandhi **called off the movement**. Then **constructive village work** (which built the base for **1930**) and **council entry** under **C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru**. **RSS** and the **CPI** were formed; **Bhagat Singh's HSRA (1928)** killed **Saunders** and bombed the **Assembly (1929) "to make the deaf hear"**; **executed 23 March 1931, aged 23**.
15. ⭐⭐ **Simon Commission (1927)** had **no Indian member** — **"Simon Go Back"**. **Purna Swaraj (1929)** under **Nehru**; **Independence Day, 26 January 1930**.
16. ⭐⭐⭐ **Dandi:** salt was chosen because it **related the general desire for freedom to a grievance shared by everybody and did not divide rich from poor**. **Over 240 miles, Sabarmati to Dandi, 6 April 1930.**
17. ⭐⭐ **Women:** **Ambabai**, married at **12** and widowed at **16**, picketed at **Udipi**, was jailed twice, and called these **the happiest days of her life**. ⚠️ **Participation had to be fought for — Gandhi was initially opposed, and Sarojini Naidu (first Indian woman Congress President, 1925) persuaded him.**
18. ⭐⭐ **GoI Act 1935** gave **provincial autonomy**; the Congress won **7 of 11 provinces in 1937** and **resigned in 1939** when Britain refused independence in return for war support.
19. ⭐⭐⭐ **Quit India, 1942:** **"do or die" — non-violently**. Leaders jailed at once, but **communications and symbols of authority were attacked** and ⭐ **in many areas people set up their own governments**. **Over 90,000 arrested, ~1,000 killed, crowds machine-gunned from aircraft** — yet it **"brought the Raj to its knees."**
20. ⭐⭐ **Bose** left Calcutta in **January 1941** via Germany to Singapore, raised the **Azad Hind Fauj**, failed at **Imphal and Kohima in 1944** — but the **INA trials** brought people **from all walks of life** onto the streets.
21. ⭐⭐⭐ **Partition:** the **1940 resolution** asked for **"Independent States"**, **not Pakistan**. The League's shift owed to the **1937 elections** convincing it Muslims would be a permanent minority, the **Congress's refusal of a coalition in the UP**, and the **Congress's failure to mobilise Muslim masses** — the League growing fastest **while Congress leaders were in jail**. The **Cabinet Mission (March 1946)** proposed a **united confederation** and failed; **Direct Action Day, 16 August 1946**, brought riots in **Calcutta**; by **March 1947** violence had spread across northern India.
22. ⭐⭐ **Hundreds of thousands were killed, millions displaced.** *"The joy of our country's independence from British rule came mixed with the pain and violence of Partition."*
