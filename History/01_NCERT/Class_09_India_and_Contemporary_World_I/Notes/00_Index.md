# Class IX — *India and the Contemporary World – I*

> **What this book actually is:** the first NCERT that is **not** about India. Three of its five chapters are **World History** — the French Revolution, the Russian Revolution, Nazism — and the other two are **social and environmental history** of colonialism. For UPSC this book is worth far more than its school-level packaging suggests, because **GS Paper I Mains has an explicit World History component** and this is where the ideas in it are first laid out.

---

## 🏷️ Where this book sits in the UPSC syllabus

The Mains GS Paper I syllabus says, word for word:

> *"**History of the world** will include events from **18th century** such as **industrial revolution, world wars, redrawal of national boundaries, colonization, decolonization, political philosophies like communism, capitalism, socialism etc.** — their **forms and effect on the society**."*

Read that list again and map it onto this book:

```
   UPSC SYLLABUS PHRASE          ->  CHAPTER THAT STARTS IT

   "events from 18th century"    ->  Ch.1  French Revolution (1789)
   "political philosophies like
    communism ... socialism"     ->  Ch.2  Socialism & Russian Revolution
   "world wars"                  ->  Ch.3  Nazism (WWI aftermath -> WWII)
   "redrawal of national
    boundaries"                  ->  Ch.3  Versailles, 1919
   "colonization ... effect
    on the society"              ->  Ch.4  Forest Society and Colonialism
                                     Ch.5  Pastoralists in the Modern World
```

**Every phrase of the World History syllabus has its seed in this one book.** Nothing else in the NCERT series covers the French and Russian Revolutions at all until Class 11's *Themes in World History*.

## 🧭 How to read these five chapters

**Chapters 1–3 form one continuous argument, and you lose most of the value if you read them separately.**

```
   1789  FRENCH REVOLUTION
         "The nation belongs to the PEOPLE, not the king."
         Invents: citizen, nation, rights, liberty, equality
                        |
                        |  But equality of WHAT?
                        |  Political rights only, or bread too?
                        v
   1848+ SOCIALISM
         "Political equality is empty while property is private."
         Marx: capitalists profit from workers' labour
                        |
                        |  Can this be won by voting, or only by revolution?
                        v
   1917  RUSSIAN REVOLUTION
         The first state to answer: revolution, then abolish
         private property. Land to the tillers, factories to workers.
                        |
                        |  Backlash. Plus a lost war and a broken economy.
                        v
   1933  NAZISM
         The counter-revolution: rejects liberty, equality AND
         socialism. Nation defined by BLOOD, not by consent.
         Ends in war and genocide.
```

Read across that arrow and you can answer almost any GS1 question on modern political philosophies. Read the chapters as four separate story-books and you will have four sets of dates and no argument.

**Chapters 4–5 are a different kind of history entirely** — no great men, no dates you must memorise, but the most **directly examinable** content in the book. Forest Rights, shifting cultivation, pastoral commons and grazing bans come up in **Prelims (Environment)**, **GS1 (society)** and **GS3 (environment, agriculture)** every single year.

## 🎯 The 12 things worth knowing cold from this book

1. **The three estates** and who paid the *taille* and the *tithe*.
2. **1789 → 1791 → 1792 → 1793 → 1804**: Estates-General, Constitution, Republic, Terror, Napoleon.
3. **The Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen** — and its **exclusions** (passive citizens, women, slaves).
4. **Olympe de Gouges** and the *Declaration of the Rights of Woman and the Female Citizen*.
5. **The abolition of slavery in 1794, its reintroduction by Napoleon, and final abolition in 1848.**
6. **Liberals / Radicals / Conservatives** — the three positions after 1815, and how they differ.
7. **Marx: capitalists, profit, and social ownership of the means of production.**
8. **February vs October 1917** — two different revolutions, months apart.
9. **Collectivisation, the kulaks, and the famine.**
10. **The Treaty of Versailles' terms and why they made Nazism possible.**
11. **Scientific forestry, the Forest Act, and *why* shifting cultivation was banned.**
12. **The Waste Land Rules, the Forest Acts and the Criminal Tribes Act — three laws with one logic: settle the mobile, tax the visible.**
