## 🎬 Start Here: Two Kinds of Society

```
   CASTE-BASED SOCIETY                 TRIBAL SOCIETY
   -------------------                 --------------
   Divided by rules of VARNA           Did NOT follow social rules and
   prescribed by BRAHMANAS,            rituals prescribed by Brahmanas
   accepted by rulers of
   large kingdoms                      NOT divided into numerous
                                       unequal classes
   Difference between high and
   low, rich and poor, INCREASED       United by KINSHIP BONDS

   Under the Delhi Sultans and         Land and pastures controlled
   the Mughals this hierarchy          JOINTLY by the group, divided
   GREW FURTHER                        among households by ITS OWN RULES
```

**Livelihoods:** many tribes farmed; others were **hunter-gatherers or herders**; **most often they combined these** to make full use of local resources. Some were **nomadic**.

**Where they lived:** **forests, hills, deserts and places difficult to reach.** They sometimes **clashed with the more powerful caste-based societies**, and **in various ways retained their freedom and preserved their separate culture.**

> ⭐ **But NCERT's key point is interdependence:** *"the caste-based and tribal societies also **depended on each other** for their diverse needs. This caused **both** societies to change."* **Not two sealed worlds — two worlds in constant exchange, each transformed by it.** That framing is what a good Mains answer needs.

### 📚 ⭐ Why tribal history is hard to write

| Problem | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Contemporary **historians and travellers give very scanty information** about tribes | The written record is thin and external |
| **A few exceptions apart, tribal people did not keep written records** | No internal documentary voice |
| **But they preserved rich customs and ORAL TRADITIONS**, passed to each new generation | ⭐ **Present-day historians have started using such oral traditions to write tribal histories** |

> 🎯 **This is Class 6 Chapter 1's "silence of the sources" again, and the methodological response is important.** Using oral tradition as evidence is a genuine methodological shift — it treats memory as a source rather than dismissing it as unreliable. **Quote it whenever you write about marginalised histories.**
>
> 💻 The written record has a **systematic sampling bias** — it captures literate, state-adjacent, tax-paying society. Oral tradition is the only channel carrying data from outside that sample.

> 🧠 **The Bhils are the best single illustration of the chapter's argument:** within one tribe, **some became settled farmers and even zamindars while others stayed hunter-gatherers.** Tribal society was not static or uniform — it **differentiated internally** under pressure from the expanding agrarian state.

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ Nomads and Mobile People

### Nomadic pastoralists

| What they did |
|---|
| **Moved over long distances with their animals** |
| **Lived on milk and other pastoral products** |
| **Exchanged wool, ghee, etc. with settled agriculturists** for **grain, cloth, utensils** and other products |
| **Bought and sold goods as they moved**, transporting them on their animals |

### 🐂 The Banjaras — the most important trader-nomads ⭐⭐⭐

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Their caravan was called** | ***TANDA*** ⭐ |
| **Alauddin Khalji** (Ch.3) | Used the Banjaras to **transport grain to the city markets** |
| **Jahangir** | Wrote in his memoirs that the Banjaras **carried grain on their bullocks** from different areas and **sold it in towns** |
| **Military role** | They **transported food grain for the Mughal army** during campaigns. ⭐ With a large army there could be **100,000 bullocks** carrying grain |

**⭐ Peter Mundy**, an **English trader** who came to India in the **early seventeenth century**, described them:

```
  "In the morning we met a TANDA of Banjaras with 14,000 OXEN.
   They were all laden with grains such as wheat and rice ...

   These Banjaras carry their HOUSEHOLD -- wives and children --
   along with them. ONE TANDA CONSISTS OF MANY FAMILIES.

   They OWN their oxen. They are sometimes hired by merchants,
   but MOST COMMONLY THEY ARE THEMSELVES MERCHANTS.

   They BUY GRAIN WHERE IT IS CHEAPLY AVAILABLE and carry it
   to places where it is DEARER. From there they again reload
   their oxen...

   They travel not more than 6 or 7 MILES A DAY -- that, too,
   in the cool weather. After unloading their oxen, they turn
   them free to graze."
```

> ⭐⭐ **Why the Banjaras deserve real attention** — NCERT's own discussion question is *"Were the Banjaras important for the economy?"* and the answer is emphatically yes:
> - They were **the bulk logistics network of medieval India**. No railways, no roads worth the name — grain moved because the Banjaras moved it
> - They were **not merely carriers but merchants** — buying cheap, selling dear, **absorbing the price risk themselves**
> - They **fed armies**, which made them **strategically essential** to both the Sultanate and the Mughals
> - **100,000 bullocks** for one campaign gives the scale
>
> 💻 They were **a distributed, self-owned logistics-and-arbitrage network** — the operators owned the assets, carried their families with them, and profited from **spatial price differences**. A state that wanted grain in a city did not build a supply chain; it **hired an existing one**.

### Other mobile groups

| Group | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Pastoral tribes** | Reared and **sold animals — cattle and horses — to prosperous people** |
| **Petty pedlars** (different castes) | Travelled village to village making and selling **ropes, reeds, straw matting, coarse sacks** |
| **Mendicants** | Sometimes acted as **wandering merchants** |
| **Castes of entertainers** | Performed in different towns and villages for a livelihood |

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ Changing Society: New Castes and Hierarchies

### From *varna* to *jati*

```
   As the ECONOMY and the NEEDS OF SOCIETY GREW,
   people with NEW SKILLS were required
                    |
                    v
   Smaller castes -- JATIS -- emerged WITHIN VARNAS
      -> new jatis appeared even amongst the BRAHMANAS
                    |
                    v
   Many TRIBES and social groups were TAKEN INTO
   caste-based society and GIVEN THE STATUS OF JATIS
                    |
                    v
   Specialised artisans -- SMITHS, CARPENTERS, MASONS --
   were recognised as SEPARATE JATIS by the Brahmanas
                    |
                    v
   *** JATI, rather than VARNA, became the BASIS for
       ORGANISING SOCIETY. ***
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **That final line is one of the most important sentences in the whole book.** Class 6 Ch.4 gave you *varna* — four neat categories from the *Purusha Sukta*. **This is where the real, lived structure takes over.** *Varna* remained the theory; **jati became the practice**, and it was flexible enough to absorb new occupations and whole tribes.
>
> 🎯 **Essential for GS1 Indian Society.** When a question asks about the caste system, the sophisticated answer distinguishes **varna (four, textual, pan-Indian, static)** from **jati (thousands, local, occupational, absorptive, ranked differently in different regions)** — and this chapter is where you get the historical mechanism.

**⭐ The Uyyakondan Udaiyar inscription** — a **twelfth-century** inscription from **Tiruchirapalli taluka, present-day Tamil Nadu** — records the deliberations of a **sabha of Brahmanas** (Ch.2!) on the status of a group called ***rathakaras*** (literally **chariot makers**). They **laid down their occupations**:

- **Architecture**
- **Building coaches and chariots**
- **Erecting gateways for temples with images in them**
- **Preparing wooden equipment used to perform sacrifices**
- **Building mandapas**
- **Making jewels for the king**

> 🎯 **This is caste being manufactured, in writing, by a committee.** A group's occupational boundaries — and therefore its rank — were **deliberated and fixed by an assembly of Brahmanas.** It's rare, concrete documentary evidence of how jati status was actually produced, rather than simply inherited. **Excellent, specific Mains material.**

### 🐎 Tribes becoming Rajputs — and the catch ⭐⭐

New **Rajput clans** became powerful by the **eleventh and twelfth centuries**, from lineages such as the **Hunas, Chandelas, Chalukyas** and others. ⭐ **Some of these had been tribes earlier.** They came to be regarded as Rajputs and **gradually replaced the older rulers, especially in agricultural areas.**

**The rise of Rajput clans set an example for tribal people to follow.** With the support of the **Brahmanas**, many tribes became part of the caste system.

> ⚠️ **⭐⭐ But read the qualification carefully — it is the sharpest social point in the chapter:**
>
> > **"But only the LEADING TRIBAL FAMILIES could join the RULING CLASS. A LARGE MAJORITY joined the LOWER JATIS of caste society."**
>
> **Assimilation was not equal.** The chief became a Rajput; his people became low-caste. **The tribe was absorbed, but its internal hierarchy was created in the process** — an egalitarian kinship society entering caste society split along the way.
>
> 🎯 This is a **first-rate GS1 point** on how absorption into a dominant social order reproduces inequality rather than dissolving it.

**⭐ The exception:** many **dominant tribes of Punjab, Sind and the North-West Frontier had adopted ISLAM quite early.** They **continued to reject the caste system.** *"The unequal social order, prescribed by orthodox Hinduism, was not widely accepted in these areas."*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐ A Closer Look: THE GONDS

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Where** | A vast forested region called ***GONDWANA*** — **"country inhabited by Gonds"** |
| **Livelihood** | Practised ***SHIFTING CULTIVATION*** ⭐ |
| **Structure** | The large tribe was **divided into many smaller clans**, each with **its own raja or rai** |
| **The opening** | About the time the **power of the Delhi Sultans was declining**, a few **large Gond kingdoms** began to dominate the smaller Gond chiefs |
| ⭐ **The source** | The ***AKBAR NAMA*** mentions the Gond kingdom of **GARHA KATANGA**, which had **70,000 VILLAGES** |

### 🏛️ Gond administration — the three-tier structure ⭐⭐

```
   THE KINGDOM
        |
        +--> divided into GARHS
        |      (each GARH controlled by a particular GOND CLAN)
        |
        +--> each garh further divided into units of 84 VILLAGES
        |      called CHAURASI          [chaurasi = 84]
        |
        +--> each chaurasi subdivided into BARHOTS
               made up of 12 VILLAGES each   [barhot ~ 12]
```

> 🧠 **Memory hook: *chaurasi* = 84** (from *chaurasi*, Hindi for eighty-four) and ***barhot* = 12**. **Garh → chaurasi (84) → barhot (12).** The numbers are asked directly.

### ⚖️ How the state changed Gond society

```
   The emergence of large states CHANGED the nature of Gond society

        Their basically EQUAL society gradually got divided
        into UNEQUAL SOCIAL CLASSES
                          |
                          v
        BRAHMANAS received LAND GRANTS from the Gond rajas
        and became influential
                          |
                          v
        Gond chiefs now wished to be RECOGNISED AS RAJPUTS
```

**⭐ The Garha Katanga story:**

| Person | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Aman Das** | The Gond raja of **Garha Katanga**; **assumed the title SANGRAM SHAH** ⭐ |
| **Dalpat** | His son; **married princess DURGAWATI**, daughter of **Salbahan**, the **Chandel Rajput raja of Mahoba** |
| **Rani Durgawati** ⭐⭐ | Dalpat **died early**; she ruled, and **under her the kingdom became even more extensive** |
| **1565** ⭐ | Mughal forces under **ASAF KHAN** attacked Garha Katanga. **A strong resistance was put up by Rani Durgawati. She was defeated and PREFERRED TO DIE RATHER THAN SURRENDER.** Her son (**Bir Narain**) also died |
| **The wealth** | Garha Katanga was **rich** — it earned much wealth by **trapping and exporting WILD ELEPHANTS** to other kingdoms ⭐ |
| **The aftermath** | The Mughals captured **a huge booty of precious coins and elephants**; **annexed part** of the kingdom and **granted the rest to Chandra Shah, an uncle of Bir Narain** |
| **Later** | Gond kingdoms survived for some time but became **much weaker**, and later struggled **unsuccessfully against the Bundelas and Marathas** |

> ⭐ **Rani Durgawati is high-value.** A woman ruling a tribal kingdom, expanding it, and dying in battle against the Mughals rather than surrendering — she belongs in any answer on **women in Indian history** alongside Raziyya (Ch.3) and Nur Jahan (Ch.4), and she is a standard name in Prelims.
>
> 🎯 **NCERT's question — "why were the Mughals interested in the land of the Gonds?" — has a concrete answer:** **elephants** (militarily essential), **precious coins**, forest produce, and **70,000 villages** worth of revenue.

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐ A Closer Look: THE AHOMS

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | Migrated to the **BRAHMAPUTRA VALLEY** from present-day **MYANMAR** in the **THIRTEENTH century** ⭐ |
| **State-building** | Created a new state by **suppressing the older political system of the *BHUIYANS* (landlords)** ⭐ |
| **Expansion** | In the 16th century annexed the kingdoms of the **CHHUTIYAS (1523)** and of **KOCH-HAJO (1581)**, and subjugated many other tribes |
| **Military technology** ⭐ | Used **firearms as early as the 1530s**; by the **1660s** they could make **high-quality gunpowder and cannons** |
| **1662** ⭐⭐ | The **Mughals under MIR JUMLA** attacked the Ahom kingdom. **Despite their brave defence, the Ahoms were defeated. But direct Mughal control over the region could not last long** |

> ⭐ **Lachit Barphukan** — the Ahom general who **defeated the Mughals in Assam** (at the Battle of Saraighat, 1671), reversing Mir Jumla's gains. He is referenced in the chapter's illustrations and is a live name in current affairs (a national statue and commemorations). **Know him.**

### 🏛️ Ahom administration ⭐⭐

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Basis** | The state **depended upon FORCED LABOUR** |
| ***PAIKS*** ⭐⭐ | Those **forced to work for the state** |
| **The mechanism** | A **CENSUS of the population was taken**. Each village had to send a number of paiks **BY ROTATION** ⭐ |
| **Population engineering** ⭐ | **People from heavily populated areas were shifted to less populated places.** ⭐ **Ahom clans were thus BROKEN UP** |
| **Result** | By the first half of the seventeenth century the **administration became quite centralised** |
| **Service** | **Almost all adult males served in the army during war.** At other times they were engaged in building **dams, irrigation systems and other public works** |
| **Agriculture** | The Ahoms **introduced new methods of rice cultivation** |
| ***KHELS*** ⭐ | Ahom society was divided into **clans or khels**; a khel **often controlled several villages** |
| **Artisans** | There were **very few castes of artisans**, so artisans **came from the adjoining kingdoms** |
| ⭐⭐ **Land rights** | **"The peasant was given land by his VILLAGE COMMUNITY. EVEN THE KING COULD NOT TAKE IT AWAY WITHOUT THE COMMUNITY'S CONSENT."** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **That last row is the most striking fact in the chapter.** In a period when Mughal jagirdars were stripping peasants (Ch.4), the Ahom state recognised **community ownership of land that constrained the king himself.** It is a genuinely different property regime.
>
> 🔗 **This is the historical ancestor of the constitutional protection of tribal land** — the **Fifth and Sixth Schedules**, restrictions on land alienation, **PESA (1996)** and the **Forest Rights Act (2006)** all rest on the principle that tribal land belongs to the community, not to individual transferable title. **Cite the Ahom rule as the precedent in a GS1/GS2 answer and it will stand out.**
>
> 💻 Note also what the **census + rotation + forced relocation** system was *for*: **breaking up the clans.** The state deliberately dissolved kinship blocs into an administrative population it could count and conscript. **That is the classic move of a centralising state against kin-based society** — and it worked.

### 🛕 Religion and culture

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Originally** | The Ahoms **worshipped their own tribal gods** |
| **Change** | The **influence of Brahmanas increased**; **temples and Brahmanas were granted land by the king** |
| ⭐ **Sib Singh (1714–1744)** | **Hinduism became the predominant religion** |
| ⭐ **But** | **The Ahom kings did NOT completely give up their traditional beliefs** after adopting Hinduism |
| **Culture** | Society was **very sophisticated** — **poets and scholars were given land grants**, **theatre was encouraged**, important **Sanskrit works were translated into the local language** |
| ***BURANJIS*** ⭐⭐ | **Historical chronicles**, written **first in the Ahom language and then in Assamese** |

> ⭐ ***Buranjis* are a favourite Prelims item.** They matter because they are **an indigenous historical tradition from outside the Sanskritic and Persianate mainstream** — a third stream of medieval Indian historiography alongside *prashastis* and *tawarikh*.
>
> 🎯 **And note the syncretism:** adopting Hinduism **without abandoning traditional beliefs**. That is the normal pattern of religious change in India, and it is a useful corrective to any account that treats conversion as a clean switch.

## 6️⃣ ⭐ The Conclusion NCERT Draws

> Considerable social change took place. **Caste-based society and tribal people constantly interacted with each other. This interaction caused BOTH kinds of societies to adapt and change.**
>
> - There were **many different tribes** taking up **diverse livelihoods**
> - Over time, **many merged with caste-based society**
> - **Others rejected both the caste system and orthodox Hinduism**
> - **Some tribes established extensive states** with well-organised systems of administration, becoming **politically powerful** — which **brought them into conflict with larger and more complex kingdoms and empires**

> 🎯 **Four different outcomes, not one.** A weak answer says "tribes were absorbed"; a strong one distinguishes **merger, rejection, state-formation, and conflict** — and gives a case for each (Bhils, the Islamised north-western tribes, the Ahoms, the Gonds).

### ⚖️ Gond vs Ahom — the comparison NCERT asks for

| | **GONDS** | **AHOMS** |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | Indigenous to **Gondwana** | **Migrated** from **Myanmar**, 13th century |
| **Base economy** | **Shifting cultivation** | Settled rice; **new methods of cultivation** |
| **State built by** | Larger Gond kingdoms absorbing smaller chiefs | **Suppressing the *bhuiyans*** |
| **Administration** | **Garh → chaurasi (84) → barhot (12)** | **Census + *paiks* by rotation**; **khels** |
| **Labour** | — | **Forced labour (*paiks*)** central |
| **Sanskritisation** | Chiefs sought **Rajput** status (**Sangram Shah**) | Brahmana influence grew; **Hinduism predominant under Sib Singh** — but **old beliefs retained** |
| **Mughal encounter** | **Defeated 1565** (Asaf Khan vs **Rani Durgawati**) | **Defeated 1662** (Mir Jumla) — **but Mughal control did not last** |
| **Historical writing** | — | ⭐ ***Buranjis*** |
| **Land** | — | ⭐ **Community-granted; king could not take it without consent** |

> 🧠 **The one-line discriminator:** **Gonds were absorbed and broken; the Ahoms were defeated but not held**, and they left their own historical chronicles.

- **"Mughal victory in 1662 gave lasting control of Assam"** → **FALSE.** It **could not last long**

### Mains

- **Indian Society (GS1) ⭐⭐⭐:** *"Assimilation into caste society reproduced inequality rather than dissolving it."* — leading families vs the majority; the *rathakara* inscription
- **Indian Society ⭐⭐:** the **varna → jati** transition as the mechanism by which caste absorbed new groups and occupations
- **Polity (GS2) ⭐⭐:** the **Ahom community land rule** as historical precedent for Fifth/Sixth Schedule protections, **PESA** and the **Forest Rights Act**
- **Historiography ⭐:** using **oral traditions** to write tribal history; the ***buranjis*** as a non-mainstream historiographical tradition
- **Economy:** the **Banjaras** as the logistics backbone of the medieval economy
- **Women in history:** **Rani Durgawati**

## 🔁 90-Second Recap

1. **Tribes** were united by **kinship**, did not follow Brahmanical rules, were **not divided into unequal classes**, and held **land and pastures jointly**.
2. Tribal history is hard to write — **no written records**, scanty external accounts — so historians now use **oral traditions**.
3. **Regional map:** Gaddis (Himalaya, shepherds), Khokhars/Gakkhars (Punjab), Langahs/Arghuns (Multan-Sind), Balochis (NW), Cheros/Mundas/Santals (Bihar-Jharkhand-Orissa-Bengal), Kolis/Berads (Maharashtra-Karnataka-Gujarat), **Bhils** (western & central), **Gonds** (Gondwana), Nagas and Ahoms (NE).
4. **Banjaras** were the great **trader-nomads**; caravan = ***tanda***; used by **Alauddin Khalji** and the Mughals; **Peter Mundy** saw a tanda of **14,000 oxen**.
5. **Jati, not varna, became the basis of social organisation** — new jatis formed for new occupations, and whole tribes were admitted as jatis.
6. The ***rathakara*** inscription shows a **sabha of Brahmanas formally fixing a group's occupations and status**.
7. **Tribes became Rajputs** (Hunas, Chandelas, Chalukyas) — **but only leading families joined the ruling class; the majority entered lower jatis.** Tribes of Punjab, Sind and the NWF adopted **Islam** and **rejected caste**.
8. **Gonds:** **Gondwana**, **shifting cultivation**, clans under rajas; **Garha Katanga** had **70,000 villages** (*Akbar Nama*); administered as **garh → chaurasi (84) → barhot (12)**.
9. **Aman Das took the title Sangram Shah**; **Rani Durgawati** expanded the kingdom and **died rather than surrender** to **Asaf Khan** in **1565**. The state was rich from **exporting wild elephants**.
10. **Ahoms** came from **Myanmar** in the **13th century**, suppressed the ***bhuiyans***, annexed the **Chhutiyas (1523)** and **Koch-Hajo (1581)**, and had **firearms by the 1530s** and **cannon by the 1660s**.
11. **Ahom administration** rested on **forced labour (*paiks*)**, a **census**, **rotation**, and the deliberate **breaking up of clans**; society was organised in ***khels***.
12. ⭐ **Ahom land was granted by the village community, and the king could not take it away without consent.**
13. Under **Sib Singh (1714–1744)** Hinduism became predominant, **but traditional beliefs were retained**. The Ahoms wrote ***buranjis***.
14. **Both societies changed through interaction** — some tribes merged, some rejected caste, some built states, and those states came into conflict with empires.
