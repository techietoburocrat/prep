## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Crisis of the Empire

### The causes — learn these as a chain, not a list

```
   [1] AURANGZEB'S DECCAN WAR
       "Emperor Aurangzeb had DEPLETED THE MILITARY AND
        FINANCIAL RESOURCES of his empire by fighting a
        LONG WAR IN THE DECCAN."
                        |
                        v
   [2] ADMINISTRATIVE BREAKDOWN UNDER HIS SUCCESSORS
       "the efficiency of the imperial administration BROKE DOWN"
       -> increasingly difficult to keep a check on powerful
          MANSABDARS
                        |
                        v
   [3] *** THE FUSION OF OFFICES ***  <-- the key structural failure
       Nobles appointed as GOVERNORS (SUBADARS) often controlled
       the offices of REVENUE and MILITARY administration
       (*** DIWANI and FAUJDARI ***) AS WELL.
       -> "This gave them EXTRAORDINARY POLITICAL, ECONOMIC AND
           MILITARY POWERS over vast regions."
                        |
                        v
   [4] REVENUE STOPS FLOWING TO THE CENTRE
       "As the governors consolidated their control over the
        provinces, the PERIODIC REMISSION OF REVENUE TO THE
        CAPITAL DECLINED."
                        |
                        v
   [5] PEASANT AND ZAMINDARI REBELLIONS in northern and
       western India -- caused by MOUNTING TAXES, or by
       CHIEFTAINS consolidating their own positions.
       *** These groups were now able to SEIZE THE ECONOMIC
           RESOURCES of the region. ***
                        |
                        v
   RESULT: the emperors after Aurangzeb were UNABLE TO ARREST
   "the gradual shifting of POLITICAL AND ECONOMIC AUTHORITY
    into the hands of PROVINCIAL GOVERNORS, LOCAL CHIEFTAINS
    and other groups."
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Point [3] is the one to understand properly, and it wins marks.**
>
> The Mughal design deliberately **separated powers**: the *subadar* governed, the *diwan* collected revenue, the *faujdar* commanded troops — three officers watching one another. When a **single noble came to hold all three**, the province acquired **its own money, its own army, and its own government**, answerable to nobody.
>
> 💻 **A separation-of-duties control collapsed.** The centre had built the safeguard and then allowed the roles to be combined — after which nothing prevented a governor from simply becoming a king. **He did not need to rebel; he only had to stop remitting.**
>
> 🔗 **Compare Ch.3:** the Delhi Sultans **rotated** *muqtis*, made *iqtas* **non-inheritable**, and **audited** them precisely to prevent this. The Mughals let the controls lapse — and lost the empire the Sultans' design had protected.

### 💰 ⭐ "Rich harvests and empty coffers"

A contemporary writer on the empire's financial bankruptcy — a superb, quotable passage:

> *"The great lords are **helpless and impoverished**. Their peasants raise **two crops a year**, but their lords see **nothing of either**, and their agents on the spot are **virtual prisoners in the peasants' hands**... So complete is the collapse of all order and administration that **though the peasant reaps a harvest of gold, his lord does not see so much as a wisp of straw**. How then can the lord **keep the armed force** he should?"*

> 🎯 **This is not an agricultural crisis — it is a COLLECTION crisis.** Production was fine; **extraction had failed**. The state's problem was never that there was no wealth, but that it could **no longer reach it**.
>
> ⭐ And note the closing logic — **no revenue means no army**, which means still less revenue. **The circle of justice (Ch.3) running backwards.** Deploy that link; it is a genuinely elegant connection between two chapters.

### ⚔️ ⭐⭐ The invasions

| Invader | From | When | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **NADIR SHAH** | Ruler of **IRAN** | ⭐ **1739** | **Sacked and plundered DELHI** and took away **immense amounts of wealth** |
| ⭐⭐ **AHMAD SHAH ABDALI** | Afghan ruler | ⭐ **Five times between 1748 and 1761** | A series of **plundering raids** on north India |

**⭐ What Nadir Shah took**, per a contemporary account:

> *"Sixty lakhs of rupees and some thousand gold coins, nearly **one crore worth of gold-ware**, nearly **fifty crores worth of jewels**, most of them unrivalled in the world — and the above included the **PEACOCK THRONE**."*

**And the effect on Delhi:**

> *"Those who had been masters were now in dire straits... **The wealthy were turned into beggars.** Those who once set the style in clothes now went naked; and those who owned property were now homeless... **The New City (Shahjahanabad) was turned into rubble.**"*

> 🔗 **Remember Ch.7:** it was **this very invasion of 1739** that drove **Mughal painters into the Himalayan foothills**, founding the **Kangra school**. **The same event that destroyed a capital created a school of painting.** That cross-reference is exactly the kind of connection that makes an answer memorable.
>
> ⭐ **Ahmad Shah Abdali's fifth invasion is the third battle of Panipat, 1761** — where he destroyed the Marathas. **The two great powers contending for north India wrecked each other, and the British inherited the field.**

### 🎭 ⭐ Faction and humiliation

The empire was further weakened by **competition amongst different groups of nobles**, divided into **two major factions**:

| Faction | Who |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **IRANIS** | Nobles of Iranian descent |
| ⭐ **TURANIS** | Nobles of **Turkish** descent |

> **"For a long time, the later Mughal emperors were PUPPETS in the hands of either one or the other of these two powerful groups."**

**⭐⭐ The humiliation, stated plainly by NCERT:**

| Emperor | Fate |
|---|---|
| **Farrukh Siyar (1713–1719)** | ⭐ **ASSASSINATED** |
| **Alamgir II (1754–1759)** | ⭐ **ASSASSINATED** |
| **Ahmad Shah (1748–1754)** | ⭐ **BLINDED** by his nobles |
| **Shah Alam II (1759–1816)** | ⭐ **BLINDED** by his nobles |

> 🎯 **Two emperors murdered and two blinded by their own nobility, within fifty years.** That single fact conveys the collapse of imperial authority more forcefully than any general statement — use it as evidence, not decoration.

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Successor States — Three Types

NCERT's material sorts into three categories, and **classifying them properly is what separates a good answer from a list**:

```
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | TYPE 1: OLD MUGHAL PROVINCES that became independent               |
  |         Governors (subadars) and great zamindars consolidated      |
  |         their authority.                                           |
  |         *** AWADH  |  BENGAL  |  HYDERABAD ***                     |
  |         -> Founded by MUGHAL OFFICERS. Still formally recognised   |
  |            the emperor. "Inherited from the inside." (Ch.4)        |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | TYPE 2: WATAN JAGIRS -- states that had always existed under       |
  |         Mughal suzerainty and now asserted autonomy                |
  |         *** THE RAJPUTS *** (Amber, Jodhpur, Mewar)                |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | TYPE 3: STATES SEIZED BY REBELLION against the Mughals             |

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> ⭐ **NCERT explicitly names Awadh, Bengal and Hyderabad** as provinces where *subadars* and great zamindars consolidated authority. **The chapter then details the Type 2 and Type 3 states.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐ The Rajputs

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Their standing** | Many Rajput kings, **particularly of AMBER and JODHPUR**, had **served under the Mughals with distinction** — in exchange they enjoyed **considerable autonomy in their *WATAN JAGIRS*** |
| **The 18th-century move** | These rulers **attempted to extend their control over adjacent regions** |
| ⭐ **The offices they claimed** | The ***subadari*** of the rich provinces of **GUJARAT and MALWA**: **Raja AJIT SINGH of JODHPUR** held the governorship of **GUJARAT**; **SAWAI RAJA JAI SINGH of AMBER** was governor of **MALWA**. These offices were **renewed by Emperor Jahandar Shah in 1713** |
| **Territorial seizures** | **NAGAUR** was conquered and annexed to the house of **Jodhpur**; **Amber seized large portions of BUNDI** |
| ⭐ **The new capital** | **Sawai Raja Jai Singh founded his new capital at JAIPUR** and was given the ***subadari* of AGRA in 1722** |
| ⚠️ **The check** | ⭐ **Maratha campaigns into Rajasthan from the 1740s put severe pressure on these principalities and CHECKED THEIR FURTHER EXPANSION** |
| **Mewar's exception** | ⭐ Many Rajput rulers accepted Mughal suzerainty, **but MEWAR was the only Rajput state which defied it**. **Rana Pratap** ascended the throne at Mewar in **1572** and **stood his ground** despite a series of envoys |

### 🏰 Forts and observatories ⭐

- Many Rajput chieftains **built forts on hill tops** which became **centres of power** — housing **urban centres, palaces, temples, trading centres, water harvesting structures**. The **Chittorgarh fort** contained **talabs (ponds), kundis (wells), baolis (stepwells)**
- ⭐⭐ **Sawai Jai Singh, the ruler of Amber, constructed FIVE ASTRONOMICAL OBSERVATORIES** — one each at **DELHI, JAIPUR, UJJAIN, MATHURA and VARANASI**. Commonly known as the ⭐ ***JANTAR MANTAR***, they had **various instruments to study heavenly bodies**

> ⭐⭐ **The Jantar Mantar is a high-frequency Prelims item and a great Mains detail.** The **five cities** are asked directly. It also makes an excellent point: in the middle of imperial collapse, a regional ruler was **funding astronomy** — evidence that the 18th century was not a cultural dark age but a period of **decentralised patronage** (exactly the Ch.7 argument about painting).
>
> **A 1732 Persian account of Jai Singh:** *"He was the governor of Agra for 12 years and of Malwa for 5 or 6 years. He possessed a large army, artillery and great wealth. **His sway extended from Delhi to the banks of the Narmada.**"*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Seizing Independence: THE SIKHS

**Follow the arc from religious community (Ch.6) to sovereign state.**

```
   17th CENTURY : the organisation of the Sikhs into a POLITICAL
   COMMUNITY (Ch.6) "helped in REGIONAL STATE-BUILDING in the PUNJAB"
        |
        |  Several battles fought by GURU GOBIND SINGH against
        |  Rajput and Mughal rulers, BEFORE AND AFTER the
        |  institution of the KHALSA in 1699
        v
   1708 : Guru Gobind Singh dies
        |
        v
   BANDA BAHADUR leads the Khalsa in REVOLT against Mughal authority
     -> *** declared SOVEREIGN RULE by STRIKING COINS in the name
            of GURU NANAK and GURU GOBIND SINGH ***
     -> established their own administration BETWEEN THE SUTLEJ
        AND THE JAMUNA
     -> CAPTURED 1715, EXECUTED 1716
        |
        v
   THE 18th CENTURY ORGANISATION
     - Sikhs organised into bands called *** JATHAS ***, later *** MISLS ***
     - Their combined forces = the grand army, *** DAL KHALSA ***
     - The entire body met at AMRITSAR at BAISAKHI and DIWALI to take
       collective decisions -- "resolutions of the Guru", *** GURMATAS ***
     - A system called *** RAKHI *** offered PROTECTION to cultivators
       on payment of a tax of *** 20 PER CENT of the produce ***
        |
        v
   THE IDEOLOGY : Guru Gobind Singh had inspired the Khalsa with the
   belief that their destiny was to rule -- *** "RAJ KAREGA KHALSA" ***
        |
        v
   Successful resistance FIRST to the Mughal governors, THEN to
   AHMAD SHAH ABDALI, who had seized the rich province of the Punjab
   and the SARKAR OF SIRHIND
        |
        v
   1765 : The Khalsa AGAIN declared sovereign rule by striking their
   own coin -- bearing THE SAME INSCRIPTION as in Banda Bahadur's time
        |
        v
   LATE 18th CENTURY : Sikh territories extended FROM THE INDUS TO THE
   JAMUNA but were DIVIDED under different rulers
        |
        v
   *** MAHARAJA RANJIT SINGH reunited these groups and established
       his capital at LAHORE in 1799 ***
```

> ⭐⭐ **Note what "striking coins" means.** Minting coinage is **the definitive assertion of sovereignty** in the Islamic-Persianate world (along with having the *khutba* read in one's name). By issuing coins **in the Gurus' names**, Banda Bahadur was making a **formal, legally legible claim to independent rule** — and doing it **twice, in 1710s and again in 1765, with the same inscription**, asserting continuity.
>
> ⭐ **The *rakhi* system is also worth understanding**, because it is state-building in miniature: **offer protection, charge 20%**. That is precisely what a state is — a protection service with a tax. **The Sikhs built a state from the revenue function upward.**
>
> 🎯 **And the *gurmata* is a genuine Polity crossover:** collective decisions taken by the assembled body at fixed festivals. Add it to your **Vajji sangha → Buddhist sangha → Chola sabha** lineage of Indian deliberative institutions (Class 6 Ch.5, Ch.6, Ch.9 and Class 7 Ch.2).

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ THE MARATHAS

### Shivaji and the foundation

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founder** | ⭐ **CHHATRAPATI SHIVAJI MAHARAJ (1630–1680)** |
| **Born** | To **Shahji and Jija Bai** at ⭐ **SHIVNERI in 1630**; guided by his mother and his guardian **Dada Konddev** |
| **Nature of the state** | *"Another powerful regional kingdom to arise out of a **sustained opposition to Mughal rule**"* |
| ⭐ **His support base** | Powerful warrior families — ⭐ ***DESHMUKHS*** |
| ⭐ **His army's backbone** | Groups of **highly mobile, peasant-pastoralists** — ⭐ ***KUNBIS*** |
| **Early conquest** | The **occupation of JAVLI** made him **undisputed leader of the MAVALA highlands**, paving the way for expansion |
| **His method** | ⭐ **Often resorted to GUERRILLA WARFARE**; his exploits against **Bijapur** and the **Mughals** made him a legendary figure |
| ⭐⭐ **His revenue system** | An efficient administration supported by revenue collection based on ⭐ ***CHAUTH* and *SARDESHMUKHI*** |

**⭐⭐ The two taxes — learn the rates:**

| Tax | Rate | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ ***CHAUTH*** | **25 per cent** of the land revenue claimed by zamindars | In the Deccan, **collected by the Marathas** |
| ⭐ ***SARDESHMUKHI*** | **9–10 per cent** of the land revenue | Paid to the **head revenue collector** in the Deccan |

> 🧠 **Memory hook: *chauth* from *chautha* = "a fourth" = 25%.** The word tells you the rate. *Sardeshmukhi* = the due of the *sardeshmukh*, the chief headman — **9–10%**.

### The Peshwas and expansion

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **After Shivaji's death** | Effective power was wielded by a family of ⭐ **CHITPAVAN BRAHMANAS** who served Shivaji's successors as ⭐ ***PESHWA* (principal minister)** |
| ⭐ **Capital** | ⭐ **POONA** became the capital of the Maratha kingdom |
| ⭐ **Military method** | Under the Peshwas the Marathas developed a **very successful military organisation**. Their success lay in ⭐ **BYPASSING the fortified areas of the Mughals**, by **RAIDING CITIES**, and by **engaging Mughal armies in areas where their SUPPLY LINES AND REINFORCEMENTS could be easily disturbed** |
| **Baji Rao I** | Son of Peshwa **Balaji Vishwanath**; a great general credited with **expanding the Maratha kingdom beyond the Vindhyas**; campaigns against **Malwa, Bundelkhand, Gujarat and the Portuguese** |

**⭐⭐ The expansion, 1720–1761:**

```
   1720s : MALWA and GUJARAT seized from the Mughals
   1730s : the Maratha king recognised as OVERLORD OF THE ENTIRE
           DECCAN PENINSULA, with the right to levy CHAUTH and
           SARDESHMUKHI in the whole region
   1737  : RAIDED DELHI
   then  : frontiers expanded rapidly --
             NORTH -> Rajasthan and the Punjab
             EAST  -> Bengal and Orissa
             SOUTH -> Karnataka, and the Tamil and Telugu countries

   *** These were NOT FORMALLY INCLUDED in the Maratha empire, but
       were MADE TO PAY TRIBUTE as a way of accepting Maratha
       sovereignty. ***
```

### ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐ Why Panipat was lost — the causal point

> **"Expansion brought enormous resources, BUT IT CAME AT A PRICE. These military campaigns also MADE OTHER RULERS HOSTILE towards the Marathas. As a result, THEY WERE NOT INCLINED TO SUPPORT THE MARATHAS during the THIRD BATTLE OF PANIPAT IN 1761."**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the single most important analytical sentence in the chapter, and it is a ready-made Mains answer.**
>
> The Marathas expanded by **extracting tribute from rulers they did not absorb**. That produced **revenue but not allies** — every tributary was a resentful neighbour rather than a stakeholder. So when Ahmad Shah Abdali came in **1761**, the Marathas faced him **alone**.
>
> 🔗 **Contrast Akbar's policy directly (Ch.4): "defeating but not humiliating."** Akbar **absorbed** defeated Rajputs into the mansabdari with *watan jagirs* and marriage alliances, converting enemies into stakeholders. **The Marathas taxed where the Mughals had co-opted — and paid for it at Panipat.**
>
> **That comparison, stated in two sentences, is worth serious marks.**

### 🏛️ ⭐⭐ Maratha administration — the part most people miss

> **"Alongside endless military campaigns, the Marathas developed an EFFECTIVE ADMINISTRATIVE SYSTEM as well."**

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Revenue** | Once conquest was complete and rule secure, **revenue demands were gradually introduced TAKING LOCAL CONDITIONS INTO ACCOUNT** |
| **Economy** | ⭐ **Agriculture was encouraged and trade revived** |
| ⭐ **The sardars** | This gave Maratha chiefs (*sardars*) — ⭐ **SINDHIA of GWALIOR, GAEKWAD of BARODA, BHONSLE of NAGPUR** — the resources to **raise powerful armies** |
| ⭐ **Cities grew** | Maratha campaigns into Malwa in the 1720s **did not challenge the growth and prosperity of the region's cities**. ⭐ **UJJAIN expanded under SINDHIA's patronage and INDORE under HOLKAR's** — "large and prosperous... important commercial and cultural centres" |
| ⭐ **New trade routes** | Emerged within Maratha-controlled areas. The **silk produced in the CHANDERI region found a new outlet in POONA**. **BURHANPUR**, earlier on the Agra–Surat trade, **expanded its hinterland to include Poona and Nagpur in the south and Lucknow and Allahabad in the east** |

> ⭐⭐ **This section is the direct rebuttal to "the 18th century was a period of chaos and decline."** Cities grew, trade routes multiplied, agriculture was encouraged, and revenue was assessed with local conditions in mind. **Economic life reorganised around new centres rather than collapsing.**
>
> 🧠 **The sardar–city pairings are asked: Sindhia–Gwalior/Ujjain, Gaekwad–Baroda, Bhonsle–Nagpur, Holkar–Indore.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐ The Jats

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **When** | Consolidated power during the **late seventeenth and eighteenth centuries** |
| ⭐ **Under Churaman** | Acquired control over territories **west of Delhi**; by the **1680s** they dominated the region **between the two imperial cities of DELHI and AGRA**. For a while they became the **virtual custodians of the city of AGRA** |
| **Their economy** | ⭐ **Prosperous agriculturists**; towns like **PANIPAT and BALLABHGARH** became important **trading centres** |
| ⭐⭐ **Under Suraj Mal** | The kingdom of ⭐ **BHARATPUR** emerged as a strong state. He consolidated the Jat state at Bharatpur during **1756–1763**; his territory included parts of **modern eastern Rajasthan, southern Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and Delhi**. He built many forts and palaces — the famous ⭐ **LOHAGARH fort at Bharatpur** is regarded as one of the strongest in the region |
| ⭐ **After 1739** | When **Nadir Shah sacked Delhi**, **many of the city's notables took refuge** in Bharatpur |
| **Jawahir Shah** | Suraj Mal's son; had **30,000 troops of his own** and **hired another 20,000 Maratha and 15,000 Sikh troops** to fight the Mughals |
| ⭐ **Architecture** | While the **Bharatpur fort** was built in a fairly traditional style, at ⭐ **DIG** the Jats built an **elaborate GARDEN PALACE combining styles seen at AMBER and AGRA**; its buildings were **modelled on architectural forms first associated with royalty under SHAH JAHAN** |

> 🎯 **Two points worth extracting:**
> 1. **Jawahir Shah hiring Maratha and Sikh troops** shows how fluid 18th-century politics was — these were **military labour markets**, not fixed national blocs. It's a useful corrective to reading the period as a clash of communities
> 2. **Dig's palace copying Shah Jahan's forms** is the Ch.7 argument again: **successor states inherited and reused Mughal cultural prestige** even while dismantling Mughal power

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Decline, or Reorganisation? — The Assessment

**This is the question UPSC actually asks. Argue both sides.**

| "The 18th century was a period of DECLINE" | "The 18th century was a period of REORGANISATION" |
|---|---|
| Imperial administration **broke down**; revenue stopped reaching Delhi | Successor states developed **effective administrative systems** taking **local conditions** into account |
| **Two emperors assassinated, two blinded** | **Ujjain, Indore, Poona, Jaipur, Bharatpur** grew as prosperous commercial and cultural centres |
| **Nadir Shah (1739)** and **Abdali (five times, 1748–61)** plundered at will | **Agriculture encouraged, trade revived, NEW TRADE ROUTES emerged** |
| Constant warfare; the Marathas isolated and broken at **Panipat 1761** | **Jantar Mantar** observatories built; **Kangra and Rajput painting** flourished (Ch.7) |
| **The British seized eastern India by 1765** | Regional **languages and cultures** consolidated (Ch.7) |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The formulation that scores:**
>
> *"The eighteenth century saw not the collapse of governance but its **relocation** — from an imperial centre that could no longer collect revenue to regional states that could. What declined was **Mughal** authority, not Indian state capacity or economic life. The decisive weakness was not disorder but **disunity**: states that extracted tribute from one another rather than building alliances faced Ahmad Shah Abdali, and then the English East India Company, one at a time."*
>
> 🔗 **And that last clause is the bridge to Class 8.** The British did not defeat "India"; **they defeated a sequence of individually strong, mutually hostile regional states.** Panipat 1761 and Plassey 1757 belong to the same story.

**Traps:**
- **"Nadir Shah invaded Bengal"** → **FALSE.** He sacked **Delhi** (NCERT asks this as true/false)
- **"Sawai Raja Jai Singh was the ruler of Indore"** → **FALSE.** He ruled **Amber** and founded **Jaipur**; Indore was **Holkar's**
- **"Chauth was 10% and sardeshmukhi 25%"** → **REVERSED.** Chauth **25%**, sardeshmukhi **9–10%**
- **"Poona was the Maratha capital from Shivaji's time"** → It became the capital **under the Peshwas, in the eighteenth century**
- **"The Marathas were defeated at Panipat because they were militarily weak"** → **FALSE.** They were **isolated** — other rulers refused to support them
- **"Banda Bahadur founded the Khalsa"** → **FALSE.** **Guru Gobind Singh, 1699**; Banda led the revolt after 1708
- **"Ranjit Singh made Amritsar his capital"** → **FALSE.** **Lahore, 1799**

## 🔁 2-MINUTE RECAP

1. **1707 → 1761.** Aurangzeb's death to the third battle of Panipat; **by 1765 the British held eastern India.**
2. **The crisis:** Aurangzeb's **Deccan war** drained men and money; administration broke down; ⭐ ***subadars* came to hold *diwani* AND *faujdari***, giving them political, economic and military power; **revenue stopped reaching Delhi**; peasant and zamindari revolts **seized the region's resources**.
3. **"Though the peasant reaps a harvest of gold, his lord does not see so much as a wisp of straw"** — a **collection** failure, not a production failure.
4. **Nadir Shah of Iran sacked Delhi in 1739**, taking the **Peacock Throne**; **Ahmad Shah Abdali** raided **five times, 1748–61**.
5. **Irani and Turani factions** made emperors puppets — **two assassinated, two blinded**.
6. **Three kinds of successor state:** Mughal provinces gone independent (**Awadh, Bengal, Hyderabad**); **watan jagir** states (**Rajputs**); and states **seized by rebellion** (**Sikhs, Marathas, Jats**).
7. **Rajputs:** **Ajit Singh → Gujarat**, **Sawai Jai Singh → Malwa**, **Jaipur** founded, **Agra subadari 1722**; **checked by the Marathas from the 1740s**. **Mewar** alone had defied the Mughals. **Jai Singh built five Jantar Mantar observatories.**
8. **Sikhs:** after 1708, **Banda Bahadur** revolted and **struck coins in the Gurus' names** (captured 1715, executed 1716); the Sikhs organised into ***jathas* and *misls***, combined as the ***dal khalsa***, met at **Amritsar** for ***gurmatas***, and ran the ***rakhi*** protection system at **20% of produce**; **Ranjit Singh** reunited them at **Lahore in 1799**.
9. **Marathas:** **Shivaji (1630–1680)** built the state on **deshmukhs** and **kunbis** with **guerrilla warfare** and the **chauth (25%) / sardeshmukhi (9–10%)** system; the **Chitpavan Brahmana Peshwas** ruled from **Poona**; expansion took **Malwa and Gujarat by the 1720s**, Deccan overlordship by the **1730s**, and **Delhi raided in 1737**.
10. ⭐ **They took tribute without absorbing anyone — so nobody helped them at Panipat in 1761.**
11. **But their administration worked:** revenue set by local conditions, **agriculture encouraged, trade revived**, **Ujjain (Sindhia)** and **Indore (Holkar)** flourished, and **new trade routes** linked Chanderi, Poona, Burhanpur, Nagpur, Lucknow and Allahabad.
12. **Jats:** **Churaman** took the Delhi–Agra tract; **Suraj Mal** built **Bharatpur** and the **Lohagarh fort**; the garden palace at **DIG** copied **Shah Jahan's** forms.
13. ⭐ **The century was a reorganisation, not a collapse** — but a **disunited** one, which is exactly what the East India Company needed.
