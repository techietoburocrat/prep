## 🎬 Start Here: The Promotion Problem

By the seventh century there were **big landlords and warrior chiefs** everywhere. Existing kings acknowledged them as subordinates — ***samantas***.

**A samanta's obligations:**
- Bring **gifts** for his king or overlord
- Be **present at his court**
- Provide **military support**

And then the pattern that defines the whole chapter:

```
         SAMANTA  (subordinate)
             |
             |  gains POWER and WEALTH
             v
      MAHA-SAMANTA  /  MAHA-MANDALESHVARA
      ("the great lord of a circle or region")
             |
             |  declares INDEPENDENCE from the overlord
             v
          KING
             |
             |  ...and now HIS OWN subordinates
             |     start doing the same thing to him
             v
        (repeat, for a thousand years)
```

> 💻 **This is privilege escalation as a governance model.** The overlord delegates authority and resources to a subordinate in order to extend reach; the subordinate accumulates enough of both to stop needing the overlord. There is **no mechanism to revoke the grant**, so every delegation is potentially permanent. You saw the identical dynamic in **Class 6 Ch.9** with *samantas* and land grants under the Guptas — this chapter is that process running to completion.

## 1️⃣ The Emergence of New Dynasties

### 👑 Case study: the Rashtrakutas ⭐⭐

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Starting position** | Subordinate to the **Chalukyas of Karnataka** |
| **The move** | In the **mid-eighth century**, **Dantidurga**, a Rashtrakuta chief, **overthrew his Chalukya overlord** |
| **The legitimation** | He performed a ritual called ***HIRANYA-GARBHA*** — literally **"the golden womb"** |
| **What it did** | Performed with the help of **Brahmanas**, it was thought to lead to the **"rebirth" of the sacrificer as a Kshatriya** — ⭐ **even if he was not one by birth** |

> ⭐⭐ ***Hiranya-garbha* is the single most important term in this chapter.** Understand what it reveals: **Kshatriya status was not purely hereditary — it could be acquired**, provided you had the power to command Brahmanas to certify it.
>
> 🔑 **The bargain, again:** the ruler gets caste legitimacy; the Brahmanas get gifts, land and confirmation that **only they can confer such status**. This is the same king–priest exchange you saw in the Ashvamedha (Class 6 Ch.5) and in temple patronage (Ch.1 of this book).

### ⚔️ Brahmanas who became kings ⭐

Two men NCERT names explicitly — both **Brahmanas who gave up their traditional professions and took to arms**:

| Person | Dynasty founded | Region |
|---|---|---|
| **Mayurasharman** | **Kadamba** | **Karnataka** |
| **Harichandra** | **Gurjara-Pratihara** | **Rajasthan** |

> 🎯 **NCERT's question is the exam-relevant one:** *was being born a Kshatriya important in order to become a ruler in this period?* **The answer the evidence gives is no.** Military success plus ritual certification did the job. Excellent for a Mains point about social mobility in early medieval India.

## 2️⃣ Administration in the Kingdoms

### Grand titles, shared power

New kings adopted **high-sounding titles**:

| Title | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***Maharaja-adhiraja*** | **Great king, overlord of kings** |
| ***Tribhuvana-chakravartin*** | **Lord of the three worlds** |

> ⚠️ **But NCERT immediately punctures this:** *"in spite of such claims, they often **shared power** with their samantas as well as with **associations of peasants, traders and Brahmanas**."*
>
> 🎯 **This is the key analytical point of the section.** Early medieval kingship was **not absolute**. The grand title and the actual distribution of power point in opposite directions — and reading the title as evidence of the power is exactly the mistake the prashasti section warns about.

### 💰 Resources and taxation

Resources were obtained from **producers — peasants, cattle-keepers, artisans** — who were *"persuaded or compelled"* to surrender a part of what they produced. This was claimed as **"rent" due to a lord who asserted that he owned the land.** Revenue was also collected from **traders**.

**⭐⭐ Four hundred taxes!**

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Chola inscriptions refer to **more than 400 terms** for different kinds of taxes |
| ⭐ **VETTI** — the most frequently mentioned; taken **not in cash but as FORCED LABOUR** |
| ⭐ **KADAMAI** — **land revenue** |
| Others | Taxes on **thatching the house**, on **the use of a ladder to climb palm trees**, a **cess on succession to family property** |

> 🧠 **The palm-tree-ladder tax is worth remembering** — it's the detail that makes the scale of medieval fiscal reach concrete and memorable. Over 400 categories means an administration that had thought very hard about extracting value from every activity.

**What the revenue funded:** the king's establishment, and the **construction of temples and forts**. Kings also **waged war**, expected to yield **plunder** and access to **land and trade routes**.

**Who collected it:** functionaries recruited from **influential families**, and **positions were often hereditary** — true of the army as well. In many cases **close relatives of the king** held these positions.

> 💻 **Hereditary office again.** As in Class 6 Ch.9, paying officials with position rather than salary means the state gradually loses the ability to reassign them. Cheap to run, impossible to reform.

## 3️⃣ Prashastis and Land Grants

### 📜 Prashastis

> A ***prashasti*** contains details that **may not be literally true.** But it tells us **how rulers wanted to depict themselves** — as valiant, victorious warriors. Composed by **learned Brahmanas**, who occasionally helped in the administration.

**⭐ The Gwalior prashasti of Nagabhata** (a **Pratihara** king), in **Sanskrit**, found in **Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh**, claims he defeated the kings of:

| Claimed defeated | Region |
|---|---|
| **Andhra** | Andhra |
| **Saindhava** | Sind |
| **Vidarbha** | Part of Maharashtra |
| **Kalinga** | Part of Odisha |
| **Chakrayudha** | Ruler of **Kanauj** |
| **Vanga** | Part of Bengal |
| **Anarta** | Part of Gujarat |
| **Malava** | Part of Madhya Pradesh |
| **Kirata** | Forest peoples |
| **Turushka** | **Turks** |
| **Vatsa, Matsya** | Kingdoms in north India |

> 🔗 **Same genre as Class 6 Ch.9's Prayag Prashasti** of Samudragupta. Same caution applies: **facts usable, evaluations not.**

### 📋 Land grants on copper plates

Kings rewarded **Brahmanas** with grants of land, **recorded on copper plates** given to the recipient. The plates were **held together by a ring secured with the royal seal**, to indicate an authentic document.

**⭐ What a Chola land grant actually conveyed** — this excerpt is worth knowing because it shows the *scope* of what was handed over:

```
  BOUNDARIES marked by earthen embankments and thorny bushes.

  THE LAND CONTAINS:
    fruit-bearing trees | water | land | gardens and orchards
    trees | wells | open spaces | pasture-land | a village
    anthills | platforms | canals | ditches | deep lakes

  THE RECIPIENT MAY:
    -> COLLECT TAXES from it (including on betel-leaves,
       woven cloth, and vehicles)
    -> build large rooms with UPPER STOREYS of baked brick
    -> get large and small WELLS dug
    -> plant trees and thorny bushes
    -> construct CANALS for irrigation
    -> (and must ensure water is not wasted, and
        embankments are built)
```

> 🎯 **Read what's really being transferred:** not just soil, but **the right to tax, to build, to irrigate — and a village with people in it.** This is the transfer of governance itself, which is exactly why land grants hollowed out central authority over time.

### 📖 Kalhana's *Rajatarangini* ⭐⭐

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | A Sanskrit **poem containing the history of the kings who ruled over Kashmir** |
| **Author** | **Kalhana** (12th century) |
| **Sources he used** | **Inscriptions, documents, eyewitness accounts and earlier histories** |
| ⭐ **Why he matters** | **Unlike the writers of prashastis, he was often CRITICAL about rulers and their policies** |

> ⭐ **This is a high-frequency Prelims fact and a great Mains point.** Kalhana is usually described as the closest thing early India has to a critical historian — he used multiple source types and evaluated rulers rather than praising them. When a question contrasts "eulogy" with "history", *Rajatarangini* is the example.

## 4️⃣ Warfare for Wealth

### 🏰 The Tripartite Struggle ⭐⭐⭐

```
                    THE PRIZE: KANAUJ
                 (in the Ganga valley)
                          ^
                          |
        +-----------------+-----------------+
        |                 |                 |
   GURJARA-            RASHTRAKUTAS       PALAS
   PRATIHARAS          (western Deccan)   (Bengal)
   (Gujarat &
    Rajasthan)

   Because there were THREE parties, historians call it
   the "TRIPARTITE STRUGGLE".
```

**Why Kanauj?** It commanded the **fertile Ganga valley** — the richest agricultural region, and the symbolic key to north Indian paramountcy (the same role it plays in Class 6 Ch.9, after Harsha).

> 🔗 **Direct continuity with Class 6 Ch.9** — the tripartite struggle was introduced there. Same three powers, same prize. **If you can narrate it from both books, you've genuinely joined the two.**

### 🛕 Temples as targets

> Rulers demonstrated power and resources by **building large temples**. So when they attacked one another's kingdoms, **they often chose to target temples, which were sometimes extremely rich.**

> 🔑 **The logic to grasp:** a temple was simultaneously a **religious centre, a treasury, and a statement of the ruler's legitimacy.** Destroying or looting one struck all three at once. **Temple attacks in this period were as much political and economic as religious** — an important nuance, and one NCERT is making deliberately.

### ⚔️ Mahmud of Ghazni ⭐⭐

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **From** | **Ghazni, Afghanistan** |
| **Raids** | **17 times**, between **1000 and 1025** ⭐ |
| **Targets** | **Wealthy temples**, including **Somnath in Gujarat** ⭐ |
| **What he did with the wealth** | Used it to create a **splendid capital city at Ghazni** |

> ⚠️ **A note on motive, handled carefully.** The current NCERT text says he raided "with a religious motive"; older editions and most academic scholarship emphasise **plunder** — the wealth funded Ghazni, and he did not attempt to hold Indian territory or convert it. **For an exam, state the facts (17 raids, 1000–1025, Somnath, wealth taken to Ghazni) and note that motives are debated between iconoclasm and plunder.** That is both accurate and safe.

> 💡 **Worth knowing beyond the text:** Mahmud's court patronised **al-Biruni**, the Central Asian scholar who wrote the ***Kitab-ul-Hind*** (*Tahqiq-i Hind*) — a remarkably careful study of Indian society, science and religion. It's a standard Prelims name and is not in the rationalised chapter, so learn it here.

### 🐎 The Chahamanas and Prithviraja III ⭐⭐

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who** | The **Chahamanas**, later known as the **Chauhans** |
| **Where** | The region around **Delhi and Ajmer** ⭐ (their two major cities) |
| **Expansion** | Tried to expand **west and east** — opposed by the **Chalukyas of Gujarat** and the **Gahadavalas of western Uttar Pradesh** |
| **Best-known ruler** | **PRITHVIRAJA III (1168–1192)** |
| ⭐⭐ **The two battles** | **Defeated** Sultan **Muhammad Ghori** in **1191** — but **lost to him the very next year, in 1192** |

> 🧠 **Memory hook: "91 win, 92 loss."** Tarain 1191 and Tarain 1192 (the battle names aren't in this chapter but are standard). **The 1192 defeat opens the door to Chapter 3** — the Delhi Sultanate begins directly from it.

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ A Closer Look: THE CHOLAS

**The most important section of the chapter.**

### 📈 From Uraiyur to Thanjavur

```
   MUTTARAIYAR
   (a minor chiefly family holding power in the KAVERI DELTA,
    subordinate to the PALLAVA kings of KANCHIPURAM)
             |
             |  mid-NINTH century: VIJAYALAYA, of the
             |  ancient Chola family from URAIYUR,
             |  CAPTURES the delta from them
             v
   VIJAYALAYA  -> builds the town of THANJAVUR
                  and a temple for the goddess NISHUMBHASUDINI
             |
             v
   His successors conquer neighbouring regions;
   PANDYAN and PALLAVA territories (south and north)
   absorbed into the kingdom
             |
             v
   RAJARAJA I  -- the most powerful Chola ruler; became king in 985;
                  expanded control and REORGANISED the administration
             |
             v
   RAJENDRA I  -- continued his father's policies;
                  expeditions to the GANGA valley, SRI LANKA and
                  SOUTH-EAST ASIA; developed a NAVY
```

> ⭐ **Key names to fix:** **Muttaraiyar** (displaced), **Pallavas of Kanchipuram** (their overlords), **Vijayalaya** (founder, from **Uraiyur**, built **Thanjavur** + **Nishumbhasudini** temple), **Rajaraja I** (985, most powerful), **Rajendra I** (Ganga + naval expeditions).

### 🛕 Splendid temples and bronze sculpture ⭐⭐⭐

| Temple | Built by |
|---|---|
| **Thanjavur** (Brihadeshwara) | **Rajaraja I** |
| **Gangaikondacholapuram** | **Rajendra I** |

Both are described as **"architectural and sculptural marvels"** — note the **tapering roof (vimana)** and the **elaborate stone sculptures decorating the outer walls**.

**⭐⭐ Why temples mattered economically — this is the exam point:**

```
   A CHOLA TEMPLE WAS NOT ONLY A PLACE OF WORSHIP.

   +-------------------------------------------------------+
   |  Temples became the NUCLEI OF SETTLEMENTS that grew    |
   |  around them -> centres of CRAFT PRODUCTION            |
   |                                                        |
   |  Temples were ENDOWED WITH LAND by rulers and others   |
   |            |                                           |
   |            v                                           |
   |  The PRODUCE of that land MAINTAINED the specialists   |
   |  who worked at the temple and lived near it:           |
   |                                                        |
   |    priests | garland makers | cooks | sweepers         |
   |    musicians | dancers                                 |
   +-------------------------------------------------------+
                            |
                            v
      "Temples were the centres of ECONOMIC, SOCIAL and
       CULTURAL life."
```

> 💻 **A temple was an endowed institution running a local economy** — land generating perpetual income, supporting a permanent staff with specialised roles, anchoring a settlement, and producing crafts. Compare the *shreni* endowments of Class 6 Ch.8: same financial instrument, larger scale.

**⭐ CHOLA BRONZES**

> Amongst the crafts associated with temples, **the making of bronze images was the most distinctive. Chola bronze images are considered amongst the finest in the world.** While most images were of deities, **sometimes images were made of devotees as well.**

> 🎯 **Very high Art & Culture yield.** Made by the **lost-wax (*cire perdue*) process** — the same technique as the Harappan Dancing Girl (Class 6 Ch.3), which makes a lovely 3,000-year continuity point. The **Nataraja** (Shiva as lord of dance) is the iconic Chola bronze. **Learn: Chola + bronze + lost-wax + Nataraja.**

### 💧 Agriculture and irrigation

The **Kaveri** branches into several small channels before emptying into the Bay of Bengal, **overflowing frequently and depositing fertile soil**. Water from the channels provides moisture for agriculture, **particularly rice**.

| The work required |
|---|
| **Forests cleared** in some regions; **land levelled** in others |
| In the delta, **embankments** built to prevent flooding, and **canals** constructed to carry water |
| Where nature wasn't enough: **wells dug**, and **huge tanks constructed to collect rainwater** |
| ⭐ A **sluice gate** — a wood or metal barrier controlling water levels — regulated flow from a tank into irrigation channels (a **ninth-century** example survives in Tamil Nadu) |

> 🔑 **NCERT's point about irrigation is institutional, not technical:** *"irrigation works require planning — **organising labour and resources, maintaining these works, and deciding how water is to be shared**."* **Water management is a governance problem.** Both rulers and villagers took an active interest. **This is a strong, concrete example for a Mains answer on water governance.**

### 🗳️ ⭐⭐⭐ The Administration of the Empire

**Settlement hierarchy:**

| Unit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***UR*** | A **settlement of peasants**, which became prosperous with the spread of irrigated agriculture |
| ***NADU*** | **A group of such villages** |
| ***SABHA*** | Assembly of a **brahmadeya** village (see below) |
| ***NAGARAM*** | **Associations of traders**, which occasionally performed administrative functions in towns |

The **village council** and the **nadu** performed several administrative functions — **dispensing justice and collecting taxes.**

**Rich peasants exercised considerable control** over the affairs of the *nadu*, under the supervision of the central Chola government. The kings gave rich landowners **titles as markers of respect**, and entrusted them with important offices:

| Title | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***Muvendavelan*** ⭐ | "A **velan** (peasant) serving **three kings**" |
| ***Araiyar*** | **Chief** |

**⭐⭐ The five categories of land** (from Chola inscriptions):

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***vellanvagai*** | Land of **non-Brahmana peasant proprietors** |
| ***brahmadeya*** | Land **gifted to Brahmanas** |
| ***shalabhoga*** | Land for the **maintenance of a school** ⭐ |
| ***devadana*, *tirunamattukkani*** | Land **gifted to temples** |
| ***pallichchhandam*** | Land **donated to Jaina institutions** ⭐ |

> 🧠 **Memory hooks:** *shala*-bhoga → *shala* = **school**. *Palli*-chchhandam → *palli* = a **Jaina** (or Buddhist) establishment. *Vellan*-vagai → *vellalar* were the **non-Brahmana landowners** (Class 6 Ch.8!).

Each **brahmadeya** was looked after by a **sabha** of prominent **Brahmana landholders**, whose decisions were **recorded in detail in inscriptions, often on the stone walls of temples.**

### 🏛️ ⭐⭐⭐ The Uttaramerur Inscription

**From Uttaramerur, Chingleput district, Tamil Nadu.** You met this in **Class 6 Ch.9** — here is the fuller detail.

**How the sabha was organised:** it had **separate committees** to look after **irrigation works, gardens, temples**, etc.

**The selection method — *kudavolai*:**

```
   1. Names of those eligible are written on SMALL TICKETS
      OF PALM LEAF
   2. The tickets are put into an EARTHENWARE POT
   3. A YOUNG BOY is asked to draw the tickets out,
      ONE BY ONE, for each committee
```

**⭐ The rules, as the inscription lays them down:**

| ✅ To be a member you must | ❌ You are disqualified if |
|---|---|
| Be an **owner of land from which land revenue is collected** | You have been **a member of any committee in the last three years** ⭐ |
| Have **your own home** | You (**or your relatives**) **have not submitted your accounts** ⭐ |
| Be **between 35 and 70 years of age** | |
| Have **knowledge of the Vedas** | |
| Be **well-versed in administrative matters and honest** | |

> 🎯 **Why this earns marks in a governance answer:** eligibility criteria, an **anti-incumbency/rotation rule**, and **mandatory audit enforced by disqualification** — in a tenth-century village inscription.
>
> ⚠️ **And the honest caveat, as always:** membership required being a **male, propertied, Veda-learned landholder**. **Sophisticated local self-government, not universal franchise.** NCERT itself asks the pointed question: *"Do you think women participated in these assemblies?"*
>
> 🔗 **The full lineage for GS2:** Vajji gana-sangha (Cl.6 Ch.5) → Buddhist sangha procedure (Cl.6 Ch.6) → **Chola sabha / Uttaramerur** (Cl.6 Ch.9 + here) → 73rd/74th Amendments.

### 👥 ⭐ What the inscriptions leave out — the *Periyapuranam*

NCERT makes a deliberate methodological point by setting a **twelfth-century Tamil work, the *Periyapuranam***, against the inscriptions.

> *"While inscriptions tell us about kings and powerful men,"* the *Periyapuranam* **informs us about the lives of ordinary men and women.**

Its description of a hamlet of **Pulaiyas** — a name used for a social group considered **"outcastes" by Brahmanas and Vellalas** — records:

- Small huts under old thatches, thresholds covered with strips of leather
- **Agrarian labourers engaged in menial occupations**
- Children wearing **bracelets of black iron**
- A **female labourer putting her baby to sleep on a sheet of leather**
- **Pulaiyar men** called to work by cocks crowing before dawn
- **Pulaiya women singing as they husked paddy**

> ⭐⭐ **NCERT's own question is the exam-worthy one: *"Why do you think temple inscriptions ignore these activities?"***
>
> **Because inscriptions were commissioned by those with the means and the motive to commission them** — kings, Brahmana landholders, temples. The labourers who husked the paddy and built the embankments had neither. **This is Class 6 Chapter 1's "silence of the sources" lesson, restated with a concrete case.** It is outstanding material for any Mains answer on marginalised histories or on the limits of epigraphic evidence.

**Traps:**
- **"Kshatriya birth was essential to become a ruler"** → **FALSE.** *Hiranya-garbha*; Brahmana founders
- **"The sabha was the assembly of an ordinary village"** → **FALSE.** That's the ***ur***; *sabha* = **brahmadeya**
- **"Prithviraja III defeated Muhammad Ghori and was never defeated by him"** → **FALSE.** Won **1191**, lost **1192**
- **"Kalhana wrote a prashasti praising Kashmir's kings"** → **FALSE.** He was **critical** of rulers
- **"Shalabhoga was land granted to temples"** → **FALSE.** Land for a **school**
- **"Mahmud of Ghazni established an empire in India"** → **FALSE.** He **raided** and withdrew

### Mains

- **Art & Culture ⭐⭐⭐:** *"Chola bronzes represent the high point of Indian metal sculpture."* — lost-wax technique, Nataraja, temple context, devotee images
- **Art & Culture:** Chola temples as **economic and social institutions**, not merely religious ones
- **Polity/Governance ⭐⭐⭐:** **Uttaramerur** and Chola local self-government as the lineage of Panchayati Raj — with the exclusion caveat
- **State formation:** *"Early medieval kingship was shared rather than absolute."* Use samantas, associations of peasants/traders/Brahmanas, and hereditary offices
- **Source criticism ⭐:** the **inscription vs *Periyapuranam*** contrast — what epigraphy systematically omits

## 🔁 90-Second Recap

1. **Samantas became kings.** Gaining wealth and power, they took titles like *maha-mandaleshvara* and declared independence.
2. **Dantidurga (Rashtrakuta)** overthrew his **Chalukya** overlord in the mid-8th century and performed ***hiranya-garbha*** — the "golden womb" ritual that conferred **Kshatriya status on someone not born to it**.
3. **Brahmanas founded kingdoms too** — **Mayurasharman** (Kadamba) and **Harichandra** (Gurjara-Pratihara).
4. Kings took grand titles but **shared power** with samantas and with associations of peasants, traders and Brahmanas.
5. **Over 400 tax terms** in Chola inscriptions; **vetti** = forced labour, **kadamai** = land revenue. Offices were **hereditary**.
6. **Prashastis** show how rulers wished to be seen (**Nagabhata**, Gwalior). **Kalhana's *Rajatarangini*** is the opposite — **critical** of rulers.
7. **Land grants on copper plates** transferred not just soil but **the right to tax, build and irrigate**.
8. **Tripartite struggle:** **Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas, Palas** for **Kanauj**. Temples were targeted because they were **rich and symbolically central**.
9. **Mahmud of Ghazni:** **17 raids, 1000–1025**, **Somnath**, wealth to Ghazni.
10. **Prithviraja III** beat **Muhammad Ghori in 1191** and **lost in 1192** — which opens Chapter 3.
11. **Cholas:** **Vijayalaya** (from Uraiyur) took the Kaveri delta from the **Muttaraiyar**, built **Thanjavur**; **Rajaraja I** (985) reorganised the empire; **Rajendra I** sent expeditions to the Ganga, Sri Lanka and South-East Asia and built a **navy**.
12. **Chola temples** (Thanjavur, Gangaikondacholapuram) were **economic and social centres** with endowed land supporting priests, cooks, musicians and dancers. **Chola bronzes are among the finest in the world.**
13. **Administration:** ***ur*** (peasant village), ***nadu*** (group), ***sabha*** (brahmadeya), ***nagaram*** (traders); five land categories including ***shalabhoga*** (school) and ***pallichchhandam*** (Jaina).
14. **Uttaramerur:** committee members drawn by **lot from a pot**, with property, age (**35–70**), Vedic and honesty qualifications, a **3-year bar** and **compulsory accounts**.
15. **The *Periyapuranam*** records the **Pulaiya labourers** whom the inscriptions ignore — the chapter's own lesson in the silence of sources.
