# UPSC Mains — History Questions (Previous Year Questions)

Descriptive History and World History questions from the UPSC Civil Services **Main** examination, GS Paper I — the paper the syllabus formally assigns History, Art & Culture, Society and World History to. One file per year.

> **Companion set:** the objective Prelims MCQs live in [`../03_PYQ_Prelims/00_INDEX.md`](../03_PYQ_Prelims/00_INDEX.md) — 239 questions, 2013–2026. Prelims tests recall and precision; Mains tests argument. Use both.

## 📌 Sourcing & fidelity

- Most years (2015–2025) came from sources that render the full 20-question GS1 paper inline, verbatim, with confirmed marks and word limits.
- **2013 and 2014** used a 25-question × 10-mark format (200 words in 2013, 150 words in 2014) — different from the 10-mark/15-mark split used from 2015 onward. Both years' questions came from a topic-wise compilation without original paper Q-numbers preserved, so those two files group questions by period instead of by number.
- **2019** carries a numbering caveat of its own — see the note at the top of [2019.md](2019.md).
- A **"borderline" tag** marks questions that genuinely span History and another subject (most often Polity's States Reorganisation / princely-integration material, or Class 9's World History chapters) — these are kept in this folder (since GS1 is where they were actually asked) but cross-linked to the other subject's fuller treatment rather than re-explained here.
- Unlike the Prelims MCQs, a Mains question stem carries no answer key to get wrong — the main fidelity risk is **paraphrasing the directive verb or word limit**, which changes what the question actually demands. Verbatim wording was checked wherever a second source was available.

## 🏷️ What comes up most (across the 87 compiled questions)

| Theme | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Deindustrialisation / colonial economic policy** | Asked from multiple angles almost every year — railways' socio-economic effects (2023), famines (2022), the Industrial Revolution's impact on Indian handicrafts (2024, 2017), general colonial economic policy (2014). Cross-link: [Spectrum file 12](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Modern_India_Spectrum/Notes/12_Topup_Tables.md), Part G. |
| **Temple architecture and dynastic art patronage** | Gupta/Chola heritage (2022), Chola architecture (2024), Chandella sculpture (2025), medieval temple sculpture and social life (2022), Krishnadeva Raya as patron (2016) — a standing high-frequency theme. |
| **Gandhi, compared or contextualised** | Vs Tagore (2023), vs Ambedkar (2015), vs Bose (2016), his constructive programme (2021), his contemporary relevance (2018) — Gandhi is rarely asked "about" directly; almost always in comparison or application. |
| **The transition from Moderates to mass movement** | The Moderates' failure/role (2017, 2021), the 1920s' new ideological strands (2020, 2017) — GS1 repeatedly asks candidates to periodise the freedom struggle's phases, not just narrate it. |
| **Post-independence consolidation as its own theme** | Princely-state integration (2021, 2013 via Bhoodan/Jai Jawan Jai Kisan), 1950s-60s foreign policy episodes (Tashkent 2013, Suez 2014), and a direct "trace India's consolidation" question (2025) — confirms [Spectrum files 04/08/09](../02_Standard_Reference_Books/Modern_India_Spectrum/Notes/) are genuinely load-bearing for Mains, not just Prelims trivia. |
| **World History as a standing 1-question fixture** | Almost every year carries exactly one World History question — French Revolution (2025), WWI (2024), interwar democracy (2021), Industrial Revolution/WWI-WWII (2015), anti-colonial global context/Suez/Lenin (2014), Meiji Japan/Scramble for Africa/American Revolution/Depression (2013, four in one year). Don't skip the [Class 9 History notes](../../01_NCERT/Class_09_India_and_Contemporary_World_I/Notes/) — they are Mains-relevant, not just background reading. |

## 📖 How to use this set

1. **Write full answers under time pressure**, not just outlines — Mains rewards structure and word-limit discipline as much as content.
2. **Use the cross-links.** Nearly every question here points to the specific Spectrum file or NCERT theme with the fuller material already written for it.
3. **Watch the "borderline" questions.** They're a signal that a topic (states reorganisation, princely integration) needs both a History and a Polity answer register — practise switching between them.
4. **Notice the repeats in theme, not wording.** Deindustrialisation, temple architecture, and "the Moderates' failure" each recur across multiple years in different phrasing — a well-prepared answer bank on these four or five themes covers a disproportionate share of the paper.
