## 📖 The source — and why it is this book

**`Textbook_PDF/Modern_India_Bipan_Chandra_1971_NCERT.pdf`** — *Modern India*, **Bipan Chandra**, published by **NCERT**, first edition **June 1971**; editorial board **S. Gopal (chief editor), S. Nurul Hasan, Satish Chandra, Romila Thapar**.

✅ **Sourced legitimately** from the Internet Archive item `history-modern-india-bipan-chandra-1971-ncert`, in the **JaiGyan / ArvindGupta** collections — the same basis as the withdrawn `ncert-*` editions used for Classes 6, 9 and 12. **It is a government-published school textbook.**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why this rather than Spectrum.** *Before Spectrum existed, **"old NCERT Bipan Chandra" WAS the standard UPSC recommendation for Modern India**, and many candidates still prefer it for Mains because it **argues** rather than lists. ⭐ **Spectrum's real advantage is exam-shaped granularity** — complete tables of Acts, Viceroys, Congress sessions, revolts. **That content is public record, not Spectrum's to own**, so it is rebuilt here as verified fact-tables (see §3 below). **Nothing examinable is lost.**

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TWO HONEST CAVEATS ABOUT THIS SOURCE — read them before trusting a lone fact.**
> 1. ⭐ **It is a 1971 OCR scan.** The text layer is good enough to write from *(16,312 lines extract cleanly)*, but **there are garbled patches, especially in headings and Devanagari.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Cross-check any date, figure or proper noun that appears only once** — the same discipline that caught three OCR date errors in the [Class 9 supplement](../../01_NCERT/Class_09_India_and_Contemporary_World_I/Notes/06_Supplement_Peasants_Farmers_and_Clothing.md).
> 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It ends at Independence and it is fifty years old.** *No post-1947 integration of states, no historiography written since, and its framing of some questions has been superseded.* **Where that matters, it is flagged in the file and corrected from the Class 12 notes, which are written from the current edition.**
