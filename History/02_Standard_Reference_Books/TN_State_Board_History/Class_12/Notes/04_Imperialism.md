# 💰 PART A — THE RISE OF IMPERIALISM

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ From mercantilism to monopoly capitalism

⭐⭐⭐ **The sequence:** **Mercantilism (1600-1700)** — state-merchant interests fused, Holland/France/England became colonial powers → **Adam Smith's *Wealth of Nations* (1776)** attacked mercantilist control, advocating free trade *(the term "laissez-faire" itself coined by French businessman de Gournay)* → **England adopted free trade, 1833**, followed by Belgium, Netherlands, Norway, Piedmont, Portugal, Spain, Sweden → **free competition capitalism gave way to monopoly capitalism** — Marx's own observation that *"little business was either crushed by big business or merged with it."* Partnerships/joint-stock companies gave way to **trusts** (US-style, controlling raw material to finished product) and **cartels** (German-style, restricting competition).

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Hobson's definition, and the mechanics of monopoly-driven empire

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **John A. Hobson's definition, worth quoting exactly:** *"Imperialism is the endeavour of the great controllers of industry to broaden the channel for the flow of their surplus wealth by seeking foreign markets and foreign investments to take off the goods and capital they cannot sell or use at home."*

**The mechanism:** monopoly profits created a capital surplus → invested abroad in colonial infrastructure (railways, electricity, roads) → loans from the "mother country" were often **tied** — England's loans to India for railways required rails/engines to be **purchased in England**, profiting English manufacturers twice over. **This alliance of industry and finance seeking markets for both goods and capital was imperialism's essential character.**

⭐⭐⭐ **Protectionism's political fallout:** the mid-1870s depression (Vienna and New York crashes) hurt domestic producers when cheap grain flooded markets. **Germany's Tariff Act (1879)** triggered a general shift to protectionism; Britain revoked free-trade treaties with France/Germany (1898). **Arms races followed directly from this economic friction.**

# 🗺️ PART B — THE SCRAMBLE FOR COLONIES

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Africa — from 10% to nearly 100% colonised in 25 years

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The scale, stated starkly:** in **1876, barely 10% of Africa** was under European rule; **by 1900, practically the whole continent** was colonised.

| Year | Event |
|:--:|---|
| **1876** | **King Leopold II of Belgium**, via his International African Association, seized the rubber-rich **Congo** |
| **1881** | France occupied **Tunis** *(angering Italy, which had wanted it)* |
| **1883** | Britain secured control of **Egypt** |
| **1884-90** | Germany acquired **Togoland, Cameroons, German South-West Africa, German East Africa** |
| **1891-95** | France took the **Ivory Coast (1891), Dahomey (1892), Madagascar (1895)** |

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Only three African territories escaped annexation entirely: Liberia, Morocco *(until later)*, and Abyssinia (Ethiopia).** New weapons — **breech-loading rifles and Gatling machine guns** — gave European armies a decisive military edge in the resulting bloody conquests.

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Asia — spheres of influence, and Japan's rise

⭐⭐⭐ Britain, France, Russia and Germany carved **"spheres of influence" in China**; **Japan took Korea and Taiwan**; **France conquered Indo-China**; the **US seized the Philippines from Spain (1898)**; Britain and Russia agreed to **partition Iran**.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Japan's rapid rise to imperial power — a genuine standalone story

| Event | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Sino-Japanese War (1894-95)** | Japan crushed China; **Treaty of Shimonoseki** gave Japan Formosa, Port Arthur, Liaotung — but the **"Triple Intervention"** (France, Britain, Russia, 1895) forced Japan to give up Liaotung, which it deeply **resented** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Russo-Japanese War (1904-05)** | Japan **allied with England (1902)**, then defeated Russia — the **Treaty of Portsmouth** (US-mediated) returned Port Arthur to Japan. **This victory brought Japan into the "charmed circle of the Great Powers"** — the first Asian power to defeat a European one in the modern era |
| **1910** | Japan **annexed Korea**, using a diplomat's assassination as pretext |
| **1915** | Japan presented **China's Twenty-One Demands** — transfer of German rights in Shantung, recognition of Japanese hold on Manchuria, Japanese advisers in the Chinese government — **China conceded most of them** |

# ⚔️ PART C — THE ROAD TO WORLD WAR I

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The alliance system, step by step

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Bismarck's original architecture (1870s):** feared French revenge for Alsace-Lorraine, sought to isolate France. His **Three Emperors' League** (Germany-Austria-Russia, 1873) failed, but he secured a firm **Austrian alliance**, expanded into the **Triple Alliance with Italy, 1882**.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The counter-bloc forms:**

| Year | Alliance |
|:--:|---|
| **1894** | **Franco-Russian secret military pact** — mutual aid if attacked by Germany/Austria/Italy |
| **1902** | **Anglo-Japanese Alliance** — Britain abandoned its "splendid isolation" |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1904** | **Entente Cordiale** *(Britain-France)* — France got a free hand in Morocco in exchange for recognising British Egypt |
| **1907** | **Anglo-Russian agreement** — division of Persia into spheres of influence |

**By 1907: the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) vs the Triple Entente (Britain, France, Russia).**

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Pre-war crises, 1905-1913

| Crisis | Year | What happened |
|---|:--:|---|
| **First Morocco Crisis** | 1905 | France treated Morocco as its protectorate; Germany protested; a European conference at **Algeciras** settled nothing but exposed Britain siding with France |
| **Agadir Crisis** | 1911 | Germany sent the gunboat *Panther* to Agadir; backed down under British pressure, but extracted **French concessions in West Africa** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Bosnian Crisis** | 1908 | Austria-Hungary **annexed Bosnia-Herzegovina** *(populated by Serbians)*; Serbia appealed to Russia, which was too weak (post-1905 defeat) to intervene — **stoking future resentment** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Balkan War I** | 1912 | The **Balkan League** (Greece, Serbia, Bulgaria, Montenegro) drove Turkey out of Europe; **Treaty of London (1913)** created Albania and divided Macedonia |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Balkan War II** | 1913 | The victors quarrelled over Macedonia — Bulgaria attacked Serbia/Greece and lost; **Treaty of Bucharest** ended it. **Bulgaria felt injured; Serbia's confidence, and Bosnia's anti-Austrian militancy, both grew** |

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The immediate trigger and the declarations of war

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **28 June 1914, Sarajevo:** **Archduke Franz Ferdinand**, heir to Austria-Hungary, assassinated by **Gavrilo Princip**, a Bosnian-Serb student. Austria blamed Serbia, issued an ultimatum demanding unconditional surrender (rejected/negotiated); **28 July** — Austria declared war on Serbia, bombarded Belgrade.

**The domino sequence:** Russia mobilised for Serbia → **Germany struck first**, declaring war on Russia (1 Aug) and France (3 Aug) → **Germany invaded Belgium** to reach France → Britain, honouring its old policy against any great power controlling the Belgian coast, issued an ultimatum → **4 August: Britain and Germany at war.**

⭐⭐⭐ **The blocs:** **Central Powers** — Germany, Austria-Hungary, Turkey, Bulgaria. **Nine opposing states** — Russia, France, Britain, Italy, USA, Belgium, Serbia, Romania, Greece. *(Italy stayed neutral until May 1915, then joined the Entente for promised Austrian/Turkish territory.)*

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Course of the war — the key battles

| Battle/Campaign | Year | Significance |
|---|:--:|---|
| **Battle of the Marne** | Sept 1914 | France stopped the German advance ~20 miles from Paris — **saved the capital**, ended hopes of a quick German victory |
| **Battle of Tannenberg** | 1914 | **Von Hindenburg** inflicted heavy Russian losses on the Eastern Front |
| **Gallipoli/Dardanelles Campaign** | 1915-16 | A **failed** Allied attempt to open a Russia-supply sea route; heavy Allied casualties |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Verdun and the Somme** | 1916 | Enormous attrition battles — **~2 million** fought at Verdun alone; decided the war's fortunes toward the Allies despite the horror |
| **Battle of Jutland** | May 1916 | The **largest naval battle** of WWI, off Denmark — inconclusive |
| **Battle of Cambrai** | Nov-Dec 1917 | First large-scale use of **tanks** — 340 British tanks surprised the Germans |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Sinking of the *Lusitania*** | May 1915 | German U-boat sank the British liner, killing **128 Americans** — a key step toward US entry |

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **America's entry:** intensified unrestricted submarine warfare finally pushed **Woodrow Wilson** (neutral for ~3 years) to declare war, **April 1917** — America's resources made Allied victory *"a foregone conclusion."*

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Eastern Front collapse:** Russia's **February 1917 Revolution** overthrew the Tsar; Soviet Russia sought peace, signing the **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (3 March 1918)** with Germany — freeing German troops for the Western Front, but too late to change the outcome.

⭐⭐⭐ **German collapse:** allies deserted one by one (Bulgaria first, then Turkey, then Austria's Emperor Charles signed an armistice, 3 Nov 1918); **Kaiser Wilhelm II abdicated and fled to Holland**; a provisional government under socialist **Friedrich Ebert** signed the **armistice, 11 November 1918.**

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Paris Peace Conference and the Treaty of Versailles

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Wilson's Fourteen Points** framed the peace — key items: open diplomacy, freedom of the seas, arms reduction, national self-determination, **Alsace-Lorraine to France**, **an independent Poland with sea access**, **a League of Nations**.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Versailles' main provisions against Germany

| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Territory** | **Alsace-Lorraine to France**; Saar coal mines to France (League-administered till a 1935 plebiscite); **Poland recreated**, with a corridor to Danzig; all overseas colonies became **League mandates** |
| **Military** | Army capped at **100,000**; Rhineland demilitarised; **no submarines, no air force** |
| **War guilt and reparations** | Germany + allies held responsible; **reparations fixed at $33 billion (1921)** |
| ⚠️ **Kaiser's trial** | Provided for, but **never enforced** — the Netherlands refused to extradite him |
| **New states in the east** | **Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania** created from territory Russia had ceded to Germany under Brest-Litovsk |

⭐⭐⭐ **Separate treaties** with Austria (St Germain), Hungary (Trianon), Bulgaria (Neuilly), Turkey (Sèvres — **failed**, resisted by Kemal Pasha).

# 🕊️ PART D — THE LEAGUE OF NATIONS

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Structure and the seeds of failure

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Wilson's brainchild** — five bodies: the **Assembly** (all members, unanimous decisions), the **Council** (executive; Britain/France/Italy/Japan/USA as permanent members, each with veto), the **Secretariat** (Geneva, first Secretary-General **Sir Eric Drummond**), the **Permanent Court of Justice** (The Hague), and the **International Labour Organisation**.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The founding flaw, stated immediately:** the **USA never joined**; Germany (defeated) and Russia were excluded at first, joining only in **1926 and 1934** respectively — **Germany resigned in 1933, Russia was expelled in 1939.**

### Early successes (1920-25)

⭐⭐⭐ Aaland Islands dispute (Sweden-Finland) resolved for Finland · Upper Silesia frontier (Poland-Germany) resolved · Greece-Bulgaria dispute (1925) — Greece ordered to pay reparations. Also credited with reducing the opium trade, refugee resettlement, and early international-law codification.

### ⚠️ Failures, in sequence

| Year | Failure |
|:--:|---|
| 1920 | Poland's seizure of **Vilna** — unaddressed |
| 1923 | Italy-Greece war threat — **Italy simply refused League mediation** |
| 1931 | Japan invaded **Manchuria** — League condemned it; **Japan resigned** |
| 1935-37 | Italy invaded **Ethiopia**; League sanctions applied; **Italy resigned in protest (1937)** |
| 1932 | The long-delayed **Disarmament Conference** rejected German arms parity — **Hitler withdrew Germany from both the Conference and the League** |
| **1939** | League's **last decisive act** — expelled Russia for attacking Finland. **Dissolved in 1946.** |

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why it failed, in the source's own analysis:** it looked like *"an organisation of the victors"*; **unanimity requirements** paralysed decision-making; it had **no military force of its own**; and it fundamentally **underestimated the power of nationalism**.

# 🇷🇺 PART E — THE RUSSIAN REVOLUTION *(delta only — full narrative at [Cl.9 Ch.2](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_09_India_and_Contemporary_World_I/Notes/02_Socialism_in_Europe_and_the_Russian_Revolution.md))*

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Points worth adding to Class 9's account

⭐⭐⭐ **"Nihilists" — a term Class 9 may not use:** young radical intellectuals waging *"merciless war against the Russian state"* — rejected all authority (state, church, family), grounding belief purely in **scientific truth**. First target: an unsuccessful attempt on Tsar Alexander II's life.

⭐⭐⭐ **Marx's own scepticism about Russia:** Marx and Engels never envisaged a successful socialist revolution in **"backward Russia"** — they thought the bourgeoisie was as necessary a precondition as the proletariat. **Lenin's organisational genius, not orthodox Marxist theory, made the Russian Revolution happen.**

⭐⭐⭐ **Lenin's precise party history:** at the 1903 London Congress of the Social Democratic Party (43 delegates), Lenin's faction won a narrow majority (*bolshinstvo* → **Bolsheviks**); the minority (*menshinstvo*) became **Mensheviks**.

⭐⭐⭐ **International impact, beyond Class 9's India focus:** communist parties formed worldwide; the USSR *"encouraged colonies to fight for their freedom"*; the source notes — worth citing for a "USSR vs capitalism" comparison — that the **Great Depression made no impact on the USSR**, while illiteracy/poverty were *"eliminated in record time."*

# 📉 PART F — THE GREAT DEPRESSION

## 1️⃣2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mechanics and global political fallout

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **How WWI set up the crash:** America profited immensely during WWI (Europe fighting, America capturing markets and investing abroad) — but European debt to America was enormous, and US capital exported abroad needed time to yield returns. When the US stock market crashed, **America recalled its foreign capital**, collapsing export volumes worldwide.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The crash itself: 24 October 1929** — mass share-selling with **no buyers**, American bank failures, forced recall of foreign-invested funds, **US loan stoppage to Germany caused two major German banks to fail**, and even the **Bank of England went technically bankrupt** from its own foreign lending exposure.

⭐⭐⭐ **The vicious cycle:** nations responded with **protectionism and currency devaluation**, which forced creditors to stop lending — a **worldwide credit contraction**. Hence "Great Depression": deep and prolonged.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Political fallout, country by country

| Country | Effect |
|---|---|
| **England** | Labour Party **defeated, 1931** |
| **USA** | Republican Party **rejected for ~20 years** of elections |
| **Italy, Germany** | **Fascist parties seized government** |
| **Argentina, Brazil, Chile** | Governments changed |

# 🖤 PART G — THE RISE OF FASCISM

## 1️⃣3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Fascist Italy — a total gap, told in full

⭐⭐⭐ **WWI's toll on Italy:** 5.5 million mobilised, **~700,000 killed**; huge financial cost, unpopular with socialists and pro-Austrian Catholics alike; **Versailles' territorial gains felt marginal** to nationalists — inflation, speculation, protests followed.

⭐⭐⭐ **The socialist surge that provoked the reaction:** Italian socialists joined the Communist International (1918); won **a third of Chamber seats (Nov 1919)**; rural **"Red Leagues"** broke up estates and forced rent cuts. **To landowners, Fascism looked like "a less dangerous form of radicalism"** — a defence against confiscation, not a threat.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mussolini's rise, step by step

⭐⭐⭐ **Benito Mussolini (1883-1945)** — a blacksmith's son, trained as a schoolteacher, became a **socialist journalist**, editor of *Avanti* (the leading socialist daily) — then joined the newly founded **Fascist Party (1919)**. Fascism drew support from **industrialists, nationalists, ex-soldiers, the middle class and disaffected youth**, and used street violence freely.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **October 1922** | The **March on Rome** by black-shirted Fascists — **King Victor Emmanuel III invited Mussolini to form a government**, impressed by the show of force |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1924 elections** | Fascists won **65%** amid intimidation; socialist **Matteotti**, who questioned the election's fairness, was **murdered**; opposition boycotted parliament |
| **1926** | Mussolini took the title **Il Duce**, banned strikes and lockouts, organised unions/employers into state-supervised **corporations** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Lateran Treaty, 1929** | Mussolini recognised the **Vatican City as an independent state**; in return the Church recognised the Kingdom of Italy, Catholicism became Italy's state religion, and school religious instruction became compulsory |
| **1938** | **Parliament abolished**, replaced by a Fascist Party/corporation body — completing Mussolini's dictatorship |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1935-36, Ethiopia** | Facing Depression-era unemployment his public-works programme (bridges, roads, hospitals) couldn't solve, Mussolini **invaded Ethiopia** to build an "economic empire" and distract from domestic troubles — his success **drew admiration from a rising Munich figure: Adolf Hitler** |

⭐ *(⚠️ **Etymology worth remembering:** "Fascism" derives from the Latin ***fasces*** — an axe bound in a rod-bundle, the ancient symbol of Roman state authority.)*

## 1️⃣4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Nazi Germany *(delta only — full narrative at [Cl.9 Ch.3](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_09_India_and_Contemporary_World_I/Notes/03_Nazism_and_the_Rise_of_Hitler.md))*

⭐⭐⭐ **Points worth adding:** the **Weimar Republic's** own timeline — socialist **Ebert** led 1919-1925, liberal-democrat **Stresemann** governed until his **1929 death**, which created a political vacuum right as the Depression hit. The **1923 French occupation of the Ruhr** (over reparations) triggered worker strikes that gave underground movements — including Hitler's Munich-based National Socialist German Workers' Party — room to organise. Industrialists, bankers and **Junkers** (Prussian landed aristocracy) pressured President **von Hindenburg** to make Hitler Chancellor (1933), believing they could **control him** — a fatal miscalculation. **Joseph Goebbels'** propaganda maxim, worth quoting: *"any lie frequently repeated will ultimately gain belief."*

### 🔴 Prelims traps

| ⚠️ The claim that looks right | ✅ The truth |
|---|---|
| *Hobson's definition of imperialism centres on national prestige and glory* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It centres entirely on ECONOMICS — surplus capital and goods seeking foreign outlets, not glory** |
| *By 1900, most of Africa remained independent* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The opposite — only Liberia, Morocco (at the time) and Abyssinia remained unannexed by 1900** |
| *Japan's rise to Great Power status came from defeating China alone* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The DECISIVE moment was defeating RUSSIA (1904-05) — a European power — not China (1894-95)** |
| *The assassination of Franz Ferdinand alone caused WWI* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It was the IMMEDIATE trigger, but the alliance system, the Balkan Wars, and a decade of crises (Morocco, Bosnia) built the conditions — a classic "necessary but not sufficient cause" trap** |
| *The Kaiser Wilhelm II was tried and punished under the Treaty of Versailles as planned* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This provision was never enforced — the Netherlands refused to extradite him** |
| *The League of Nations included the USA as a founding permanent member* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The USA, despite being Wilson's own brainchild, NEVER joined the League at all** |
| *The Great Depression primarily affected only industrial nations* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Agriculture was hit HARDEST by falling prices — a global effect, not confined to industry** |
| *Mussolini began his political career as a Fascist* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He began as a SOCIALIST journalist and editor of Avanti, the leading socialist daily — before joining the Fascist Party in 1919** |
| *The Lateran Treaty (1929) separated Church and State in Italy* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The opposite — it recognised the Vatican as independent BUT made Catholicism Italy's state religion and compulsory in schools** |
