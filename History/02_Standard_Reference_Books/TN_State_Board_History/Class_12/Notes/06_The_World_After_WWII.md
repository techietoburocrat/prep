# 🧊 PART A — THE COLD WAR

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The bipolar world's origin

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Post-war Europe:** ~60 million dead overall, 6 million Jews in the Holocaust, devastated cities, Britain/France's prestige "badly undermined." Many European states turned to **welfare-state capitalism**; distressed masses in **Greece and Turkey** leaned toward communism; by 1948 the USSR had installed left-wing governments across Eastern Europe (**Romania, Bulgaria, Hungary**; **Yugoslavia under Tito** already communist by its own elections).

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The term's coinage, precisely:** **George Orwell** first coined "Cold War"; **Bernard Baruch**, US presidential adviser, popularised it in an **April 1947** speech to describe US-USSR relations.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two founding speeches:**

| Speech | Speaker | Date | Significance |
|---|---|:--:|---|
| **The "Long Telegram"** | George Kennan | 22 Feb 1946 | Argued the USSR saw no possibility of peaceful coexistence — proposed **"containment"** of communism |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Iron Curtain" speech** | Winston Churchill | March 1946, Fulton, Missouri | *"From Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic, an iron curtain has descended across the continent."* **Considered the symbolic start of the Cold War** |

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Berlin — the divided city:** Germany split into US/UK/French/USSR zones; by 1948 the three Western zones merged and (with Marshall Plan aid) grew rapidly, provoking the **Berlin Blockade (June 1948)** — USSR cut all road/rail links to West Berlin; the West responded with the **Berlin Airlift**, ~11 months of supply flights, until the USSR lifted the blockade (May 1949). **August 1949 — Federal Republic of Germany (West); October 1949 — German Democratic Republic (East).** *"If the division of Germany marked the real beginnings of the Cold War, the reunification of Germany in 1990 signalled the end of Cold War."*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Six Cold War strategies

### (a) Economic Aid

| Plan | Power | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Truman Doctrine, 1947** | USA | Britain, unable to fund anti-communist forces in **Greece and Turkey**, withdrew — the US stepped in, committing financial/military aid wherever communism was "ascendant" — **the doctrine that defined US Cold War policy till its end** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Marshall Plan, 1947-52** | USA | *"Our Policy is directed not against any country or doctrine but against hunger, poverty, desperation and chaos"* — **$13 billion** pumped into Western Europe over four years; USSR's **Molotov** dubbed it *"dollar imperialism"* |
| **Molotov Plan, 1949** | USSR | The Soviet counter-package; **Cominform** (1947) linked European communist parties; **Comecon** coordinated USSR-bloc economic policy |

### (b) Military Pacts

| Pact | Founded | Members/Aim |
|---|:--:|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NATO** | 1949 | Grew from the **Brussels Treaty** (Britain, France, Benelux) to include the USA, Italy, Canada, Iceland, Denmark, Norway, Ireland, Portugal — **an attack on one is an attack on all**, but **only within Europe/North America**, excluding colonial conflicts |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Warsaw Pact** | May 1955 | USSR's response to **West Germany joining NATO** — USSR + Albania, Poland, Romania, Hungary, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Bulgaria |
| **SEATO** | Sept 1954 | US, France, Britain, NZ, Australia, **Philippines, Thailand, Pakistan** — an "Asian NATO" against communism after **China turned communist (1949)**; **only 2 of 8 South-East Asian states actually joined**; disbanded 1977 after Vietnam |
| **CENTO (from the Baghdad Pact)** | 1955 | Iraq-Turkey pact, joined by Britain/Pakistan/Iran; **Iraq left in 1958**, renamed CENTO; **Iran withdrew after its 1979 Revolution**; disbanded 1979 |
| **ANZUS** | 1951 | US-Australia-New Zealand, against communism's spread from China to Korea |

### (c)-(f) Propaganda, Espionage, Brinkmanship, Surrogate Wars

⭐⭐⭐ **Propaganda:** pro-American values via film/TV/music vs Soviet promotion of "collective work" and dismissal of democracy as a *"façade to mask exploitative capitalism."*

⭐⭐⭐ **Espionage:** **CIA (1947, USA)** vs **KGB (1954, USSR)** — mutual intelligence-gathering that fuelled popular spy fiction (James Bond).

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Brinkmanship:** coined from **John Foster Dulles'** 1956 remark — *"if you are scared to go to the brink [of war], you are lost."* Textbook case: **Cuban Missile Crisis, 1962** — USSR placed nuclear missiles in Cuba, US responded with a naval blockade; resolved by negotiation.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Surrogate/proxy wars:** **Korean War (1950-53)** and the **Vietnam Wars (1955-75)** — USSR backed the northern communist regimes, USA the southern ones in each case; both **"turned out to be civil wars as well."**

# 🌍 PART B — THIRD WORLD COUNTRIES AND NON-ALIGNMENT

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Bandung Conference and NAM's origins *(delta — India-specific detail at [Spectrum file 09](../../Modern_India_Spectrum/Notes/09_The_Nehru_Years.md))*

⭐⭐⭐ **"Third World"** was coined by French demographer **Alfred Sauvy (1952)**; **"non-alignment"** by **V.K. Krishna Menon at the UN, 1953**.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Bandung Conference, 1955, Indonesia:** **29 states** (Egypt, Indonesia, India, Iraq, People's Republic of China among them) adopted a *"Declaration on Promotion of World Peace and Cooperation"* — including **Nehru's Panchsheel** and ten guiding principles that fed directly into NAM.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **First NAM Summit: Belgrade, 1961** — five founding figures: **Nehru (India), Sukarno (Indonesia), Nasser (Egypt), Nkrumah (Ghana), Tito (Yugoslavia)**. Objectives: end imperialism/colonialism, promote peace/disarmament, end racism — and explicitly **deprecated military pacts with great powers or foreign military bases on member soil**.

# 🕊️ PART C — THE UNITED NATIONS AND GLOBAL DISPUTES

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Structure and founding

⭐⭐⭐ **Founding sequence:** Moscow Declaration (1943, recognised the need to replace the League) → **Dumbarton Oaks (Aug-Oct 1944)** — China, USSR, US, UK drafted proposals → Yalta (Feb 1945) — Security Council voting settled → **San Francisco Conference (April 1945)** finalised the Charter → **UN founded, 24 October 1945, 51 members.**

⭐⭐⭐ **Six main organs:** General Assembly, Security Council, Economic and Social Council, Trusteeship Council, International Court of Justice, Secretariat. **First Secretary-General: Trygve Lie (Norway).** Key specialised agencies: **ILO (Geneva), FAO (Rome), IMF (Washington), UNESCO (Paris), WHO (Geneva), World Bank (Washington).**

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The structural weakness, stated directly:** *"the birth of UNO coincided with the beginning of the Cold War"* — the UN **prevented wars successfully** in many disputes, but **"in disputes involving the permanent members of the Security Council, the UNO was a mute spectator."**

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Case studies — the UN in action (and inaction)

### Korean War, 1950-53

⭐⭐⭐⭐ Korea split at the **38th parallel** (1945) — North (USSR-backed, **Kim Il Sung**) vs South (US-backed, **Syngman Rhee**). North invaded South, **25 June 1950**. With the USSR **boycotting** the Security Council (over China's UN seat), the Council authorised UN forces under **General MacArthur**; **16 nations contributed troops**. USSR's return led the US to pass the **"Uniting for Peace" resolution** — letting the **General Assembly** act if the Security Council is deadlocked. **Armistice, July 1953.**

### Suez Crisis, 1956

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Egypt's **Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal** (July 1956); Israel invaded Sinai, then Britain/France bombed and landed troops. **Britain and France vetoed** Security Council action, so the **General Assembly** (at US initiative) condemned the invasion and created the **UN Emergency Force (UNEF)** — the first UN peacekeeping force. Israel/Britain/France withdrew; **Nasser emerged a hero of pan-Arab nationalism.**

### Hungarian Crisis, 1956

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ Premier **Imre Nagy**, reformist, introduced a multiparty system; the USSR crushed the rising by force (**4 November 1956**). The USSR **vetoed** Security Council action; the General Assembly's resolution demanding withdrawal had **no effect** — *"showed that if a Great Power was determined to defy the UNO... the UN was helpless."* **Coincided exactly with the Suez Crisis.**

### Palestine and the Arab-Israeli Wars

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **UNSCOP (1947)** recommended partition — though Arabs held ~85% of the land, the plan gave **55% to the Jewish state**. **Israel declared independence, 14 May 1948.** **1967 "Six Day War":** Israel occupied the **West Bank, Gaza, East Jerusalem, Golan Heights, Sinai** — the UN resolution called for Israeli withdrawal but *"referred to Palestinians only... as refugees,"* a reflection of **US influence** on the drafting. The **PLO (1964, Yasser Arafat)** later became the main Palestinian voice.

# 🇪🇺 PART D — EUROPEAN INTEGRATION

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ From coal and steel to the euro

| Stage | Year | Detail |
|---|:--:|---|
| **OEEC** | 1948 | Disbursed Marshall Aid among 16 states |
| **Council of Europe** | May 1949 | Ten states, London — Strasbourg HQ |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Schuman Plan → ECSC** | 1950-51 | French FM **Robert Schuman** proposed pooling France-Germany coal/steel under a supranational **High Authority**; West Germany's **Konrad Adenauer** welcomed it; **France, West Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg** signed, 1951. **Britain refused to join.** Steel output rose ~50% in five years |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EEC (Treaty of Rome)** | 1957 | The same six formed the **European Economic Community/Common Market**, Brussels HQ; free movement of goods/services/capital/labour, a **Common Agricultural Policy** |
| **EFTA** | 1960 | Britain's **rival, weaker** bloc (Britain, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Switzerland, Austria, Portugal) — no common policies, no supranational authority |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Britain's entry** | 1973 | After **de Gaulle blocked it in 1961**, Britain (with Ireland, Denmark) finally joined |
| **Single European Act** | 1987 | Weighted voting by population; strengthened the **European Parliament** (functioning since 1952) |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Maastricht Treaty → EU** | 1991/93 | Established the **European Union** with a single market, plus common foreign policy/internal security; paved the way for the **euro** |
| ⚠️ **Brexit** | 2017 | Britain voted to leave the EU |

⭐ *(The **OEEC → OECD** transition, 1961, added the USA/Canada, then Japan (1964) — now 37 members, Paris HQ, committed to free-market democracy.)*

# ☭ PART E — THE END OF THE COLD WAR

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Détente, then Gorbachev's reforms

⭐⭐⭐ **Détente (late 1960s-70s):** eased US-USSR tension — **SALT (1972, 1979)** and later **START (1991)** arms-limitation treaties. Underlying driver: by 1969 the USSR had **nuclear parity** with the US, making **Mutual Assured Destruction (MAD)** a shared fear, and the arms race a shared fiscal burden.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gorbachev (President from March 1985)** — two linked reforms:

| Reform | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Perestroika** | "Restructuring" — loosened central economic control, letting businesses/farmers decide production/pricing |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Glasnost** | "Openness/transparency" — reduced Communist Party's grip, allowed multicandidate elections, permitted criticism of officials and freer media — *"Soviet citizens no longer had to worry about arrest and exile for articulating negative opinions"* |

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 1989-91 — the collapse, month by month

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1989, "a watershed year":** free elections in **Poland** — the **Solidarity movement** routed the Communists; Gorbachev, at the Council of Europe, **explicitly rejected the Brezhnev Doctrine**: *"Any interference in domestic affairs... are inadmissible."* **November 1989 — the Berlin Wall fell.** West German Chancellor **Helmut Kohl** unilaterally proposed **German reunification**. By year's end, **Eastern Europe's Communist governments fell everywhere except Bulgaria.**

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Boris Yeltsin's rise:** a Communist Party insider (from 1961) whom Gorbachev himself elevated to fight Moscow corruption (1985) — turned reformist critic of Gorbachev's *"slow pace"* — elected **President of the Russian Republic, May 1990**, against Gorbachev's wishes; became the **first popularly elected leader** after the USSR's fall.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The end: 8 December 1991** — the Soviet Union **disintegrated**; **25 December 1991** — Gorbachev **resigned**; Yeltsin became President of the Russian Republic. **The Cold War ended with it.**

## ⚡ UPSC FOCUS

### 🔴 Prelims traps

| ⚠️ The claim that looks right | ✅ The truth |
|---|---|
| *The term "Cold War" was coined by Winston Churchill* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It was coined by GEORGE ORWELL, popularised by Bernard Baruch — Churchill's "Iron Curtain" speech is a separate, though related, landmark** |
| *NATO's collective defence covers attacks on any member's colonial territories* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Explicitly excluded — it applies ONLY to attacks in Europe/North America** |
| *SEATO had wide South-East Asian membership* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Only the Philippines and Thailand, of the region's own states, actually joined — most SEATO members were outside powers** |
| *The UN General Assembly cannot act if the Security Council is deadlocked* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The "Uniting for Peace" resolution (Korean War era) explicitly created this workaround** |
| *The UN successfully forced Soviet withdrawal from Hungary in 1956* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It failed completely — the USSR simply vetoed/ignored it, proving the UN "helpless" against a defiant great power** |
| *Britain was a founding member of the European Coal and Steel Community* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Britain REFUSED to join the ECSC (1951) and the EEC (1957); it only entered in 1973** |
| *Glasnost and Perestroika mean the same thing* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Perestroika = economic restructuring; Glasnost = political openness/transparency — distinct, linked reforms** |
| *The Soviet Union collapsed immediately after the Berlin Wall fell* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Wall fell in November 1989; the USSR itself did not dissolve until December 1991 — a two-year gap** |

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CONTAINMENT** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **George Kennan's strategy of stopping communist expansion without direct war — the intellectual basis of the Truman Doctrine** |
| **BRINKMANSHIP** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Pushing a crisis to the edge of war to extract concessions — John Foster Dulles' term** |

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"COLD WAR" WAS COINED BY GEORGE ORWELL, NOT CHURCHILL — CHURCHILL GAVE THE "IRON CURTAIN" SPEECH INSTEAD**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BERLIN AIRLIFT (1948-49) KEPT WEST BERLIN ALIVE FOR 11 MONTHS AGAINST A SOVIET LAND BLOCKADE**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NATO'S COLLECTIVE DEFENCE EXPLICITLY EXCLUDES COLONIAL TERRITORY — A GENUINE PRELIMS TRAP**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE WARSAW PACT WAS A DIRECT RESPONSE TO WEST GERMANY JOINING NATO, NOT AN INDEPENDENT INITIATIVE**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "UNITING FOR PEACE" RESOLUTION LET THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY BYPASS A DEADLOCKED SECURITY COUNCIL — BORN OUT OF THE KOREAN WAR**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SUEZ (1956) AND HUNGARY (1956) HAPPENED SIMULTANEOUSLY BUT HAD OPPOSITE UN OUTCOMES — SUCCESS AGAINST BRITAIN/FRANCE, FAILURE AGAINST THE USSR**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BRITAIN REFUSED TO JOIN EUROPEAN INTEGRATION AT EVERY EARLY STAGE (ECSC, EEC) BEFORE FINALLY JOINING IN 1973 — AND LEAVING AGAIN IN 2017**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DÉTENTE WAS DRIVEN BY MUTUAL FEAR OF NUCLEAR PARITY (BY 1969) AND THE FISCAL COST OF THE ARMS RACE, NOT IDEOLOGICAL SOFTENING**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PERESTROIKA (ECONOMIC) AND GLASNOST (POLITICAL) WERE GORBACHEV'S TWO DISTINCT REFORMS — GLASNOST IS THE ONE THAT ENABLED THE COLLAPSE**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BERLIN WALL FELL IN NOVEMBER 1989, BUT THE USSR ITSELF SURVIVED UNTIL DECEMBER 1991 — TWO SEPARATE EVENTS, TWO YEARS APART**
