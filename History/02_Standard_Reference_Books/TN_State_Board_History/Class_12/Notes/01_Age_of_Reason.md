# 🎭 PART A — THE ITALIAN RENAISSANCE

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why Italy, and how it spread

⭐⭐⭐ **Why Italy first:** Italian cities sat at the **centre of Mediterranean trade**, making them fabulously rich by the 14th century — Florence under the **Medici family** (patrons of Michelangelo and Leonardo) and Milan under the **Sforza family** were the two prosperous engines. The **1453 fall of Constantinople** sent Byzantine scholars fleeing west, carrying classical manuscripts — **Giovanni Aurispa alone brought back ~250 manuscripts** (Sophocles, Euripides, Thucydides) between 1413-1423. **Popes themselves patronised the movement** — Nicholas V, Pius II, Julius II, Leo X commissioned the era's greatest artists.

⭐⭐ **Byzantium's second gift:** paper (Chinese-origin, reaching Germany by the 14th century) plus **Johannes Gutenberg's movable-type printing press** — the combination that let Renaissance ideas spread "swiftly."

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Renaissance in Literature

| Figure | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Dante (1265-1321)** | *Divine Comedy* — salvation through reason and divine grace, but also human love and *"the desire for a free and united Italian nation"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Petrarch (1304-1374)** | Considered the **"father of Italian Renaissance literature"**; rediscovered Cicero's letters — a key moment in the 14th-century revival |
| **Boccaccio (1313-1375)** | *Decameron* — 100 stories told by ten young Florentines fleeing the **Black Death** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Niccolò Machiavelli (1469-1527)** | *The Prince* — the ruler's supreme obligation is state power and safety, **"no consideration of justice or mercy... should stand in his way."** *"A Prince must know how to play at once man and beast, lion and fox... it is very disadvantageous always to be honest; useful... to appear pious and faithful."* — political realism as its own doctrine |

⭐⭐⭐ **English Renaissance, under Elizabeth I (1558-1603) — the "Elizabethan Age":** **William Shakespeare** (38 plays — comedies: *As You Like It*, *The Taming of the Shrew*; tragedies: *Othello*, *Hamlet*, *King Lear*, *Romeo and Juliet*); **Christopher Marlowe** (*Dido, Queen of Carthage*; *Tamburlaine the Great*); **Francis Bacon**, father of empiricism — *inductive reasoning* as the base of scientific knowledge, argued in his *Novum Organum*.

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Renaissance in Art

| Artist | Signature works |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Leonardo da Vinci (1452-1519)** | *Virgin of the Rocks*, *Last Supper*, *Mona Lisa* — self-taught, dissected corpses to study anatomy, **first to discover blood circulation**; **"considered a great example of the Renaissance Man"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Raphael (1483-1520)** | *Madonna and Child*; *School of Athens* — a fresco for Pope Julius II highlighting classical influence on Renaissance art, in which Raphael painted himself alongside Leonardo and Michelangelo |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Michelangelo (Michelangelo di Lodovico Buonarroti Simoni, 1475-1564)** | The **dome of St Peter's Basilica**, the statue of **David**, the **Sistine Chapel ceiling**, the **Pietà** *(carved from a single block of Carrara marble)* |

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Renaissance in Science and Technology

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Scientific Revolution's core clash — heliocentrism vs the Church:**

- **Nicolaus Copernicus (1473-1543)**, a Polish scientist, propounded the **heliocentric theory** (Sun at the centre) against the Church's own **geocentric** doctrine — he **postponed publishing** *On the Revolutions of the Celestial Spheres* almost until his death, fearing the Church's reaction.
- **Giordano Bruno**, an Italian, was **burned at the stake in Rome (1600)** for insisting the Earth orbits the Sun.
- **Galileo Galilei (1564-1642)** — discovered Jupiter's satellites, Saturn's rings and sunspots via telescope; **tried for heresy and kept under house arrest** for accepting Copernicus's view.
- **William Harvey (1578-1657)** — finally proved the **circulation of blood** in the human body.

# ⛵ PART B — DISCOVERY OF NEW SEA ROUTES

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why Europe went looking for a sea route to the East

⭐⭐⭐ **The trigger:** the **Turkish conquest and 1453 fall of Constantinople** cut off the old land-route dependence on Ottoman-controlled trade.

**Eight contributing causes**, per the source: (a) Crusade-era exposure to eastern goods, especially **spices as food preservatives**, previously funnelled through Arab-controlled land/maritime trade; (b) the profit motive — European states wanted to **control** sea-borne trade themselves, not just buy from Italian middlemen; (c) *"let fools contest elections and the adventurous go and explore"* — an age spirit of ambition; (d) the spread of Christianity, especially during the **Counter-Reformation**; (e) **cartography advances** — Gerardus Mercator's latitude/longitude techniques on Ptolemy's redrawn maps; (f) the **caravel**, a lighter ship suited to shallow water; (g) firearms and cannons reducing voyage hazard; (h) the **Mariner's compass**, freeing sailors from dependence on clear skies.

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The voyages, one by one

| Explorer | Sponsor | Achievement |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Prince Henry the Navigator** | Portugal | *Never sailed himself* — founded a **navigation school at Sagres**; his captains reached the **Guinea Coast (1442)**; **Bartholomew Diaz rounded the Cape of Good Hope (1488)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Christopher Columbus (1451-1506)** | Spain *(Ferdinand and Isabella)* | Sailed **3 August 1492** with the *Santa Maria*, *Pinta* and *Niña*; reached the Americas believing it was India, hence **"Indians"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Vasco da Gama** | Portugal | 1497 voyage; reached **Kapad, near Kozhikode, Kerala** — opened direct India trade, leading eventually to **Goa's colonisation** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Pedro Cabral** | Portugal | 1500 — discovered **Brazil** *("Island of the True Cross")*; followed Da Gama's route to Kozhikode; after conflict with Arab traders, established a trading post at **Cochin**, then **Cannanore** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Ferdinand Magellan** | Spain | 1519 — found the **Strait of Magellan**, named the **Pacific Ocean** *("Pacifico" = peaceful)*; **killed in the Philippines**; his ship the ***Vittoria* was the first to circumnavigate the globe (1522)** |
| **John Cabot** | England | Saw Canada, claimed it for England |
| **Giovanni da Verrazano** | France | Annexed provinces in eastern Canada |
| **Henry Hudson** | England | Failed to find the Pacific passage; the **Hudson River** bears his name |

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Papal Bull (1493) and the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494):** Spain, anxious about Portugal's head start, got **Pope Alexander VI** to draw a north-south demarcation line **320 miles west of Cape Verde** — everything west belonged to Spain. Portugal renegotiated the following year, shifting the line to **1,185 miles west of Cape Verde** — everything east (including, as it turned out in 1500, **Brazil**) belonged to Portugal.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **The catastrophic conquest:** **Hernán Cortés** conquered the **Aztec Empire (Mexico)** for Spain with a handful of men — firearms, steel, and a **smallpox epidemic** that devastated the Aztecs while Cortés regrouped. **Pizarro** crossed the Isthmus of Panama (1530) and destroyed the **Inca Empire (Peru)**.

⭐⭐⭐ **The Spanish Armada, 1588:** rising English competition (Francis Drake's California claim) led **Philip II of Spain** to send an armada of **130 vessels, 31,000 soldiers** against England — destroyed by the more manoeuvrable English fleet, marking **England's rise as a modern power**.

# 💰 PART C — THE COMMERCIAL REVOLUTION

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Definition, causes, effects

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Definition:** the transition from the *"semi-static, localised, non-profit economy of the late Middle Ages to the dynamic, world-wide, capitalistic regime"* of the 14th century onward — gradual, not sudden.

**Five causes:** (a) Italian capture of Mediterranean trade; (b) the Italian-Hanseatic League trade link with northern Europe; (c) new coinage (Venice's *duca*, Florence's *florin*); (d) surplus accumulation from trade/shipping/mining; (e) new monarchs encouraging commerce for **taxable wealth**.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Results

| Result | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Growth of banking** | Usury was religiously disapproved in the Middle Ages, but by the 14th century Italian commercial houses normalised profit-lending; spread to Germany/France; **Bank of Sweden (1657)**, **Bank of England (1694)** |
| **New business organisation** | **Regulated companies** (e.g. the English Merchant Adventurers, trading with the Netherlands/Germany) gave way in the 17th century to the **joint-stock company** — a **Dutch innovation**, spreading risk/profit across many investors, with **limited liability** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Rise of the middle class** | Merchants, bankers, ship-owners, investors and industrial entrepreneurs rose to economic power, **allying with the king against the feudal aristocracy** |
| ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Revival of slavery** | Slavery had *"virtually disappeared"* from Europe by the first millennium's end, but colonial mining/plantation demand revived it — attempts to enslave Native Americans **failed** *(they "proved too tough to manage")*, so the demand was met by **importing Africans** — **the transatlantic slave trade exported over 11 million Africans**, which the source itself calls *"a sordid story that is a shame on the making of the modern world"* |
| **Mercantilism** | A doctrine of **state intervention** to grow national prosperity and treasury wealth — not just trade volume for its own sake |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Seedbed for the Industrial Revolution** | Created a capitalist class and a mercantilist stimulus to manufacturing — **cotton textile factory production is the outstanding example** |

# ⛪ PART D — THE PROTESTANT REFORMATION AND THE CATHOLIC COUNTER-REFORMATION

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Causes of the Reformation

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Corruption of Church authorities:** the sale of **indulgences** (buying papal pardon for sin), **nepotism**, and **simony** (selling church posts) — Albert of Mainz bought the archbishopric from Pope Leo X, who admitted half the money would fund St Peter's Basilica. Members of great families like the **Medicis became Popes** to enrich their own line. Clergymen drew income from multiple churches while never visiting any (**absenteeism**). The peasantry saw the Church as an **oppressive landowner**; princes eyed its vast property.

⭐⭐⭐ **Early dissenters, two centuries before Luther:** **John Wycliffe**, an English clergyman, was the **first translator of the Bible into English** — his bones were dug up and burned in 1415, thirty-one years after his death, on Church orders. His ideas reached **John Huss** in Bohemia, head of Prague University — excommunicated, then **burned alive at Constance despite a promised safe conduct**.

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Three reformers, three cities

| Reformer | City | Core position |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Martin Luther (1483-1546)** | Wittenberg | Nailed **95 Theses** to the church door after a disillusioning visit to Rome; **Bible alone is supreme**, not Pope/Bishops; only **baptism and Holy Communion** as valid rituals; **salvation by faith alone**; translated the **Bible into German**; permitted priests to marry |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Huldrych Zwingli (1484-1531)** | Zurich | Influenced by Erasmus; disagreed with Luther on the Eucharist (Christ lives **"in the heart of the believer," not in the bread and wine**); wrote 67 reformist articles; opposed monastic celibacy, indulgence, fasting, pilgrimage |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **John Calvin (1509-1564)** | Geneva | A French Protestant, excommunicated for opposing the Church; settled in Geneva and effectively **controlled the city**; *Institutes of the Christian Religion*; Calvinism spread as **Huguenots (France), Puritans (England), Presbyterians (Scotland)** |

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Diet of Worms:** the Pope's attempt to make peace with Luther via a hearing at Worms **failed** — his books were burned, he was **outlawed** by the Emperor. His radical influence reached **Thomas Müntzer**, who fought for a classless society and sparked the **German Peasants' Rebellion** — **Luther himself sided with the feudal lords and denounced the peasants.**

⭐⭐⭐ **The English Reformation — started by a king, not a theologian:** **Henry VIII**, initially a devout Catholic, sought a **divorce from Catherine of Aragon** to remarry Anne Boleyn — the Pope refused, so Henry passed the **Act of Supremacy (1534)**, severing England from Rome, founding the **Anglican Church**, and confiscating monastery lands.

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Effects of the Reformation

| Effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Religious division within nations** | North Germany → Lutheran, South Germany → Catholic; England → Protestant, Scotland/Ireland → Catholic |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Literacy** | Printing spread reformist teachings; vernacular Bible translations opened scripture to ordinary readers |
| **Status of women** | Married clergy (no celibacy) strengthened women's household/church roles; women were encouraged to **read the Bible themselves** |
| **Power of kings** | Henry VIII became head of **both state and Church** |
| **Race for colonies** | Both faiths sought converts — **Jesuit priests** followed Spanish conquest in South America; Puritans, Catholics and Anglicans all set up churches in the **thirteen British colonies** |

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Catholic Counter-Reformation

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Society of Jesus (Jesuits):** founded by **Ignatius of Loyola**, a Spaniard, in **Paris, 15 August 1534** — he and six students (one of them **Francis Xavier**, later a missionary to India and Japan) pledged chastity, poverty, obedience, and a pilgrimage to Jerusalem. The Jesuits used **education** as their instrument to teach Catholicism and raise the Church's own standards.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Council of Trent (1545-1563):** convened by **Pope Paul III**, met three times over eighteen years — reaffirmed **the Bible, the seven sacraments, priestly celibacy, and Papal supremacy**; addressed abuses (indulgences, nepotism, absenteeism); required **catechism** instruction; supported image worship.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Inquisition:** special Church courts punishing heretics via **recantation, flogging, or burning at the stake**. The **Roman Inquisition** targeted Protestants; **witch-hunts** targeted mostly widowed/single women blamed for crop failures and disease. **~110,000 people were tried and ~60,000 put to death.**

⭐⭐⭐ **Effects:** Europe divided along religious lines, sparking wars including the **Thirty Years' War (1618-48)** across the Holy Roman Empire; Protestant churches stayed austere while Catholic churches grew more ornamental; **both sides used education as an instrument of religious spread.**

# 👑 PART E — THE RISE OF NEW MONARCHIES / NATION-STATES

## 1️⃣2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why feudalism declined

| Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Structural weakness** | Kings depended on nobles for arms in exchange for **fiefs** (tax-free land) — nobles had their own sub-vassals and armies, undermining royal control |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The Black Death** | Killed thousands of peasants, **crippling noble tax revenue and workforce**; also weakened the Church's authority since it **could not explain the plague** |
| **New warfare technology** | Gunpowder and the **English longbow** made mounted knights obsolete — visible in the Thirty Years' War |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Church overreach** | The Church's own wealth (tax-exempt land, the **10% tithe**) and its parallel justice system (**ecclesiastical courts**, outside royal reach) rivalled royal power. **Excommunication** could humiliate a king before his own nobles — **Henry IV of Germany was excommunicated by Pope Gregory VII** for defying Church ordinances |

## 1️⃣3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Three nation-states, three routes

| Nation | How it consolidated |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Spain** | **Ferdinand of Aragon married Isabella of Castile** — together they drove out the Moors and unified Spain (power fully consolidated 1479). Devout Catholics, they **forced Moor/Jewish conversion** and set up the **Spanish Inquisition**. Columbus's discoveries and the Inca/Aztec conquests then made Spain **enormously wealthy and politically dominant** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **England** | The **Wars of the Roses** (York vs Lancaster) ended with **Henry Tudor's** victory — he became **Henry VII**, married Elizabeth of York, uniting the factions. He **abolished noble private armies (livery and maintenance)**, created the **Star Chamber** to try rebellious nobles, and used merchant-class support in Parliament to secure taxation rights |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **France** | The **Hundred Years' War** with England (14th-15th c.) — **Joan of Arc** helped Charles VII win at Orléans, was later **captured, tried as a witch, and burned at the stake (1430)**; **canonised in 1920**. Charles VII's son **Louis XI** absorbed the troublesome vassal state of **Burgundy (c.1483)**, built France's **first standing royal army** independent of noble support, and packed his Royal Council with lawyers over nobles |

### 🔴 Prelims traps

| ⚠️ The claim that looks right | ✅ The truth |
|---|---|
| *Machiavelli's "The Prince" argues rulers should govern through justice and mercy* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The opposite — Machiavelli explicitly says no consideration of justice or mercy should stand in the ruler's way; "it is very disadvantageous always to be honest"** |
| *Galileo was persecuted for inventing the telescope* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He was tried for HERESY, for accepting Copernicus's heliocentric theory — the telescope was his evidence, not his crime** |
| *Columbus set out believing he had discovered a New World* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He believed till his death that he had reached INDIA — hence "Indians"; he never accepted it was a new continent** |
| *The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) gave Portugal everything west of the demarcation line* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The reverse — Portugal got everything EAST of the renegotiated line (which is why Brazil, discovered west of the ORIGINAL 1493 line, still fell to Portugal)** |
| *The joint-stock company with limited liability was an English innovation* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It was a DUTCH innovation** |
| *Martin Luther supported the German Peasants' Rebellion his ideas inspired* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He denounced it and sided with the feudal lords against Thomas Müntzer's peasants** |
| *Henry VIII broke from Rome for primarily theological reasons* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It began as a personal matter — his desire to divorce Catherine of Aragon and marry Anne Boleyn** |
| *The Council of Trent abolished priestly celibacy as a Counter-Reformation concession* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It UPHELD priestly celibacy and Papal supremacy — it made no such concession** |
| *Joan of Arc was canonised soon after her death* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **She was burned as a witch in 1430; canonised only in 1920, nearly 500 years later** |

## ✍️ Practice Questions

### Prelims-style
