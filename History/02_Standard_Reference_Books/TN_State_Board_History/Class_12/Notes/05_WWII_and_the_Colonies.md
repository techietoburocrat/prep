# ⚔️ PART A — WORLD WAR II: CAUSES

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Seven causes, working together

| # | Cause |
|:--:|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 1 | **The "unjust" Versailles settlement** — harsh reparations, lost colonies/territory, a reduced army, fed German resentment; Italy felt aggrieved over **Dalmatia going to Yugoslavia**; Austria was **barred from uniting with Germany** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 2 | **The League of Nations' weakness** — an *"alliance of the victors against the vanquished."* Even the **Locarno Pact (1925)** and **Kellogg-Briand Pact (1928)** *(all nations pledging to renounce war "as an instrument of national policy")* had no enforcement teeth |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 3 | **The Great Depression** — intensified economic nationalism; **Japan seized Manchuria (1931)** to secure a market amid collapsing silk/cotton exports |
| ⭐⭐⭐ 4 | **German big business + patriot grievance** — Germany, industrially powerful but colony-less, campaigned to overturn Versailles; the *"living space"* (Lebensraum) grievance was stated in exact figures: the average German had **0.004 sq. mile** vs the average Briton's **~3 sq. miles** of imperial territory |
| ⭐⭐⭐ 5 | **Mussolini's expansionism** — his militarised state-capitalist economy needed raw materials, driving territorial grabs (Ethiopia, eyeing Albania) |
| ⭐⭐⭐ 6 | **Japan's imperial policy** — after a 1936 military coup, Japan eyed the Dutch East Indies, British Malaya/Singapore, French Indochina, and the US Philippines |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 7 | **Hitler's own step-by-step defiance**, in exact sequence *(below)* |

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Hitler's treaty violations, one by one

| Step | Year | Event |
|:--:|:--:|---|
| 1 | **1935** | **Saar plebiscite** — 90% voted to rejoin Germany; incorporated in March 1935 |
| ⭐⭐⭐ 2 | **1935** | Announced **conscription** to over 500,000 men — the **first breach** of Versailles; League condemned but **took no action**; Britain even signed a **naval agreement** letting Germany build to 35% of British naval strength |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 3 | **1936** | **Remilitarised the Rhineland** — France, though militarily stronger, was paralysed by Depression-era political instability |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 4 | **1938 (March)** | **Annexed Austria (Anschluss)** — Chancellor Schuschnigg was given a "Hobson's choice": legalise the Nazi Party or face invasion; **Italy withdrew support** after the Rome-Berlin Axis formed |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 5 | **1938 (Sept)** | **Sudetenland crisis** — the **Munich Agreement** (Britain, France, Germany, Italy) let Germany occupy the Sudetenland — the high point of **"appeasement"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ 6 | **1939** | **Occupied the rest of Czechoslovakia**, using Slovak-Czech tension as pretext — proving Munich's "peace in our time" illusory |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 7 | **Aug 1939** | **Nazi-Soviet (Molotov-Ribbentrop) Pact** — a non-aggression pact with secret clauses dividing Eastern Europe/Poland into German-Soviet spheres |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 8 | **1 Sept 1939** | **Invaded Poland** — Britain's ultimatum was ignored; **WWII began** |

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Historiographical debate, worth citing:** some blame Versailles itself, others blame **appeasement**, others the Western failure to ally with the USSR. **Most historians hold Hitler personally responsible** — *"The Second World War was Hitler's war. He planned it, began it and ultimately lost it."*

# 🌍 PART B — COURSE OF THE WAR

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Europe, 1939-41 — the Blitzkrieg years

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Poland (Sept 1939)** — defeated in two weeks, divided between Germany and the USSR. **Norway (April 1940)** — secured Swedish iron-ore supply routes. **10 May 1940** — invasion of the Netherlands, Luxembourg, Belgium, France — the ***Blitzkrieg*** ("lightning war"). All defeated in **six weeks**.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Dunkirk (May-June 1940):** ~198,000 British + 140,000 Allied (mostly French) troops evacuated under fire — the evacuees formed the nucleus of **General de Gaulle's Free French** government-in-exile.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Battle of Britain (July-Oct 1940):** Hitler's **first defeat** — RAF Spitfires/Hurricanes, aided by the new **radar** technology, defeated the German bombing campaign (the "Blitz" on London, from Sept 1940).

⭐⭐⭐ **22 June 1941 — Operation Barbarossa:** Germany invaded the **Soviet Union**; German forces reached the outskirts of Leningrad and Moscow by year's end — Hitler's empire at its **zenith**.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Nazi occupation's brutality:** **7+ million Europeans** taken as forced labour; one-third of German war costs met by occupied-territory tribute; **the Holocaust** — ~6 million Jews murdered in industrial state-sponsored genocide.

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Asia-Pacific — Pearl Harbor and the turning tide

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **7 December 1941 — Pearl Harbor:** Japan attacked the US Pacific Fleet in Hawaii — instead of demoralising America, it **galvanised** the US into declaring war, making it a genuinely global conflict. Japan then seized **Burma, Malaya, Singapore** (Britain's *"shameful retreat"*) and the **Dutch East Indies**.

| Battle | Date | Significance |
|---|:--:|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Midway** | 4-7 June 1942 | US naval victory — **the turning point** in the Pacific |
| **Guadalcanal** | Aug 1942-Feb 1943 | Combined army-navy offensive; a crushing Japanese defeat |
| **1944** | — | Japanese attempt to invade **north-east India** repulsed by British-Indian forces; Allies then retook **Burma, Malaya, Singapore** |

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Eastern Front and North Africa — the war's real hinge points

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Stalingrad (1942):** Hitler refused a strategic withdrawal even after defeat became clear, fearing the prestige hit — condemning his army to *"cold, starvation and death."* **~20 million Russians died in total** (13 million soldiers, 7 million civilians) across the whole war. **Many historians argue the war's fate was decided at Stalingrad.**

⭐⭐⭐ **El Alamein (North Africa, 1942-43):** British forces defeated **Erwin Rommel's** German-Italian army — leading to Italy's surrender: **King Victor Emmanuel III** (the same king who had handed power to Mussolini in 1922) replaced him with **General Badoglio**; Italy formally surrendered, **3 September 1943**. *(Mussolini was later executed by Italian partisans, though the source only notes his house arrest.)*

⭐⭐⭐ **D-Day and after:** **Casablanca (Jan 1943)** and **Tehran (1943, with Stalin present)** conferences set **6 June 1944** for the Anglo-American Normandy invasion under **General Eisenhower**. **Paris liberated, 25 August 1944.** The **bombing of Dresden (Feb 1945)** — a "terror bombing" that killed much of a German city with limited military value, part of ~600,000 German civilian deaths from Allied bombing.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The end in Europe:** **30 April 1945** — Hitler committed suicide in his Berlin bunker as Soviet forces closed in; **Berlin fell, 2 May 1945.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The atomic bombs and Japan's surrender

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **6 August 1945 — Hiroshima:** 60,000-80,000 killed instantly, ~140,000 dead by year's end. **9 August 1945 — Nagasaki.** Death tolls rose past 200,000 in subsequent decades from radiation-linked cancers. **Japan surrendered immediately** — America had won the race to develop the bomb ahead of Germany.

# 🕊️ PART C — THE PEACE SETTLEMENT

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Atlantic Charter and the post-war conferences

⭐⭐⭐ **The Atlantic Charter** (Roosevelt-Churchill) set the framework — no territorial changes without popular consent, self-determination, equal trade access, disarmament of aggressor states.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Yalta (Feb 1945, Roosevelt/Churchill/Stalin):** planned Germany's unconditional surrender, post-war control mechanisms, and the **United Nations** — with the USA committed to joining this time, unlike 1919. Also agreed the **Bretton Woods system** (World Bank, IMF, GATT) for post-war economic reconstruction.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Potsdam (Churchill→Attlee, Stalin, Truman):** established a **Council of Foreign Ministers** for peace-treaty drafting.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Germany's fate — the key outcome

| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Territory** | East Prussia split — north to USSR, south to Poland; Poland got Danzig |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Occupation zones** | Germany divided into **4 zones** — USSR, Britain, USA, France; **Berlin**, deep in the Soviet zone, similarly quartered |
| **Long-term outcome** | **April 1949** — the **German Democratic Republic** proclaimed in the Soviet zone; the **Federal Republic of Germany** established in the West the same year — **the origin of a divided Germany** |

⭐⭐⭐ **Poland** shifted ~200 miles west, losing territory to the USSR, gaining less from Germany. **Japan** lost all conquests since 1931, including Formosa and its Pacific islands; **Korea became independent.**

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Results — the world remade

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The human cost: ~60 million died.** Warsaw, Kiev, Tokyo, Berlin reduced to rubble.

**Political aftermath, worth remembering as a list:**

⭐⭐⭐ Germany ceased to be a great power; **Europe's global prestige collapsed**, with the **USA and USSR emerging as the two dominant powers** — the ideological split between them made post-war cooperation impossible (setting up the Cold War, covered in file 6) · **monarchies fell** in Romania, Yugoslavia, Bulgaria, Italy — surviving only as constitutional monarchies in Britain, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Holland, Belgium · the shared war sacrifice strengthened democratic welfare-state commitments — **Britain's 1945 Labour government built the welfare state**; **French and Italian women finally got the vote.**

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Most important for this book's purpose:** *"The most significant outcome of the War was the transformation that had taken place in colonies."* Japan's defeat of Western forces in South-East Asia, and Germany's early humbling of Britain and France, **shattered the myth of white invincibility**. Old empires themselves realised *"the Age of Empire was definitely at the end."*

# 🐉 PART D — THE CHINESE REVOLUTION, 1949

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Background — China's 19th-century humiliation

⭐⭐⭐ **Structural weakness:** the corrupt, inept **Manchu (Qing) dynasty** (since 1650); landed **mandarins** defended privilege while peasants suffered high rents/taxes; industry stayed slow despite abundant coal/iron.

| Event | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **First Opium War (1839-42)** | Britain forced the opium trade open; **Treaty of Nanking** — China ceded **Hong Kong**, paid indemnity |
| **Second Opium War (1856-60)** | Britain + France (over a pretext); destroyed the **Summer Palace**; **Beijing Agreement** — opened more ports, permitted foreign missionaries and **legal British opium trade** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Taiping Rebellion (1850-64)** | Led by **Hung Hsiu-chuan**, preaching equality and land redistribution; took **Nanking**, ran 40% of China as a rival state; crushed by an imperial army under British officer **Major Gordon** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Sino-Japanese War (1894-95)** | Crushing Chinese defeat — **Formosa to Japan, Korea independent** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Boxer Rebellion (1899-1901)** | The **Yihequan ("Righteous and Harmonious Fists")** — anti-Manchu AND anti-foreigner; killed foreigners/Chinese Christians; crushed by a multinational force; **Boxer Protocol** — huge reparations, foreign troops permanently stationed in Beijing |

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The 1911 Revolution and the Republic's troubled birth

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **October 1911, Wuchang mutiny** → spreading revolt → **1 Jan 1912, a republic proclaimed at Nanking**; **Sun Yat-sen** (founder of the **Kuomintang/Nationalist Party**, advocating **Nationalism, Democracy, Socialism**) became provisional President, but **resigned in favour of Yuan Shih-kai**, who controlled the army. **12 Feb 1912** — the Manchu dynasty formally abdicated.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Yuan Shih-kai's betrayal:** banned the Kuomintang, made himself **president for life**, and **conceded Manchuria/Shantung to Japanese demands** — died 1916, leaving 12 years of warlord-dominated central government in name only.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Kuomintang-Communist story — Sun Yat-sen to Mao

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Soviet-backed reorganisation:** Sun Yat-sen sought USSR help; **Michael Borodin** reorganised the Kuomintang as a mass party; the **Whampoa Military Academy** was founded near Canton — its first director: **Chiang Kai-shek**; its political-affairs head: **Chou En-lai** — a genuine **Kuomintang-Communist alliance**, at first.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Chiang's betrayal, 1927:** during his **"Northern Expedition"** from Canton, as Shanghai's 600,000-strong general strike gave workers control of the city, Chiang arranged a **pre-dawn attack** on left-wing unions via criminal gangs and merchant collusion — **purged all Communists from the Kuomintang.**

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Mao's rural strategy, and the Long March (1934):** unlike Chiang's urban base, **Mao concentrated on organising peasants** from a mountain base on the Kiangsi-Hunan border. Facing Chiang's encirclement, Mao's army undertook the **Long March (1934)** — of **100,000 who set out, only 20,000 reached northern Shensi**, after nearly **6,000 miles**. By 1937 Mao ruled over **10 million people** from this new base.

⭐⭐⭐ **Japanese occupation and the civil war's end:** Japan occupied eastern China during WWII; Mao pragmatically tolerated Chiang to present a united anti-Japanese front, though Kuomintang armies still fell easily. After Japan's 1945 surrender, US-mediated ceasefires (General **George Marshall**) failed; civil war resumed; Chiang's demoralised, US-backed army disintegrated against Mao's higher-morale forces. **By end of 1949, Chiang fled to Taiwan; the People's Republic of China was established.**

# 🇮🇩🇵🇭 PART E — LIBERATION IN INDONESIA AND THE PHILIPPINES

## 1️⃣2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Indonesia (the Dutch East Indies)

⭐⭐⭐ **Colonial base:** Dutch control since ~1640 (Java, Sumatra); mainly **economic**, not political, interest — plantations plus post-1900 oil discovery.

| Milestone | Year | Detail |
|---|:--:|---|
| **Boedi Oetomo** | 1908 | First native political society — a **cultural**, not political, body |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Sarekat Islam** | Founded to counter Chinese economic power | By 1916 demanded self-government; membership hit **2.5 million** by 1918 |
| **Indonesian Communist Party** | 1919 | Formed by communists who split from Sarekat Islam after failing to capture it |
| **Sukarno's Indonesian Nationalist Party** | — | Sukarno **imprisoned, party dissolved, 1931** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Independence, 1945-49** | Japan surrendered to Indonesian nationalists (1945); Dutch resisted, but **UN Security Council pressure — led by Jawaharlal Nehru** among others — forced a settlement; **The Hague Round Table Conference**; **Indonesia independent, December 1949** |

## 1️⃣3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Philippines

⭐⭐⭐ **Colonial base:** Spanish rule from 1565; the **Cavite Uprising (1872)**, crushed with three priests publicly executed as martyrs, was an early nationalist spark.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **From Spain to America:** the **Spanish-American War (1898)**, triggered by the Cuba dispute, was an easy US win — the **Treaty of Paris (Dec 1898)** transferred the Philippines to the USA. **Emilio Aguinaldo**, having already fought and beaten Spain, declared the **First Philippine Republic** — but was **captured by American forces, 1901**, dissolving it. *"The nationalists thought the issue for America was Cuba. They soon found out they had only exchanged one master for another."*

| Milestone | Year | Detail |
|---|:--:|---|
| **American institution-building** | From 1902 | English education, an exam-based civil service, elected local government, an elected national legislature |
| **Nationalist Party's demand** | — | Leader **Quezon**: *"We should prefer to rule ourselves in Hell to being ruled by others in Heaven."* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Tydings-McDuffie Act** | 1934 | A **ten-year transition** to independence, ratified by plebiscite (1935); Quezon became President 1935-41 |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Japanese conquest** | Dec 1941 | Considered the **worst military defeat in US history** — ~23,000 US personnel and ~100,000 Filipino soldiers killed/captured |
| **Hukbalahap** | WWII-1950s | Communist-led guerrilla resistance against Japan, later turned against the **returning American forces**; crushed by the mid-1950s |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Independence** | **4 July 1946** | Honouring the 1934 Act's promise; the Philippines joined **SEATO (1954)** as a loyal US ally |
