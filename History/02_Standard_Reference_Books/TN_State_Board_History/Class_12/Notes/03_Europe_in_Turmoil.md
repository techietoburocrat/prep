# 🚩 PART A — THE RISE OF SOCIALIST IDEAS AND THE BIRTH OF COMMUNISM

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Utopian Socialism — before Marx

⭐⭐ **Roots:** the Physiocrats first studied production/distribution scientifically; **Étienne-Gabriel Morally**'s *Code de la Nature* (1755) denounced private property; **François Babeuf**, a French Revolutionary-era agitator, argued the Revolution never delivered *economic* equality, only political.

| Thinker | Core idea |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Claude-Henri Saint-Simon (1760-1825)** | A French aristocrat who fought in the American War of Independence; proposed **scientists replace priests** in the social order; *New Christianity* urged concern for the poor |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Charles Fourier (1772-1837)** | Rejected "original sin"; believed social conditions caused human misery; envisaged self-contained cooperative communities called ***phalanstères*** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Robert Owen (1771-1858)** | A Manchester factory owner himself — **refused to employ children under 10**; his reforms helped pass the **Factory Act, 1819**; *A New View of Society* (1818) advocated national education, public works, Poor Law reform; founded the **Grand National Consolidated Trades Union (1834)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Pierre-Joseph Proudhon (1809-1865)** | A **working-class** anarchist (unlike the middle-class Utopians) — *"All property is theft"* (*What is Property?*); wanted **federations of autonomous communes** replacing the nation-state; influenced Russian anarchist **Bakunin** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Louis Blanc (1811-1882)** | Founded *Revue du Progrès* (1839); proposed **state-financed, worker-controlled "social workshops"**; as a member of France's 1848 provisional government, actually implemented unemployment workshops |

⭐ **Term origin:** *"Utopian Socialism"* was coined by **Marx and Engels themselves**, to describe the socialists who preceded them.

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Marx, Engels, and "Scientific Socialism"

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Karl Marx (1818-1883) and Friedrich Engels (1820-1895)** published ***The Communist Manifesto*** on the eve of the 1848 Revolution — its rallying cry: *"Workers of the world, unite! You have nothing to lose but your chains."*

**Core theory:** Marx believed socialism would replace capitalism just as capitalism had replaced feudalism, driven by an inherent **class conflict** between employers and the employed. In **1867**, Marx published *Das Kapital*, critiquing capitalism through the lens of **proletariat exploitation by the bourgeoisie**.

⭐⭐⭐ **Organisational history:** the **International Working Men's Association (1864)** — Marx worked to exclude moderates and denounced rivals like **Lassalle and Bakunin**; it declined by 1876. Parties that followed: **German Social Democratic Party (1875)**, **Belgian Socialist Party (1879)**, the **Paris Commune (1871)** *(see Part D)*, and the **Second International (Paris, 1889)**, influential until WWI.

# 🎗️ PART B — CHARTISM IN ENGLAND

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The People's Charter — six demands

⭐⭐⭐ **Character:** *"not a riot or revolt... an organised movement."* Sparked by 1830's French example and the ensuing labour agitation. Propagated via **The Poor Man's Guardian, The Charter, The Northern Star** *(founded 1837, circulation rivalling The Times)* **and The Chartist Circular**.

**The People's Charter**, drafted by **William Lovett** of the London Working Men's Association, demanded:

1. **Universal (male) suffrage**
2. **Voting by secret ballot** *(to prevent intimidation)*
3. **No property qualification** for candidates
4. **Payment of MPs** *(so poor candidates could actually serve)*
5. **Equal electoral districts**
6. **Annual parliaments**

⭐⭐⭐ **Course and outcome:** mass meetings 1838-39; the **1842 Lancashire general strike**, spreading factory to factory; a **1848 revival**, inspired by France's February Revolution, that **fizzled** as a 50,000-strong crowd at Kennington dispersed and the government arrested most leaders. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Chartism failed in its own time — but its six demands were later delivered piecemeal through the Reform Acts of 1867 and 1884.**

# 🇫🇷 PART C — THE JULY AND FEBRUARY REVOLUTIONS

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ July Revolution, 1830

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Trigger:** Bourbon king **Charles X** issued **four ordinances (26 July 1830)** — dissolving the Chamber of Deputies, suspending press freedom, stripping three-quarters of the electorate of votes, calling new elections. Paris rose; royal forces couldn't contain it; Charles X was exiled and replaced by **Louis Philippe of Orléans**, backed by the middle class.

⭐⭐⭐ **Ripple effects across Europe:** **Belgium separated from the Netherlands** (successful); **Greece won independence from Turkish rule (1832)** with Great Power backing; **Poland's revolt against the Tsar was crushed**.

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ February Revolution, 1848 — "the springtime of nations"

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In France:** Louis Philippe abdicated and fled (Feb 1848). A spontaneous Paris rising, chanting *"Vive la réforme,"* swept the palaces; **Lamartine** (poet) and **Louis Blanc** led the opposition. April 1848 elections (universal manhood suffrage) returned moderates over socialists. When the new Assembly **shut down Louis Blanc's workshops**, workers revolted — the **"Bloody June Days"** (24-26 June): thousands killed, 11,000 imprisoned/deported.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Louis Napoleon** (Napoleon Bonaparte's nephew) was elected President (Dec 1848); crowned himself **Emperor Napoleon III** via plebiscite, January 1852.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Across Europe — a "distinct triumph for nationalism," briefly:** **Metternich fled Vienna in disguise**; Hungary and Bohemia claimed independence; Milan expelled the Austrians; **Venice became an independent republic**; **Charles Albert of Sardinia** declared war on Austria. ⚠️ **By summer, the counter-revolution had crushed the democratic movements in Berlin, Vienna and Milan — absolutism survived after all.**

# 🇮🇹 PART D — ITALIAN UNIFICATION *(delta only — full narrative at [Cl.10 Ch.1](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/01_The_Rise_of_Nationalism_in_Europe.md))*

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Granular detail worth adding to what Class 10 already gives you

⭐⭐⭐ **Pre-1848 groundwork Class 10 skips:** the **Carbonari**, secret societies of the 1820s advocating liberal/patriotic ideas, kept nationalism alive through crushed revolts in Naples, Piedmont and Lombardy. **Cavour's newspaper**, started 1847, gave the movement its name — ***Il Risorgimento*** ("the resurrection of Italian spirit").

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Cavour's actual diplomatic sequence:** joined the **Crimean War** to win international attention → **July 1858 agreement with Napoleon III** → provoked Austria into attacking Piedmont-Sardinia (April 1859) → victory at the **Battle of Solferino** → Napoleon III unexpectedly made a **separate peace with Austria at Villa Franca (11 July 1859)**, disappointing Cavour, who resigned → **Treaty of Zurich (Nov 1859)**: Austria ceded **Lombardy**, kept **Venetia**. Cavour returned as PM (1860); **Parma, Modena and Tuscany joined Piedmont-Sardinia via plebiscite**; **Savoy and Nice were ceded to France** in exchange for its support.

⭐⭐⭐ **Garibaldi's epithet and route, more precisely:** exiled to South America after joining a Mazzini-linked Piedmont rebellion — fought for **Rio Grande and Uruguay** against Argentine occupation, earning the title **"Hero of Two Worlds."** His volunteer force (from 1843) was the **Italian Legion, known as the Red Shirts**. Landed in Sicily with ~1,000 men, defeated a 20,000-strong Neapolitan force **without losing a single life**, then submitted his conquests to King Victor Emmanuel II — **Cavour, wary of Garibaldi, blocked his march on Rome.**

⭐⭐⭐ **Completion, year by year:** **May 1861** — Victor Emmanuel II proclaimed King of Italy · **1866** — Italy allied with Prussia in the Austro-Prussian War, rewarded with **Venetia** · **1871** — Italy annexed **Rome** as French troops withdrew during the Franco-Prussian War, completing unification.

# 🇩🇪 PART E — GERMAN UNIFICATION *(delta only — full narrative at [Cl.10 Ch.1](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/01_The_Rise_of_Nationalism_in_Europe.md))*

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Intellectual roots and the Frankfurt Assembly — new material

⭐⭐⭐ **Two named intellectuals, worth citing precisely:** **Johann Gottfried Herder (1744-1803)** — civilisation as a product of the *Volk* (common people), coined *Volksgeist* ("national spirit"); **J.G. Fichte (1762-1814)** — his *Addresses to the German Nation* claimed the German spirit was **superior**, not merely one among many.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Before Bismarck — the failed liberal route:** the **1848 Frankfurt Assembly**, mostly liberals, split between **"Great Germany"** (include Austria minus Hungary, crown to the Austrian Emperor) and **"Little Germany"** (exclude Austria, crown to Prussia's king). Austria withdrew; the Assembly offered the crown to **Frederick William of Prussia**, who **refused** it — unwilling to accept a crown from a revolutionary body. **The liberal route to unification failed before Bismarck's military one began.**

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bismarck's three wars, with the diplomatic mechanics Class 10 compresses

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Bismarck's own words:** *"Not through speeches and resolutions of majorities will the mighty problems of the age be solved, but by blood and iron."*

| War | Trigger and mechanics |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Denmark, 1864** | The **Schleswig-Holstein Question** — Denmark annexed both duchies (1863); Prussia and Austria jointly defeated Denmark; by the **Treaty of Vienna**, Denmark ceded both. Dispute over their fate: the **Convention of Gastein (1865)** split control — **Holstein to Austria, Schleswig to Prussia** — but Holstein, deep in Prussian territory, was hard for Austria to govern; when Austria referred the matter to the German Confederation's Diet, Bismarck declared this a **violation of Gastein** and occupied Holstein |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Austria, 1866 ("Seven Weeks' War")** | Bismarck secured **Russian and French neutrality** plus **Piedmont-Sardinia's support** *(wanting Venetia)* — forced Austria to attack. Decisive Prussian victory at **Sadowa/Königgrätz, Bohemia**. Bismarck **restrained his own army from marching on Vienna** — a deliberately moderate peace (**Treaty of Prague**): Austria left the German Confederation; the **North German Confederation** (22 states) formed under Prussia; Italy got Venetia for its support |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **France, 1870-71 ("Franco-Prussian War")** | Trigger: the **Spanish throne succession crisis** — Prince Leopold (a Prussian royal relative) was offered Spain's throne, alarming France; he declined, but France's foreign minister **Gramont** then demanded Prussia promise never to revisit the claim. The Prussian king's account of this meeting reached Bismarck by telegram at **Ems** — **Bismarck edited the "Ems Telegram"** to make both sides feel insulted, deliberately engineering war. France declared war; defeated at the **Battle of Sedan (2 September 1870)**, Napoleon III captured; Bismarck's forces then took **Paris**. **Treaty of Frankfurt (1871)**: France ceded **Alsace-Lorraine** and paid a huge indemnity |

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The proclamation, and what Class 10 already tells you:** King William I was declared **German Emperor at Versailles**, uniting the North German Confederation with the southern states — **the same fact Cl.10 Ch.1 gives you**, cross-linked rather than repeated here.

# 🩸 PART F — THE PARIS COMMUNE, 1871

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A genuine total gap — Europe's first working-class government

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Origins:** during the Prussian siege of Paris (part of the Franco-Prussian War's endgame), Parisians — workers, artisans, their families — suffered starvation and cold while the National Assembly returned ever-more monarchists. The appointment of the elderly **Thiers** felt like a betrayal. When the regular army (disbanded under the Prussian peace terms) tried to disarm the working-class **National Guard**, General **Lecomte's** troops **refused to fire on the crowd**, fraternised with them instead, and arrested Lecomte. **Thiers's government fled Paris — the city fell into the hands of armed workers.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Commune's own measures

⭐⭐⭐ Banned **night work in bakeries** · handed **abandoned workshops/factories to workers' associations** · provided **pensions for widows** and **free education for every child** · **stopped debt collection** incurred during the siege.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Suppression — brutal, and precisely quantified:** Thiers negotiated with Bismarck to release French POWs, who then, alongside fresh recruits, crushed the Commune. **An estimated 20,000-30,000 were killed**; of 40,000 arrested Communards, **5,000 were deported and 5,000 imprisoned**.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Marx's own verdict, worth quoting directly:** *"It represented the greatest challenge the new world of capital had yet faced and the greatest inspiration to the new class created by capital in opposition to it."*

# 📉 PART G — THE LONG DEPRESSION (1873-1896)

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Boom, bust, and the labour backlash — a genuine gap

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The boom before the bust, 1865-73:** German unification triggered a boom — **857 new companies founded**, the railway network **nearly doubling (1865-75)**. In the USA, post-Civil War industrialisation produced the **"Gilded Age"** (1870s-1900) — real wages grew **60% (1860-90)**, average industrial wages rose **$380 (1880) → $564 (1890)** — alongside mass immigrant poverty and extreme wealth concentration.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The crash:** triggered by the **collapse of the Vienna Stock Market, May 1873** — worldwide, lasting until 1896, hence **"the Long Depression."** American railroads went bankrupt; **German shares fell 60%**; agriculture was hit hardest by falling prices; many nations responded with **protective tariffs**.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Labour flashpoints of the Depression era

| Event | Country | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Homestead Strike, 1892** | USA | Carnegie Steel Company — a **gun battle** between unionised workers and company-hired strikebreakers; the state backed management; **the strike was lost** |
| **Pullman Strike, 1894** | USA | A national railroad strike (American Railway Union) — crushed by **armed police and Pinkerton detectives** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Match Girls' Strike, 1888** | Britain | Women and teenage girls at the **Bryant and May match factory** — a rare labour **victory** |
| **London Dock Strike, 1889** | Britain | **Cardinal Manning** mediated between strikers and dock owners |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Germany's SPD, under Bismarck's anti-socialist laws** | Germany | Bismarck legislated against socialism, yet the **Social Democratic Party's** Reichstag seat share still grew from **3% (1887) to 20% (1903)** after the laws were repealed post-1890 |
