# 🎖️ PART A — THE AMERICAN WAR OF INDEPENDENCE

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Thirteen Colonies — founding and character

⭐⭐⭐ **Founding sequence:** **Jamestown, Virginia (1607)** — first permanent English colony; **Plymouth, Massachusetts** — the Pilgrims via the *Mayflower*; **New Amsterdam** (Dutch, 1624, near the Hudson) — later seized by the English and **renamed New York**. By **1770, over 2 million people** lived in the thirteen colonies. ⚠️ **Sir Walter Raleigh's earlier attempt (1587)** at Roanoke Island, North Carolina — named "Virginia" for Elizabeth I — became **"the Lost Colony,"** vanishing without trace.

**North-South divide:** the **South** (fertile land, African slave labour, cotton/wheat/tobacco) vs the **North** (no farmland, mills for timber/shipbuilding/grain, sea-borne trade).

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The transatlantic slave trade:** the **Portuguese began it in the early 17th century**; the **first slaves reached America on a Dutch ship (1619)**; **John Hawkins**, the first Englishman to profit from it, was **knighted by Elizabeth I** for his "success." **Over 11 million Africans** were transported in total.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Native Americans, dispossessed:** ~10 million indigenous people lived in present US territory by 1492; by 1900, **fewer than 300,000**. Disease was a deliberate strategy in places — **General Jeffrey Amherst's 1763 letter** to Colonel Bouquet explicitly proposed **inoculating Native Americans with smallpox via blankets**, to *"extirpate this execrable race."*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Causes — colonial governance and the tax Acts

⭐⭐⭐ **Navigation Acts:** required colonial produce to be exported **only in British ships**, and restricted colonial manufacturing (e.g. cloth) — England treated the colonies as existing for the mother country's benefit.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Seven Years' War (1756-63)** was the direct trigger: colonial assemblies under-supplied the war effort, and once the **French threat in Canada was removed**, the colonies felt less need to submit to British demands.

### Six taxes/Acts, in sequence

| Year | Measure | Colonial response |
|:--:|---|---|
| **1764** | **Sugar and Molasses Tax** — first new tax | *"No taxation without representation"* |
| **1765** | **Stamp Act** — tax on legal-document stamps | Boycott; **repealed** |
| **1767** | **Townshend Act** *(Chancellor of the Exchequer Townshend)* — tax on imported goods | Resistance |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1770** | Most taxes abolished **except on tea** — to assert Parliament's taxing right | **Boston Massacre**, 1770 — British troops fired on protesters |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1773** | Tea tax retained | **Boston Tea Party** — ~100 activists dressed as Native Americans threw **342 tea chests** into the harbour |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1774** | **Intolerable/Coercive Acts** *(4 Acts: Boston Port Bill, Massachusetts Government Act, Administration of Justice Act, and a Quartering Act replica)* + the **Quebec Act** (granted Ohio-Mississippi lands to Quebec, angering NY/PA/VA and provoking Protestant colonists with French Civil Law/Catholicism) | **First Continental Congress**, Philadelphia — boycotted British goods, sent the **"Olive Branch Petition"** to George III, who **refused peace** |

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The war, and independence

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Outbreak:** **1775, Lexington** — farmers fought the British, then besieged **Bunker Hill**. **4 July 1776** — all thirteen colonies declared independence; the **Declaration of Independence** was drafted by **Thomas Jefferson** (with Benjamin Franklin and John Adams), proposed initially by **Richard Lee**.

**Course of the war:** British **William Howe** vs American **George Washington**. Howe won early battles at Brooklyn and New Jersey, but the tide turned: **1777, Battle of Saratoga** — General Burgoyne surrendered; **1781, Yorktown** — final British surrender, though Howe held New York almost to the end.

⭐⭐⭐ **International solidarity:** **France, Spain and the Dutch** backed the colonies; Prussia/Sweden/Denmark formed an **"Armed Neutrality"** against Britain. **Thomas Paine's pamphlet *Common Sense* (1776)** — drawing on Hobbes, Locke, Voltaire, Rousseau — **sold over 150,000 copies**, making libertarian ideas accessible to ordinary readers.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **The colonists split**, not unanimously patriotic: **Patriots** (independence) vs **Loyalists/Tories** (crown loyalty, often Anglican) — **a civil war within the revolution**.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Treaty of Paris, 1783:** Britain recognised **US independence**; the US gained territory to the Mississippi/31st parallel; **France gained territories in the West Indies, India and Africa**; **Spain got Florida**.

### Significance

Democracy and republicanism spread as models · **federalism** became a global template · education gained prominence · the Revolution was, in the source's own words, **"a setback for colonialism"** whose demand for independence *"became widespread in many parts of the world."*

# ⚜️ PART B — THE FRENCH REVOLUTION *(delta only — see [Cl.9 Ch.1](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_09_India_and_Contemporary_World_I/Notes/01_The_French_Revolution.md) for the full narrative)*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Points worth adding beyond Class 9's account

⭐⭐⭐ **The financial-bankruptcy angle, more explicit here than in Class 9:** France's Seven Years' War costs, PLUS its **support to the American colonists** (which France "could not really afford"), left the treasury drained — nobles/higher clergy **refused to give up tax exemptions**, worsened by Louis XVI's own extravagance.

⭐⭐⭐ **Women's specific roles, named:** women from poorer Paris districts, backed by 20,000 armed men, **marched on Versailles** and forced the king back to Paris under surveillance. **Olympe de Gouges**, dissatisfied that the Declaration of the Rights of Man excluded women, wrote the **Declaration of the Rights of Woman and of the Citizen**.

⭐⭐⭐ **Named intellectual contributions, precisely attributed:** **Montesquieu** (*The Spirit of Laws* — separation of powers), **Voltaire** (*The Age of Louis XIV* — anti-clerical/anti-absolutist), **Rousseau** (*Social Contract* — *"Man is born free but everywhere he is in chains"*), **John Locke** (*Two Treatises of Government* — against divine right).

⭐⭐ **The factional detail:** **Girondins** (moderate Jacobins, aka Brissotins) vs the more radical **Robespierre/Danton**; the **September Massacres (1792)** — ~1,200 prisoners summarily killed on rumours of counter-revolutionary plotting; the **guillotine's actual inventor was Antoine Louis**, though named for Dr Joseph-Ignace Guillotin, who merely proposed the concept.

# 🏴 PART C — REVOLUTION IN LATIN AMERICA

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Haitian Revolution — the only successful slave revolution

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Haiti (Saint-Domingue)** was France's **richest Caribbean colony** — by 1789: **500,000 African slaves, 32,000 European colonists, 24,000 free mulattoes ("affranchis")**. News of the Bastille's fall triggered **Vincent Ogé's 1790 uprising** *(captured, executed)*; citizenship was granted to wealthier mulattoes (May 1791); **August 1791 — a mass slave rebellion**. Commissioner **Léger-Félicité Sonthonax** abolished slavery in the colony (1793), later **ratified by France**.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Toussaint L'Ouverture**, a former slave turned military leader, took control of several regions. **Napoleon sent 12,000 troops** to retake the colony — Toussaint was **kidnapped and killed** after being misled into believing conciliation possible; his lieutenant **Dessalines** organised resistance and **defeated Napoleon's army**. **Saint-Domingue became independent Haiti, 1804.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Simón Bolívar and Venezuela/Gran Colombia

⭐⭐⭐ **Trigger:** Napoleon deposed Spain's Charles IV and Ferdinand VII (1808), installing his brother Joseph — leaving Spain's colonies without a stable metropole for **six years**, opening space for revolt.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Bolívar's campaign, in stages:** declared a break from Spain (1811) → driven out by loyalist **José Tomás Boves** → by 1815 the independence movement seemed dead → **1817, a revitalised movement** with **José Antonio Páez's *llaneros* (cowboys)** joining → **Battle of Boyacá** (crushing victory) → **1819, Congress of Angostura** names Bolívar president of **Gran Colombia** (Venezuela + Colombia + Panama + Ecuador) → full liberation of New Granada and Venezuela, **1821**.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Bolívar's own politics, worth quoting precisely:** in his **"Jamaica Letter" (1815)**, written in exile, he argued for an **autonomous, centralised republican government** — but the source notes his vision was *"very much an oligarchic one,"* since he believed no virtuous government was possible if the nation were divided by ethnicity. **He remained an inspiring figure for left-wing Latin American movements even into the late 20th century.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mexico and Brazil

| Country | How independence came |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Mexico** | Led first by the priest **Miguel Hidalgo** (defeated, killed 1811), then **José María Morelos** (declared independence 1813, defeated 1815). ⚠️ **Ironic twist**: the officer who finally **defeated Morelos** later switched sides and **declared Mexican independence himself (1821)**, made himself emperor, and was overthrown — **Mexico became a republic in 1824** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Brazil** | A **Portuguese colony** — when Napoleon invaded Portugal (1808), King **João VI fled to Brazil** and modernised it (land reforms, military/medical/art schools). His son **Dom Pedro** was left in charge; when Portugal tried to recall him, **Pedro declared Brazil's independence (1822)** and became **Emperor Pedro I** — Brazil, uniquely, became **South America's only constitutional monarchy** |

⭐⭐⭐ **San Martín and Bolívar's Guayaquil meeting:** Argentina's liberator **San Martín** and Chile's **Bernardo O'Higgins** won Chile (1818) and Peru (1820); San Martín and Bolívar met at **Guayaquil, Ecuador** to discuss unification — disagreed (San Martín wanted constitutional monarchy, Bolívar wanted a republic) — **San Martín withdrew**, letting Bolívar complete South American liberation by **1826**. Bolívar's dream of a **"United States of Latin America"** (Gran Colombia) collapsed under geography and internal power struggles.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **The aftermath, honestly stated:** most Latin American states became republics in name, but remained **economically dependent on foreign powers** — the source notes 20th-century US influence via *"pliant dictatorial governments (often referred to as banana republics)."*

# ⚙️ PART D — THE INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Definition and the ten causes of England's head start

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Definition:** the shift from small-scale artisan/cottage production to **large-scale factory production**.

**Ten causes, per the source:**

| # | Cause |
|:--:|---|
| 1 | **Commercial Revolution's capitalist class**, seeking investment outlets |
| 2 | **Britain's colonial supremacy** — a quarter of the world's territory and population by the early 18th century, generating demand |
| 3 | **Domestic market growth** — England's population: 4 million (1600) → 6 million (1700) → 9 million (end of 18th c.) |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 4 | **Drain of wealth from colonies, "notably from India"** — capital for industrial investment |
| 5 | **Political stability and liberalism**, relative to continental Europe |
| ⭐⭐⭐ 6 | **Coal and iron abundance** — Britain produced **10 million tons of coal by 1800, 90% of world output** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ 7 | **Agricultural Revolution first** — enclosures, crop rotation, mechanisation raised output but **displaced agricultural labourers into the urban factory workforce** |
| 8 | **Established coastal ports** for internal/external trade |
| 9 | **Geographic safety** from invasion |
| 10 | **Temperate climate**, favourable to cotton manufacture |

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Key inventions, matched to inventor and year

### Cotton textiles

| Invention | Inventor | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Flying shuttle | John Kay | 1733 |
| Spinning Jenny *(8 threads at once)* | James Hargreaves | 1764 |
| Water Frame *(128 threads at once)* | Richard Arkwright | 1769 |
| Crompton's Mule *(Jenny + Water Frame combined)* | Samuel Crompton | — |

⭐⭐⭐ **Scale of change:** Britain imported **500 tons of raw cotton in 1700**; by **1860, 500,000 tons a year**. Manchester earned the nickname **"Cottonopolis."**

### Iron, steam and transport

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Abraham Darby (Derbyshire, ~1709)** discovered **coke** could smelt iron, solving the charcoal-deforestation crisis. **Thomas Newcomen (1712)** built the first mine-water pump engine, improved by **James Watt (1769)** with entrepreneur **Matthew Boulton** — over 500 steam engines produced. **George Stephenson's "Rocket"** ran the **first passenger railway, Liverpool-Manchester, 1830**, at 30+ mph. The **first electric telegraph, 1835**; the **first transatlantic undersea cable**, sixteen years later. **John Loudon McAdam** invented macadamised road construction.

⭐⭐⭐ **The Eiffel Tower (1889, 324m)** — a direct architectural showcase of the era's new confidence in iron.

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Second Industrial Revolution (1870-1914) — Germany and the USA

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Germany:** the **Zollverein** (Prussia-led customs union, free trade among German states) removed internal tariffs even before political unification; **1871 unification** accelerated industry further. **Rudolf Diesel's diesel engine**, plus **Daimler and Benz** automobiles, made Germany Europe's automotive leader. **Siemens** led in electricals; **Bayer and Hoechst** led in chemicals, dyes and pharmaceuticals — Germany **surpassed Britain** as the most industrialised nation by century's end.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The USA:** **Samuel Slater**, an Englishman with mill experience, built the **first water-powered roller spinning mill in the Americas (1793)** for industrialist Moses Brown — **President Andrew Jackson called him the "Father of the American Industrial Revolution."** Later landmarks: **Samuel Morse's telegraph**, **Elias Howe's sewing machine**, the **first transcontinental railroad (1869)**, **Edison's electric bulb (1879)**, **Alexander Graham Bell's telephone (1876)**. **14 million immigrants arrived, 1860-1900**, fuelling rapid urbanisation.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Impact — new classes, and the labour backlash

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The verdict, stated directly by the source:** *"The Industrial Revolution solved the problem of production. But not the problem of distribution of new wealth created."* New wealth flowed to a small owner class; **women and children's cheap labour displaced able-bodied men**; factories and mines were **dangerous and unsanitary**.

**Two new classes emerged:** the **industrial bourgeoisie** (hostile to government intervention except protective tariffs and strike suppression) and the **proletariat**, whose resistance moved through **machine-breaking → mass demonstrations → trade unions**.

### Three labour flashpoints, named and dated

| Event | Year | What happened |
|---|:--:|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Peterloo Massacre** | 1819 | Radical leader **Henry Hunt's** peaceful 60,000-strong Manchester demonstration was **charged by the yeomanry** — 700+ injured, 17 killed |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Tolpuddle Martyrs** | 1834 | **Six labourers** organising against wage cuts were convicted and **transported to an Australian penal colony** for seven years |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Haymarket Affair** | 1886 | A Chicago rally for an **eight-hour day** turned violent after a bomb killed police officers — **7 police + 4+ civilians died**. Commemorated as **1 May, "Labour Day"/"May Day"/"International Workers' Day"** |

*(A parallel US event: the **Great Railroad Strike, 1877** — crushed by vigilantes, the National Guard and the Federal Army.)*

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **NAVIGATION ACTS** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **British laws confining colonial trade to British ships — a root grievance of the American colonies** |
| **INTOLERABLE ACTS** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The four/five 1774 punitive measures after the Boston Tea Party, which directly triggered the First Continental Congress** |
| **AFFRANCHIS** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Free mulattoes (mixed African-European descent) in pre-revolution Saint-Domingue/Haiti** |

| **ZOLLVEREIN** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Prussian-led German customs union that removed internal tariffs, laying the economic base for later political unification** |
| **PROLETARIAT** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The new industrial working class created by the factory system** |

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BOSTON TEA PARTY (1773) LED TO THE INTOLERABLE ACTS (1774), WHICH LED DIRECTLY TO THE FIRST CONTINENTAL CONGRESS — A CLEAR CAUSAL CHAIN**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THOMAS PAINE'S "COMMON SENSE" SOLD 150,000+ COPIES, TRANSLATING ENLIGHTENMENT PHILOSOPHY INTO POPULAR LANGUAGE**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE COLONISTS WERE NOT UNANIMOUS — PATRIOTS VS LOYALISTS/TORIES WAS A GENUINE CIVIL WAR WITHIN THE REVOLUTION**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HAITI IS THE ONLY SUCCESSFUL SLAVE-LED REVOLUTION IN WORLD HISTORY — TOUSSAINT L'OUVERTURE STARTED IT, DESSALINES FINISHED IT**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BOLÍVAR'S OWN "GRAN COLOMBIA" DREAM FAILED — GEOGRAPHY AND POWER STRUGGLES BROKE IT INTO SEPARATE NATIONS**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BRAZIL BECAME SOUTH AMERICA'S ONLY CONSTITUTIONAL MONARCHY, VIA A PORTUGUESE PRINCE WHO REFUSED TO GO HOME**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE DRAIN OF WEALTH FROM INDIA IS NAMED EXPLICITLY AS A CAUSE OF BRITAIN'S INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION — NOT JUST AN INDIAN GRIEVANCE, A WORLD-HISTORICAL FACT**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE AGRICULTURAL REVOLUTION CAME BEFORE THE INDUSTRIAL ONE IN ENGLAND, AND DISPLACED THE VERY WORKFORCE THE FACTORIES THEN ABSORBED**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GERMANY'S ZOLLVEREIN (ECONOMIC UNION) PRECEDED ITS 1871 POLITICAL UNIFICATION, NOT THE OTHER WAY ROUND**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PETERLOO (1819) → TOLPUDDLE (1834) → HAYMARKET (1886) TRACE THE LABOUR MOVEMENT'S ESCALATION FROM PROTEST TO ORGANISED INTERNATIONAL COMMEMORATION**
