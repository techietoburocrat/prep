## 2️⃣ South India in Mauryan times ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "The ASHOKAN EDICTS (c. 270-30 BCE) present FOR THE
      FIRST TIME a picture of the political condition in south
      India." **

   ================================================================
   ** ROCK EDICT II -- the four southern peoples **
   ================================================================
   ** It lists as NEIGHBOUR RULERS, "LYING BEYOND HIS DOMAIN": **
     ** CHOLAS · PANDYAS · KERALAPUTRAS · SATIYAPUTRA **

   ⭐ ** And it records that he made provision there for
      ** TWO TYPES OF MEDICAL TREATMENT: FOR HUMANS AND FOR
      ANIMALS. ** **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE POLITICAL POINT: "The Mauryan empire at that
      time included NORTHERN PARTS OF KARNATAKA AND ANDHRA,
      while ** THE TAMIL KINGDOMS WERE TREATED AS INDEPENDENT
      NEIGHBOURS. ** " **

   ** After Mauryan decline and before the Satavahanas, "MANY
      SMALL PRINCIPALITIES emerged"; their coins and inscriptions
      "reveal that they were CHIEFS WHO CONTROLLED SMALL
      TERRITORIES." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Tamil kingdoms are the ONLY Indian polities Ashoka names as lying outside his empire — and he still legislated welfare provision for them.** ⭐ **That single edict does two jobs: it fixes the **southern limit of the Maurya empire**, and it gives you the **earliest datable reference to the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas.**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note the naming trap: *SATIYAPUTRA* in Ashoka's edict is, in the Sangam poems, the ***ATHIYAMAN*** — and he is a ***VELIR* CHIEF, not one of the three crowned kings.** *So the edict lists three kings and one chief, which is exactly what §5's political ladder would predict.*

## 3️⃣ The Satavahanas ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** Emerged in the ** FIRST CENTURY BCE ** in the Deccan; ruled
      parts of ** ANDHRA, MAHARASHTRA, KARNATAKA and MADHYA
      PRADESH. ** **
   ⭐ ** Recent archaeology: they STARTED IN THE TELANGANA AREA,
      moved to MAHARASHTRA to rule the GODAVARI BASIN with
      ** PRATHISTAN (PAITHAN) ** as capital, then moved EASTWARDS
      to control coastal Andhra. **
   ** PLINY records ** 30 WALLED TOWNS ** , a large army, cavalry
      and elephant force in the Andhra country. **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE THREE RULERS TO KNOW **
   ================================================================
   ** GAUTAMIPUTRA SATAKARNI ** -- "the GREATEST of the Satavahana
      kings."
      * ⭐ ** Defeated the SHAKA ruler NAHAPANA and REISSUED HIS
        COINS WITH HIS OWN ROYAL INSIGNIA **
      * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** The inscription of his mother GAUTAMI BALASHRI
        at NASHIK mentions him as ** CONQUEROR OF THE SHAKAS,
        PAHLAVAS AND YAVANAS ** **
      * ** Performed the ASVAMEDHA sacrifice **

   ** VASISHTHIPUTRA PULUMAYI ** -- his successor; "expanded the
      frontiers"; his coins are ** found scattered in many parts
      of south India **

   ⭐ ** YAGNASHRI SATAKARNI ** -- "issued coins with A SHIP MOTIF,
      ** indicating the importance of the OVERSEAS TRADE ** during
      his reign"

   ** KING HALA ** -- credited with the ** GAHA SATTASAI ** , "a
      collection of ** 700 LOVE POEMS ** ", in ** MAHARASHTRI
      PRAKRIT ** , with ⭐ "themes SIMILAR to those found in the
      Tamil Sangam poetry."

   ** DECLINE around the 3rd century CE -> replaced by the
      IKSHVAKUS, followed by the PALLAVAS in Andhra and the
      KADAMBAS in northern Karnataka. **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WHY THE SATAVAHANAS MATTER — the textbook gives three reasons, and the first is the one with real analytical weight.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(i) LAND GRANTS BEGIN.** *"Offering land grants was an important development."* **Beneficiaries were mostly BUDDHISTS AND BRAHMINS**; the ***NANEGHAT* inscription** records **tax exemptions on lands granted to Buddhist monks**. ⭐ **The consequence, stated plainly: *"These land donations created A GROUP OF PEOPLE WHO DID NOT CULTIVATE, BUT OWNED LAND. This led to the development of a LAND-BASED SOCIAL HIERARCHY and divisions in society."***
> - ⭐ **(ii) *"For the FIRST TIME a big state covering a major part of the Deccan was established."***
> - ⭐ **(iii) Rock-cut caves for the Buddhist *sangha* sat **on the trade routes linking the interior to the Konkan coast** — and it was a period of **brisk Indo-Roman trade.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Point (i) is the same mechanism your [Class 12 Part I Theme 3 notes](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/03_Theme3_Kinship_Caste_and_Class.md) describe for the north — and it is the origin of everything in [Unit 11](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md).** ⭐ **The Chola *brahmadeya*, the tax exemption, the *brahmadeya-kilavar* at the top of the social order, and *kudi neekki* all descend from this Satavahana-era innovation.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Say it as one line: *the tax-exempt religious land grant is the single most consequential institution in South Indian social history, and it starts here.***

```
   ** PERUNARKILLI performed the Vedic ** RAJASUYAM ** . **

   ** They invaded southern Kerala and controlled ** NELKYNDA ** . **
   ** "According to tradition, they PATRONIZED THE TAMIL SANGAMS
```

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *THINAI* — the five eco-zones

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the single most distinctive idea in Sangam studies, and it is a genuinely sophisticated piece of ancient social geography. Learn all five with their occupations.**

| ***THINAI*** | Landscape | ⭐ Occupation |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **KURINJI** | **Hilly** region | **Hunting and gathering** |
| **MULLAI** | **Forested** region | **Pastoralism combined with SHIFTING CULTIVATION** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MARUTHAM** | **Riverine tract** | **Agriculture using PLOUGH AND IRRIGATION** |
| **NEYTHAL** | **Coastal** land | **Fishing and SALT MAKING** |
| ⚠️ **PALAI** | **Parched** land | *"Unsuitable for cultivation, and hence people took to **CATTLE LIFTING AND ROBBERY**"* |

```
   ⭐ ** "Each region had DISTINCT CHARACTERISTICS -- a PRESIDING
      DEITY, PEOPLE and CULTURAL LIFE according to the
      ENVIRONMENTAL CONDITIONS." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE ANALYTICAL PAYOFF, in the textbook's own
      words: "In a way this thinai classification is said to
      reflect ** THE UNEVEN SOCIO-ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENTS OF THE
      DIFFERENT LOCALITIES. THAT IS SEEN IN THE POLITICAL FORMS
      TOO. ** " **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE *THINAI* IS NOT JUST A POETIC CONVENTION — IT IS AN ECONOMIC MAP, AND THE POLITICS FOLLOWS IT DIRECTLY. THIS IS THE BEST INSIGHT IN THE UNIT.**
> - ⭐ ***MARUTHAM*, the irrigated riverine tract, is where PLOUGH AGRICULTURE AND SURPLUS ARE POSSIBLE** — and §5 tells you the ***VENDAR*, the crowned kings, controlled exactly "larger, FERTILE territories."**
> - ⭐ **The *VELIR* chiefs held "mainly HILLY AND FOREST areas" — *kurinji* and *mullai* — *"IN BETWEEN the muvendar's fertile territories."***
> - ⚠️ **And *PALAI*, the parched zone, produces **cattle-lifting and robbery** — which is why §5 records *"frequent wars among these chiefs ON ACCOUNT OF CAPTURE OF CATTLE."**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the one-line thesis: *in Sangam Tamizhagam, the ecology determined the economy, the economy determined the scale of the polity, and the poets classified all three with the same five words.*** *(⭐ **This also explains the [Unit 11](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md) finding that a Chola *nadu* was defined AROUND IRRIGATION SOURCES — the same logic, five centuries on.**)*

```
      AGE (c. 1100-300 BCE) emerged the Vendar… certain chiefs
      attained higher status THROUGH THE LARGER AND EFFECTIVE
      CONTROL OF PASTORAL AND AGRICULTURAL REGIONS, while others
      IN THE MARGINAL REGIONS REMAINED AS CHIEFTAINS (velir)." **
   ⭐ ** Example: the ATHIYAMANS -- the SATIYAPUTRA of Ashoka's
      inscription -- "became weak and DID NOT ATTAIN THE STATUS
      OF KINGS." **

   ** HOW THE VENDAR DISPLAYED POWER: **
   * ** TITLES ** -- KADUNGO · IMAYAVARAMBAN · VANAVARAMBAN ·
     PERUVAZHUTHI
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** PATRONAGE OF BARDS AND POETS, entertained in
     their courts (AVAIYAM), "to GLORIFY THEIR NAME AND FAME and
     also their TERRITORIES AND TOWNS" ** -- e.g. Karikalan gave
     "a huge amount of GOLD COINS to URUTTIRANKANNANAR who

```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE PATRONAGE POINT IS A SOURCE-CRITICISM POINT IN DISGUISE, AND IT IS WORTH MAKING EXPLICITLY.** ⭐ **The Sangam poems are our principal source for the Sangam age — and the textbook has just told you that **kings PAID poets in gold to glorify their name, their fame, their territories and their towns.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the corpus is, in part, **commissioned praise-poetry** — which is exactly the caution [Theme 8's *Ain*](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) and the [Mughal chronicles](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/09_Supplement_Kings_and_Chronicles_The_Mughal_Courts.md) required.** *Different century, different language, same relationship between patron and record.*

### 6.1 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ STATE or CHIEFDOM? — a ready-made "critically examine" answer

```
   ================================================================
   ** "There are DIFFERING VIEWS AMONG SCHOLARS with regard to
      the political organization of the three kingdoms." **
   ================================================================

   ** THE EARLIER AND DOMINANT VIEW: **
      ⭐ ** the Sangam Age society was A WELL-ORGANISED STATE
         SOCIETY **

   ** THE RECENT VIEW (put forward in recent decades): **
      ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** the polities of the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas
         were ** PRE-STATE CHIEFDOMS ** **

   ================================================================
   ** FOUR ARGUMENTS FOR "CHIEFDOM" **
   ================================================================
   ** 1. NO SOCIAL STRATIFICATION is noticed **
   ** 2. PROPER TERRITORIAL ASSOCIATION IS ABSENT **
   ** 3. DESTRUCTIVE WARFARE did not allow the development of
        agriculture and SURPLUS PRODUCTION for the emergence of
        the state **
   ** 4. NO EVIDENCE OF TAXATION as in the governments of North
        India **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE FIVE COUNTER-ARGUMENTS **
   ================================================================
   ** 1. "A closer look at the Sangam literature reveals that
        ** SOCIAL DIFFERENTIATION IS EVIDENT IN THE MARUTHAM
        REGION. ** " **
   ** 2. "The TERRITORIAL ASSOCIATIONS ARE VERY CLEAR in the case
        of the MUVENDAR, and their important position is
        ** CORROBORATED BY THE GRECO-ROMAN TEXTS from the first
        century CE. ** " **
   ** 3. "WARFARE FOR TERRITORIAL EXPANSION was a major theme of
        ** PURATTHINAI ** " **
   ** 4. ⭐ "Evidence for TAXATION at the HIGHWAYS and in the PORT
        OF KAVIRIPATTINAM is cited. The CHERA king is spoken as
        RECEIVING THE RESOURCES FROM THE HILLS AND THE PORT OF
        MUSIRI." **
   ** 5. "TRADE played an important role between the late first
        century BCE and third century CE." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BEST MAINS MATERIAL IN THE UNIT — a live scholarly disagreement, laid out with arguments on both sides. Answer it in four moves:**
> - ⭐ **(1) State the two positions**, and name the shift: *the older view read the Sangam kings as a state; recent scholarship reads them as **pre-state chiefdoms**.*
> - ⭐ **(2) Give the chiefdom case** — no stratification, no fixed territory, warfare preventing surplus, no taxation.
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(3) Give the counter-case, and note that the strongest items are the INDEPENDENT ones** — *taxation at highways and at Kaviripattinam port*, and **corroboration by GRECO-ROMAN TEXTS**, which are **outside the praise-poetry system entirely.**
> - ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(4) Then take a defensible middle position, which the textbook's own §6 supplies:** *the *thinai* framework shows **uneven development across eco-zones** — so **Marutham looks state-like** *(differentiation, plough surplus, taxable trade)* **while *kurinji*, *mullai* and *palai* remain chiefdoms.** ⭐ **The right answer is not "state" or "chiefdom" but *"a state forming unevenly, fastest where the water was."***

```
   ** CHATTU ** -- the itinerant or MOBILE merchants

   ================================================================
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Roman coins circulated as BULLION" is the sharpest economic point here, and most students miss what it means.** ⭐ **A coin circulating as bullion is being valued **by its metal weight, not by the authority that struck it.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So Rome was not exporting monetary sovereignty to India — it was exporting **gold and silver**, and the Tamil economy weighed it.** *(⭐ **Exactly the mechanism [Theme 8 §7](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) describes for the seventeenth century — Europe having little India wanted, and settling the balance permanently in bullion. **Same structural relationship, fifteen centuries earlier.** And the **Coimbatore hoards** are the physical residue of it.)*

## 8️⃣ ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ Keeladi and Porunai — important, in the news, and *contested*

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ THIS FRAMING FIRST.** *The textbook devotes several pages to excavations by the **Tamil Nadu State Department of Archaeology**. **The excavations are real and the artefacts are real.** But several of the **INTERPRETATIONS** — particularly the earliest dates and the claimed Indus links — are **actively debated in the wider archaeological community and are not settled consensus**. ⭐ **For UPSC: know the sites, the rivers, the artefact types and the claimed dates AS CLAIMS. Do not assert the contested conclusions as established fact** — the same `[VERIFY]` discipline used for volatile figures elsewhere in these notes.

```
   ================================================================

   ================================================================

      were sent to ** BETA ANALYTIC LAB, MIAMI, FLORIDA ** for
      ** AMS dating ** . ⚠️ "The results reveal that the sample
      collected at a depth of ** 353 cm goes back to 580 BCE ** ."

      HIGH LITERACY LEVEL of Tamil people by the sixth century
      BCE." **
   ** AGRICULTURE / CATTLE: Deccan College, Pune tested 70 animal
```

```
      reached south India and Sri Lanka." **
   ⭐ ** Ashoka's daughter is considered to have taken a sapling
      of the BODHI TREE to Sri Lanka; and there is a legend of
      CHANDRAGUPTA MAURYA moving to the Karnataka region. **

   ** The SATAVAHANAS, SANGAM KINGS and IKSHVAKUS supported
      ** VEDIC SACRIFICES ** ; the movement of Brahmins and Vedic

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "BUT THE ** VARNASRAMA IDEOLOGY WAS YET TO TAKE

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** BUDDHISM vs JAINISM IN THE TAMIL COUNTRY -- and the
      verdict is the opposite of what most people assume **
   ================================================================
   ** BUDDHISM: "widely found in south India"; the KRISHNA and
      GODAVARI delta of ANDHRA "had many important Buddhist
      centres" -- ** AMARAVATHI, NAGARJUNAKONDA ** . In Tamil
      Nadu, ** KAVIRIPATTINAM and KANCHIPURAM ** have Buddhist
      stupas. **
   ⭐ ** "BUT compared to Jainism, the evidence for Buddhism is
      ** RESTRICTED TO A FEW SITES IN TAMIL NADU. ** " **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** JAINISM: "The NUMEROUS CAVE SHELTERS WITH TAMIL-
      BRAHMI INSCRIPTIONS found in Tamil Nadu show that
      ** JAINISM WAS MORE INFLUENTIAL IN THE TAMIL COUNTRY. ** " **
   ** "We have evidence for the ** MERCHANTS AND LAY DEVOTEES
      supporting Jain monks ** by providing ROCK SHELTERS AND
      OFFERINGS." **
   ⭐ ** "In the post-Sangam centuries ** JAINS CONTRIBUTED
      SUBSTANTIALLY TO TAMIL LITERATURE. ** " **
```

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two corrections here that are worth carrying.** ⭐ **(i) *Varnasrama had NOT taken root* in the Tamil region in this period — so do not project the northern four-fold order onto Sangam society.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii) In Tamil Nadu, **JAINISM was more influential than Buddhism**, and the evidence is physical: **numerous cave shelters with Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions**, funded by **merchants and lay devotees**.** *(⭐ **And this sets up the whole religious drama of the next few centuries — the Jain strength here is what the [bhakti Nayanars and Alvars](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/06_Theme6_Bhakti_Sufi_Traditions.md) later defined themselves against, and it explains **Arikesari the "persecutor of Jains"** in [Unit 11](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md).**)*

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Kalabhras — and why the "dark age" was abolished

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE PERIOD: roughly ** c. 300 CE - 600 CE ** , between the
      Sangam Age and the Pallava-Pandya period. **
   ================================================================
   ** "As the three traditional kingdoms DISAPPEARED in this
      interval due to the occupation of their territory by
      ** a warlike group called the KALABHRAS ** , this period was
      called an ** INTERREGNUM or 'DARK AGE' by earlier
      historians ** . It was also supposed that MANY GOOD TRAITS
      of earlier Tamil culture DISAPPEARED." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "THIS IDEA OF THE KALABHRA INTERREGNUM IS
      ** NO MORE ACCEPTED AS CORRECT. ** " **

   ================================================================
   ** THE THREE-PART REFUTATION **
   ================================================================
   ** 1. THE LITERATURE ARGUMENT ** -- ⭐ "RATHER, THIS IS THE TIME
        WHEN ** THE GREATEST TAMIL WORK, THIRUKKURAL, WAS
        WRITTEN ** , along with many other works grouped as the
        EIGHTEEN MINOR WORKS. ** The epics SILAPPATHIKARAM and
        MANIMEKALAI also belong to this period. ** "
        -> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** A "dark age" that produced the Thirukkural
           and both great epics is not a dark age. **

   ** 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE MOTIVE ARGUMENT -- who called it dark, and
        why: ** "As this was the time when THE NON-ORTHODOX
        RELIGIONS, JAINISM AND BUDDHISM, BECAME MORE INFLUENTIAL,
        ** THE SCHOLARS OF THE ORTHODOX VEDIC-PURANIC SCHOOL SEEM
        TO HAVE CREATED THE IMPRESSION THAT THE RULING KALABHRAS
        OF THE TIME WERE EVIL IN NATURE. ** "

   ** 3. THE TRANSITION ARGUMENT ** -- "The recent interpretation
        takes it as ** A PERIOD OF TRANSITION leading to ENLARGED
        STATE SOCIETIES ** under the PALLAVAS in northern
        Tamilnadu and the PANDYAS in the south from the sixth
        century onwards." ⭐ Those new rulers "TO START WITH…
        WERE PATRONS OF THE JAIN AND BUDDHIST RELIGIONS and
        GRADUALLY came under the spell of the orthodox
        Vedic-Puranic religion emerging in the form of THE BHAKTI
        CULTS OF SAIVISM AND VAISHNAVISM."

   ================================================================
   ** WHAT WE ACTUALLY KNOW ABOUT THE KALABHRAS **
   ================================================================
   ⭐ ** The ** PULANGURICHI ** inscriptions (SIVAGANGAI district),
      datable to about the ** MIDDLE OF THE FIFTH CENTURY ** ,
      name two kings -- ** CHENDAN and KURRAN ** . "Though there
      is NO MENTION about their family or dynasty name, some
      scholars identify them as KALABHRA RULERS." **
   ** "The Kalabhra kingdom seems to have been UPROOTED BY THE
      PANDYAS around the THIRD QUARTER OF THE SIXTH CENTURY
      CE." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BEST SINGLE PARAGRAPH IN THE UNIT AND ONE OF THE BEST HISTORIOGRAPHICAL POINTS IN THE WHOLE TN BOOK. LEARN THE ARGUMENT, NOT JUST THE CONCLUSION.**
>
> ⭐ **A period was labelled a "dark age" by later scholars. The labelling had a MOTIVE: the era's rulers patronised **Jainism and Buddhism**, and it was **orthodox Vedic-Puranic scholars** who wrote them up as *"evil in nature."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The label tells you about the people who applied it, not about the period — and the period's actual output was the *THIRUKKURAL*, the *SILAPPATHIKARAM* and the *MANIMEKALAI*.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the *exact* move you already know from [Class 11 Theme 3](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_11_Themes_in_World_History/Notes/03_Theme3_Nomadic_Empires.md) — where the worst exaggerations of Mongol massacres came from chronicles written UNDER MONGOL PATRONAGE, because a monstrous ancestor flattered the present ruler.** ⭐ **Same discipline: *always ask who benefits from the characterisation.*** *(⭐⭐ **And a modern parallel worth a sentence in an Essay: "dark age" is a label European historiography applied to its own early medieval centuries and has since largely abandoned, for closely similar reasons.**)*

| The Sangam age was a proven "dark age" after 300 CE | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"This idea of the Kalabhra interregnum is **NO MORE ACCEPTED AS CORRECT**"* — it produced the **Thirukkural** and both epics |
| *Thirukkural* and *Silappathikaram* are Sangam works | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **POST-SANGAM.** *Thirukkural* and *Naladiyar* are in the **PATHINEN KILKANAKKU (18 minor works)**; the epics belong to the **4th–6th centuries CE** |
| The Eight Anthologies are the *Paththuppattu* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REVERSED — *ETTUTHOGAI* = the EIGHT ANTHOLOGIES; *PATHTHUPPATTU* = the TEN IDYLLS** |
| *Tholkappiyam* is purely a grammar | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The earliest extant Tamil grammatical text, dealing "NOT ONLY WITH POETRY BUT ALSO THE SOCIETY AND CULTURE of the times"** |
| *Patitruppathu* is about the Pandyas | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It "speaks of EIGHT CHERA KINGS"** |
| The Chola emblem was the fish | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CHOLA = TIGER · CHERA = BOW AND ARROW · PANDYA = FISH** |
| The Chola capital in the Sangam age was Thanjavur | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **URAIYUR** *(near Tiruchirappalli)*, with **PUHAR/KAVIRIPATTINAM** as port and alternative residence. *(Thanjavur is **Vijayalaya's**, in 850 CE — see [Unit 11](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md))* |
| Korkai is called Kolkoi in the Arthasastra | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In the PERIPLUS**; it was famed for **PEARL FISHERY and CHANK DIVING** |
| Karikalan's great victory was at Talayalanganam | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **VENNI** — over the Cheras and Pandyas backed by **ELEVEN Velir chieftains**. *(**TALAYALANGANAM** is **NEDUNCHEZHIYAN's**, over the Chera, Chola and **FIVE** Velir chiefs)* |
| Chenguttuvan's north Indian expedition is well attested | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ It appears in the ***SILAPPATHIKARAM*** but is ***"NOT MENTIONED IN THE SANGAM POEMS"*** |
| Ashoka's Rock Edict II names the Satavahanas | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CHOLAS, PANDYAS, KERALAPUTRAS and SATIYAPUTRA** — as **independent neighbours**, with provision for **medical treatment for humans AND animals** |
| Satiyaputra was one of the three crowned kings | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Satiyaputra = the ATHIYAMAN, a *VELIR* CHIEF** in the Sangam poems — who *"became weak and did not attain the status of kings"* |
| The Satavahana capital was Amaravati | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***PRATHISTAN* (PAITHAN)**, in the **Godavari basin**; they began in **Telangana** and moved east later |
| Gautamiputra Satakarni defeated Rudradaman | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ He defeated the **Shaka ruler NAHAPANA** and **reissued his coins with his own insignia**; the **Nashik inscription of GAUTAMI BALASHRI** calls him conqueror of **Shakas, Pahlavas and Yavanas** |
| Hala wrote the *Gatha Saptasati* in Sanskrit | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In MAHARASHTRI PRAKRIT** — **700 love poems** with themes similar to Tamil Sangam poetry |
| The ship-motif coins were Gautamiputra's | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***YAGNASHRI SATAKARNI's*** — indicating **overseas trade** |
| *Marutham* is the pastoral zone | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***MARUTHAM* = RIVERINE, plough agriculture with irrigation.** *MULLAI* is forest/pastoral; *KURINJI* hilly/hunting; *NEYTHAL* coastal/fishing and salt; *PALAI* parched, **cattle-lifting and robbery** |
| *Velir* chiefs held the fertile river valleys | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The *VENDAR* held "larger, FERTILE territories"; the *VELIR* held "mainly HILLY AND FOREST areas… IN BETWEEN" them** |
| *Kizhar* were the crowned kings | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Heads of villages or a small territory *(later called nadu)*, "chiefs of TRIBAL COMMUNITIES"** |
| Scholars agree the Sangam polity was a state | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A live debate** — the recent view is **PRE-STATE CHIEFDOMS**; the counter-case cites **social differentiation in *Marutham*, Greco-Roman corroboration, and taxation at highways and Kaviripattinam** |
| Salt merchants were called *chattu* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***UMANAR*** were the **salt merchants** *(travelling in bullock carts with their families)*; ***CHATTU*** = the itinerant/mobile merchants |
| Roman coins circulated as legal currency | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Roman coins circulated **AS BULLION**"* — valued by metal, and found in hoards especially in the **COIMBATORE** region |
| *Manimegalai* mentions only Tamil craftsmen | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MAGADHA artisans, MARATHA mechanics, MALVA smiths and YAVANA carpenters** working with Tamil craftsmen |
| Buddhism was stronger than Jainism in Tamil Nadu | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REVERSED** — the **numerous cave shelters with Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions show JAINISM was MORE INFLUENTIAL**; Buddhist evidence is *"restricted to a few sites"* |
| *Varnasrama* was well established in Sangam Tamizhagam | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"the **varnasrama ideology was YET TO TAKE ROOT** in the Tamil region"* |
| Keeladi is on the Thamiraparani | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **On the VAIGAI.** *(**Adichanallur** and **Sivakalai** are on the **Thamiraparani/Porunai**; **Korkai** is the ancient port)* |
| Adichanallur was first excavated by the ASI | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **By the German explorer Dr. JAGOR in 1876**; then **Alexander Rea, 1899–1905**; the **ASI in 2004** |
| The Keeladi dates are settled fact | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **They are the excavators' AMS results and interpretations** *(580 BCE at 353 cm; Sivakalai paddy at 1155 BCE)* **and remain debated.** Report as claims |
| About 100 Sangam poets were women | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Of over 450 poets, ABOUT THIRTY were women**, composing **more than 150 poems**; **AVVAIYAR** was the most prolific |

### 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | How to build it |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Was the Sangam polity a state or a chiefdom?"** *(the standing "critically examine")* | ⭐ **Both positions, then the four arguments and five counter-arguments** *(see §6.1)*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the resolution the textbook makes available: the *THINAI* framework shows UNEVEN development — *Marutham* looks state-like, the marginal zones remain chiefdoms.** **Verdict: a state forming unevenly, fastest where irrigation made surplus possible** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The *thinai* concept as social geography"** | ⭐ **The five zones with occupations and deities**, then the analytical payoff — *"reflects the UNEVEN SOCIO-ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENTS of the different localities. **That is seen in the political forms too**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then map polity onto ecology: *vendar* on *marutham*, *velir* on hill and forest, cattle-raiding out of *palai*.** ⭐ **Carry it forward to the Chola *nadu*, defined around irrigation sources** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Indo-Roman trade and its evidence"** | ⭐ **Literary** *(Periplus, Pliny, Ptolemy, the Peutingerian Table)* · **archaeological** *(Roman amphorae and glassware at the ports; Arikamedu, Pattanam)* · **numismatic** *(gold and silver hoards, esp. **Coimbatore**; coins as **BULLION**)* · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **and the killer detail — TAMIL-BRAHMI POTSHERDS AT BERENIKE AND QUSEIR AL QADHIM IN EGYPT.** **Close with the structural point: bullion flowed one way, as it would again in the seventeenth century** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Why the 'Kalabhra dark age' is no longer accepted"** | ⭐ **The three-part refutation** — **the literature** *(Thirukkural, Silappathikaram, Manimekalai)*, ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **the MOTIVE** *(orthodox Vedic-Puranic scholars characterising rulers who patronised Jainism and Buddhism as "evil in nature")*, **and the transition reading** *(toward Pallava and Pandya state societies)*. ⭐ **Then the transferable lesson: a periodisation label is evidence about its authors** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Land grants and the origins of social hierarchy in South India"** | ⭐ **Start with the SATAVAHANAS** — the *Naneghat* tax exemptions, beneficiaries "mostly Buddhists and Brahmins", and *"a group of people who DID NOT CULTIVATE, BUT OWNED LAND."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then trace it forward to the Chola *brahmadeya*, the *brahmadeya-kilavar*, *devadana*, and *KUDI NEEKKI*** ([Unit 11](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md)). **One institution, eight centuries** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Sangam literature as a historical source"** | ⭐ **Its scale and structure** *(2,400 poems, 450+ poets, the anthologies)* · **what it uniquely gives** *(thinai, social life, women's voices)* · ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **and its limits — kings PAID poets in gold "to glorify their name and fame", so it is partly COMMISSIONED PRAISE; the *Silappathikaram*'s northern expedition is absent from the contemporary poems; and Pandya "succession and regnal years are NOT CLEAR."** **Remedy: Greco-Roman texts, Tamil-Brahmi epigraphy and archaeology, which sit outside the patronage system** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Women in Sangam society"** | ⭐ **Thirty named women poets of 450+, over 150 poems, AVVAIYAR foremost** · *"women were **actively engaged in economic production**"* · **the *thinai* poems as evidence of everyday life**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the comparative point: these women are NAMED, whereas [Mughal-era women petitioners had their names excluded from the record](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md). The difference is in the archive, not only in the society** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Jainism and Buddhism in the Tamil country"** | ⭐ **Jainism was the stronger** — *"numerous cave shelters with Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions"*, funded by **merchants and lay devotees**; Buddhism *"restricted to a few sites"* in TN though strong in Andhra *(Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda)*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the consequence: this is what the bhakti Nayanars and Alvars later defined themselves AGAINST**, and why [Arikesari is remembered as the persecutor of Jains](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md) |

| ***Tholkappiyam*** | **The earliest extant Tamil grammar** — also on society and culture |
| ***Ettuthogai* / *Paththuppattu*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Eight Anthologies / the Ten Idylls** |

| ***Puratthinai*** | ⭐ The **"outer" poetic theme** — war and public life *(warfare for territorial expansion is its major subject)* |

| ***Asvamedha* / *Rajasuyam*** | **Vedic royal sacrifices** — performed by **Gautamiputra Satakarni** and **Perunarkilli** respectively |
| **Naneghat inscription** | ⭐ Records **Satavahana tax exemptions on land granted to Buddhist monks** |
| ***Gaha Sattasai*** | **700 love poems in Maharashtri Prakrit**, by the Satavahana king **HALA** |
| **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ A **Greek text of the 1st century CE** — calls Korkai ***Kolkoi*** |

| **Arretine ware** | ⭐ **Roman red earthenware of the 2nd century BCE**, resembled by finds at Keeladi |

## 🔁 Recap — the unit in ten lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Tamil kingdoms enter history in ASHOKA'S ROCK EDICT II**, which names the **Cholas, Pandyas, Keralaputras and Satiyaputra** as **independent neighbours beyond the empire** — and records medical provision for **humans and animals** there.
2. ⭐ **The SATAVAHANAS built the first big Deccan state**, and their **land grants to Buddhists and Brahmins** created *"a group of people who did not cultivate, but owned land"* — **the origin of South India's land-based hierarchy.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The *MUVENDAR*** — **Cholas** *(Uraiyur, Puhar, TIGER, Karikalan)*, **Cheras** *(Vanji, Musiri, BOW AND ARROW, Chenguttuvan)*, **Pandyas** *(Madurai, Korkai, FISH, Nedunchezhiyan)*.
4. ⭐ **KARIKALAN won at VENNI against the Cheras, Pandyas and ELEVEN Velir chiefs**, and is credited with **embanking the Kaveri and building reservoirs** — the ancestor the [Later Cholas](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md) would claim.
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The *THINAI* divided Tamizhagam into five eco-zones**, each with its own occupation — and **the politics followed the ecology**: *vendar* on the irrigated *marutham*, *velir* in hill and forest, cattle-raiding out of the parched *palai*.
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Whether this was a STATE or a PRE-STATE CHIEFDOM is an open debate** — and the strongest evidence for statehood is the evidence from **outside the praise-poetry**: **taxation at highways and Kaviripattinam**, and **Greco-Roman corroboration**.
7. ⚠️ **The Sangam corpus is partly COMMISSIONED** — kings gave poets gold *"to glorify their name and fame"*, and the *Silappathikaram*'s northern expedition is **absent from the contemporary poems**.
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Indo-Roman trade was real and physical** — **Roman amphorae and glassware at the ports, gold hoards in Coimbatore, coins circulating AS BULLION**, and ⭐ **Tamil-Brahmi potsherds at BERENIKE and QUSEIR AL QADHIM in EGYPT.**
9. ⭐ **In the Tamil country JAINISM was more influential than Buddhism** *(the cave shelters prove it)*, and ***varnasrama* had NOT yet taken root.**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the "KALABHRA DARK AGE" has been abolished** — the period produced the **Thirukkural** and both epics, and the "darkness" was **a characterisation written by orthodox scholars about rulers who patronised Jainism and Buddhism.** *Always ask who applied the label.*
