## 1️⃣ The Cholas — origin and the imperial line ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHY "LATER" OR "IMPERIAL" CHOLAS -- the naming
      is itself a Prelims point **
   ================================================================
   ** The Cholas were one of the THREE crowned kings (MUVENDHAR)
      of Sangam-age Tamizhagam ** -- alongside Cheras and Pandyas.

   ⚠️ ** THEN THEY VANISH FROM THE RECORD: "after the Sangam
      period until about the NINTH CENTURY CE, there are NO
      RECORDS about them." **

   ** They re-emerge as SUBORDINATES TO THE PALLAVAS in the
      Kaveri region -- and then: **

     ⭐ ** VIJAYALAYA (850-871) ** conquered the ** KAVERI DELTA **
        from the ** MUTTARAIYAR ** , built the city of
        ** THANJAVUR ** , and established the kingdom in ** 850. **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** HENCE "LATER CHOLAS" or "IMPERIAL CHOLAS" ** --
      to distinguish this ninth-century dynasty from the
      Sangam-age Cholas.
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GENEALOGY IS A CLAIM, NOT A FACT — AND THAT IS THE INTERESTING PART.** ⭐ **The copper plates of Vijayalaya's successors trace their ancestry to *KARIKALA*, the most famous Sangam-age Chola, and name *KILLI, KOCHENGANAN and KARIKALAN* in the line, with an eponymous king "Chola" as progenitor.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A dynasty that was a Pallava subordinate is reaching back across a five-century documentary gap to attach itself to a heroic age.** *(⭐ **Exactly the move [Vijayanagara made with *"Hindu Suratrana"* and the Virupaksha signature](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md), and the Mughals made by calling themselves TIMURIDS. **New power buys old ancestry.**)*

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE LINE TO FIX -- four rulers carry the whole story **
   ================================================================
   ** VIJAYALAYA      (850-871)   ** founder; took Thanjavur
   ** PARANTAKA I     (907-955)   ** "set the tone for expansion
                                     of the territory and
                                     broadened the base of its
                                     governance"
                                  ⭐ the UTTARAMERUR inscriptions
                                     (919, 921) are HIS reign
   ** RAJARAJA I      (985-1014)  ** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ builder of the
                                     BRIHADISHVARA temple,
                                     Thanjavur
   ** RAJENDRA I      (1014-1044) ** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ naval expedition to
                                     SRI VIJAYA; Gangaikonda
                                     Cholapuram
   ** KULOTHUNGA III  (1163-1216) ** the last of the great line
```

### 1.1 Territory — and the word *Coromandel* ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ** The Chola country = CHONADU or CHOLANADU. **
   ** The core, the Kaveri-fed delta = ** CHOLAMANDALAM. **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "This term came to be CORRUPTED AS 'COROMANDEL' IN
      THE EUROPEAN LANGUAGES" ** -- which the Europeans then
      applied to ** the entire eastern coast of South India. **

   ================================================================
   ** THE FOUR CONQUERED REGIONS -- learn the Tamil names **
   ================================================================
   ** TONDAINADU **      -> the NORTHERN portion of Tamizh country
   ** PANDINADU **       -> the SOUTHERN portion
   ** GANGAIVADI **      -> portions of SOUTHERN KARNATAKA
   ** MALAIMANDALAM **   -> the KERALA territory
   ⭐ ** MUMMUDI-CHOLAMANDALAM ** -> north-eastern SRI LANKA
```

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Coromandel" is a lovely one-line answer to a question about linguistic traces of Indian polities in European usage** — *a dynasty's name for its own rice-delta became Europe's name for a thousand kilometres of coastline.*

## 2️⃣ Empire building — Rajaraja I and Rajendra I ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** RAJARAJA I (985-1014) -- "the most celebrated of the Chola
      kings" **
   ================================================================
   * ** Naval expeditions; victorious on the WEST COAST and in
     SRI LANKA; ⭐ CONQUERED THE MALDIVES in the Indian Ocean **
   * ** Sri Lanka's northern and eastern portions came under
     DIRECT Chola control ** ; he appointed a Tamil chief to
     govern, and ordered a temple built -- locally called
     ** SIVA DEVALE ** (shrine of Siva)
   * ** The Chola official in Sri Lanka built a temple at
     MAHATITTA, called RAJARAJESVARA **
   * ⭐ ** Invaded the WESTERN CHALUKYA empire in 1003 **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** Built the BRIHADISHVARA temple at THANJAVUR **
   * ⭐ ** Standardised revenue collection (see §4) **

   ================================================================
   ** RAJENDRA I (1014-1044) **
   ================================================================
   ⭐ ** APPOINTED HEIR APPARENT WHILE HIS FATHER LIVED -- they
      ruled JOINTLY FOR TWO YEARS. ** (A deliberate solution to
      the succession problem; contrast the Mughals, who had
      ** no law of succession ** and so had a fratricidal war
      every time.)

   * ** Attacked the Western Chalukyas; the boundary extended to
     the TUNGABHADRA river **
   * ⭐ ** When Rajaraja I attacked Madurai, the PANDYAS ESCAPED
     WITH THEIR CROWN AND ROYAL JEWELS to SRI LANKA -- Rajendra
     conquered Sri Lanka and CONFISCATED THE PANDYA CROWN **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** 1023: the expedition to NORTHERN INDIA ** -- led
     it up to the ** GODAVARI ** , and sent his general beyond.
     ** The GANGAIKONDA CHOLAPURAM temple was built to
     commemorate it **
   * ** Invaded the Western Chalukyas in 1009; sent his son to
     ransack KALYANI. ⭐ The DWARAPALA (doorkeeper) image brought
     from Kalyani was installed at the DARASURAM temple near
     Kumbhakonam -- and can be seen today **
```

### 2.1 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Sri Vijaya expedition — the most examinable single fact

```
   ================================================================
   ** "During the Chola reign, the NAVAL ACHIEVEMENTS OF THE
      TAMILS REACHED ITS PEAK." **
   ================================================================
   * ** The Cholas controlled BOTH the COROMANDEL and MALABAR
     coasts **
   * ** The Chola navy ventured into the BAY OF BENGAL for some
     decades **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** Rajendra's naval operation was directed against
     SRI VIJAYA -- "southern Sumatra… one of the prominent
     MARITIME AND COMMERCIAL STATES that flourished from
     c. 700 to c. 1300 in South-east Asia" **
   * ⭐ ** KHEDA (KADARAM), a FEUDATORY kingdom under Sri Vijaya,
     was also conquered **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** RAJENDRA I'S FOUR TITLES -- each names a conquest.
      A perfect match-the-pairs. **
   ================================================================
   ** MUDIKONDA CHOLAN **   -> "the crowned Chola"
   ** GANGAIKONDAN **       -> "conqueror of the GANGES"
   ** KADARAMKONDAN **      -> "conqueror of KADARAM"
   ** PANDITA CHOLAN **     -> "the scholarly Chola"
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WHY AN INDIAN KING ATTACKED SUMATRA — and this is the Mains-grade point, not the fact itself.** ⭐ **Sri Vijaya sat astride the Strait of Malacca, the choke point of the China trade.** *§5 will show you that the Chola economy ran on maritime commerce and that the merchant guild ***ainutruvar*** *"controlled the maritime trade covering South-east Asian countries."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the expedition was not a raid for glory — it was a state acting to keep a trade corridor open on behalf of its merchants.** *(⭐ **That makes the Cholas the one Indian dynasty with a genuinely* **blue-water** *strategic doctrine, and it is why the Indian Navy's own historiography invokes them.**)*

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Chola administration — and the four locality bodies

### 3.1 The king

```
   ** A HEREDITARY MONARCHY, and the king was "VENERATED ON PAR
      WITH GOD." **

   ** TITLES: **
   * ** PERUMAN / PERUMAGAN ** -- "big man"
   * ** ULAGUDAIYAPERUMAL, ULAGUDAIYANAYANAR ** -- "the lord of
     the world"
   * ** Later: CHAKKARAVARTI (emperor), TIRIBHUVANA
     CHAKKARAVARTI (emperor of three worlds) **
   * ⭐ ** At coronation the suffix DEVA was added to the name **
   * ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** Legitimacy was claimed by being "a COMRADE OF GOD"
     -- THAMBIRAN THOZHAR **

   ** BRAHMINS as spiritual preceptors -- RAJAGURUS ("the
      kingdom's guide"). Rajaraja I and Rajendra I name rajagurus
      and SARVA-SIVAS in their inscriptions. ⭐ "Patronising
      Brahmins was seen to ENHANCE THEIR PRESTIGE AND LEGITIMACY"
      -- hence huge land grants as BRAHMADEYAMS and
      CHATURVEDIMANGALAMS. **
```

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***THAMBIRAN THOZHAR* — "comrade of god" — is worth noticing beside [Vijayanagara's *"Shri Virupaksha"* signature](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md).** ⭐ *Vijayanagara's kings claimed to rule **ON BEHALF OF** the god — an executive. **The Chola king claims to be the god's COMRADE — a peer.*** *A subtly bolder claim, and both are doing the same work.*

### 3.2 Provinces and army

```
   ** PROVINCES: Rajaraja I integrated the conquered territories
      and appointed "VICEROYS": **
     ** CHOLA-PANDYA **      in Pandinadu
     ** CHOLA-LANKESWARA **  in Sri Lanka (Mummudi-cholamandalam)
     ** CHOLA-GANGA **       in Gangavadi, southern Karnataka

   ⭐ ** Lesser chiefs -- the IRUKKUVELS, ILANGOVELS, MAZHAVAS,
      BANAS -- were "inducted into the state system as its
      FUNCTIONARIES" ** rather than destroyed.

   ================================================================
   ** THE ARMY -- three conventional divisions **
   ================================================================
   ** INFANTRY ** · ** CAVALRY (kudirai sevagar) ** ·
   ** ELEPHANT CORPS (anaiyatkal) **

   ** Also: BOWMEN (villaligal) · SWORD-BEARERS (valilar) ·
      SPEARMEN (konduvar) **
   ⭐ ** TWO RANKS: PERUNDANAM (upper) and CIRUDANAM (lower) **

   ** A 13th-century CHINESE GEOGRAPHER: the Chola army owned
      ⭐ "SIXTY THOUSAND WAR ELEPHANTS that, when fighting,
      carried on their backs HOUSES… full of soldiers who shoot
      arrows at long range." ** The navy is described as
      ** "with NUMBERLESS SHIPS." **

   ** VOCABULARY: **
   * ** PADAIPPARRU **  -- military holding rights soldiers enjoyed
   * ** PADAIVIDU **    -- cantonments in the capital
   * ** NILAIPADAI **   -- military outposts in conquered territory
   * ** NAYAGAM **      -- captain of a regiment (later PADAIMUDALI)
   * ** SENAPATI / DANDANAYAGAM ** -- commander-in-chief
```

### 3.3 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE FOUR LOCALITY BODIES — the heart of the chapter

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT's own framing, and it is the sentence to quote:** *these bodies were **"relatively AUTONOMOUS organisations"** and **"they are considered THE BUILDING BLOCKS USING WHICH THE EDIFICE OF THE CHOLA STATE WAS BUILT."***

| Body | Its settlement | Who they were | What they did |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **URAR** | ***UR*** — a **peasant settlement** | **Landholders in the village**, acting as **spokesmen** | Upkeep of **temples**; maintenance of **tanks** and the water in them; **revenue collection**, **law and order**, obeying royal orders |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SABHAIYAR** | ***BRAHMADEYA*** — a **Brahmin settlement** | The ***SABHA*** | Affairs of the settlement **including the temples at its core and its assets**; **irrigation tanks attached to temple lands**; and — like the *ur* — **administrative, fiscal and judicial** functions **as agents of the state** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NAGARATTAR** | ***NAGARAM*** — a **traders' settlement** *(also skilled artisans: masonry, ironsmithing, goldsmithing, weaving, pottery)* | The ***NAGARATTAR*** | Regulated the settlement's **association with temples**, which needed their **financial assistance** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NATTAR** | ***NADU*** — **a grouping of several *urs*, EXCLUDING *brahmadeyas*, formed around irrigation sources** | **Assembly of the landholders of *vellanvagai* villages** | ⭐ *"functioned as **PILLARS OF THE STATE STRUCTURE**"* — administrative, fiscal and judicial duties; held **hereditary land rights**; **remitted the nadu's tax to the state** |

```
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** NOTICE THE DEFINITION OF *NADU* -- it is the
      subtlest and most examinable thing here: **

      a nadu is formed ** AROUND IRRIGATION SOURCES ** -- canals
      and tanks -- and it ** EXCLUDES the brahmadeyas. **

   ⭐ ** So the Chola state's basic administrative unit was
      defined by ** WATER ** , not by distance or by lineage --
      and the tax-exempt Brahmin villages sat OUTSIDE it,
      governed separately by their own Sabha. **

   ** NATTAR honorific titles: **
     ** UDAIYAN ** (possessor of land) · ** ARAIYAN ** (leader)
     · ** KILAVAN ** (headman)
   ** NATTAR functionaries: NAATTUKANAKKU and NATTUVIYAVAN
      recorded the proceedings. **

   ⭐ ** NAGARAM detail: in Rajaraja I's reign MAMALLAPURAM was
      administered by a body called ** MAANAGARAM. ** Goods
      exchanged in nagarams, per Chinese accounts: ** SILK,
      PORCELAIN, CAMPHOR, CLOVES, SANDALWOOD, CARDAMOM. **
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** KULOTUNGA revoked the toll fee (SUNGAM) to promote
      inland and sea trade -- hence his title
      ** 'SUNGAM THAVIRTHA CHOZHAN'. ** **
```

### 3.4 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ UTTARAMERUR — the single most examined South Indian inscription

**📖 THE SOURCE — two inscriptions, dated ⭐ *919 and 921 CE*, from a *brahmadeya* called **UTTARAMERUR** *(historically **Uttaramallur Caturvedimangalam**)*, in the reign of **PARANTAKA I**.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE PROCEDURE -- learn every step; this is the whole point **
   ================================================================

   ** 1. THE WARDS **
      ⭐ "This village was divided into ** THIRTY WARDS. **
         ** ONE MEMBER was to be elected FROM EACH WARD. ** "

   ** 2. THE FIVE COMMITTEES ** these members would join:
      ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
      * ** PUBLIC WORKS committee **
      * ** TANK committee **
      * ** GARDEN committee **
      * ** FAMINE committee **
      * ** GOLD committee **

   ** 3. THE QUALIFICATIONS ** -- "clearly spelt out":
      * ** MALE **
      * ⭐ ** ABOVE 35 BUT BELOW 70 **
      * ** having A SHARE OF PROPERTY **
      * ** and A HOUSE OF HIS OWN **
      * ⭐ ** with KNOWLEDGE OF THE VEDAS AND BHASYAS **

   ** 4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE BALLOT -- the KUDAVOLAI ("pot-ticket") **
      * ** "The names of qualified candidates from each ward were
        written on PALM-LEAF SLIPS and PUT INTO A POT." **
      * ** "THE ELDEST OF THE ASSEMBLY ENGAGED A BOY TO PULL OUT
        ONE SLIP and would READ THE NAME of the person
        selected." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ THE QUALIFICATIONS HONESTLY — THIS IS WHERE MOST ANSWERS GO WRONG, AND WHERE THE MARKS ARE.** ⭐ **This is routinely presented as "ancient Indian democracy." It was not.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The franchise was restricted to **men**, aged **35–70**, who owned **property** and a **house**, and who knew the **Vedas and their commentaries** — and it operated inside a ***BRAHMADEYA*, a tax-exempt Brahmin settlement**, not an ordinary village.**
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The accurate formulation, and the one to write: *Uttaramerur records a sophisticated, rule-bound, WRITTEN electoral procedure for a narrow propertied and lettered élite.*** ⭐ **What is genuinely remarkable is not inclusiveness but **PROCEDURE** — codified eligibility, ward-wise representation, functional committees, and a **randomised draw** rather than a show of hands.** *(⭐⭐ **And the *kudavolai* is a lottery, not a vote — a boy drawing a slip. **That removes canvassing and factional pressure at a stroke**, which is a genuinely thoughtful piece of institutional design.**)*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The GS2 comparison examiners like: set Uttaramerur beside the *73rd Amendment (1992)*.** ⭐ **Shared: ward-based representation, functional committees, local control of water and public works.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Opposite: the modern panchayat rests on **universal adult franchise with reservation for women and SC/ST** — precisely inverting a system whose entry test was property, age, sex and Vedic learning.** *Say both halves and the answer is complete.*

## 4️⃣ Economy — revenue, survey and the irrigation system ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE REVENUE DEPARTMENT **
   ================================================================
   ⭐ ** PURAVUVARI-TINAIKKALAM ** -- the "department of land
      revenue"; its chief the
      ** PURAVUVARI-TINAIKKALANAYAGAM **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE THREE GREAT LAND SURVEYS -- dates are
      examinable **
   ================================================================
   ** RAJARAJA I      -- 1001 **
   ** KULOTUNGA I     -- 1086 **
   ** KULOTUNGA III   -- 1226 **
   ⭐ ** The surveyors, NADUVAGAISEYKIRA, "too hailed from the
      LANDHOLDING COMMUNITIES" ** -- i.e. the state surveyed
      through the very class it was taxing.

   ** UNITS of land measurement: KULI · MA · VELI · PATTI ·
      PADAGAM ** (with local variations)

   ================================================================
   ** THE TAXES **
   ================================================================
   ** IRAI · KANIKADAN · IRAIKATTINA-KANIKADAN · KADAMAI **
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** KUDIMAI ** -- "an important category", paid by
      ** CULTIVATING TENANTS ** to ** BOTH the government AND the
      landlords ** (the bearers of udaiyan, araiyan, kilavar)
   ** OPATI ** -- levied and collected by the king and local chiefs
   ** IRAIKATTINA-NELLU ** -- tax paid in kind

   ⭐ ** RATES were fixed "depending on THE FERTILITY OF THE SOIL
      and THE STATUS OF THE LANDHOLDER." **
   ⚠️ ** TEMPLES AND BRAHMINS WERE EXEMPTED FROM PAYING TAXES. **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** RAJARAJA I'S STANDARDISATION -- a clean number **
   ================================================================
   ** Paddy tax was collected by a unit called KALAM (28 kg). **
   ** Rajaraja I collected ** 100 KALAM from one VELI
      (about 6.5 acres) ** , "the standard veli being variable
      according to fertility of the soil and the number of crops
      raised." **

   ⚠️ ** HONEST LIMIT: "All these were MOSTLY REALISED FROM THE
      KAVERY DELTA BUT NOT WIDELY IN THE OUTSKIRTS of the
      kingdom." **
   ** Collection: URAR at the ur level, NATTAR at the nadu level. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That last caveat is worth carrying into an answer, because it is the same finding as [Theme 8's *jama* against *hasil*](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md).** ⭐ **A well-documented revenue system tells you what the state INTENDED. This sentence tells you where it actually reached — the delta, and not much beyond.**

### 4.1 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The *vati–vaykkal* system — a GS3-usable piece of engineering

>

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BEST CAUSAL CHAIN IN THE WHOLE UNIT, AND IT IS EXACTLY WHAT A MAINS ANSWER ON THE SULTANATE'S SOUTHWARD EXPANSION NEEDS.** ⭐ **A father names one son co-regent → the other son murders him → the loser of the civil war goes to Delhi for help → Malik Kafur arrives in 1311 → the loot funds Alauddin Khalji's factional politics in Delhi → a Sultanate province is planted in Madurai → it declares independence in 1335 → and Vijayanagara's Kumarakampana destroys it a generation later.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note the honest detail that stops this being a simple conquest narrative: *Malik Kafur found the city EMPTY.*** ⭐ **The most celebrated southern campaign of the Delhi Sultanate met no army — it was a **treasure raid into a kingdom that had already destroyed itself.*** *(⭐ And the loot's destination is the sharpest fact of all: **it was spent buying Delhi nobles' loyalty.** The wealth of Madurai financed a succession dispute two thousand kilometres away.)*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The horse trade is the strongest cross-chapter link this unit offers, and you should make it.** ⭐ **[Theme 7 §2.1](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) says: *"warfare during these times depended upon effective cavalry, [so] the import of horses from Arabia and Central Asia was very important"*, controlled initially by **Arab traders**, with local ***KUDIRAI CHETTIS*** participating, and the **Deccan Sultans called *ashvapati*, "lord of horses."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is the SAME word — *kudirai chetti* — and the same structural dependence, two centuries earlier, in the Pandya country.**
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the one-line thesis for any answer on peninsular geopolitics: *South India could not breed war-horses, so every southern power from the Pandyas to Vijayanagara depended on an Arab-controlled maritime import, and whoever held the ports held the cavalry.*** *That single dependency explains Kayal, explains Goa in 1510, and explains why the Portuguese mattered so quickly.*

## 🔁 Recap — the unit in ten lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The "Later Cholas" are a NINTH-CENTURY dynasty, not a Sangam continuation** — **Vijayalaya took the Kaveri delta from the Muttaraiyar in 850** and built **Thanjavur**, after a five-century documentary blank in which the Cholas were **Pallava subordinates**. Their descent from **Karikala** is a **later claim in copper plates**.
2. ⭐ **Four rulers carry the story** — **Parantaka I** *(Uttaramerur, 919/921)*, **Rajaraja I** *(Brihadishvara, the Maldives, revenue standardisation)*, **Rajendra I** *(Sri Vijaya, the Ganges expedition, Gangaikonda Cholapuram)*, and **Kulothunga III**.
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The state rested on FOUR autonomous locality bodies** — ***urar*, *sabhaiyar*, *nagarattar*, *nattar*** — described as *"the building blocks using which the edifice of the Chola state was built."* ⭐ **And the *nadu* was defined by WATER, not distance.**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Uttaramerur records a real electoral PROCEDURE** — **30 wards, 5 committees, a written eligibility test, and a *kudavolai* LOTTERY drawn by a boy** — **inside a tax-exempt Brahmin village, for propertied literate men aged 35–70.** *Procedure, not democracy.*
5. ⭐ **Revenue rested on three great surveys (1001, 1086, 1226)**, an elaborate tax vocabulary, and **exemptions for temples and Brahmins** — but was *"mostly realised from the Kavery delta."*
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Water was the state's real business** — the ***vati–vaykkal*** criss-cross system, **assignable water rights**, a **tax ring-fenced for tank repair**, and Rajendra's **sixteen-mile embankment he called his "PILLAR OF VICTORY IN WATER."**
7. ⭐ **The temple was a bank, a school, an employer and a landholder**, with audited accounts — **and in famine, oil-pressers sold themselves to it as servants.**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Rajendra I attacked SRI VIJAYA because his merchants' guild, the *AINUTRUVAR*, controlled the South-east Asian trade** — the one genuinely blue-water Indian strategic doctrine.
9. ⭐ **The Cholas fell to the PANDYAS in 1279**, after local chiefs like the **Sambuvarayars** hollowed out the centre — and the Pandyas rose to their **zenith under Jatavarman Sundarapandyan (1251–68)**, reaching **Nellore**.
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then a family quarrel destroyed them:** a co-regency in **1302** → **a son murdered his father** → the losing brother **fled to Alauddin Khalji** → **Malik Kafur reached Madurai in 1311 and found the city EMPTY** → the loot **bought Delhi nobles' loyalty** → a **Madurai Sultanate** stood until **1335**, and Vijayanagara's **Kumarakampana** finished it.
