# 🏵️ PART A — HARSHA (606–647 CE)

## 1️⃣ The political map Harsha inherited ⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** "NORTH INDIA SPLINTERED INTO SEVERAL WARRIOR KINGDOMS
      AFTER THE DOWNFALL OF THE GUPTA EMPIRE. " **
   ================================================================

   ⭐ ** "Excepting in the areas that were SUBDUED BY THE HUNS
      ( modern PUNJAB, RAJASTHAN and MALWA ), ** REGIONAL
      IDENTITY BECAME PRONOUNCED ** with the emergence of many
      small states." **

   ** MAITHRAKAS ** ... a powerful state in ** SOURASHTRA
                      (GUJARAT) ** , capital ** VALABHI **
   ** MAUKHARIS ** ... ** AGRA and AWADH ** organised into "an
                      INDEPENDENT AND SOVEREIGN STATE " ,
                      capital ** KANAUJ **
   ** VAKATAKAS ** ... "had RECOVERED their position of
                      ascendency in the ** WESTERN DECCAN ** "
   ⭐ ** PUSHYABHUTIS ** ... ** THANESWAR ** , "lying NORTH OF
                      DELHI, ** BETWEEN SUTLEJ AND YAMUNA ** " --
                      ** "It rose to prominence under HARSHA. " **

   ** And the SUMMARY adds: the ** LATER GUPTAS of MAGADHA ** and
      ** YASODHARMAN of MANDASOR ** as sub-regional kingdoms. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE KEY SENTENCE IS *"REGIONAL IDENTITY BECAME PRONOUNCED"*, AND IT IS THE THESIS OF THE WHOLE UNIT.** ⭐ **The post-Gupta centuries are usually taught as a failure — the absence of an empire.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This unit teaches them as the PRESENCE of something else: the moment when Gujarat, Bengal, Kanauj and the Deccan became durable political units with their own languages, dynasties and art.** *(⭐ **And that is the same argument [Unit 6's conclusion](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md) makes about the post-Mauryan centuries — *"fragmentation is not the same as decline."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Here the evidence is even stronger: the Pala bronzes, the Rashtrakuta rock-cut temples, and the birth of KANNADA as a literary language all belong to this "fragmented" period, not to an imperial one.**)*

**📚 THE SOURCES — and Bana's significance is the point:**

| Type | Sources |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Literary** | **BANA's *HARSHACHARITA*** · **HIEUN TSANG's *SI-YU-KI*** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Epigraphical** | **MADHUBAN copper plate** · **SONPAT inscription on copper seal** · **BANSKHERA copper plate** · **NALANDA inscription on clay seals** · ⭐ **the AIHOLE inscription** *(i.e. the enemy's record — see [Unit 9](03_South_Indian_Culture_and_Temples.md))* |

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BOX WORTH MEMORISING:** *"**Bana's *HARSHACHARITA* was THE FIRST FORMAL BIOGRAPHY OF A KING. It INAUGURATED A NEW LITERARY GENRE IN INDIA.**"*
>
> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Notice what that means for source criticism, because it is the same lesson in a new form.** ⭐ **A *prashasti* praises a king in verse on stone; a *charita* narrates his LIFE in prose at length — and Bana was Harsha's own court poet.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the first Indian royal biography is also the first Indian authorised biography.** *(⭐ **Set it beside [Theme 2's rule](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/02_Theme2_Kings_Farmers_and_Towns.md) — *"treasure them as works of POETRY rather than as accounts that were LITERALLY TRUE"* — and beside the [*Kuram* plates](03_South_Indian_Culture_and_Temples.md) and the [Mughal chronicles](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/09_Supplement_Kings_and_Chronicles_The_Mughal_Courts.md). ⭐ **And note the elegant check available here: the AIHOLE INSCRIPTION, commissioned by Pulikesin II's poet Ravikirti, records the SAME war from the losing side's perspective. Two commissioned poets, one conflict — which is exactly the situation [Unit 9 §1](03_South_Indian_Culture_and_Temples.md) describes.**)*

## 2️⃣ The Pushyabhutis, and how Harsha got Kanauj ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

| Ruler | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **PUSHYABHUTI** | **Founder of the VARDHANA dynasty**, ruling from **THANESWAR**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"He served as **A MILITARY GENERAL UNDER THE IMPERIAL GUPTAS** and rose to power **after the fall of the Guptas**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PRABHAKARA VARDHANA** *(580–605)* | *"the Pushyabhuti family became **strong and powerful**."* **Fought the GURJARAS and the HUNS**, establishing authority **as far as MALWA and GUJARAT**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gave his daughter RAJYASRI in marriage to the MAUKHARI king GRAHAVARMAN of KANAUJ — "thus making Kanauj HIS ALLY"** |
| **RAJYAVARDHANA** *(605–606)* | The eldest son — ⚠️ ***"TREACHEROUSLY MURDERED by SASANKA, the GAUDA ruler of BENGAL"*** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HARSHAVARDHANA** *(606–647)* | *"Harsha had **KNOWN THE WEAKNESS OF A GROUP OF SMALL KINGDOMS** and conquered his neighbours **to INTEGRATE THEM INTO HIS EMPIRE**"* |

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  HOW HARSHA BECAME KING OF KANAUJ -- A SET
              QUESTION, AND ENTIRELY ABSENT FROM YOUR NOTES
   ================================================================

   ** "The MAGNATES OF KANAUJ ( the capital of the MAUKHARI
      kingdom ), on the advice of their minister ** PONI ** ,
      ** INVITED HARSHA TO ASCEND THE THRONE ** ." **

   ⭐ ** "A ** RELUCTANT HARSHA ** accepted the throne ** ON THE
      ADVICE OF AVALOKITESVARA BODHISATVA ** , with the title of
      ** RAJPUTRA ** and ** SILADITYA ** ." **

   ** "Thus THE TWO KINGDOMS OF THANESWAR AND KANAUJ BECAME
      UNITED under Harsha's rule. Consequently, Harsha
      TRANSFERRED HIS CAPITAL TO KANAUJ." **

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  WHY THE CAPITAL MOVED -- TWO REASONS, BOTH GIVEN
   ================================================================
     (1) ** "THANESAR WAS TOO CLOSE TO THE THREATS FROM THE
         NORTH-WEST. " **
     (2) ** "KANAUJ WAS LOCATED IN THE RICH AGRICULTURAL REGION
         OF THE WESTERN GANGES PLAIN. " **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE KANAUJ INVITATION IS THE MOST POLITICALLY INTERESTING FACT IN PART A, AND IT EXPLAINS SOMETHING THAT RUNS FOR THE NEXT FOUR CENTURIES.** ⭐ **Harsha did not conquer Kanauj. Its own nobles, having lost their king Grahavarman, INVITED his brother-in-law to take the throne — and he took it as a legitimate accession, not an annexation.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And that is why KANAUJ, not Thaneswar, became "the symbolic key to north Indian paramountcy": the union of the two kingdoms made it the seat of the largest state in the north, and every later contender — [Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas in the TRIPARTITE STRUGGLE](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_07_Our_Pasts_II/Notes/02_Kings_and_Kingdoms.md) — fought for the city Harsha had made central.** *(⭐ **Then note the shrewd geography of the move: away from the exposed north-western frontier, into the richest agricultural belt available. **[Unit 7 showed that Samudragupta's destruction of the Punjab tribal republics opened the corridor the Hunas came through](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md); Harsha, two centuries later, simply moved his capital out of range of it.**)*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the religious detail is worth keeping: he accepted *"on the advice of AVALOKITESVARA BODHISATVA."*** *A Buddhist deity's sanction is being cited for a political succession — which is [the Gupta *parama-bhagavata* manoeuvre](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md) in a Mahayana register.*

## 3️⃣ Military conquests ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**⚔️ First, Rajyasri — and the textbook's version differs slightly from your Class 6 notes:**

> ⭐ *"On his accession in 606 CE Harsha focused his attention on the affairs of his sister **RAJYASRI**, who **had fled to the VINDHYA MOUNTAINS to escape from the EVIL DESIGN OF DEVA GUPTA OF MALWA** to covet her. Harsha went to the Vindhya ranges and succeeded in **saving his sister, who was about to BURN HERSELF after KILLING DEVA GUPTA**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Later **Rajyasri TOOK TO BUDDHISM and was INSTRUMENTAL IN CONVERTING HARSHA TO BUDDHISM**."*

**📜 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ BANA's ULTIMATUM LIST — "either surrender or be prepared for a battle":**

| # | Target |
|:--:|---|
| **1** | ⭐ **SASANKA, the GAUDA ruler of BENGAL** |
| **2** | **The MAITHRAKAS of VALABHI** and the **GURJARA of BROACH** |
| **3** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The CHALUKYA king PULIKESIN II, in the Deccan** |
| **4** | **Rulers of SINDH, NEPAL, KASHMIR, MAGADHA, ODRA** *(northern Odisha)* **and KONGODA** |

**🗡️ How each went:**

| Opponent | Outcome |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **SASANKA** | Harsha allied with the king of **KAMARUPA (PRAGJYOTISHA)** — modern **ASSAM**. ⚠️ *"there is **HARDLY ANY DETAIL** of the war."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He "seems to have SUCCESSFULLY SUBJUGATED THE GAUDA EMPIRE — which included MAGADHA, GOUDA, ODRA and KONGODA — ONLY AFTER THE DEATH OF SASANKA"** |
| ⭐ **The MAITHRAKAS** | *"hostilities… **ended in the MARRIAGE of DHRUVABHATTA with the DAUGHTER OF HARSHA**. Soon, **Valabhi became a SUBORDINATE ALLY**"* |
| ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PULIKESIN II** | *"Harsha sought to extend his authority **southward into the Deccan**. **However the Chalukya king Pulikesin II, who controlled the region, HUMBLED HARSHA.** ⭐ **In commemoration of his victory over Harsha, PULIKESIN ASSUMED THE TITLE OF *PARAMESWARA*.** **Inscriptions in Pulikesin's capital VATAPI attest to this victory**"* |

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Sasanka line is a small masterpiece of careful phrasing and you should notice it: Harsha subjugated Gauda *"ONLY AFTER THE DEATH OF SASANKA."*** ⭐ **In other words, the man Harsha spent his reign vowing to destroy died of other causes, and only then did the territory fall in.** *[Class 6](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_06_Our_Pasts_I/Notes/09_New_Empires_and_Kingdoms.md) tells you Sasanka killed Rajyavardhana; this tells you Harsha never actually avenged it.* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A court biography naturally leaves that shape blurred — and the textbook's *"there is HARDLY ANY DETAIL of the war"* is the historian's way of saying the *Harshacharita* goes quiet exactly where a defeat or a stalemate would be.**

## 4️⃣ Extent, and the Chinese connection ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  THE CLAIM vs THE REALITY -- QUOTE BOTH HALVES
   ================================================================

   ** THE CLAIM: "it is CLAIMED that Harsha's Empire comprised
      ** ASSAM, BENGAL, BIHAR, KANAUJ, MALWA, ODISHA, THE PUNJAB,
      KASHMIR, NEPAL AND SINDH ** ." **

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE REALITY: "...his ** REAL SWAY DID NOT EXTEND
      BEYOND A COMPACT TERRITORY BETWEEN THE GANGES AND YAMUNA
      RIVERS ** ." **

   ** THE FEUDATORIES: ** JALANDHAR (Punjab), KASHMIR, NEPAL and
      VALABHI ** . ** SASANKA OF BENGAL REMAINED HOSTILE. ** **

   ** AND THE VERDICT: "The kingdom of Harsha ** DISINTEGRATED
      RAPIDLY INTO SMALL STATES AFTER HIS DEATH ** ." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GAP BETWEEN THE CLAIMED AND THE REAL EXTENT IS THE ANSWER TO ANY QUESTION ABOUT HARSHA'S "EMPIRE", AND THE PHRASE TO CARRY IS *"A COMPACT TERRITORY BETWEEN THE GANGES AND YAMUNA."*** ⭐ **Ten regions claimed; one doab actually administered; the rest held as feudatories, tributaries or simply asserted.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the proof is the ending: an empire that *"disintegrated RAPIDLY into small states after his death"* was never an integrated structure to begin with — it was a personal hegemony that could not outlive the person.** *(⭐ **Compare [Samudragupta, whose "DIRECT CONTROL was confined to the Ganges valley"](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md) despite a *prashasti* listing conquests to Kanchipuram.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two "great north Indian empires", three centuries apart, both administering the same doab and claiming the subcontinent. That recurrence is the real finding.**)*

**🇨🇳 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ HARSHA AND CHINA — and the sequel is extraordinary:**

> *"Harsha had **CORDIAL RELATIONS WITH CHINA**. The contemporary **T'ANG emperor, TAI TSUNG, sent an embassy to his court in 643 AND AGAIN IN 647 CE**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **On the second occasion, the Chinese ambassador found that HARSHA HAD RECENTLY DIED. On learning that THE THRONE HAD BEEN USURPED BY AN UNDESERVING KING, the Chinese ambassador RUSHED TO NEPAL AND ASSAM TO RAISE A FORCE TO DISLODGE THE USURPER. Later, the king who had usurped the throne WAS TAKEN TO CHINA AS A PRISONER.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS ONE OF THE STRANGEST AND BEST STORIES IN THE WHOLE BOOK, AND IT IS A GIFT FOR ANY QUESTION ON INDIA'S HISTORICAL LINKS WITH EAST ASIA.** ⭐ **A T'ang ambassador, arriving to find his host dead and the throne seized, does not withdraw — he crosses into NEPAL AND ASSAM, raises troops locally, overthrows the usurper, and carries him back to China as a prisoner.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A private diplomat conducting a military intervention on the strength of a dead king's friendship tells you how substantial the relationship must have been.** *(⭐ **The ambassador was **Wang Xuance**, and the episode is recorded from the Chinese side — which is what makes it usable: it is not a court biography's claim about its patron.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Deploy it with the Sailendra grant in §10 and Hieun Tsang's 657 volumes in §7, and you have three independent pieces of evidence for a functioning India–Asia network in the seventh century.**)*

## 5️⃣ Administration — and the debate about it ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  BURTON STEIN'S ARGUMENT -- THE BEST
              HISTORIOGRAPHY IN UNIT 8
   ================================================================

   ** "According to historian ** BURTON STEIN ** , ** A
      CENTRALISED ADMINISTRATION DID NOT EVEN EXIST UNDER THE
      POWERFUL GUPTAS ** . It was ** RESTRICTED ONLY TO THE
      CENTRAL PART OF THE GANGETIC PLAIN BETWEEN PATALIPUTRA AND
      MATHURA ** . ** BEYOND THAT ZONE, THERE WAS NO CENTRALISED
      AUTHORITY ** ." **

   ⭐ ** "The ONLY DIFFERENCE between Guptas and Vardhanas is
      that ** THE FORMER HAD FORMIDABLE ENEMIES LIKE HUNS, WHILE
      THE LATTER HAD NO SUCH OPPONENTS ** ." **
```

**📜 The evidence — and it is a single land grant:**

> ⭐ *"The **COPPER PLATES OF 632 CE** record **a gift of land to TWO BRAHMANS**. The names of certain political personages with state power, **as PROTECTORS OF THE GIFT**, are mentioned in them."* — and they fall into **three categories**:

| Category | Nature |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MAHASAMANTAS** | *"**allied to the king but of a SUBORDINATE STATUS**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Independent MAHARAJAS** | *"but **ACKNOWLEDGED FEUDATORIES of Harsha**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A third category** | *"rulers who **PLEDGED THEIR LOYALTY to Harsha and PROFESSED TO BE AT HIS SERVICE**"* |

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"THIS IS THE CHARACTERISTIC OF HARSHA'S IMPERIAL AUTHORITY IN NORTH INDIA."**

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE THREE CATEGORIES ARE A GRADIENT OF SUBORDINATION, NOT A CHAIN OF COMMAND, AND THAT DISTINCTION IS THE WHOLE ANSWER.** ⭐ **A *mahasamanta* is subordinate; an "independent *maharaja* who acknowledged feudatory status" is a contradiction the record simply lives with; and a ruler who merely *"professed to be at his service"* is subordinate only rhetorically.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **An empire whose subordinates come in three grades of not-quite-subjection is not centralised — it is a NETWORK OF NEGOTIATED RELATIONSHIPS held together by one man's prestige, which is exactly why it dissolved when he died.** *(⭐ **And notice the METHOD: the whole conclusion is drawn from the WITNESS LIST of one land grant. **Who is named as "protector of the gift" tells you who mattered, without anybody intending to record it** — which is [Theme 2's habit](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/02_Theme2_Kings_Farmers_and_Towns.md) of reading a source for what it lets slip.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This also connects straight to [Unit 7's *uparikas* titled *maharaja*](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md) and to R.S. Sharma's *samanta* feudalism. Stein is arguing that the "decline into feudalism" was never a decline, because the centralised state it supposedly declined FROM never existed.**)*

**🏛️ The council and the officials:**

The emperor was assisted by a **council of ministers (*MANTRI PARISHAD*)**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The council played an important role in THE SELECTION OF THE KING as well as FRAMING THE FOREIGN POLICY of the empire. **⚖️ Justice — harsher than the Guptas:**

LIFE IMPRISONMENT was the punishment for violations of the laws and for PLOTTING AGAINST THE KING.**"*

⭐ **Hieun Tsang's version, which is gentler:** *"**PERFECT LAW AND ORDER prevailed throughout the empire**, as the law-enforcing agencies were strong… **CORPORAL PUNISHMENT for serious offences was in practice, BUT THE DEATH PENALTY WAS USUALLY AVOIDED. Offences against social morality and defiance of law were punished by MAIMING.**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"Harsha **TRAVELLED ACROSS THE KINGDOM FREQUENTLY** to ensure his familiarity with his people. He was **ACCESSIBLE TO PEOPLE and kept a CLOSER WATCH ON HIS TRIBUTARY RULERS**."*

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The constant royal touring is not a personality trait — it is the administrative technique that a non-centralised state REQUIRES.** ⭐ **If your authority rests on three grades of feudatory who have only "professed to be at your service" (§5), you must keep appearing among them.** *(⭐ **The same logic as [Asoka's edict about receiving reports wherever he is](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/02_Theme2_Kings_Farmers_and_Towns.md), and the same as the Mughal itinerant court.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A king who cannot rely on a bureaucracy has to BE the bureaucracy, in person.**)*

## 6️⃣ Hieun Tsang's India ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BOX — the numbers are asked:** *"**HIUEN TSANG, hailed as THE PRINCE OF PILGRIMS**, visited India during Harsha's reign. **Born in CHINA in 612 CE**, he became **a Buddhist monk at the age of TWENTY**. He **spent about FIVE YEARS at the UNIVERSITY OF NALANDA**… He carried with him **150 PIECES OF THE RELICS OF BUDDHA**, a large number of **Buddha images in GOLD, SILVER and SANDALWOOD**, and above all ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **657 VOLUMES OF VALUABLE MANUSCRIPTS**."* ⭐ **He spent nearly THIRTEEN YEARS in India (630–643 CE)**, and was called **"prince of pilgrims" because he visited the important pilgrim centres associated with the life of the Buddha.**

| Theme | What he reports |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Cities** | *"India was **the land of INNUMERABLE VILLAGES, NUMEROUS TOWNS and BIG CITIES like China**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"PATALIPUTRA LOST ITS PROMINENCE and its place was taken by KANAUJ"** — whose **lofty structures, beautiful gardens and museum of rare collections** he describes, along with residents' **refined appearance, costly dress and love for learning and art** |

| ⭐ **Harsha's building** | *"Harsha constructed **a large number of VIHARAS, MONASTERIES and STUPAS on the bank of the GANGES**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Welfare** | **"CHARITABLE INSTITUTIONS for the stay of travellers and to care for THE SICK AND THE POOR. FREE HOSPITALS and REST HOUSES (*DHARMASALAS*) IN ALL THE TOWNS"** — with **medical treatment FREE OF CHARGE** |

| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Women** | *"**Women wore PURDAH.** Hieun Tsang, however, added that **THE PURDAH SYSTEM WAS NOT FOLLOWED AMONG THE HIGHER CLASS** — he pointed out that **RAJYASRI DID NOT WEAR PURDAH when listening to his discourse**."* ⚠️ **"*SATI* was in practice. YASOMATIDEVI, wife of PRABHAKARA VARDHANA, IMMOLATED HERSELF after the death of her husband"** |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TWO OF THESE ENTRIES CONTRADICT EACH OTHER, AND NOTICING IT IS WORTH MARKS.** ⭐ **The textbook says the caste system was *"RIGID"* AND that *"there was NO SOCIAL CONFLICT."* And in the same breath it records that **butchers, fishermen, dancers and sweepers were required to live OUTSIDE THE CITY WALLS.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Physical exclusion from the city is not the absence of conflict — it is conflict that has been settled so decisively that it no longer needs to be fought.** *(⭐ **The honest formulation for an answer: what a foreign observer records as social harmony is the VIEW FROM THE TOP of a stable hierarchy. Hieun Tsang moved among kings, monks and scholars; the sweepers outside the wall did not leave an account.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Same caution [Unit 7 applies to Fahien's "idyllic picture"](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md) — and the two pilgrims are the two great examples for a question on the value and limits of foreign travellers' accounts.**)*

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Rajyasri detail is the good one on women: *purdah* is reported as general, and then immediately qualified — the highest-status woman in the empire sat UNVEILED at a public discourse.** *A prescription and its most conspicuous exception, recorded in the same paragraph — which is [Theme 3's method](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/03_Theme3_Kinship_Caste_and_Class.md) exactly.*

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The election of Gopala, 750 CE

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  THE MOST UNUSUAL FOUNDING STORY IN THE BOOK
   ================================================================

   ** BEFORE: ** SASHANKA ** , the Gauda ruler, "believed to have
      ruled between ** 590 and 625 CE ** , is considered ** THE
      FIRST PROMINENT KING OF ANCIENT BENGAL ** ." **

   ⚠️ ** "After the fall of the Gauda kingdom, there was
      ** NO CENTRAL AUTHORITY, WHICH LED TO RECURRING WARS
      BETWEEN PETTY CHIEFTAINS ** ." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE SOLUTION: "So, in ** 750 CE, A GROUP OF CHIEFS
      MET AND DECIDED ON A 'KSHATRIYA CHIEF' NAMED GOPALA TO BE
      THEIR RULER ** ." **

   ⭐ ** THE LEGEND: "There is a story that Gopala was elected
      because of ** HIS ABILITY TO KILL A 'DEMONESS' THAT HAD
      KILLED THOSE WHO WERE PREVIOUSLY ELECTED LIKE HIM ** ." **

   ** THE OUTCOME: "Though Gopala ** DID NOT HAVE ROYAL
      ANTECEDENTS ** , he succeeded in acquiring a kingdom. His
      political authority was ** SOON RECOGNISED BY SEVERAL
      INDEPENDENT CHIEFS ** . His original kingdom was in
      ** VANGA or EAST BENGAL ** ." **
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A DYNASTY FOUNDED BY ELECTION IS RARE ENOUGH IN INDIAN HISTORY TO BE WORTH A PARAGRAPH IN ANY ANSWER ON EARLY MEDIEVAL POLITY — AND THE REASON IT HAPPENED IS THE INTERESTING PART.** ⭐ **The chiefs of Bengal were destroying each other in *"recurring wars"*. Election was not an ideological commitment to consent; it was **a coordination solution to anarchy** — a way for rivals none of whom could win to agree on someone.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note the giveaway in the "demoness" legend: previous elected rulers had been killed. **The office had been filled and emptied repeatedly before Gopala made it stick.** *(⭐ **Then read the legend as politics. A "demoness" that destroys each newly elected ruler is a perfectly good folk description of **the faction that murders whoever the others choose.** Gopala's achievement was to be the first appointee who could not be removed — and the story dresses that in myth.**)*
>
> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Now place it in your notes: *"Though Gopala DID NOT HAVE ROYAL ANTECEDENTS, he succeeded in acquiring a kingdom."*** ⭐ **That is [Theme 3's thesis](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/03_Theme3_Kinship_Caste_and_Class.md) — *"political power was effectively open to anyone who could muster support and resources"* — arriving in eighth-century Bengal.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the solutions the different dynasties found for the same problem make a superb comparison: the SATAVAHANAS claimed to be *eka bamhana*; the [GUPTAS advertised a Lichchavi mother](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md); the [RASHTRAKUTAS performed *HIRANYA-GARBHA* to be reborn Kshatriya](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_07_Our_Pasts_II/Notes/02_Kings_and_Kingdoms.md); [SHIVAJI claimed Yadava and Sisodia descent](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md); and GOPALA was simply CHOSEN.** *Five routes to legitimacy, and only one of them ran through an election.*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TAKKOLAM (949) IS A SET QUESTION — *"Point out the significance of the battle of Takkolam"* — AND ITS SIGNIFICANCE IS ENTIRELY ABOUT WHAT IT DELAYED.** ⭐ **[Unit 11](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md) gives you the Chola imperial century: **Parantaka I** *(Uttaramerur)*, then **Rajaraja I** and **Rajendra I** at the height. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Takkolam falls in between — a Rashtrakuta army defeating and killing PARANTAKA's son RAJADITYA, taking Kanchi and Thanjavur, and holding Thondaimandalam.** *The Chola ascent was INTERRUPTED for roughly a generation, and resumed only after Krishna III's death and the Rashtrakuta collapse in 973.* *(⭐ **So the two units answer each other: Unit 11 explains why the Cholas mattered; Unit 8 explains who stopped them, and for how long.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A Deccan power reaching RAMESHVARAM and raising a victory pillar is also a useful corrective to the assumption that the far south was the Cholas' uncontested ground.**)*

**⚠️ And the closing verdict on the Kanauj obsession — worth quoting, because it is the same lesson as Panipat:**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"It was under him the Rashtrakutas **JOINED THE CONTEST… FOR CONTROL OF KANAUJ**. **THE CONTINUED CONFLICT OVER THE POSSESSION OF KANAUJ EMBOLDENED SOME OF THE LOCAL RAJAS TO ASSERT THEIR INDEPENDENCE. THEIR DEFIANCE DESTROYED THE POSSIBILITY OF A SINGLE KINGDOM RULING NORTHERN INDIA with its centre at Kanauj.** **INVASIONS FROM THE NORTH-WEST also prevented any such attempts.** But the successors of Krishna III were **too weak to save the kingdom from its decline**."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT IS THE THESIS OF THE TRIPARTITE STRUGGLE AND IT IS BETTER STATED HERE THAN ANYWHERE ELSE IN YOUR NOTES: THE CONTEST FOR KANAUJ DESTROYED THE THING IT WAS FOUGHT FOR.** ⭐ **Three great powers spent two centuries fighting for the city that symbolised north Indian paramountcy — and the fighting itself so weakened all three that local rajas broke free, and paramountcy became impossible for anyone.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A prize that cannot be won without destroying its own value.** *(⭐ **And the second clause is the hinge into [Unit 10](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md): *"INVASIONS FROM THE NORTH-WEST also prevented any such attempts."* **The Ghaznavids arrive into a north India that three Indian empires had already exhausted.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Pair this with [the Marathas' "undue interests in Delhi" before Panipat](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md) — eight centuries apart, the same strategic error: chasing the symbol of paramountcy at the cost of the substance of power.** **That comparison, made in two sentences, is a first-class close.**)*

| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Its model** | *"It is **SIMILAR TO THE LOKESVARA TEMPLE AT PATTADAKAL, in Karnataka, built by the Chalukya king VIKRAMADITYA II TO COMMEMORATE HIS VICTORY OVER THE PALLAVAS**"* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LOKESVARA COMPARISON IS THE BEST ART-HISTORICAL POINT IN UNIT 8, AND IT CLOSES A CIRCLE THAT RUNS ACROSS THREE OF YOUR FILES.** ⭐ **[Unit 9](03_South_Indian_Culture_and_Temples.md) told you the **VIRUPAKSHA (Lokesvara) temple at PATTADAKAL was built at the order of QUEEN LOHAMAHADEVI to commemorate VIKRAMADITYA II's CONQUEST OF KANCHIPURAM.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the RASHTRAKUTAS, who destroyed the Chalukyas, then took that temple as the MODEL for their own supreme monument at Ellora.** *(⭐ **A conquering dynasty copying the victory monument of the dynasty it overthrew is a striking act. **It says: we have not merely replaced them, we have inherited them** — and it is exactly the claim the *hiranya-garbha* ritual and the *Rathika* genealogy were also making.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Architecture as a claim of succession. Say that once and the whole section coheres.**)*

**🕉️ ELEPHANTA — and the textbook rates it ABOVE Ellora:**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**THE MAIN SHRINE AT ELEPHANTA IS ARTISTICALLY SUPERIOR TO THE SHRINES AT ELLORA.** The sculptures such as **NATARAJA and SADASHIVA EXCEL EVEN THAT OF THE ELLORA SCULPTURES in beauty and craftsmanship.** ⭐ **ARDHANARISHVARA and MAHESHAMURTI are the other famous sculptures.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The latter, A THREE-FACED BUST OF LORD SHIVA, IS 25 FEET TALL and considered ONE OF THE FINEST PIECES OF SCULPTURE IN INDIA.** **The paintings are still seen in the PORCH OF THE TEMPLE OF KAILASA and the CEILINGS OF THE MAHESAMURTI SHRINE at Ellora.**"*

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Learn the four Elephanta sculptures as a set, because they are a Prelims cluster and they belong together thematically: NATARAJA *(Shiva dancing)* · SADASHIVA *(the eternal)* · ARDHANARISHVARA *(half-man, half-woman)* · MAHESHAMURTI *(the three-faced bust, 25 feet)*.** ⭐ **All four are aspects of ONE deity, and the shrine is essentially an argument in stone that Shiva contains opposites — creator and destroyer, male and female, serene and terrible.** *(⚠️ **On the "25 feet", see the [VERIFY] register — the usual figure is around 20 feet.** ⭐ **And note the useful cross-check the passage supplies: PAINTINGS survive at Ellora too, in the Kailasa porch — which slightly qualifies [Unit 9's clean rule](03_South_Indian_Culture_and_Temples.md) that "Ellora = SCULPTURES, Ajanta = PAINTINGS." **The rule is right as a rule of thumb and wrong as an absolute.**)*

### 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | How to build it |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Was Harsha's empire centralised?"** *(the best question in this unit)* | ⭐ **Lead with BURTON STEIN: "A CENTRALISED ADMINISTRATION DID NOT EVEN EXIST UNDER THE POWERFUL GUPTAS", being restricted to the plain between Pataliputra and Mathura.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the evidence for Harsha: the claimed extent (ten regions) against the real one ("A COMPACT TERRITORY BETWEEN THE GANGES AND YAMUNA"); the THREE GRADES OF SUBORDINATE in the 632 CE copper plates; the CONSTANT ROYAL TOURING; and the fact that the empire "DISINTEGRATED RAPIDLY INTO SMALL STATES AFTER HIS DEATH."** ⭐ **Concede the other side: a real ministerial system, named officials, a fixed 1/6 land tax and a four-part budget.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Verdict: an administered CORE plus a negotiated PERIPHERY held by one man's prestige — which is why it did not survive him. Then note that this is [Unit 7's Gupta pattern](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md) repeating** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Highlight the condition of North India as described by Hieun Tsang."** *(a set question)* | ⭐ **Organise by theme, not by page: LAW AND ORDER** *(strong agencies, corporal punishment, death penalty usually avoided, maiming for moral offences)* **· CITIES** *(walls and gates, narrow lanes, wooden balconies with lime mortar, cow-dung floors, multi-storeyed monasteries of kiln-fired brick and marble; **Pataliputra eclipsed by Kanauj**)* **· WELFARE** *(free hospitals and *dharmasalas* in all towns)* **· CASTE** *(rigid; and butchers, fishermen, dancers, sweepers OUTSIDE the city)* **· WOMEN** *(purdah, but not among the higher class — Rajyasri unveiled; sati, and Yasomatidevi)* **· DIET and EDUCATION.** ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Close on the SOURCE CRITICISM: he was a Buddhist pilgrim moving among kings and monks, so "no social conflict" is the view from the top — the same caution [Unit 7 applies to Fahien](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Explain the religious policy of Harsha."** | ⭐ **The trajectory** *(Saiva to at least 631, then Mahayana Buddhist under Rajyasri and Hieun Tsang)*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the crucial qualifier — he "HELD DISCOURSES AMONG LEARNED MEN OF VARIOUS CREEDS" and "treated Vedic scholars and Buddhist *bikshus* ALIKE, distributing charities EQUALLY."** ⭐ **Then the two assemblies of 643 — KANAUJ (20 kings, Buddhist, Jain AND Vedic scholars, the golden statue) and PRAYAG (the QUINQUENNIAL *Mahamoksha Parishad*, wealth distributed to Buddhists, Vedic scholars AND the poor).** ⭐ **Then the policy** *(slaughter and meat restricted)* **and the buildings on the Ganges.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Read the assemblies as politics too: a ruler dependent on feudatories redistributing his treasury in front of twenty kings** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"What were the contributions of the Palas to Buddhism?"** *(a set question, and a total gap in your other notes)* | ⭐ **THE FOUNDATIONS: GOPALA → ODANTAPURA; DHARMAPALA → VIKRAMASILA (Bhagalpur) and the SOMAPURA MAHAVIHARA (Paharpur, Bangladesh); MAHIPALA I → repairs at SARANATH, NALANDA and BODH GAYA.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE INTERNATIONAL DIMENSION: Devapala's FIVE VILLAGES to BALAPUTRADEVA of the SAILENDRAS to maintain a Sumatran king's monastery at Nalanda; and "BUDDHISM IN BENGAL PROVIDED A LINK BETWEEN EASTERN INDIA AND JAVA AND SUMATRA."** ⭐ **THE SCHOLARS: Atisha, Saraha, Tilopa, Silabhadra; Haribhadra as Dharmapala's preceptor; Buddhist TANTRIC works translated into Sanskrit.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE VERDICT: "BENGAL REMAINED ONE OF THE FEW PLACES WHERE BUDDHIST MONASTERIES CONTINUED TO EXIST"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Account for the greatness of the Rashtrakutas."** *(a set question)* | ⭐ **POLITICAL: from Chalukya feudatories to overlords of the Deccan** — Dantidurga's overthrow, Dhruva defeating **both the Pratihara and the Pala**, Govinda III's supremacy, Krishna III at **Takkolam** and **Rameshvaram**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CULTURAL — and this is where the marks are: AMOGHAVARSHA's *KAVIRAJAMARGA*, the EARLIEST KANNADA WORK ON POETICS, written by the emperor; and the THREE GEMS — PAMPA, PONNA, RANNA.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ARTISTIC: the MONOLITHIC KAILASANATHA at ELLORA by KRISHNA I, and ELEPHANTA.** ⭐ **RELIGIOUS BREADTH: Shiva and Vishnu worship, five Jain caves under Amoghavarsha, and Ellora's Buddhist, Hindu and Jain caves on one hillside.** ⚠️ **Close honestly on the Kanauj obsession that destroyed them** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The tripartite struggle destroyed the prize it was fought for."** *(the sharpest thesis available here)* | ⭐ **Set up the contest** *(Palas, Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas; Kanauj as the key to northern paramountcy since Harsha)* using [Class 7's diagram](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_07_Our_Pasts_II/Notes/02_Kings_and_Kingdoms.md). ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then quote this unit: "THE CONTINUED CONFLICT OVER THE POSSESSION OF KANAUJ EMBOLDENED SOME OF THE LOCAL RAJAS TO ASSERT THEIR INDEPENDENCE. THEIR DEFIANCE DESTROYED THE POSSIBILITY OF A SINGLE KINGDOM RULING NORTHERN INDIA."** ⭐ **Add the mutual destruction — "the rivalry between the Pratiharas and the Rashtrakutas proved DESTRUCTIVE TO BOTH", recorded by AL-MASUDI.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the consequence: "INVASIONS FROM THE NORTH-WEST also prevented any such attempts" — the [Ghaznavids](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md) arrive into a north India three empires had exhausted.** ⭐ **Close with the parallel: the [Peshwa's "undue interests in Delhi" before Panipat](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md) — eight centuries apart, the same error** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"India's civilisational links with East and Southeast Asia in the early medieval period."** *(a GS1/GS2 hybrid, and this unit is unusually rich for it)* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Four concrete, independently attested instances from this one unit: (1) HIEUN TSANG's 13 years, 5 at Nalanda, carrying home 150 relics and 657 VOLUMES; (2) the T'ANG EMBASSIES of 643 and 647 — and the ambassador who raised a force in Nepal and Assam to avenge Harsha; (3) NALANDA drawing students from CHINA, JAPAN, MONGOLIA, SRI LANKA, TIBET and Southeast Asia; (4) DEVAPALA's FIVE VILLAGES endowing the SAILENDRA king's monastery.** ⭐ **Then the structural point: "BUDDHISM IN BENGAL PROVIDED A LINK BETWEEN EASTERN INDIA AND JAVA AND SUMATRA", and the Palas drew revenue from "WIDE COMMERCIAL CONTACTS IN SOUTH-EAST ASIA."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Close on the long arc: monastic endowment in the 9th century, [Chola naval expedition against Sri Vijaya in the 11th](01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md) — the same partner, two different instruments** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Fragmentation produced regional cultures."** | ⭐ **Open with this unit's own line — after the Guptas, "REGIONAL IDENTITY BECAME PRONOUNCED with the emergence of many small states."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the evidence: KANNADA becomes a literary language under the Rashtrakutas (*Kavirajamarga* + the three gems); the PALA SCHOOL of sculpture and bronze under Dhiman and Vitapala; ELLORA and ELEPHANTA; VIKRAMASHILA and SOMAPURA; and *MAHIPALAGEET* still sung in rural Bengal a thousand years later.** ⭐ **Then the general claim, which [Unit 6 also supports](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md): political unity and cultural achievement are different variables.** ⚠️ **Keep it honest — the same fragmentation left the north unable to resist invasion** |

## 🔁 Recap — the unit in ten lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **After the Guptas, north India *"splintered into several warrior kingdoms"* and *"REGIONAL IDENTITY BECAME PRONOUNCED"*** — **MAITRAKAS** at Valabhi, **MAUKHARIS** at Kanauj, **VAKATAKAS** in the western Deccan, and the **PUSHYABHUTIS** at Thaneswar, *"north of Delhi, between Sutlej and Yamuna."*
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Harsha did not CONQUER Kanauj — its own magnates INVITED him, on the advice of the minister PONI, and he accepted *"on the advice of AVALOKITESVARA BODHISATVA"* with the titles RAJPUTRA and SILADITYA.** ⭐ **He moved the capital there because Thanesar was exposed to the north-west and Kanauj sat in the richest agricultural belt.**
3. ⭐ **Bana's *HARSHACHARITA* was *"THE FIRST FORMAL BIOGRAPHY OF A KING"* — a new genre — and the check on it is the AIHOLE inscription, the enemy's commissioned poet recording the same war.**
4. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Harsha was *"HUMBLED"* by PULIKESIN II, who took the title *PARAMESWARA* to commemorate it; he subjugated Gauda *"ONLY AFTER THE DEATH OF SASANKA"*; and his *"REAL SWAY DID NOT EXTEND BEYOND A COMPACT TERRITORY BETWEEN THE GANGES AND YAMUNA."***
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BURTON STEIN's argument is that *"a centralised administration DID NOT EVEN EXIST UNDER THE POWERFUL GUPTAS"* — and the 632 CE copper plates show Harsha's authority resting on THREE GRADES of feudatory, from *mahasamantas* to rulers who merely *"professed to be at his service."*** ⭐ **Hence the constant royal touring, and hence the rapid disintegration after 647.**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The machinery is nonetheless real: a *MANTRI PARISHAD*, EIGHT NAMED OFFICIALS *(including SARVAGATA for the secret service)*, *BHAGA* at one-sixth in kind and *HIRANYA* in cash, and CROWN LAND IN FOUR PARTS — one of them *"for REWARDING MEN OF LETTERS."***
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In 643 Harsha held TWO Buddhist assemblies — KANAUJ (20 kings, Buddhist, Jain and Vedic scholars) and the QUINQUENNIAL *MAHAMOKSHA PARISHAD* at PRAYAG, where he gave away his wealth over four days.** ⭐ **And the T'ang embassies of 643 and 647 produced the strangest sequel in the unit: a Chinese ambassador who raised troops in Nepal and Assam and carried Harsha's usurper back to China as a prisoner.**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The PALAS began with an ELECTION — *"in 750 CE, a group of chiefs met and decided on a 'Kshatriya chief' named GOPALA to be their ruler"*, a man who *"did not have royal antecedents."*** ⭐ **DHARMAPALA founded VIKRAMASILA and SOMAPURA and ruled Kanauj through a nominee; DEVAPALA endowed the SAILENDRA king BALAPUTRADEVA's Nalanda monastery with FIVE VILLAGES; MAHIPALA I revived the dynasty; and the SENAS ended it around 1150.**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The RASHTRAKUTAS rose from Chalukya feudatories to overlords of the Deccan** — **DANTIDURGA** the founder, **DHRUVA** defeating both the Pratihara and the Pala, **GOVINDA III** at maximum extent, **AMOGHAVARSHA** ruling 64 years, and **KRISHNA III** taking Kanchi and Thanjavur in 943 and beating the Cholas at ⭐ **TAKKOLAM in 949**, before raising a victory pillar at **Rameshvaram**.
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Their most durable achievement was linguistic and artistic: AMOGHAVARSHA wrote *KAVIRAJAMARGA*, the EARLIEST KANNADA WORK ON POETICS, and the THREE GEMS — PAMPA, PONNA, RANNA — followed; KRISHNA I hewed the MONOLITHIC KAILASANATHA at ELLORA out of a single rock, on the model of the Chalukyas' own victory temple at PATTADAKAL; and at ELEPHANTA the three-faced MAHESHAMURTI stands, in the textbook's judgement, *"artistically SUPERIOR to the shrines at Ellora."*** ⚠️ **And the contest for KANAUJ that consumed all three powers *"DESTROYED THE POSSIBILITY OF A SINGLE KINGDOM RULING NORTHERN INDIA"* — leaving the field open to the invasions from the north-west.**
