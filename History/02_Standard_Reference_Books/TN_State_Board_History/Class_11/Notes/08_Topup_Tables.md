# 1️⃣ UNIT 3 — The Sixteen Mahajanapadas

> ⚠️ **[Class 6 Ch.5](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_06_Our_Pasts_I/Notes/05_Kingdoms_Kings_and_an_Early_Republic.md) and [Theme 2](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/02_Theme2_Kings_Farmers_and_Towns.md) explain what a mahajanapada WAS and why Magadha won. This section supplies only THE LIST and the SHORT ADDITIONS.**

|:--:|---|:--:|---|
| **1** | **GANDHARA** | **9** | **KURU** |
| **2** | **KAMBOJA** | **10** | **PANCHALA** |

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **USE THE LIST, BUT KEEP [NCERT's CAUTION](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/02_Theme2_Kings_Farmers_and_Towns.md) ATTACHED TO IT: *"although the lists VARY."*** ⭐ **There is no single canonical set of sixteen — the Buddhist *Anguttara Nikaya*, the Jaina *Bhagavati Sutra* and the Puranas give overlapping but different lists.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The names that recur across all of them — and therefore the ones safe to assert — are **VAJJI, MAGADHA, KOSALA, KURU, PANCHALA, GANDHARA and AVANTI**.** *(⭐ **A Prelims question will normally test whether a NAMED state was among the sixteen; a Mains answer should mention that the lists differ. **Both are handled by learning this table and remembering the hedge.**)*

**⭐ The two political types — the short version:**

| Type | Character |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MONARCHIES** *(the Gangetic plain)* | *"**Vedic orthodoxy was an ESTABLISHED PRACTICE**… the **PRIESTLY CLASS enjoyed a PREEMINENT STATUS**, unlike in the *gana-sanghas*."* ⭐ **Kingship HEREDITARY, succession "in most cases based on the LAW OF PRIMOGENITURE."** The king assisted by councils — ***PARISHAD* and *SABHA*** — which were ⚠️ ***"ADVISORY IN NATURE"*** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***GANA-SANGHAS*** *(oligarchies)* | *"**centred on CLANS**… These kingdoms **DID NOT COME UNDER THE SINGLE DECISION-MAKING AUTHORITY OF A KING**, but decisions were taken **ON A COLLECTIVE BASIS BY THE HEADS OF THE DIFFERENT CLANS TOGETHER**."* ⭐ **The VRIJJIS were "one of the BEST KNOWN", with VAISHALI as their capital in the MITHILA region** |

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the nice literary cross-check the unit adds:** *"the names of the clans, such as **IKSHVAKU and VRISHNI**, as well as these early kingdoms, are **ALL MENTIONED IN THE TWO EPICS, *RAMAYANA* and *MAHABHARATA***."*

**💰 The four taxes of the mahajanapadas — a compact table, and it completes a series you have been tracking:**

| Tax | Basis |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***BALI*** | **"A tax imposed based on THE AREA OF CULTIVABLE LAND"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***BHAGA*** | **"Obtained as A SHARE OF THE PRODUCE"** |
| ***KARA*** and ***SHULKA*** | *"some of the other taxes collected during this period"* |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **YOU HAVE NOW MET *BALI* AND *BHAGA* IN FOUR PERIODS, AND TRACING THEM IS A GENUINELY STRONG ANSWER TO ANY QUESTION ON THE EVOLUTION OF THE INDIAN STATE'S FISCAL CLAIM.**
>
> | Period | ***BALI*** | ***BHAGA*** |
> |---|---|---|
> | ⭐ **RIG VEDIC** *([Unit 2](05_Megalithic_and_Iron_Age.md))* | **A VOLUNTARY contribution** — *"some scholars say it was an IMPOSED TAX"* | — |
> | ⭐ **LATER VEDIC** *([Unit 2](05_Megalithic_and_Iron_Age.md))* | **"Voluntary OR COMPULSORY"** — *"such voluntary contributions **BECAME TRIBUTES**"* | — |
> | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MAHAJANAPADAS** *(here)* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A TAX ON THE AREA OF CULTIVABLE LAND** — *i.e. now ASSESSED and MEASURED* | **A share of the produce** |
> | ⭐ **GUPTA** *([Unit 7](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md))* | ⚠️ *"A VOLUNTARY offering… but LATER BECAME COMPULSORY. **IT WAS AN OPPRESSIVE TAX**"* | **The king's customary share — ONE-SIXTH** |
> | ⭐ **HARSHA** *([Unit 8](07_Harsha_and_Regional_Kingdoms.md))* | ⚠️ *"there is **NO REFERENCE** to the tax *Bali*"* | **The land tax, in kind — ONE-SIXTH** |
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The arc is unmistakable: a GIFT becomes a TRIBUTE, becomes an ASSESSED TAX ON MEASURED LAND — and then, under the Guptas, hardens again into something the textbook itself calls OPPRESSIVE.** *(⭐ **And the rate holds astonishingly steady: ONE-SIXTH from the mahajanapadas through the Guptas to Harsha — before [the Delhi Sultanate demanded HALF](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md). **One number, twenty centuries, and then a step change.** That single comparison, drawn across five files, is the kind of thing that makes an answer look like knowledge rather than recall.**)*

**⚠️ And the social note this unit adds — the emergence of untouchability:**

> *"The richer landowners were called ***GRIHAPATIS***. These landowners employed labourers called ***DASAS* or *KARMAKARAS***. The smaller landowners were known as ***KASSAKAS* or *KRISHAKAS***. ⭐ **The society was stratified on the basis of *VARNA*. It emerged as A MARKER OF STATUS. Cultivators and artisans were identified as the SHUDRAS.** ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A NEW SOCIAL CATEGORY that emerged during this period was PLACED BELOW THE SHUDRAS in the social hierarchy and CONSIDERED UNTOUCHABLES. They were FORCED TO LIVE ON THE FRINGES OF THE SETTLEMENTS and subsisted on HUNTING AND GATHERING… ⭐ THEY HAD THEIR OWN LANGUAGE, WHICH WAS DIFFERENT FROM THAT SPOKEN BY THE INDO-ARYANS.**"*

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LANGUAGE DETAIL IS THE ONE TO CARRY, BECAUSE IT CHANGES WHAT THE CATEGORY MEANS.** ⭐ **A group pushed below the four *varnas*, living outside the settlement, subsisting by hunting and gathering — AND SPEAKING A DIFFERENT LANGUAGE.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is not a rank produced from within Vedic society; it is the ABSORPTION of an existing, linguistically distinct population INTO the bottom of an expanding one.** *(⭐ **Which fits [Unit 2's evidence](05_Megalithic_and_Iron_Age.md) exactly — 300 Munda and Dravidian words already in the *Rig Veda*, and Munda-speaking people in the Ganga valley as the Aryan speakers moved east.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The frontier of Aryanisation and the bottom of the *varna* order are the same boundary, seen from two directions.** **Hieun Tsang would find the same people [outside the city walls a thousand years later](07_Harsha_and_Regional_Kingdoms.md).**)*

**🧘 The six heterodox teachers — a compact matching item NCERT does not give:**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"A Buddhist text, the ***SAMANNAPHALA SUTTA***, while making a reference to **AJATASHATRU of MAGADHA** meeting Gautama Buddha, mentions that **BEFORE his meeting, the former had a philosophical discourse with the leaders of the various sects**"* —

| Teacher | Note |
|---|---|
| **PURANA KASSAPA** | — |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MAKKHALI GOSALA** | **The greatest of the AJIVIKAS** |
| **AJITA KESAKAMBALIN** | ⭐ **The materialist** *(see the quotation below)* |
| **PAKUDHA KACHCHAYANA** | — |
| **SANJAYA BELATTHIPUTTA** | — |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NIGANTHA NATAPUTTA** | **= MAHAVIRA** |

⭐ **The three terms the text uses for them:** *"**HOMELESS WANDERERS** of longstanding *(**CHIRA-PABBAJITO**)*, **FOUNDERS OF SECTS** *(**TITHAKARO**)* and **LEADERS OF THEIR ORDERS** *(**GANACHARIYO**)*."* ⚠️ *And the source's bias is worth naming: *"Their doctrines were **SHOWN UNSATISFACTORY** while that of Buddha was **ACCEPTABLE TO AJATASHATRU**"* — **a Buddhist text reporting a debate that a Buddhist wins.***

**⭐ The AJIVIKA succession** — *"According to Buddhist records, **NANDA VACCHA** was considered the founder, succeeded by **KISA SAMKICCA**, followed by **MAKKHALI GOSALA**, the THIRD and the GREATEST."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gosala met Mahavira at NALANDA; "their friendship lasted for SIX YEARS" before they separated over doctrine; he then went to SRAVASTI, "where he was PATRONISED BY A RICH POTTER WOMAN CALLED HALAHALA", and believed in **the doctrine of REANIMATION**.**

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AJITA KESAKAMBALIN's materialism, quoted in full because it is the most startling passage in Unit 3 and almost nothing survives of these thinkers in their own words:** *the body is made of **earth, water, wind and sense**; after death these elements **return to the earth**; **there is no life after death.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**GENEROSITY IS TAUGHT BY IDIOTS. The words of those who speak of existence after death are FALSE, EMPTY CHATTER. With the breakup of the body, THE WISE AND THE FOOLISH ALIKE ARE ANNIHILATED, DESTROYED. THEY DO NOT EXIST AFTER DEATH.**"*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note the irony that makes this usable: we possess this uncompromising denial of the afterlife ONLY because a BUDDHIST text preserved it in order to refute it.** *(⭐ **The same situation as [Theme 4's Ajivikas](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md), *"whose own texts are lost."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Almost everything we know about the sixth-century BCE materialists and fatalists comes from their opponents' summaries — which is a first-class point about the survival of dissent in the historical record, and worth making whenever a question touches the intellectual ferment of this period.**)*

# 2️⃣ UNIT 4 — The Mauryan Administrative Vocabulary

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ THIS CAUTION FIRST, AND REPRODUCE IT IN ANY ANSWER.** *The textbook itself says: the fullest account of Mauryan administration is in the ***ARTHASASTRA*** — ⭐ **"THOUGH THE WORK ITSELF IS NOW DATED TO A FEW CENTURIES LATER"** — and **"it must be remembered that the *Arthasastra* was A PRESCRIPTIVE TEXT, WHICH LAID DOWN THE GUIDELINES for royal administration."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So this vocabulary describes an IDEAL administration set down centuries after the Mauryas, not a photograph of one.** *(⭐ **[Unit 6 §4](04_Post_Mauryan_and_Guptas.md) dates the text's final form to the **second century CE** — five hundred years after Chandragupta.** ⭐ **The textbook's own fix is the right one: *"If we add to this the information from ASHOKA'S EDICTS and the work of MEGASTHENES, we get a more comprehensive picture."* **Three sources, cross-checked — never the *Arthashastra* alone.**)*

**⚖️ And the centralisation question, which the unit handles well:**

> ⭐ *"**GREEK HISTORIANS, taking their lead from MEGASTHENES, described the Mauryan state as A CENTRALISED STATE.** What we should infer… is that **A UNIFORM PATTERN OF ADMINISTRATION was established throughout the very large area of the empire.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BUT, GIVEN THE EXISTING STATE OF TECHNOLOGY IN COMMUNICATIONS AND TRANSPORT, A DECENTRALISED ADMINISTRATIVE SYSTEM HAD TO BE IN PLACE.**"*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT DISTINCTION IS THE ANSWER TO "WAS THE MAURYAN STATE CENTRALISED?" AND IT IS BETTER THAN EITHER SIMPLE ANSWER.** ⭐ **UNIFORM ≠ CENTRALISED.** *The Mauryas replicated the SAME PATTERN of revenue, judicial and bureaucratic offices across the empire — but each province ran its own copy, because at fourth-century-BCE communication speeds nothing else was physically possible.* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A standard template, locally executed.** *(⭐ **Set it beside [BURTON STEIN's argument in Unit 8](07_Harsha_and_Regional_Kingdoms.md) that *"a centralised administration did not even exist under the powerful Guptas"*, and the two make one continuous point: **premodern Indian empires were uniform in FORM and devolved in FACT** — and that is a much better thesis than the tired "Mauryan centralisation vs Gupta feudalism" contrast.**)*

**💰 The salary figures — a startling and quotable fact:**

| Post | Salary |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Chief minister · the *PUROHITA* · the army commander** | **48,000 *PANAS*** |
| **Soldiers** | **500 *PANAS*** |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT IS A RATIO OF NINETY-SIX TO ONE, AND THE TEXTBOOK DRAWS THE RIGHT CONCLUSION FROM IT.** ⭐ *"**If we MULTIPLY THIS BY THE NUMBER OF INFANTRY AND CAVALRY, we get an idea of THE ENORMOUS RESOURCES needed to maintain the army and the administrative staff.**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is the fiscal explanation of Mauryan state-building: a salaried bureaucracy and a standing army on that scale can only exist if the revenue machinery can reach every village — so the administrative apparatus described below is not a luxury of empire, it is its PRECONDITION.** *(⭐ **The same causal logic as [Alauddin Khalji's market reforms](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md) — a cash-paid standing army forces the state to reorganise the economy around paying for it.**)*

The textbook says the ACTUAL Mauryan take was *"usually about ONE-FOURTH."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The famous "one-sixth" is a PRESCRIPTIVE figure, and the state routinely exceeded it by half again — which is exactly the prescription-versus-practice gap [Theme 3](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/03_Theme3_Kinship_Caste_and_Class.md) trains you to look for, applied to taxation.**

## ⚖️ THE TWO COURTS — a clean Prelims pair ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

| Court | Jurisdiction |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***DHARMASTHIYA*** | **CIVIL LAW** — *"relating to **MARRIAGE, INHERITANCE and other aspects of civil life**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***KANTAKASODHANA*** | ⭐ **"REMOVAL OF THORNS"** — *"to **CLEAR THE SOCIETY OF ANTI-SOCIAL ELEMENTS** and various types of crimes… **it functioned MORE LIKE THE MODERN POLICE, and relied on A NETWORK OF SPIES** for information"* |

⭐ **Both were presided over by THREE JUDGES and THREE *AMATYAS* (secretaries)**, and courts *"were established in **ALL THE MAJOR TOWNS**."* ⚠️ **"Punishments for crimes were usually QUITE SEVERE."**

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TEXTBOOK'S CLOSING JUDGEMENT ON MAURYAN JUSTICE IS THE BEST SENTENCE IN UNIT 4 AND IT DESERVES TO BE QUOTED: *"THE OVERALL OBJECTIVE OF THE JUDICIAL SYSTEM AS IT EVOLVED WAS TO EXTEND GOVERNMENT CONTROL OVER MOST ASPECTS OF ORDINARY LIFE."*** ⭐ **Justice is being described not as the settlement of disputes but as **an instrument of state penetration** — and the *kantakasodhana*, "removal of thorns", with its spy network, is the mechanism.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two court systems, one for CIVIL DISPUTES and one for CLEARING THE SOCIAL FIELD OF OBSTACLES, is a striking institutional design, and the second name is unusually honest about what it was for.**

## 🕊️ ASOKA'S ALTERNATIVE — the three touring officers ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐ *"**Ashoka's rule gives us AN ALTERNATIVE MODEL of a RIGHTEOUS KING AND A JUST STATE.**"*

| Officer | Role |
|---|---|
| ⭐ ***YUKTAS*** | **Subordinate officials** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***RAJJUKAS*** | **Rural administrators** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***PRADESIKAS*** | **Heads of the districts** |

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **All three were instructed "to GO ON TOURS EVERY FIVE YEARS TO INSTRUCT PEOPLE IN *DHAMMA*" — MAJOR ROCK EDICT 3.**

> ⭐ **And the instruction to the officers and city magistrates, from KALINGA ROCK EDICT 1:** *"**ALL THE PEOPLE WERE HIS CHILDREN** and he wished for his people what he wished for his own children — that they should **obtain welfare and happiness in this world and the next**. These officials should **recognise their own responsibilities and STRIVE TO BE IMPARTIAL, and see to it that MEN WERE NOT IMPRISONED OR TORTURED WITHOUT GOOD REASON**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"He added that HE WOULD SEND AN OFFICER EVERY FIVE YEARS TO VERIFY IF HIS INSTRUCTIONS WERE CARRIED OUT."**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PUT ASOKA'S EDICT BESIDE THE *KANTAKASODHANA* AND YOU HAVE THE WHOLE TENSION OF THE MAURYAN STATE IN ONE COMPARISON — AND IT IS A READY-MADE MAINS ANSWER.** ⭐ **The *Arthashastra* model: courts backed by spies, severe punishments, and an objective of *"extending government control over most aspects of ordinary life."*** ⭐ **The Asokan model: officers touring every five years to teach *dhamma*, told to be impartial and to ensure nobody is *"imprisoned or tortured without good reason"* — with a **verification officer sent to check that they complied**.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Same state, same decade, two utterly different theories of what administration is FOR.** *(⭐ **And note that Asoka's model is not soft: **a five-yearly audit of whether your officials obeyed you** is itself a serious instrument of control. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He did not abandon state penetration — he changed what it was penetrating FOR.** That reading is what [Theme 2's account of *dhamma mahamattas*](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/02_Theme2_Kings_Farmers_and_Towns.md) supports, and it is far better than the usual "Asoka became peaceful" line.**)*

## 3.2 The Bahmani rulers ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

| Ruler | Achievements |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ALAUDIN HASAN BAHMAN SHAH** *(1347–1358)* | ⭐ **Divided the kingdom into FOUR territorial divisions called *TARAFS*, each under a GOVERNOR** — *"as followed in the Delhi Sultanate."* ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Each governor COMMANDED THE ARMY of his province (GULBARGA, DAULATABAD, BIDAR and BERAR) and was SOLELY RESPONSIBLE FOR BOTH ITS ADMINISTRATION AND THE COLLECTION OF REVENUE. The system worked well UNDER A POWERFUL KING, BUT ITS DANGERS BECAME APPARENT UNDER A WEAK RULER."** ⭐ Spent most of his eleven years *"subduing the unruly"*; his attempt to exact tribute from **WARANGAL** and the **REDDI kingdoms of RAJAHMUNDRY and KONDAVIDU** led to frequent wars. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Assumed the title *SECOND ALEXANDER* on his coins** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MOHAMMED I** *(1358–1375)* | ⚠️ **A decade-long war with Vijayanagar over the Raichur doab** — *"**NEITHER SIDE GAINED LASTING CONTROL** over the region, in spite of the huge fatalities."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **But his attack on WARANGAL in 1363 brought "a large indemnity, including THE IMPORTANT FORTRESS OF GOLKONDA and THE TREASURED TURQUOISE THRONE, which thereafter BECAME THE THRONE OF THE BAHMANI KINGS."** ⭐ **Established a system of government "FOLLOWED BY ALL THE SUCCESSOR SULTANATES AS WELL AS BY THE MARATHAS LATER"** — see the eight ministers below. **Took strong measures against HIGHWAY ROBBERY**; **built two mosques at Gulbarga**, of which **the GREAT MOSQUE, completed 1367, "remains an impressive building"** |
| **The next hundred years** | ⚠️ *"a number of Sultans one after another, **BY SUCCESSION OR USURPATION**. **All of them fought with their southern neighbour, BUT WITHOUT GAINING MUCH TERRITORY.**"* ⭐ **1425 — WARANGAL subdued**, but further eastward progress **challenged by the ORISSAN rulers**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1429 — THE CAPITAL SHIFTED FROM GULBARGA TO BIDAR** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MOHAMMAD III** *(1463–1482)* | *"worthy of mention **BECAUSE OF HIS LIEUTENANT MOHAMMED GAWAN, A GREAT STATESMAN**"* |

> 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TURQUOISE THRONE — a Prelims-grade detail with a nice literary tail:** *"**TURQUOISE** is a semi-precious stone, **SKY BLUE** in colour. **The turquoise throne is one of the bejewelled royal seats of PERSIAN KINGS described in FIRDAUSI's *SHAH NAMA*.**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So a Deccan sultan captured from a Telugu dynasty at Warangal a throne named for the seats of Persian kings in an eleventh-century Persian epic — and it became the Bahmani throne.** *(⭐ **Compare [Unit 10's Bhattavatara](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md), who modelled a Sanskrit history of Kashmir on the same *Shah Nama*. **Firdausi's poem was a shared reference point across the subcontinent's courts, Persian-speaking or not.**)*

## 3.3 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ MAHMUD GAWAN — the reformer and his execution

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Who he was** | *"**A PERSIAN BY BIRTH**, well-versed in **ISLAMIC THEOLOGY, PERSIAN and MATHEMATICS**. He was also **A POET AND A PROSE-WRITER**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **His monument** | **"The MOHAMMED GAWAN MADRASA in BIDAR, with a LARGE LIBRARY CONTAINING 3,000 MANUSCRIPTS, is illustrative of his scholarship"** |
| **His post** | *"served with great distinction as **PRIME MINISTER under MOHAMMAD III**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Military innovation** | *"He **USED PERSIAN CHEMISTS TO TEACH THE PREPARATION AND THE USE OF GUNPOWDER**. **In his war against the Vijayanagar kings AT BELGAUM, HE USED GUNPOWDER**"* |
| **Wars** | Successful campaigns against the rulers of **KONKAN, ORISSA and VIJAYANAGAR** |

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  GAWAN'S FOUR ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS -- ALL AIMED
              AT ONE TARGET: THE PROVINCIAL GOVERNORS
   ================================================================

   ** THE PROBLEM: the governors "FUNCTIONED VIRTUALLY AS
      KINGS." ** ( -- exactly the danger built into Bahman Shah's
      four ** TARAFS ** , §3.2 )

   ⭐ ** (1) ** HE DIVIDED THE EXISTING FOUR PROVINCES INTO
       EIGHT ** -- "This not only ** LIMITED THE AREA UNDER THE
       RULE OF EACH GOVERNOR ** but also made the provincial
       administration ** MORE MANAGEABLE ** ." **

   ** (2) ** He placed ** SOME DISTRICTS DIRECTLY UNDER CENTRAL
       ADMINISTRATION ** . **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** (3) ** He curtailed their MILITARY power "by
       ** ALLOWING THEM TO OCCUPY ONLY ONE FORT ** in their
       territory. ** THE OTHER FORTS REMAINED UNDER THE SULTAN'S
       DIRECT CONTROL ** ." **

   ** (4) ** "The royal officers who were given ** LAND
       ASSIGNMENTS AS PAY ** were made ** ACCOUNTABLE TO THE
       SULTAN FOR THEIR INCOME AND EXPENDITURE ** ." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REFORM (3) IS THE CLEVEREST AND THE MOST TRANSFERABLE — *"ONE FORT EACH."*** ⭐ **A provincial governor with an army and every fort in his province is a king waiting to happen. A governor with an army and ONE fort is a servant, because he has nowhere to retreat to.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gawan did not reduce the governors' revenue or their troops; he removed their DEFENSIBILITY.** *(⭐ **Set the four reforms beside [Shivaji's three provincial rules](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md) — no *jagirs*, income without jurisdiction, no hereditary office — and beside [Alauddin Khalji's measures against the nobles](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md). ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Three states, three centuries, one recurring problem: how to let a governor govern without letting him secede. Every Indian state in your notes is somewhere on that spectrum, and the ones that got it wrong are the ones that fragmented.**)*

```
   ================================================================
   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  AND THE REFORMS KILLED HIM
   ================================================================

   ** "The administrative reforms introduced by Gawan
      ** IMPROVED THE EFFICIENCY of the government, BUT CURTAILED
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Kohinoor now has a full itinerary across your notes: mined and traded at GOLKONDA → carried off by [NADIR SHAH in 1739 with the Peacock Throne](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md) → and eventually to Britain.** *A single stone that ties the Deccan, the Mughals, Persia and the Raj together — and a very good opening for an answer on plunder as a theme in Indian history.*

In 1565 they finally COMBINED — and destroyed Vijayanagar at TALIKOTA.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And *"within a century"* they were themselves swallowed by the Mughals — [Bijapur in 1686 and Golkonda in 1687](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md), the conquests that exhausted Aurangzeb's treasury and opened the door for the Marathas.** *(⭐ **So the sequence is: the sultanates destroy Vijayanagar → the Mughals destroy the sultanates → the Deccan wars destroy the Mughals → [the Marathas rise from the wreckage](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md), led by men whose fathers had served those same sultanates.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Four powers, each destroyed by the victory over the last. ### 🔴 Prelims traps — the claim vs the truth

| ❌ The plausible-sounding claim | ✅ What the source actually says |
|---|---|
| The sixteen mahajanapadas are listed only in Buddhist texts | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"According to PURANIC, BUDDHIST AND JAINA traditions"** |
| Vaishali was the capital of Magadha | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Of the VRIJJIS, "one of the best known of the *gana-sanghas*", in the MITHILA region** |
| In the *gana-sanghas* the king took decisions with a council | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **They "DID NOT COME UNDER THE SINGLE DECISION-MAKING AUTHORITY OF A KING" — decisions were "TAKEN ON A COLLECTIVE BASIS BY THE HEADS OF THE DIFFERENT CLANS TOGETHER"** |
| In the monarchies the *parishad* and *sabha* could overrule the king | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The councils were "ADVISORY IN NATURE"**; kingship was hereditary, succession usually by **PRIMOGENITURE** |

| The Mauryan empire had five provinces | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FOUR — SUVARNAGIRI, UJJAIN, TAXILA and TOSALI** — plus **the capital region of PATALIPUTRA, DIRECTLY ADMINISTERED** |

| The *samaharta* was the treasurer | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The COLLECTOR-GENERAL, "in effect LIKE A MINISTER OF FINANCE."** *A separate **TREASURER** kept the record of tax revenues* |
| A *gopa* headed a district | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A *STHANIKA* headed the DISTRICT; a *GOPA* was in charge of FIVE TO TEN VILLAGES; a *NAGARIKA* handled URBAN administration; a *GRAMANI* headed the village** |

| The Mauryan land tax was one-sixth in practice | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Usually the king was entitled to ONE-SIXTH… IN REALITY, IT WAS OFTEN MUCH HIGHER, USUALLY ABOUT ONE-FOURTH"** |
| Salt manufacture was privately run | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A STATE MONOPOLY**, with royal lands, **FORESTS and MINES** |
| *Kantakasodhana* courts handled marriage and inheritance | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REVERSED — *DHARMASTHIYA* = CIVIL law (marriage, inheritance); *KANTAKASODHANA* = "REMOVAL OF THORNS", functioning "MORE LIKE THE MODERN POLICE", with A NETWORK OF SPIES** |
| Mauryan courts had five judges | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE JUDGES and THREE *AMATYAS*** — in **both** court types |
| *Rajjukas* were district heads | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***RAJJUKAS* = RURAL ADMINISTRATORS; *PRADESIKAS* = HEADS OF THE DISTRICTS; *YUKTAS* = subordinate officials.** *All three toured **EVERY FIVE YEARS** to instruct in *dhamma* — **MAJOR ROCK EDICT 3*** |
| Asoka's "all men are my children" appears in Major Rock Edict 3 | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In KALINGA ROCK EDICT 1** — which also promises **an officer sent EVERY FIVE YEARS TO VERIFY compliance** |
| The Bahmani kingdom was founded at Bidar | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ZAFAR KHAN declared independence at DEVAGIRI in 1345 and SHIFTED HIS CAPITAL TO GULBARGA**, taking the title **BAHMAN SHAH**. *The capital moved to **BIDAR only in 1429*** |
| The Bahmani kingdom predates Vijayanagar | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **VIJAYANAGAR was founded in 1336 by HARIHARA and BUKKA; the Bahmani dynasty dates from 1347** *(independence declared 1345)* |

| The two states fought only over the Raichur doab | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Also over "THE SEA PORTS OF GOA, HONAVAR etc. on the west coast, WHICH WERE THE SUPPLY POINTS OF THE HORSES NEEDED FOR THEIR ARMY"** |

| Bahman Shah took the title "Second Alexander" after conquering Vijayanagar | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **After his victories over WARANGAL and the REDDI kingdoms of RAJAHMUNDRY and KONDAVIDU** — and the title appears **ON HIS COINS** |
| Mohammed I won lasting control of the Raichur doab | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"NEITHER SIDE GAINED LASTING CONTROL over the region, IN SPITE OF THE HUGE FATALITIES."** *His gains came from **WARANGAL in 1363*** |
| The turquoise throne came from Vijayanagar | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **From WARANGAL, in the 1363 indemnity — along with the FORTRESS OF GOLKONDA** |
| Mohammed I appointed a council of six ministers | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **EIGHT** — *VAKIL-US-SULTANA · WAZIR-I-KULL · AMIR-I-JUMLA · WASIR-I-ASHRAF · NAZIR · **PESHWA** · KOTWAL · SADR-I-JAHAN* |
| The *wazir-i-kull* was the finance minister | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He "SUPERVISED THE WORK OF ALL OTHER MINISTERS."** *Finance = **AMIR-I-JUMLA**; assistant finance = **NAZIR**; chief justice and religious affairs = **SADR-I-JAHAN*** |
| The office of *Peshwa* was a Maratha invention | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It is one of MOHAMMED I's EIGHT BAHMANI MINISTERS** — and his system was "FOLLOWED BY ALL THE SUCCESSOR SULTANATES AS WELL AS BY THE MARATHAS LATER" |

### 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | How to build it |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Was the Mauryan state centralised?"** | ⭐ **Open with the textbook's own distinction: the Greeks "taking their lead from MEGASTHENES" called it centralised, but what that means is "A UNIFORM PATTERN OF ADMINISTRATION", and "GIVEN THE EXISTING STATE OF TECHNOLOGY… A DECENTRALISED ADMINISTRATIVE SYSTEM HAD TO BE IN PLACE."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the evidence for UNIFORMITY: the ladder replicated in every province — *samaharta*, *sthanika*, *gopa*, *nagarika*, *gramani*; two standard court types; a single tax structure.** ⭐ **Then the evidence for DEVOLUTION: four provinces under ROYAL PRINCES, the capital region administered separately, and villages "SEMI-AUTONOMOUS" under a council of elders.** ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the source caution — the *Arthashastra* is PRESCRIPTIVE and centuries later.** **Close by connecting to [Burton Stein on the Guptas](07_Harsha_and_Regional_Kingdoms.md): premodern Indian empires were uniform in FORM and devolved in FACT** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Two models of the Mauryan state."** *(a superb comparative question, and the material is right here)* | ⭐ **THE *ARTHASHASTRA* MODEL: revenue maximisation, warehouses and regulated markets, a spy network, the *KANTAKASODHANA* court, severe punishments — and the objective the textbook names outright, "TO EXTEND GOVERNMENT CONTROL OVER MOST ASPECTS OF ORDINARY LIFE."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ASOKAN MODEL: *YUKTAS*, *RAJJUKAS* and *PRADESIKAS* touring every five years to teach *dhamma*; "ALL THE PEOPLE WERE HIS CHILDREN"; officials told to be IMPARTIAL and to ensure nobody is "IMPRISONED OR TORTURED WITHOUT GOOD REASON"; and a verification officer sent every five years.** ⭐ **Then the sophisticated close: Asoka did NOT abandon state penetration — a five-yearly audit of his own officials is a serious instrument of control. **He changed what the penetration was FOR** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Trace the evolution of the Indian state's fiscal claim."** *(a synthesis question this file makes possible)* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Run *BALI* and *BHAGA* across five files: Rig Vedic *bali* as a VOLUNTARY gift → Later Vedic "voluntary OR COMPULSORY… became TRIBUTES" → mahajanapada *bali* as **A TAX ON MEASURED CULTIVABLE LAND** → Gupta *bali* as "AN OPPRESSIVE TAX" → and no reference to it at all under Harsha.** ⭐ **Then the RATE: one-sixth from the mahajanapadas to Harsha — but the Mauryas took "USUALLY ABOUT ONE-FOURTH" in practice, and [the Delhi Sultanate demanded HALF](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md).** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thesis: the normative rate barely moved for two thousand years; what changed was the state's CAPACITY TO ASSESS and its willingness to exceed the norm** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Assess Mahmud Gawan."** *(and it is a complete tragedy in five moves)* | ⭐ **THE ACHIEVEMENT: a Persian-born scholar-statesman — theology, Persian, mathematics, poetry, prose; the BIDAR MADRASA with 3,000 manuscripts; GUNPOWDER introduced via Persian chemists and used at Belgaum; victories in Konkan, Orissa and against Vijayanagar.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE REFORMS: four provinces into EIGHT; some districts placed under central administration; ⭐ ONE FORT PER GOVERNOR; and land-assignment holders made ACCOUNTABLE for income and expenditure.** ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FALL: the reforms fell on the DECCANI nobles, sharpening the DECCANI–PARDESI rivalry; a FORGED LETTER; a Sultan "NOT HAPPY WITH GAWAN'S DOMINANCE"; execution — "ALTHOUGH HE REMAINED FAIR AND NEUTRAL."** ⭐ **THE CONSEQUENCE: the foreign nobles withdrew to their provinces and the Sultanate disintegrated.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thesis: a state whose elite is split between locals and immigrants cannot centralise, because every reform reads as one faction moving against the other** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The Bahmani–Vijayanagar conflict and its consequences."** | ⚠️ **Do NOT re-narrate Vijayanagara — [Theme 7](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/07_Theme7_An_Imperial_Capital_Vijayanagara.md) is your source for that side.** ⭐ **THE CAUSES: the RAICHUR DOAB, and — the half most answers miss — **GOA and HONAVAR as the supply points for IMPORTED WAR-HORSES**.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE PATTERN: two centuries of war in which "NEITHER SIDE GAINED LASTING CONTROL", "IN SPITE OF THE HUGE FATALITIES."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE OUTCOME: the Bahmani break-up into FIVE sultanates; their coalition at TALIKOTA (1565) destroying Vijayanagar; and then "WITHIN A CENTURY" the Mughals absorbing them all.** ⭐ **Close on the chain: sultanates destroy Vijayanagar → Mughals destroy the sultanates → the Deccan wars exhaust the Mughals → [the Marathas rise](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Administrative continuity in the Deccan."** *(a link almost nobody makes)* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Start from MOHAMMED I's EIGHT MINISTERS, and the textbook's own claim that his system was "FOLLOWED BY ALL THE SUCCESSOR SULTANATES AS WELL AS BY THE MARATHAS LATER."** ⭐ **Note that ***PESHWA*** is on that fourteenth-century Bahmani list — a **PERSIAN** word for "foremost".** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then [Shivaji's *ASHTA PRADHAN*](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md), also EIGHT, with a chief minister, a finance minister, a foreign secretary, a chief justice and a religious head.** ⭐ **Then the human link: [Shahji Bhonsle served Ahmadnagar and Bijapur](06_Turks_and_Marathas.md), and the Marathas "had earlier served under the BAHMANI SULTANS."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thesis: the Maratha state inherited its administrative form from the sultanates it went on to destroy** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The origins of untouchability."** *(GS1 Society)* | ⭐ **From this unit: a NEW category appearing WITH the mahajanapadas, "PLACED BELOW THE SHUDRAS", living "ON THE FRINGES OF THE SETTLEMENTS", subsisting on hunting and gathering, "GIVEN ONLY MENIAL JOBS AS URBANISATION WAS ON THE RISE" — and ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ speaking "THEIR OWN LANGUAGE, DIFFERENT FROM THAT OF THE INDO-ARYANS."** ⭐ **Then the linguistic evidence from [Unit 2](05_Megalithic_and_Iron_Age.md): 300 Munda and Dravidian words already in the *Rig Veda*, and Munda speakers in the Ganga valley.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thesis: exclusion tracked the FRONTIER OF ARYANISATION — an absorbed, linguistically distinct population placed at the bottom of an expanding order — and Hieun Tsang found the same groups [outside the city walls a thousand years later](07_Harsha_and_Regional_Kingdoms.md)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The intellectual ferment of the sixth century BCE."** | ⚠️ **[Cl.6 Ch.6](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_06_Our_Pasts_I/Notes/06_New_Questions_and_Ideas.md) and [Theme 4](../../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md) own the Buddha and Mahavira — use this unit only for BREADTH.** ⭐ **The SIX teachers of the *SAMANNAPHALA SUTTA*, with **NIGANTHA NATAPUTTA = MAHAVIRA** and **MAKKHALI GOSALA** the greatest Ajivika.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AJITA KESAKAMBALIN's materialism, quoted — "the wise and the foolish alike are ANNIHILATED."** ⭐ **The FIVE causes the unit lists** *(Vedic rigidity, territorial identities, the incomplete penetration of Vedic religion, merchants seeking status matching their wealth, and the Kshatriya grievance over the *ashramas*)*. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Close on the source problem: we know the materialists ONLY through a Buddhist text that preserved their views IN ORDER TO REFUTE THEM** |

## 🔁 Recap — the three top-ups in six lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SIXTEEN MAHAJANAPADAS**, per *"Puranic, Buddhist and Jaina traditions"* — **Gandhara, Kamboja, Asmaka, Vatsa, Avanti, Shurasena, Chedi, Malla, Kuru, Panchala, Matsya, Vajji, Anga, Kasi, Kosala, Magadha** — ⚠️ **but keep NCERT's hedge: *"although the lists VARY."*** ⭐ **Two types: MONARCHIES with advisory *parishad* and *sabha*, and *GANA-SANGHAS* where "the heads of the different clans" decided collectively.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The mahajanapada period is where UNTOUCHABILITY appears — "a NEW SOCIAL CATEGORY… PLACED BELOW THE SHUDRAS", on the fringes of settlements, and ⭐ speaking "THEIR OWN LANGUAGE, DIFFERENT FROM THAT OF THE INDO-ARYANS."** ⭐ **And the six heterodox teachers of the *SAMANNAPHALA SUTTA* — including *NIGANTHA NATAPUTTA*, who is MAHAVIRA — survive mainly because a Buddhist text preserved them in order to refute them.**
3. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **On the Mauryas, read the *ARTHASHASTRA* with its warning label: "DATED TO A FEW CENTURIES LATER" and "A PRESCRIPTIVE TEXT" — triangulate with ASOKA'S EDICTS and MEGASTHENES.** ⭐ **And hold the distinction that answers the centralisation question: UNIFORM ≠ CENTRALISED, because "given the existing state of technology… A DECENTRALISED ADMINISTRATIVE SYSTEM HAD TO BE IN PLACE."**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE MAURYAN LADDER: king + council + *purohita* + *MAHAMATRIYAS* → four provinces (SUVARNAGIRI, UJJAIN, TAXILA, TOSALI) under ROYAL PRINCES, with PATALIPUTRA administered directly → *STHANIKA* (district) → *GOPA* (5–10 villages) → *GRAMANI* (village, CENTRALLY APPOINTED) — plus the *NAGARIKA* for towns and the *SAMAHARTA* as collector-general.** ⭐ **Two courts: *DHARMASTHIYA* for civil law, *KANTAKASODHANA* — "removal of thorns" — as a spy-backed police court whose object was "TO EXTEND GOVERNMENT CONTROL OVER MOST ASPECTS OF ORDINARY LIFE."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Against which Asoka set *YUKTAS*, *RAJJUKAS* and *PRADESIKAS* touring every five years to teach *dhamma*, with an auditor sent to check they had.**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BAHMANIS began when ZAFAR KHAN declared independence at Devagiri in 1345, moved to GULBARGA and became BAHMAN SHAH — eleven years AFTER Vijayanagar's founding.** ⭐ **Two centuries of war followed over the RAICHUR DOAB **and over GOA and HONAVAR, the ports through which both sides imported their WAR-HORSES**.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MOHAMMED I took GOLKONDA and the TURQUOISE THRONE from Warangal in 1363 and built a council of EIGHT MINISTERS — including a ***PESHWA*** — that "was followed by all the successor sultanates AS WELL AS BY THE MARATHAS LATER."**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MAHMUD GAWAN split four provinces into EIGHT, took all but ONE FORT from each governor, and made land-assignees accountable — and was executed on a FORGED LETTER because the reforms fell on the DECCANI nobles and he was a PARDESI, "although he remained FAIR AND NEUTRAL."** ⚠️ **The Sultanate then broke into FIVE — Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Berar, Golkonda and puppet Bidar — which combined to destroy Vijayanagar at TALIKOTA (1565), and were themselves absorbed by the Mughals "within a century."**
