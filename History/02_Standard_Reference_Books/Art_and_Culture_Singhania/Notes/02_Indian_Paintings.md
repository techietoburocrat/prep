# 📚 PART A — PRINCIPLES AND PREHISTORY

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The `Shadanga` — Vatsyayana's six limbs

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Source: `Kamasutra`, Vatsyayana, 3rd century AD.** *(A practice-question answer, and a perfect opening line for any painting essay.)*

| # | Limb | Meaning |
|:--:|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐ **Rupabheda** | **Variety of form** |
| 2 | ⭐ **Sadrisyam** | **Portrayal of likeness** of the subject |
| 3 | ⭐ **Bhava** | **Creation of lustre and gleam** with colours |
| 4 | ⭐ **Varnikabhanga** | **Mixing of colours** to resemble the effects of modelling |
| 5 | ⭐ **Pramanam** | **Proportion** of the object or subject |
| 6 | ⭐ **Lavanyayoganam** | **Immersion of emotions** |

> 🧠 **Memory hook: "RS-BV-PL"** — *Rupabheda, Sadrisyam, Bhava, Varnikabhanga, Pramanam, Lavanyayoganam.*

⭐⭐ **The named painting types** *(from Brahmanical and Buddhist literature)*:

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Lepya Chitra** | Myths and lore represented **on TEXTILES** |
| ⭐ **Lekhya Chitra** | **Line drawings and sketches** |
| **Dhuli Chitra / Pata Chitra** | *(dust/floor painting / cloth painting)* |

⭐ **From Vishakhadatta's *Mudrarakshasa*** — three *pata* styles:

| Style | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Cauka Pitaka** | **Isolated framed** drawings |
| **Dighala Pitaka** | ⭐ **Long SCROLLS** |
| **Yama Pitaka** | **Isolated** paintings |

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Prehistoric painting — the three phases

⭐⭐ **Rock engravings are called `PETROGLYPHS`.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Bhimbetka's paintings were discovered in 1957–58 by V. S. WAKANKAR.**

| Phase | Dates | ⭐ Colours | ⭐ Themes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Upper Palaeolithic** | **40000–10000 BC** | **Ochre/geru** with lime and water; **red, white, yellow, green**. ⭐⭐ **White, dark red and green for LARGE ANIMALS; RED for HUNTERS; GREEN mostly for DANCERS** | Bison, elephant, rhino, tigers |
| ⭐ **Mesolithic** | **10000–4000 BC** | ⭐⭐ **Mainly RED** | ⭐ **Paintings become SMALLER**; **group hunting**, grazing, riding |
| ⭐ **Chalcolithic** | — | ⭐⭐ **More GREEN and YELLOW** | ⭐ **BATTLE scenes**; men riding horses and elephants, bows and arrows |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Bhimbetka — the facts that get asked

- **South of Bhopal**, in the **Vindhyan ranges, Madhya Pradesh**; ⭐⭐ **UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2003**
- ⭐ **More than 500 rock paintings**; oldest estimated at **30,000 years**
- ⭐⭐ **Continuous occupation from 100,000 BC to 1000 AD** — with **paintings painted on top of one another**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Spans Upper Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Chalcolithic, early historic AND medieval — but MOST belong to the MESOLITHIC**
- ⭐ Called the **"Zoo Rock Shelter"** for its elephant, rhinoceros, cattle, snake, spotted deer, barasingha
- ⭐ **Haematite ores** gave the red

⭐ **Other prehistoric sites**: **Mirzapur**, **Panchmarhi**, **Narsinghgarh (MP)** *(⭐ **spotted deer skins left drying — evidence that tanning was perfected**)*, **Jogimara (Chhattisgarh)**, and the Chhattisgarh cluster — **Kanker** *(Udkuda, Garagodi, Khairkheda, Gotitola, Kulgaon)*, **Ghodsar and Kohabaur (Koriya)**, ⭐ **Chitwa Dongri (Durg)** — *which has a **Chinese figure riding a donkey**, **dragons** and agricultural scenes*.

# 📚 PART B — MURALS

> ⭐⭐ **Definition and date range: works on walls or a solid structure, datable between the 10th century BC and the 10th century AD.** ⭐ **Their defining constraint is SIZE — they cannot be contained on paper.** Used by **Buddhism, Jainism and Hinduism**.

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The mural sites — *the delta beyond Ajanta*

| Site | State | ⭐ What distinguishes it |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Ajanta** | **Maharashtra** | ⭐ **Murals in caves 9 and 10 belong to the SHUNGA period; the rest are GUPTA.** Caves 1 and 2 are the **most recent**. Both **murals and true fresco** *(on wet plaster)*, in **tempera**; ⭐⭐ **every female figure has a UNIQUE HAIRSTYLE**; **animals and birds shown with emotions**; **emotions expressed through hand gestures**; outlines in **red ochre**, contours brown/black/deep red |
| ⭐ **Ellora** | **Maharashtra** | ⭐ **Five caves, mostly the Kailasa temple; TWO phases** — the first during carving, the second **centuries later in GUJARATI style** *(a procession of Shaiva holy men)*. Earlier phase: **Vishnu and Lakshmi borne by Garuda**. All **three religions** |
| ⭐ **Bagh** | **Madhya Pradesh** | ⭐⭐ **"An extension of the Ajanta school"** — but the figures are **more tightly modelled, with stronger outline, more earthly and human**. ⭐ **Cave 4 = `Rang Mahal`**. More **secular** in feel |
| ⭐⭐ **Sittanavasal** *(Arivar Koil)* | **Tamil Nadu** | ⭐⭐ **JAIN**, near Pudukkottai. Paintings on **walls, ceiling AND pillars**. ⭐⭐⭐ **Central element: a POND WITH LOTUSES**, monks collecting flowers, ducks, swans, fish — depicting ⭐ **`SAMAVASARANA`, the audience hall where Tirthankaras preach after attaining *kevala-gnana***. Attributed either to **Mahendravarman I (Pallava)** or a **Pandya** renovation, 7th c. |
| **Armamalai** | **Tamil Nadu** | ⭐ **Vellore district; a natural cave converted into a JAIN temple in the 8th c.**; depicts the ⭐ **`Astathik Palakas`** — deities protecting the eight corners |
| ⭐ **Ravan Chhaya** | **Odisha** | ⭐⭐ **Keonjhar district; the rock shelter is shaped like a HALF-OPENED UMBRELLA**; believed to be a **royal hunting lodge**; a **royal procession** of the **7th c.**, plus **Chola-period remains from the 11th c.** |
| ⭐⭐ **Lepakshi** | **Andhra Pradesh** 🔴 | ⭐ **Veerabhadra temple, 16th c., VIJAYANAGARA period**; Ramayana, Mahabharata, Vishnu incarnations. ⭐⭐ **Marked by the COMPLETE ABSENCE OF PRIMARY COLOURS, especially BLUE**, and by ⭐ **a DECLINE in quality**; forms outlined in **black** |
| **Jogimara** | **Chhattisgarh** | ⭐ **Artificially carved, Surguja district, c. 1000–300 BC**; an **inscription of a LOVE STORY in BRAHMI**; attached to an **amphitheatre**; dancing couples, elephants, fish; distinct **red outline**. ⭐ The **Sitabenga rock-cut theatre** is nearby |
| **Badami** | **Karnataka** | ⭐⭐ **Among the EARLIEST SURVIVING HINDU paintings**, 6th–7th c.; resembles Ajanta and Bagh; **big half-closed eyes, protruding lips**; ⭐ **Cave 3 has a four-armed BRAHMA on his swan**; Chalukyan kings depicted |

> ⚠️⚠️ **[VERIFY] — A CLEAR ERROR, AND THE BOOK CONTRADICTS ITSELF.** *This chapter says the Ajanta caves were carved **"in 4th century AD"** and that the paintings took four to five centuries **"under the reign of the MAURYAN EMPIRE."*** ⭐⭐⭐ **Both halves are wrong, and Singhania's own Chapter 1 says so** — there it correctly gives **200 BC–650 AD** under **VAKATAKA** patronage *(Harishena)*. **The Mauryas ended c. 185 BC and had nothing to do with Ajanta.** ⭐ **Use: 2nd century BC to 7th century AD, Satavahana then Vakataka.**

### ⭐⭐ Ajanta's named paintings

- **Bodhisattvas in *tribhanga* pose, Cave 1** — ⭐ **`Padmapani` (Avalokiteshvara — COMPASSION)**, **`Vajrapani` (POWER)**, **`Manjusri` (WISDOM)**
- ⭐ **The Dying Princess — Cave 16**
- ⭐ **Shibi Jataka** *(King Shibi offers his own flesh to save a pigeon)* · **Matri-Poshaka Jataka** *(the ungrateful man saved by an elephant betrays it to the king)*
- ⭐ *(→ **Mahaparinirvana in Cave 26** and the **Naga king in Cave 19** are in [Ch.1 §9](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md))*

> ⭐ **Jataka tales** = the Buddha's **previous births, in human AND animal form** — as king, outcast, god or elephant, always exhibiting a virtue. **Named ones**: The Ass in the Lion's Skin · The Swan with Golden Feathers · King Shibi · The Turtle Who Couldn't Stop Talking · The Ox Who Envied the Pig.

# 📚 PART C — MINIATURES

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ What makes a miniature

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The etymology is a trap.** ***"Miniature" comes from the Latin `MINIUM` — RED LEAD PAINT***, used in illuminated manuscripts. ⭐⭐ **It does NOT come from "minimum" and does not primarily mean "small."**

**⭐⭐⭐ The two technical rules:**
1. **The painting must not be larger than 25 square inches.**
2. ⭐ **The subject must be painted at not more than ONE-SIXTH of actual size** *(an adult head of 9 inches → painted no larger than 1.5 inches)*.

**⭐⭐ The conventions:** human figures usually in **side profile**, with **bulging eyes, pointed nose, slim waist**; ⭐⭐ **skin BROWN in Rajasthani miniatures, FAIRER in Mughal ones**; **divine beings like Krishna are BLUE**; women have **long black hair and black eyes**; men wear **turbans**.

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The two early schools

| | ⭐ **PALA** | ⭐ **APABHRAMSA** |
|---|---|---|
| **Dates** | **750–1150 AD** | **11th–15th c.** |
| **Where** | **Eastern India** *(Bengal/Bihar)* | ⭐ **Gujarat and the Mewar region of Rajasthan** — **western India** |
| **Religion** | ⭐ **BUDDHIST** — used by monks; **Vajrayana** patronage | ⭐ **JAIN** first, later appropriated by the **Vaishnava** school |
| **Medium** | ⭐⭐ **Palm leaf or vellum.** ⭐ **Because the religion practised non-violence to all living beings, only BANANA or COCONUT leaves could be used** | **Palm leaf early, PAPER later** |
| ⭐ **Style** | ⭐⭐ **Sinuous lines, subdued background tones; LONELY SINGLE FIGURES — group paintings are rare**; simple compositions | ⭐⭐ **Mural painting in reduced dimension** — no separate style developed. ⭐ **Colours carry symbolic meaning**: red, yellow, ochre; later **bright and gold** |
| ⭐ **Faces** | — | ⭐⭐ **FISH-SHAPED BULGING EYES, pointed nose, DOUBLE CHIN**; angular faces in the **third and fourth profile**; figures **stiff**; women with **enlarged hips and breasts**; ⭐ **animals and birds represented like TOYS** |
| **Painters / works** | **Dhimman** and **Vitapala** | ⭐ ***Kalpasutra*** and ***Kalakacharya Katha*** *(15th c.)*. Introduced ⭐ **Gita Govinda and SECULAR LOVE** into a Jain-dominated iconography |

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The transition and the Sultanate

⭐ **Transition-period features**: colours applied **flat**; dress and outlines demarcated in **black**; ⭐ **faces at a THREE-QUARTER angle**, giving a **detached appearance**; landscapes full of trees and rocks that **do not try to replicate nature**.

| Sultanate miniature | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ ***Nimatnama*** | ⭐⭐⭐ **A BOOK ON COOKERY**, under **Nasir Shah of Mandu** — *"the finest example"*; shows the **synthesis of indigenous and Persian styles** |
| ⭐ **Lodi Khuladar** | A style prevalent **between Delhi and Jaunpur**; ⭐ **became the basis of "the Sultanate formulae"** |

> ⭐⭐ **Then three styles dominate the medieval landscape — MUGHAL, RAJPUT and DECCAN — all taking from Sultanate precedents but developing their own individuality.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mughal miniature, ruler by ruler

> ✅ **[Cl.12 Suppl.](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/09_Supplement_Kings_and_Chronicles_The_Mughal_Courts.md) owns the IDEOLOGY** — Abu'l Fazl's *"magical art"*, the *ulama*'s objection, the halo and allegory, the anonymous paper-makers. ⭐ **Take only the TECHNIQUE and the ruler-wise changes below, and cross-link.**

⭐⭐ **The defining shift: from depicting GOD to GLORIFYING THE RULER** — hunting scenes, historical events, court life. ⭐ **Brilliant colours; accuracy of line drawing; diverse themes EXCEPT religious ones.** ⭐⭐ **They brought `FORESHORTENING` into the Indian painter's repertoire** — *"objects drawn so that they look closer and smaller than they really are"* *(→ the same technique appears in [Ch.1 §15](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md) as an architectural device)*.

| Ruler | ⭐ What changed |
|---|---|
| **Babur** | No time to commission much; said to have patronised the Persian artist **Bihzad** |
| ⭐⭐ **Humayun** | ⭐⭐⭐ **THE PIVOT.** Exiled to Persia, he acquired **`ABDUS SAMAD` and `MIR SAYYID ALI`**, who returned with him — ⭐ **these two brought the PERSIAN influence into Mughal painting** |
| ⭐⭐ **Akbar** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Established a formal studio — the `TASVIR KHANA`** — with **salaried artists**; awarded painters who made lifelike images; ⭐⭐ **invited INDIAN artists who had served previous rulers → "the Indian influence" begins**. Features: ⭐ **three-dimensional figures**, continued **foreshortening**, **calligraphy in paintings**; ⭐⭐ **the transformation of POPULAR art into COURT art**. Painters: ⭐ **Daswant, Basawan, Kesu**. Manuscripts: ⭐ ***Tutinama*, *Hamzanama*, *Anvar-i-Suhaili*, *Gulistan of Sadi*** |
| ⭐⭐ **Jahangir** | ⭐⭐⭐ **THE ZENITH.** ⭐ **A naturalist — preferred FLORA AND FAUNA**; emphasised **naturalism in portraiture**; ⭐⭐ **introduced DECORATED MARGINS, sometimes as elaborate as the paintings**. Himself a good artist with a private workshop. ⭐⭐ **Famous naturalistic studies: the ZEBRA, the TURKEY, the COCK.** ⭐⭐⭐ **`USTAD MANSUR`** — expert in the most complex faces. ⭐ ***Ayar-i-Danish*** *(Touchstone of Knowledge)* illustrated |
| ⭐ **Shah Jahan** | ⭐⭐ **Reversed the naturalism** — liked **artificial elements** and *"unnatural stillness"*, inspired by **European influence**. ⭐⭐ **Replaced CHARCOAL with the PENCIL** for drawing; **increased gold and silver**; **brighter palette**. *The atelier grew but the style changed* |
| **Aurangzeb** | ⭐⭐ **Did not encourage painting** → ⭐⭐⭐ **court painters MIGRATED to the provincial courts of Rajasthan** — *which is exactly what seeds the Rajasthani schools below* |

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Rajasthani schools — **the five sub-schools**

> ⭐⭐ **Singhania is honest about the debate on origins, and it is worth a Mains line: did Rajput courts patronise painting *in emulation of the Mughals*? Or did **artists arriving from the Mughal atelier** at Bikaner/Jodhpur/Kishangarh sow them? Or did **artworks from the DECCAN sultanates** decide it? Or did **local indigenous traditions PRE-DATE Mughal influence** entirely? ⭐ **"There is no single answer" is itself the sophisticated answer.**

| School | ⭐ The identifier |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Mewar** | ⭐ **Resisted Mughal suzerainty longest** — acceded only under **Shah Jahan**; capital moved **Ranthambhore → Chittorgarh → wilderness → Udaipur**. ⭐⭐ **Dominated by the figure of `SAHIBDIN`**, who depicted the ***Rasikapriya*, *Ramayana* and *Bhagavata Purana***. ⭐⭐ After his death the style shifted to **court life** — the extraordinary ⭐ **`TAMASHA` paintings** showing **court ceremonial and city views in unprecedented detail** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Kishangarh** | ⭐⭐⭐ **THE `BANI THANI`** — the most famous single image in Rajasthani painting. Bound up with the romance of ⭐ **Sawant Singh** *(the prince, who wrote as the poet **Nagari Das**)* and **Bani Thani**, painted by ⭐ **`NIHAL CHAND`**. ⭐⭐ **Said to resemble RADHA: a distinctive profile, LARGE LUSTROUS EYES, THIN LIPS, POINTED CHIN, and an `odhni` defining the side profile.** Radha–Krishna themes |
| ⭐ **Bundi** *(with Kota = **`HADOTI`**)* | ⭐ **Twin kingdoms** split between two brothers. ⭐⭐ **Their kings were such devout Krishna-bhaktas that in the 18th c. they declared themselves MERE REGENTS ruling on the god's behalf** *(as at Udaipur and Jaipur)*. ⭐⭐ **Features: local VEGETATION painted in detail; ROUND faces with pointed noses; the SKY in varied colours, usually with a RED RIBBON visible.** Later patron: **Rao Ram Singh II** |
| ⭐ **Amber–Jaipur** *(= **`DHUNDAR`** school)* | ⭐ Earliest evidence in the **wall paintings at BAIRAT**; also the **Amer palace walls and mausoleum**. ⭐ **Men in Mughal dress but the overall finish is FOLK-STYLED.** ⭐⭐ **Zenith under `SAWAI PRATAP SINGH` (18th c.)** — deeply religious; his ⭐ **`suratkhana`** *(department of painting)* illustrated the **Bhagavata Purana, Ramayana, Ragamala** and portraits |
| ⭐ **Marwar** | ⭐⭐ **The most EXTENSIVE** — covers **Jodhpur and Bikaner** *(both **Rathod**)* and **Jaisalmer** *(**Bhati**)*. **15th–16th c.**: colourful clothing on both sexes, following **Mughal** patterns; ⭐⭐ **after the 18th c. the RAJPUT element becomes predominant — LINEAR RHYTHM with BRIGHT COLOURS.** ⭐ **Peak under `MAN SINGH` (1803–43)** — commissioned the **Shiva Purana, Natacharitra, Durgacharitra, Panchatantra** |

### 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mughal vs Rajput — **the 1985 Mains answer**

| Point | **Rajput** | **Mughal** |
|---|---|---|
| **Type** | ⭐ **Initially MURAL and FRESCO; miniature dominant only later** | ⭐ **Based on the PERSIAN miniature style** |
| ⭐⭐ **Themes** | ⭐ **Devotional / religious** | ⭐ **The emperor and his household** — royal pomp, battles, hunting |
| ⭐⭐ **Peculiarity** | ⭐ **Hindu symbols used frequently — LOTUS, PEACOCK, SWAN** | ⭐ **Focus on the person, or on TREES, CAMELS and FALCONS** |

| School | ⭐ Detail |

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Ragamala — *where painting meets [Ch.5 Music](05_Indian_Music.md)*

> ⭐⭐⭐ ***"Garland of Ragas"*** — **a series of illustrative paintings personifying Indian musical ragas.** ⭐⭐ **A classical example of the amalgamation of ART, POETRY and CLASSICAL MUSIC.** Made in **most Indian schools from the 16th–17th centuries** — hence **Pahari Ragamala, Rajasthani/Rajput Ragamala, Deccan Ragamala, Mughal Ragamala**.

⭐⭐ **Each raga is personified by a colour**, telling the story of a **`nayaka` and `nayika`** in a mood, **and elucidating the SEASON and the TIME of day or night the raga is to be sung** — and many also name the **Hindu deity attached to the raga** *(Bhairava/Bhairavi → Shiva; Sri → Devi)*.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The six principal ragas in the Ragamala: `BHAIRAVA, DEEPAK, SRI, MALKAUSH, MEGHA, HINDOLA`** — ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **exactly the six main ragas in [Ch.5 §5](05_Indian_Music.md). Learn the two lists together; they are the same six, and a question can approach from either side.**

|---|---|---|

# 📚 PART E — FOLK PAINTINGS

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Bridge these to [Cl.7 Ch.7](../../../01_NCERT/Class_07_Our_Pasts_II/Notes/07_The_Making_of_Regional_Cultures.md)'s line — *"ordinary women and men painted as well, on pots, walls of houses, floors, cloth."*** *That sentence is the reason this section exists, and it is the best framing for a Mains answer on folk art.*

| Painting | State | ⭐ The identifiers |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Madhubani** *(Mithila)* | **Bihar** *(+ Nepal Terai)* | ⭐⭐ **Done by WOMEN**; Hindu religious motifs; ⭐ **symbolic figures — FISH = good luck and fertility**; traditionally on walls with **rice paste and vegetable colours on a cow-dung-and-mud base**; ⭐⭐ **NO SHADING → TWO-DIMENSIONAL**; ⭐ **double line border, bold colours, ornate floral patterns, exaggerated facial features**; gaps filled with flowers/trees/animals. ⭐⭐ **Origin story: the king of Mithila ordered walls painted for SITA and RAMA's marriage.** ⭐⭐ **Recognition came in 1970 when the President honoured `JAGDAMBA DEVI`**; also Sita Devi, Ganga Devi, Bua Devi. ⭐⭐ **Has GI status** |
| ⭐⭐ **Pattachitra** | **Odisha** | ⭐ ***Patta*** *(cloth)* + ***chitra*** *(picture)*. Base is **treated cloth**; colours from **burnt coconut shells, Hingula, Ramaraja, lamp black**; ⭐⭐ **NO pencil or charcoal — the brush draws the outline in RED or YELLOW first**; finished with a ⭐ **LACQUER coating for gloss**. Themes: ⭐ **Jagannath and the Vaishnava cult**. ⭐ **`RAGHURAJPUR`** is the centre; resembles the murals of **Puri and Konark**. ⭐⭐ **On palm leaf it is `TALAPATTACHITRA`** — layers glued with **window-like openings revealing a second image beneath** |
| ⭐⭐ **Patua art** | **West Bengal** | ⭐ **~1,000 years old**; began with painters telling ⭐ **`MANGAL KAVYAS`**; done on ⭐ **`pats`/SCROLLS**; the **patuas travelled village to village SINGING their stories for food or money**. ⭐ **Today: poster paints on sewn paper sheets, commenting on POLITICAL AND SOCIAL ISSUES.** ⭐ **From MIDNAPUR**; those calling themselves ***chitrakar*** are from **24 Parganas and Birbhum** |
| ⭐⭐ **Kalighat** | **West Bengal** | ⭐⭐ **19th-c. urban Calcutta** — by **rural migrants settled around the Kalighat temple**. ⭐ **Watercolour on MILL PAPER, brushes of CALF and SQUIRREL hair**; a **plaque-like effect on a neutral background**. ⭐⭐⭐ **Began religious, then turned to SOCIAL SENTIMENT — considered THE FIRST IN INDIA TO EXPRESS SUBALTERN SENTIMENTS and address customers directly**; satirises **the hypocrisies of the newly rich** and shows **changing roles of women and men** |
| **Paitkar** | **Jharkhand** | ⭐ **Tribal SCROLL painting; among the most ancient schools**; linked to ⭐ **Ma Mansa**; associated with **alms-giving and yajnas**. ⭐⭐ **Theme: "WHAT HAPPENS TO HUMAN LIFE AFTER DEATH."** ⭐ **On the verge of extinction** |
| ⭐⭐ **Kalamkari** | **Andhra Pradesh** 🔴 | ⭐ From ***kalam*** = **pen** — ⭐⭐ **a SHARP-POINTED BAMBOO pen regulating the flow of colour**, soaked in ⭐ **fermented jaggery and water**; **cotton fabric**, **vegetable dyes**. ⭐⭐ **Two centres: `SRIKALAHASTI`** *(free-hand, Hindu mythology, wall hangings)* **and `MACHILIPATNAM`** *(cartwheel, lotus, animals, interlacing floral patterns)* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Warli** | **Maharashtra–Gujarat border** | ⭐ By the **Warli** people; tradition traced to **2500–3000 BC**; resembles **Bhimbetka murals**. ⭐⭐ **Central motif: the `CHAUK`/`CHAUKAT`**, surrounded by fishing, hunting, farming, dance. ⭐ **Goddess `PALAGHATA` (fertility)**. ⭐⭐⭐ **A basic graphic vocabulary of THREE SHAPES from nature — CIRCLE (sun/moon), TRIANGLE (trees/mountains), SQUARE (sacred enclosure)**; ⭐⭐ **humans and animals = TWO TRIANGLES JOINED AT THE TIP with a circle for the head**. ⭐⭐ **Base of mud, branches and cow dung → RED OCHRE; ONLY WHITE pigment used**, of **gum and rice powder** |
| ⭐⭐ **Thangka** | **Sikkim, HP, Ladakh, Arunachal** | ⭐ Buddhist; by **monks**; **cotton canvas, white background**, vegetable/mineral dyes; framed in ⭐ **silk brocade**. ⭐⭐⭐ **The COLOUR CODE: RED = intensity of passion (love or hatred) · GOLDEN = life/birth · WHITE = serenity · BLACK = anger · GREEN = consciousness · YELLOW = compassion.** ⭐⭐ **Three types: (1) the Buddha's life from birth to enlightenment; (2) abstract — the WHEEL OF LIFE; (3) for offerings or meditation.** ⭐ Earnings help sustain the **monasteries** |
| ⭐⭐ **Manjusha** | **Bihar** *(Bhagalpur)* | ⭐ **Also `ANGIKA` art** — *"ang"* refers to one of the **Mahajanapadas**. ⭐⭐⭐ **Called SNAKE PAINTING because snake motifs are always present.** ⭐ **Executed on BOXES of jute and paper** |
| ⭐⭐ **Phad** | **Rajasthan** | ⭐ **A SCROLL type**; religious — the local deities ⭐ **`PABUJI` and `DEVNARAYAN`**; vegetable colours on cloth called ***phad***, ⭐⭐ **15 or 30 FEET long**; **large eyes, round faces**; **processions** common |
| ⭐⭐ **Cheriyal Scroll** | **Telangana** | ⭐ A type of ⭐ **`NAKASHI` art**; ⭐⭐ **a continuous story like a COMIC or ballad**, narrated by the **Balladeer community** with music as they travel; Hindu epics and Puranas; ⭐⭐ **up to 45 FEET high**. ⭐⭐ **GI status in 2007** |
| **Pithora** | **Gujarat & MP** | ⭐ **Tribal; ritual, on house WALLS, to bring peace and prosperity**, drawn on special family occasions; ⭐ **HORSES especially common** |
| ⭐⭐ **Saura** | **Odisha** | ⭐ By the **Saura tribe; similar to WARLI**; a **wall mural**, ritualistic. ⭐⭐ **Called `ITALONS`/`IKONS`, dedicated to `IDITAL`, the main Saura deity.** ⭐ **Painted mostly in WHITE on a RED or YELLOW backdrop**; ⭐ **human shapes GEOMETRICAL and STICK-LIKE** |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in ten lines

1. **Vatsyayana's `shadanga` — six limbs — is the theoretical starting point**, and *Mudrarakshasa* supplies the three *pata* formats.
2. **Prehistory runs in three phases**: **Palaeolithic** *(large animals; red hunters, green dancers)* → **Mesolithic** *(mainly red, smaller, group hunting — and MOST of Bhimbetka)* → **Chalcolithic** *(green and yellow, battle scenes)*.
3. **Murals are defined by size**, run **10th c. BC–10th c. AD**, and go well beyond Ajanta — **Sittanavasal (Jain, the lotus pond), Lepakshi (no blue), Ravan Chhaya, Armamalai, Jogimara, Badami**.
4. ⚠️ **Ajanta is VAKATAKA, not Mauryan** — the book errs here and corrects itself in Chapter 1.
5. **"Miniature" comes from *minium*, red lead** — not from "small"; the rules are **≤25 sq in** and **≤1/6 actual size**.
6. **Two early schools: PALA (Buddhist, palm leaf, lonely single figures) and APABHRAMSA (Jain then Vaishnava, fish-shaped bulging eyes, toy-like animals).**
7. **Mughal painting pivots on HUMAYUN's two Persian recruits**, institutionalises under **Akbar's *tasvir khana***, peaks with **Jahangir's naturalism and Ustad Mansur**, stiffens under **Shah Jahan** — and **collapses under Aurangzeb, scattering painters into Rajasthan**.
8. **Five Rajasthani schools**: **Mewar** *(Sahibdin, tamasha)*, **Kishangarh** *(Bani Thani)*, **Bundi–Kota** *(Krishna-regents, red ribbon in the sky)*, **Amber–Jaipur** *(Dhundar, suratkhana)*, **Marwar** *(Man Singh)*.
9. **Pahari splits Northern (Jammu/Dogra) from Southern (Basohli, Kangra)** — **Basohli bold, Kangra lyrical**, the latter triggered by **Nadir Shah in 1739**.
10. **Folk painting is where the state-matching marks are**: Madhubani and Manjusha *(Bihar)*, Pattachitra and Saura *(Odisha)*, Patua and Kalighat *(Bengal)*, Paitkar *(Jharkhand)*, Kalamkari *(AP)*, Warli *(Maharashtra)*, Phad *(Rajasthan)*, Cheriyal *(Telangana)*, Pithora *(Gujarat/MP)*, Thangka *(Himalayan)*.
