# 📚 PART A — CLASSICAL SANSKRIT THEATRE ⭐⭐⭐⭐

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Origins and vocabulary

- ⭐⭐ **The ruins at `SITABENGA` and `JOGIMARA` (Chhattisgarh) are held to be the world's oldest amphitheatres** *(→ [Ch.2 §3](02_Indian_Paintings.md))*.
- ⭐⭐ ***Nataka*** derives from ***nata*** — **"a dancer."** Other terms: ***rupaka, drishyakavya, preksakavya***.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Two kinds of play:**

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Lokadharmi** | ⭐ **REALISTIC** depictions of daily life |
| ⭐ **Natyadharmi** | ⭐ **CONVENTIONAL** — stylised narration, **overt symbolism** |

- ⭐ **Ten types of play** — *Anka, Bhana, Dima, Ihamriga, Nataka, Prahasana, Prakarana, Samavakara, Vithi, Vyayoga*. ⭐⭐ **The Natya Shastra describes only TWO of them — `Nataka` and `Prakarana`.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The playwrights — *a certain match-the-following*

| Playwright | Work(s) | ⭐ Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Ashvaghosha** | ***Sariputraprakarana*** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The FIRST example of classical Sanskrit drama** — a **nine-act** play |
| ⭐ **Bhasa** | **13 plays** | An early major dramatist ⚠️ |
| ⭐⭐ **Sudraka** | ***Mricchakatika*** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The FIRST to introduce CONFLICT — and the first play with an ANTAGONIST** alongside hero and heroine |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Kalidasa** | ***Malavikagnimitra*, *Vikramorvashiyam*, *Abhijnanashakuntalam*** | ⭐⭐ **Unmatched on "the eternal conflict between DESIRE and DUTY"** |
| ⭐⭐ **Bhavabhuti** | ***Uttararamacharita*, *Mahaviracharita*** | — |
| ⭐⭐ **Vishakhadatta** | ***Mudrarakshasa*** | *(→ its three *pata* styles in [Ch.2 §1](02_Indian_Paintings.md))* |
| ⭐⭐ **Harshavardhana** | ***Ratnavali*** | 🔴 **A named trap — Ratnavali is HARSHA's, not Ashvaghosha's** |

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY]** — *Singhania dates **Bhasa** to the **"4th–5th century B.C."*** ⭐⭐ **This is almost certainly wrong** — Bhasa is conventionally placed between the **2nd century BCE and the 4th century CE**, and in any case *after* Ashvaghosha *(1st–2nd c. CE)*, whom the book itself calls the first Sanskrit dramatist. **The book's own sequence contradicts its date.** *Say "early centuries CE" and avoid a precise claim.*

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The conventions — **a ready-made Mains paragraph**

| Convention | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| **Length** | Generally **four to seven acts** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Endings** | ⭐⭐ **ALWAYS HAPPY — unlike Greek tragedy.** The hero **wins or does not die**; ⭐ **portrayal of tragedy was almost non-existent** |
| **Protagonist** | ⭐ **Male, and always attains the object of his desire** |
| **Structure** | Well-defined **opening, progression, development, pause, conclusion** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The ritual progression of a performance

**(1)** Pre-play rituals — ⭐ **`purva-raga`**, mostly **behind the curtain** → **(2)** the ⭐⭐ **`SUTRADHARA`** *(stage manager and director)* enters **dressed in white** with assistants, **worships the deity** → **(3)** the **leading lady** is summoned; the Sutradhara announces the **time and place** and introduces **the playwright**.

⭐ **The theatre held about 400 people** *(per Bharata)*. ⭐⭐ **Stages were TWO-STOREYED — the upper floor represented the CELESTIAL sphere, the lower the TERRESTRIAL.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Curtains were used; MASKS WERE NOT.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The three character classes

| Class | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nayaka** *(hero)* | Types — **Lalita** *(kind-hearted)*, **Shanta** *(calm)*, **Uddhata** *(agitated/arrogant)*; the antagonist is the ⭐ **`Pratinayaka`** *(Ravana, Duryodhana)* |
| **Nayika** *(heroine)* | Queens, friends, **courtesans (*ganika*)**, **divine ladies (*divya*)** |
| ⭐⭐ **Vidushaka** *(clown)* | ⭐⭐⭐ **Noble and good-hearted, often the hero's friend — and he QUESTIONS PREVAILING SOCIAL NORMS THROUGH SATIRE.** ⭐⭐ **Traditionally he spoke PRAKRIT while others spoke SANSKRIT** *(the same convention that survives in Kutiyattam, where he speaks Malayalam — → [UNESCO §9](04_and_10_UNESCO_Heritage_Lists.md))* |

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why Sanskrit theatre declined — *a direct Mains question*

1. ⭐ **Dramatists diverged towards POETRY** — lyrical writing gained popularity over dramatic work
2. ⭐⭐ **RIGID ORTHODOXY** of the form restricted new playwrights' creative space
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **Sanskrit became increasingly EMBELLISHED and lost popularity among the masses** — confined to the religious sphere and to Brahmins, while ⭐ **Pali and Prakrit took its place**
4. ⭐ **With the Muslim rulers, theatre took a back seat as DANCE AND MUSIC gained patronage**

> ⭐⭐ **`KUTIYATTAM` is India's OLDEST CONTINUING theatre form — surviving since the 10th century AD in Kerala**, and ⭐ **the only one that fully adheres to the Natya Shastra.** ⭐ Performed in **Sanskrit, Prakrit AND Malayalam**, with **Mizhavu and Edakka**; ⭐⭐ **every character opens with `NIRVAHANA`, a recollection of past events**. Exponent: **Margi Madhu Chakyar**.

# 📚 PART B — FOLK THEATRE ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐ **The arc worth knowing**: most surviving folk theatre emerged **c. 15th–16th century with DEVOTIONAL themes**, later took on **love ballads and local heroes** *(secular)*, and ⭐⭐ **after Independence became a vehicle for DISSEMINATING SOCIAL WISDOM rather than mere entertainment.** *That three-stage arc is a Mains paragraph in itself.*

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Ritual theatre

| Form | State | ⭐ Identifier |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Ankia Nat** | **Assam** | ⭐ **One-act play by SHANKARADEVA and his disciple Madhavadeva, 16th c.**; opera style, Krishna's life; ⭐ **Sutradhar with the `Gayan-Bayan Mandali`** playing **khol and cymbals**; ⭐⭐ **USES MASKS** |
| **Kala** | — | Vaishnavite; Vishnu's incarnations. Branches: **Dashavatar Kala, Gopal Kala, Gaulan Kala** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ramlila** | **Uttar Pradesh** | *(→ [UNESCO §9](04_and_10_UNESCO_Heritage_Lists.md))* |
| ⭐ **Raslila** | **Gujarat** ⚠️ | Radha–Krishna adolescent love *(⚠️ more usually **Braj/UP** — → [Ch.6 §14](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))* |
| ⭐ **Bhuta** | **Karnataka** *(Kannara)* | ⭐ **Worship of DEAD ANCESTORS** *(cf. **Bhoota Aradhane**, [Ch.6 §14](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))* |
| ⭐⭐ **Ramman** | **Uttarakhand** | ⭐ **Bhandari caste wear the sacred NARASIMHA mask** *(→ [UNESCO §9](04_and_10_UNESCO_Heritage_Lists.md))* |

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Theatres of entertainment

| Form | State | ⭐ Identifier |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Bhavai** | **Gujarat & Rajasthan** *(Kutch, Kathiawar)* | ⭐ **A series of small plays called `VESHA`/`SWANGA`**, each with its own plot; **romantic**; instruments **bhungala, jhanjha, tabla**; ⭐⭐ **the Sutradhara is called `NAYAKA`** |
| ⭐ **Daskathia** | **Odisha** | ⭐ **Two narrators — `GAYAKA` (chief singer) and `PALIA` (co-narrator)**; the wooden instrument ⭐ **`kathia`**. Variant: **Chhaiti Ghoda** *(dhol + mohuri, three narrators)* |
| **Garodas** | **Gujarat** | ⭐ **The Garoda community narrating with PAINTED PICTURES** |
| ⭐⭐ **Jatra** | **Eastern India / Bengal** | ⭐⭐ **Open-air; initiated by SRI CHAITANYA** to propagate Krishna's teachings while travelling rural Bengal. Variants **Rama/Shiv/Chandi Jatra**; later **secular, historical and PATRIOTIC** themes. ⭐ **`Sahi Jatra`** is the Odisha street form |
| **Kariyila** | **Himachal Pradesh** | Open-air, at village fairs, **through the night** |
| ⭐ **Maach** | **Malwa, Madhya Pradesh** | ⭐ **Originated at UJJAIN, 17th c.**; ⭐⭐ **dialogues delivered as COUPLETS called `RANGAT DOHAS`** |
| ⭐⭐ **Nautanki** | **North India** | ⭐⭐ **An offshoot of SWANG; the most popular North Indian form; finds mention in ABUL FAZL's *AIN-I-AKBARI***; lyrical dialogue over the ⭐ **`nagara`** drum; ⭐ **two schools — KANPUR and LUCKNOW** |
| ⭐ **Oja-Pali** | **Assam** | ⭐ Tied to the festival of ⭐ **MANASA, the serpent goddess**; three parts — **Baniya, Bhatiyali, Deva Khanda**; ⭐ **`Oja`** the narrator, ⭐ **`Palis`** the chorus |
| ⭐⭐ **Powada** | **Maharashtra** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Written after SHIVAJI KILLED AFZAL KHAN** — operatic ballads of valour, sung by ⭐ **`Gondhalis` and `Shahirs`** |
| ⭐ **Swang** | **Punjab & Haryana** | Musical dramas in verse; **ektara, harmonium, sarangi, dholak, khartal** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Tamasha** | **Maharashtra** | ⭐ **Humour and EROTIC content**; ⭐⭐⭐ **THE UNIQUE FEATURE: FEMALE ACTORS PLAY EVEN THE MALE ROLES** *(the exact inverse of Ramlila and Kathakali)*; accompanied by ⭐ **LAVANI** songs *(→ [Ch.5 §13](05_Indian_Music.md))* |
| ⭐ **Villu Pattu** | **Deccan / TN** | ⭐ **"Bow-song"** — Ramayana narrated with **bow-shaped instruments** |
| ⭐⭐ **Bhand Pather** | **Jammu & Kashmir** | ⭐⭐ **Social satire; SECULAR in outlook although PERFORMED BY MUSLIMS** |
| ⭐ **Bhaona** | **Assam** *(Majuli)* | Presentation of **Ankia Naat**; Vaishnavite |
| ⭐ **Dashavatar** | **Konkan** *(Sindhudurg)* **& North Goa** | ⭐ **By FARMERS**; honours Vishnu's ten incarnations; two parts — ⭐ **`poorva-ranga`** and **`uttara-ranga`** *(the second is the main act)* |
| ⭐ **Naqal / Bhand** | **Punjab** | ⭐⭐ **Mimicry; the artist is a `BEHROOPIYA` or `NAQALCHI` (impersonator)**; comedy carrying a social or political message; **two artists** |

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Theatres of South India

> ⭐⭐ **The structural point worth making in an answer: when Sanskrit theatre declined in the North around the 8th century, it GAINED popularity in the South — and the southern traditions emphasise DANCE where the northern emphasise MUSIC.**

| Form | State | ⭐ Identifier |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Yakshagana** | **Karnataka & AP** | ⭐⭐ **Perhaps the OLDEST surviving theatre tradition**; ⭐ **originated in the VIJAYANAGARA royal courts**, performed by the ⭐ **`Jakkula Varu`** community. Began as a **single-artist descriptive dance-drama**. ⭐⭐ **Variants elsewhere: `LALITA` (Maharashtra), `BHAVAI` (Gujarat), `GANDHARVA GANA` (Nepal).** Plays: *Garudachalam* (Obayya Mantri), *Krishna-Hiramani* (Srinatha), *Sugriva Vijayam* (Rudra Kavi) |
| ⭐⭐ **Burra Katha** | **Andhra Pradesh** | ⭐ Named after the ⭐ **`burra`** percussion instrument; **one narrator + two `vanthas`** giving rhythm and chorus |
| **Pagati Veshaalu** | **Telangana & Krishna dist., AP** | ⭐ **Role-playing around a principal `vesham` (disguise)** |
| ⭐ **Bayalata** | **Karnataka** | ⭐ **Open-air, at the worship of the local deity**; ⭐⭐ **FIVE types — Dasarata, Sannata, Doddata, Parijata, Yakshagana**; ⭐ **Parijata and Yakshagana use a single Sutradhar; the other three a chorus of 3–4 with a Vidushaka** |
| ⭐ **Tal-Maddale** | **Karnataka** | ⭐⭐ **Considered the PREDECESSOR of Yakshagana**; ⭐⭐⭐ **performed SITTING, with NO costume, dance or acting** — narration by a **Bhagavata** aided by **Arthadharis** |
| ⭐⭐ **Theyyam** | **Kerala** | ⭐ **Open-air, before local temples**, honouring **ancestral spirits** and gods; ⭐⭐ **similar to Karnataka's `BHUTA KOLA`**; elaborate headgear |
| ⭐ **Krishnattam** | **Kerala** | ⭐ **Mid-17th c.; based on the *Krishna Geethi*; an EIGHT-DAY carnival over eight consecutive nights** *(→ an ancestor of Kathakali, [Ch.6 §7](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))* |
| ⭐ **Kuruvanji** | **Tamil Nadu** | ⭐ **~300 years old**; classical Tamil poetry; first composed by **Thirukutarajappa Kaviyar**; ⭐⭐ ***kuruvanji* means "FORTUNE-TELLER"*, who predicts the love-struck heroine's fate; danced in **Bharatanatyam** |

# 📚 PART D — PUPPETRY ⭐⭐⭐⭐

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Origins

- ⭐ **Harappa and Mohenjo-daro yielded puppets WITH SOCKETS ATTACHED** — suggesting the art existed then.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The OLDEST WRITTEN reference is in the Tamil classic `SILAPPADIKARAM`** *(1st–2nd c. BC)* — *the same text that gives the earliest mention of the yakshi *(→ [Ch.1 §4](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md))*.
- ⭐⭐ **The philosophical layer worth quoting**: in the ***Bhagavata***, **God is a PUPPETEER controlling the universe with three strings — `Satta`, `Raja` and `Tama`**; and ⭐⭐ **the theatre narrator is the `SUTRADHARA` — literally "the HOLDER OF STRINGS."**
- ⚠️ **Decline**: *"lack of devoted audience and financial security."*

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The four types — **the classification IS the question**

| Type | ⭐ Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **STRING** *(marionette)* | **8–9 inch** wooden figures; strings through holes in **hands, head and back** | **Kathputli, Kundhei, Gombeyatta, Bommalattam** |
| ⭐⭐ **SHADOW** | ⭐ **Flat LEATHER figures painted IDENTICALLY ON BOTH SIDES**, against a **white screen with light from behind** | **Togalu Gombeyatta, Ravanchhaya, Tholu Bommalata** |
| ⭐⭐ **GLOVE** *(sleeve/hand/palm)* | ⭐ **Head worked by the INDEX finger; the two hands by THUMB and MIDDLE finger** | **Pavakoothu** |
| ⭐⭐ **ROD** | ⭐ **Larger glove puppets controlled by RODS from behind a screen**; mainly **Eastern India** | **Yampuri, Putul Nachh** |

| Puppet | State | Type | ⭐ Identifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Kathputli** | **Rajasthan** | String | ⭐ ***kath*** *(wood)* + ***putli*** *(doll)*; ⭐⭐⭐ **THE UNIQUE FEATURE: NO LEGS**; strings tied to the puppeteer's **fingers** |
| ⭐ **Kundhei** | **Odisha** | String | **Light wood, LONG SKIRTS**; ⭐ **more joints → more flexibility**; strings on a **triangular prop**; ⭐ **marked ODISSI influence** |
| ⭐ **Gombeyatta** | **Karnataka** | String | ⭐ **Styled on YAKSHAGANA characters**; ⭐⭐ **MORE THAN ONE PUPPETEER manipulates each puppet** |
| ⭐⭐ **Bommalattam** | **Tamil Nadu** | ⭐ **Rod + String** | ⭐⭐⭐ **THE LARGEST AND HEAVIEST marionettes in India — up to 4.5 ft and 10 kg**; strings tied to an **iron ring worn on the puppeteer's HEAD**; ⭐ **four stages: Vinayaka Puja, Komali, Amanattam, Pusenkanattam** |
| ⭐ **Togalu Gombeyatta** | **Karnataka** | Shadow | ⭐⭐⭐ **PUPPET SIZE VARIES BY SOCIAL STATUS — kings and religious figures are LARGE, common people and servants SMALL** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ravanchhaya** | **Odisha** | Shadow | ⭐ **DEER SKIN; bold dramatic postures**; ⭐⭐ **NO JOINTS at all — making it the most complex**; uses **non-human puppets (trees, animals)**; *"the most theatrical"* |
| ⭐ **Tholu Bommalata** | **Andhra Pradesh** | Shadow | **Larger puppets, coloured on BOTH sides**; classical music; Puranic themes |
| ⭐ **Pavakoothu** | **Kerala** | Glove | ⭐ **18th c.**; ⭐⭐ **heavy KATHAKALI influence** — colourful headgear, feathers, face paint |
| ⭐ **Yampuri** | **Bihar** | Rod | ⭐⭐ **Carved from a SINGLE piece of wood, with NO JOINTS** |
| ⭐ **Putul Nachh** | **WB–Odisha–Assam** | Rod | ⭐ **3–4 ft, dressed as JATRA characters**; ⭐⭐ **three joints — neck and both shoulders**; each puppeteer works **one puppet by a rod tied at his WAIST**, moving behind a high curtain |

# 📚 PART E — THE CIRCUS ⭐⭐

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐ The story, and why it is examinable

| | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| **Birth** | ⭐ **The first Indian circus, c. 1880** *(per the English circus master **Philip Astley**)* |
| ⭐⭐ **The Great Indian Circus** | Founded by ⭐ **VISHNUPANT CHATRE**, an equestrian and singer, under the patronage of the **Raja of Kurduwadi**; ⭐ **first performance 20 March 1880**; toured **Ceylon and South-East Asia**; ⭐ **failed in North America** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Keeleri Kunhikannan** | ⭐⭐ **"THE FATHER OF INDIAN CIRCUS."** A **martial-art and gymnastics teacher** at Tellicherry, Malabar; ⭐⭐⭐ **opened a circus school at CHIRAKKARA near Tellicherry in 1901** — the epicentre of the Indian circus. ⭐⭐ **Hence KERALA is "the CRADLE of Indian Circus."** Died 1939; succeeded by **M. K. Raman** |

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Decline and remedies — *the Mains shape*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The headline statistic: ~300 Indian circuses in 1990 → just 30 by 2017.**

**Five causes:** **(1)** ⭐ **trade kept a guarded, hereditary secret**, blocking good managers · **(2)** ⭐⭐ **the Supreme Court's 2011 ban on employing children under 14** cut the training pool *(acrobatics was taught from childhood)* · **(3)** ⭐⭐ **the 1997 ban on using WILD ANIMALS for entertainment** removed a core attraction · **(4)** perceived as **dangerous**, so no performer dynasties formed · **(5)** competition from **world-class gymnastics and e-entertainment**.

**Five remedies:** **(1)** stricter **safety regulation** to improve the career's perception · **(2)** ⭐ **use circus to showcase DYING ARTS** · **(3)** **government protection and compensation** — ⭐⭐ **most artists retire by 40 and then work as manual labourers** · **(4)** the **Circus Academy at Thalassery (2010)** — ⭐ **itself near closure for want of trainees and funds** · **(5)** ⭐⭐⭐ **move circus from the Department of Sports and Youth Affairs to the MINISTRY OF CULTURE — which would also acknowledge it as an ART FORM.**

## 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | The line to take |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Causes of the decline of Sanskrit theatre"** *(a standing question)* | ⭐ **The four causes in §4 — but ORDER them causally**: the language ossified → the form ossified with it → dramatists migrated to poetry and to Prakrit/Pali → and the change of patronage under the Sultanate finished it. ⭐⭐ **Then the elegant closing move: it did not die, it MIGRATED — south, into Kutiyattam, which still performs by the Natya Shastra's rules a thousand years on** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Write a sentence each on five traditional theatre forms"** *(2011 — Bhand Pather, Swang, Maach, Bhaona, Mudiyettu, Dashavatar)* | ⭐⭐ **The single most efficiently answerable question in the book** — one crisp identifier per form from §5–7. **Note that the 2011 list mixes RITUAL and ENTERTAINMENT forms and spans J&K, Punjab, MP, Assam, Kerala and Konkan — the examiner is testing geographic spread** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Sanskrit play as an amalgamation of entertainment and religious tradition"** | ⭐ **Use the ritual progression (§3)**: *purva-raga* behind the curtain, the Sutradhara worshipping the deity in white, the two-storey stage splitting **celestial from terrestrial** — ⭐⭐ **the theatre is literally built as a cosmology**. Then the **Vidushaka**, who **satirises social norms in Prakrit while gods speak Sanskrit** — ⭐⭐ **entertainment and irreverence were built INTO the sacred form, not opposed to it** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Theatre as political instrument** | ⭐⭐⭐ **A strong, under-used arc**: the ⭐ **Dramatic Performances Act 1876** shows the British feared it; ***Nildarpan*** on indigo; **Jatra turning patriotic**; ⭐⭐ **IPTA (1943) as the Communist Party's cultural wing** performing on the Bengal Famine; and post-Independence folk theatre as **"dissemination of social wisdom."** ⭐ **Cross-link to [Cl.10 Ch.4](../../../01_NCERT/Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/04_The_Age_of_Industrialisation.md) and the freedom-struggle notes** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **"Scope for the puppet industry"** | **Low cost of production; unrestricted freedom of form and colour; educational uses.** ⭐ **Then the honest constraint — "lack of devoted audience and financial security"** — and the same **GI/market argument** as folk painting *(→ [Ch.2](02_Indian_Paintings.md))* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **"Circus as a marginal industry"** | ⭐ **Use the 300→30 statistic**, the five causes and five remedies in §11. ⭐⭐ **The sharpest single recommendation: shift it from Sports and Youth Affairs to the MINISTRY OF CULTURE — a jurisdictional argument that also settles the question of whether circus is sport or ART** |

## 🔁 Recap — in eight lines

1. ***Nataka*** comes from ***nata***, "dancer"; plays are **Lokadharmi** *(realistic)* or **Natyadharmi** *(stylised)*.
2. ⭐ **Ashvaghosha's *Sariputraprakarana* is the first Sanskrit drama; Sudraka's *Mricchakatika* introduced CONFLICT and the first ANTAGONIST; Kalidasa owns "desire vs duty."**
3. **Sanskrit plays ALWAYS ended happily, used curtains but NEVER masks, and staged the celestial above the terrestrial on a two-storey stage.**
4. **Sanskrit theatre declined for four reasons** — the drift to poetry, rigid orthodoxy, the language's own ossification, and the shift of patronage to music and dance — ⭐ **but survived as KUTIYATTAM, India's oldest continuing form.**
5. **Folk theatre moved through three stages**: devotional *(15th–16th c.)* → secular ballads → **post-1947 vehicle for social messaging**.
6. ⭐ **The forms that get asked**: **Tamasha** *(women play male roles)*, **Nautanki** *(in the Ain-i-Akbari)*, **Jatra** *(Chaitanya)*, **Bhavai** *(Gujarat, Nayaka as sutradhar)*, **Maach** *(Rangat Dohas)*, **Bhand Pather** *(secular, Muslim performers)*, **Yakshagana** *(Vijayanagara courts)*.
7. **Puppetry has four types** — **string** *(Kathputli has NO LEGS)*, **shadow** *(Ravanchhaya has NO JOINTS; Togalu sizes puppets by RANK)*, **glove** *(Pavakoothu)*, **rod** *(Yampuri, Putul Nachh)* — with **Bommalattam** combining rod and string and being the **largest and heaviest**.
8. **The circus was born in 1880 under Vishnupant Chatre**, made in Kerala by ⭐ **Keeleri Kunhikannan** *("the cradle of Indian circus")*, and has fallen from **300 companies in 1990 to 30 in 2017**.
