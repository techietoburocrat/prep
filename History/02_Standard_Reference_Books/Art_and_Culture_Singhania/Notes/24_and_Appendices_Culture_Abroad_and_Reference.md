# 📚 PART A — INDIAN CULTURE ABROAD ⭐⭐⭐⭐

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Who carried it, and how

| Carrier | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The Romas** | ⭐⭐ **Wanderers who travelled via Iran and Iraq to Turkey and on to Europe, where they became known as the GYPSIES** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ashoka** | ⭐ **Sent his SON AND DAUGHTER to SRI LANKA to spread Buddhism** |
| ⭐ **Traders** | **1st c. BC — Indian traders to INDONESIA and CAMBODIA in search of GOLD**; the **Kalinga** dynasty's ties with Sri Lanka; trade with Vietnam, Italy, China |
| **Travellers and monks** | Two-way cultural exchange |

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY] — a rhetorical claim, not a fact.** *The chapter opens: **"India never invaded any country in her last 10,000 years of history."*** ⭐⭐ **Do NOT reproduce this in an answer.** *It is contradicted by material in this very book — the **Chola naval expeditions** to Srivijaya, and the imperial expansion the whole of [Ch.1](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md) and your [TN Cl.XI](../../TN_State_Board_History/Class_11/Notes/01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md) notes describe. **Say "cultural influence spread largely through trade and religion rather than conquest" — defensible, and it makes the same point.***

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The ancient ports — **a strong match-the-following**

> ⭐⭐ **The two source texts: the *`PERIPLUS OF THE ERYTHRAEAN SEA`* and Ptolemy's *`GEOGRAPHIA`*.** ⭐ **Under the Mauryas the `NAVADHYAKSHA` — Superintendent of Shipping — ran the administration**; ⭐ **the coins of the last Satavahana, YAJNA SRI SATAKARNI, carry a SHIP.**

| Port | Region | Dynasty / date | ⭐ Known for |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Lothal** | **Gujarat** | **IVC, 2400 BC** | ⭐ **Copper, hardwood, ivory, pearls, carnelian and gold to MESOPOTAMIA; timber and lapis lazuli to SUMERIA** |
| ⭐ **Barygaza** *(Bharuch)* | **Gujarat** | 3rd c. BC on; **Roman trade under the Western Satraps** | **Spice and silk with Arab countries** |
| ⭐⭐ **Muziris** | **Malabar, Kerala** | 1st c. BC; **CHERA** | ⭐ **Mentioned in SANGAM literature.** Exported **spices, BERYL, pearls, diamonds, ivory, Chinese silk, Gangetic spikenard, tortoise shells** to Persia, Rome, Greece, Egypt |
| ⭐ **Korkai** | **Tamil Nadu** | Early **PANDYA** | ⭐⭐ **PEARL FISHERY**; in Sangam literature |
| ⭐ **Puhar** *(Kaveripoompattinam)* | **Tamil Nadu** | Early **CHOLA**, 400 BC–200 AD | ⭐ **Mentioned in the *SILAPPADIKARAM*** |
| ⭐⭐ **Poduke** *(Arikamedu)* | **Puducherry** | 2nd c. BC–8th c. AD | ⭐⭐ **The GREEK trading port for ROME** — gems, pearls, spices, silk out; ⭐ **WINE in** *(→ Roulette pottery, [Ch.3 §5](03_Indian_Handicrafts.md))* |
| **Barbarikon** | **Near Karachi** | Parthians, Scythians | Imported glass, silver and gold plate, wine; exported **turquoise, lapis lazuli, Seric skins, indigo** |
| **Sounagora** | **Bangladesh** | 450 BC, Mauryan | ⭐ **Mentioned by PTOLEMY** |
| ⭐ **Masulipatnam** | **Andhra Pradesh** | 3rd c. BC on | ⭐ **MUSLIN traded by the Greeks**; ⭐ **the principal seaport of the GOLCONDA kingdom, 15th–17th c.** |
| ⭐⭐ **Tamralipti** *(Tamluk)* | **West Bengal** | **MAURYAN** | ⭐⭐ **The EXIT POINT of the Mauryan trade route to the south and south-east.** ⭐ **Called `TAN-MO-LIH-TI` by Hiuen Tsang, who visited in 639 AD; FA-HIEN stayed two years.** ⭐ **The DUDHPANI rock inscription (8th c.) is its last record as a port** |
| ⭐ **Palur** | **Odisha** | 2nd c. AD, **KALINGA** | ⭐ **Point of voyage to South-East Asia and China**; pottery trade |

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ What travelled — mathematics, science, religion, architecture

| Export | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The number system** | ⭐ **By 500 BCE India had distinct symbols for 1–9**; ⭐⭐ **the Arabs adopted the notation and the DECIMAL system via maritime traders, and acknowledged the debt by calling mathematics `HINDISAT`** *(→ [Ch.16 §1](16_and_23_Science_Technology_and_Coins.md))*; Europe took it from there |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The binary system** | ⭐⭐ **First described by the Vedic scholar `PINGALA` in the *`CHANDAHSASTRA`*, the earliest Sanskrit treatise on PROSODY, 3rd/2nd c. BC** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The Fibonacci sequence** | ⭐⭐ **Appeared first in Indian mathematics as `MATRAMERU`, in Pingala's prosody** — methods later given by **Virahanka, Gopala and Hemachandra**, ⭐ **well before Fibonacci introduced it to Western Europe** |
| ⭐ **Religion** | Buddhism via Ashoka's mission; Hinduism through the sea-faring **Chola, Kalinga**, the **Champa** (Vietnam), **Khmer** (Indochina) and **Majapahit** (Java) polities |
| ⭐⭐ **Temple architecture** | ⭐ **Angkor Wat, Prambanan, Preah Vihear, Pashupatinath, Katas Raj, Munneswaram** *(→ the full table in [Ch.1 §25](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md))* |
| ⭐ **Martial arts** | ⭐ **Kalaripayattu → China via BODHIDHARMA**, claimed as the root of judo and karate; ⭐ **Silambam → Malaysia** *(→ [Ch.21](17_18_21_Calendars_Festivals_and_Martial_Arts.md))* |
| ⭐ **Universities** | **Nalanda's** influence on **Tibetan Buddhism**; scholars from Central and South-East Asia *(→ [Ch.1 §26](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md))* |

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Appendix 5 — Geographical Indications

⭐⭐ **What a GI is**: an indication tied to a **definite geographical territory**, certifying that the product has **specific qualities, traditional methods or a reputation due to its origin**, and ⭐ **restricting the name to registered authorised users**. ⭐ **The three tests: the goods must be produced/processed/prepared in that region, and have a special quality or reputation.**

⭐ **Eleven categories**: agricultural, manufactured, natural goods, handicraft, food, clothing and textile, handmade carpets, spices, sweets, security, ⭐ **and "holy food."**

| ⭐ Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **First GI in India** | ⭐ **DARJEELING TEA, 2004-05** |
| ⭐⭐ **The only Indian DISH with a GI** | ⭐ **HYDERABAD HALEEM** |
| ⭐ **Nilambur teak** *(Kerala)* | ⭐ **The world's OLDEST teak plantation; hosts the world's FIRST TEAK MUSEUM** |
| ⭐ **Uttarakhand tejpatta** | **The first indigenous product of that state to get a GI** |
| **Total registered** | ⚠️ **267 in the book (Aug 2017) → ~650+ today** |

⭐ **Named examples worth knowing**: Kanchipuram silk, Alphonso mango, Nagpur orange, Kolhapuri chappal, ⭐ **Banganapalle mango (AP)**, **Gobindobhog rice (WB)**, **Joha rice (Assam)**, **Bardhaman Sitabhog and Mihidana (WB)**, ⭐ **Blue Pottery of Jaipur** and **Molela clay work** *(Rajasthan)*, **Kashmiri hand-knotted carpet**, **Jamnagari bandhani**, **Mysore silk**, **Banaras metal repoussé** *(→ many of these are in [Ch.3](03_Indian_Handicrafts.md))*.

## 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | The line to take |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Indian culture abroad as SOFT POWER** *(a standing GS2 question)* | ⭐⭐⭐ **Structure it as three vectors, not a list.** **(1) RELIGION** — Ashoka's mission to Sri Lanka, Nalanda shaping Tibetan Buddhism, and **Sarnath's 2026 UNESCO inscription tying India to the global Buddhist circuit** *(→ [UNESCO](04_and_10_UNESCO_Heritage_Lists.md))*. **(2) TRADE** — the port network from Lothal to Tamralipti, and the temples it seeded at **Angkor, Prambanan, Preah Vihear**. **(3) KNOWLEDGE** — the numerals the Arabs called ***hindisat***, the decimal system, Pingala's binary and *matrameru*. ⭐⭐ **Then the modern instruments: YOGA (2016) and DEEPAVALI (2025) on the UNESCO list, the ICCR, the diaspora.** ⚠️ **But do NOT use the "never invaded anyone" line** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Maritime history and cultural diffusion** | ⭐ **The *Periplus* and *Geographia* as sources**; the **Navadhyaksha** as evidence of state capacity; ⭐ **the Satavahana ship-coin**; the archaeological confirmation at **Arikamedu**; and ⭐⭐ **the point that the same routes carried goods, gods and grammar** — spices out, wine in, and temple architecture across the Bay of Bengal |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **GI tags — protection or freezing?** | ⭐ **FOR**: secures provenance and price, blocks misappropriation, supports artisan incomes *(Madhubani, Cheriyal, Pochampalli, Channapatna)*. ⭐⭐ **AGAINST**: **only REGISTERED authorised users may use the name**, which can exclude unregistered artisans; it **codifies a "traditional method"** and so **freezes design innovation**; and enforcement is weak. ⭐⭐⭐ **Then the sharpest framing, which ties the whole book together: GI protects the PRODUCT, but what is dying is the PRACTICE — Tappa, Paitkar, the circus, the endangered Austro-Asiatic languages. A tag on the object does not keep the skill alive** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Delhi as a palimpsest** | ⭐ **Seven cities, each a dynasty's architectural signature** — read them against the Sultanate style sequence in [Ch.1 §16](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md): **Mameluke at Qila Rai Pithora's edge, Seljuk at Siri, batter at Tughlaqabad, and the Mughal climax at Shahjahanabad** — ⭐⭐ **then Lutyens' Neo-Roman eighth city** *(→ [Ch.1 §23](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md))*. **A city where you can walk the whole chapter** |

## 🔁 Recap — in six lines

1. **Culture travelled by TRADE, RELIGION and MIGRATION** — the Romas to Europe, Ashoka's children to Sri Lanka, traders to Indonesia and Cambodia.
2. ⭐ **The ports: Lothal (Mesopotamia), Barygaza (Rome), Muziris (Chera, Sangam), Korkai (pearls), Puhar (Silappadikaram), Poduke/Arikamedu (Greek-Roman), Masulipatnam (muslin, Golconda), Tamralipti (the Mauryan exit port), Palur (Kalinga).**
3. ⭐⭐ **Sources: the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* and Ptolemy's *Geographia*; the Mauryan `Navadhyaksha` ran shipping.**
4. ⭐⭐ **Mathematics went out and came back named: the Arabs called it `hindisat`. `Pingala` gave the BINARY system and the Fibonacci sequence as `matrameru` — in a treatise on POETIC METRE.**
5. ⭐ **Delhi's seven cities: Qila Rai Pithora → Siri → Tughlaqabad → Jahanpanah → Firozabad → Shergarh → Shahjahanabad.**
6. ⭐⭐ **GI: Darjeeling tea first (2004-05); Hyderabad Haleem the only dish; ~650+ registered today, not the book's 267.**
