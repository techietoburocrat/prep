# 📚 PART A — THE GRAMMAR *(open every dance answer with this)*

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The origin myth — and why it is examinable

> Brahma, asked by the gods for a *pastime*, combined elements of the four Vedas into a **fifth Veda — the `Natya Veda`**.

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The four-way derivation is a clean Prelims fact and a superb opening line for Mains:**

| Element | Meaning | From which Veda |
|---|---|---|
| **Pathya** | **Words** | **Rig Veda** |
| **Abhinaya** | **Gestures** | **Yajur Veda** |
| **Geet** | **Music** | **Sama Veda** |
| **Rasa** | **Emotions** | **Atharva Veda** |

- ⭐⭐⭐ **`Natya Shastra` — Bharata Muni** — the **first formal mention of dance**; compiled between **200 BCE and the 2nd century AD**. Covers techniques, postures, emotions, ornaments, **the stage and even the audience**.
- ⭐⭐ **Bharata calls dance the *"complete art"*** — it encompasses **music, sculpture, poetry and drama**. *(A ready-made Mains thesis sentence.)*
- ⭐ **Evidence of antiquity**: **Bhimbetka** community-dancing engravings; the **Harappan bronze Dancing Girl**; **Shiva's tandava** as the cycle of creation–preservation–destruction, answered by **Parvati's** feminine response.
- ⭐⭐ **`Guru-shishya parampara`** is stated as the **principal rule of the Natya Shastra** — true transfer of knowledge comes **only through a guru**, who passes on the ***sampradayas*** *(traditions)*.

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The two grammars — **memorise both, they unlock every question**

### From the *Natya Shastra* — the two **aspects**

| Aspect | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **`Lasya`** | **Grace** — *bhava, rasa, abhinaya*. The **FEMININE** aspect |
| ⭐ **`Tandava`** | **Rhythm and movement**. The **MASCULINE** aspect |

### From the *Abhinaya Darpan* — **Nandikeshwara's** three **elements**

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **`Nritta`** | **Pure dance steps** — rhythmic, **DEVOID of expression or mood** |
| ⭐⭐ **`Nritya`** | **Sentiment and emotion** — mime, expression, **mudras** |
| ⭐⭐ **`Natya`** | **Dramatic representation** — the **story** elaborated through the recital |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The single most confusable pair in the whole chapter is `Nritta` vs `Nritya`.** *One letter apart, opposite meanings.* **Nrit-TA = TAal = pure rhythm, no expression. Nrit-YA = bhava, all expression.** **Fix this now — it is exactly the kind of pair a Prelims setter builds a question around.**

> ⭐ **`Nayaka-Nayika Bhava`** *(also Nandikeshwara)*: **the eternal deity is the hero (Nayaka), the devotee-performer is the heroine (Nayika).**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The nine rasas

| Rasa | Emotion |
|---|---|
| **Shringara** | **Love** |
| **Roudra** | **Anger** |
| **Bibhatsa** | **Disgust** |
| **Veera** | **Heroism** |
| **Shaant** | **Peace, tranquillity** |
| **Haasya** | **Laughter, comedy** |
| **Karuna** | **Tragedy** |
| **Bhayanak** | **Horror** |
| **Adbhuta** | **Wonder** |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐ **A known distractor: `LAVANYA` is NOT one of the nine rasas** *(it means grace/beauty)*. Singhania's own practice set uses exactly this trap.

⭐ **Mudras**: emotions are emoted through **hand gestures + body postures**. ⭐⭐ **There are 108 fundamental mudras**, combined to depict a particular emotion.

# 📚 PART B — THE EIGHT CLASSICAL FORMS

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE COUNT QUESTION, ANSWERED PRECISELY:** **`Sangeet Natak Akademi` recognises EIGHT.** **The `Ministry of Culture` recognises NINE — the ninth being `CHHAU`.** *This discrepancy is real, it is stated in the book, and it is the reason you will see both numbers in different sources. **If a question says "as per Sangeet Natak Akademi", answer 8.***

| Form | State | ⭐ Element | Dominant aspect | The one-line identifier |
|---|---|:--:|---|---|
| **Bharatanatyam** | **Tamil Nadu** | 🔥 **FIRE** | **Both equal**, major emphasis on **mudras** | *"Fire dance"* — movements resemble a **dancing flame** |

| **Odissi** | **Odisha** | 💧 **WATER** | — | ⭐ *"**Mobile sculpture**"* — **tribhanga** + **chowk** |

| **Sattriya** | **Assam** | — | **Both** | **Male monks (*bhokots*)** in **Vaishnava *sattras*** |

> 🧠 ⭐⭐⭐ **Memory hook for the five elements — "BhaKKMO":** **Bha**ratanatyam=**fire**, **Ku**chipudi=**earth**, **Ka**thakali=**ether**, **Mo**hiniattam=**air**, **O**dissi=**water**. *(Kathak, Manipuri and Sattriya are **not** assigned elements — do not invent one.)*

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bharatanatyam — Tamil Nadu

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | From **Bharata Muni** + ***natyam*** *(dance, in Tamil)*. ⭐ Alternative reading: **BHA**va + **RA**ga + **TA**la |
| ⭐⭐ **Origin** | From ***Sadir*** — the **solo dance of the `devadasis`** of Tamil Nadu; hence also called ***Dashiattam*** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Revival** | Nearly died with the **decline of the devadasi system**; revived by ⭐ **E. Krishna Iyer**, a **freedom fighter**; ⭐ **Rukmini Devi Arundale** gave it **global recognition** |
| **Status** | ⭐ **The OLDEST of the classical forms** |

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The recital sequence — **the Tanjore Quartet's seven stages**

| # | Stage | What it is |
|:--:|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐ **Alarippu** | **Invocatory** — basic postures + rhythmic syllables, **seeking God's blessing** |
| 2 | ⭐ **Jatiswaram** | ⭐⭐ **The NRITTA component** — poses and movement, **no expression** |
| 3 | **Shabdam** | **Dramatic**, with **expressed words**; in praise of God |
| 4 | ⭐⭐ **Varnam** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The NRITYA component — THE most important part.** Dance + emotion, synchronised to **tala and raga** |
| 5 | **Padam** | Mastery of ***abhinaya*** of the spiritual message — *music light, dance emotional* |
| 6 | **Jawali** | **Short love-lyrics**, faster tempo |
| 7 | ⭐ **Thillana** | ⭐ **The CONCLUSION** — pure **Nritta**, exuberant, intricate rhythm |

> ⭐⭐ **The Tanjore Quartet** — **Chinniah, Ponniah, Vadivelu and Shivanandam** — four early-19th-century Thanjavur teachers who defined the recital. Under them Bharatanatyam was also called ***Tanjore natyam***.

**Other features:** ⭐ **`Kataka Mukha Hasta`** — principal mudra, **three fingers joined to symbolise "Om"** · **knees mostly bent, weight equal on both feet** · ⭐⭐ **`Ekcharya lasyam`** — **one dancer plays many roles**.

**Proponents:** Yamini Krishnamurthy, Lakshmi Viswanathan, Padma Subramaniam, Mrinalini Sarabhai, Mallika Sarabhai.

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Kuchipudi — Andhra Pradesh

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | From the village **Kuchelapuram / Kusselavapuri**; original performers the ***Kusselavas***, who travelled village to village |
| ⭐⭐ **Formaliser** | **Siddhendra Yogi (17th c.)** — systematised it and wrote ***Bhama Kalapam*** |
| **Vaishnava turn** | Became a **monopoly of male Brahmins**, performed in temples; **Bhagavata Purana** the central theme; dancers called ***Bhagavathalus***. Patronised by **Vijayanagara and Golconda** rulers |
| ⭐ **Modernisation** | **Lakshminarayan Shastry** introduced **solo recitals and female participation** |

**Features:** difficult footwork, usually a **team** performance · **secular themes** despite Puranic stories, **Shringara predominant** · ⭐⭐ **each character self-introduces with a `daaru`** *(a short dance-song composed for that character)* · all three of Nritta/Nritya/Natya · **`Sollakath`/`Patakshara`** = the Nritta part, **`Kavutvams`** = the Nritya part **with extensive acrobatics** · ⭐⭐ **the dancer may also SING** → a **dance-drama** · accompanied by **Carnatic music**, **violin and mridangam**, in **Telugu**.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The three famous solo items

| Item | What happens |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Tarangam** | **Dances with the feet on the EDGES of a BRASS PLATE**, balancing **a pot of water** on the head *(or a set of diyas)* |
| **Manduk Shabdam** | Tells the **story of a frog** |
| **Jala Chitra Nrityam** | ⭐ **Draws pictures on the floor with the TOES** while dancing |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PRELIMS 2012 ASKED EXACTLY THIS.** *"Dancing on the brass plate by keeping the feet on its edges is a feature of **Bharatnatyam** but Kuchipudi does not have such movements"* → **FALSE, it is reversed — the brass plate is KUCHIPUDI (Tarangam).** *And* *"Dancers occasionally speaking dialogues are found in Kuchipudi but not in Bharatnatyam"* → **TRUE.** **So the answer was (i) only.**

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY]** — *Singhania credits the 20th-c. revival to **"Balasaraswati and Ragini Devi."*** **Balasaraswati was a BHARATANATYAM dancer, not a Kuchipudi one** — this attribution is doubtful. **Safe names for Kuchipudi: Siddhendra Yogi (formaliser), Lakshminarayan Shastry (moderniser), Raja and Radha Reddy, Yamini Krishnamurthy, Indrani Rehman.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Kathakali — Kerala

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | ***Katha*** *(story)* + ***kali*** *(drama)* |
| ⭐⭐ **Ancestors** | Two temple dance-dramas under **feudal lords** — ⭐ ***Ramanattam*** *(Ramayana)* and ***Krishnattam*** *(Mahabharata)*. Closely related to ⭐ **Koodiyattam** *(Sanskrit drama)* and to **martial arts** |
| ⭐ **Revival** | Declined with the feudal order; revived in the **1930s** by the Malayali poet ⭐ **V. N. Menon**, under the patronage of **Mukunda Raja** |

**Features:** ⭐ **essentially an ALL-MALE troupe** · **minimal props** but **very elaborate facial make-up + headgear** · ⭐ **language is `Manipravalam` — a mixture of Malayalam and Sanskrit** · ⭐⭐ **the `Navarasas` conveyed through EYE and EYEBROW movement** — the crown jewel of the form · performed in **open-air theatres or temple premises**, lit by a **brass lamp** · ⭐ **drums `chhenda` and `maddala`** mark beginning and end at dawn · themes are the **eternal conflict of good and evil**; called the ⭐ ***"ballad of the east"***.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The make-up colour code — *a very clean Prelims table*

| Colour | Signifies |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Green** | **Nobility, divinity, virtue** |
| **Red patches beside the nose** | **Royalty** |
| **Black** | **Evil and wickedness** |
| **Yellow** | **Saints and women** |
| **Fully red-painted face** | **Evil** |
| **White beard** | **Higher consciousness and divinity** |

**Proponents:** Guru Kunchu Kurup, Gopi Nath, Kottakkal Sivaraman, Rita Ganguly.

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mohiniattam — Kerala

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | ***Mohini*** *(beautiful/enchanting woman)* + ***attam*** *(dance)* — **"Dance of the Enchantress"** |
| ⭐ **Development** | Developed by **Vadivelu** in the 19th c.; ⭐ **patronage of `Swathi Thirunal`, ruler of Travancore**; revived from obscurity by **V. N. Menon with Kalyani Amma** |

**Features:** ⭐⭐ **combines the GRACE of Bharatanatyam with the VIGOUR of Kathakali** · ⭐ **no thumping of footsteps — gentle footwork** · narrates the **feminine dance of Vishnu** · ⭐⭐ **LASYA dominant → mainly performed by women, solo** · ⭐ **costume: white and off-white with gold brocade; NO elaborate facial make-up**; leather strap with **ghungroo** · ⭐⭐ **`Atavakul`/`Atavus` = the collection of FORTY basic dance movements** · instruments: cymbals, veena, drums, flute.

**Proponents:** Sunanda Nair, Kalamandalam Kshemavathy, Madhuri Amma, Jayaprabha Menon.

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Odissi — Odisha

| | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Earliest evidence** | The **Udayagiri–Khandagiri caves** *(→ [Ch.1 §6](01_Architecture_Sculpture_and_Pottery.md))*; patronised by the **Jain king Kharavela** |
| **Name** | From ***Odra nritya***, mentioned in the **Natya Shastra** |
| ⭐⭐ **The three lineages** | **`Maharis`** *(temple women — original practitioners)* → after the Mahari system lapsed with Vaishnavism, **`Gotipuas`** *(**young BOYS dressed as females**)* → **`Nartala`**, the version continued **at the royal courts** |
| **Revival** | **Charles Fabri** and **Indrani Rehman**, mid-20th c. |

**Features:** ⭐⭐⭐ **`tribhanga`** — the **three-bend** posture, **innate to Odissi**; ⭐ **`chowk`** — hands spread out, **depicting masculinity** · **lower body largely static, the TORSO moves** · ⭐⭐ **known as *"MOBILE SCULPTURE"*** — the body creates **intricate geometrical shapes** · accompanied by ⭐ **HINDUSTANI** classical music *(**not** Carnatic — a discriminator)*, with **manjira, pakhawaj, sitar, flute** · lyrics from ⭐ **Jayadeva's *Gita Govinda*** · elaborate hairstyle and **silver** jewellery.

### ⭐⭐ The recital sequence

**Mangalacharan** *(a flower offered to mother earth)* → **Batu Nritya** *(the dance proper — tribhanga and chowk)* → **Pallavi** *(facial expression, the song represented)* → **Tharijham** *(pure dance before the close)* → ⭐ **the conclusion, of two types**: **`Moksha`** *(joyous movements signifying liberation)* or **`Trikhanda Majura`** *(the performer takes leave of **the gods, the audience and the stage**)*.

**Proponents:** Guru Pankaj Charan Das, Guru Kelucharan Mohapatra, Sonal Mansingh, Sharon Lowen *(USA)*, Myrla Barvie *(Argentina)*.

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Manipuri — Manipur

| | |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | Mythologically the **celestial dance of Shiva and Parvati** in the Manipur valleys with local **Gandharvas**; traced to the festival of ⭐ **`Lai Haraoba`** |
| **Vaishnava turn** | Gained prominence with **Vaishnavism in the 15th c.** → **Krishna** becomes the central theme |
| ⭐⭐ **Revival** | **Raja Bhagyachandra** (18th c.); then ⭐⭐ **Rabindranath Tagore brought it to limelight by introducing it at SANTINIKETAN** |

**Features:** ⭐⭐⭐ **emphasis on DEVOTION, NOT sensuality** — *the cleanest single discriminator in the chapter* · ⭐⭐ **faces covered with a thin VEIL; facial expression is of LESSER importance** — hand gestures and gentle footwork carry the meaning · both Tandava and Lasya, but ⭐ **Lasya emphasised** · women wear **unique long skirts** · ⭐⭐ **`Naga Bandha mudra`** — the body connected through curves in the shape of an **"8"** · ⭐ **Ras Leela** a recurring theme · ⭐⭐ **the drum `PUNG` is intrinsic**; also flute, **khartals** (wood clappers), dhols · compositions of **Jayadeva and Chandidas** · ⭐ **Thang-Ta and Sankirtana are influenced by it**.

**Proponents:** the **Jhaveri sisters** *(Nayana, Suverna, Ranjana, Darshana)*, Guru Bipin Singha.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Kathak — Uttar Pradesh *(read alongside [Cl.7 Ch.7](../../../01_NCERT/Class_07_Our_Pasts_II/Notes/07_The_Making_of_Regional_Cultures.md))*

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The four gharanas — **this settles an open question in your Cl.7 notes**

| Gharana | ⭐ Emphasis | Who |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Lucknow** | **Expression and grace** | Peaked under **Nawab Wajid Ali Shah** |
| ⭐ **Jaipur** | **Fluency, speed, long rhythmic patterns** | Initiated by **Bhanuji** |
| **Raigarh** | ⭐ **Percussion music** | **Raja Chakradhar Singh** |
| **Banaras** | ⭐ **Floorwork; symmetry** | **Janakiprasad** |

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY] — a name error in the book.** *Singhania writes **"Nawab Wajid Ali Khan."*** **The correct name is `WAJID ALI SHAH`, the last Nawab of Awadh** — and **your Cl.7 notes already have it right**. Use **Shah**.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The recital elements

**Ananda** *(entry)* → **Thaat** *(soft, varied movements)* → **Todas and Tukdas** *(short fast-rhythm pieces)* → ⭐⭐ **Jugalbandi** *(**the main attraction — a competitive play between the DANCER and the TABLA player**)* → ⭐ **Padhant** *(the dancer **recites** complicated *bols* and then demonstrates them)* → **Tarana** *(pure rhythm before the end; ≈ Bharatanatyam's thillana)* → **Kramalaya** *(the conclusion — intricate fast footwork)*. ⭐ **`Gat bhaav` = dance WITHOUT music or chanting**, used to outline mythological episodes.

⭐ **Music**: generally **dhrupad**; **taranas, thumris and ghazals** entered during the Mughal period. ⭐ **Signature technique: intricate footwork and PIROUETTES.**

**Proponents:** Birju Maharaj, Lacchu Maharaj, Sitara Devi, Damayanti Joshi.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Sattriya — Assam

| | |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Founder** | **Shankaradeva**, the Vaishnava saint, **15th c.** — *inspired by the **Bhakti movement*** |
| **Name** | From the Vaishnava monasteries — ⭐ **`sattras`** |
| **Antiquity claim** | Finds mention in the **Natya Shastra**; an amalgamation of Assamese forms, mainly ⭐ **Ojapali and Devadasi** |

**Features:** devotional, narrating **Vishnu** mythology · ⭐⭐ **performed in group by MALE MONKS — `bhokots`** — as part of daily ritual or at festivals · ⭐ **instruments: `khol` (drum), `manjira` (cymbals), flute**; songs are Shankaradeva's ⭐ **`Borgeets`** · strict rules for **hand gestures and footwork**; combines **Lasya and Tandava** · **costume**: men wear **dhoti and `paguri` (turban)**; women wear **`ghuri` and `chador` in Pat silk** · ⭐ two modern streams — **Gayan-Bhayanar Nach** and **Kharmanar Nach**.

> ⭐⭐ **`Ankia Naat`** — a **type of Sattriya**: a **musical play/drama**, originally written in ⭐ **`Brajavali`, an Assamese–Maithili mixed language**; also called ⭐ **`Bhaona`**; tells stories of **Krishna**.

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PRELIMS 2014 TRAP.** *Statement: "Sattriya is based on classical ragas and talas of devotional songs composed by **Tulsidas, Kabir and Mirabai**."* → ⭐⭐ **FALSE. The songs are `Borgeets`, composed by SHANKARADEVA.** *The other two statements — that it combines music, dance and drama, and that it is a centuries-old living tradition of Assamese Vaishnavites — are **true**. Answer: (i) and (ii) only.*

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in ten lines

1. **Dance descends from the `Natya Veda`** — **Pathya** (Rig), **Abhinaya** (Yajur), **Geet** (Sama), **Rasa** (Atharva) — first formalised in **Bharata's *Natya Shastra*** *(200 BCE–2nd c. AD)*.
2. **Two grammars govern everything**: **Lasya/Tandava** *(Natya Shastra)* and **Nritta/Nritya/Natya** *(Nandikeshwara's *Abhinaya Darpan*)*. ⭐ **Nritta = no expression; Nritya = all expression.**
3. **Nine rasas, 108 mudras** — and **Lavanya is not a rasa**.
4. ⭐ **EIGHT classical forms per the Sangeet Natak Akademi; NINE per the Ministry of Culture, which adds Chhau.**
5. **Five carry elements**: **Bharatanatyam–fire, Kuchipudi–earth, Kathakali–ether, Mohiniattam–air, Odissi–water.**
6. **Bharatanatyam** is the **oldest**, from the **devadasis' *sadir***, revived by **E. Krishna Iyer** and **Rukmini Devi Arundale**, with the **Tanjore Quartet's** seven-stage recital.
7. **Kuchipudi** lets the **dancer sing**, uses the **brass plate (*Tarangam*)** and **Carnatic music in Telugu**; **Kathakali** is **all-male**, colour-coded, in **Manipravalam**, the **"ballad of the east"**.
8. **Mohiniattam** is **lasya**, white-costumed, with **40 atavus**; **Odissi** is **"mobile sculpture"** built on **tribhanga** and **chowk**, sung to **Hindustani** music and the ***Gita Govinda***.
9. **Manipuri** uniquely **de-emphasises the face** *(veiled)* and **rejects sensuality for devotion**; **Sattriya** is danced by **male monks** in **sattras** to **Shankaradeva's Borgeets**.
10. **Kathak has FOUR gharanas** — **Lucknow** *(expression)*, **Jaipur** *(speed)*, **Raigarh** *(percussion)*, **Banaras** *(floorwork)* — which **resolves the two-vs-four tension your Cl.7 notes flagged**.
