# 📚 PART A — THE FOUR FAMILIES

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The shares — *a certain Prelims fact*

| Family | ⭐ Share | Where |
|---|:--:|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Indo-Aryan** | ⭐⭐ **~74%** | North, west, east — **the largest by far** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Dravidian** | ⭐⭐ **~25%** | South |
| ⭐ **Sino-Tibetan** | ⭐ **~0.6%** | Himalaya, N. Bihar, N. Bengal, Assam, the North-East |
| ⭐ **Austric** *(Austro-Asiatic)* | — | Central, eastern and north-eastern India |

⭐ **Two definitions to keep apart**: a **language family** groups languages descended from **a common ancestor predating recorded history**; a **dialect** is a **local form of a language**, and *several dialects can derive from one language*.

⭐⭐ **Two ancient Sanskrit labels worth knowing**: the **Sino-Tibetan** speakers were called ⭐ **`KIRATAS`**, and the **Austric** speakers ⭐ **`NISADAS`** — *and both groups are held to PREDATE the Aryans.*

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Indo-Aryan — the three-stage ladder

### ⭐⭐ Stage 1: **OLD Indo-Aryan** *(from c. 1500 BC)* → **SANSKRIT**

- ⭐ **Vedic Sanskrit** is the ancient form; **Upanishads, Puranas, Dharmasutras** all in Sanskrit.
- ⭐⭐ **Panini's *Ashtadhyayi* (c. 400 BC) is the OLDEST book of Sanskrit grammar.**
- ⭐⭐ **The "chaste"/classical form developed c. 300–200 BC**, refining Vedic Sanskrit.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The first EPIGRAPHIC evidence of (classical) Sanskrit is the `RUDRADAMAN` inscription at JUNAGADH.** *A frequently asked pairing.*
- ⭐ **Buddhist Sanskrit texts**: the **Mahavastu** *(Hinayana)*, the **Lalitavistara** *(the most sacred Mahayana text)*, **Ashvaghosha's *Buddhacharita***.
- ⭐ **Gupta period** = the age of **Mahakavyas (epics)** and **Khandakavyas (semi-epics)**, in an **ornate style**.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Stage 2: **MIDDLE Indo-Aryan** *(600 BC – 1000 AD)* → **PRAKRIT**

> ⭐⭐ ***Prakrit*** means **natural, original, casual** — ⭐⭐⭐ **it had NO strict rules and was the COMMON tongue, while Sanskrit was orthodox, fixed-rule and elite (chiefly Brahmin).** ⭐ **Writing in Prakrit is LATER than writing in Sanskrit.**

| Prakrit | ⭐ Key facts |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Pali** | Spoken in **Magadha**, popular **5th–1st c. BC**; texts written in **Brahmi**; ⭐⭐ **the *Tripitaka* is in Pali**; ⭐ **the lingua franca of THERAVADA Buddhism**. 🔴 **BUT: it is believed the Buddha HIMSELF did not speak Pali — he preached in ARDHA-MAGADHI** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Magadhi / Ardha-Magadhi** | ⭐ **The most important Prakrit.** ⭐⭐ **Buddha AND Mahavira probably spoke it**; the court language of some **Mahajanapadas and the MAURYAS**; ⭐ **Jain texts and Ashokan rock edicts** in it. ⭐⭐ **Evolved into BENGALI, ASSAMESE, ODIA, MAITHILI, BHOJPURI** |
| ⭐⭐ **Shauraseni** | ⭐ **Used to write DRAMAS**; predecessor of North Indian languages; ⭐ **Jain monks wrote mainly in it** — the Digambara ***Shatkhandagama*** |
| ⭐⭐ **Maharashtri** | Spoken to the **9th c.**; ⭐ **predecessor of MARATHI and KONKANI**; ⭐⭐ **the official language of the SATAVAHANAS**; works — ⭐ ***Gaha Kosha* by King HALA**, ***Gaudavaho*** by Vakpati |
| **Elu** | ⭐ **The ancient form of SINHALA** (Sri Lanka); similar to Pali |
| ⭐ **Paishachi** | ⭐⭐ **Also called `BHUTA-BHASA` — "dead language."** ⭐ **Gunadhya's *BRIHATKATHA*** is in it |

⭐⭐ **`APABHRAMSHA`** *(6th–7th c.)* — literally **"corrupt / non-grammatical."** An umbrella term for **dialects other than Sanskrit or Prakrit**; ⭐⭐ **the TRANSITION from Middle to Modern Indo-Aryan.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Bhamaha of Kashmir divided poetry into THREE — Sanskrit, Prakrit and Apabhramsha** — and **Dandin called Apabhramsha the dialect of the common folk.** ⭐ Sustained by **Jain monks**: **Pushpadanta's *Mahapurana***, **Dhanapala's *Bhavisayattakaha***.

### ⭐ Stage 3: **MODERN Indo-Aryan** *(after 1000 AD)*

**Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi, Punjabi, Rajasthani, Sindhi, Odia, Urdu.**

### 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Indo-Aryan vs Dravidian — the grammatical contrast

| | ⭐ **Dravidian** | ⭐ **Indo-Aryan** |
|---|---|---|
| **Root words** | Different | Different |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Grammar** | ⭐⭐ **`AGGLUTINATIVE`** — roots are **united with little or no change of form** | ⭐⭐ **`INFLECTED`** — the **word ending or spelling CHANGES** with its grammatical function |

# 📚 PART B — THE OFFICIAL AND CLASSICAL FRAMEWORKS

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Official language — *revise from Polity, verify here*

- ⭐⭐ **Article 343(1)**: *"The official language of the Union shall be **HINDI in DEVANAGARI script**."* English was to cease for official purposes **15 years** after commencement — i.e. **26 January 1965** — unless Parliament decided otherwise.
- ⭐⭐ **Protests by non-Hindi speakers** led to the ⭐⭐⭐ **OFFICIAL LANGUAGES ACT, 1963**, which made English a ⭐ **"subsidiary official language."**
- 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THERE IS NO NATIONAL LANGUAGE OF INDIA. Hindi is NOT a national language — neither the Constitution nor any Act defines one.**
- ⭐⭐ **States choose their own official language, and it NEED NOT be in the Eighth Schedule.** Examples: ⭐ **Tripura — Kokborok** *(Sino-Tibetan)* · ⭐ **Puducherry — FRENCH** · **Mizoram — Mizo** · ⭐⭐ **Nagaland and Meghalaya — ENGLISH**.
- 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐ **ENGLISH IS NOT among the 22 Eighth Schedule languages.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Classical language status

> ⭐⭐ **Introduced in 2004.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The four criteria:**

1. ⭐ **High antiquity of early texts / recorded history over 1500–2000 years**
2. ⭐ **A body of ancient literature/texts considered a valuable heritage by generations of speakers**
3. ⭐⭐ **The literary tradition must be ORIGINAL and NOT BORROWED from another speech community**
4. ⭐⭐ **The classical language and literature being DISTINCT from the modern — there may be a DISCONTINUITY between the classical language and its later forms**

### ✅ The current list of ELEVEN

| Year | Language |
|:--:|---|
| **2004** | ⭐ **Tamil** *(the first)* |
| **2005** | **Sanskrit** |
| **2008** | **Telugu · Kannada** |
| **2013** | **Malayalam** |
| **2014** | **Odia** |
| 🆕 **2024** *(3 October)* | ⭐⭐ **Marathi · Pali · Prakrit · Assamese · Bengali** |

⭐⭐ **The three benefits of classical status:** **(1)** ⭐ **two major international awards annually** for scholars of eminence · **(2)** ⭐ **a Centre of Excellence for Studies in Classical Languages** · **(3)** ⭐ **UGC-created professorial chairs**, at least in Central Universities.

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐ National Translation Mission, and two concepts

⭐ **NTM** — a Government of India scheme to make **knowledge texts accessible in Indian languages**, covering ⭐ **all 22 Eighth Schedule languages**, aimed at higher education. ⭐ **Objectives**: certify and train translators; maintain databases; run **Translator Education Programme** orientation courses; ⭐ **promote MACHINE-AIDED translation**; build dictionaries and thesauri; offer fellowships for **Natural Language Processing** research. ⭐⭐ **The stated ambition is to establish translation AS AN INDUSTRY in India.**

| Concept | ⭐ Definition |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Linguistic Diversity Index (LDI)** | ⭐ **The probability that two people chosen at random have DIFFERENT mother tongues** — ranges **0** *(everyone shares one mother tongue)* to **1** *(no two share one)*. ⭐⭐ **The Index of Linguistic Diversity (ILD) measures CHANGE over time — a global ILD of 0.8 means a 20% LOSS of diversity since 1970.** ⚠️ **Limits: it ignores second-language use, ignores how DIFFERENT the languages are, and the language/dialect line is "fluid and often political"** |
| ⭐ **Lingua franca** | A **bridge/trade/vehicular language** used between people not sharing a native tongue — usually **a THIRD language distinct from both** |

# 📚 PART C — THE SCRIPTS ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The organising claim: `BRAHMI IS THE MOTHER OF SCRIPTS`.** Devanagari, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Odia, Assamese/Bengali all descend from it, and **all surviving Indic scripts in South-East Asia** do too. ⭐ **The exceptions: URDU (from Arabic via Persian) and a few minor languages such as SANTHALI, which use independent scripts.**

```
   INDUS SCRIPT (undeciphered)
        |
   BRAHMI  ------------------- KHAROSTHI (sister script)
     |
     +-- GUPTA -- +-- NAGARI ----- DEVANAGARI
     |            +-- SHARADA ---- GURMUKHI
     |            +-- SIDDHAM ---- Tibetan
     +-- TAMIL-BRAHMI -- VATTELUTTU
     +-- KADAMBA ------- Kannada-Telugu
     +-- GRANTHA ------- Malayalam, Tigalari, Sinhala
```

| Script | ⭐ Key facts |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Indus** | ⭐⭐ **Inscriptions extremely short; NOT CLEAR whether it records a language at all** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Brahmi** | ⭐ **The oldest writing system of the subcontinent**; possibly from a **Semitic script or the Indus script**. ⭐⭐ **Best known from Ashoka's rock edicts, 250–232 BCE**; ⭐⭐⭐ **deciphered by JAMES PRINSEP in 1837**. ⭐ **Written LEFT to RIGHT**; ⭐⭐ **an `ABUGIDA` — each letter is a consonant, vowels written as obligatory diacritics (`matras`)** |
| ⭐⭐ **Gupta** | Used for **Sanskrit**; ⭐ **gave rise to NAGARI, SHARADA and SIDDHAM**, and through them to **Devanagari, Gurmukhi, Assamese, Bengali and TIBETAN**. ⭐ **All Brahmi descendants are collectively "Brahmic scripts"** |
| ⭐⭐ **Kharosthi** | ⭐ **3rd c. BC – 3rd c. AD, ancient GANDHARA** *(Afghanistan/Pakistan)*, for **Gandhari Prakrit and Sanskrit**; ⭐⭐ **a SISTER script of Brahmi, not a descendant**; also deciphered by **Prinsep**. ⭐⭐ **Written RIGHT to LEFT** *(mostly)*; ⭐ **has numerals resembling ROMAN numerals** |
| ⭐ **Vatteluttu** | An abugida of **South India**, developed from **Tamil-Brahmi** |
| ⭐ **Kadamba** | ⭐⭐ **Marks the birth of a dedicated script for KANNADA**; under the **Kadambas, 4th–6th c.**; became the **Kannada-Telugu** script |
| ⭐ **Grantha** | **6th–20th c.**, Tamil Nadu and Kerala, to write **Sanskrit** and ⭐ **Manipravalam**; ⭐⭐ **MALAYALAM script is a direct descendant**, as are **Tigalari and Sinhala** |
| ⭐ **Sharada** | ~**8th c.**; for **Sanskrit and Kashmiri**; ⭐ **now used only ceremonially by Kashmiri Pandits** |
| ⭐ **Gurmukhi** | ⭐⭐ **Developed from SHARADA; standardised in the 16th c. by GURU ANGAD**; ⭐ **the whole Guru Granth Sahib is in it** |
| ⭐⭐ **Devanagari** | ⭐ **Used for over 120 languages** — Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, **Pali**, Konkani, Bodo, Sindhi, Maithili, and classical Sanskrit |
| ⭐ **Modi** | ⭐⭐ **The old official script of MARATHI**, until Devanagari's *Balbodh* style replaced it in the 20th c. |
| ⭐ **Urdu** | ⭐ **Right-to-left**, a modification of the **Persian** alphabet *(itself from Arabic)*, from the **13th c.**; tied to the **Nastaliq** style; ⭐⭐ **its extended form is `SHAHMUKHI`**, used for Punjabi and Saraiki |

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY] — a typo worth catching.** *The book writes **"Brahmi is also an abugida like Brahmi"** in the Kharosthi section. **It means KHAROSTHI is an abugida like Brahmi.***

## 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | The line to take |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Sanskrit is the mother of all Indian languages" — critically examine** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The word "critically" is the whole question, and the honest answer is NO.** **Concede**: Sanskrit is the fount of the Indo-Aryan family (~74%), supplied vocabulary across the subcontinent, and *"transcended the barriers of region."* **Then refute**: ⭐⭐ **the DRAVIDIAN family (~25%) has an independent proto-ancestor and an AGGLUTINATIVE grammar unrelated to Sanskrit's INFLECTED structure** — Tamil's literary tradition is independently ancient; ⭐ **Sino-Tibetan and Austric speakers (Kiratas, Nisadas) PREDATE the Aryans**; ⭐ and **classical status itself requires an ORIGINAL, non-borrowed tradition — which the Government has now certified for Tamil, Kannada, Telugu, Malayalam and Odia.** ⭐⭐ **Conclude: Sanskrit is the mother of ONE family and the enricher of the rest** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Criteria for classical language status"** | The four in §6, then ⭐⭐ **the live controversy**: Singhania (2018) records criticism that **Pali was excluded despite meeting every criterion** — ⭐⭐⭐ **and Pali was duly added in October 2024, along with Prakrit, Marathi, Assamese and Bengali.** *Showing you know both the critique and its resolution is what separates the answer* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Language and national integration** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Build this from [Cl.12 Th.12](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/12_Theme12_Framing_the_Constitution.md), not from this chapter** — Gandhi's composite **Hindustani** as a rule of **addition**, its splitting into Sanskritised Hindi and Persianised Urdu as communal politics deepened, and the Constituent Assembly's compromise. ⭐ **Then this chapter's evidence that the compromise held**: the 1963 Act, **no national language**, states free to adopt **non-Schedule** languages *(Kokborok, French, Mizo, English)*. ⭐⭐ **The argument: India managed language by REFUSING to settle it** |

## 🔁 Recap — in eight lines

1. **Four families: Indo-Aryan ~74%, Dravidian ~25%, Sino-Tibetan ~0.6%, Austric** — the last two called **Kiratas** and **Nisadas** and **predating the Aryans**.
2. **Indo-Aryan runs Old (Sanskrit, 1500 BC) → Middle (Prakrit, 600 BC–1000 AD) → Modern (after 1000 AD)**, with **Apabhramsha** the hinge.
3. **Prakrits**: **Pali** *(Tripitaka, Theravada)*, **Ardha-Magadhi** *(Buddha and Mahavira spoke it; became Bengali/Assamese/Odia)*, **Shauraseni** *(dramas, Jain)*, **Maharashtri** *(Satavahana official; → Marathi/Konkani)*.
4. **Dravidian = 21 languages in 3 groups**; ⭐ **Telugu largest, Tamil oldest, Malayalam smallest and youngest, Brahui in Baluchistan.**
5. ⭐ **Dravidian grammar is AGGLUTINATIVE; Indo-Aryan is INFLECTED.**
6. ⭐⭐ **Eighth Schedule = 22 (since 2003). Classical languages = 11 (since Oct 2024). PALI and PRAKRIT are classical but NOT scheduled. There is NO national language.**
7. **Classical status needs 1500–2000 years of antiquity, a valued literature, an ORIGINAL tradition, and distinctness from the modern form.**
8. ⭐ **Brahmi is the mother of scripts** — deciphered by **Prinsep (1837)**; **Kharosthi is its right-to-left SISTER**; **Gupta → Nagari/Sharada/Siddham → Devanagari, Gurmukhi, Tibetan**.
