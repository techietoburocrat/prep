## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The lost-wax technique — *cire perdue*

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This is a standing Prelims fact and a total gap in your notes.** The method, in the book's own sequence:

```
   1. A WAX figure is modelled.
   2. It is coated in WET CLAY and left to dry.
   3. The clay-coated figure is HEATED -- the wax inside melts.
   4. The molten wax is POURED OUT through a tiny hole.
   5. LIQUID METAL is poured into the hollow mould.
   6. Metal cools -> the CLAY COAT IS BROKEN OFF.
   -> A metal figure of exactly the wax figure's shape.

   ** STILL PRACTISED IN INDIA TODAY. **
```

| Example | Note |
|---|---|
| **Bronze Dancing Girl, Mohenjo-daro** | ⚠️`[VERIFY]` Singhania calls it *"the world's oldest bronze sculpture"* — **a contested superlative; do not assert it in Mains.** Safe facts: **~4 inches**, stands in **`tribhanga`** *(three-bend)* posture, **right hand on hip**, bangles on **left** arm, amulet + bracelet on **right** |
| **Bronze bull, Kalibangan** | — |

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Seals — the detail Cl.12 does not give

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Shapes** | Mostly **square**; also **triangular, rectangular, circular** |
| **Materials** | ⭐ **Steatite** *(commonest — a soft river-bed stone)*; also **agate, chert, copper, faience, terracotta**; rarely **gold and ivory** |
| **Script direction** | Mostly **right to left** — but ⭐ **bi-directional (boustrophedon)** examples exist: right-to-left on one line, left-to-right on the next |
| **Animal motifs** | unicorn, humped bull, rhinoceros, tiger, elephant, buffalo, bison, goat, markhor, ibex, crocodile |
| 🔴 **THE TRAP** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NO EVIDENCE OF THE COW ON ANY SEAL.** *This was **Prelims 2001** almost verbatim* |
| **Uses** | Commercial/trade *(found in Mesopotamia and Lothal)* · **amulets** *(some found with holes, on dead bodies — possibly identification)* · possibly **educational** *(mathematical images)* · **swastika-like** designs found |

> **Pashupati seal** *(steatite, Mohenjo-daro)*: a cross-legged figure in a **three-horned headgear**. ⭐ **Get the animal positions right** — **elephant + tiger on the LEFT**, **rhinoceros + buffalo on the RIGHT**, **two antelopes BELOW the seat.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Pottery, terracotta, ornaments

| Item | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Pottery** | Two kinds — **plain** and **painted**. ⭐ Painted = **"Red and Black pottery"** *(red background, glossy black designs)*. Themes: trees, birds, animals, geometric patterns. Mostly **wheel-made**; polychrome rare |
| ⭐⭐ **Three uses** | **(1)** plain → household storage · **(2)** **miniature vessels** *(under half an inch)* → decorative · **(3)** ⭐ **perforated pots** *(large hole in base, small holes on sides)* → **probably for straining liquor** |
| **Terracotta** | **Fire-baked clay**, made by the **pinching method**; **cruder** than the bronzes; concentrated at **Gujarat sites and Kalibangan**. Examples: **Mother Goddess**, mask of horned deity |
| **Ornaments** | Both sexes wore necklaces, fillets, armlets, finger rings. ⭐ **Girdles, earrings and anklets — WOMEN ONLY.** Bead factories at ⭐ **Chanhudaro and Lothal** |
| **Stone sculpture** | **Bearded priest** — shawl with **trefoil** pattern, elongated half-closed eyes, armlet on right hand, woven fillet across head · **Red sandstone male torso** — frontal posture, socket holes in neck and shoulders for attaching head and arms |

# 📚 PART B — MAURYAN ART: *the Court/Popular split*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The organising frame

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This two-way classification is Singhania's, and it is a genuinely useful answer skeleton** — it lets you structure a Mains answer on Mauryan art in five seconds.

```
   MAURYAN ART
   |
   +-- COURT ART (state patronage)      +-- POPULAR ART (individual effort)
   |     * Palaces (Pataliputra,        |     * Cave architecture
   |       Kumrahar)                    |       (Barabar, Nagarjuni)
   |     * PILLARS                      |     * Sculpture (Yaksha/Yakshi)
   |     * STUPAS                       |     * Pottery (NBPW)
```

| Court art | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Palaces** | Chandragupta's palace **inspired by Achaemenid Persepolis**; **wood** the principal material; **Megasthenes** called it *"one of the greatest creations of mankind"*. Ashoka's Kumrahar palace — **three-storey wooden**, high central pillar |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Pillars** | Average **40 ft**, usually **Chunar sandstone**, **four parts**: **shaft** *(monolithic base)* → **capital** *(lotus- or bell-shaped)* → **abacus** *(circular/rectangular)* → **animal figure**. ⭐ Bell capitals + the **lustrous polish** show **Iranian influence** |
| **Stupas** | ⭐ **A VEDIC tradition popularised by Buddhists**, not a Buddhist invention. **~84,000** raised under Ashoka. **Core: unburnt brick; outer surface: burnt brick + thick plaster.** **Sanchi** most famous; ⭐ **Piprahwa (UP) is the OLDEST** |

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Ashokan vs Achaemenid pillars — a two-row table that answers a whole question

| Basis | **Ashokan** | **Achaemenid** |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition** | ⭐ **Monolithic** — shaft cut from a **single** piece of stone *(Chunar sandstone)* | Shaft **assembled from several pieces** of sandstone cemented together |
| **Location** | **Independently erected** by royal direction | Generally **attached to state buildings** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The National Emblem — the Sarnath abacus decoded

The **abacus + animals** of the Sarnath pillar = India's **State Emblem**. Four animals, four directions, and each carries a **Buddha-biography meaning**:

| Animal | Direction | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Horse** | **West** | **Kanthaka** — the horse on which Buddha left princely life |
| **Bull** | **East** | **Taurus** — the zodiac month of Buddha's birth |
| **Elephant** | **South** | **Queen Maya's dream** of a white elephant entering her womb |
| **Lion** | **North** | **Attainment of enlightenment** |

> ⭐ **`Satyameva Jayate`** — from the **Mundaka Upanishad**, in **Devanagari**, below the abacus. Crowned by the **Dharma Chakra**. The **lions** = Buddha spreading Dhamma in all directions; built to commemorate the **first sermon** (*Dhammachakraparivartana*).

| Popular art | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Caves** | Used as **viharas** by Jain and Buddhist monks. ⭐ **Earliest caves were for the ĀJĪVIKA sect**, only later Buddhist. Marked by **highly polished interior walls** + decorative gateways. **Barabar and Nagarjuni, Bihar** |
| **Sculpture** | **Yaksha and Yakshi** — objects of worship across **all three** religions. ⭐ **Earliest mention of yakshi is in *Silappadikaram*, a TAMIL text.** Every **Jain tirthankara** is associated with a yakshi |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Pottery** | **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)** — black paint, highly lustrous, a **luxury item**; *"the highest level of pottery"* |

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY] — an internal contradiction in the book you should know about.** *Singhania's main text says Barabar/Nagarjuni were made in the time of **Dasharatha, GRANDSON of Ashoka**. Its own **answer key** to a 2013 PYQ says *"Ashoka and his **SON** Dasaratha."** **The main text is right** — Dasharatha Maurya was Ashoka's **grandson**. *(Conventional attribution: **Barabar** dedicated under **Ashoka**, **Nagarjuni** under **Dasharatha**.)*

# 📚 PART C — POST-MAURYAN: the three sculpture schools

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Gandhara vs Mathura vs Amaravati — **the highest-value table in the chapter**

> 🔴 **Mains has asked this FOUR times: 2014 (Gandhara/Romans/Greeks), 2007, 2000 (Gandhara), 1993 (Mathura).** Learn it as a table, answer it as a table.

| Basis | **Gandhara** | **Mathura** | **Amaravati** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **External influence** | ⭐ **Heavy Greek/Hellenistic** → *"Indo-Greek"* / **Greco-Indian** art | **Indigenous**, no external influence | **Indigenous**, no external influence |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Material** | **Bluish-grey sandstone** *(early)*; **mud and stucco** *(later)* | ⭐ **Spotted RED sandstone** | ⭐ **White marble** |
| **Religion** | Mainly **Buddhist**, on a Greco-Roman pantheon | ⭐ **All three** — Hindu, Jain, Buddhist | Mainly **Buddhist** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Patron** | **Kushanas** | **Kushanas** | ⭐ **Satavahanas** |
| **Region** | **North-West Frontier**, modern Kandahar/Peshawar | **Mathura, Sonkh, Kankalitila** *(⭐ Kankalitila = Jain sculptures)* | **Krishna–Godavari lower valley**, Amaravati and **Nagarjunakonda** |
| **Buddha's treatment** | **Spiritual state**, **wavy hair**, fewer ornaments, seated as a **yogi**, **half-closed meditative eyes**, **protuberance (ushnisha)** on head | **Delighted, smiling**; **muscular** body, **tight dress**; ⭐ **face and head SHAVEN**; **padmasana**, various mudras; same protuberance | ⭐ **Narrative art, not portraiture** — less emphasis on individual features; depicts **Jataka tales** and Buddha's life, in **human and animal** form |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The one-line discriminators**: **Gandhara = grey stone + Greek + wavy hair.** **Mathura = red spotted sandstone + shaven head + all three religions.** **Amaravati = white marble + Satavahana + narrative + `tribhanga`.**

**Two more Mathura details worth having:** modelled on the earlier **Mauryan Yaksha** images; uses **symbolism for Hindu gods via their *ayudhas*** *(Shiva as **linga / mukhalinga**)*; Buddha's **halo is LARGER than Gandhara's and decorated with geometric patterns**; flanked by **Padmapani** *(lotus)* and **Vajrapani** *(thunderbolt)*.

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY] — a dating error in the book.** *Singhania writes the Mathura School flourished **"between 1st and 3rd centuries B.C."** — **this is wrong.** Kushana patronage puts it in the **1st–3rd centuries CE/AD**. Almost certainly a typo; **use CE**.*

### ⭐⭐ Greek vs Roman, since Gandhara fuses both

| Greek | Roman |
|---|---|
| **Idealistic** — muscular gods showing strength and beauty; marble; Parthenon mythological figures | **Realistic** — art for **ornamentation and decoration**; depicts **real people and historical events**; used **concrete**; famous for **murals** |

*(⭐ This is precisely the distinction the **2014 Mains** question — *"Gandhara sculpture owed as much to the Romans as to the Greeks"* — wants you to draw.)*

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Rock-cut caves and stupas after the Mauryas

- ⭐ **The CHAITYA hall is a POST-Mauryan development.** *Viharas came first (Mauryan); chaityas — quadrangular, flat-roofed prayer halls — were developed in this period.* **Karle chaitya** is the type-example.
- ⭐ **The Shungas introduced the *torana*** — the decorated gateway — showing **Hellenistic influence**. Stone increasingly replaced wood and brick. **Bharhut**, and the **Sanchi toranas**.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Udayagiri & Khandagiri, Odisha — *a genuine gap*

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who / when** | Kalinga king **Kharavela**, **1st–2nd c. BC**, near Bhubaneswar |
| **Faith** | **JAIN** *(monks' residences)* |
| **Count** | ⭐ **18 caves at Udayagiri, 15 at Khandagiri** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Hathigumpha inscription** | In **Udayagiri**, **Brahmi script, Prakrit language, 17 lines**; opens with the ⭐ **Jain Namokar Mantra**; the **main source for Kharavela** |
| **Ranigumpha** | **Double-storeyed**, finely sculpted |

> ⚠️ **Do not confuse** these with the **Udayagiri caves at Vidisha, Madhya Pradesh** *(5th c. AD, Chandragupta II, **Hindu**, famous **Varaha** panel)*. **Two different Udayagiris — and UPSC knows it.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The ten mudras — *a clean Prelims list you do not have anywhere*

| # | Mudra | Meaning | The give-away gesture |
|:--:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bhumisparsha** | *"Calling the Earth to Witness"* — **the moment of enlightenment** | Left hand palm-up in lap, ⭐ **right hand touching the earth**. Associated with the **blue Buddha Akshobhya** |
| 2 | **Dhyana** *(Samadhi/Yoga)* | **Meditation**; spiritual perfection | **Both hands in lap**, back of right resting on palm of left; thumbs touching to form a **mystic triangle**. Used in the **final meditation under the Bodhi tree** |

| 6 | **Anjali** | **Greeting, devotion, adoration** *(namaste)* | Both palms joined vertically at chest. ⭐ **TRAP: true Buddhas do NOT make this gesture — it is for BODHISATTVAS** |

| 9 | **Karana** | **Warding off evil** | Palm forward, ⭐ **thumb presses the two middle fingers down; index and little finger raised** |
| 10 | **Vajra** | **Knowledge** | ⭐ **Erect forefinger of the LEFT hand held in the FIST of the right.** Better known in **Korea and Japan** |

# 📚 PART D — THE GUPTA AGE AND THE BIRTH OF THE TEMPLE

> ⭐ *"Golden period of Indian architecture."* Three principal deities by region: **Vishnu — North/Central**, **Shiva — South**, **Shakti — East + Malabar/South-west**.

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The five stages of temple evolution — **learn this as a ladder**

| Stage | What changed | ⭐ Example |
|:--:|---|---|
| **1** | **Flat roof**, **square** plan, portico on **shallow pillars**, **low platform** | **Temple No. 17, Sanchi** |
| **2** | Same, but **higher/upraised platforms**; some **two-storeyed**; ⭐ **covered ambulatory (*pradakshina*) added around the garbhagriha** | **Parvati temple, Nachna Kuthara (MP)** |
| **3** | ⭐⭐⭐ **SHIKHARA replaces the flat roof** *(still low, near-square, curvilinear)*; ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **`PANCHAYATAN` introduced** | **Dashavatara temple, Deogarh (UP)**; **Durga temple, Aihole** |
| **4** | Main shrine becomes **more rectangular** | **Ter temple, Sholapur** |
| **5** | ⭐ **CIRCULAR temples** with shallow rectangular projections | **Maniyar Math, Rajgir** |

### 🔴⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ `PANCHAYATAN` — the definition to memorise verbatim

> **A style of temple construction in which the principal deity's shrine is surrounded by FOUR SUBSIDIARY SHRINES**, placed opposite each other on either side of the *mandapa*, so that the **ground plan takes a crucified/cruciform shape**. The main temple is **square with an elongated *mandapa*** in front.

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This was a straight Prelims question in 2014**: *"With reference to the cultural history of India, the term Panchayatan refers to?"* → **(c) A style of temple construction.** **It is in none of your other notes. This single term may be the highest return-per-word in the file.**

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐ Caves and sculpture under the Guptas

- ⭐ **Cave development stayed constant — the ADDITION was MURAL PAINTING.** *(Ajanta, Ellora.)*
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Ajanta cave arithmetic** *(commonly asked)*: **29 caves = 25 viharas + 4 chaityas**; **5 Hinayana-phase + 24 Mahayana-phase**; **200 BC – 650 AD**; **Vakataka** patronage *(**Harishena**)*; ⭐ **no blue pigment anywhere**; outlines drawn in **red** first. Referenced by **Fa Hien and Hiuen Tsang**.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Ellora cave numbering** *(a favourite match-the-following)*: **34 caves — 1–12 Buddhist, 13–29 Hindu, 30–34 Jain (Digambara)**; **5th–11th c. AD**; carved by guilds from **Vidarbha, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu**.

| Ellora cave | What it is |
|:--:|---|

# 📚 PART E — NAGARA AND ITS THREE SUB-SCHOOLS

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Nagara — the features your spine does not list

> ✅ *You already have the **Nagara vs Dravida vs Vesara** basics and the **shikhara-vs-vimana trap** from [Cl.6 Ch.10](../../../01_NCERT/Class_06_Our_Pasts_I/Notes/10_Buildings_Paintings_and_Books.md). **These are the additions.***

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Plan** | Generally **panchayatan**; **mandapas** in front of the principal shrine |
| ⭐⭐ **River goddesses** | Images of **Ganga and Yamuna** placed **outside the garbhagriha** |
| 🔴⭐⭐⭐ **No water tanks** | **NO tanks or reservoirs in the premises** — *this is the cleanest Nagara/Dravida discriminator after the shikhara* |
| **Platform / portico** | Built on **upraised platforms**; **pillared** approach |
| **Crowning** | Shikhara ends in a horizontal fluted disc — the **`amalaka`** — topped by a spherical **`kalash`** |
| **Ambulatory** | **Covered** pradakshina path |
| **Boundary** | ⭐ Generally **NO elaborate boundary walls or gateways** |

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three shikhara types

| Type | Shape |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Latina** *(rekha-prasada)* | **Square base**, walls **curve inward to a point** at the top |
| ⭐ **Phamsana** | **Broader base, SHORTER** than Latina; slopes upward in a **straight line** |
| ⭐ **Valabhi** | **Rectangular base**, roof rises into **vaulted chambers** — *"**wagon-vaulted**"* |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The ratha series — vertical wall divisions

**Triratha** *(3 planes)* → **pancharatha** *(5)* → **saptaratha** *(7)* → **navaratha** *(9)*. *The planes serve as panels for **narrative sculpture**.*

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three Nagara sub-schools

| | **1. ODISHA** *(Kalinga)* | **2. KHAJURAHO** *(Chandela)* | **3. SOLANKI** *(Maru-Gurjara)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where / who** | Kalinga empire | **Central India, Chandela rulers** | **Gujarat + Rajasthan, Solanki rulers** |
| ⭐⭐ **Wall decoration** | **Exterior lavishly carved, INTERIOR PLAIN** | ⭐ **BOTH interior and exterior lavishly carved** | ⭐ **Walls DEVOID of carving** |
| **Signature feature** | ⭐ **No pillars in the porch — IRON GIRDERS support the roof** | ⭐ **Erotic sculpture**, drawn from **Vatsyayana's *Kamasutra*** | ⭐⭐ **STEP-TANK (`surya-kund`)** near the temple, its steps lined with small temples |
| **Local vocabulary** | ⭐ shikhara = **`rekhadeul`** *(near-vertical, then curving in sharply)*; mandapa = **`jagmohan`** | Three chambers: **garbhagriha, mandapa, ardha-mandapa**; some have **`antarala`** *(vestibule)* | Garbhagriha connected to mandapa **internally AND externally**; **`torana`** arched gateways |
| **Materials** | — | **Sandstone** | **Sandstone, black basalt, soft marble** |
| 🔴 **Odd one out** | ⭐ **Surrounded by a BOUNDARY WALL — like Dravida, unlike normal Nagara** | **North- or east-facing**; **high platforms**; subsidiary shrines also get **rekha-prasada shikharas** → *"mountain range" effect*; **Hindu AND Jain** | ⭐ Mostly **east-facing**, aligned so that at the **equinoxes the sun falls directly into the central shrine**; **wooden carvings** |
| **Examples** | **Konark Sun Temple**, **Jagannath (Puri)**, **Lingaraja (Bhubaneswar)** | **Kandariya Mahadeva**, **Lakshmana**, **Vishwanath** | **Modhera Sun Temple** *(1026–27, **Bhima I**)* |

# 📚 PART F — SOUTH INDIA: PALLAVA → CHOLA → THE REST

## 1️⃣2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The four Pallava stages — *a chronology your spine lacks*

> ✅ *[TN Cl.XI Unit 9](../../TN_State_Board_History/Class_11/Notes/03_South_Indian_Culture_and_Temples.md) already gives you Mamallapuram and the Pallava rock-cut story in depth. **What it does not give is this four-name ladder**, which converts the same material into an instantly writable answer.*

| # | Group | Ruler | ⭐ The architectural move |
|:--:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Mahendra** | **Mahendravarman** | **Rock-cut** temples. ⭐⭐ **TRAP: here "mandapa" means the WHOLE TEMPLE**, not just the assembly hall as in Nagara |
| **2** | **Narasimha** | **Narasimhavarman** | Rock-cut temples **decorated with intricate sculpture**; mandapas divided into separate **`rathas`**. ⭐ Largest = **Dharmaraja ratha**, smallest = **Draupadi ratha**. **The Dravida style descends from the Dharmaraja ratha** |
| **3** | **Rajsimha** | **Rajsimhavarman** | ⭐⭐ **Real STRUCTURAL temples replace rock-cut** — **Shore Temple (Mahabalipuram)**, **Kailashnath (Kanchipuram)** |
| **4** | **Nandivarman** | **Nandivarman** | **Smaller** temples; features essentially Dravidian |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Mahabalipuram — **UNESCO 1984, "Group of Monuments at Mahabalipuram"**, four components

1. **Pancha Rathas** *(Pandava Rathas)* — ⭐ **the earliest rock-cut temples in India**, ~7th c. AD: **Dharmaraja** *(largest)*, **Bhima**, **Arjuna**, **Nakula-Sahadeva**, **Draupadi**
2. **Rock-cut caves** — **Varaha**, **Krishna**, **Panchapandava**, ⭐ **Mahishasuramardini mandapa** *(Durga slaying Mahishasura)*
3. ⭐⭐ **Open-air rock relief** — **"Descent of the Ganges"** *(= **Arjuna's Penance** / **Bhagiratha's Penance**)*, carved on **two huge boulders**
4. **Shore Temple complex** — two small + one large temple, **two-tier compound wall studded with Nandi images**; mainly Shiva, with an **Anantashayana Vishnu**

## 1️⃣3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Dravida (Chola) — the features that contrast with Nagara

| Feature | Dravida |
|---|---|
| **Boundary** | ⭐ **HIGH boundary walls** |
| **Entrance** | ⭐ **`Gopuram`** — high gateway in the front wall |
| **Plan** | **Panchayatan** — principal temple + four subsidiary shrines |
| **Spire** | ⭐ **`Vimana`** — a **stepped pyramid rising LINEARLY**, not curved |
| **Crown** | ⭐⭐ **`Shikhara`** here = only the **octagonal crowning element** *(≈ Nagara's kalash, but **NOT spherical**)* |
| 🔴⭐⭐⭐ **Vimana count** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ONLY ONE vimana, on the main temple. Subsidiary shrines get NO vimana** — *unlike Nagara, where they do* |
| **Connection** | Assembly hall joined to garbhagriha by a vestibular tunnel — **`antarala`** |
| **Doorway** | Sculptures of **Dwarapala, mithuna and yakshas** |
| 🔴⭐⭐⭐ **Water tank** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A water tank INSIDE the enclosure — unique to Dravida** *(Nagara has none)* |
| **Examples** | **Brihadeshwara, Thanjavur** *(**Raja Raja I, 1011 AD**)*; **Gangaikondacholapuram** *(**Rajendra I**, for his Gangetic victory)* |

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Nataraja iconography — decoded element by element

> *Earliest known Nataraja: **Ravana Phadi cave, Aihole**, under the **early Chalukyas** — but the form **peaked under the Cholas**.*

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Upper RIGHT hand — drum (*damaru*)** | **The sound of CREATION** — all creation springs from it |
| **Upper LEFT hand — eternal fire** | **DESTRUCTION** — the inevitable counterpart of creation |
| **Lower RIGHT hand — `abhaya mudra`** | **Benediction**; reassuring the devotee not to fear |
| **Lower LEFT hand — points to upraised foot** | **The path of SALVATION** |
| **The dwarf underfoot** | **Ignorance and the ego** |
| **Matted flowing locks** | The **river Ganga** |
| ⭐ **One male earring, one female** | **Fusion of male and female — `ardhanarishvara`** |
| **Snake around the arm** | ⭐ **`Kundalini`** power, dormant in the spine |
| **Nimbus of flames** | The **unending cycles of time** |

## 1️⃣4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The other southern schools

| School | Period / who | ⭐ Signature |
|---|---|---|
| **Nayaka** *(= **Madurai** school)* | **16th–18th c.**, Nayaka rulers | Dravida-like but **much larger**; **Islamic influence**; ⭐ **`prakarams`** — huge corridors around the garbhagriha with roofed ambulatories; ⭐⭐ **the art of the gopuram peaks — Meenakshi temple, Madurai has the TALLEST gopuram in the world** |
| **Vesara** *(= **Karnataka** school)* | **Later Chalukyas, mid-7th c.** | ⭐ **A hybrid**: **curvilinear shikhara + square base from NAGARA**; **intricate carving, vimana design, terraced shikhara from DRAVIDA**. **Open** ambulatory. | **Hoysala** | **1050–1300**, **Belur, Halebid, Sringeri** | ⭐⭐⭐ **STELLATE (star-shaped) ground plan** — *not* the panchayatan cross; **multiple shrines around a central pillared hall**; ⭐ **soft soapstone (chlorite schist)**; obsessive decoration *(even the deities' jewellery is carved)*; all chambers get shikharas linked by horizontal mouldings; ⭐ built on a **1-metre platform — `jagati`**; **zigzag** walls and stairs. **Hoysaleshwara (Halebid)**, **Chennakesava (Belur)** |
| **Pala & Sena** *(Bengal)* | **8th–12th c.** | **Palas = Buddhist (Mahayana)** — built **Nalanda, Jagaddala, Odantapuri, Vikramshila**, and **Somapura Mahavihara (Bangladesh)**; **Senas = Hindu** — **Dhakeshwari temple**. ⭐⭐ **The curved/sloping "BANGLA ROOF" copied from bamboo huts — later adopted by the MUGHALS**; **terracotta (burnt brick)** the main material; tall curving shikhara with a large **amalaka** *(like Odisha)*; ⭐ sculptures in **stone and metal**, with a **highly lustrous finish** |

## 2️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐ Parsi fire temples — *nobody revises this, and it is very askable*

**Three grades**, in descending order of sanctity:

1. ⭐ **Atash Behram** — *"Fire of Victory"*, the highest
2. **Adarian**
3. **Atash Dadgah** *(Dar-e-Mehr)*

> ⭐⭐ **Counts to remember: EIGHT Atash Behrams in India; more than 100 Dadgahs**, mostly in **Maharashtra and Gujarat**. Exteriors are deliberately **plain** — the point is to **hold the sacred fire and the Yasna ceremony**, not to glorify the faith. There is an **inner sanctum** for the fire and **vents for smoke**. Ceremonies performed by high priests, the **Dasturs**.

**The eight:** ⭐ **Iranshah, Udvada (Gujarat) — 8th century, the oldest** · **Desai, Navsari (18th c.)** · **Dadiseth, Wadia, Banaji, Anjuman — all Mumbai** · **Modi and Vakil — Surat**.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Indo-Gothic *(= **Victorian** style)*

A blend of **Indian + Persian + Gothic**. Six features:

- Constructions **extremely large and elaborate**
- ⭐ **Walls THINNER** than Indo-Islamic
- ⭐⭐ **Arches POINTED**, unlike the **curving** arches of the Indo-Islamic era
- ⭐ **Large windows**
- Churches on a **crucified ground plan**
- ⭐ **British structural engineering — steel, iron and poured concrete**

**Examples: Victoria Memorial (Kolkata), Gateway of India (Mumbai), St Paul's Cathedral (Kolkata).**

| Basis | **Iberian** | **Gothic** |
|---|---|---|
| **Material** | ⭐ **Brick** main; **wood** for roofs and stairs | ⭐ **Red sandstone and coarse limestone** |
| **Structural variation** | ⭐ **None — the Portuguese kept their western traditions** | ⭐ **The British ADOPTED Indian motifs → Indo-Gothic** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Neo-Roman *(Neo-classical)* — **post-1911**

**New Delhi by Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker**; *"the Rome of Hindustan."* ⭐⭐ **Note that Singhania's treatment is openly CRITICAL — and that criticism is itself the answer to a Mains question in his own practice set:**

- Constructions **anonymous, without interesting features**
- ⭐ **A confluence of all styles → congested; cramped artistic expression**
- **Simplicity, modernity and utility compromised** by the hybrid nature
- Focus on **circular buildings**
- ⭐ **Overuse of oriental motifs to realise western designs**
- ⭐⭐ **Introduced the UPTURNED DOME** — visible atop the **Supreme Court** and **Rashtrapati Bhavan**

## 2️⃣4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Post-Independence

⭐ **Two schools — Revivalist and Modernist — but *"both could not break away from the colonial hangover,"* which Singhania blames for a decline in standards.** Punjab hired **Le Corbusier** (French) to design **Chandigarh**.

| Architect | ⭐ Why they matter |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Laurie Baker** | *"**The Architect of the Poor**"* — revolutionised **mass housing in Kerala**; **nominated for the Pritzker Prize in 2006** *("the Nobel of architecture")*. **(1)** environment-friendly buildings from **locally available material**; **(2)** ⭐ **introduced the FILLER SLAB, cutting steel and cement use**; **(3)** emphasis on **ventilation and thermal comfort** |
| ⭐⭐ **Charles Correa** | **Urban architecture and spatial planning** — adapted modern principles to local need. **MP Assembly building**, **Mahatma Gandhi Memorial Museum (Ahmedabad)**, **LIC building, Connaught Place**. ⭐ **Padma Vibhushan, 2006**. *(The **Kanchanjunga Apartments, Mumbai** are his.)* |

| The plausible claim | The truth |
|---|---|
| *"Panchayatan is an assembly of village elders / an administrative functionary."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It is a STYLE OF TEMPLE CONSTRUCTION** — four subsidiary shrines around a main one. **Asked exactly like this in 2014** |

| *"In Dravida temples the subsidiary shrines also have vimanas."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Only ONE vimana, on the main temple.** In **Nagara**, subsidiary shrines **do** get shikharas |
| *"Water tanks are common to Nagara and Dravida."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Water tank inside the enclosure is DRAVIDA-only.** Nagara has **none** |
| *"'Shikhara' means the same thing in both styles."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ In **Nagara** it is the **whole tower**; in **Dravida** it is **only the octagonal crowning element** on top of the vimana |

BCE, Ashoka/Dasharatha)* is far older. ⚠️ *Singhania's answer key says **Bhimbetka** is the oldest — but **Bhimbetka is a natural rock SHELTER with paintings, not rock-cut architecture**. **For "rock-cut caves", answer Barabar.*** |
| *"Barabar caves were made for Ajivikas by Chandragupta Maurya."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Wrong ruler.** Made for the **Ajivikas** — that part is right — but under **Ashoka and Dasharatha**, not Chandragupta |

| *"Babur introduced the arch and dome to India."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **No** *(2015)* — arch and dome arrived with the **Delhi Sultanate**, ~3 centuries earlier. Babur **did** bring **gunpowder** and **founded the Timurid dynasty** here |

## 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | The line to take |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Distinguish Nagara from Dravida"** *(1995, 1989, and a standing practice question)* | **Do NOT narrate. TABULATE**: shikhara vs vimana · amalaka+kalash vs octagonal shikhara · **no boundary wall vs high walls + gopuram** · **no tank vs tank inside** · **subsidiary shrines with shikharas vs only one vimana** · covered vs open ambulatory · Ganga-Yamuna at the door |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Gandhara owed as much to the Romans as to the Greeks"** *(2014)* | **Concede the Greek half first** *(idealised muscular form, Apollo-like Buddha, Hellenistic drapery)*, **then argue the Roman half** *(realism, portraiture conventions, stucco and concrete technique, mural tradition)*. **Then close on the Indian half — the ICONOGRAPHY is wholly Buddhist: ushnisha, urna, mudras, the Bodhisattva types.** *A three-part answer beats a two-part one* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Chola architecture is a high watermark"** *(2013, 1999)* | **Argue it as CULMINATION, not invention**: the Pallava four-stage ladder made structural temples possible; **the Cholas scaled them** *(Brihadeshwara, 1011)*, **integrated sculpture** *(Nataraja)*, **and made the temple an economic institution** — ⭐ **cross-link to [TN Cl.XI](../../TN_State_Board_History/Class_11/Notes/01_Cholas_and_Pandyas.md) for the temple-as-landholder material, which is what lifts this out of pure art history** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Gupta age = golden age of Indian architecture"** | ⭐ **The strong answer uses the FIVE STAGES as its spine** — flat roof → raised platform + ambulatory → **shikhara + panchayatan** → rectangular → circular. **A visible evolutionary argument beats a list of temples** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Early Buddhist stupa-art expounds Buddhist ideals through folk motifs"** *(2016)* | ⭐ **This is [Cl.12 Th.4](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/04_Theme4_Thinkers_Beliefs_and_Buildings.md)'s question, not Singhania's** — use **yakshi/shalabhanjika, the tree, the empty seat, the naga** and the point that **Buddhism attached itself to an EXISTING sacred geography**. Singhania only adds the **Shunga *torana*** and the **Hellenistic influence** on it |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **"Indus urban planning as an input to present-day urbanisation"** *(2014)* | **Grid layout, segregated citadel/lower town, standardised bricks, covered drains with cesspits, wells, granary ventilation, dockyard at Lothal, Dholavira's water harvesting.** ⚠️ **But use [Cl.12 Th.1](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_I/Notes/01_Theme1_Bricks_Beads_and_Bones_Harappan.md)'s HEDGING discipline** — write *"has been interpreted as"*, not *"was"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **"Laurie Baker was the Architect of the Poor"** | **Three planks**: local materials → cost + carbon; **filler slab** → less steel and cement; **ventilation/thermal comfort** → no mechanical cooling. ⭐ **Then generalise to today: this IS the low-cost sustainable-housing debate** — a clean GS1↔GS3 bridge |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **"Neo-Roman was a congested style"** | ⭐ **Singhania hands you the critique ready-made** — anonymous, hybrid, congested, oriental motifs forced into western forms, utility sacrificed. **A balanced answer should still concede Lutyens' Delhi its axial planning and civic scale** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Environmental adaptation in pre-modern architecture** | ⭐⭐ **An underused, high-impact angle.** **Malwa/Pathan school**: large windows, light arched pavilions, **baulis**, local materials, batter. **Harappan**: granary air ducts, drains. **Solanki**: equinox solar alignment, **surya-kund**. **Kashmir**: sloping roofs for snow. **Then land on Laurie Baker** — a single continuous argument from Mohenjo-daro to Kerala |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in twelve lines

1. **Harappan art** = **lost wax** bronzes, **steatite seals** *(no cow)*, **Red-and-Black** and **perforated** pottery, **terracotta by pinching**.
2. **Mauryan art splits into COURT** *(palaces, pillars, stupas)* **and POPULAR** *(caves, yaksha/yakshi, NBPW)* — and **Ashokan pillars are monolithic and free-standing**, unlike the assembled, building-attached **Achaemenid** ones.
3. **Post-Mauryan gives three sculpture schools** — **Gandhara** *(grey stone, Greco-Roman, Kushana)*, **Mathura** *(red spotted sandstone, all three faiths, Kushana)*, **Amaravati** *(white marble, narrative, Satavahana)*.
4. **The chaitya hall is post-Mauryan; the vihara is Mauryan.** The **Shungas** added the **torana**.
5. **The Gupta temple evolves in five stages**, and stage 3 introduces the two things that matter — **the shikhara** and **panchayatan**.
6. **Nagara's discriminators**: covered ambulatory, **no tank**, **no boundary wall**, Ganga-Yamuna at the door, **amalaka + kalash**, subsidiary shrines get their own shikharas.
7. **Nagara has three sub-schools**: **Odisha** *(plain interior, iron girders, rekhadeul)*, **Khajuraho** *(both walls carved, erotic, sandstone)*, **Solanki** *(bare walls, surya-kund, equinox alignment)*.
8. **The Pallavas climb four stages** — Mahendra *(rock-cut)* → Narasimha *(rathas)* → **Rajsimha (structural)** → Nandivarman — and **the Dharmaraja ratha is the ancestor of Dravida**.
9. **Dravida's discriminators**: **gopuram**, **high walls**, **water tank inside**, **one vimana only**, antarala.
10. **The other southern schools**: **Vesara** *(hybrid)*, **Hoysala** *(stellate, soapstone, jagati)*, **Vijayanagara** *(gopurams all round, Yali pillars, secular buildings)*, **Nayaka** *(prakarams, tallest gopuram)*, **Pala-Sena** *(Bangla roof, terracotta)*.
11. **Indo-Islamic = arcuate replacing trabeate**, plus **arabesque, pietra dura, charbagh, jaali, foreshortening** — and the Sultanate is read **dynasty by dynasty**: **Mameluke → Seljuk → batter → double dome**, with **four provincial schools** *(Bengal, Malwa/Pathan, Jaunpur/Sharqi, Bijapur/Deccan)*.
12. **The colonial layer is real architecture**: **Iberian/Baroque** → **French grid and "anonymous architecture"** → **Indo-Gothic** → **Neo-Roman with its upturned dome** → and after 1947, **Le Corbusier, Laurie Baker and Charles Correa**.
