# 📚 PART A — ORIGINS AND THE TEXT CHAIN

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Where music comes from

- ⭐ **Narada Muni** is said to have brought music to earth, and taught of ⭐ **`Naada Brahma`** — the sound pervading the universe.
- ⭐⭐ **IVC instruments**: a **seven-holed flute** and the **Ravanahatha** have been recovered.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The Vedic layer** — *a very askable cluster*:

| Source | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Sama Veda** | **All seven notes of the raga `Kharaharapriya`** appear in it **in descending order** |
| ⭐⭐ **Gandharva Veda** | **The science of music — an `Upaveda` of the SAMA VEDA** |
| **Aitareya Aranyaka** | Mentions **the parts of the veena** |
| **Jaimini Brahmana** | Speaks of **dance and music together** |
| **Kausitaki Brahmana** | Puts **dance, vocal and instrumental music together as one art** |

- ⭐ **`Om`** is held by musicologists to be **the source of all ragas and notes**.
- ⭐⭐ **Panini (c. 500 BCE)** made the **first proper reference to making music**; the **first reference to musical THEORY is in Bharata's *Natyashastra*** *(200 BC–200 AD)*.
- ⭐ **Early ritual forms**: ***Sangama*** *(later Vedic — chanting verses set to musical patterns)* and ***Jatigan*** *(the epics set to narrative music)*.

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The musicological text chain — **a clean match-the-following**

| Text | Author | Century | ⭐ Contribution |
|---|---|:--:|---|
| ⭐⭐ ***Natyashastra*** | **Bharata** | 200 BC–200 AD | **The first work on musicology**; identified the **octave and its 22 keys** |
| ⭐ ***Dattilam*** | — | — | Endorsed **22 shrutis per octave**, suggesting these are **all the human body can produce** |
| ⭐ ***Brihaddeshi*** | **Matanga** | **9th** | ⭐⭐ **Focused on DEFINING the word "raga"** |
| ***Sangeeta Makaranda*** | **Nanda** ⚠️ | **11th** | Enumerated **93 ragas**, classified into **feminine and masculine** forms |
| ⭐⭐⭐ ***Sangeet Ratnakara*** | **Sarangadeva** | **13th** | ⭐⭐ **The classic text.** Defined **~264 ragas** including **both North Indian and Dravidian** repertoires; ⭐ **its greatest contribution was identifying and classifying the MICROTONES** |
| ***Swaramela-Kalanidhi*** | **Ramamatya** | **16th** | On ragas |
| ***Chaturdandi-prakasika*** | **Venkatamakhin** | **17th** | Musicology |

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY]** — *Singhania attributes the ***Sangeeta Makaranda*** to **"Nanda."*** **It is conventionally attributed to `NARADA`.** *Treat the attribution as unsettled; the **93 ragas** and the **masculine/feminine classification** are the examinable parts.*

⭐⭐ **The transmission system was the `Gurukul`/Ashram** — the guru-shishya tradition: students lived in the hermitage **12 years**, learning by **serving** the master; hermitages were **patronised by kings and the wealthy**; ⭐ **prince and commoner were treated identically, without discrimination**.

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The Persian turn

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The one causal sentence to remember:** *the influx of **Islamic and Persian elements** changed North Indian music — the devotional ***Dhruvapada*** **transformed into the `DHRUPAD` style by the 15th century**, and **by the 17th century a new form had evolved: `KHAYAL`.*** *(Cross-link this to [TN Cl.XI U.6](../../TN_State_Board_History/Class_11/Notes/06_Turks_and_Marathas.md), which supplies the instruments — **rabab and sarangi** — and Firuz Tughlaq's ***Rag Darpan*** translation.)*

# 📚 PART B — THE ANATOMY *(the technical core — this is the real delta)*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three pillars

> **`RAGA` · `TALA` · `SWARA`.** ⭐⭐ **Learn this as: *ragas form the basis of the MELODY; tala becomes the basis of the RHYTHM*.** 🔴 **Singhania's own practice set reverses these to trap you.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Swara — the notes

| Pitch | Function | Abbrev. |
|---|---|:--:|
| **Shadaja** | **Tonic** | **Sa** |
| **Rishabha** | Supertonic | re |
| **Gandhara** | Mediant | Ga |
| **Madhyama** | Sub-dominant | Ma |
| **Panchama** | **Dominant** | Pa |
| **Dhaivata** | Sub-mediant | Dha |
| **Nishada** | Subtonic | Ni |

⭐ **The seven together = `Saptak` or `Sargam`.**

### 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Swara vs Shruti — **the highest-value distinction in the chapter**

| | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **`Shruti`** | **The SMALLEST gradation of pitch** — a microtone, representing quality of frequency |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The numbers** | **22 shrutis, of which only 12 are AUDIBLE.** Those 12 = ⭐ **7 `Suddha` swaras + 5 `Vikrita` swaras** |

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Raga

⭐ **Etymology: from Sanskrit `ranj` — "to delight, to make happy."**

> ⭐⭐ **Singhania's careful definition, worth quoting:** *a raga is **neither a scale nor a mode**, but **"a scientific, precise, subtle and aesthetic melodic form with its own peculiar ascending and descending movement,"** consisting of **a full octave, or a series of 5, 6 or 7 notes.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three `jaatis` — by note count

| Jaati | Notes | Term |
|---|:--:|---|
| ⭐ **Audava / Odava** | **5** | **Pentatonic** |
| **Shadava** | **6** | **Hexatonic** |
| **Sampurna** | **7** | **Heptatonic** |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Every raga must have AT LEAST FIVE notes.** *(A direct practice-question answer.)*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The three `Raga Bhed`

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Shuddha** | Playing notes **absent** from the composition **does NOT change** its nature and form |
| **Chhayalag** | Playing absent notes **DOES change** its nature and form |
| **Sankeerna** | ⭐ **A combination of two or more ragas** |

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The note hierarchy — *a certain Prelims item*

| Term | Role |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **`Vadi`** | **THE "KING"** — the principal note the raga is built on; **used most often** |
| ⭐⭐ **`Samvadi`** | **THE "QUEEN"** — the **second most important**; the **4th or 5th note** relative to the vadi |
| **`Anuvadi`** | **All other notes** present in the composition |
| ⭐ **`Vivadi`** | ⭐⭐ **The notes NOT present** in the composition |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Movement and speed

- ⭐ **`Aaroha`** = **ascent** *(Sa re ga ma pa dha ni)* · ⭐ **`Avaroha`** = **descent** *(ni dha pa ma ga re sa)*
- ⭐⭐ **`Laya` — the three speeds**: **`Vilambit`** *(slow)* · **`Madhya`** *(medium)* · **`Drut`** *(fast)*

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The six main ragas — **time, season, mood**

> ⭐ *These arise from **72 `melas`/parent scales**, but the Hindustani repertoire reduces to six mains, and ⭐⭐ **all are time- AND season-specific**.*

| Raga | Time | Season | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bhairav** | **Dawn** | Any | **Peace** |
| **Hindol** | Morning | **Spring** | Sweetness of a young couple |
| **Deepak** | Night | **Summer** | **Compassion** |
| **Megh** | Late night | **Rainy** | **Courage** |
| **Shree** | **Evening** | **Winter** | Gladness |
| **Malkaush** | **Midnight** | **Winter** | **Veer / brave** |

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Tala

- ⭐ **Rhythmic groupings of beats**; cycles range from **3 to 108 beats**. ⭐⭐ **Tala is INDEPENDENT of the music that accompanies it** and has its own divisions. Its tempo is the **`laya`**.
- ⭐⭐ **Practical count: musicologists claim 100+ talas, but only ~30 are known and only 10–12 actually used.** Named: **dadra, kaharba, rupak, ektal, jhaptal, teental, ada chautal**. ⭐ **`Teental` — 16 beats — is the composers' usual choice.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Carnatic tala is far more rigid**: built from **`laghu`, `dhrutam`, `anudhrutam`**; ⭐⭐ **35 original talas × 5 `ghatis` = 175 talas**.

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Thaat — and how it differs from raga

⭐⭐ **`Thaat` is a SYSTEM OF CLASSIFYING ragas into groups.** ⭐⭐⭐ **V. N. Bhatkhande's 10-thaat classification** is the one Hindustani music uses:

> **Bilawal · Khamaj · Kafi · Asavari · Bhairavi · Bhairav · Kalyan · Marwa · Poorvi · Todi**

| Point | **Thaat** | **Raga** |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | **Scales** produced from the 12 swaras | ⭐ **Ragas BELONG to the genre of thaats** |
| **Notes** | ⭐ **Must have exactly 7** | ⭐ **Must have at least 5** |
| **Direction** | ⭐⭐ **Only AAROHA (ascending)** | ⭐⭐ **Must have BOTH aaroha and avaroha** |
| **Melody** | ⭐⭐ **NOT sung — need not be melodious** | ⭐⭐ **Sung — must be melodious** |
| **Vadi/Samvadi** | ⭐ **None** | ⭐ **Has them** |
| **Naming** | ⭐ **Named after popular RAGAS** | ⭐ **Named after the EMOTION they evoke** |

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Samay, and the other components

⭐⭐ **`Samay` — every raga has a specific performance time**, because those notes are held to be more effective then:

| Half | Hours | Ragas | Saptak used |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Poorva Bhaag** | **12 AM – 12 PM** | **Poorva ragas** | **Sa to Ma** |
| ⭐ **Uttar Bhaag** | **12 PM – 12 AM** | **Uttar ragas** | **Pa to Sa** |

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **`Alap`** | **Gradual exposition** of the raga emphasising vadi/samvadi, in **slow tempo**, at the **beginning**; ⭐ sung in **`aakaar`** — **no syllables, only the vowel sound "aa"** |
| ⭐ **`Sthayee`/`Mukhda`** | **The FIRST part** of the composition — the most used |
| ⭐ **`Antara`** | **The SECOND part** |
| ⭐⭐ **`Taan`** | **Basic notes at FAST tempo** — technical, showing dexterity in weaving note-patterns |
| ⭐ **`Murki`** | ⭐ **A SHORT taan of 3–4 notes**, sung very fast |
| ⭐ **`Alankara`** | **Ornamentation** — a melodic pattern followed in succession *(e.g. "Sa re ga", "ga ma pa", "ma pa dha")* |

# 📚 PART C — THE TWO CLASSICAL SYSTEMS

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Hindustani vs Carnatic — **the 2009 Mains question, tabled**

> ⭐ **Both descend from Bharata's *Natyashastra*; they DIVERGED IN THE 14TH CENTURY.**

| Point | **Hindustani** | **Carnatic** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Influence** | ⭐ **Arab, Persian, Afghan** | ⭐ **Indigenous** |
| ⭐ **Sub-styles** | ⭐⭐ **Several → hence GHARANAS** | ⭐⭐ **One prescribed style — no gharanas** |
| **Instruments** | **Equally important as vocals** | ⭐ **More emphasis on VOCAL music** |
| **Ragas** | **6 major ragas** | **72** ⚠️ |
| ⭐⭐ **Time** | ⭐ **Adheres to time** | ⭐ **Does NOT adhere to time** |
| ⭐⭐ **Instruments** | **Tabla, sarangi, sitar, santoor** | **Veena, mridangam, mandolin** |
| **Region** | North India | South India |
| ⭐ **Shared** | ⭐⭐ **FLUTE and VIOLIN — common to both** | *(the same)* |
| **Focus** | **Musical structure + possibilities of improvisation**; uses the **Shuddha Swara Saptaka** *("octave of natural notes")* | ⭐ **`Kriti`-based**; focuses on ***saahitya*** — **the LYRIC quality** |

> ⚠️⚠️ **[VERIFY] — A DIRECT SELF-CONTRADICTION IN THE BOOK, AND YOU SHOULD NOT REPRODUCE IT.** *Singhania's comparison table says Carnatic has **"No freedom to improvise."*** **This is wrong, and the book's own text on the same page contradicts it** — it describes ***Ragam Thanam Pallavi*** as the part *"where the artist has **great scope for improvisation**"* and ***Swara-Kalpana*** as *"an **improvised** section."* ⭐⭐⭐ **In an answer, write that BOTH systems are improvisatory; the real difference is that Hindustani improvises around a more open structure while Carnatic improvises within a more RIGID, composition-anchored one.** *That is both correct and a better sentence.*

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY]** — *"6 ragas vs 72 ragas" mixes categories.* **72 is the number of `melakarta` PARENT SCALES in Carnatic music**, not its raga count *(which runs to hundreds)*. **Say "72 melakarta parent scales", not "72 ragas."**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The parts of a Carnatic composition

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Pallavi** | The **first/second thematic lines**, repeated in each stanza. ⭐⭐ **The *Ragam Thanam Pallavi* is the *pièce de résistance*** — greatest scope for improvisation |
| **Anu Pallavi** | **Two lines following the Pallavi**; sung at the beginning and sometimes the end, ⭐ **but NOT necessarily repeated after every charanam** |
| ⭐ **Varnam** | ⭐⭐ **Sung at the BEGINNING — it REVEALS THE RAGA to the audience.** Two halves: **Purvanga** and **Uttaranga** |
| ⭐ **Ragamalika** | ⭐ **The CONCLUDING part** — free improvisation, but **all artists must return to the original theme** |

⭐ **Also**: **`Swara-Kalpana`** *(improvised, with the drummer, at medium and fast pace)* · ⭐⭐ **`Thanam` = melodic improvisation in free rhythm WITH mridangam; `Ragam` = the same WITHOUT mridangam.**

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Hindustani styles — **Dhrupad and Khyal**

⭐ **The ten named styles**: Dhrupad, Dhamar, Hori, Khayal, Tappa, Chaturang, Ragasagar, Tarana, Sargam, Thumri.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Dhrupad

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | ⭐ **The oldest and grandest** Hindustani form; mentioned in the *Natyashastra*; roots in **Prabandha and Dhruvapada** |
| **Name** | ***Dhruva*** *(literally "**unmoving**")* + ***pada*** — denotes **both the verse form and the singing style**. ⭐⭐ **It implies the return of `Swara` (tonal), `Kala` (time) and `Shabda` (textual) trajectories to a FIXED POINT** |
| ⭐⭐ **Peak** | Consolidated in the **13th c.**, **zenith in AKBAR's court** — **Tansen** *(one of the **navaratna**)*, **Baba Gopal Das**, **Swami Haridas**; **Baiju Bawra** speculated to have sung there. Also **Raja Man Singh Tomar's court at GWALIOR**. ⭐ **Declined in the 18th c.** |
| **Form** | Starts with ⭐ **alap sung WITHOUT WORDS** *("pure music without distraction of words")*, tempo rising gradually — **the major part of the performance**. Then Dhrupad proper begins and the ⭐ **pakhawaj** enters. Uses **Sanskrit syllables**; ⭐ **of TEMPLE ORIGIN**. Compositions have **4–5 stanzas**, ⭐⭐ **performed by a DUO, generally two MALE vocalists**, with **tanpura and pakhawaj** |

**⭐⭐⭐ The four Dhrupad *banis* / gharanas:**

| Gharana | *Bani* | ⭐ Note |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Dagari** | **Dagar vani** | Great emphasis on **alap**; men trained and performed **in pairs** for generations. ⭐⭐ **The Dagars are MUSLIMS but sing HINDU texts of gods and goddesses** — *a striking syncretism point*. E.g. the **Gundecha Brothers** |
| **Darbhanga** | **Khandar + Gauhar vani** | Emphasise **raga alap AND composed songs** over improvised alap; incorporate **layakari**. The **Mallik** family |
| **Bettiah** | **Nauhar + Khandar vani** | Techniques known **only within the families**; the **Mishras**. ⭐ **Bettiah and Darbhanga Dhrupad together are called the `HAVELI` style** |
| **Talwandi** | **Khandar vani** | ⭐ **Based in PAKISTAN** — hard to keep within the Indian system |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PRELIMS 2012.** *"Dhrupad **originated and developed in the RAJPUT kingdoms during the Mughal period**"* → ⭐⭐ **FALSE.** *"Dhrupad is primarily **devotional and spiritual**"* → **TRUE.** *"Dhrupad uses **Sanskrit syllables from mantras**"* → **TRUE.** **Answer: (ii) and (iii).**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Khyal

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | ⭐ **From PERSIAN — "idea" or "imagination."** Origin attributed to ⭐ **Amir Khusrau** |
| **Patron** | ⭐ **Sultan Mohammad Sharqi** gave it the biggest patronage in the **15th c.** |
| **Form** | Short songs of **2–8 lines**; a composition is called a ⭐ **`bandish`**. ⭐⭐ **Defined by the use of `TAAN` — and therefore ALAP GETS MUCH LESS ROOM THAN IN DHRUPAD** *(the cleanest Dhrupad/Khyal discriminator)*. ⭐ **Two songs per performance: `Bada Khyal` (slow tempo) and `Chhota Khyal` (fast)**. Themes usually **romantic**, even when divine |

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Khyal gharanas:**

| Gharana | ⭐ Known for | Names |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Gwalior** | ⭐⭐ **The OLDEST and most elaborate**; **equal emphasis on melody and rhythm**; complex singing but **prefers SIMPLE ragas** | **Nathu Khan**, **Vishnu Paluskar** |
| ⭐ **Kirana** | Named after **Kirana town, UP**; ⭐ **precise tuning and expression of notes**; mastery of **slow-tempo** ragas; clarity of pronunciation; prefers **traditional ragas/sargam** | Founded by **Nayak Gopal**; popularised by **Abdul Karim Khan** and **Abdul Wahid Khan**; ⭐⭐ **Bhimsen Joshi** and **Gangubai Hangal** |
| ⭐ **Agra** *(renamed **Rangeela**)* | ⭐ **A BLEND of Khyal and Dhrupad**; special emphasis on **bandish** | **Khuda Baksh** *or* **Haji Sujan Khan**; revived by **Faiyaz Khan** |
| ⭐ **Patiala** | **Ghazal, thumri, khyal**; greater **rhythm**; **ornamentation (alankaras)**; **intricate taans**; unique **taans, gamak and tarana-style gayaki** | **Bade Fateh Ali Khan** and **Ali Baksh Khan**; ⭐⭐ **Bade Ghulam Ali Khan** — famed for **Raga Darbari** |
| **Bhendibazaar** | ⭐⭐ **Singers trained to CONTROL BREATH — long passages in a SINGLE BREATH**; ⭐ **uses some CARNATIC ragas** | **Chhajju Khan, Nazir Khan, Khadim Hussain Khan** |

> ⚠️ **[VERIFY] — another internal contradiction.** *One of Singhania's own practice questions asks who belongs to *"the **Kirana gharana of DHRUPAD** style."*** ⭐⭐ **Kirana is a KHYAL gharana — the book's own text says so.** *(The intended answer, Bhimsen Joshi and Gangubai Hangal, is right; the label "Dhrupad" is wrong.)*

⭐⭐ **The gharana concept itself:** from Urdu/Hindi ***ghar*** = **family/house** — *"a system of social organisation linking musicians or dancers by **lineage or apprenticeship**, and by adherence to a particular musical style,"* indicating **a comprehensive musicological ideology**. ⭐ **Named gharanas: Agra, Gwalior, Indore, Jaipur, Kirana, Patiala.**

⭐ **Tarana style**: **rhythm is crucial**; a short melody repeated with variation, plus a contrasting higher-note melody; many words sung at fast tempo, demanding **rhythmic manipulation**. *(Pandit Rattan Mohan Sharma of the **Mewati** gharana was titled **"Tarana ke Baadshah"** in 2011.)*

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The semi-classical trio

> ⭐ **What makes a style "semi-classical"**: still based on **swara**, but **deviates from strict raga structure** *(lighter ragas like Bhoopali, Malkaush)*, uses **lighter tala** and **madhyam/drut laya**, and ⭐⭐ **emphasises `bhava` and LYRICS over alap-jod-tan-jhala**.

| Style | ⭐ Key facts |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Thumri** | **Mixed ragas**; **romantic or devotional**; ⭐ **inspired by the Bhakti movement — the text usually the girl's love for Krishna**; in **Hindi, Awadhi or Braj Bhasha**; ⭐ **usually a FEMALE voice**; ⭐⭐ **characterised by inherent SENSUALITY** and great **flexibility with the raga**. ⭐ **Two types: `Purbi` (slower) and `Punjabi` (fast, lively)**. ⭐ **Gharanas at BANARAS and LUCKNOW**; the great voice is ⭐ **Begum Akhtar**. ⭐⭐ **"Thumri" also covers lighter forms — Dadra, Hori, Kajari, Saavan, Jhoola, Chaiti** |
| ⭐⭐ **Tappa** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Originated in the FOLK SONGS OF THE CAMEL RIDERS of North-West India** — *a memorable and very askable origin*; legitimised as semi-classical at the **Mughal court of Muhammad Shah**. **Fast, subtle, knotty constructions**; **very quick turns of phrase**. ⭐ Cultivated in the ***baithaki*** style under **zamindari elites** in their **baithak-khanas** and **jalsaghars**. ⭐⭐ **Today nearly EXTINCT** — few exponents *(Mian Sodi, Pandit Laxman Rao of Gwalior, Shanno Khurana)* |
| ⭐⭐ **Ghazal** | **Rhyming couplets + a refrain, all lines sharing one metre**; ⭐ **originated in IRAN, 10th c. AD**; ⭐⭐ **never exceeds 12 `ashaar` (couplets)**. Spread in South Asia in the **12th c.** via **Sufi mystics and the Sultanate courts**; **zenith under the Mughals**; ⭐ **Amir Khusrau among the first exponents**. ⭐⭐ **Traditionally deals with ONE subject: love — unconditional and superior**, with the beloved **deliberately ambiguous between divine and human**. Names: **Ghalib, Iqbal, Rumi, Hafez, Kazi Nazrul Islam** |

## 🔴 Prelims traps — the claim vs the truth

| The plausible claim | The truth |
|---|---|
| *"Raga is the basis of rhythm; tala the basis of melody."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REVERSED. Raga = MELODY. Tala = RHYTHM** |
| *"Thaat and raga are the same kind of thing."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thaat: 7 notes, ascending only, NOT sung, no vadi/samvadi. Raga: min. 5 notes, both directions, sung, has vadi/samvadi** |
| *"A raga has only aaroha."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is a THAAT. A raga must have BOTH aaroha and avaroha** |
| *"There are 22 audible shrutis."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **22 shrutis exist; only 12 are AUDIBLE** *(7 shuddha + 5 vikrita)* |
| *"Odava raga has 6 notes."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Odava/Audava = 5 (pentatonic).** Shadava = 6, Sampurna = 7 |
| *"Antara is the first part of a composition."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **`Sthayee`/`Mukhda` is FIRST; `Antara` is SECOND** |
| *"Jaltarang is a wind instrument."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It is a GHANA VADYA (idiophone).** Shehnai, flute and pungi are **sushira** |
| *"Ananda / Lavanya is one of the nine rasas."* | ⭐⭐⭐ **Neither is** *(→ the nine in [Ch.6 §3](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))* |
| *"Alha — Maharashtra."* | ⭐⭐⭐ **MADHYA PRADESH.** *(Maharashtra's are Lavani, Powada, Ovi, Bhavageete)* |
| *"Manganiyars are known for martial arts in the North-East."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NO** *(2014)* — ⭐ **a MUSICAL tradition of NORTH-WEST India** *(Rajasthan)* |
| *"Dhrupad originated in the Rajput kingdoms in the Mughal period."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FALSE** *(2012)*. It is **older — 13th c., from Prabandha/Dhruvapada, of TEMPLE origin** — though it **peaked at Akbar's court** |
| *"Bhimsen Joshi belongs to the Gwalior gharana."* | ⭐⭐⭐ **KIRANA.** *(Gwalior: Nathu Khan, Vishnu Paluskar)* |
| *"Khyal gives more room to alap than Dhrupad."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Reversed** — ⭐ **Khyal's use of `taan` means alap gets MUCH LESS room than in Dhrupad** |
| *"Carnatic music adheres to time-of-day like Hindustani."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It does NOT.** ⭐ **Hindustani adheres to time; Carnatic does not** |
| *"Tappa originated in the Mughal court."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It was LEGITIMISED there — it ORIGINATED in the folk songs of the CAMEL RIDERS of North-West India** |
| *"Tabla accompanies Carnatic music."* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Tabla → Hindustani. MRIDANGAM → Carnatic.** ⭐ **Flute and violin are common to both** |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in ten lines

1. **Music's textual chain runs *Natyashastra* → *Dattilam* → *Brihaddeshi* (defines "raga") → *Sangeet Ratnakara* (Sarangadeva, 13th c., ~264 ragas, the microtones).**
2. ⭐ **Three pillars: RAGA (melody), TALA (rhythm), SWARA (note).**
3. **22 shrutis, only 12 audible** = **7 shuddha + 5 vikrita**; **7 swaras = saptak**.
4. **Ragas: min. 5 notes; Audava 5 / Shadava 6 / Sampurna 7; vadi = king, samvadi = queen, vivadi = absent.**
5. ⭐ **Thaat ≠ raga**: thaat has **7 notes, ascending only, is not sung, has no vadi/samvadi**, and is **named after ragas** — while ragas are **named after the emotion** they evoke.
6. **Persian contact turned *Dhruvapada* into DHRUPAD (15th c.) and produced KHYAL (17th c.)** — Khyal is **taan-led, so its alap is short**; Dhrupad is **alap-led, temple-origin, sung by a male duo with pakhawaj**.
7. **Four Dhrupad banis** *(Dagari, Darbhanga, Bettiah, Talwandi)*; **five Khyal gharanas** *(Gwalior oldest, Kirana slow-tempo, Agra a Khyal-Dhrupad blend, Patiala rhythmic, Bhendibazaar breath-control)*.
8. **Semi-classical: thumri (sensual, female voice, Braj/Awadhi), tappa (CAMEL-RIDERS' folk origin, now nearly extinct), ghazal (Iran, 10th c., max 12 couplets, one subject — love).**
9. **Hindustani vs Carnatic: Persian vs indigenous · gharanas vs one style · time-bound vs not · tabla/sitar vs veena/mridangam — sharing flute, violin and a common origin that split in the 14th century.**
10. **Instruments in four groups — Avanaddha, Sushira, Ghana, Tata** *(bowed/plectral/struck)* — **the 2012 Mains answer in one line.**
