# 📚 PART A — CALENDARS ⭐⭐⭐⭐

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three systems

| System | ⭐ Definition | ⭐ Length |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Solar** | Earth's orbit from a point of the ecliptic *(solstice/equinox)* back to it | ⭐⭐ **365 days, 5 hrs, 48 min, 46 sec.** ⭐ **Keeps the CLOSEST correspondence with the SEASONS** |
| ⭐ **Lunar** | 12 **lunations** — a synodic month between two full/new moons | ⭐⭐ **354 days — SHORT of the solar year by 11 DAYS** *(a lunar month varies 29.26–29.80 days)* |
| ⭐ **Luni-Solar** | ⭐⭐ **YEAR by the solar cycle, MONTHS by the lunar** — the Hindu pattern, reconciled by intercalation and suppression | — |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **`ADHIK MASA` — the intercalary month.** ⭐ **Added every 2.5 years to close the 11-day gap.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The precise definition: the Sun's entry into a zodiac sign is a `SANKRANTI` (12 per year); `ADHIK MASA` IS THE LUNAR MONTH IN WHICH THERE IS NO SANKRANTI.** ⭐⭐ **Conversely, a month with TWO sankrantis is `KSHAYA MASA` — the month that is DELETED.** *(Adhik Masa is also called **Mala Masa**.)*

⭐ **Two fortnights**: ⭐⭐ **`SHUKLA PAKSHA` (bright half) starts the day AFTER the NEW moon; `KRISHNA PAKSHA` (dark half) starts the day AFTER the FULL moon.** ⭐ **Two month-reckonings: `Amanta`** *(ends at new moon)* **and `Purnimanta`** *(ends at full moon)*. ⭐⭐ **The LUNAR month is followed in the greater part of the nation.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Time units — a clean conversion table

| Unit | Equals |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Tithi / Vasara** | **The LUNAR day** — avg. **23 hrs 37 min**, i.e. ⭐ **23 minutes SHORTER than a solar day** |
| **Divasa** | The **SOLAR** day = **24 hours = 60 ghatikas** |
| **1 ghatika** | **60 palas = 24 minutes** |
| **1 pala** | **60 vipalas = 24 seconds** |
| ⭐⭐ **1 Muhurta** | ⭐ **2 ghatikas = 48 MINUTES** *(so 2.5 muhurtas = 2 hours)* |

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Hindu calendar — `Panchanga`

⭐⭐ ***Panch* + *anga*** = **five limbs**: ⭐⭐⭐ **`TITHI, VAARA, NAKSHATRA, YOGA, KARANA`** *(alternatively: year, month, paksha, tithi, ghatika)*.

⭐ **There are 28 nakshatras**, unequal in size; ⭐ **each rashi contains two to three of them.** ⭐⭐ **The solar year splits into `UTTARAYANA`** *(Makara → Karka Sankranti, Jan–June — **the God's DAY**)* **and `DAKSHINAYANA`** *(July–Dec — **the God's NIGHT**)*; ⭐ **one solar year = one day and night of God.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The four Yugas

| Yuga | ⭐ Years | Character |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Satya / Krta** | **1,728,000** | ⭐ **The golden age — one religion, no ceremonies needed, no agriculture or mining** *(the earth yielded on its own)*, no disease, no sects |
| **Treta** | **1,296,000** | Virtue slightly diminished; wars; **agriculture, labour and mining begin**; lifespan 1,000–10,000 years |
| **Dvapara** | **864,000** | Tamasic qualities; disease rampant; lifespan a few centuries |
| ⭐ **Kali** | **432,000** | ⭐⭐ **The present age — began 3102 BC**; lifespan ~100 years, falling to 20 by its end |

⭐⭐ **The four = one `MAHAYUGA` = 4.32 million human years. 1,000 Mahayugas = ONE DAY OF BRAHMA = 4.32 billion years. A `MAHAKALPA` = 100 years of Brahma.**

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The eras — **the highest-yield table here**

| Era | ⭐ Zero year | Type | ⭐⭐ Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Vikram Samvat** | ⭐ **56 BC** | ⭐ **LUNAR** | ⭐ **56.7 years ahead of the Gregorian**; **354 days**; in force **almost all over India EXCEPT BENGAL**. ⭐⭐ **Traditionally established by KING VIKRAMADITYA OF UJJAIN to mark victory over the SAKAS** — ⚠️ **but many historians hold it was originally the `MALAWA GANA` era, renamed after Chandragupta Vikramaditya conquered Malwa c. 400 AD.** ⭐ **New year: first day after the new moon in CHAITRA (March–April)**; ⭐ in most of India the era **starts with KARTIKA**; ⭐ **adds Adhik Masa every 3 and every 5 years** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Saka Samvat** | ⭐ **78 AD** | ⭐⭐ **LUNI-SOLAR** *(lunar months, solar year)* | ⭐ **Initiated by KING SHALIVAHAN**. ⭐⭐ **365 days, with a FIXED number of days in each month.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Year zero near the vernal equinox of 78; the year BEGINS ON 22 MARCH — except in Gregorian leap years, when it begins 21 MARCH.** Same twelve month-names as Vikram, starting **Chaitra** |
| ⭐⭐ **Hijri** | ⭐ **622 AD** | ⭐ **LUNAR** | ⭐ **Previously called `Amulfil`**; renamed to mark the Prophet's ***hijrat*** from **Mecca to Madina in the 52nd year of his life**. ⭐⭐ **354 days; THE DAY BEGINS AT SUNSET.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The difference from the solar year is NEVER adjusted — so its months have NO relation to the seasons at all.** ⭐ **Four sacred months: the 1st, 7th, 11th and 12th.** *(Ramadan is the **ninth**; Muharram the first, when business and travel are prohibited.)* |

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The `SAKA SAMVAT` is India's NATIONAL CALENDAR** — a fact worth pairing with "there is no national language" *(→ [Ch.11 §5](11_Languages_in_India.md))*.

# 📚 PART B — FAIRS AND FESTIVALS ⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐ **The chapter's four buckets: NATIONAL · RELIGIOUS · SECULAR · NORTH-EAST**, plus **FAIRS**. ⭐⭐ **Concentrate on the North-East and the fairs — the religious festivals you already know, and the NE is the acknowledged blind spot.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ North-East festivals — **the blind spot, closed**

| Festival | State | ⭐ Identifier |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Saga Dawa** | **Sikkim** | ⭐ **Buddhist; the full moon of the Tibetan month "Saga Dawa" (May–June), the "MONTH OF MERITS."** ⭐⭐ **Commemorates the Buddha's BIRTH, ENLIGHTENMENT AND PARINIRVANA — all three at once.** Three teachings observed: ⭐ ***dana* (generosity), *sila* (morality), *bhavana* (meditation)** |
| ⭐⭐ **Losoong** | **Sikkim** | ⭐ **The Sikkimese NEW YEAR, December; a HARVEST festival.** ⭐⭐ **Traditionally of the BHUTIA tribe, now also the Lepchas**; ⭐ **the locally brewed wine `CHAANG`**; ⭐ **Cham and BLACK HAT dances** at the monasteries; archery |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Bihu** | **Assam** | ⭐⭐ **THREE Bihus: `BOHAG`/Rongali (the New Year, from 14 April — the biggest), `KATI`/Kongali, `MAAGH`/Bhogali.** ⭐⭐ **Day 1 is `GORA BIHU` — cows and bulls are bathed and fed.** ⭐ **Day 2: exchange of the `GAMOSA`** *(hand-woven cotton towel)*; the dish ⭐ **`PITHA`**; the **Bihu dance** *(→ [Ch.6 §14](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Hornbill** | **Nagaland** | ⭐⭐ **A WEEK-LONG festival from 1 DECEMBER at the `KISAMA` Heritage Village**; all major Naga tribes; ⭐ **the Kohima Night Bazaar**; ⭐ **pork-eating and KING CHILLI-eating competitions** |
| ⭐⭐ **Kharchi Puja** | **Tripura** | ⭐ **Began as a ROYAL FAMILY festival**; **10 days in July**; ⭐⭐ **honours Shiva, who ordered the worship of FOURTEEN OTHER DEITIES**, housed in the **Puran Haveli, Old Agartala** |
| ⭐⭐ **Cheiraoba** | **Manipur** | ⭐ **The Manipuri NEW YEAR (April), first day of the month Sajibu**; the domestic deity ⭐ **`SANAMAHI`**. ⭐⭐⭐ **The striking custom: a person is chosen as the `CHAHITABA`, who bears RESPONSIBILITY FOR THE SINS — and the good and ill luck — OF THE WHOLE COMMUNITY FOR THE YEAR, and the year is NAMED AFTER HIM.** Selected by the **Maiba** community by horoscope |

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The fairs

| Fair | ⭐ Key facts |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Kumbh Mela** | ⭐⭐ **The largest religious gathering in the world** *(→ **UNESCO Intangible, 2017**, [UNESCO §9](04_and_10_UNESCO_Heritage_Lists.md))*. ⭐⭐⭐ **Four sites and their rivers: ALLAHABAD** *(Ganga-Yamuna-Saraswati confluence)* **· HARIDWAR** *(Ganga)* **· NASHIK-TRIMBAK** *(Godavari)* **· UJJAIN** *(Shipra)*. ⭐⭐ **Held once every 3 years by rotation, so once every 12 years at any given place**; dates set by the **zodiac positions of Sun, Moon and JUPITER**. ⭐ **At Nashik and Ujjain, when a planet is in Leo it is the `SIMHASTHA` Kumbh.** ⭐⭐ **`ARDH KUMBH` every 6th year at Haridwar and Allahabad; `MAHA KUMBH` once in 144 YEARS; `MAAGH KUMBH` annually at Allahabad (Jan–Feb)** |
| ⭐⭐ **Sonepur Mela** | ⭐ **Bihar, at the Ganga-Gandak confluence, on KARTIK POORNIMA (November)**; ⭐⭐ **one of ASIA'S LARGEST CATTLE FAIRS and the ONLY fair where ELEPHANTS are sold in large numbers** — legend has **Chandragupta Maurya** buying elephants and horses here |
| ⭐ **Pushkar Fair** | **Rajasthan**, from **Kartik Poornima**, about a week; ⭐ **one of the world's largest CAMEL and cattle fairs** |
| ⭐ **Chitra Vichitra** | ⭐ **The largest TRIBAL fair in GUJARAT** — **Gharasia** and **Bhil** tribes; ⭐⭐ **on the *amavasya* after Holi the tribal women go to the river to MOURN their departed**, and festivities begin the next day |
| **Shamlaji Fair** | **Gujarat**; **Lord Shamlaji, "the Dark Divine"**, an incarnation of Krishna/Vishnu, called ⭐ **"Kaliyo Dev"** by the **Bhils**; bathing in the **Meshno**; three weeks in November |

# 📚 PART C — MARTIAL ARTS ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The one historical fact that ties this chapter to the freedom struggle: `KALARIPAYATTU` and `SILAMBAM` — and `THANG-TA` — WERE BANNED UNDER BRITISH RULE, and resurfaced after Independence.** *That is both a Prelims answer and a Mains point about colonial cultural policy.*

| Art | State | ⭐ Identifier |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Kalaripayattu** | **Kerala** | ⭐ **Among the OLDEST; 4th c. AD**; ***kalari*** = the training hall; ⭐ **legendarily begun by sage PARASURAMA**. ⭐⭐ **NO drumming or song**; ⭐ **FOOTWORK is the key**. Techniques: **Uzhichil** *(gingili-oil massage)*, **Otta** *(an **S-shaped stick**)*, **Maipayattu** *(body exercises)*, **Puliyankam** *(sword)*, **Verumkai** *(bare-handed)*, **Angathari/Kolthari** *(metal weapons / sticks)*. ⭐ **Women practise it — the heroine `UNNIYARCHA`** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Silambam** | **Tamil Nadu** | ⭐⭐ **STAFF FENCING.** Promoted by **Pandya, Chola and Chera** kings; ⭐⭐⭐ **the sale of silambam staves to Roman, Greek and Egyptian traders is recorded in the `SILAPPADIKARAM` (2nd c. AD)**. ⭐ **Origin credited to LORD MURUGAN and sage AGASTYA**; ⭐ **travelled to MALAYSIA**. ⭐ **Four staves — a "torch" staff with lighted cloth balls, a swishing one, a clattering non-elastic one, and a short powerful one.** ⭐⭐ **Three ways to win: dispossess the opponent of his staff, count "touches", or protect a money pouch kept at the feet — and marking the opponent's FOREHEAD wins** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Thang-Ta & Sarit Sarak** | **Manipur** | ⭐ **Created by the MEITEI**; ***thang*** = **sword**, ***ta*** = **spear**; ⭐⭐ **the two together are called `HUYEN LANGLON`** — **Thang-Ta armed, SARIT SARAK unarmed**. ⭐ **17th century; used against the British, then BANNED.** ⭐⭐ **Practised three ways: purely RITUALISTIC (tantric), as spear-and-sword DANCE, and as actual FIGHTING** |
| ⭐ **Cheibi Gad-ga** | **Manipur** | ⭐ **Sword and shield — now a leather-encased STICK and leather shield**; a **7-metre circle** with two lines 2 m apart; stick **2–2.5 ft** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Pari-khanda** | **Bihar** | ⭐ **Created by RAJPUTS**; ***pari*** = **shield**, ***khanda*** = **sword**. ⭐⭐⭐ **IT FORMS THE BASIS OF THE CHHAU DANCE — all its elements are absorbed there** *(→ [Ch.6 §13](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))* |
| ⭐⭐ **Thoda** | **Himachal Pradesh** | ⭐⭐ **ARCHERY-based; held at BAISAKHI (13–14 April)**; deities **Mashoo and Durga**. ⭐ **Named after the ROUND WOODEN PIECE on the arrowhead that blunts it.** ⭐⭐ **Two teams — `PASHIS` and `SAATHIS`, believed descended from the PANDAVAS and KAURAVAS**, ~500 people each *(mostly dancers, not archers)*; ⭐ **archers aim BELOW THE KNEE — hits elsewhere score negative** |
| ⭐⭐ **Gatka** | **Punjab** | ⭐ **Performed by SIKHS**; possibly from Sanskrit ***gadha*** *(mace)*; weapons **stick, kirpan, talwar, kataar** |
| ⭐ **Mardani Khel** | **Maharashtra** *(Kolhapur)* | ⭐ **Swords, swift movement, LOW STANCES suited to hill terrain**; the ⭐ **`PATA` (sword) and `VITA` (corded lance)** |
| ⭐ **Lathi** | **Punjab & Bengal** | ⭐ **A 6–8 ft cane stick, sometimes metal-tipped** — *"one of the world's oldest weapons"*, still used by police for crowd control |
| ⭐ **Inbuan Wrestling** | **Mizoram** | ⭐ **From 1750 AD, Dungtlang village**; ⭐⭐ **strict rules — NO stepping out of the circle, NO kicking, NO knee-bending**; ⭐ **win by LIFTING the opponent off his feet**; involves catching the waist BELT |
| ⭐⭐ **Kuttu Varisai** | **Tamil Nadu** | ⭐⭐ **"EMPTY HAND COMBAT"; first mentioned in SANGAM literature (1st–2nd c. BC)**; ⭐ **the UNARMED component of Silambam**; uses **animal sets — snake, eagle, tiger, elephant, monkey** |
| ⭐ **Musti Yuddha** | **Varanasi** | ⭐ **Unarmed, resembling BOXING**; ⭐⭐ **four categories named after gods: `Jambuvanti` (locking/holding), `Hanumanti` (technical superiority), `Bhimaseni` (sheer strength), `Jarasandhi` (limb and joint breaking)** |
| **Paika Akhadha** | **Odisha** | ⭐ **Dance + combat** *(→ [Ch.6 §14](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))* |
| **Sqay** | **Kashmir** | Swords and shields |
| **Kathi Samu** | **Andhra Pradesh** | ⭐ **Mastered by the royal armies** |
| **Bandesh** | India | ⭐⭐ **Unarmed; uses lock-holds against an ARMED opponent WITHOUT KILLING him** |
| ⭐ **Malla Yuddha** | South India | ⭐ **Combat wrestling; famous practitioners include SIDDHARTHA GAUTAMA and KRISHNADEVARAYA** |
| ⭐ **Malla Khamb** | **Maharashtra, 12th c.** | ⭐ **POLE and rope; demands great concentration** |
| **Insu Knawr** | **Mizoram** | Round wooden rod, within a circle |
| **Kirip / Saldu** | **Nicobar** | ⭐ **Nicobarese wrestling** |
| ⭐ **Varma Ati** | **Tamil Nadu** | ⭐⭐ **Attacks aimed at the body's VITAL SPOTS** |

## 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | The line to take |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Martial arts and colonial cultural policy** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The strongest argument in this file.** ⭐ **Kalaripayattu, Silambam and Thang-ta were all BANNED** — the last after the British captured Manipur, precisely because **Manipuri kings had used it against them.** ⭐⭐ **Pair with the DRAMATIC PERFORMANCES ACT 1876** *(→ [Ch.7](07_08_09_Theatre_Puppetry_and_Circus.md))*: **the colonial state suppressed martial and theatrical traditions alike, because both organised bodies and crowds.** ⭐ **Then the revival after 1947 as cultural nationalism** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Martial arts as the root of performance** | ⭐⭐ **A genuinely elegant cross-chapter argument**: ⭐ **Pari-khanda IS the basis of Chhau**; **Kalaripayattu underlies Kathakali's training** *(→ [Ch.6 §7](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))*; **Thang-ta has a pure dance mode**; **Paika is both dance and combat**; and ⭐ **five folk dances are outright martial** *(→ [Ch.6 §14](06_Indian_Dance_Forms.md))*. ⭐⭐ **The claim to make: in India the line between combat training and performance was never drawn** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Calendar reform and national integration** | ⭐ **The Saka Samvat is the NATIONAL calendar**, yet Vikram Samvat governs most religious life and the Hijri calendar runs independently of the seasons. ⭐⭐ **Argue as with language** *(→ [Ch.11](11_Languages_in_India.md))*: **India adopted a single official calendar without displacing the plural ones — accommodation rather than substitution** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Festivals, ecology and the economy** | ⭐ **Most festivals are HARVEST or SEASON markers** *(Bihu, Losoong, Baisakhi/Thoda, Pai, Onam)* — which is why **Adhik Masa exists at all: to stop the lunar calendar drifting away from the seasons.** ⭐⭐ **Then the fairs as ECONOMIC institutions — Sonepur's cattle and elephants, Pushkar's camels, Chitra Vichitra's tribal handicrafts** — and the modern pressures of **tourism and commodification** |

## 🔁 Recap — in eight lines

1. **Three systems: solar (365d), lunar (354d), luni-solar.** ⭐ **The lunar year falls 11 days short.**
2. ⭐⭐ **`Adhik Masa` = the month with NO sankranti, added every 2.5 years. `Kshaya Masa` = the month with TWO, which is deleted.**
3. **1 muhurta = 48 minutes; a tithi is 23 minutes shorter than a divasa; the Panchanga's five limbs are tithi, vaara, nakshatra, yoga, karana.**
4. ⭐⭐ **Vikram Samvat: 56 BC, lunar, not used in Bengal. Saka Samvat: 78 AD, luni-solar, 365 days, begins 22 March — India's NATIONAL calendar. Hijri: 622 AD, lunar, never adjusted, day begins at sunset.**
5. **Four Yugas descending from Satya to the present Kali (from 3102 BC); four = one Mahayuga = 4.32 million years.**
6. ⭐ **North-East festivals: Saga Dawa and Losoong (Sikkim), Bihu (Assam, three of them), Hornbill (Nagaland, 1 Dec), Kharchi Puja (Tripura, 14 deities), Cheiraoba (Manipur, the Chahitaba who bears the year's sins).**
7. ⭐⭐ **Kumbh at four sites; every 12 years at each; Ardh every 6; MAHA Kumbh once in 144 years.**
8. ⭐⭐ **Martial arts: Kalaripayattu (Kerala, footwork), Silambam (TN, staff), Thang-Ta (Manipur), Pari-khanda (Bihar — the basis of Chhau), Thoda (HP, archery), Gatka (Punjab) — and the British banned the first three.**
