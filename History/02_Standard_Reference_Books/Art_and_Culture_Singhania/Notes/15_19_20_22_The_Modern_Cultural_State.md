# 📚 PART A — LAW AND CULTURE ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three constitutional provisions

| Article | ⭐ What it does |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Article 29** — *Protection of Interests of Minorities* | ⭐ *"Any section of citizens… having a **distinct language, script or culture** of its own shall have the **right to conserve the same**."* ⭐⭐ **Also: no citizen may be denied assistance from a state-maintained institution on grounds of religion, caste, language or race.** *Protects tribal populations and numerically small groups such as the Parsis* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Article 49** — *Protection of Monuments and Places and Objects of National Importance* | ⭐⭐ **A DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLE — an OBLIGATION OF THE STATE** to protect every monument, place or object of artistic or historic interest, ⭐ **saving those declared of national importance from "spoliation, disfigurement, destruction, removal, disposal or export."** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Article 51A(f)** — *Fundamental Duty* | ⭐⭐ **A duty of every CITIZEN "to value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture."** |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE STRUCTURE IS THE EXAM POINT: Article 29 is a FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT (of minorities), Article 49 is a DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLE (a duty of the STATE), and Article 51A(f) is a FUNDAMENTAL DUTY (of the CITIZEN). One subject, three different constitutional instruments — that classification is worth more than the text of any of them.**

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The statutes — in order

| Act | ⭐ What it did |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Indian Treasure Trove Act, 1878** | ⭐ Treasure found accidentally must be **declared to the District Collector or nearest Government treasury**; ⭐ **penalties for concealing, altering or failing to report**; the landowner who fails to share a percentage faced **six months' jail or fine or both** |
| ⭐⭐ **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act, 1904** | ⭐ Aimed at monuments in **private/individual custody** — a **government-owner agreement**; ⭐⭐ **the owner may not add to, demolish, alter or disfigure**; ⭐ **if the land is sold, the government has FIRST RIGHT OF PURCHASE.** *Amended 1932* |
| ⭐⭐ **Antiquities (Export Control) Act, 1947** | ⭐⭐ Passed as the British were **leaving with hoarded artefacts**; ⭐ **the Director General must license any export**, and ⭐⭐ **his decision on whether an object IS an antiquity is BINDING** |
| ⭐ **Declaration of National Importance Act, 1951** | ⭐ **Re-declared the 1904 monuments as of national importance; added ~450 sites to the original list** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act (AMASR), 1958** | ⭐⭐ **The principal statute**, broadening cover to urban and rural archaeological settlements and to **physical artefacts — sculptures, carvings**. ⭐ **Central Government may declare any ancient or medieval monument of national importance; the DG may take guardianship, purchase or lease it, acquire antiquities, control their movement, and levy penalties** |
| ⭐⭐ **AMASR (Amendment and Validation) Act, 2010** | The current amending law |
| ⭐⭐ **Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972** | ⭐ **Directs the ASI to PREVENT THE ILLEGAL EXPORT of Indian antiquities** |
| ⭐ **Delivery of Books (Public Libraries) Act, 1954** | ⭐ **Publishers must deliver a copy of every book**; defines "book" and "newspaper" |

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Censorship — the Cinematograph regime

| | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Indian Cinematograph Act, 1952** | Constitutes the ⭐⭐ **Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC)** — ⭐ **originally set up in 1950 as the Central Board of Film CENSORS, renamed under the 1952 Act**; under the **Ministry of Information and Broadcasting**; ⭐ **head office MUMBAI**, regional offices at Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bangalore, Guwahati, Cuttack, Thiruvananthapuram, Hyderabad. ⭐ **Board of 12–25 members plus a Chairman** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **The federal split** | ⭐⭐ **CERTIFICATION is a CENTRAL subject; ENFORCEMENT of censorship lies with the STATE governments.** *A clean federalism point* |
| ⭐⭐ **The four certificates** | ⭐ **`U`** *(universal)* and **`A`** *(adults only)* from 1952; ⭐⭐ **`UA`** *(parental guidance under 12)* and ⭐⭐ **`S`** *(specialised audiences — doctors, engineers)* **added by the Cinematograph (Certification) Rules, 1983** |
| 🔴⭐⭐⭐ **FCAT** | Created under **s.5D** to hear appeals against CBFC decisions — ⭐⭐⭐ **but ABOLISHED in April 2021. Appeals now lie to the HIGH COURTS.** *The book predates this and is wrong on it* |

# 📚 PART B — CINEMA ⭐⭐⭐

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ The origin story — a dated sequence

| Year | ⭐ Event |
|:--:|---|
| **1896** | ⭐⭐ **The LUMIÈRE BROTHERS exhibit six soundless short films in BOMBAY** |
| **1897** | The first film shot in India — ***Coconut Fair and Our Indian Empire*** |
| ⭐ **1899** | ⭐⭐ **The first venture BY AN INDIAN — `HARISHCHANDRA BHATVADEKAR` ("SAVE DADA")**, two shorts shown on an **Edison Projecting Kinetoscope** |
| **1900** | ⭐ **Major Warwick opens India's FIRST CINEMA HOUSE, in MADRAS** |
| **1903** | **Hiralal Sen**, *Indian Life and Scenes* |
| **1907** | ⭐ **Jamshedjee Madan opens the ELPHINSTONE PICTURE HOUSE, Calcutta** |
| **1912** | ⭐ **First Indo-British silent collaboration — *PUNDALIK*, by N. G. Chitre and R. G. Torney** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1913** | ⭐⭐⭐ **`DADASAHEB PHALKE`'s *RAJA HARISHCHANDRA* — the first indigenous Indian feature. He is "the FATHER OF INDIAN CINEMA."** Also *Mohini Bhasmasur*, *Satyavan Savitri* |
| **1916** | **Universal Studios opens the first Hollywood agency in India** |
| **1917** | ⭐ **Phalke's *LANKA DAHAN* — the first BOX-OFFICE HIT** |
| **1918** | **Kohinoor Film Company** and **Phalke's Hindustan Cinema Films Company** |
| ⭐ **1922–23** | ⭐ **ENTERTAINMENT TAX imposed — Calcutta 1922, Bombay 1923** |

⭐ **Silent films dominated 1910–20** — ⭐⭐ **but "they were not totally mute": screenings were accompanied by LIVE sarangi, tabla, harmonium and violin.** ⭐ ***ALAM ARA*** is the landmark talkie. ⭐ **Early themes were MYTHOLOGY and HISTORY.** ⭐ **Notable early films: *Achhut Kanya* (Bombay Talkies, 1936), *Street Singer* (New Theatres, 1938), *Anarkali* (Ardeshir Irani, 1928).**

⭐⭐ **The two-cinema distinction the book draws**: **entertainment cinema** and ⭐ **"ALTERNATE" or "PARALLEL" cinema** showing everyday realities. ⭐ **Full FDI is now permitted in the film sector.**

# 📚 PART C — AWARDS ⭐⭐⭐

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bharat Ratna and the Padma awards

| | ⭐ Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Bharat Ratna** | ⭐ **"Jewel of India" — the HIGHEST civilian award; first given 1954.** ⭐⭐ **Originally for art, science, literature and public service; EXPANDED IN DECEMBER 2011 to "ANY FIELD OF HUMAN ENDEAVOUR."** ⭐ **The PM recommends to the President; NOT MORE THAN THREE in a year.** ⭐⭐ **No money — a PEEPAL-LEAF-shaped medal and a *sanad*.** ⭐ **Ranked SEVENTH in the Order of Precedence.** 🔴⭐⭐⭐ **Under ARTICLE 18(1) it CANNOT be used as a prefix or suffix to the recipient's name** |
| ⭐⭐ **Padma awards** | ⭐ **Introduced 1954; announced on REPUBLIC DAY.** ⭐⭐ **Three grades: `PADMA VIBHUSHAN` (2nd-degree honour) · `PADMA BHUSHAN` (3rd) · `PADMA SHRI` (4th).** ⭐ **Rules: a higher grade only after FIVE years; rarely posthumous; ⭐⭐ there must be "an element of PUBLIC SERVICE — excellence PLUS"; ⭐ GOVERNMENT SERVANTS (including PSU staff) ARE NOT ELIGIBLE, except DOCTORS and SCIENTISTS.** ⭐⭐ **Not awarded in 1977, 1980, and 1993–97** |

⭐⭐ **Notable Bharat Ratna facts**: ⭐ **the first three (1954) were C. Rajagopalachari, C. V. Raman and S. Radhakrishnan**; ⭐⭐ **the first POSTHUMOUS award was to LAL BAHADUR SHASTRI (1966)**; Ambedkar in **1990**; Sachin Tendulkar and C. N. R. Rao in **2014**.

⭐ **National Film Awards** *(from 1954; organised by the **Directorate of Film Festivals** since **1973**)*: ⭐⭐ **`SWARNA KAMAL` (Golden Lotus) — 5 categories** incl. Best Feature Film and the **Indira Gandhi Award for Best Debutant Director**; ⭐ **`RAJAT KAMAL` (Silver Lotus) — ~25 categories**, split into **12 Eighth-Schedule languages** and **5 non-Schedule languages** *(English, Bhojpuri, Kokborok, Monpa, Tulu)*; ⭐⭐ **plus the `DADASAHEB PHALKE` Lifetime Achievement Award.** ⭐ **Sahitya Akademi Award — instituted 1954**, by the **National Academy of Letters**.

## 🟢 Mains angles

| Angle | The line to take |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The constitutional architecture of heritage protection** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The best answer in this file, and it is a STRUCTURE argument**: heritage is protected through **three different instruments at once** — a **Fundamental Right** for minorities *(Art.29)*, a **Directive Principle** binding the State *(Art.49)*, and a **Fundamental Duty** binding the citizen *(Art.51A(f))*. ⭐⭐ **Then show the statutes descending from Art.49 — 1878 → 1904 → 1947 → 1951 → 1958 → 1972 → 2010** — and note that ⭐ **the two colonial-era Acts (1878, 1904) were about STOPPING BRITISH LOOTING, and 1947 was passed precisely as the British left.** ⭐ **The heritage law of independent India is built on anti-colonial foundations** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Censorship and free speech** | ⭐ **The 1952 Act's grounds** *(offence to a geographical area, age group, religious denomination or political group)* against **Article 19(1)(a)** and its **19(2)** restrictions. ⭐⭐ **Then the 2021 abolition of the FCAT** — argued as **reducing tribunal pluralism and pushing litigants to the High Courts**, raising cost and delay. ⭐ **Cross-link the DRAMATIC PERFORMANCES ACT 1876** *(→ [Ch.7](07_08_09_Theatre_Puppetry_and_Circus.md))* — **the colonial state censored theatre; the independent state certifies film** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Cinema as a national institution** | ⭐ **Singhania's own claim is usable**: post-1947 films *"have shaped our identity as a nation and of an individual living in India."* ⭐⭐ **Evidence**: mythological themes at the outset; the **parallel cinema** tradition; the **National Film Awards recognising 12 Eighth-Schedule AND 5 non-Schedule languages** *(→ [Ch.11](11_Languages_in_India.md))* — ⭐ **the award structure is itself a federal statement.** ⭐ **Then FDI and globalisation as the modern pressure** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Institutional capacity for heritage** | ⭐ **ASI's 24 circles and its underwater wing** against the scale of the task; the **IGNCA's six units** as a model of research-plus-dissemination; ⭐⭐ **but note the recurring failure across this whole book — Tappa nearly extinct, Paitkar dying, the Thalassery circus academy near closure, Austro-Asiatic languages endangered.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The argument: India's heritage law protects MONUMENTS well and PRACTICES badly — which is exactly the gap the 2003 UNESCO Convention was written to fill** *(→ [UNESCO §8](04_and_10_UNESCO_Heritage_Lists.md))* |

## 🔁 Recap — in six lines

1. ⭐⭐ **Three constitutional instruments: Art.29 (Fundamental RIGHT, minorities), Art.49 (DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLE, the State), Art.51A(f) (Fundamental DUTY, the citizen).**
2. **Statutes: Treasure Trove 1878 → Ancient Monuments 1904 → Antiquities Export Control 1947 → National Importance 1951 → AMASR 1958 → Antiquities and Art Treasures 1972 → AMASR Amendment 2010.**
3. ⭐ **CBFC born 1950 as the Board of CENSORS, renamed 1952; U and A from 1952, UA and S from 1983; certification central, enforcement with states; FCAT ABOLISHED 2021.**
4. ⭐⭐ **Cinema: Lumières in Bombay 1896 → Save Dada 1899 → first cinema house Madras 1900 → *Pundalik* 1912 → *Raja Harishchandra* 1913 (Phalke, the father of Indian cinema) → *Lanka Dahan* 1917, the first hit.**
5. ⭐⭐ **Bharat Ratna: 1954, max three a year, peepal-leaf medal, 7th in precedence, no prefix under Art.18(1), widened to "any field" in Dec 2011. Padma awards: three grades, Republic Day, five-year gap, no government servants except doctors and scientists.**
6. ⭐ **Institutions: ASI (Ministry of Culture, 24 circles), Crafts Council (1976, Kamaladevi), IGNCA (1985, six units — Kala Nidhi the library), AIR (414 stations, 23 languages, 146 dialects), NMML (Teen Murti House).**
