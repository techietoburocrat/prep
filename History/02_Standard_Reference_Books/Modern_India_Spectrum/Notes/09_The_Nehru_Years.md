# 🌐 PART A — FOREIGN POLICY'S ROOTS AND PANCHSHEEL'S REAL ORIGIN

## 1️⃣ Pre-1947 foundations ⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **A striking fact most candidates never learn: India's foreign policy did not begin in 1947.** *At independence, **India was ALREADY a member of 51 international organisations and a signatory to ~600 treaties.*** *It had signed **Versailles** after WWI (having sent over a million soldiers); was a **founding member of the LEAGUE OF NATIONS, the ILO and the ICJ** in the 1920s; and had an **Indian High Commissioner in London from 1920.***

**⚙️ FOUR PHASES OF NATIONALIST FOREIGN POLICY, 1880-1947:**

| Phase | Character |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **1880 – WWI** | **Anti-imperialism and pan-Asian feeling** — solidarity with Russia, Ireland, Egypt, Turkey, Ethiopia, Sudan, Burma, Afghanistan; condemnation of Burma's 1885 annexation; **inspiration from Japan's industrial rise**, then condemnation of Japan joining the suppression of China's Boxer (I-Ho-Tuan) uprising; **Japan's defeat of Czarist Russia (1905) "exploded the myth of European superiority"** |
| **WWI** | Initial cooperation, hoping for reciprocal democratic gains; **1920 — Congress urged people NOT to join the army for the West**; **1925 — condemned Indian troops sent against Sun Yat-sen's Chinese nationalists** |
| ⭐⭐ **1920s-30s** | **Identifying with socialists** — Nehru's 1926-27 European contacts; **1927 — Nehru at the Congress of Oppressed Nationalists, Brussels**, an honorary president alongside **Einstein**; **1927 — Nehru visited the USSR**, impressed by its planning |
| ⭐⭐ **After 1936** | **Anti-fascism** — support for Ethiopia, Spain, China, Czechoslovakia against fascist aggression; **1939, Tripuri — Congress dissociated from British pro-fascist policy**; a **medical mission under Dr Atal sent to China**; **on Palestine — sympathy for Jews, but opposed their displacement and OPPOSED Partition**, wanting a direct Jewish-Arab settlement |

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Panchsheel — the real origin story

⚠️ **Your NCERT notes give Panchsheel a passing mention in [Cl.11 Th.6](../../../01_NCERT/Class_11_Themes_in_World_History/Notes/06_Theme6_Displacing_Indigenous_Peoples.md). Nobody has ever told you where the words actually came from — and Spectrum, unusually, does.**

**📜 THE FIVE PRINCIPLES**, first formally enunciated **29 April 1954**, in the preamble to the **Agreement on Trade and Intercourse between the Tibet Region of China and India:**

1. **Mutual respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty**
2. **Mutual non-aggression**
3. **Mutual non-interference**
4. **Equality and mutual benefit**
5. **Peaceful co-existence**

⭐⭐ **On 18 June 1954**, Nehru and Chinese premier **ZHOU ENLAI** issued a joint statement elaborating Panchsheel as a framework for their own relations AND as a model for relations generally.

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHO ACTUALLY COINED IT -- AND IT WAS NOT NEHRU **
   ================================================================

   Most Indians credit Nehru. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE CREDIT SHOULD GO
   TO ZHOU ENLAI. ** On ** 31 DECEMBER 1953 ** , receiving the
   Indian delegation for the Tibetan trade talks, Zhou enunciated
   the principles as ** "five principles governing China's
   relations with foreign countries." **

   ⭐⭐ ** T.N. KAUL ** , a joint secretary at External Affairs,
   was impressed and conveyed the significance to NEHRU. Kaul
   put the principles at the OUTSET of his DRAFT AGREEMENT
   TEXT ( January 1954 ) -- but the Chinese foreign office
   ( Zhou then absent from China ) rejected this.

   ** ZHOU'S COMPROMISE, on returning: ** keep the principles
   OUT of the main text -- put them in the PREAMBLE instead.
   India accepted.

   ⭐⭐ ** TWO MONTHS LATER, on Zhou's June 1954 Delhi visit,
   Nehru and Kaul EMPHASISED the principles in their JOINT
   STATEMENT -- ** and it was THIS moment, with Indian
   sponsorship, that caught world attention.

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** SO: ZHOU PROPOUNDED THEM. KAUL PICKED THEM
   UP. NEHRU PROPAGATED THEM. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST CORRECTIVE FACT IN THE ENTIRE SPECTRUM SERIES ON FOREIGN POLICY, AND IT IS ALMOST NEVER TAUGHT.** *"Panchsheel" is popularly remembered as **Nehru's gift to the world** — and the documentary record, per Spectrum's own citation of **V.V. Paranjpe** (a former Chinese-language expert at the MEA, writing in the Hindustan Times, 2004), says the principles were **Zhou's formulation, an Indian civil servant's initiative, and Nehru's platform.*** ⭐⭐ **Neither man benefited from the eventual outcome** — Spectrum notes both **"died disenchanted men"**, their friendship undone by the 1962 war (§9). *A serious Mains answer on Panchsheel should state the origin accurately rather than repeat the popular myth.*

⭐⭐ **Panchsheel's SPREAD:** *incorporated into the **Ten Principles of the April 1955 Bandung Conference** (29 Afro-Asian countries)* → *a **UN General Assembly resolution, 11 December 1957** (co-sponsored by India, Yugoslavia, Sweden), adopted unanimously* → *accepted as **the basic principles of NAM at the 1961 Belgrade Conference.***

## 3️⃣ Non-Alignment — five criteria, five founders ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **The term gained currency AFTER Bandung (1955). It is NOT neutrality — Spectrum insists on this distinction.**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"Neutrality is equivalent to PASSIVITY… non-alignment is to have POSITIVE AND CONSTRUCTIVE opinions on international issues."*** *A neutral state stays inactive in wartime; non-alignment applies **in both war and peace.***

**⚖️ THE FIVE CRITERIA** *(laid down by the Preparatory Committee of the first NAM conference)*:

1. **An independent policy based on peaceful co-existence and non-alignment**
2. **Consistent support for national freedom movements elsewhere**
3. **NOT a member of multilateral military alliances tied to super-power conflict**
4. **Any conceded military bases must NOT be in the super-power-conflict context**
5. **Any bilateral/regional defence arrangement must NOT be in the super-power context**

**👥 FIVE PIONEERING LEADERS:**

***President Tito (Josip Broz) — Yugoslavia · President Nasser — Egypt · President Nkrumah — Ghana · President Sukarno — Indonesia · PM Nehru — India.***

⭐ **Nehru at the first NAM Summit, Belgrade 1961:** *"non-aligned may be differently interpreted, but basically… nations which object to lining up for war purposes, military blocs, military alliances… we want to throw our weight… in favour of peace."*

# 🗳️ PART B — THE FIRST GENERAL ELECTIONS, 1952

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Building the machinery from nothing

⚠️ **This entire event is absent from your NCERT archive — the largest exercise in democratic history at the time, run by a state with almost no prior electoral infrastructure.**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional provisions** | **Part XV, Articles 324-329** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Election Commission set up** | **25 January 1950** — first CEC **SUKUMAR SEN**, an ICS officer, assumed office **21 March 1950**. ⚠️ **A SINGLE-MEMBER body for long after** |
| **Article 324** | Vests the EC with superintendence, direction, control of Parliament/state legislature/President-VP elections |
| ⭐⭐ **Representation of the People Act, 1950** | Voter qualifications, electoral rolls, seat allocation to states |
| ⭐⭐ **Representation of the People Act, 1951** | Member qualifications/disqualifications, conduct of polls, expenses, counting |
| **Seats** | **489 House of the People seats — 72 for SC, 26 for ST.** State assemblies: **3,283 seats — 477 SC, 192 ST** |

**📊 SCALE:** *Voting age **21+**. **~173 million** enrolled (excl. J&K) — **~45% women** — against a **1951 census population of ~357 million** (excl. J&K): **49% of the total population enrolled as voters.** First phase held **from 15 October 1951**, though the government had hoped for 1950.*

**⚠️ CHALLENGES — a rich, examinable set:**

1. **House-to-house voter registration**, hampered by ignorance/apathy, weak party organisation, inexperienced state machinery
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "NAME" PROBLEM: local custom led many women to give their identity only as "mother/wife of so-and-so," refusing to disclose their own names.** ⭐⭐ **The EC INSISTED the actual name be recorded — and REFUSED to register women who would not give one.** ⚠️ **Result: ~2-3 MILLION of ~80 million women voters went UNREGISTERED** — mostly from **Bihar, UP, Madhya Bharat, Rajasthan, Vindhya Pradesh**
3. **Partition-displaced persons** in West Bengal, Punjab, Delhi, Assam — a floating population, hard to register
4. **Political parties gave little help** with rolls — the burden fell almost entirely on state machinery
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **With over 70% illiteracy, candidates needed SYMBOLS** — painted initially **on the ballot BOXES** *(later moved to ballot papers)*; **INDELIBLE INK** introduced to prevent impersonation — *a practice that continues today*
6. ⭐⭐ **A "mushroom growth" of new parties** — **178 parties** contested; most vanished afterward, many candidates losing their deposits

**🗳️ THE CONDUCT:** *first-past-the-post; **314 single-seat + 86 two-seat + 1 three-seat constituencies (401 total, 489 seats)**; **over 224,000 polling booths**, ~1 booth per 1,000 voters; **over 2 million steel ballot boxes**; **~1 million officials.** ⭐ **Turnout 45.7%**; invalid votes only **3-4%**; **~40% of eligible women voted.***

## 5️⃣ Parties and results ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**🏛️ THE FOURTEEN NATIONAL PARTIES contesting, and new formations just before the poll:** *⭐⭐ **Shyama Prasad Mookherjee broke away to found the BHARATIYA JANA SANGH, October 1951** · **Ambedkar revived the Scheduled Castes Federation** (later the Republican Party) · **J.B. Kripalani founded the Kisan Mazdoor Praja Party** · **Lohia/JP were behind the Socialist Party** · **the (still united) communists contested, having just abandoned the Telangana insurgency.*** *(**53 parties total** in the fray; **1,874 candidates, 533 independents**.)*

**🏆 THE RESULT:**

| Party | Vote % | Seats |
|---|:--:|:--:|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Indian National Congress** | **44.99%** | **364** |
| **Communist Party of India** | 3.29% | 16 |
| **Socialist Party** | 10.59% | 12 |
| **Kisan Mazdoor Praja Party** | 5.79% | 9 |
| **Bharatiya Jana Sangh** | 3.06% | 3 |
| **Independents** | 15.9% | 37 |

⭐⭐ **Notable results:** *future PMs **Gulzari Lal Nanda and Lal Bahadur Shastri** won seats. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **DR AMBEDKAR himself LOST in Bombay (North Central), a reserved seat, to his own former assistant, Congress candidate NARAYAN SADOBA KAJROLKAR** — Ambedkar later entered via the RAJYA SABHA. **Acharya Kripalani lost in Faizabad — but his WIFE, SUCHETA KRIPALANI, WON in Delhi against the Congress candidate.*** **First Lok Sabha Speaker: G.V. MAVALANKAR.** *The first Lok Sabha was constituted 2 April 1952, ending the Constituent Assembly's role as interim legislature.* ⭐ **Congress also swept the state legislatures (2,248 seats) but did NOT get an outright majority in Madras, Travancore-Cochin, Orissa and PEPSU.**

### 🚩 7.1 The Socialist Party's long fragmentation

```
   ** 1934 ** -- Congress Socialist Party ( CSP ) formed, own
   constitution/discipline, WITHIN Congress.
   ⭐ ** 28 MARCH 1948, NASHIK ** -- disaffiliated, protesting
      Congress's rightward, authoritarian drift.
   ⭐⭐ ** SEPT 1952 ** -- CSP + KMPP MERGE -> ** PRAJA
      SOCIALIST PARTY (PSP) ** , KRIPALANI chairman, ASHOKA
      MEHTA general secretary -- LARGEST opposition, all-India
      presence.
   ⭐ ** JUNE 1953, BETUL ** -- MEHTA calls for SUPPORTING the
      ruling party (development first); rejected in favour of
      ** LOHIA's line of "EQUIDISTANCE" ** from Congress AND
      communists, and militant mass action.
   ** END 1955 ** -- Lohia's group LEAVES the PSP.
   ** 1957 ** -- JP leaves ACTIVE POLITICS for "partyless
      democracy" (Bhoodan).
   ** 1960 ** -- Kripalani leaves. ** 1963 ** -- Mehta joins
      CONGRESS, becomes Planning Commission deputy chair.
   ⭐ ** 1964 ** -- Lohia's Socialist Party MERGES with PSP ->

   ** 1965 ** -- splits AGAIN: Lohia keeps the SSP label; a
      fresh PSP forms among his critics.
```

### 🚩 7.2 The CPI split into CPI and CPM, 1964

⭐⭐ **The CPI's evolving line:** *first, India's independence dismissed as still an "agent of imperialism"; then, sovereignty accepted but government policy called pro-capitalist; **1958, Amritsar — the party declared socialism achievable through PEACEFUL, PARLIAMENTARY means**, promising civil liberties even for opposition; **1961, Vijayawada — a "struggle-plus-unity" line toward Congress.***

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FRACTURE, 1962-64:** *the **Sino-Soviet ideological split** and the **1962 Sino-Indian War** (§9) forced a choice — some communists **supported the government fully against China**; others, though critical of China's border claims, **still opposed unconditional support for Nehru** on grounds of the government's "class character." ⭐⭐ **1964: the party split into CPI** (the earlier right/centrist trend) **and CPM/CPI(M)** (the earlier left trend, more sympathetic to the Chinese position). **The CPM held the Indian state was ruled by a big bourgeoisie collaborating with foreign capital**, viewed the Constitution as **"anti-democratic,"** and believed an **agrarian revolution plus armed struggle** was necessary.*

### ⚔️ 7.3 The right — Jana Sangh and Swatantra

|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BHARATIYA JANA SANGH** | **Founded** | **21 October 1951**, driven by **SHYAMA PRASAD MOOKHERJEE** *(who had resigned from Nehru's cabinet, April 1951, over the Liaquat-Nehru Pact — see [file 04 §12](04_Integration_and_the_New_Nation.md))* |
| | **Roots** | ⭐⭐ **Per Bipan Chandra, a "creation of the RSS"**, drawing its organisational strength, centralisation and ideological homogeneity from it |

| ⭐⭐⭐ **SWATANTRA PARTY** | **Founded** | **August 1959** — **C. RAJAGOPALACHARI** (ex-Congress), **Minoo Masani, N.G. Ranga, K.M. Munshi** |
| | ⭐ **Social base, four groups** | *(i)* industrialists/business, fearing controls/quotas/nationalisation · *(ii)* **landlords, jagirdars, princes**, aggrieved at lost status · *(iii)* ex-landlord-turned-capitalist and rich/middle farmers, welcoming zamindari abolition but fearing further land reform · *(iv)* retired civil servants |

**🗺️ THE REGIONAL/COMMUNAL FIELD:** *the **Hindu Mahasabha** (Malaviya, 1915, Haridwar) declined after 1952, losing ground to the Jana Sangh; the **Muslim League** went dormant after Partition, later reviving in Tamil Nadu/Kerala; the **Akali Dal** stayed Punjab-limited; other regional parties included the **DMK, the J&K National Conference, the Jharkhand Party, the Ganatantra Parishad (Orissa), the All Parties Hill Leaders' Conference (Assam)**, plus single-state left parties — the **Forward Bloc (West Bengal)** and the **Peasants and Workers Party (Maharashtra)**.*

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The 1957 Kerala dismissal — the first use of President's Rule against an elected government

```
   ** 1957 -- KERALA ** : the CPI emerged the LARGEST single
   party; ⭐ ** E.M.S. NAMBOODIRIPAD formed India's FIRST
   DEMOCRATICALLY ELECTED COMMUNIST GOVERNMENT ** ,
   with independent support.

   ⭐⭐ ** THE TRIGGER: ** an EDUCATION BILL -- "in actuality a
   PROGRESSIVE measure" -- was FIERCELY OPPOSED by the
   CATHOLIC CHURCH, which ran many of the state's schools
   and saw it as an encroachment.

   -> the LOCAL Congress ( having lost the election ) organised
      state-wide protests and strikes; the government used
      LATHI-CHARGES and firing; many jailed.

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ ** NEHRU'S POSITION: ** privately had little objection
   to the bill; publicly stayed NEUTRAL; admonished the state
   for excessive force while trying ( and failing ) to restrain his
   own party workers.

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** JULY 1959 -- NEHRU ADVISED DISMISSAL of the
   EMS government and PRESIDENT'S RULE. ** ⚠️ ** The FIRST
   time a democratically elected government was dismissed
   under emergency powers in independent India. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE ORIGIN POINT OF A PRACTICE THAT SHAPED INDIAN FEDERALISM FOR DECADES — Article 356's use against opposition state governments — and it happened under the man usually remembered as democracy's greatest custodian.** ⭐⭐ **A fair Mains answer notes the tension directly: Nehru "succumbed to pressure from within and outside his party," per Spectrum's own words, rather than acting on pure constitutional principle.**

## 9️⃣ Planning and science under Nehru ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**📊 THE PLANNING COMMISSION:** *genesis in the **1938 National Planning Committee** (under Congress) and the **1944 Bombay Plan**. **Set up March 1950 by a SIMPLE RESOLUTION** — an **EXTRA-CONSTITUTIONAL body** — with the **PM as ex-officio chairman**. The **National Development Council (NDC), 6 August 1952**, gave final plan approval.*

| Plan | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| **First (1951-56)** | **Harrod-Domar model**; agrarian focus, dams/irrigation (**Bhakra Nangal**), land reform |
| ⭐⭐ **Second** | Under **P.C. MAHALANOBIS** — **heavy industries**, the **"socialistic pattern of society"**, tariff protection for domestic industry |
| **Third** | Similar to the Second; critics note an **"urban bias"**, industry wrongly prioritised over agriculture |

⭐ **A "mixed economy"** — Nehru's **"democratic socialism"**: state control of key heavy industry and infrastructure, private hands for most agriculture/trade/small industry. ⚠️ **Criticised from both sides** — the right for licence-permit corruption ("licence raj"), the left for underinvestment in education/health and the rise of an unaccountable salaried middle class. **Achievements: Bhakra-Nangal, DVC, Hirakud; HMT, Sindri Fertiliser, Chittaranjan and Integral Coach factories, Hindustan Antibiotics.**

**🔬 SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY:** *the **Scientific Policy Resolution, March 1958** — but institutions predated it: **National Physical Laboratory, January 1947** (India's first national lab; a network of 17 followed) · **first IIT, Kharagpur, 1952** (MIT-modelled) · ⭐⭐ **Atomic Energy Commission, August 1948, under HOMI J. BHABHA** → a separate **Department of Atomic Energy, 1954** → **India's (and Asia's) first nuclear reactor, TROMBAY, critical August 1956** · **INCOSPAR + the Thumba Rocket Launching Facility (TERLS), 1962** · **decimal coinage and metric conversion, phased 1955-62.***

# ⚔️ PART D — FOREIGN RELATIONS UNDER NEHRU

## 🔟 Kashmir, the Indus Waters, and the neighbours ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**🏔️ KASHMIR:** *Pakistan refused Kashmir's **26 October 1947 accession**; India responded with military action supported by local forces under **Sheikh Abdullah** — but **Nehru complained to the UN Security Council in January 1948**, leading to a **ceasefire, 1 January 1949**. **India agreed to a plebiscite, then withdrew the offer in 1955.** No actual war followed up to 1964.*

**💧 THE INDUS WATERS TREATY:** *Partition left India **5 of 28 million irrigated Indus-system acres**, with most western-river flow going to the Arabian Sea while Pakistani canals depended on eastern rivers through East Punjab. **World Bank-mediated interim agreement, 17 April 1959** → **the comprehensive INDUS WATERS TREATY, 19 September 1960, Karachi.***

**🏔️ TIBET, PANCHSHEEL, AND THE ROAD TO WAR:**

```
   1949 -- India recognised MAO's new government (having
          first recognised Chiang Kaishek's Nationalist China).
   1950 -- CHINESE ARMY OCCUPIES TIBET.
   1954 -- Nehru's agreement with China ( THE ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
          PANCHSHEEL AGREEMENT ) FORMALISED Chinese
          occupation of Tibet.
   1959 -- a Tibetan uprising is suppressed; ⭐ DALAI LAMA
          FLEES TO INDIA. China uses this as a pretext to occupy
          LONGJU and 12,000 SQ MI of Ladakh.
   Apr 1960 -- Chou En-lai visits Delhi to negotiate the
          border; talks fail.
   Oct 1962 -- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ CHINA ATTACKS in NEFA (Arunachal)
          AND LADAKH. India suffers a MILITARY DEBACLE --
          the Chinese held geographical advantage and superior
          arms.
   Nov 1962 -- China DECLARES A UNILATERAL WITHDRAWAL
          -- but KEEPS a large part of Ladakh, a strategic link
          between Sinkiang and southern China.
   Dec 1962 -- Afro-Asian mediation ( Indonesia, Cambodia,
          Burma, UAR, Ghana, Ceylon ) at COLOMBO fails.
   1964 -- China tests its first nuclear device.
```

**⚡ SIX CONSEQUENCES:**

1. **A blow to India's self-respect**
2. ⭐⭐ **Non-alignment itself came under question**
3. **Congress lost three by-elections in a row; Nehru faced his FIRST NO-CONFIDENCE MOTION**
4. **The Third Five-Year Plan was hit as resources went to defence**
5. ⭐⭐ **A foreign-policy REALIGNMENT toward the US/UK**, who had offered support during the crisis
6. ⭐⭐⭐ **Pakistan, encouraged by India's debacle and covertly aided by China, would attack in 1965**

**🗺️ THE OTHER NEIGHBOURS:** *⭐ **Nepal Treaty, July 1950** (sovereignty/territorial integrity recognised, mutual notification commitment) · **Bhutan Treaty, August 1949** (India non-interference domestically; Bhutan guided by India on external relations) · **Sri Lanka** — the **1958 Tamil-Sinhala riots** drew Indian sympathy for Sri Lankan Tamils, but India held it an internal matter and kept friendly relations; **Sri Lanka itself supported NAM and joined no military alliance.***

# 🏛️ PART E — AFTER NEHRU: THE SHASTRI YEARS

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Lal Bahadur Shastri, June 1964 – January 1966

⚠️ **A total gap, and Spectrum treats him with unusual warmth and detail — worth reading in full, since he rarely gets more than a sentence anywhere else.**

**🤝 THE SUCCESSION:** *Nehru died **May 1964**; **GULZARILAL NANDA** served as interim PM. The **"SYNDICATE"** — a Congress power group led by party president **K. KAMARAJ** — steered the choice between **MORARJI DESAI** (senior, able, but rigid/right-leaning) and **SHASTRI** (mild, flexible, widely trusted). **Desai chose not to contest**; Shastri became PM, **9 June 1964.**

**👤 EARLY LIFE:** *born **2 October 1904, UP**; **dropped his caste surname "Srivastava"** in opposition to caste; **"Shastri"** was an academic title from **Kashi Vidyapeeth (1928)**. ⭐⭐ **Opposed dowry — on marrying Lalita Devi (1928), accepted only "five yards of khadi and a spinning wheel."** Joined Non-Cooperation (1921, arrested but released as a minor); took part in **Salt Satyagraha, Individual Satyagraha, and Quit India**; imprisoned repeatedly, reading reformist and Western philosophy in jail. **UP Legislative Assembly, 1937.**

**📈 POST-INDEPENDENCE CAREER:** *UP minister for **Police and Transport** under **G.B. Pant** — appointed **women bus conductors**; ordered **water jets instead of lathis** for crowd control · **AICC General Secretary, 1951**, nominated to Rajya Sabha · **Railways Minister — resigned 1956, taking moral responsibility for a rail accident** · **Home Minister, 1961** — set up the **K. SANTHANAM Committee on Prevention of Corruption**, whose recommendations created the **CENTRAL VIGILANCE COMMISSION.***

⭐⭐ **AS PM:** *retained External Affairs and Atomic Energy briefly (later handed External Affairs to **Swaran Singh**); drew **INDIRA GANDHI** into his cabinet as **Information and Broadcasting Minister**; became the **FIRST PM WITH A PERSONAL SECRETARY (L.K. JHA)**, laying the foundation of the modern PMO.*

**📜 QUOTED:** *"There comes a time in the life of every nation when it stands at the cross-roads of history…" — and on secularism: **"Whereas Pakistan proclaims herself an Islamic State… we Indians have the freedom to follow whatever religion we may choose… each of us is as much an Indian as the other."***

**⚡ THREE MAJOR CRISES:**

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **The 1965 anti-Hindi riots**, especially violent in **Madras state** — resolved by Shastri's assurance that English would continue **as long as non-Hindi states wanted it**; the army was called to quell riots in places
2. **Widespread food shortages**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The second war with Pakistan**

**💰 ECONOMIC IDEAS — Spectrum's own claim, worth quoting:** *Shastri tried to **decentralise decision-making away from the Planning Commission** to line ministries, and in **1965 announced a reconsideration of economic controls**, easing regulation in steel and cement. ⚠️ **His economic team (L.K. Jha, I.G. Patel, Dharma Vira, S. Bhoothalingam) leaned toward agricultural modernisation and private-sector freedom** — and he had **decided IN PRINCIPLE on rupee devaluation**, though it was **Indira Gandhi who implemented it.** ⭐⭐⭐ **P.N. Dhar's judgement, quoted by Spectrum: Shastri might have pursued "an agenda of economic reform of the kind taken up only twenty-five years later" — i.e., under NARASIMHA RAO in 1991.**

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SLOGAN: *"JAI JAWAN JAI KISAN"*** — linking the soldier's security to the farmer's, given during the war.

## 1️⃣2️⃣ The 1965 War and Tashkent ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   April 1965 -- Pakistan tests India in the RANN OF KACHCHH;
   early clashes go Pakistan's way; ceasefire brokered by
   BRITAIN.

   ⭐⭐⭐ August 1965 -- ** "OPERATION GRANDSLAM" ** : Pakistan,
   reading Kashmir unrest (encouraged by ZULFIKAR ALI
   BHUTTO) and India's post-1962 weakness, sent infiltrators
   into Kashmir to spark an uprising via the one significant
   overland route.

   ⭐⭐ SHASTRI'S RESPONSE: unlike Nehru, DECISIVE -- ordered
   the Indian army to CROSS THE CEASEFIRE LINE and seal
   the passes. Defence minister ⭐ Y.B. CHAVAN backed him.

   September 1965 -- full war: Indian tanks in a THREE-
   PRONGED attack toward LAHORE AND SIALKOT; Lahore came
   within Indian artillery range; the IAF's first combat use
   since independence.

   23 September 1965 -- UN-brokered CEASEFIRE.
```

**🤝 TASHKENT, JANUARY 1966:** *a Soviet-sponsored peace conference, mediated by **PRESIDENT ALEXEI KOSYGIN**. **AYUB KHAN and SHASTRI signed the TASHKENT DECLARATION, 10 January 1966** — mutual troop withdrawal to pre-August-1965 positions by **25 February 1966**, non-interference, restored diplomatic relations, discussion of refugee/POW issues, continued high-level contact.* ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **CRITICISED IN INDIA for lacking a no-war pact and any commitment against Pakistani "guerrilla aggression" in Kashmir; in Pakistan received even more angrily — BHUTTO broke with Ayub Khan over it, later forming his own party.**

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SHASTRI'S DEATH:** *early morning, **11 January 1966** — the day AFTER signing — **Shastri died of a heart attack at Tashkent.** ⚠️ **Persistent rumours of poisoning, and CIA involvement, have never been substantiated.*** **INDIRA GANDHI became the third Prime Minister on 19 January 1966.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CLOSE ANY ANSWER ON THIS PERIOD WITH THE CONTRAST BETWEEN 1962 AND 1965.** *Nehru, facing China, hesitated and turned to allies; Shastri, facing Pakistan three years later, **acted decisively and without hesitation** — a genuinely different leadership style that restored, in the space of one war, some of the military self-respect the 1962 debacle had cost. ⭐⭐ **His death within a day of the peace he had just secured is one of the more poignant near-misses in independent India's political history — a PM who might, on Spectrum's own account, have anticipated 1991 by a quarter-century.***

### 🔴 Prelims traps

| ⚠️ The claim that looks right | ✅ The truth |
|---|---|
| *Panchsheel was Nehru's original formulation* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The principles were first enunciated by ZHOU ENLAI on 31 December 1953; T.N. Kaul brought them to Nehru's attention, and Nehru propagated them internationally from June 1954** |
| *Non-alignment means neutrality in international affairs* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Explicitly NOT — neutrality is passive; non-alignment involves active, constructive positions on issues, in both war and peace** |
| *The first Chief Election Commissioner was appointed after the 1952 elections* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SUKUMAR SEN took office on 21 March 1950, well before the 1952 polls, which his office spent nearly two years preparing** |
| *All eligible women were registered to vote in 1952* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **2-3 million of ~80 million women went unregistered because local custom kept them from disclosing their own names, and the EC refused to register an unnamed voter** |
| *Dr. Ambedkar won his Lok Sabha seat in the 1952 elections* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He LOST, in a reserved Bombay constituency, to his own former assistant — and entered Parliament via the Rajya Sabha instead** |
| *The Bharatiya Jana Sangh and the Swatantra Party held similar positions on non-alignment* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Jana Sangh's core issue was cultural nationalism and anti-Pakistan sentiment; it was the SWATANTRA PARTY that explicitly denounced non-alignment and sought closer US ties** |
| *The CPI-CPM split occurred over the Telangana insurgency* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It occurred in 1964, over the Sino-Soviet split and differing responses to the 1962 Sino-Indian War — decades after Telangana** |
| *The 1957 Kerala communist government was dismissed by the state governor independently* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Nehru himself advised the dismissal and imposition of President's Rule, in July 1959, under pressure from within and outside his own party** |
| *The Planning Commission was established by a constitutional amendment* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **By a SIMPLE RESOLUTION of the Government of India, March 1950 — it was an extra-constitutional body** |
| *China attacked India in 1962 in Ladakh alone* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **In BOTH NEFA (Arunachal Pradesh) and Ladakh** |
| *China's 1962 withdrawal returned all occupied territory* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **China withdrew UNILATERALLY in November 1962 but KEPT a large part of Ladakh** |
| *Lal Bahadur Shastri died in 1965* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He died on 11 JANUARY 1966, the day after signing the Tashkent Declaration** |
| *The Tashkent Declaration was negotiated directly between India and Pakistan without mediation* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It was mediated by Soviet President Alexei Kosygin at Tashkent** |

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **INDIAN FOREIGN POLICY DID NOT BEGIN IN 1947 — 51 international memberships and ~600 treaties already existed at independence**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PANCHSHEEL WAS ZHOU'S FORMULATION, RELAYED BY T.N. KAUL, PROPAGATED BY NEHRU — not Nehru's own invention, per Spectrum's own cited source**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NON-ALIGNMENT ≠ NEUTRALITY — five criteria, five founding leaders (Tito, Nasser, Nkrumah, Sukarno, Nehru)**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1952'S FIRST ELECTION: 173 MILLION VOTERS, A SINGLE-MEMBER EC UNDER SUKUMAR SEN, AND 2-3 MILLION WOMEN LOST TO THE ROLLS OVER A NAMING CUSTOM**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AMBEDKAR LOST HIS OWN 1952 SEAT TO HIS FORMER ASSISTANT**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SOCIALIST AND COMMUNIST STREAMS BOTH FRAGMENTED REPEATEDLY OVER THE SAME QUESTION — HOW CLOSE TO STAND TO CONGRESS**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1959, KERALA — NEHRU HIMSELF ADVISED THE FIRST DISMISSAL OF AN ELECTED STATE GOVERNMENT, UNDER PARTY PRESSURE**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE PLANNING COMMISSION WAS AN EXTRA-CONSTITUTIONAL BODY CREATED BY A SIMPLE RESOLUTION**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE 1962 WAR: SIX CONSEQUENCES, FROM A LOST NO-CONFIDENCE VOTE TO A REALIGNMENT TOWARD THE WEST TO PAKISTAN'S EMBOLDENED 1965 ATTACK**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SHASTRI ACTED DECISIVELY IN 1965 WHERE NEHRU HAD HESITATED IN 1962 — AND DIED THE DAY AFTER SIGNING TASHKENT, 11 JANUARY 1966**
