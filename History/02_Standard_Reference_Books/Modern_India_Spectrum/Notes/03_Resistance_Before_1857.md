# 🧭 PART A — THE FRAME

## 1️⃣ Who "the people" were, and the four forms ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **Read this section before any of the lists. Everything else in the chapter is an instance of it, and the frame is what UPSC actually tests in Mains.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** WHO IS "THE PEOPLE" IN "PEOPLE'S RESISTANCE"? **
   ================================================================

   Spectrum's answer is deliberately broad -- ** every section of
   Indian society that the alien rule damaged ** :

     - ** PEASANTS **            - ** ARTISANS **
     - ** TRIBALS **             - ** RULING CLASSES ** ( active
                                    ** or dispossessed ** )
     - ** MILITARY PERSONNEL **  -- both those ** under the
       Company ** and the ** DEMOBILISED SOLDIERS of ex-rulers **
     - ** RELIGIOUS LEADERS **   ( Hindu ** and ** Muslim )

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE KEY SENTENCE, AND IT IS QUOTABLE: ** the
      interests of these resistances ** DIFFERED ** -- each section
      had ** DIFFERENT GRIEVANCES ** -- but they ** CONVERGED ON A
      COMMON OBJECTIVE: TO END BRITISH RULE. **
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FOUR FORMS — memorise this as a set of four, because it is the skeleton of the whole chapter.** *Spectrum takes **three** from **BIPAN CHANDRA** — **CIVIL REBELLIONS · TRIBAL UPRISINGS · PEASANT MOVEMENTS** — and then says explicitly that it has **added a fourth, MILITARY REVOLTS**, "to make the study of people's resistance more comprehensive."* ⚠️ **That attribution is worth carrying into an answer: the three-fold classification is Bipan Chandra's, the fourth is the compiler's.**

**🏙️ AND NOTE THE URBAN STRAND, which most students miss entirely** — three tax agitations in which *"artisans, petty shopkeepers, and the urban poor fought together with the prosperous urban gentry"*:

| Year | Place | Against |
|:--:|---|---|
| **1810** | ⭐ **BENARES** | A **HOUSE TAX** |
| **1814** | **SURAT** | The **SALT DUTY** |
| **1816** | ⭐ **BAREILLY** | **POLICE TAX and MUNICIPAL TAXES** *(this one turned into a full rising — see §4)* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why this little table earns its place: it is the one piece of evidence in the chapter that the resistance was NOT purely rural and NOT purely elite-led.** *A house tax in Benares put the rich and the poor of one city on the same side — which is exactly the coalition the Congress would spend the 1920s trying to build deliberately.*

## 2️⃣ Genesis: why colonial extraction produced revolt where pre-colonial extraction had not ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **Do not skip this. It answers the obvious objection — *"Indian rulers also taxed harshly; why did THIS produce a century of revolt?"***

```
   ================================================================
   ** SPECTRUM'S ANSWER, IN THREE MOVES **
   ================================================================

   (1) ** PROTEST WAS NOT NEW. ** In pre-colonial India, protests
       against rulers and officials were ** "NOT UNCOMMON" ** --
       high revenue demand, corrupt officials, harsh attitudes.

   (2) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** WHAT WAS NEW WAS THAT NOBODY WAS LISTENING. **
       "There was ** NO ONE TO HEAR THEIR GRIEVANCES ** or pay
       attention to their problems. The Company was ** MERELY
       INTERESTED IN EXTRACTING REVENUE. ** "

   (3) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE LAW HAD CHANGED SIDES. ** "The colonial
       law and judiciary ** SAFEGUARDED THE INTEREST OF THE
       GOVERNMENT AND ITS COLLABORATORS ** -- the landlords, the
       merchants and money-lenders."

       ** Thus the people, left with no options, chose to take up
          arms and defend themselves. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE ARGUMENT, AND IT IS BETTER THAN IT LOOKS.** *A pre-colonial peasant facing an extortionate official could appeal **upwards** — to the king, to custom, to the moral obligation of the ruler. ⭐ **[Cl.12 Th.8's petitions from the Rajasthan and Maharashtra archives](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md) are precisely that channel in working order — "the right to the basic minimum for survival was SANCTIONED BY CUSTOM."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Colonial rule did not merely raise the demand; it CLOSED THE APPEAL. When the only remaining channel is armed, the response will be armed.** *(That single cross-link between Theme 8 and this chapter is worth more in a Mains answer than any list of revolt names.)*

**⚙️ THE FIVE CAUSATIVE FACTORS — Spectrum's own list, and it is examinable as a list:**

| # | Factor |
|:--:|---|
| **1** | ⭐ **Colonial LAND REVENUE SETTLEMENTS**, heavy new taxes, **eviction of peasants**, and **encroachment on tribal lands** |
| **2** | **Exploitation in rural society** through the growth of **intermediary revenue collectors, tenants and money-lenders** |
| **3** | ⭐ **Expansion of revenue administration OVER TRIBAL LANDS** — costing tribals their hold on **agricultural and forest land** |
| **4** | ⭐⭐ **Promotion of British manufactures + heavy duties on Indian industry** *(especially export duties)* → **devastation of handloom and handicrafts** |
| **5** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The knock-on: destroyed industry pushed workers BACK INTO AGRICULTURE**, increasing **pressure on land** |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FACTOR 5 IS THE ONE TO REMEMBER, BECAUSE IT IS THE MECHANISM AND NOT JUST A COMPLAINT.** *Deindustrialisation and agrarian distress are not two separate grievances — **they are one process seen at two ends**. The weaver who cannot sell cloth becomes a labourer competing for land, which pushes rents up for the peasant who was already struggling to pay revenue.* ⭐ **[Cl.10 Ch.4 *(The Age of Industrialisation)*](../../../01_NCERT/Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/04_The_Age_of_Industrialisation.md) gives you the weaver's half; this gives you where he went.**

## 3️⃣ Civil uprisings: what the word "civil" is doing here ⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **A vocabulary trap.** *"Civil" here does **NOT** mean *civilian* in the modern sense, and it does **NOT** mean *civil disobedience*. Spectrum defines it as **everything not related to defence/military** — and then narrows it further to a specific social type.*

```
   ================================================================
   ** WHO ACTUALLY LED A "CIVIL" UPRISING **
   ================================================================

   ** AT THE CENTRE ** -- the ** ERSTWHILE POWER-WIELDING
      CLASSES ** :
        - ** DEPOSED NATIVE RULERS ** or their descendants
        - ** FORMER ZAMINDARS and LANDLORDS **
        - ** POLIGARS ** ( see PART C )
        - ** EX-RETAINERS AND OFFICIALS ** of conquered kingdoms
        - sometimes ** RELIGIOUS LEADERS **

   ** THE MASS SUPPORT ** came from :
        - ** RACK-RENTED PEASANTS **
        - ** UNEMPLOYED ARTISANS **
        - ** DEMOBILISED SOLDIERS **

   ⚠️ ** So a civil uprising is a DISPOSSESSED ELITE at the head
      of a DISTRESSED POPULATION. ** Both halves are necessary --
      the elite supplied leadership, forts and legitimacy; the
      distressed supplied numbers.
```

**📋 THE FIVE MAJOR CAUSES OF CIVIL UPRISINGS** *(distinct from the five general factors in §2 — ⚠️ UPSC has room to ask either)*:

1. **Rapid changes** in economy, administration and land revenue that **went against the people**
2. ⭐ **Zamindars and poligars who had LOST CONTROL of land and revenues had personal scores to settle**
3. ⭐⭐ **WOUNDED STATUS** — their *"ego… was hurt due to being sidelined in rank by government officials and a new class comprising of merchants and money-lenders"*
4. ⭐⭐ **The ruin of handicrafts impoverished artisans — worsened by the DISAPPEARANCE OF THEIR TRADITIONAL PATRONS** *(princes, chieftains, zamindars)*
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **THE PRIESTLY CLASSES INSTIGATED REBELLION — because they were DEPENDENT on the traditional landed and bureaucratic elite.** *"The fall of zamindars and feudal lords directly affected the priestly class"*
6. **The foreign character of the rulers** and their **contemptuous treatment of the natives**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CAUSES 3, 4 AND 5 ARE ONE CHAIN, AND SEEING IT IS WORTH A PARAGRAPH IN ANY ANSWER.** *When a zamindar falls, he does not fall alone. He stops commissioning the goldsmith, stops feeding the temple, stops paying the pundit, stops retaining the swordsman. ⭐⭐ **A single displacement at the top of a locality dismantles the entire patronage economy beneath it — and every one of those people now has a grievance and a leader in the same man.*** *(This is why the revolts look "feudal" and yet are genuinely popular: the dependants were real dependants.)*

**⚠️ THE TWO GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS — and both are damning:**

| # | Characteristic | Why it matters |
|:--:|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The leaders were BACKWARD-LOOKING and TRADITIONAL** — *"their basic objective was to **RESTORE EARLIER FORMS OF RULE AND SOCIAL RELATIONS**"* | ⚠️ **They were not fighting for a new India; they were fighting to get the old one back.** *This is the single most important qualification to make in any answer that calls these "early nationalism"* |
| **2** | ⭐⭐⭐ **LOCAL causes, LOCALISED consequences** | The reason forty risings never became one rising — see **§21** |

## 5️⃣ The seven that deserve more than a row ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 🕉️ 5.1 The Sanyasi Revolt (1763–1800) — and the novel that came out of it

**Who they actually were:** ⚠️ **not wandering mystics.** *Spectrum is explicit: they were **"originally peasants, even some evicted from land"**, joined by **dispossessed small zamindars, disbanded soldiers and the rural poor**.* They **raided Company factories and treasuries** and fought Company forces; it took **prolonged action by WARREN HASTINGS** to subdue them.

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | ⭐ **The disastrous famine of 1770** + the harsh new economic order |
| **Also called** | **The FAKIR REBELLION** — because ⭐⭐ ***"EQUAL PARTICIPATION OF HINDUS AND MUSLIMS characterised the uprisings"*** |
| **Leaders** | **Majnum (Majnu) Shah · Chirag Ali · Musa Shah · Bhawani Pathak ·** ⭐⭐⭐ **DEBI CHAUDHURANI** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The literary afterlife** | ***ANANDAMATH*, the semi-historical novel by BANKIM CHANDRA CHATTOPADHYAY, IS BASED ON THE SANYASI REVOLT.** Bankim also wrote ***DEVI CHAUDHURANI*** — because *"he saw the importance of women too taking up the struggle"* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE HIGHEST-VALUE SINGLE FACT IN PART B, AND IT CLOSES A LOOP IN YOUR OWN NOTES.** *[Cl.10 Ch.2](../../../01_NCERT/Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/02_Nationalism_in_India.md) teaches you **Bankim, *Vande Mataram* and the Bharat Mata imagery** as nationalist symbols — but not **where the song came from**. ⭐⭐ ***Vande Mataram* was written into *Anandamath*, and *Anandamath* is about THIS REVOLT.*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the anthem of the Swadeshi movement (1905) is a novel (1882) about a peasant-sanyasi rising (1770s) — a hundred and thirty years of memory doing political work.** *That sentence is a ready-made opening for any question on **"cultural nationalism"** or on **how the memory of early resistance was later mobilised**.*

⭐⭐ **And note DEBI CHAUDHURANI on her own account** — Spectrum says her participation *"recognises the women's role in early resistances against the British."* ⚠️ **She is the FIRST of THREE women you now have for this period; the other two — VELU NACHIYAR and KUYILI — are in PART C, and none of the three is in your NCERT notes.**

### 🌾 5.2 Gorakhpur, Basti and Bahraich (1781) — what an *izara* was, and the experiment behind it

**The device:** ⭐ *Warren Hastings, needing money for the **Maratha and Mysore wars**, put **ENGLISH OFFICERS IN AS *IZARADARS* (revenue farmers) IN AWADH**.* **Major Alexander Hannay** took the *izara* of **Gorakhpur and Bahraich** in 1778 for **₹22 lakh for one year**.

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SPECTRUM'S MOST REVEALING SENTENCE IN THE WHOLE CHAPTER: it was *"in fact, a SECRET EXPERIMENT by the Company to see for itself just HOW MUCH SURPLUS MONEY WAS ACCESSIBLE IN PRACTICE."*** ⭐ **Read that as a stress test on a province — deliberately push the demand to the point of failure and record where it breaks.** *It broke fast: a region **"which had been in a flourishing state under the Nawab"** was **"panic-stricken"**, and within **weeks** of the 1781 rising **all of Hannay's subordinates were killed or besieged by zamindari guerrilla forces.*** ⭐⭐ **The rebellion was suppressed — and yet HANNAY WAS DISMISSED AND HIS IZARA FORCIBLY REMOVED.** *The Company punished its own man because the experiment had produced its answer.*

### 🐅 5.3 Pazhassi Raja (1793–1805) — the guerrilla war Malabar remembers

| Phase | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Background** | The **Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–92)** extended English paramountcy over **Kottayam** — ⚠️ **in violation of an earlier 1790 agreement that had recognised Kottayam's independence** |
| **The insult** | The English installed ⭐ **VIRA VARMA, the raja's own UNCLE**, as Raja of Kottayam — who then **levied exorbitant taxes** to meet the Company's revenue target |
| **Phase 1 (1793–97)** | **Mass peasant resistance under Pazhassi Raja**, fought by ⭐ **GUERRILLA WARFARE**; a **peace treaty in 1797** |
| **Phase 2 (1800–05)** | A dispute over ⭐ **WAYANAD** reopened the war. He raised **a large force of NAIRS, supplemented by MAPPILAS and PATHANS** — ⚠️ **the Pathans being Tipu's DEMOBILISED SOLDIERS, unemployed after 1799** |
| **End** | **November 1805** — died **in a gun-fight at MAVILA TODU**, near the present **Kerala–Karnataka border** |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two things make Pazhassi examinable beyond his name.** *(a)* ⭐ **He fought HYDER AND TIPU FIRST and the BRITISH AFTER** — *"apart from resisting Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan, Kerala Varma fought against the British between 1793 and 1805"* — which quietly demolishes any neat "Indians vs British" framing. *(b)* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **His army was built out of TIPU'S UNEMPLOYED SOLDIERS.** *Every conquest in [file 01](01_Expansion_and_Consolidation.md) produced a **demobilised army**, and demobilised armies are the raw material of the next revolt. **That is the causal thread linking Part B of this file to the whole of file 01 — and it recurs with the GADKARIS in 1844 and the RAMOSIS in 1822.***

### 🗡️ 5.4 The Massacre of Benares (1799)

**Wazir Ali Khan**, the **fourth Nawab of Awadh**, was installed **with British help in September 1797** — and **replaced within months by his uncle SAADAT ALI KHAN II**, with a **pension at Benares**.

```
   JANUARY 1799 -- ** THE MASSACRE OF BENARES **

     Wazir Ali ** KILLED THE BRITISH RESIDENT, GEORGE FREDERICK
     CHERRY ** -- ⚠️ ** who had invited him to LUNCH. **
     His guards killed ** two other Europeans ** and attacked the
     ** MAGISTRATE OF BENARES. **

     -> raised ** several thousand men ** ; defeated by
        ** GENERAL ERSKINE **
     -> fled to ** BUTWAL ** ; given ** asylum by the ruler of
        JAIPUR **
     -> ⭐ ** ARTHUR WELLESLEY ** requested extradition
     -> ⭐⭐ ** EXTRADITED ON CONDITION that he would NEITHER BE
        HANGED NOR PUT IN FETTERS **
     -> surrendered Dec 1799; confined at ** FORT WILLIAM. **
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The extradition condition is the memorable detail** — *a defeated rebel was handed over on **negotiated terms that the British kept**, which is why he died in confinement rather than on a scaffold. ⚠️ **Contrast Kattabomman in §10, tried by a "mockery of trial" and hanged within weeks.** The difference is not law; it is that **Wazir Ali had a sovereign protector and Kattabomman did not.***

**The mechanism is the clearest illustration of the Subsidiary Alliance's trap in the whole chapter** — and you already have the *theory* of that trap in [file 01](01_Expansion_and_Consolidation.md).

      EXACTLY AS DESIGNED: **

   1805  ** TRAVANCORE accepts a SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE ** under
         Wellesley, on ** "harsh conditions". **
      -> the ruler ** CANNOT PAY THE SUBSIDY ** and ** FALLS

      -> ⭐ Prime Minister ( ** DALAWA ** ) ** VELU THAMPI **

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE KUNDARA PROCLAMATION IS THE SINGLE MOST "NATIONALIST-SOUNDING" DOCUMENT IN THIS CHAPTER — and its fate is the chapter's whole argument in miniature.** *A minister called publicly for the expulsion of the British; **his own king went over to the other side**. ⭐⭐ **The Subsidiary Alliance did not merely subordinate a state — it split the state's own leadership, because the ruler's throne was now guaranteed by the very power his minister was fighting.*** *(Compare the **Raja of Pudukottai betraying Kattabomman** in §10 and **Serfoji of Thanjavur standing "firmly by the British"** in §11 — ⭐ **three different southern courts, one pattern.**)*

### 🏰 5.6 Bundelkhand (1808–12) — and the *ikarnamah* device

**The Bundela chiefs resisted from forts "numbering nearly ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY."** Three successive commanders: **Lakshaman Dawa** *(killadar of **Ajaygarh** — surrendered Feb 1809)*, **Darya Singh** *(killadar of **Kalanjar** — suppressed Jan 1812)*, and ⭐ **GOPAL SINGH**, *"a famous military adventurer"* who **eluded all British vigilance and military tactics FOR FOUR YEARS.**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The solution the British reached for is the point:** *unable to reduce 150 forts one at a time, they **"had to adopt a policy of BINDING DOWN THE HEREDITARY CHIEFTAINS OF BUNDELKHAND BY A SERIES OF CONTRACTUAL OBLIGATIONS — *IKARNAMAHS*."*** ⭐⭐ **This is conquest by paperwork rather than artillery, and it is the same instinct as the Subsidiary Alliance: when force is expensive, buy the chief's signature instead.** *(⭐ Worth pairing with the **Doctrine of Lapse** in [file 01](01_Expansion_and_Consolidation.md) — **the Company's most effective weapons were legal instruments, not armies.**)*

### 🧂 5.7 Bareilly (1816) and Surat (1840s) — the two urban risings, and the two different endings

| | **BAREILLY, 1816** | **SURAT, 1844 & 1848** |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | ⭐ **The POLICE TAX** | ⭐ **Salt duty raised from 50 paise to ONE RUPEE** |
| **Escalation** | ⭐⭐ **Mufti MUHAMMAD AIWAZ**, *"a venerated old man"*, petitioned the magistrate (March 1816) — then **the police, collecting the tax, INJURED A WOMAN** | A **popular movement** and **attacks on Europeans (1844)**; in **1848**, **boycott and passive resistance** against **Bengal Standard Weights and Measures** |
| **Spread** | Within **two days**, armed men from **Pilibhit, Shahjahanpur and Rampur** rose *"for the defence of the faith and the Mufti"*; in April they **murdered the son of Leycester**, the provincial court judge | — |
| **Outcome** | ⚠️ **Crushed — over 300 rebels killed**, more wounded and imprisoned | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GOVERNMENT WITHDREW THE SALT LEVY — AND THEN WITHDREW THE WEIGHTS-AND-MEASURES MEASURE TOO** |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PUT THESE TWO SIDE BY SIDE AND YOU HAVE A REAL ANALYTICAL POINT, NOT A LIST.** *Bareilly took up arms and was destroyed; **Surat used BOYCOTT AND PASSIVE RESISTANCE and won — twice.*** ⚠️ **Do not overclaim this into "Gandhi was anticipated in 1848"** — Spectrum makes no such claim and the Surat actions were local and economic. ⭐⭐ **But the empirical observation stands and is safe to state: in this entire chapter, the methods that actually extracted concessions from the colonial state were the NON-MILITARY ones — Surat's boycott, and the Paikas' post-defeat bargaining.** *Everything fought with swords was crushed.*

⭐ **Spectrum's own verdict on Bareilly is worth quoting because it is unusually reflective:** *the upsurge *"seems to have been the product **more of DISCONTENT than of actual GRIEVANCE** — the elements of discontent lying in **the very nature of the alien administration**."* ⭐⭐ **i.e. the police tax was the occasion, not the cause. Remove the tax and the discontent remains.**

### ☪️ The Wahabi Movement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founder** | ⭐ **SYED AHMED of RAI BARELLY** |
| **Inspired by** | **Abdul Wahab (1703–87) of Saudi Arabia** and ⭐ **SHAH WALIULLAH of Delhi** |
| **Programme** | Condemned **western influence on Islam**; advocated **a return to pure Islam** as in *"the Arabia of the Prophet's time"* |
| **Organisation** | ⭐⭐ **Syed Ahmed acclaimed as *IMAM*; a countrywide organisation with an ELABORATE SECRET CODE, run by spiritual vice-regents (*KHALIFAS*)** |
| **Base** | ⭐ **SITHANA**, in the north-western tribal belt |
| **Indian centre** | ⭐ **PATNA** *(with missions in Hyderabad, Madras, Bengal, United Provinces, Bombay)* |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE 1849 PIVOT IS A PERFECT PRELIMS TRAP AND A PERFECT MAINS POINT AT ONCE.** *The Wahabis did not start as an anti-British movement — **they became one by ACCIDENT OF CONQUEST**. Their enemy was the Sikh state; the British destroyed the Sikh state and thereby **inherited the enmity along with the territory**.* ⭐⭐ **A statement like *"the Wahabi movement was founded to fight British rule"* is FALSE, and it is exactly the kind of sentence UPSC puts in a two-statement question.**

### 🪯 The Kuka Movement — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ the one that punches far above its weight

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | **1840**, in **western Punjab**, by **BHAGAT JAWAHAR MAL (Sian Saheb)** |
| **Major later leader** | ⭐ **BABA RAM SINGH** — *"He founded the **NAMDHARI** Sikh sect"* |
| **The transformation** | ⭐⭐ **After the British took Punjab, it changed from a RELIGIOUS PURIFICATION campaign into a POLITICAL one** |
| **Social tenets** | **Abolition of caste and similar discriminations among Sikhs · discouraging meat, alcohol and drugs · permitting INTERMARRIAGE · WIDOW REMARRIAGE · encouraging women to step out of seclusion** |
| **Political tenets** | **Remove the British and restore Sikh rule over Punjab** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The methods** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WEARING HAND-WOVEN CLOTH · BOYCOTT of English LAWS, EDUCATION and PRODUCTS** |
| **Suppression** | Crushed **between 1863 and 1872**; ⭐ **Ram Singh DEPORTED TO RANGOON in 1872** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SPECTRUM'S OWN CLAIM, AND IT IS THE MOST QUOTABLE LINE IN THE CHAPTER: *"So the concepts of SWADESHI and NON-COOPERATION were propagated by the KUKAS, MUCH BEFORE they became part of the Indian national movement in the early twentieth century."*** ⭐⭐⭐ **Hand-woven cloth, boycott of foreign goods, boycott of colonial courts and schools — that is the Non-Cooperation programme of 1920–22, assembled in Punjab in the 1860s.** *(⭐ Set this beside [Cl.10 Ch.2](../../../01_NCERT/Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/02_Nationalism_in_India.md) and you can argue that **Gandhi's method had Indian precedents, not only Tolstoyan and South African ones** — a genuinely strong Mains line, provided you present it as **anticipation, not influence**, since Spectrum claims no causal link.)*

⚠️ **And note the social radicalism, which is easy to miss beside the politics:** ⭐⭐ **intermarriage, widow remarriage and women leaving seclusion put the Kukas in the SAME conversation as the reform movements of [Cl.8 Ch.7](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/07_Women_Caste_and_Reform.md)** — *and unlike most of those, this reform programme came with an anti-colonial one attached.*

## 7️⃣ The *palayakkarar* system — what it actually was ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **You cannot understand the Poligar wars without this, and Spectrum's one-line gloss — *"holders of territory or palayam… granted by the rulers, mainly the Nayakas, in return for military service and tribute"* — is not enough.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** PALAYAKKARAR ( anglicised: POLIGAR ) -- THE INSTITUTION **
   ================================================================

   ** ORIGIN ** -- dates to the ** 1530s ** ; believed to have been
      practised earlier in the ** KAKATIYA kingdom of WARANGAL. **

   ⭐ ** THE ARCHITECTS ** -- after Vijayanagara declined,
      ** NAGAMA NAYAK ** ( sent as viceroy to Madurai ) and his son
      ** VISWANATHA NAYAK ** asserted independence. Under the prime
      minister ** ARIYANATHA MUDALIYAR ** the little kingdoms of
      the old Pandya country were converted into ** 72 PALAYAMS ** .
      Viswanatha built a Madurai fort of ** SEVENTY-TWO BASTIONS ** ,
      ** each placed under one chief. **

   ** THE WORD ** -- ** "holder of a CAMP" ** as well as
      ** "holder of an ESTATE on MILITARY TENURE." **

   ** WHAT HE OWED ** -- a ** FIXED ANNUAL TRIBUTE ** or ** TROOPS **
      to the king, and ** ORDER AND PEACE ** over his area.
   ** WHAT HE GOT ** -- ** villages granted for revenue
      collection ** , ** titles and privileges ** , and ⭐⭐
      ** JUDICIAL POWERS over CIVIL AND CRIMINAL CASES. **

   ⭐ ** WHO THEY REPLACED ** -- before the system, ** SERVAIKARARS
      and TALAYARIS ** collected fees for police work. After the
      palayams were created, the ** SERVAIKARARS BECAME
      PALAYAKKARARS ** and ** SUBORDINATED the TALAYARIS. **
```

**🗺️ AND THE SPLIT THAT DECIDED WHO REBELLED WITH WHOM:**

| Type | Where | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **WESTERN palayams** | Western **Tirunelveli** | **MARAVAR chieftains** |
| ⭐ **EASTERN palayams** | The **black-soil tracts** of eastern Tirunelveli | **NAYAK palayakkarars** *(descendants of **Telugu migrants**)* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ANALYTICAL POINT, AND IT IS WHY THE REVOLT WAS SO HARD TO CRUSH: a *palayakkarar* was not a landlord. He was a LOCAL GOVERNMENT — he taxed, he judged, he policed, and he kept an army.** ⭐⭐ **So when the Company took over revenue collection, it was not adjusting a rent; it was abolishing a functioning state and leaving the man who ran it alive, armed, and in place.** *Compare the ***kaval*** right in §11: an **ancient hereditary village-police office with specified rights and responsibilities** — the Company treated it as a fee to be transferred, and the holders treated its loss as the destruction of an office their families had held for generations.*

**⚙️ HOW THE CONFLICT STARTED — the debt chain that put the Company into Tamil country at all:**

```
   ** THE NAWAB OF ARCOT had BORROWED HEAVILY, PLEDGING VILLAGES **
   across Tamil country
        -> he ** ENTRUSTED THE COLLECTION OF REVENUE ARREARS TO
           THE COMPANY. **
        -> ⭐ ** 1755 ** : at the Nawab's request, ** 500 Europeans
           and 200 sepoys ** were ordered into ** "the countries of
           MADURAI and TIRUNELVELI." **
        -> ⚠️ ** "The encroachment of East India Company
           administration into palayakkarar's authority aroused
           STIFF RESISTANCE." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** SO THE FIRST SOUTHERN REVOLTS ARE NOT AGAINST
      "BRITISH RULE" AT ALL. ** They are against ** A DEBT
      COLLECTOR ACTING FOR AN INDIAN RULER ** -- and that is
      exactly how the Company preferred to arrive.
```

⭐ **Set this beside [Spectrum file 02](02_Advent_of_the_Europeans.md) and [file 01](01_Expansion_and_Consolidation.md)** — *the Carnatic debts are the same instrument as the Bengal *diwani*: **acquire the REVENUE FUNCTION first, and the sovereignty follows quietly behind it.***

## 🔟 Veera Pandiya Kattabomman (1798–99) — the First Poligar War ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**⚠️ Spectrum compresses this into a paragraph. It is worth the space, because the sequence of events is a documented case study in HOW A REVOLT IS MANUFACTURED BY ADMINISTRATIVE HUMILIATION — and that is a Mains argument, not a story.**

|---|---|

| **Sept 1798** | Tribute **fell into arrears** and **Collector JACKSON wrote to him "in his characteristic arrogance."** ⚠️ **The country was in SEVERE DROUGHT and the palayakkarars could not collect taxes** |

| **18 Aug 1798** | Jackson summoned him to **Ramanathapuram within two weeks** — then **left on tour** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The 400 miles** | Jackson halted at **Chokkampatti, Sivagiri, Sattur and Srivilliputhur** collecting tribute. **Kattabomman sought an interview and was refused** — told he could be met only at Ramanathapuram. ⭐⭐ **He then FOLLOWED THE COLLECTOR FOR TWENTY-THREE DAYS OVER 400 MILES**, reaching Ramanathapuram on **19 September** |

| **May 1799** | ⭐ **WELLESLEY ordered forces from Tiruchirappalli, Thanjavur and Madurai into Tirunelveli. TRAVANCORE TROOPS JOINED THE BRITISH. MAJOR BANNERMAN commanded** |
| **1 June 1799** | Kattabomman went to **Sivaganga with 500 men**, conferred with **Marudu at Palayanur**, and returned with **500 more armed men** |
| **1 Sept 1799** | **Bannerman's ultimatum**; Kattabomman dodged it; the army reached **Panchalamkurichi on 5 September** |

Communications cut; ⭐ **successive attacks REPULSED — Colonel Welsh recorded the gallantry of Kattabomman's soldiers in his memoirs.** Reinforcements arrived **16 September**; the garrison **evacuated to Kadalgudi**; **Sivasubramania Pillai taken at Kalarpatti** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ARGUMENT THIS EPISODE SUPPORTS — and it is a far better Mains point than "he was a brave freedom fighter."** *Kattabomman **paid his arrears**. He **was acquitted by the Company's own committee**. **The collector who provoked him was dismissed.** ⭐⭐ **And he still rebelled — because what had been done to him was not a financial demand but a public humiliation: made to follow an official 400 miles, refused a chair, and told he had "saved himself from ruin."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Spectrum's own §3 cause — *"the EGO of traditional zamindars and poligars was hurt due to being SIDELINED IN RANK"* — is not a trivial grievance. For a man whose authority WAS his standing, the loss of standing was the loss of everything.** *(⭐ And note the trial's form: **an assembly of his fellow palayakkarars was made to watch**. The execution was addressed to them, not to him.)*

⚠️⭐⭐ **THE BETRAYAL PATTERN, now for the third time:** **Ettappan of Pudukottai / Vijaya Ragunatha Tondaiman betrays Kattabomman · the Maharaja of Travancore abandons Velu Thampi (§5.5) · Serfoji of Thanjavur stands "firmly by the British" (§11).** ⭐⭐⭐ **Spectrum's own summary of the poligar rebellion names *"once again a betrayal"* as the decisive event — and TN's account of 1801 shows the British consciously manufacturing such splits. That is not a moral failing of southern rulers; it is a POLICY WORKING.**

## 1️⃣1️⃣ The Marudu brothers and the South Indian Rebellion of 1801 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**The Second Poligar War — larger than the first, and the one with a genuinely political programme.**

**⚙️ THE GRIEVANCE, and it is precise:** ⭐⭐ *by the **TREATY OF 1772** the Arcot Nawab had authorised the Company to collect the ***STALAM KAVAL*** and ***DESAKAVAL*** — **the watch-and-ward dues**. This **struck the KAVAL CHIEFS in BOTH palayakkarar AND non-palayakkarar territories**, and *"the aggrieved kavalkarars and their chiefs joined the palayakkarars in their fight against the Nawab and the Company."** ⚠️ **Spectrum records the same grievance for NORTH ARCOT (1803–05), where the poligars rose "when they were DEPRIVED OF THEIR RIGHT TO COLLECT THE KAVAL FEES."**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***KAVAL* was, in Spectrum's own words, *"an ANCIENT INSTITUTION of Tamil Nadu… a HEREDITARY VILLAGE POLICE OFFICE with SPECIFIED RIGHTS AND RESPONSIBILITIES."*** ⭐⭐ **So the Company's revenue reform abolished a hereditary public office. The men who lost it were the men who had kept order — which is why the region became, in Spectrum's phrase, *"lawless"* immediately afterwards.**

### 🎯 13.2 The four "innovations" — the immediate cause

| # | The order | Why it enraged |
|:--:|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A NEW MODEL TURBAN**, designed and introduced under **Adjutant-General AGNEW's** direct supervision | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Its LEATHER COCKADE was made of ANIMAL SKIN — *"PIG SKIN was anathema to Muslims, while UPPER-CASTE HINDUS shunned anything to do with the COW'S HIDE"*** |
| **2** | **Prohibition of ALL FOREHEAD MARKINGS** denoting caste and religion | Erased visible religious identity on parade |
| **3** | **Moustaches to be cut to a set pattern**; **earrings** objected to | *"Infringed the religious customs of BOTH Hindu and Muslim soldiers"* |
| **4** | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **The front of the uniform was converted into a CROSS** | *"To make matters worse"* — read alongside the rest, it looked like **conversion by uniform** |

⚠️ **The official justification is worth knowing, because it is the authentic voice of the administration:** *the orders were defended on the ground that although such things *"had not been prohibited previously by any formal order, **IT HAD NEVER BEEN THE PRACTICE IN ANY WELL-REGULATED CORPS** for the men to appear with them on parade."*

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **May 1806** | ⭐ **The 2nd battalion of the 4th regiment REFUSED TO WEAR THE NEW TURBAN.** Reported by **Col. FANCOURT**, commandant of the garrison |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE HINDU–MUSLIM SYMMETRY IS THE FACT TO CARRY INTO AN ANSWER — 10 Muslims and 11 Hindus tried together, one of each flogged, over a single leather cockade that offended both.** ⭐⭐ **This is the earliest documented instance in your notes of a colonial measure producing a JOINT Hindu–Muslim military grievance — the precise combination that made 1857 possible.** *(⭐ Compare the **Sanyasi–Fakir revolt's *"equal participation of Hindus and Muslims"*** in §5.1: the same alignment, half a century earlier, in a different arena.)*

# 🌾 PART D — PEASANT MOVEMENTS WITH RELIGIOUS OVERTONES

## 1️⃣4️⃣ The four Bengal-and-Malabar peasant risings ⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **Spectrum's category name is doing real work: these are PEASANT movements — against eviction, rent increases and moneylenders, aiming at OCCUPANCY RIGHTS — that were ORGANISED THROUGH A RELIGIOUS SECT.** *The religion supplied the network; the grievance was agrarian.* ⭐⭐ **Three of the four are in East Bengal, and all four are absent from your NCERT notes.**

| Movement | Years | Region | Leader | The agrarian core |
|---|:--:|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NARKELBERIA UPRISING** | **c.1831** | **24 Parganas, West Bengal** | ⭐ **MIR NITHAR ALI (1782–1831) — "TITU MIR"** | Muslim tenants against **mainly Hindu landlords** who imposed a ⭐⭐ **BEARD-TAX on the Faraizis**, and against **British indigo planters**. ⚠️ ***"Often considered the FIRST ARMED PEASANT UPRISING against the British"*** — later **merged into the WAHABI movement** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **THE PAGAL PANTHIS** | **1825–35** | **Mymensingh** *(then Bengal)* | Founded by **KARAM SHAH**; organised by his son ⭐ **TIPU** | A **semi-religious group of HAJONG and GARO tribal peasants**; ⭐⭐ **REFUSED TO PAY RENT ABOVE A CERTAIN LIMIT** and attacked zamindars' houses. ⚠️ **The government introduced *"an EQUITABLE ARRANGEMENT to protect these peasants"* — and the movement was still violently suppressed** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **FARAIZI REVOLT** | **1838–57** | **Faridpur, East Bengal** | ⚠️ **HAJI SHARIAT-ALLAH** and ⭐ **DUDU MIAN (1819–60)** — *see the [VERIFY] register* | Advocated **radical religious, social AND political change**; aimed to ⭐ **EXPEL THE ENGLISH FROM BENGAL**; **supported tenants against zamindars**. **Most Faraizis later joined the WAHABI ranks** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MOPLAH UPRISINGS** | **1836–54** | **Malabar, Kerala** | *(no single leader)* | ⭐⭐ **TWENTY-TWO SEPARATE REBELLIONS in eighteen years** — against **hikes in revenue demand, REDUCTION OF FIELD SIZE, and official oppression.** ⚠️ ***"None, however, proved successful"*** |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE MOPLAH ENTRY NEEDS ITS TN CONTEXT, BECAUSE SPECTRUM GIVES THE SYMPTOM AND TN GIVES THE CAUSE.** *TN Unit 18 explains the mechanism precisely: the **MAPPILAS** were descendants of Arab traders who had married Malabar women and become **cultivating tenants, landless labourers, petty traders and fishermen**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The traditional system shared the net produce THREE WAYS — the *JANMI* (janmam-holder), the *KANAMDAR* (kanam-holder) and the CULTIVATOR. When the British took Malabar in 1792 they "revamped land relations by creating INDIVIDUAL OWNERSHIP", recognising THE *JANMI* AS ABSOLUTE OWNER — with A RIGHT TO EVICT THAT HAD NOT EXISTED BEFORE.*** ⭐⭐ **Add over-assessment, illegal cesses and a pro-landlord judiciary and police, and you have twenty-two risings.**

⚠️ **TN also dates the serious incidents precisely — *Manjeri, August 1849 · Kulathur, August 1851* (both south Malabar) *· Mattannur, January 1852* (north) — and notes that repression bought **about twenty years of peace** before the Mappilas rose again in **1870**.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ANALYTICAL POINT, and it generalises far beyond Malabar:** *the British did not invent a tax here. **They converted a SHARE into an OWNERSHIP.** A three-way split of the produce is a relationship in which nobody can be removed; **absolute ownership is a relationship in which everybody below the owner can be.*** ⭐⭐ **That is the same operation as the Permanent Settlement in [Cl.12 Th.9](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) and the *janmi* is Malabar's zamindar — which is why the two chapters can be answered with one argument.**

⚠️ **NOTE THE SECOND MOPLAH REBELLION, and keep it separate:** *Spectrum flags it in parentheses — the **1921 Moplah rising** occurred when the Mappilas were organised by **Congress and Khilafat supporters during Non-Cooperation**, but *"Hindu-Muslim differences distanced the Congress and the Moplahs from each other"* and they were **subdued by 1921**. ⭐ **Out of this chapter's window — but a standard Prelims distractor, so know which is which.**

### 🌾 14.1 The peasants and 1857 — Spectrum's short, sharp verdict

⚠️ **This paragraph is easy to skip and is genuinely examinable, because it corrects a common assumption.**

- **Peasant participation in 1857 was ACTIVE ONLY IN SOME AREAS** — *"mainly those in **western Uttar Pradesh**."*
- ⭐⭐ **Where they did rise, peasants "united WITH the local FEUDAL LEADERS"** — ⚠️ **not against them**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AFTER the revolt their position got WORSE**, because the government decided **to win the support of the LANDED classes while IGNORING the peasants**
- ⭐ **In Awadh, land was RESTORED TO THE TALUQDARS** with revenue and other powers, and **peasants could not avail of the 1859 BENGAL RENT ACT**
- ⚠️ ⭐⭐ **In some regions peasants had to pay AN ADDITIONAL CESS AS PUNISHMENT for having participated**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE SAME ARGUMENT AS [Cl.12 Th.8](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_II/Notes/08_Theme8_Peasants_Zamindars_and_the_State.md)'s ARGUMENT FROM SILENCE, ARRIVING FROM THE OTHER DIRECTION — and the pairing is a first-class Mains observation.** *Theme 8 shows that in the seventeenth century **peasants sided WITH zamindars against the STATE**, and that the bhakti saints attacked **the revenue official, never the zamindar**. ⭐⭐ **Spectrum shows that in 1857 peasants again sided WITH their local lords.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Two centuries, two empires, the same alignment — the primary antagonism in the Indian countryside was VILLAGE vs STATE, not peasant vs landlord.** And the colonial state's response after 1857 — **buy the landlords, abandon the peasants** — is the moment it finally split that alliance deliberately.*

# 🏹 PART E — TRIBAL REVOLTS

## 1️⃣5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The distinction that carries the Mains marks: MAINLAND vs FRONTIER

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Spectrum's opening claim is a quotable superlative: tribal movements under British rule were *"THE MOST FREQUENT, MILITANT AND VIOLENT OF ALL MOVEMENTS."*** ⚠️ **But the analytically valuable part is the split that follows — and it is the one thing in this chapter that your NCERT notes, for all their depth on the Santhals and Birsa Munda, do not contain at all.**

**🌲 WHY THE MAINLAND TRIBES ROSE — six causes:**

1. ⭐ **The land settlements broke the JOINT-OWNERSHIP tradition** and *"disrupted their social fabric"*
2. **Settled agriculture was extended → tribals lost land, and NON-TRIBALS FLOWED IN**
3. ⭐⭐ **SHIFTING CULTIVATION was curbed; RESERVED FORESTS were created; timber use and grazing restricted** — ⚠️ **driven by *"the increasing demand from the Company for TIMBER — FOR SHIPPING AND THE RAILWAYS"***
4. **Exploitation by POLICE, TRADERS and MONEY-LENDERS** — *"most of them **OUTSIDERS**"*
5. **General laws resented as INTRUSIVE**, since the tribals had their own customs
6. ⭐⭐ **CHRISTIAN MISSIONARIES interfered with traditional customs and were *"perceived as REPRESENTATIVES OF THE ALIEN RULE"***

⭐ **Cause 3 is already yours in depth** — [Cl.9 Ch.4 *(Forest Society and Colonialism)*](../../../01_NCERT/Class_09_India_and_Contemporary_World_I/Notes/04_Forest_Society_and_Colonialism.md) gives Brandis, the Forest Acts and the scientific-forestry apparatus. ⭐⭐ **What Spectrum adds is the demand side in five words: SHIPPING AND THE RAILWAYS.**

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE THREE DIFFERENCES -- FRONTIER ( NORTH-EAST )
              REVOLTS WERE NOT LIKE MAINLAND ONES **
   ================================================================

   ** (1) DIFFERENT GOAL. ** Tribes with ** kin and cultural links
       ACROSS THE BORDER "DID NOT CONCERN THEMSELVES MUCH WITH THE
       NATIONALIST STRUGGLE." ** Their revolts were for
       ** POLITICAL AUTONOMY WITHIN THE INDIAN UNION -- or
       COMPLETE INDEPENDENCE. **

   ** (2) NOT LAND-BASED. ** ⚠️ These were ** NOT forest-based or
       agrarian revolts ** , because ** these tribals GENERALLY
       STILL CONTROLLED THEIR LAND AND FOREST. ** ⭐ ** The British
       entered the north-east MUCH LATER than the non-frontier
       tribal areas. **

   ** (3) LONGER, AND CULTURALLY OPPOSITE. ** Frontier revolts
       ** CONTINUED FOR A LONGER TIME ** ; and where the mainland
       saw ** SANSKRITISATION ** , the north-east saw
       ⭐⭐ ** DE-SANSKRITISATION ** -- e.g. ** THE MEITEIS **
       organised a movement during ** Churchand Maharaja's rule
       ( 1891-1941 ) to DENOUNCE THE MALPRACTICES OF THE
       NEO-VAISHNAVITE BRAHMINS. **
       ⚠️ ** "Sanskritisation movements were ALMOST TOTALLY ABSENT
       in the north-east frontier region in the colonial period." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BEST MAINS ASSET IN THE CHAPTER, AND IT IS DIRECTLY USABLE ON MODERN QUESTIONS.** *The north-eastern movements were **not about losing land — they were about NOT LOSING AUTONOMY**, and their demands were framed as **autonomy within the Union or independence from it**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is the entire post-1947 north-east question — the Sixth Schedule, the autonomous councils, the insurgencies — visible in its colonial-era form.** ⚠️ **A GS1 answer on tribal movements that treats the north-east as just more of the same has missed the only structural distinction that exists in the topic.**

**⭐ THE FOUR CHARACTERISTICS COMMON TO TRIBAL REVOLTS:**

| # | Characteristic |
|:--:|---|
| **1** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ETHNIC SOLIDARITY — but NOT ALL "OUTSIDERS" WERE ENEMIES.** ⚠️ ***"The poor who lived by their MANUAL LABOUR or profession and had a socially/economically SUPPORTIVE ROLE in the village were LEFT ALONE; the violence was directed towards the MONEY-LENDERS AND TRADERS who were seen as EXTENSIONS OF THE COLONIAL GOVERNMENT"*** |
| **2** | Resentment against **laws imposed by a "foreign government"** seen as destroying their **traditional socio-economic framework** |
| **3** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Leadership by MESSIAH-LIKE FIGURES** who promised an end to the suffering caused by outsiders |
| **4** | ⚠️ **They were *"DOOMED FROM THE BEGINNING"* — outdated arms against modern weapons and techniques** |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CHARACTERISTIC 1 IS THE ONE WORTH AN EXTRA MINUTE, BECAUSE IT DEMOLISHES THE LAZIEST THING SAID ABOUT TRIBAL REVOLTS — that they were indiscriminate xenophobia.** *They were **precisely targeted**. The tribal rebels distinguished between the outsider who **worked among them** and the outsider who **extracted from them**, and killed only the second. ⭐⭐ **That is a political judgement about who was and was not part of the colonial machine — and it is exactly the discrimination [Cl.8 Ch.4's](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/04_Tribals_Dikus_and_the_Vision_of_a_Golden_Age.md) *diku* concept encodes.**

# ⚖️ PART G — WHY NONE OF IT WORKED

## 1️⃣9️⃣ The six weaknesses ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

| # | Weakness |
|:--:|---|
| **1** | ⭐ **Large numbers of participants — but LOCALISED, and occurring AT DIFFERENT TIMES in DIFFERENT REGIONS** |
| **2** | **They arose out of LOCAL GRIEVANCES** |
| **3** | ⭐⭐⭐ **SEMI-FEUDAL LEADERSHIP — backward-looking, traditional, and *"their resistance did NOT OFFER ALTERNATIVES to the existing social set-up"*** |
| **4** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE CENTRAL SENTENCE — see below** |
| **5** | **The rebellions were *"CENTURIES-OLD IN FORM and IDEOLOGICAL / CULTURAL CONTENT"*** |
| **6** | ⭐⭐ **Those who were less obstinate were *"PACIFIED THROUGH CONCESSIONS"*** — ⚠️ **divide and settle, the same instrument as the Woya Thevar recognition in §11** |
| **7** | ⭐ **Obsolete methods and arms against modern weapons and strategy — *"as well as DECEPTION AND CHICANERY"*** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WEAKNESS 4 IS THE MOST IMPORTANT SENTENCE IN THE ENTIRE CHAPTER, AND IF YOU MEMORISE ONE QUOTATION FROM THIS FILE, MAKE IT THIS ONE:**
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"If many of these revolts seemed SIMILAR to one another in wanting to oust the alien rule, it was NOT BECAUSE OF SOME 'NATIONAL' IMPULSE OR COMMON EFFORT, but because they were PROTESTING AGAINST CONDITIONS THAT WERE COMMON TO THEM."***

```
   ================================================================
   ** WHAT THAT SENTENCE ACTUALLY CLAIMS **
   ================================================================

   ** RESEMBLANCE IS NOT CONNECTION. **

   Forty risings across ninety years look alike because
   ** THE SAME REVENUE SYSTEM, THE SAME FOREST POLICY AND THE
   SAME COURTS WERE APPLIED TO ALL OF THEM ** -- not because
   the rebels were in touch, shared a programme, or thought of
   themselves as one people.

   ⭐ A ** UNIFORM CAUSE ** produces ** UNIFORM EFFECTS ** in
     places that have never heard of each other.

   ⚠️ ** SO: ** " early nationalism " is the WRONG label.
      ** " PRIMARY RESISTANCE " ** -- TN's term -- is the right
      one: resistance by those being conquered, in the ** IDIOM
      OF THE ORDER BEING DESTROYED ** , not of the one to come.
```

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HOW TO USE THIS IN AN ANSWER WITHOUT SOUNDING DISMISSIVE — and this is the balanced position an examiner is looking for.** *Hold **two things** at once: *(a)* **these revolts were NOT nationalist** — their aims were **restorative**, their leadership **semi-feudal**, their solidarities **local, caste-based or ethnic**; and *(b)* **they were nonetheless the ONLY resistance there was for a century**, they **cost the Company real money and real time**, they **forced policy reversals at Surat and after the Paika rising**, and ⭐⭐⭐ **their MEMORY was later mobilised — *Anandamath* out of the Sanyasi revolt, folk ballads out of Kattabomman, Vellore read backwards through 1857.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The nationalism came later and USED them. That is a real relationship — just not the one the phrase "first war of independence" implies.**

⚠️ **AND KEEP TN'S VOCABULARY, because it is more precise than Spectrum's:** *TN Unit 18 calls the revolts of deposed kings, uprooted zamindars and palayakkarars ⭐ ***"PRIMARY RESISTANCE"***, and treats the **dispossessed peasant and tribal uprisings as INDEPENDENT of them**. ⭐⭐ **Two different social processes running in the same decades — which is precisely why Bipan Chandra separates civil rebellions from tribal and peasant movements in the first place.**

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***PALAYAKKARAR*** *(anglicised **POLIGAR**; Tamil **palayakkarargal**)* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Holder of a *PALAYAM* — "holder of a CAMP" and "holder of an ESTATE on MILITARY TENURE."** Owed a **fixed annual tribute or troops** and kept order; held **revenue villages, titles and JUDICIAL POWERS over civil and criminal cases** |
| ***PALAYAM*** | The **fief/territory of a palayakkarar** — **72** were created in the old Pandya country under **Viswanatha Nayak** |
| ***KAVAL* / *STALAM KAVAL* / *DESAKAVAL*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"Watch"* — an ANCIENT TAMIL INSTITUTION: a HEREDITARY VILLAGE POLICE OFFICE with specified rights and responsibilities, and the FEES attached to it.** Its transfer to the Company **caused both the North Arcot rising (1803–05) and the 1801 rebellion** |
| ***SERVAIKARAR* / *TALAYARI*** | The **pre-palayam police officers** who collected fees for watch duty; **Servaikarars became palayakkarars and subordinated the Talayaris** |
| ***IZARA* / *IZARADAR*** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Revenue FARMING / a revenue FARMER** — the right to collect a district's revenue for a lump sum. **Hannay's izara of Gorakhpur–Bahraich (₹22 lakh/yr) triggered the 1781 rising** |
| ***IKARNAMAH*** | ⭐⭐ **A CONTRACTUAL OBLIGATION** used to bind the **hereditary chieftains of Bundelkhand** when 150 forts could not be reduced by force |
| ***KILLADAR*** | **Fort commander** *(Lakshaman Dawa of Ajaygarh; Darya Singh of Kalanjar)* |
| ***DALAWA*** | ⭐ **Prime minister** — in Travancore *(**Velu Thampi**)*; in Mysore, the office **Haider Ali held** before becoming de facto ruler |
| ***PAIK* / *PAIKA*** | ⭐ **Literally "FOOT SOLDIER" — Odisha's traditional LANDED MILITIA**, holding **rent-free tenures** for military and policing service |
| ***GADKARI*** | ⭐ **A HEREDITARY MILITARY CLASS garrisoned in the Maratha FORTS** — disbanded in the Kolhapur reorganisation after 1844 |
| ***BURKANDAZ*** | **Demobilised soldiers** of the Muslim armies and zamindars *(Assam, 1792)* |
| ***DAMNI-KOL*** | The status the British conceded to **Pahariya** territory in 1778 to buy peace |
| ***DAR-UL-HARB* / *DAR-UL-ISLAM*** | ⭐ **"Territory of War/Chaos" / "Land of Islam"** — the Wahabi framing that made **Punjab, not British India, the first target** |
| ***KHALIFA*** *(in the Wahabi movement)* | **Spiritual vice-regent** running the movement's **countrywide secret organisation** |
| ***HOOL*** | ⭐ **The Santhal word for the 1855–56 rebellion** |
| ***ULGULAN*** | ⭐ **"Great tumult" — Birsa Munda's rising, 1899–1900** *(post-1857; yours already)* |
| ***JANMI* / *KANAMDAR*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Malabar tenures — holder of *janmam* and of *kanam*.** Under custom the **net produce split THREE WAYS (janmi–kanamdar–cultivator)**; the British made the **janmi an ABSOLUTE OWNER with the right to EVICT** |
| ***PAGODA*** *(Tamil **varagan**)* | ⭐ **The dominant gold coin at the Europeans' arrival, of Vijayanagara descent — ~3½ rupees under Tipu.** ⭐⭐ ***"SHAKING THE PAGODA TREE"* was the English phrase for making a quick fortune in India** |
| ***JEMADAR*** | **Indian junior officer rank** — *Jemadar Sheik Kasim, a principal accused at Vellore* |
| **COCKADE** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The LEATHER ornament on the new 1806 turban — pig or cow hide, and therefore offensive to Muslims and high-caste Hindus alike** |
| **PRIMARY RESISTANCE** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TN's term for the revolts of deposed rulers, uprooted zamindars and palayakkarars** — as distinct from the **independent** uprisings of dispossessed peasants and tribals |

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FOUR FORMS: civil rebellions · tribal uprisings · peasant movements** *(the three are **Bipan Chandra's**)* **+ military revolts** *(Spectrum's addition)*
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The genesis is not "harsher taxes" — it is that COLONIAL RULE CLOSED THE APPEAL. There was "no one to hear their grievances", and the law now protected the state and its collaborators**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A civil uprising = a DISPOSSESSED ELITE at the head of a DISTRESSED POPULATION — and when a zamindar fell, his artisans, priests and retainers fell with him**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***ANANDAMATH*, and therefore *VANDE MATARAM*, comes out of the SANYASI REVOLT** — the 1770s reaching 1905 through an 1882 novel
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SOUTH IS THE WHOLE POINT OF THIS FILE: PULI THEVAR (whose confederacy dissolved when the French lost Pondicherry) · YUSUF KHAN (hanged by the Company he served) · VELU NACHIYAR and KUYILI · KATTABOMMAN (acquitted, humiliated, betrayed, hanged at Kayatar 1799) · the MARUDUS and the 1801 rebellion (broken by recognising a rival claimant) · CHINNAMALAI (1805)**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **VELLORE, 10 JULY 1806 — cockade not cartridge; 3,000 Mysore loyalists round a fort holding Tipu's sons; 13 officers and 82 privates killed; Gillespie rode 25 km from Arcot and retook it in fifteen minutes; the regulations were WITHDRAWN and GOVERNOR WILLIAM BENTINCK was RECALLED**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MAINLAND ≠ FRONTIER: north-eastern tribes still HELD their land, wanted AUTONOMY OR INDEPENDENCE rather than the nationalist struggle, fought LONGER, and went in for DE-Sanskritisation**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Tribal violence was TARGETED, not indiscriminate — labourers spared, moneylenders and traders attacked as extensions of the colonial state**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The MALABAR mechanism generalises: the British converted a THREE-WAY SHARE into an ABSOLUTE OWNERSHIP, and thereby invented the power to evict**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SENTENCE: the revolts resembled one another *"NOT BECAUSE OF SOME 'NATIONAL' IMPULSE OR COMMON EFFORT, but because they were protesting against CONDITIONS THAT WERE COMMON TO THEM."*** ⭐ **Resemblance is not connection**
