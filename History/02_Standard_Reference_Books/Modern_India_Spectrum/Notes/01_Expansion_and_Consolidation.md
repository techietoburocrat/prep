# 🏛️ PART A — THE CONQUESTS

## 1️⃣ The imperial frame, and the debate ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE TWO BRITISH EMPIRES **
   ================================================================
   ** THE 'FIRST EMPIRE' ** -- "stretching across the ATLANTIC
      towards ** AMERICA AND THE WEST INDIES ** ."
   ⭐ ** THE 'SECOND EMPIRE' ** -- "beginning around ** 1783
      ( PEACE OF PARIS ) ** and swinging towards ** THE EAST --
      ASIA AND AFRICA ** ."

   ** THE STARTING POINT: "The imperial history of Britain
      started with ** THE CONQUEST OF IRELAND in the SIXTEENTH
      CENTURY ** . The English then sprang up as the ** 'NEW
      ROMANS' ** , charged with civilising so-called backward
      races throughout the world." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE MECHANISM: "for this, the
      POST-ENLIGHTENMENT INTELLECTUALS of Britain... started
      ** CERTIFYING THEMSELVES AS CIVILISED VIS-A-VIS THE ORIENT
      PEOPLES ** ." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT LAST CLAUSE IS THE SHARPEST SENTENCE IN THE OPENING AND IT IS WORTH QUOTING — *"certifying THEMSELVES as civilised."*** ⭐ **The civilising mission was not a conclusion reached about India; it was a self-description adopted in Europe and then applied outward.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the chronology matters: the "second empire" turns east in **1783** — the same decade the first empire lost America.** *India was not Britain's first choice of imperial theatre; it became the centrepiece **after** the Atlantic empire collapsed.* *(⭐ **This connects directly to [Cl.10 Ch.3](../../../01_NCERT/Class_10_India_and_Contemporary_World_II/Notes/03_The_Making_of_a_Global_World.md) on the making of a global world, and to [Cl.8 Ch.6](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/06_Civilising_the_Native_Educating_the_Nation.md), which shows the "civilising" claim being turned into EDUCATION POLICY.**)*

**⚖️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ "WAS THE BRITISH CONQUEST ACCIDENTAL OR INTENTIONAL?" — a set question, and the answer is a third position:**

| Position | The claim |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ACCIDENTAL SCHOOL** *(John Seeley)* | **"Our acquisition of India was made BLINDLY. Nothing great that has ever been done by Englishman was done so UNINTENTIONALLY and so ACCIDENTALLY, as the conquest of India."** ⭐ *Spectrum's gloss: conquest in **"a FIT OF ABSENT-MINDEDNESS"** — the English came to trade, and were *"UNWILLINGLY DRAWN INTO the political turmoil created by the Indians themselves"** |
| ⭐ **THE INTENTIONAL SCHOOL** | *The British came *"with the CLEAR INTENTION of establishing a large and powerful empire, a plan which they completed by working on it BIT BY BIT."* They **dismiss as PROPAGANDA** the claim of peaceful intent* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **JUDITH BROWN's caution** | **"The deeper reasons of intention and motive… are MORE OBSCURE… for the expansion occurred in SUCH DIFFERENT PARTS OF INDIA AT DIFFERENT TIMES. In each particular situation the precise British interests at stake VARIED, and the perceived danger to them; as did the relative weight in decision-making of DIFFERENT BRITISH GROUPS"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Spectrum's own verdict** | *"**BOTH THE SCHOOLS APPEAR TO BE OVERSTATING THEIR VIEWPOINTS.** Initially, perhaps, the Company officials started acquiring territory **just to promote and protect their TRADE interests**… **But LATER ON** the British politicians back in Britain… worked on **a CLEAR DESIRE AND PLAN to acquire territories**"* |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ANSWER TO THIS QUESTION IS A CHRONOLOGY, NOT A SIDE — AND B.L. GROVER'S DATES ARE THE WAY TO SAY IT.** ⭐ *Spectrum quotes him: **"FROM 1798 TO 1818 THE BRITISH MOTIVES WERE CONSCIOUSLY IMPERIALISTIC.** Lord Wellesley resorted to aggressive application of the subsidiary alliance system… **Lord Hastings further carried the policy of Wellesley and TREATED INDIA AS A CONQUERED RATHER THAN AN ACQUIRED COUNTRY.** Thereafter, the British seemed to work on **A SET DESIGN to conquer the whole of India, and even some neighbouring states.**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So: opportunistic before Wellesley, deliberate after him. That single sentence resolves the debate without picking a school — and it explains why the frontier policy of Part B exists at all.** *(⭐ **And notice Judith Brown's real point, which is subtler than either school: there was no single "British interest" — there were **different British groups** with different stakes, making different decisions in different provinces. **"The British" is not one actor.**)*

**📅 WHEN DID "THE BRITISH PERIOD" BEGIN? — three candidate dates:**

| Year | The claim |
|:--:|---|
| **1740** | *The **Anglo-French struggle** begins, in the wake of the **War of Austrian Succession*** |
| ⭐ **1757** | ***PLASSEY*** |
| ⭐ **1761** | ***THE THIRD BATTLE OF PANIPAT*** — the Marathas defeated by Abdali |

⚠️ **And Spectrum's honest verdict:** *"**ALL SUCH CHRONOLOGICAL LANDMARKS ARE SOMEWHAT ARBITRARY** because the political transformation which began around that time **TOOK ABOUT EIGHTY YEARS TO COMPLETE**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"it would probably be **a MISTAKE to interpret [that period] in terms of what we know in the present**"* — *in 1761 a contemporary would still have rated **the Marathas** and **Haidar Ali** as serious contenders.*

## 2️⃣ The six causes of British success ⭐⭐⭐⭐

| # | Cause | The substance |
|:--:|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐ **Superior arms and strategy** | **Muskets and cannon "better than the Indian arms both in SPEED OF FIRING and in RANGE."** ⚠️ *Indian rulers imported European arms and officers — but **"in the absence of ORIGINALITY, the military officers and armies of Indian rulers became MERE IMITATORS"*** |
| **2** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Discipline and REGULAR SALARY** | *"A regular system of payment of salaries and a strict regime of discipline."* ⚠️ **"Most of the Indian rulers did not have enough money to pay salaries regularly. THE MARATHAS AT TIMES DIVERTED THEIR MILITARY CAMPAIGNS TO COLLECT REVENUE SO AS TO PAY THEIR TROOPS"** |
| **3** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Civil discipline and FAIR SELECTION** | Company officers promoted **"on the basis of RELIABILITY AND SKILL and NOT on HEREDITARY or CASTE AND CLAN ties."** ⚠️ *Indian officers appointed **"on the basis of caste and personal relations, often DISREGARDING MERIT"*** |
| **4** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Leadership AND SECOND-LINE LEADERS** | **Clive, Warren Hastings, Elphinstone, Munro, Dalhousie** — plus **"a long list of SECONDARY LEADERS like Sir EYRE COOTE, LORD LAKE and ARTHUR WELLESLEY."** ⚠️ *India had **Haidar Ali, Tipu, Chin Kulich Khan, Madhu Rao Sindhia, Yashwant Rao Holkar** — but **"often LACKED A TEAM OF SECOND LINE trained personnel"*** |
| **5** | **Strong financial backup** | Company income covered **dividends AND wars**; Britain's world trade supplied reserves, *"thanks to their superiority in SEA POWER"* |
| **6** | ⚠️ **"Nationalist pride"** | *Spectrum's weakest cause — see the [VERIFY] register* |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CAUSE 4 CARRIES THE REAL ARGUMENT, AND SPECTRUM STATES IT IN ONE DEVASTATING LINE: *"THE INDIAN LEADERS WERE AS MUCH FIGHTING AGAINST ONE ANOTHER AS AGAINST THE BRITISH… THEY OFTEN SUPPORTED THE BRITISH AGAINST NEIGHBOURING RULERS. THE CONSCIOUSNESS OF 'INDIA' WAS LACKING."*** ⭐ **And this file proves it four times over: the **Nizam and the Marathas fought WITH the English against Tipu** in the Third and Fourth Mysore Wars; **Ranjit Singh let British troops cross his territory twice**; **the Sikh commanders LAL SINGH and TEJA SINGH betrayed their own army** at Mudki and Sobraon.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The British did not defeat an Indian coalition. There was never an Indian coalition to defeat.** *(⭐ **This is the identical structural point [Class 7 makes about Panipat](../../../01_NCERT/Class_07_Our_Pasts_II/Notes/08_Eighteenth_Century_Political_Formations.md) — the Marathas' expansion "produced revenue but not allies", so they faced Abdali alone. **Same disease, different decade.**)*
>
> ⚠️ **And cause 2 deserves a moment, because it is the least glamorous and possibly the most decisive: the Company paid on time.** *A Maratha commander diverting a campaign to collect the revenue to pay his own troops is not a footnote — **it is an army whose logistics dictate its strategy.***

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bengal — from Plassey to Dual Government

> ⚠️ **[Class 8 Ch.2](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/02_From_Trade_to_Territory.md) owns Plassey and the Diwani. Take from here the NAWABS, MIR KASIM, BUXAR, ALLAHABAD and DUAL GOVERNMENT.**

**💰 Why Bengal — the numbers that explain everything:**

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The richest province** | Bengal *(including today's **Bangladesh**, with authority over **Bihar and Odisha**)*. Exports: **saltpetre, rice, indigo, pepper, sugar, silk, cotton textiles, handicrafts** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The share** | **"Nearly 60 PER CENT of the British imports from ASIA consisted of goods from BENGAL"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The bargain** | **The Company paid the Mughal emperor Rs 3,000 (£350) PER ANNUM to trade freely — while its exports from Bengal were worth MORE THAN £50,000 PER ANNUM** |
| ⭐ **Growth** | **Calcutta's population rose from 15,000 (1706) to 100,000 (1750)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Why Bengal was rich** | *"the rest of India was disturbed by **inter-border disputes, the MARATHA INVASIONS, JAT REVOLTS**, and external invasions by **NADIR SHAH and AHMED SHAH ABDALI**. **The region of Bengal was FORTUNATE ENOUGH TO ESCAPE THESE**"* |

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **£350 IN, £50,000 OUT. That ratio — roughly 1:143 — is the whole colonial relationship in two numbers, and it is worth quoting.** ⭐ **And Spectrum names the resulting friction precisely: *"almost ALL the governors of Bengal STRONGLY RESENTED the special privileges enjoyed by the English company as it meant A HUGE LOSS TO THE PROVINCIAL EXCHEQUER. So the friction between the English commercial interests and the Bengal government became THE CHIEF CAUSE FOR CONFLICT."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Plassey and Buxar were not wars of conquest that happened to have a commercial background. They were TAX DISPUTES that escalated.**

**👑 The nawabs of Bengal — the sequence:**

| Nawab | Dates | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **MURSHID QULI KHAN** | **1700–1727** | Became **Dewan of Bengal** in 1700 |
| **SHUJAUDDIN** | to **1739** | His son-in-law |
| **SARFARAZ KHAN** | **1739–40** | *"an INCAPABLE son of Murshid Quli Khan"* — **killed by Alivardi Khan** |
| ⭐ **ALIVARDI KHAN** | **1741–1756** | Deputy Governor of Bihar; killed Sarfaraz and **paid the Mughal emperor to certify himself Subahdar**. ⭐ **Fought off the Marathas — and STOPPED PAYING TRIBUTE to the Mughal emperor.** ⚠️ *Late in life he was **"urged to EXPEL THE EUROPEANS from Bengal"** after seeing what had happened in the Carnatic — **but died in April 1756*** |
| ⭐ **SIRAJ-UD-DAULA** | **1756–57** | Alivardi's **grandson** *(son of his youngest daughter)*, **"a youth just in his TWENTIETH year"** |
| **MIR JAFAR** | 1757–60, then 1763–65 | |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MIR KASIM** | **1760–63** | See below |
| **NAJIM-UD-DAULA** | from 1765 | Mir Jafar's minor son — ⚠️ *"the **REAL POWER lay in the hands of the NAIB-SUBAHDAR, who could be APPOINTED OR DISMISSED BY THE ENGLISH**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Alivardi detail is the great "what if" of the period: the one nawab strong enough to expel the Europeans realised it too late and died the same year.** *His grandson inherited the problem at twenty.*

**⚔️ SIRAJ'S FIVE PROBLEMS — before the English are even counted:**

> ⭐ **(1)** a rival cousin, **SHAUKAT JANG, Nawab of Purnea** · **(2)** a hostile aunt, **GHASITI BEGUM** · **(3)** a rebellious army commander, **MIR JAFAR** · **(4)** *"an ALARMED (Hindu) subject population"* · **(5)** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **a dominant court group — JAGAT SETH, OMICHAND, RAI BALLABH, RAI DURLABH — "who were OPPOSED TO HIM."**

⚠️ *"**Impulsive by nature and lacking experience, Siraj felt insecure**, and this prompted him to act in ways which proved **COUNTER PRODUCTIVE**."* ⭐ He **defeated and killed Shaukat Jang**, **divested Ghasiti Begum of her treasures**, and **dismissed Mir Jafar**, replacing him with **MIR MADAN**; the Kashmiri officer **MOHAN LAL** became overall administrator.

> ⚠️ 📦 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ON THE "BLACK HOLE TRAGEDY" — Spectrum's handling is careful and you should copy it:** *"Siraj-ud-daula is **BELIEVED TO HAVE** imprisoned 146 English persons… due to which 123 of them died of suffocation. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HOWEVER, HISTORIANS EITHER DO NOT BELIEVE THIS STORY, OR SAY THAT THE NUMBER OF VICTIMS MUST HAVE BEEN MUCH SMALLER.**"* ⭐ **Never assert the 146/123 figures. State them as the claim, and state the doubt** — the episode's real historical importance is as **a JUSTIFICATION manufactured after the fact**, not as an event.

### 3.1 MIR KASIM — the puppet who wouldn't ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**📜 The Treaty of 1760** *(with Vansittart, Governor of Calcutta)* — Mir Kasim agreed to:

> **(i)** cede **BURDWAN, MIDNAPUR and CHITTAGONG** · **(ii)** give the Company **half the share in the CHUNAM trade of SYLHET** · **(iii)** pay off outstanding dues · **(iv)** pay **Rs 5 LAKH** towards the Company's war in southern India · ⭐ **(v)** *"Mir Kasim's enemies were the Company's enemies, and his friends, the Company's friends"* · **(vi)** neither side's tenants to settle on the other's lands.

⭐ *Mir Jafar resigned in his favour, on **a pension of Rs 1,500 per annum**.*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ What Mir Kasim then did — and this is why he matters:**

> *"Mir Kasim was **THE ABLEST NAWAB AMONG THE SUCCESSORS OF ALIVARDI KHAN**. After assuming power, he **SHIFTED THE CAPITAL FROM MURSHIDABAD TO MUNGER IN BIHAR** — ⭐ **the move was taken to allow A SAFE DISTANCE FROM THE COMPANY AT CALCUTTA**. His other steps were **REORGANISING THE BUREAUCRACY with men of his own choice** and **REMODELLING THE ARMY to enhance its skill and efficiency**."*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MIR KASIM IS THE MOST INTERESTING FIGURE IN THIS CHAPTER AND HE IS ALMOST ABSENT FROM YOUR NOTES — [Class 8 mentions him in ONE file](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/02_From_Trade_to_Territory.md).** ⭐ **Every move he made was a deliberate attempt to escape the trap Plassey had set: move the capital 300 miles upriver, replace the officials the Company had cultivated, and rebuild the army.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He is the test case for whether the post-Plassey settlement could be reversed from inside — and the answer, at Buxar, was no.** *(⭐ **And the pathos of the ending is worth a line: *"Mir Kasim… spent the rest of his life in ABJECT MISERY AS A HOMELESS WANDERER and died in June 1777."***)*

### 3.2 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The *DASTAK* abuse — the real cause of Buxar

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  HOW A TRADE PERMIT CAUSED A WAR
   ================================================================

   ** THE ** DASTAK ** = a trade permit "which EXEMPTED THE GOODS
      SPECIFIED FROM PAYMENT OF DUTIES." **
   ** By an IMPERIAL FARMAN the Company had the right to trade
      in Bengal ** WITHOUT PAYING TRANSIT DUES OR TOLLS ** . **

   ⚠️ ** THE FOUR ABUSES: **
     (1) ** "The SERVANTS of the Company ALSO CLAIMED THE SAME
         PRIVILEGES FOR THEIR ** PRIVATE ** TRADE." **
     (2) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** "The Company's servants also ** SOLD DASTAK
         TO INDIAN MERCHANTS FOR A COMMISSION ** ." **
     (3) ** "They used ** COERCIVE METHODS to get goods at
         CHEAPER RATES ** ." **
     (4) ** The result: ** LOSS OF TAX REVENUE to the nawab ** ,
         and ** LOCAL MERCHANTS FACING UNEQUAL COMPETITION ** . **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** MIR KASIM'S SOLUTION -- AND IT IS THE HINGE OF
      THE WHOLE EPISODE: **
     ** "Mir Kasim decided to ** ABOLISH THE DUTIES ALTOGETHER ** ,
       BUT ** THE BRITISH PROTESTED AGAINST THIS AND INSISTED
       UPON HAVING PREFERENTIAL TREATMENT ** as against other
       traders." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **STOP AND LOOK AT WHAT MIR KASIM ACTUALLY DID, BECAUSE IT IS THE CLEVEREST MOVE ANY INDIAN RULER MAKES IN THIS CHAPTER — AND IT IS WHY THE BRITISH HAD TO FIGHT HIM.** ⭐ **The Company's advantage was not free trade; it was a *DIFFERENTIAL* — it paid no duty while Indian merchants did. So Mir Kasim abolished the duty **for everybody**.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That did not cost the Company a rupee. It cost the Company its MONOPOLY — and the British "protested" and demanded **preferential treatment**, which is an admission that the privilege, not the trade, was the point.** *(⭐ **Deploy this whenever a question asks whether British expansion was commercial or political. **Here the two are the same thing: the Company went to war to preserve a tax exemption it could only enjoy if Indians did not share it.** ⭐ **It also pairs exactly with [Class 8's account of the trade-privilege abuse before Plassey](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/02_From_Trade_to_Territory.md) — the same grievance, escalated.**)*

⭐ **The war of 1763:** the English won successively at **KATWAH, MURSHIDABAD, GIRIA, SOOTY and MUNGER**. **Mir Kasim fled to AWADH** and formed a confederacy with **SHUJA-UD-DAULAH, Nawab of Awadh**, and the Mughal Emperor **SHAH ALAM II**.

### 3.3 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE BATTLE OF BUXAR — 22 October 1764

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Date** | **22 OCTOBER 1764** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **English commander** | **MAJOR HECTOR MUNRO** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The defeated coalition** | **MIR KASIM** *(Bengal)* + **SHUJA-UD-DAULAH** *(Awadh)* + **SHAH ALAM II** *(the Mughal Emperor)* |
| **The battle** | *"a **CLOSELY CONTESTED** battle"*; the campaign **"SHORT BUT DECISIVE"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The significance** | *"the importance lay in the fact that **NOT ONLY THE NAWAB OF BENGAL BUT ALSO THE MUGHAL EMPEROR OF INDIA WAS DEFEATED** by the English. The victory made the English **A GREAT POWER IN NORTHERN INDIA and CONTENDERS FOR THE SUPREMACY OVER THE WHOLE COUNTRY**"* |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE STANDARD EXAM POINT IS THAT BUXAR MATTERED MORE THAN PLASSEY, AND SPECTRUM GIVES YOU THE EXACT REASON: AT PLASSEY THE ENGLISH BEAT A PROVINCIAL GOVERNOR; AT BUXAR THEY BEAT THE MUGHAL EMPEROR HIMSELF.** ⭐ **Plassey was won by conspiracy — Mir Jafar's defection. Buxar was won in a *"closely contested"* fight against three allied powers.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Plassey made the Company the power behind the nawab of one province. Buxar made it the power that could dictate to the emperor of India — and that is the difference between a local coup and a claim to paramountcy.**
>
> 📖 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **G.B. MALLESON's verdict, worth quoting:** *"Whether regarded as a duel between the foreigner and the native, or as an event pregnant with vast permanent consequences, **BUXAR TAKES RANK AMONGST THE MOST DECISIVE BATTLES EVER FOUGHT.** Not only did the victory of the English **SAVE BENGAL**, not only did it **ADVANCE THE BRITISH FRONTIER TO ALLAHABAD**, but it **BOUND THE RULERS OF AWADH TO THE CONQUEROR**… for the **NINETY-FOUR YEARS that followed**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *(Ninety-four years from 1764 lands on **1858** — the year after the Revolt, in which Awadh was the epicentre. **Malleson's own arithmetic dates the end of the arrangement to the year it broke.**)*
>
> 📖 ⭐ **And PERCIVAL SPEAR on Clive:** *"**Clive was NOT A FOUNDER but A HARBINGER of the future. He was not a planner of empire but AN EXPERIMENTER WHO REVEALED SOMETHING OF THE POSSIBILITIES.**"*

### 3.4 The Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Clive concluded TWO treaties at Allahabad — and you need both halves:**

| With **SHUJA-UD-DAULA** *(Awadh)* | With **SHAH ALAM II** *(the Emperor)* |
|---|---|
| **(i)** surrender **ALLAHABAD and KARA** to Shah Alam II | **(i)** reside at **ALLAHABAD**, ceded by Awadh, **under the Company's protection** |
| **(ii)** pay **Rs 50 LAKH** to the Company as **war indemnity** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **(ii)** issue a ***FARMAN* granting the DIWANI of BENGAL, BIHAR and ORISSA to the Company**, in lieu of **an annual payment of Rs 26 LAKH** |
| **(iii)** give **BALWANT SINGH, Zamindar of Banaras**, full possession of his estate | **(iii)** a provision of **Rs 53 LAKH** to the Company in return for ***NIZAMAT* functions** *(military defence, police, and administration of justice)* |

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  WHY CLIVE DID **NOT** ANNEX AWADH -- A SET
              QUESTION, AND THE ANSWER IS STRATEGIC
   ================================================================
   ** "Clive did not want to annex Awadh because it would have
      placed the Company ** UNDER AN OBLIGATION TO PROTECT AN
      EXTENSIVE LAND FRONTIER FROM THE AFGHAN AND THE MARATHA
      INVASIONS ** ." **

   ⭐ ** "The treaty made the Nawab ** A FIRM FRIEND ** of the
      Company, and ** TURNED AWADH INTO A BUFFER STATE ** ." **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND ON THE EMPEROR: "Clive's arrangement with
      Shah Alam II was inspired by ** PRACTICAL CONSIDERATIONS.
      IT MADE THE EMPEROR A USEFUL 'RUBBER STAMP' OF THE
      COMPANY ** . Besides, ** THE EMPEROR'S FARMAN LEGALISED THE
      POLITICAL GAINS OF THE COMPANY IN BENGAL ** ." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE MOST INSTRUCTIVE PAIR OF DECISIONS IN THE CHAPTER, AND TOGETHER THEY DEFINE THE COMPANY'S METHOD FOR THE NEXT NINETY YEARS.** ⭐ **Awadh was NOT annexed — because annexation means owning a frontier, and frontiers cost money.** ⭐ **The Emperor was NOT deposed — because a defeated emperor who signs a *farman* makes the conquest **LEGAL**.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Take the revenue, leave the responsibility; take the power, leave the title.** *That is the ring-fence and the Subsidiary Alliance of Part B already in embryo — and it is why the Company grew so fast: **it was collecting the profits of sovereignty without paying its costs.***

### 3.5 DUAL GOVERNMENT, 1765–72 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **What it was** | ***"the rule of the TWO — the Company and the Nawab"*** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The two rights** | ***DIWANI*** = **collecting revenues** · ***NIZAMAT*** = **police and judicial functions** — *both came under Company control* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **How each was acquired** | **The *DIWANI* from THE EMPEROR; the *NIZAMAT* from THE SUBAHDAR of Bengal** *(exercised through the Company's right to **nominate the deputy subahdar**)* |
| ⭐ **The deputies** | **MOHAMMAD REZA KHAN for BENGAL** *(also deputy nazim)* and **RAJA SITAB ROY for BIHAR** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The advantage** | *"It left **THE APPEARANCE OF AUTHORITY to the puppet Indian ruler, while keeping THE SOVEREIGN POWER in the hands of the Company.** The nawab was **RESPONSIBLE FOR MAINTAINING PEACE AND ORDER, but he depended BOTH FOR FUNDS AND FORCES upon the Company**"* |
| ⚠️ **The result** | *"led to **AN ADMINISTRATIVE BREAKDOWN** and proved **DISASTROUS FOR THE PEOPLE OF BENGAL. NEITHER THE COMPANY NOR THE NAWAB CARED FOR ADMINISTRATION AND PUBLIC WELFARE**"* |
| ⭐ **Ended by** | **WARREN HASTINGS, in 1772** |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DUAL GOVERNMENT IS THE PUREST EXAMPLE IN INDIAN HISTORY OF POWER WITHOUT RESPONSIBILITY, AND THAT PHRASE IS THE ANSWER TO ANY QUESTION ABOUT IT.** ⭐ **The Company held the revenue and the army. The Nawab held the duty to keep order — and neither the funds nor the forces to do it.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Accountability was assigned to the party without capacity, and capacity to the party without accountability. Bengal starved in the gap** *(the great famine of 1770 falls squarely inside these seven years)*. *(⭐ **This is the administrative half of the story [Class 8 tells economically](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/02_From_Trade_to_Territory.md) — the Diwani let the Company buy Indian goods with Indian revenue. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Put the two together: **the Company extracted the surplus AND disclaimed the government.** That is the completed argument, and neither book gives you both halves alone.**)*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Mysore — Haidar Ali, Tipu, and the four wars

**🐅 Why Mysore mattered to the English:**

> ⭐ *"The English felt their political and commercial interests in south India were threatened because of **MYSORE'S PROXIMITY WITH THE FRENCH** and Haidar Ali and Tipu's **CONTROL OVER THE RICH TRADE OF THE MALABAR COAST**. Mysore's power was also seen as **A THREAT TO THE CONTROL OF THE ENGLISH OVER MADRAS**."*

**⚙️ The rise of Haidar Ali — and note that he is a usurper, not an heir:**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **The dynasty he displaced** | ⭐ **The WODEYARS**, a Hindu kingdom **emerging in Mysore in 1612** after Talikota. **CHIKKA KRISHNARAJA WODEYAR II ruled 1734–66** — reduced to *"a mere puppet"* by two brothers, **NANJARAJ (the *sarvadhikari*) and DEVARAJ (the *Dulwai*)** |
| ⭐ **His origins** | **Born 1721 "in an OBSCURE FAMILY"**, starting as **a HORSEMAN in the Mysore army**. ⭐ *"**Though UNEDUCATED**, he possessed a keen intellect and was a man of great energy and determination"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **When he took over** | **De facto ruler of Mysore in 1761** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **His three-part military diagnosis** | *"He realised that **(1) the exceedingly mobile MARATHAS could be contained only by A SWIFT CAVALRY**; **(2) the cannons of the FRENCH-TRAINED NIZAMI ARMY could be silenced only by AN EFFECTIVE ARTILLERY**; and **(3) the SUPERIOR ARMS FROM THE WEST could only be matched by ARMS BROUGHT FROM THE SAME PLACE OR MANUFACTURED WITH THE SAME KNOW-HOW**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **What he did about it** | **Took French help to set up an ARMS FACTORY AT DINDIGUL** *(now Tamil Nadu)*, and **introduced WESTERN METHODS OF TRAINING** |
| **His conquests** | **DOD BALLAPUR, SERA, BEDNUR, HOSKOTE (1761–63)** — and ⭐ **brought the POLIGARS of South India to submission** |
| ⚠️ **His setbacks** | **Defeated by the Marathas under MADHAVRAO in 1764, 1766 and 1771**; after Madhavrao's death in 1772 he **raided them repeatedly in 1774–76 and recovered everything** |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HAIDAR ALI'S THREE-PART DIAGNOSIS IS THE BEST STRATEGIC THINKING BY ANY INDIAN RULER IN THIS CHAPTER, AND IT ANSWERS SPECTRUM'S OWN CHARGE AGAINST INDIAN ARMIES.** ⭐ **§2 said Indian rulers who imported European arms became *"MERE IMITATORS"* for lack of originality. Haidar did the opposite: he identified **three different enemies requiring three different answers** — cavalry for the Marathas, artillery for the Nizam, and **domestic manufacture** for the Europeans.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Building an arms factory at Dindigul is not imitation; it is an attempt to end the dependency that made imitation necessary.** *(⭐ **And note the pattern this file keeps producing: like [Gopala in Bengal](../../TN_State_Board_History/Class_11/Notes/07_Harsha_and_Regional_Kingdoms.md) and [Shivaji](../../TN_State_Board_History/Class_11/Notes/06_Turks_and_Marathas.md), Haidar had **no dynastic claim at all** — an uneducated horseman from an obscure family who became the most formidable opponent the Company faced in the south.**)*

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE FOUR ANGLO-MYSORE WARS — the table to memorise

| War | Dates | Trigger | Course | Treaty / Outcome |
|:--:|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **FIRST** | **1767–69** | The English allied with **the NIZAM (1766, giving them the NORTHERN CIRCARS)** and the Marathas against Haidar | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Haidar out-diplomatted them — "PAID THE MARATHAS TO TURN THEM NEUTRAL" and "PROMISING TO SHARE CONQUERED TERRITORIES, CONVERTED THE NIZAM INTO HIS ALLY."** Then **appeared suddenly before the gates of MADRAS**, causing *"complete chaos and panic"* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TREATY OF MADRAS, 4 April 1769** — *"a very **HUMILIATING** treaty"* for the English: **exchange of prisoners, mutual restitution of conquests**, and ⭐ **the English promised to HELP HAIDAR IF HE WERE ATTACKED BY ANY OTHER POWER** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SECOND** | **1780–84** | ⭐ **The English BROKE the Treaty of Madras** — when the Marathas attacked Haidar in 1771 **they did not come to his aid**. Also, the English **tried to capture MAHE**, a French possession on the Malabar coast **which Haidar regarded as under his protection** | Haidar allied with **the Marathas and the Nizam**, **captured ARCOT**, and **defeated Colonel BAILLIE (1781)**. ⚠️ **Sir EYRE COOTE detached both allies** and beat Haidar at **PORTO NOVO (Nov 1781)** — but Haidar regrouped and **captured the English commander BRAITHWAITE**. ⭐ **HAIDAR DIED OF CANCER, 7 December 1782**; **TIPU carried on for a year** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TREATY OF MANGALORE, March 1784** — *"each party gave back the territories it had taken from the other"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIRD** | **1790–92** | ⭐ **TRAVANCORE** purchased **JALKOTTAL and CANNANORE** from the Dutch in **COCHIN** — *and as **Cochin was a feudatory of Tipu**, he treated it as **a violation of his sovereign rights** and declared war on Travancore in April 1790* | The English sided with Travancore. **Tipu defeated General MEADOWS in 1790**; then ⭐ **CORNWALLIS took command**, marched via **Ambur and Vellore to BANGALORE (captured March 1791)** and on to **SERINGAPATAM**; with **Maratha and Nizam support** he attacked Seringapatam a second time | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TREATY OF SERINGAPATAM, 1792** — **NEARLY HALF of Mysore taken.** **BARAMAHAL, DINDIGUL and MALABAR → the English**; the Tungabhadra region → **the Marathas**; Krishna-to-Pennar → **the Nizam**. ⭐ **A war damage of THREE CRORE RUPEES**, half immediately — and ⚠️ **TIPU'S TWO SONS TAKEN AS HOSTAGES** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FOURTH** | **1799** | ⭐ **WELLESLEY** succeeded Sir John Shore in 1798 — *"**AN IMPERIALIST TO THE CORE**"* — and **aimed at "ANNIHILATING TIPU'S INDEPENDENT EXISTENCE or forcing him into the SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE."* The charge-sheet: Tipu had **sent emissaries to Arabia, Afghanistan, Kabul, Zaman Shah, Isle of France (Mauritius) and Versailles** | **17 April – 4 May 1799.** Tipu defeated by **General STUART** and then **General HARRIS**; ⭐ **ARTHUR WELLESLEY, the Governor-General's brother, took part**. **The Marathas and the Nizam again helped the English** | ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TIPU LAID DOWN HIS LIFE FIGHTING**; family interned at **VELLORE**, treasures confiscated. **A boy of the old Hindu WODEYAR line made maharaja — and given the SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE** |

**🗺️ MYSORE AFTER TIPU — the partition, and the long tail:**

> ⭐ **Wellesley offered SOONDA and HARPONELLY to the Marathas — *they REFUSED*.** · **The Nizam got GOOTY and GURRAMKONDA.** · ⭐ **The English took KANARA, WYNAD, COIMBATORE, DWARAPORAM and SERINGAPATAM.** · **The new Mysore state went to KRISHNARAJA III** under the Subsidiary Alliance. · ⚠️ **1831 — WILLIAM BENTINCK took control "on grounds of MISGOVERNANCE."** · ⭐ **1881 — LORD RIPON RESTORED the kingdom to its ruler.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ THE FOUR WARS AS ONE ARGUMENT ABOUT ALLIANCES AND YOU HAVE A FIRST-CLASS ANSWER.** ⭐ **War 1: Haidar BOUGHT OFF the Marathas and TURNED the Nizam — and won a humiliating treaty from the English. War 2: the English DETACHED both from him — and it became a draw. Wars 3 and 4: the Marathas and the Nizam fought ON THE ENGLISH SIDE — and Mysore was destroyed.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The military balance barely changed across thirty years. What changed was who the Marathas and the Nizam were standing with — and that is **§2's "consciousness of India was lacking"** demonstrated in four instalments.** *(⭐ **And note the price the allies paid for their choice: the Nizam signed the Subsidiary Alliance before the fourth war, and the Marathas were destroyed by the English within twenty years of Tipu's death. **They helped dismantle the only southern power that could have balanced the Company, and were dismantled next.**)*

## 5️⃣ The Marathas — cross-link, plus "why they lost" ⭐⭐⭐

> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **[TN Unit 15](../../TN_State_Board_History/Class_11/Notes/06_Turks_and_Marathas.md) has all three Anglo-Maratha Wars in full** — **Salbai 1782**, **Bassein 1802** *(Rs 26 lakh)*, the **Treaty of Poona 1817**, Ashta/Kirkee/Korgaon, **Baji Rao II's surrender to Elphinstone in 1818**, and the **five outcomes** *(Peshwai abolished, jagirs restored, Pratap Singh at Satara, the Confederacy dissolved, Elphinstone as Governor of Bombay)*. **Do not rewrite it.** ⭐ **Take only what follows.**

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The conquest of Sindh — the episode the British could not justify

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **The predecessors** | **The KALLORA chiefs.** ⭐ *An English factory at **THATTA in 1758** on a *parwana* from **GHULAM SHAH**, who in **1761** ratified the treaty and **excluded other Europeans** — an advantage held until **1775**, when **SARFRAZ KHAN** made the English close the factory* |
| ⭐ **The TALPURAS** | *"In the 1770s, a **BALUCH tribe called TALPURAS** descended from the hills… **excellent soldiers**."* ⭐ **1783 — under MIR FATH (FATAH) ALI KHAN they took complete hold and exiled the Kallora prince.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **On Mir Fath's death in 1800, his brothers — the *CHAR YAR* — divided the kingdom, calling themselves the AMIRS or LORDS OF SINDH** |
| **The first rebuff** | ⭐ **1799 — Wellesley sought commercial relations to counter the FRENCH–TIPU–ZAMAN SHAH alliance.** ⚠️ **In October 1800 the Amir ORDERED THE BRITISH AGENT (CROW) TO QUIT SINDH WITHIN TEN DAYS — "and the Company QUIETLY SUFFERED THE INSULT"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Treaty of "ETERNAL FRIENDSHIP"** | *After the **1807 Treaty of TILSIT** *(Napoleon + Alexander I, with a combined land invasion of India as a condition)*, **Lord MINTO sent three missions — METCALFE to LAHORE, ELPHINSTONE to KABUL, MALCOLM to TEHERAN** — and **NICHOLAS SMITH to Sindh**. ⭐ **The Amirs' FIRST-EVER treaty with the English: exclude the FRENCH, exchange agents.** *Renewed **1820**, adding **the exclusion of AMERICANS**.* |

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Punjab — Ranjit Singh and the two Anglo-Sikh Wars

**⚔️ From Banda Bahadur to the *MISLS*:**

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Banda Bahadur** | Revolted after **Guru Govind Singh's** murder; **defeated by FARRUKHSIYAR in 1715 and put to death in 1716** |
| ⚠️ **The split** | The Sikh polity divided into **BANDAI (liberal)** and **TAT KHALSA (orthodox)** — ⭐ *the rift **ended in 1721 under BHAI MANI SINGH*** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DAL KHALSA** | **Organised in 1784 by KAPUR SINGH FAIZULLAPURIA**, to unite the Sikhs **politically, culturally and economically** — in two sections: ⭐ **BUDHA DAL, "the army of the VETERANS", and TARUNA DAL, "the army of the YOUNG"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The *MISLS*** | *"military brotherhoods with **A DEMOCRATIC SET-UP**."* ⭐ ***MISL* is an ARABIC word meaning "EQUAL or ALIKE"** *(another meaning: "STATE")*. **1763–1773 — many misls ruled Punjab "from SAHARANPUR in the east to ATTOCK in the west… to MULTAN in the south."** ⭐ *Central administration rested on the ***GURUMATTA SANGH*** |
| ⭐ **The twelve** | **AHLUWALIYA · BHANGI · DALLEWALIA · FAIZULLAPURIA · KANHAIYA · KROSASINGHIA · NAKKAI · NISHANIYA · PHULAKIYA · RAMGARHIYA · SUKARCHAKIYA · SHAHEED** |

**👑 RANJIT SINGH — born 2 November 1780**, son of **MAHAN SINGH** of the **SUKARCHAKIYA** misl; his father died when he was **twelve**.

> ⭐ *"Towards the close of the 18th century, **all the important misls EXCEPT SUKARCHAKIYA were in a state of DISINTEGRATION**. **Afghanistan was also engulfed in a CIVIL WAR**… These events were fully exploited by Ranjit Singh, who followed **a ruthless policy of 'BLOOD AND IRON'**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1799 — appointed GOVERNOR OF LAHORE by ZAMAN SHAH of Afghanistan.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1805 — acquired JAMMU and AMRITSAR, so that "the POLITICAL capital (Lahore) and the RELIGIOUS capital (Amritsar) came under his rule."** *He kept good relations with the **Dogras and Nepalese** and enlisted them.*

**📜 THE TREATY OF AMRITSAR, 25 April 1809 — the hinge of his reign:**

> ⭐ **Background:** *Fearing a **joint Franco-Russian invasion**, Minto sent **METCALFE to Lahore in 1807**. Ranjit Singh offered an offensive-and-defensive alliance **on condition that the English stay neutral in a Sikh–Afghan war and recognise him as sovereign of THE ENTIRE PUNJAB, INCLUDING THE MALWA (CIS-SUTLEJ) TERRITORIES.* ⚠️ **The negotiations failed** — and with the Napoleonic danger receding and the English more assertive, **he signed instead.**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **What it did:** *"It **CHECKED ONE OF THE MOST CHERISHED AMBITIONS OF RANJIT SINGH — to extend his rule over THE ENTIRE SIKH NATION — by accepting the RIVER SUTLEJ AS THE BOUNDARY LINE.** ⭐ **Now he directed his energies WESTWARD** and captured **MULTAN (1818), KASHMIR (1819) and PESHAWAR (1834)**."*

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AMRITSAR 1809 IS THE MOST CONSEQUENTIAL TREATY IN THIS SECTION AND ITS EFFECT WAS GEOGRAPHICAL: IT TURNED RANJIT SINGH AROUND.** ⭐ **He wanted to unify all the Sikhs — including those east of the Sutlej. The treaty made the Sutlej his eastern wall, so **thirty years of formidable military energy went WEST instead**: Multan, Kashmir, Peshawar.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The British did not have to fight him. They redirected him** — and the cis-Sutlej Sikh states stayed outside his kingdom and inside the British sphere. *(⭐ **And Spectrum's verdict on his later position is unsparing: *"The relations of Raja Ranjit Singh with the Company, from 1809 to 1839, clearly indicate the former's WEAK POSITION. Although he was CONSCIOUS of his weak position, HE TOOK NO STEP TO ORGANISE A COALITION OF OTHER INDIAN PRINCES OR MAINTAIN A BALANCE OF POWER."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ****§2's charge again** — the strongest indigenous state of the era made no attempt at a coalition.**)*
>
> ⚠️ **One act of defiance to record, though: in the Tripartite Treaty of June 1838 he was compelled to sign — but he **REFUSED TO GIVE PASSAGE TO THE BRITISH ARMY** through his territories to attack Dost Mohammad.** *He died in **June 1839**.*

**⚰️ PUNJAB AFTER RANJIT SINGH — the collapse, in five deaths:**

> ⭐ **KHARAK SINGH** *(the only legitimate son)* was inefficient; **his sudden death in 1839** and **the accidental death of his son NAV NIHAL SINGH — returning from his father's funeral** — produced anarchy. **SHER SINGH** succeeded and was **murdered in late 1843**. ⭐ **DALEEP SINGH**, a minor son, was proclaimed Maharaja with **RANI JINDAN as regent** and **HIRA SINGH DOGRA as wazir** — *Hira Singh was **murdered in 1844**; the next wazir **JAWAHAR SINGH**, Jindan's brother, was **deposed and put to death in 1845**; then **LAL SINGH**, "a lover of Rani Jindan", became wazir, with **TEJA SINGH** commanding.*

⚠️ **And the structural weakness Spectrum names:** *"**The army — THE PILLAR OF THE SIKH STATE — was FAR LESS STRONG THAN IT APPEARED.** Ranjit Singh's able generals — **MOHKAM CHAND, DEWAN CHAND, HARI SINGH NALWA, RAM DAYAL** — were already dead. **Discontent was growing among the troops as a result of IRREGULARITY OF PAYMENT.** The appointment of **unworthy officers led to INDISCIPLINE.**"*

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE TWO ANGLO-SIKH WARS

| War | Causes | Battles | Treaty / Result |
|:--:|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FIRST (1845–46)** | ⭐ *Attributed to **the Sikh army crossing the Sutlej on 11 December 1845** — but really: **(i)** the anarchy at Lahore and **the power struggle between the court and "the ever powerful… local army"**; **(ii)** Sikh suspicion after the British **annexed GWALIOR and SINDH in 1841** and campaigned in **Afghanistan in 1842**; **(iii)** the **increase in English troops near the border*** | ⚠️ **British 20,000–30,000; Sikhs about 50,000 under LAL SINGH** — but ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"THE TREACHERY OF LAL SINGH AND TEJA SINGH caused FIVE SUCCESSIVE DEFEATS":** **MUDKI (18 Dec 1845) · FEROZESHAH (21–22 Dec) · BUDDELWAL · ALIWAL (28 Jan 1846) · SOBRAON (10 Feb 1846)**. ⭐ **Lahore fell on 20 February 1846 WITHOUT A FIGHT** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TREATY OF LAHORE, 8 March 1846** — **indemnity of over 1 CRORE**; **the JALANDHAR DOAB** *(between Beas and Sutlej)* **annexed**; **a British resident at Lahore under HENRY LAWRENCE**; **the Sikh army reduced**; Daleep Singh recognised under Jindan and Lal Singh. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Unable to pay the indemnity, KASHMIR INCLUDING JAMMU WAS SOLD TO GULAB SINGH FOR RS 75 LAKH** — formalised **16 March 1846**. ⭐ Then the **TREATY OF BHAIROWAL (Dec 1846)**: **Rani Jindan REMOVED as regent**, and **a council of regency of 8 SIKH SARDARS PRESIDED OVER BY THE ENGLISH RESIDENT** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SECOND (1848–49)** | ⚠️ *The humiliation of Lahore and Bhairowal, and **"INHUMAN TREATMENT meted out to RANI JINDAN, who was sent to BENARES as a pensioner."*** ⭐ **The immediate cause: MULRAJ, governor of MULTAN, replaced over a revenue increase, revolted and murdered two English officers; SHER SINGH, sent to suppress him, JOINED HIM** — a mass uprising. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DALHOUSIE, "a HARDCORE EXPANSIONIST", got his pretext** | **RAMNAGAR** *(under Sir HUGH GOUGH)* · **CHILLIANWALA, January 1849** · ⭐ **GUJARAT, 21 February 1849** *(a town on the JHELUM)* — **the Sikh army surrendered at RAWALPINDI and their Afghan allies were chased out of India** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PUNJAB ANNEXED.** *Dalhousie **thanked by Parliament and promoted to MARQUESS**.* ⭐ **A THREE-MEMBER BOARD to govern Punjab — the LAWRENCE BROTHERS (HENRY and JOHN) and CHARLES MANSEL.** ⭐ **1853 — the board nullified; JOHN LAWRENCE became the first CHIEF COMMISSIONER** |

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SALE OF KASHMIR IS THE SINGLE MOST CONSEQUENTIAL CLAUSE IN THIS ENTIRE CHAPTER, AND IT IS EASY TO READ PAST AS AN ACCOUNTING DETAIL.** ⭐ **The Sikhs could not pay a one-crore indemnity. So **Kashmir, including Jammu, was SOLD to GULAB SINGH for Rs 75 lakh**, formalised by the separate **Treaty of Amritsar of 16 March 1846**.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A Dogra dynasty acquired Kashmir by PURCHASE, from a defeated Sikh state, to settle a British war debt — and it was that dynasty's accession in 1947 that made Kashmir the subject it remains.** *(⭐ **Deploy this in any question touching the origins of the Kashmir issue. **The princely state whose accession was disputed in 1947 was created by a bill of sale in 1846.**)*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And note the treachery pattern: the Sikhs outnumbered the British roughly two to one in the First War and lost five straight battles because their own commander-in-chief and general were working against them.** ***§2's fourth cause** — the absence of reliable second-line leadership — but in its most extreme form: **the second line was actively hostile.**
>
> ⭐ **Spectrum's closing note is generous and worth keeping:** *"The Anglo-Sikh wars gave the two sides **A MUTUAL RESPECT FOR EACH OTHER'S FIGHTING PROWESS. The Sikhs were to fight LOYALLY ON THE BRITISH SIDE IN THE REVOLT OF 1857** and in many other campaigns up till 1947."* ⚠️ *That loyalty in 1857 is a major reason the Revolt failed in the north-west — worth carrying into [Theme 10](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/10_Theme10_Rebels_and_the_Raj_1857.md).*

# 🕸️ PART B — PARAMOUNTCY WITHOUT WAR, AND THE FRONTIER

> ⭐ **Spectrum's own framing:** *"The process of imperial expansion… was carried on during **1757–1857** through **A TWO-FOLD METHOD: (a) annexation BY CONQUEST OR WAR; and (b) annexation BY DIPLOMACY AND ADMINISTRATIVE MECHANISMS.**"* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Part A was method (a). This is method (b) — and it took more of India than the wars did.**

## 8️⃣ The Ring-Fence policy — Warren Hastings ⭐⭐⭐⭐

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Whose** | ⭐ **WARREN HASTINGS**, taking charge *"at a critical period… when the British were to encounter the powerful combination of **THE MARATHAS, MYSORE AND HYDERABAD**"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The idea** | *"aimed at **CREATING BUFFER ZONES to defend the Company's frontiers**… broadly, **THE POLICY OF DEFENCE OF THEIR NEIGHBOURS' FRONTIERS FOR SAFEGUARDING THEIR OWN**"* |
| ⭐ **The case** | **AWADH.** *The Company undertook to defend Awadh's frontiers **on condition that the Nawab DEFRAY THE EXPENSES** — because **"THE DEFENCE OF AWADH CONSTITUTED THE DEFENCE OF BENGAL"*** |
| ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The sting** | *"the states brought under the ring-fence system were assured of military assistance against external aggression — **BUT AT THEIR OWN EXPENSE**… required to **maintain SUBSIDIARY FORCES organised, equipped and COMMANDED BY THE OFFICERS OF THE COMPANY who, in turn, were TO BE PAID BY THE RULERS**"* |
| ⭐ **The succession** | *"**Wellesley's policy of subsidiary alliance was, in fact, AN EXTENSION OF THE RING-FENCE SYSTEM**, which sought to reduce the Indian states into **A POSITION OF DEPENDENCE**"* |

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The ring-fence is the ancestor, and naming it correctly is worth marks: Warren Hastings invented the SHAPE of the arrangement — YOUR frontier defended by MY troops at YOUR cost — and Wellesley merely generalised it and added the political clauses.** ⭐ **Most answers begin the story at Wellesley. Beginning it at Hastings shows you know the system was twenty-five years older than the man usually credited with it.**

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Subsidiary Alliance — Wellesley

> ⚠️ **[Class 8 Ch.2 §5](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/02_From_Trade_to_Territory.md) has the basic terms. Take from here THE FOUR STAGES and the EVOLUTION.**

**📋 The terms — the allying ruler had to:**

> ⭐ **(1)** accept **the permanent stationing of a British force** in his territory and **PAY A SUBSIDY** for it · **(2)** accept **a BRITISH RESIDENT** at his court · **(3)** **employ NO EUROPEAN without prior Company consultation** · **(4)** **neither go to war nor negotiate with any Indian ruler without consulting the Governor-General**. ⭐ *In return: **the British would defend him**, and **adopt "a policy of NON-INTERFERENCE in internal matters."***

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The French motive behind clause (3):** *"the fear of **NAPOLEON's expedition towards the East** was very real… the British felt the French could attack the western coast **FROM THEIR COLONY OF MAURITIUS**. Hence the clause requiring Indian rulers to **DISMISS EUROPEANS (other than the British) from their service.**"*

⚠️ **The four costs to the Indian ruler, as Spectrum lists them:** *"**They LOST THEIR INDEPENDENCE BY BUYING SECURITY.** They were **not free of interference from the British Resident**. They **LOST MUCH OF THEIR REVENUE**, paying for the British troops. Also, **the alliance made the Indian rulers WEAK AND IRRESPONSIBLE; the subjects were exploited, and it was practically IMPOSSIBLE TO DEPOSE THE OPPRESSIVE RULERS AS THEY WERE PROTECTED BY THE BRITISH.**"*

```
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  THE FOUR STAGES -- THE BEST THING IN THIS SECTION
              AND ALMOST NOBODY KNOWS IT
   ================================================================

   ** STAGE 1 ** -- ** The Company OFFERS TROOPS ** to help a
      friendly Indian state fight ** ITS OWN WARS ** .

   ** STAGE 2 ** -- ** COMMON CAUSE ** : the Company takes the
      field ** WITH ITS OWN SOLDIERS AND THOSE OF THE STATE ** .

   ** STAGE 3 ** -- ⭐ ** The ally is asked ** NOT FOR MEN BUT FOR
      MONEY ** . In return the Company recruits, trains and
      maintains ** A FIXED NUMBER OF SOLDIERS UNDER BRITISH
      OFFICERS ** , available for the ruler's protection.

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** STAGE 4 ** -- ** "the money or the protection fee
      was FIXED, USUALLY AT A HIGH LEVEL; WHEN THE STATE FAILED
      TO PAY THE MONEY IN TIME, IT WAS ASKED TO CEDE CERTAIN
      PARTS OF ITS TERRITORIES TO THE COMPANY IN LIEU OF
      PAYMENT." **

   ================================================================
   ** AND THEN: "The Company's entry into the affairs of the
      state had begun; now it would be for ** THE BRITISH
      RESIDENT ** to INITIATE, SUSTAIN AND HASTEN THE PROCESS OF
      ** EVENTUAL ANNEXATION ** ." **
   ================================================================
```

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FOUR STAGES TURN THE SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE FROM A TREATY INTO A MACHINE, AND THAT IS THE INSIGHT TO WRITE DOWN.** ⭐ **Stage 4 is the trap: the fee is set *"USUALLY AT A HIGH LEVEL"*, so default is not a risk — **it is the design**. And default is paid in TERRITORY.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **So the Subsidiary Alliance is not a defensive pact that sometimes went wrong; it is **an instalment plan for annexation**, in which the ruler pays for the army that will eventually absorb him.** *(⭐ **You have already watched all four stages run in this file: **SINDH** went protectorate 1838 → subsidiary force at Shikarpur and Bukkar with Rs 3 lakh a year 1839 → annexed 1843. **Five years, four stages.**)*

**📜 The evolution — three firsts worth knowing:**

| First | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The originator** | *"It was probably **DUPLEIX** who first **GAVE ON HIRE European troops to Indian rulers** to fight their wars"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The first state in the trap** | **AWADH, 1765** — the treaty by which the Company pledged to defend its frontiers **on condition the Nawab defrayed the expense** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The first "no foreign relations" clause** | **1787**, in **CORNWALLIS's treaty with the NAWAB OF CARNATIC** *(February 1787)* |
| ⭐ **Wellesley's contribution** | *"**It was Wellesley's genius to make it A GENERAL RULE to negotiate for THE SURRENDER OF TERRITORY IN FULL SOVEREIGNTY for the maintenance of the subsidiary force.**"* |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Note that the FIRST state to fall into the trap was AWADH in 1765 — the same year and the same treaty settlement as **the Allahabad settlement**.** *Clive declined to annex Awadh because a frontier costs money; the arrangement he made instead **became the template for conquering the rest of India without annexing it either.***

## 🔟 The Doctrine of Lapse — Dalhousie ⭐⭐⭐⭐

> ⚠️ **[Class 8 Ch.2 §8](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/02_From_Trade_to_Territory.md) has the doctrine and the Awadh annexation. Take only the LEGAL CRITIQUE and the ATTRIBUTION correction.**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **The rule** | *"the **ADOPTED SON could be heir to his foster father's PRIVATE PROPERTY, BUT NOT THE STATE**; it was for **THE PARAMOUNT POWER to decide** whether to bestow the state on the adopted son **or to ANNEX it**"* |
| ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The legal objection** | *"The doctrine was **STATED TO BE BASED ON HINDU LAW AND INDIAN CUSTOMS, BUT HINDU LAW SEEMED TO BE SOMEWHAT INCONCLUSIVE ON THIS POINT**, and the instances of an Indian sovereign annexing a vassal's state on account of 'lapse' were **RATHER RARE**"* |
| ⭐ **The precedents that did exist** | **Maharaja RANJIT SINGH annexed a few feudatory principalities on lapse**; **the Company acquired a few petty CIS-SUTLEJ states in 1820** for want of heirs. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BUT: "there was NO CLEAR-CUT INSTANCE OF AN ADOPTED SON BEING DEPRIVED OF AN ENTIRE STATE"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The attribution correction** | *"Though this policy is attributed to **LORD DALHOUSIE (1848–56), HE WAS NOT ITS ORIGINATOR. It was A COINCIDENCE that during his governor-generalship several important cases arose** in which the Doctrine could be applied. **Dalhousie showed TOO MUCH ZEAL in enforcing this policy, which had been THEORETICALLY ENUNCIATED ON SOME PREVIOUS OCCASIONS**"* |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LEGAL CRITIQUE IS THE PART WORTH CARRYING, BECAUSE IT SHOWS THE DOCTRINE DOING SOMETHING SUBTLER THAN THEFT.** ⭐ **The British did not simply seize states. They **claimed to be applying HINDU LAW** — and Spectrum's point is that Hindu law **was inconclusive**, and that the practice they claimed as precedent **had essentially no precedent** for depriving an adopted son of a whole state.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The doctrine was an INVENTION dressed as a CUSTOM — the paramount power writing Indian tradition to suit itself, which is exactly what [Theme 9 shows the Company doing with land records](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md).**
>
> ⚠️ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And be precise about Dalhousie in an answer: he did not invent the doctrine, but he applied it with "too much zeal."** *The distinction matters, because it moves the explanation from one man's villainy to **a systemic policy that happened to find an unusually willing executor.***

> 📦 ⭐ **On AWADH — Spectrum's added detail:** *Awadh was **"the OLDEST of the surviving states brought under the Subsidiary Alliance"**, and *"the cruel impact of the system resulted in its **CONTINUOUS MALADMINISTRATION under profligate and extravagant nawabs for A LONG SPELL OF 80 YEARS**."* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The chronic bankruptcy was "PARTLY DUE TO THE HEAVY CHARGES REALISED BY THE BRITISH GOVERNMENT FOR MAINTENANCE OF THE SUBSIDIARY TROOPS"**, plus **large contributions extracted by Hastings, Amherst and Bentinck "for purposes ENTIRELY UNCONNECTED with the affairs of Awadh."** *In **1819** the Nawab was given the title and status of **king**.*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **That is a complete circular argument exposed: the Subsidiary Alliance BANKRUPTED Awadh, and Awadh was then annexed FOR MISGOVERNMENT.** *The disease was prescribed as the diagnosis.* *(⭐ **Pair it with [Class 8's note that Awadh was annexed on a DIFFERENT argument](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/02_From_Trade_to_Territory.md) from Lapse — and with [Theme 10](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/10_Theme10_Rebels_and_the_Raj_1857.md), where the resentment of dispossessed Awadh *taluqdars* becomes a principal engine of 1857.**)*

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The frontier — five neighbours, and two doctrines

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This entire section is a gap in your notes, and it is where History becomes GS2: these campaigns drew the borders India still has.**

| Neighbour | The story |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **BHUTAN** | *The **occupation of ASSAM in 1826** brought contact. **Frequent Bhutanese raids**, and **bad treatment of Elgin's envoy in 1863–64** — with a treaty forcing the British to **surrender the passes leading to Assam** — led to **British annexation of the passes** and **stopping the Bhutanese allowance**. ⭐ **1865 — the Bhutanese surrendered the passes in return for AN ANNUAL SUBSIDY**, and *"the surrendered district became **a productive area with TEA GARDENS**"** |

| ⭐ **BURMA** | *Three wars, driven by **"the lure of the FOREST RESOURCES, a MARKET for British manufactures, and the need to CHECK FRENCH AMBITIONS in South-East Asia."*** **FIRST (1824–26)** — Burmese expansion westward into **ARAKAN and MANIPUR** and the threat to **Assam and the Brahmaputra valley**. **SECOND** — the British **occupied PEGU, "the only remaining COASTAL province"**, after **intense guerrilla resistance**. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIRD (1885)** — King **THIBAW** was hostile, negotiated **commercial treaties with FRANCE, GERMANY and ITALY**, the **French planned a rail link from MANDALAY**, and he **imposed a humiliating fine on a British timber company**; **DUFFERIN ordered the invasion and annexation of upper Burma** |

| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TIBET** | *"ruled by **a THEOCRACY of Buddhist monks (lamas) under NOMINAL SUZERAINTY OF CHINA."* ⭐ **Chinese suzerainty was INEFFECTIVE and RUSSIAN influence at LHASA was increasing**, with reports of **Russian arms**. **CURZON sent a small GORKHA contingent under COLONEL YOUNGHUSBAND.** ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The Tibetans REFUSED TO NEGOTIATE and OFFERED NON-VIOLENT RESISTANCE."** Younghusband **pushed into LHASA in August 1904** while **the Dalai Lama fled** |

**⚔️ THE FORWARD POLICY AND THE FIRST ANGLO-AFGHAN WAR:**

> ⭐ **AUCKLAND (1836)** advocated **a FORWARD POLICY** — protecting the boundary **"either through TREATIES with the neighbouring countries or by ANNEXING THEM COMPLETELY."** ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Amir DOST MOHAMMED "WANTED BRITISH FRIENDSHIP but made it CONDITIONAL ON THE BRITISH HELPING HIM RECOVER PESHAWAR FROM THE SIKHS — A CONDITION WHICH THE BRITISH REJECTED. Dost Mohammed now turned to RUSSIA AND PERSIA."**

**📜 The TRIPARTITE TREATY, 1838** *(British + Sikhs + Shah Shuja)* — Shah Shuja had been **deposed in 1809** and lived since as **a British pensioner at LUDHIANA**:

> ⭐ **(i)** Shah Shuja **enthroned with the armed help of the Sikhs**, ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *"**the Company remaining in the background, 'JINGLING THE MONEY-BAG'**"* · **(ii)** he would **conduct foreign affairs with the advice of the Sikhs and the British** · **(iii)** **give up his sovereign rights over the Amirs of Sindh** for money · **(iv)** **recognise Ranjit Singh's claims** over Afghan territories on the right bank of the Indus.

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THEN THE ORIGINAL REASON EVAPORATED:** *"there came about **A DRASTIC CHANGE in the political situation because of the REMOVAL OF THE ORIGINAL IRRITANTS — PERSIA LIFTED ITS SIEGE OF HERAT and RUSSIA RECALLED ITS ENVOY FROM KABUL. NEVERTHELESS, THE BRITISH DECIDED TO GO AHEAD WITH THEIR FORWARD POLICY.**"*

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE AFGHAN EPISODE IS THE MOST DAMNING SEQUENCE IN THE CHAPTER, AND IT IS BETTER THAN SINDH AS A "CRITICALLY EXAMINE" CASE, BECAUSE THE FAILURE IS SO COMPLETE.** ⭐ **Dost Mohammed WANTED British friendship. He asked one price — help recovering Peshawar. Britain refused, drove him to Russia, and then invaded to replace him with a pensioner nobody in Afghanistan accepted.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the stated reason for the war disappeared — *Persia lifted the siege of Herat and Russia recalled its envoy* — and the British went ahead anyway. Three years, one and a half crore rupees and 20,000 lives later, **they restored Dost Mohammed to the throne they had removed him from.*** *(⭐ **And the consequences ran outward: the Afghan reverses are why **SINDH was annexed** — Elphinstone's "bully who went home to beat his wife". ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ One rejected request for Peshawar produced a war, a humiliation, a compensatory annexation, and eventually the two competing doctrines of MASTERLY INACTIVITY and PROUD RESERVE that governed the north-west for the rest of the Raj.**)*

> 💡 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE GS2 PAYOFF — say this in any question on India's boundaries:** ⭐ **Almost every land frontier India argues about today was drawn in this chapter.** *The **SAGAULI line (1816)** fixed the Nepal border and gave Britain Kumaon, Garhwal and the Himalayan hill stations; **Nepal's withdrawal from Sikkim** and the **Chumbi Valley clause of 1904** shaped the Sikkim–Tibet corridor; **Bhutan's passes** went in 1865; **Burma was annexed in 1885 and detached in 1935**; and the **Afghan buffer** was the whole object of the north-west policy.* ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **These were not border agreements between neighbours. They were the settlements a single expanding power imposed on all of them — which is precisely why they are still contested.**

| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Was the British conquest of India accidental or intentional?"** | ⭐ **Give SEELEY** *("blindly… unintentionally… accidentally")* **and the INTENTIONAL school, then JUDITH BROWN's caution that motives varied by place, time and BRITISH FACTION.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then resolve it as a CHRONOLOGY, using B.L. Grover: opportunistic to protect trade at first — "FROM 1798 TO 1818 THE BRITISH MOTIVES WERE CONSCIOUSLY IMPERIALISTIC" — Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance, Hastings treating India "as A CONQUERED rather than an ACQUIRED country", and thereafter "A SET DESIGN."** ⭐ **Close on the evidence from this chapter: Sindh and Afghanistan were not accidents; they were pretexts manufactured in advance** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Why did the British succeed?"** | ⭐ **Run the five DEFENSIBLE causes** *(arms; regular pay and discipline; merit-based selection; leadership WITH a second line; finance and sea power)*, ⚠️ **recasting Spectrum's sixth as THE ABSENCE OF A PAN-INDIAN IDENTITY rather than "lack of materialistic vision."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then prove the decisive one from this chapter alone: the Marathas and the Nizam fought FOR the English in the Third and Fourth Mysore Wars; Ranjit Singh let British troops cross twice; LAL SINGH and TEJA SINGH betrayed their own army at Mudki and Sobraon.** ⭐ **Thesis: "the consciousness of 'India' was lacking" — there was never a coalition to defeat** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Buxar was more decisive than Plassey."** | ⭐ **Plassey (1757): a provincial nawab, defeated by CONSPIRACY — Mir Jafar's defection.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Buxar (1764): a CLOSELY CONTESTED battle against THREE allied powers, including THE MUGHAL EMPEROR HIMSELF — "the victory made the English A GREAT POWER IN NORTHERN INDIA."** ⭐ **Then the settlement: the TREATY OF ALLAHABAD gave the DIWANI by IMPERIAL FARMAN — legalising the conquest — while Awadh was left as a BUFFER.** ⭐ **Quote MALLESON** *("amongst the most decisive battles ever fought")* **and note his own "ninety-four years" lands on 1858** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Dual Government was power without responsibility."** | ⭐ **Define it precisely: *DIWANI* from the EMPEROR, *NIZAMAT* through the right to nominate the deputy subahdar — "it left THE APPEARANCE OF AUTHORITY to the puppet ruler, while keeping THE SOVEREIGN POWER" with the Company.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the structural fault: the Nawab was "RESPONSIBLE FOR MAINTAINING PEACE AND ORDER, but DEPENDED BOTH FOR FUNDS AND FORCES upon the Company."** ⭐ **Then the outcome — "AN ADMINISTRATIVE BREAKDOWN… DISASTROUS FOR THE PEOPLE OF BENGAL", ended by WARREN HASTINGS in 1772.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Close by joining it to [Class 8's economic argument](../../../01_NCERT/Class_08_Our_Pasts_III_Part1/Notes/02_From_Trade_to_Territory.md): the Company took the SURPLUS and disclaimed the GOVERNMENT** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Assess Tipu Sultan."** | ⭐ **ACHIEVEMENTS: European-model army with Persian commands; the BOARD OF ADMIRALTY (1796) and three dockyards; "PIONEER OF ROCKET TECHNOLOGY" with his own military manual; SERICULTURE; four languages.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BIGOTRY CHARGE, answered evenly: he crushed the Hindu COORGS and NAIRS **and** punished the Muslim MOPLAHS; he is reported to have demolished temples in Kerala **and** funded the repair of the SRINGERI temple damaged by a MARATHA raid.** ⭐ **CITIZEN TIPU and the Tree of Liberty for the other side of the portrait.** ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Close with MOHIBBUL HASAN: calling him "the first Indian nationalist" is "A WRONG VIEW ARRIVED AT BY PROJECTING THE PRESENT INTO THE PAST" — and with Spectrum's own rule against judging the past "with modern yardsticks"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The Subsidiary Alliance was an instalment plan for annexation."** | ⭐ **Trace the ancestry: DUPLEIX hiring out troops → WARREN HASTINGS's RING-FENCE → AWADH 1765 → the CARNATIC 1787 "no foreign relations" clause → WELLESLEY generalising it.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then THE FOUR STAGES, with the trap in stage 4: the fee "FIXED USUALLY AT A HIGH LEVEL", default settled in TERRITORY, and the RESIDENT to "INITIATE, SUSTAIN AND HASTEN THE PROCESS OF EVENTUAL ANNEXATION."** ⭐ **Prove it with SINDH: protectorate 1838 → subsidiary force 1839 → annexed 1843.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the cost to the states, in Spectrum's own words: they "LOST THEIR INDEPENDENCE BY BUYING SECURITY", and oppressive rulers became impossible to depose because the British protected them — which is the AWADH circle: bankrupted by the alliance, annexed for misgovernment** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The conquest of Sindh was morally indefensible."** *(an essay-grade case)* | ⭐ **Narrate the escalation: the 1809 "eternal friendship", the 1832 treaty explicitly BARRING warships and war material on the Indus, the 1838 protectorate, the 1839 subsidiary force — each step extracted "UNDER THREAT OF SUPERIOR FORCE."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the motive, in Spectrum's words: the Afghan reverses meant a "LOSS OF PRESTIGE. TO COMPENSATE FOR THIS, THEY ANNEXED SINDH" — Elphinstone's "bully who… went home to beat his wife."** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then let the conquerors convict themselves: NAPIER's "we have NO RIGHT to seize Sindh, yet we shall do so"; OUTRAM's "I am SICK OF YOUR POLICY"; ROBERTS's "MORALLY INDEFENSIBLE."** ⭐ **Close on the structure: the same voice supplies both the crime and the civilising alibi** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Why did the Sikh kingdom fall so fast after 1839?"** | ⭐ **Ranjit Singh's own limitation first: he was "CONSCIOUS of his weak position" yet "TOOK NO STEP TO ORGANISE A COALITION… OR MAINTAIN A BALANCE OF POWER", and the TREATY OF AMRITSAR (1809) had already turned him WEST.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the succession collapse — five violent deaths in six years.** ⭐ **Then the army: "FAR LESS STRONG THAN IT APPEARED" — the great generals dead, pay irregular, unworthy officers appointed.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Then the decisive factor: TREACHERY, with LAL SINGH and TEJA SINGH producing five straight defeats despite a two-to-one advantage.** ⭐ **Close on the price: the Jalandhar Doab annexed, and KASHMIR SOLD to Gulab Singh for Rs 75 lakh** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"British frontier policy drew the borders independent India inherited."** *(GS1 + GS2)* | ⭐ **NEPAL: SAGAULI (1816) — Garhwal, Kumaon, the Terai, withdrawal from SIKKIM, the Himalayan hill stations, and the Gorkhas entering the Indian Army.** ⭐ **BHUTAN: the passes, 1865, for an annual subsidy.** ⭐ **BURMA: three wars to 1885, then SEPARATED IN 1935 "to weaken the link" with the INC.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TIBET: Younghusband, the flight of the Dalai Lama, and the CHUMBI VALLEY held for 75 years — the origin of the Sikkim–Tibet question.** ⭐ **AFGHANISTAN: the forward policy, the Tripartite Treaty, a war whose causes had evaporated, and the swing between MASTERLY INACTIVITY and PROUD RESERVE.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Thesis: these were not negotiated borders between neighbours but settlements imposed by one expanding power — which is why several remain contested** |

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***DASTAK*** | **A trade permit exempting the specified goods from duties.** ⚠️ *Abused by Company servants **for PRIVATE trade** and **SOLD to Indian merchants for a commission** — the cause of the Buxar war* |
| ⭐ ***FARMAN*** | **An imperial order** — the instrument by which **Shah Alam II granted the *diwani*** and thereby **legalised the conquest** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***DIWANI*** | **The right to COLLECT REVENUE** — acquired **from the EMPEROR** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***NIZAMAT*** | **POLICE and JUDICIAL functions** *(and military defence)* — acquired **from the SUBAHDAR**, through the right to nominate the **deputy subahdar** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Dual Government** | ***"the rule of the TWO"*** *(1765–72)* — **the appearance of authority to the Nawab, the sovereign power to the Company.** Ended by **Warren Hastings** |
| **Naib-subahdar** | ⭐ **The deputy governor** — *"could be APPOINTED OR DISMISSED BY THE ENGLISH"* |
| ⭐ **Ring-fence** | **WARREN HASTINGS's policy** — ***"the defence of their NEIGHBOURS' frontiers for safeguarding their OWN"*, at the neighbours' expense** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Subsidiary Alliance** | **WELLESLEY's system** — a British force stationed and paid for, a Resident accepted, no Europeans employed, no war or negotiation without the Governor-General. ⭐ **Four stages, ending in territory ceded for unpaid fees** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Doctrine of Lapse** | **The adopted son may inherit PRIVATE PROPERTY but NOT THE STATE.** ⚠️ *Attributed to Dalhousie, who **"was NOT its originator"** but applied it with **"too much zeal"*** |
| ⭐ **Forward policy** | **AUCKLAND's** — protecting the boundary *"either through TREATIES… or by ANNEXING THEM COMPLETELY"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Masterly inactivity** | **JOHN LAWRENCE (1864–69)** — non-intervention beyond the frontier, *"a REACTION TO THE DISASTERS OF THE FIRST AFGHAN WAR"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Proud reserve** | **LYTTON's** return to forward engagement |
| ⭐ **"Scientific frontier"** | The search for a defensible north-western line — *"the PASSES of the north-west seemed to hold the key"* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***MISL*** | ⭐ **An ARABIC word — "EQUAL or ALIKE"** *(also "state")* — the **Sikh military brotherhoods "with A DEMOCRATIC SET-UP"**, twelve of them at Ranjit Singh's birth |
| ⭐ ***DAL KHALSA* · *BUDHA DAL* · *TARUNA DAL*** | **The united Sikh body (1784, Kapur Singh Faizullapuria) · the army of the VETERANS · the army of the YOUNG** |
| ⭐ ***GURUMATTA SANGH*** | **The central administration of a *misl*** — political, social and economic |
| ⭐ ***CHAR YAR*** | **"The four friends"** — Mir Fath Ali Khan's brothers, who divided Sindh in 1800 as **the AMIRS** |
| ⭐ ***SARVADHIKARI* / *DULWAI*** | The offices held by **NANJARAJ and DEVARAJ**, who reduced the Wodeyar to a puppet |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Chumbi Valley** | **The territory between BHUTAN and SIKKIM**, occupied for **75 years** as security under the **Treaty of Lhasa (1904)** |

## 🔁 Recap — the chapter in twelve lines

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Britain's "SECOND EMPIRE" turned east around 1783 — after the Atlantic empire was lost — and its intellectuals began "CERTIFYING THEMSELVES AS CIVILISED" to justify it.**
2. ⭐ **On "accidental or intentional", the answer is a chronology: opportunistic before Wellesley, and from B.L. Grover's date — "FROM 1798 TO 1818 THE BRITISH MOTIVES WERE CONSCIOUSLY IMPERIALISTIC" — deliberate after him.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The British won on ARMS, REGULAR PAY, MERIT-BASED SELECTION, SECOND-LINE LEADERSHIP and FINANCE — but the decisive factor was that "THE CONSCIOUSNESS OF 'INDIA' WAS LACKING."** *There was never an Indian coalition to defeat.*
4. ⭐ **Bengal supplied 60% of Britain's Asian imports, and the Company paid £350 a year for privileges worth £50,000.** *Plassey and Buxar began as TAX DISPUTES.*
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **MIR KASIM was the ablest nawab after Alivardi — he moved his capital to MUNGER, rebuilt the army, and then ABOLISHED TRANSIT DUTIES FOR EVERYONE.** ⚠️ **The British protested and demanded "PREFERENTIAL TREATMENT" — an admission that the privilege, not the trade, was the point.**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BUXAR, 22 October 1764, under HECTOR MUNRO, defeated THREE powers including the MUGHAL EMPEROR — which is why it mattered more than Plassey.** ⭐ **The TREATY OF ALLAHABAD then took the *DIWANI* by *FARMAN*, left AWADH as a BUFFER, and made the emperor "A USEFUL RUBBER STAMP."**
7. ⭐ **DUAL GOVERNMENT (1765–72) gave the Company the revenue and the army, and the Nawab the duty to keep order without either.** ⚠️ **Power without responsibility — and Bengal paid for it.**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HAIDAR ALI, an uneducated horseman, diagnosed three enemies needing three answers and built an ARMS FACTORY AT DINDIGUL.** ⭐ **Across FOUR MYSORE WARS the military balance barely moved — what moved was the Marathas and the Nizam, from Haidar's side to the English side.** *Mysore fell in 1799 and was partitioned.*
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TIPU was neither a bigot nor a proto-nationalist: he funded the SRINGERI temple repaired after a MARATHA raid, punished Muslim Moplahs as well as Hindu Coorgs, and called himself CITIZEN TIPU with a Tree of Liberty.** ⚠️ **MOHIBBUL HASAN's caution — "projecting the present into the past" — is the line to quote.**
10. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SINDH was annexed in 1843 to compensate for humiliation in AFGHANISTAN — "a bully who has been knocked in the street and went home to beat his wife."** ⭐ **NAPIER wrote "WE HAVE NO RIGHT TO SEIZE SINDH, YET WE SHALL DO SO", and OUTRAM, his own deputy, said "I AM SICK OF YOUR POLICY."**
11. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The TREATY OF AMRITSAR (1809) turned RANJIT SINGH west by making the Sutlej his wall; after his death in 1839 five violent deaths in six years wrecked the state, and TREACHERY by LAL SINGH and TEJA SINGH lost five straight battles despite a two-to-one advantage.** ⚠️ **Unable to pay the indemnity, the Sikhs saw KASHMIR SOLD TO GULAB SINGH FOR RS 75 LAKH.**
12. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And more of India was taken by POLICY than by war: HASTINGS's RING-FENCE, WELLESLEY's SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE in FOUR STAGES — the fee set high so that default would be paid in TERRITORY — and DALHOUSIE's LAPSE, "stated to be based on Hindu law" which "seemed to be somewhat INCONCLUSIVE."** ⭐ **Beyond the borders, SAGAULI, the Bhutan passes, three Burmese wars, the CHUMBI VALLEY and an Afghan war whose stated causes had already evaporated drew the frontiers independent India inherited.**
