> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FACT WORTH CARRYING INTO AN ANSWER IS THE SHAPE OF THAT CABINET, NOT ITS NAMES.** *The first government of independent India contained **a leader of the Scheduled Castes Federation** and **a leader of the Hindu Mahasabha** — two men who had spent the freedom struggle **outside and often against** the Congress. ⭐⭐ **Both resigned within four years.** The cabinet was deliberately built wider than the party that won, and the width did not hold.* ⚠️ **Note also that PATEL held BOTH Home AND States** — the integration of the princely states and the internal security of the new republic were the same portfolio, in the same hands.

⭐ **Also worth fixing:** **Mountbatten, and then C. RAJAGOPALACHARI**, served as **Governor-General until 26 January 1950**, when India became a republic and **RAJENDRA PRASAD** became the first President. **Patel served as Deputy PM until his death in December 1950.**

   ================================================================

   ** NUMBER **   ⭐ ** 562 ** ( Spectrum ) --  ⚠️ Laxmikanth and

                  register; ** both figures are in circulation **
   ** AREA **     ** 7,12,508 SQUARE MILES **
   ** LARGEST **  ⭐ ** HYDERABAD -- "AS LARGE AS ITALY" ** , with
                  a population of ** 14 MILLION **
   ** SMALLEST ** ⭐⭐ ** BILBARI -- A POPULATION OF 27 PERSONS **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AND THE GEOGRAPHY IS THE ARGUMENT: ** the Company
      took, in the process of conquest, ** THE COASTAL TRACTS, THE
      NAVIGABLE RIVER VALLEYS, AND THE RICH, DENSELY POPULATED
      AGRICULTURAL LAND. ** What was left to the princes were

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT LAST SENTENCE EXPLAINS THE WHOLE MAP, AND MOST CANDIDATES NEVER LEARN IT.** *The princely states are not scattered at random. **They are the residue** — what a conquering commercial power did not want, because it was hard to reach and did not pay. ⭐⭐ **The Company took the profitable third; the princes kept the rest and were allowed to call it sovereignty.*** *(Set this beside [file 01's](01_Expansion_and_Consolidation.md) ring-fence logic — **take the revenue, leave the responsibility** — and the two halves of the same policy fit together exactly.)*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE SIX STAGES — the spine of this chapter

⚠️ **This periodisation is Spectrum's most useful single contribution to the subject, and it is a straight memory item. It also silently re-frames everything [file 01](01_Expansion_and_Consolidation.md) taught you about conquest.**

| # | Stage | Years | What defined it |
|:--:|---|:--:|---|
| **I** | ⭐ **STRUGGLE FOR EQUALITY from a position of SUBORDINATION** | **1740–1765** | The Company begins **below** the Indian powers. **Anglo-French rivalry from Dupleix (1751)** → **capture of ARCOT (1751)** → **PLASSEY (1757)** makes it second only to the Bengal nawabs → ⭐ **the DIWANI (1765)** makes it a significant political power |
| **II** | ⭐⭐ **RING FENCE** | **1765–1813** | **Buffer zones to defend the Company's frontiers** — Hastings' wars against the **Marathas and Mysore**; the threat is the **Marathas and Afghan invaders**; ⭐ **the Company organises AWADH's defence to protect BENGAL.** **Wellesley's SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE is an EXTENSION of ring fence** |
| **III** | ⭐⭐⭐ **SUBORDINATE ISOLATION** | **1813–1857** | **The theory of PARAMOUNTCY develops.** States **surrender all EXTERNAL sovereignty but keep INTERNAL administration**. ⭐⭐ **Residents change from DIPLOMATIC AGENTS OF A FOREIGN POWER into EXECUTIVE AND CONTROLLING OFFICERS OF A SUPERIOR GOVERNMENT.** **1833** Charter Act ends commercial functions; **prior sanction required for all successions**; **1834** the Directors issue guidelines **to annex wherever possible** → **Dalhousie usurps EIGHT states**, including **Satara and Nagpur** |
| **IV** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SUBORDINATE UNION** | **1857–1935** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Annexation ABANDONED — because of the states' loyalty in 1857 and their value as "BREAKWATERS IN POLITICAL STORMS OF THE FUTURE".** The new rule: ⭐ **"PUNISH OR DEPOSE BUT NOT ANNEX."** The Mughal fiction ends; **succession now sanctioned by the CROWN**; ⭐⭐ **the ruler inherits the gaddi NOT AS A MATTER OF RIGHT BUT AS A GIFT from the paramount power** |
| **V** | **EQUAL FEDERATION — a NON-STARTER** | **1935–1947** | The **GoI Act 1935** offered the princes **125 of 375** seats in the Federal Assembly and **104 of 160** in the Council of States — ⚠️ **conditional on ratification by states holding over half the population.** ⭐ **It never came into existence and was dropped after September 1939** |
| **VI** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **INTEGRATION AND MERGER** | **1947–1950** | See **§7–§10** |

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **STAGE IV IS THE ONE TO UNDERSTAND, BECAUSE IT CONTAINS THE WHOLE LOGIC OF THE RAJ AFTER 1857.** *A wall built to hold back a flood is worth more standing than demolished for its bricks. ⭐⭐ **The princes had proved in 1857 that they would hold — so the policy stopped being ANNEXATION and became PRESERVATION.*** ⚠️ **But preservation on terms: the same stage strips the succession of its independent basis, so the prince keeps his throne and loses the right to it.** ⭐⭐⭐ **By 1858 an Indian ruler holds his gaddi as a GIFT. That single change is what made 1947 possible — a title held by grant can be re-granted, and a paramountcy that never rested on treaty could simply lapse.**

⭐ **Two supporting facts for Stage IV worth keeping:** *(a)* **modern communications did the encroaching** — *railways, roads, telegraph, canals, post offices, the press and public opinion*; *(b)* the **Butler Commission's formula of 1927**: *"for the purpose of international relations, **STATE TERRITORY IS IN THE SAME POSITION AS BRITISH TERRITORY** and state subjects in the same position as British subjects."*

## 5️⃣ Curzon, the Chamber of Princes, and the Butler Committee ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**🎩 CURZON'S APPROACH** — he *"stretched the interpretation of old treaties"* to make the princes, **in their capacity as SERVANTS OF THE PEOPLE**, work *"side-by-side with the governor-general"*. ⭐ **His policy is summed up in two words: PATRONAGE and "INTRUSIVE SURVEILLANCE".** ⭐⭐ **His own formulation of the relationship is worth quoting: neither FEUDAL nor FEDERAL, but *"a type not based on a treaty"* — a series of relationships grown up under different historical conditions and gradually conforming to a single line.** ⚠️ **The trend was to reduce all states to ONE TYPE, uniformly dependent, and to treat them as an integral part of the Indian political system.**

**🏛️ THE CHAMBER OF PRINCES (*NARENDRA MANDAL*)** — set up on the recommendations of the **Montford Reforms**, in **1921**:

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nature** | ⚠️ **A CONSULTATIVE AND ADVISORY BODY ONLY** |
| **Powers** | ⭐ **NO say in the internal affairs of individual states, and NO power to discuss matters concerning existing rights and freedoms** |
| ⭐ **Three categories** | **(1) Directly represented — 109** · **(2) Represented through representatives — 127** · **(3) Recognised as feudal holdings or *jagirs*** |

**⚖️ THE BUTLER COMMITTEE (1927)** — appointed to examine the nature of the relationship. Two findings:

1. ⭐ **Paramountcy must REMAIN SUPREME**, fulfilling its obligations and *"adapting and defining itself according to the shifting necessities of the time"*
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **States must NOT be handed over to an Indian Government in British India, responsible to an Indian legislature, WITHOUT THE CONSENT OF THE STATES**

> 🔴 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FINDING 2 IS THE TIME BOMB, AND IT WENT OFF IN 1947.** *If paramountcy cannot be transferred to an Indian government without the states' consent, then when the British leave, **paramountcy does not pass to anyone — it simply LAPSES**, and 562 rulers become, in their own reading, sovereign. ⭐⭐ **That is precisely the position Cripps confirmed in 1942 and the princes tried to occupy in 1947.*** ⚠️ **And Spectrum's verdict on the Committee's evasion is the best line in the chapter: *"paramountcy" was left undefined, and this ⭐ "HYDRA-HEADED CREATURE" was left to feed on USAGE, THE CROWN'S PREROGATIVE AND THE PRINCES' IMPLIED CONSENT.***

## 6️⃣ 1947 — how the states were actually brought in ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE SEQUENCE THAT DECIDED IT **
   ================================================================

   ** THE BRITISH ATTEMPTS ** -- Cripps Mission ( 1942 ) , Wavell
   Plan ( 1945 ) , Cabinet Mission ( 1946 ) , Attlee's statement
   ( February 1947 ) .

   ⚠️ ** CRIPPS ** held that the British government ** DID NOT
      CONTEMPLATE TRANSFERRING PARAMOUNTCY TO ANY OTHER PARTY
      IN INDIA. **
   -> the states tried schemes to ** FORGE A UNION OF THEIR OWN ** ,
      as ** sovereigns ** or as ** A THIRD FORCE ** in Indian
      politics.
   -> ⭐ the ** JUNE 3rd PLAN ** and ** ATTLEE'S STATEMENT ** made
      clear the states were ** FREE TO JOIN EITHER DOMINION ** --
      and ** MOUNTBATTEN REFUSED THEM SOVEREIGN STATUS. **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE PATEL-MENON ARGUMENT: ** Patel, in charge of
      the ** STATES MINISTRY ** and helped by ** V.P. MENON ** as
      secretary, appealed to the ** PATRIOTIC FEELING of the
      rulers ** to join in ** DEFENCE, COMMUNICATION and EXTERNAL
      AFFAIRS ** only --

      ⚠️⭐⭐ ** THE THREE AREAS WHICH HAD ANYWAY BEEN PART OF THE
         CROWN'S PARAMOUNTCY, AND OVER WHICH THE STATES HAD
         NEVER HAD ANY CONTROL. **

   ** RESULT: by 15 AUGUST 1947, 136 STATES had joined ** --
   " but others remained precariously outside. "
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GENIUS OF THE OFFER IS THAT IT ASKED FOR NOTHING.** *A tenant who has never been allowed to touch the roof, the drains or the front gate is being asked to sign a paper confirming that the landlord handles the roof, the drains and the front gate. **There is no concession in it.*** ⭐⭐⭐ **Patel and Menon asked the princes to surrender only the three subjects they had never possessed — and framed even that as patriotism rather than as terms.** ⚠️ **Everything else — the merger of the states into provinces, the loss of internal sovereignty, the eventual abolition of the privy purses — came LATER, and separately, once accession was a fact.** ⭐⭐ **Accession first, integration afterwards: that is the two-step, and it is the answer to "how were 562 states absorbed in three years?"**

| **The ruler** | A **MUSLIM NAWAB** who wanted **Pakistan** | The **NIZAM**, who wanted ⭐ **SOVEREIGN STATUS** | A **HINDU PRINCE**, who wanted ⭐ **SOVEREIGN STATUS** |
| **The people** | ⭐ **A HINDU MAJORITY** wanting India | — | ⭐ **A MUSLIM MAJORITY** |
| **The course** | The nawab's **repressive attitude** | **STANDSTILL AGREEMENT, November 1947**; Indian troops withdrew; ⭐ **the Nizam's police and stormtroopers, the RAZAKARS, took over.** ⚠️ **The Nizam wanted AN OUTLET TO THE SEA (GOA)** | ⭐⭐ **He procrastinated — and PAKISTAN SENT ITS FORCES BEHIND A FRONT OF TRIBAL MILITIA, moving menacingly towards SRINAGAR** |
| ⭐ **The method** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A PLEBISCITE**, which decided for India | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **ARMY ACTION, 1948** — *"a **POLICE ACTION** to restore law and order"*, prompted by **the violence and the supply of FOREIGN ARMS** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AN INSTRUMENT OF ACCESSION, October 1947** — **forced by the invasion**, and ⭐ **endorsed by the popular leader SHEIKH ABDULLAH** |
| **The outcome** | Acceded to India | ⭐ **Acceded in NOVEMBER 1949** | Indian troops despatched; India complained to the **UN SECURITY COUNCIL** and offered **a plebiscite**; ⚠️ **a ceasefire left 84,000 SQ KM under Pakistani occupation**; ⭐ **ARTICLE 370** recognised the state's special status |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE THREE CASES ARE THREE DIFFERENT INSTRUMENTS, AND THAT IS THE POINT OF LEARNING THEM TOGETHER.** *⭐ **JUNAGADH was settled by a VOTE. HYDERABAD by an ARMY. KASHMIR by a SIGNATURE under duress, and then by a WAR that has not ended.*** ⭐⭐⭐ **The new state used whichever instrument the case allowed — and in each case it used the principle that suited it: popular will at Junagadh where the people were Hindu, the ruler's signature in Kashmir where the ruler was Hindu.** ⚠️ **That asymmetry is exactly the charge Pakistan has made ever since, and a strong Mains answer states it plainly rather than avoiding it — while noting that Pakistan's own position was the mirror image, and that it was Pakistan's invasion, not India's argument, that forced the Kashmir accession.**

⚠️⭐⭐ **THE WORD "POLICE ACTION" IS ITSELF EXAMINABLE.** *Spectrum puts it in quotation marks, and so should you. **Calling an army operation a police action is a claim about SOVEREIGNTY** — police act inside a state, armies act between states. ⭐ **To call Hyderabad a police action was to assert that Hyderabad was already internal territory, which was the very question at issue.***

⭐ **And note the Nizam's demand for an outlet to the sea through GOA** — landlocked sovereignty was not viable, which is the practical reason his position was hopeless quite apart from the politics.

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Gradual integration — the part everyone skips and UPSC asks

**⚙️ THE PROBLEM WAS TWO-FOLD, and Spectrum states it precisely:**

1. **Transforming the states into VIABLE ADMINISTRATIVE UNITS**
2. **Absorbing them into the CONSTITUTIONAL structure**

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE THREE-PART SOLUTION **
   ================================================================

   ⭐ ** (1) ABSORPTION into contiguous provinces -- 216 SMALL
       STATES ** , listed in ** PART A. **
       e.g. ** 39 states of Orissa and Chhattisgarh -> CENTRAL
       PROVINCES and ORISSA ** ; ** the GUJARAT states ->
       BOMBAY. **

   ⭐ ** (2) CENTRALLY ADMINISTERED for strategic or special
       reasons -- 61 STATES ** , listed in ** PART C. **
       e.g. ** HIMACHAL PRADESH · VINDHYA PRADESH · MANIPUR ·
       TRIPURA · BHOPAL. **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** (3) FIVE UNIONS created from the rest: **
        - ** UNITED STATES OF KATHIAWAR **
        - ** UNITED STATES OF MATSYA **
        - ** PATIALA AND EAST PUNJAB STATES UNION ( PEPSU ) **
        - ** RAJASTHAN **
        - ** UNITED STATES OF TRAVANCORE-COCHIN ** ( later
          ** KERALA ** )
```

**📜 AND THEN THE DEEPENING, in two steps:**

- ⭐ **Initially the states acceded on only THREE SUBJECTS — defence, communication, external affairs.** *"Later they felt that a CLOSER ASSOCIATION was necessary."*
- ⭐⭐ **The five unions and MYSORE then accepted Union jurisdiction over Union and Concurrent subjects — EXCEPT TAXATION — subject to Article 238 and to the SUPERVISORY POWER OF THE UNION FOR TEN YEARS**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SEVENTH AMENDMENT (1956) ABOLISHED PART B STATES AS A CLASS** and made one class out of Parts A and B; the special provisions for Part B states were deleted. ⭐ ***"The Indian states thus became part of one uniform political set-up."***

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ THE THREE-PART DEVICE AS A SORTING PROBLEM AND IT BECOMES MEMORABLE.** *562 objects of wildly different size had to be fitted into a filing system built for provinces. ⭐ **The small ones were folded into whatever was next to them (Part A). The strategically awkward ones were kept in head office (Part C). The middling ones were glued together into new units big enough to stand up (the five unions).*** ⭐⭐⭐ **Then, once everything was inside the cabinet, the dividers were removed — that is the Seventh Amendment.**

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS ALSO THE POINT AT WHICH THIS FILE HANDS OVER.** *The Seventh Amendment of 1956 belongs to the **STATES REORGANISATION ACT** and the **FAZL ALI COMMISSION**, i.e. the **LINGUISTIC** reorganisation — and that is owned by **[Laxmikanth Ch.5 *(Union and its Territory)*](../../../../Polity/02_Laxmikanth/05_Union_and_its_Territory.md)**, which also carries Articles 1–4 and the 2019 J&K reorganisation. ⭐⭐ **Read the two files back to back: this one takes you from 562 states to a uniform set-up; that one takes you from a uniform set-up to the map you know.**

# 🏚️ PART E — REFUGEES, THE MURDER, AND THE INSURGENCY

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Rehabilitation ⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **Spectrum's opening definition is worth keeping because it is doing analytical work:** *the displaced were **"refugees" in the sense that THEY HAD NOT LEFT THEIR HOMES VOLUNTARILY** — and ⭐ **"the two new governments DID NOT ORGANISE AN ORDERLY EXCHANGE OF POPULATION."***

**🏗️ THE MACHINERY:** an **emergency committee of the cabinet** for the Delhi crisis, and a ⭐ **MINISTRY OF RELIEF AND REHABILITATION** *(under **K.C. Neogy** — see §1)*.

**⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE "EVACUEE PROPERTY" FICTION:** *the notion that property left behind would be **protected by government for the future return of those who had left** became ⭐ **"an empty rhetoric", because it was almost impossible to force out the refugees who had settled in the empty houses.*** ⭐⭐⭐ **And Spectrum draws the consequence explicitly: this is what MADE THE RETURN OF THE REFUGEES IMPOSSIBLE.** *A promise that nobody enforced turned a temporary displacement into a permanent one.*

| | ⭐ **EAST PUNJAB** | ⭐⭐ **BENGAL** |
|---|---|---|
| **Pattern** | A **concentrated, early** movement | ⚠️ **Much more PROLONGED and COMPLICATED** |
| **Response** | **Camps run until 1949**; for **urban** refugees, **industrial and vocational training** and **grants to start small businesses**; for **rural** refugees, **land, agricultural loans and housing subsidies** | By 1948 only **a small group of high-caste, landed or middle-class Hindus** had moved, arranging **exchanges of property or jobs individually** |
| **The escalation** | — | ⭐ **Dec 1949 – Jan 1950: fresh violence at KHULNA** drove out peasants → **anti-Muslim riots in Feb 1950 forced about ONE MILLION MUSLIMS out of West Bengal** → further anti-Hindu violence → ⭐ **by 1951 about 15 LAKH Hindu refugees had arrived in West Bengal** |
| ⚠️ **The reception** | ⭐⭐⭐ **DIFFERENTIATED ENTITLEMENTS: refugees with SOCIAL AND CULTURAL CAPITAL — class and caste status and political connections — "often got the better deal, while THE DEPRESSED CLASSES WERE GIVEN LITTLE OR NO CONSIDERATION"** | ⚠️⭐⭐ **The Indian government DID NOT RECOGNISE these migrants as refugees, and NEHRU TRIED TO SEND THEM BACK** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE EAST PUNJAB / BENGAL CONTRAST IS THE ANSWER TO A QUESTION UPSC LIKES: why is Partition remembered as a Punjab event?** ⭐ **Because Punjab's was fast, concentrated, and met with a real (if unequal) rehabilitation programme, while Bengal's was slow, ran for years, and was met with official reluctance to admit that it was happening at all.** ⚠️ **Pair this with [Cl.12 Suppl. A's](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/13_Supplement_A_Understanding_Partition.md) line that Partition was "most bloody in the Punjab" but Bengal's was "less concentrated but AS AGONISING" — Spectrum supplies the administrative reason NCERT only gestures at.**

⭐⭐ **AND THE CASTE POINT DESERVES ITS OWN SENTENCE.** *Relief was not distributed by need. **The refugee with connections got land and a loan; the Dalit refugee got little or nothing.*** ⭐ **A catastrophe was administered through the existing social hierarchy — which is the same finding [Cl.12 Th.9](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) reaches about famine relief and revenue remission.**

**📍 WHERE THEY SETTLED — a clean Prelims item:** *Delhi — **LAJPAT NAGAR, RAJINDER NAGAR, PUNJABI BAGH, NIZAMUDDIN EAST, KINGSWAY CAMP** · West Pakistan migrants → **Punjab (then including Haryana) and Himachal** · ⭐ **SINDHI HINDUS → Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh**, with ⭐⭐ **ULHASNAGAR ("city of joy") in Maharashtra developed specifically for them** · East Pakistan migrants → **West Bengal, Assam, Tripura and the north-east**, and ⭐ **some to the ANDAMAN ISLANDS — which is why Bengalis are the largest linguistic group in parts of the Andamans today.***

**30 January 1948, at the evening prayer meeting at Birla House.** ⚠️ **You already have the event from [Cl.12 Th.11](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/11_Theme11_Mahatma_Gandhi_and_the_Nationalist_Movement.md). What follows is new.**

2. **Renounce its agenda of VIOLENCE AND SECRECY**
3. **Profess PUBLICLY loyalty to the Indian CONSTITUTION AND FLAG (the tricolour)**
4. **Organise itself on DEMOCRATIC principles**

## 1️⃣4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The communist insurgency — the total gap

⚠️ **Nothing anywhere in your notes covers this, and it is the origin point of the Indian left's relationship with the Indian state.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** "YE AZADI JHOOTI HAI" **
   ================================================================

   ** DECEMBER 1947 ** -- the ** CPI DENOUNCED INDIAN
      INDEPENDENCE AS "FAKE" ** -- ⭐ ** the slogan
      "YE AZADI JHOOTI HAI" ** -- and termed Nehru's Congress
      government ** "the stooges of ANGLO-AMERICAN IMPERIALISM
      and the FEUDAL FORCES within the country". **

   ** FEB-MARCH 1948 ** -- at its ** SECOND CONGRESS in CALCUTTA **
      the CPI adopted its ⭐ ** "POLITICAL THESIS" ** , formally
      declaring the national government of 15 August 1947
      ** "the MAJOR ENEMY of the Indian people" ** , to be changed
      ** through GENERAL REVOLUTION. **

   ⭐⭐ ** THE B.T. RANADIVE LINE ** ( after the CPI's then general
      secretary ) : ** "the present state will be replaced by a
      PEOPLE'S DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC -- a republic of workers,
      peasants and oppressed middle classes." **

   ** WHERE IT SPREAD: ** ** TELANGANA ** ( Hyderabad ) and
      ** WEST BENGAL ** -- the revival of the ** TEBHAGA
      MOVEMENT ** and an ** URBAN INSURGENCY IN CALCUTTA. **
```

**⚙️ WHY — Spectrum gives three reasons, and all three are usable:**

1. ⭐ **A tactical argument:** class struggle and armed insurgency were held necessary **to shift the attention of the masses AWAY FROM THE POLITICS OF COMMUNAL HATRED** that shrouded the country after Partition
2. ⭐⭐ **The international moment:** communist successes in **China, Malaya, Indonesia, the Philippines and Burma**, plus Russia's ⭐ **"A. ZHDANOV THESIS" of September 1947** — the answer to the **Marshall Plan** — which encouraged activism by communist parties everywhere
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **A misreading, per RAMACHANDRA GUHA:** the leadership, encouraged by early Telangana successes, **mistook *"scattered disillusionment with the Congress"* for REVOLUTIONARY POTENTIAL**, and thought it *"the beginning of Red India"*

**⚔️ HOW IT ENDED — and the ending is the important half:**

| | What happened |
|---|---|
| **The limits** | The movement **stayed localised in Hyderabad and West Bengal**; mass support was ⭐ *"sporadic and conditional, as people were NOT READY TO REJECT THE CONGRESS SO SOON AFTER INDEPENDENCE"* |
| **The repression** | **"Police action" continued in Hyderabad**; ⭐ **the CPI was BANNED in West Bengal in March 1948**, and a **security act allowed imprisonment without trial** |
| **The splits** | Divisions between the **"CHINESE LINE"** and the **"RUSSIAN LINE"**, widening after **a proposed railway strike failed on 9 May 1949** |
| ⭐⭐ **The turn** | **September 1950 — AJOY GHOSH, S.A. DANGE and S.V. GHATE criticised the party's strategy and its failure to see independent India as it was** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The decision** | **OCTOBER 1951, THIRD PARTY CONGRESS, CALCUTTA — the Telangana movement was WITHDRAWN** and an inclusive front of peasants, workers and the middle classes adopted. ⭐ **The ban was lifted, and the communists CONTESTED THE 1951–52 GENERAL ELECTION — "thus moving from an INSURRECTIONIST PATH to the path of CONSTITUTIONAL DEMOCRACY"** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BEST SINGLE PIECE OF EVIDENCE IN THE WHOLE FILE FOR WHY INDIAN DEMOCRACY HELD, AND IT IS ALMOST NEVER USED IN ANSWERS.** *Within five months of independence the largest organised force to the left of the Congress declared the new state **the principal enemy** and took up arms. ⭐⭐ **Four years later it stood for election instead.*** ⭐⭐⭐ **Not because it was defeated militarily — it was not — but because *"people were not ready to reject the Congress so soon after independence"*, and because the party itself concluded it had misread the country.** ⚠️ **A first-class answer notes both halves: the state used real repression (a ban, detention without trial, the army in Hyderabad) AND the insurgency lost its argument. Democracies are consolidated by both, and pretending otherwise is not history.**

⭐⭐ **And the loose thread worth flagging for GS3:** *the Telangana movement is the direct ancestor of the Naxalbari rising of 1967 and of the Maoist movement, and the Tebhaga movement links straight back to the **jotedars and *bargadars*** of [Cl.12 Th.9](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) — *tebhaga* means **"three shares"**, the sharecropper's demand for two-thirds rather than one-half.*

## ⚡ UPSC FOCUS

### 🔴 Prelims traps

| ⚠️ The claim that looks right | ✅ The truth |
|---|---|
| *Sardar Patel was Home Minister only* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He was Deputy PM and held HOME, STATES, and Information & Broadcasting. The States portfolio is the one that mattered here** |
| *Ambedkar and Shyama Prasad Mookherjee were Congress ministers* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Ambedkar belonged to the SCHEDULED CASTES FEDERATION and Mookherjee to the HINDU MAHASABHA. Mookherjee resigned FIRST, in April 1950, over the Liaquat-Nehru Pact; Ambedkar in 1951** |
| *India became a republic on 15 August 1947* | ⭐⭐⭐ **On 26 JANUARY 1950. Mountbatten and then C. RAJAGOPALACHARI were Governor-General until that date** |
| *The Radcliffe report was published before independence* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It was READY BY 12 AUGUST and MOUNTBATTEN INTENTIONALLY WITHHELD IT UNTIL AFTER 15 AUGUST, so that responsibility for the consequences would not fall on the British** |
| *Radcliffe was given six months* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SIX WEEKS — with out-of-date maps, a faulty 1941 census, and no prior experience of India** |
| *The boundary force was a British formation that failed to act* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **50,000 men; NEHRU DECIDED NOT TO ALLOW BRITISH TROOPS IN, and the force itself DIVIDED ALONG COMMUNAL LINES** |
| *The Joint Defence Council was headed by Mountbatten* | ⭐⭐⭐ **By AUCHINLECK as Supreme Commander — and the post was ABOLISHED amid the disagreements** |
| *The sterling balance dispute was about India's debt to Britain* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The reverse — about ₹550 million BRITAIN OWED JOINTLY TO BOTH DOMINIONS for Indian contributions to the Second World War, which India withheld from Pakistan over Kashmir** |
| *The RSS ban was imposed after the Punjab violence in 1947* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **On 4 FEBRUARY 1948, after Gandhi's assassination — and lifted in JULY 1949 on four conditions** |
| *The Liaquat-Nehru Pact was signed in 1947* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **8 APRIL 1950, and it is also called the DELHI PACT ON MINORITIES** |
| *The CPI supported the transfer of power in 1947* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It called it FAKE — "ye azadi jhooti hai" — and at its Calcutta Congress of 1948 declared the national government "the MAJOR ENEMY of the Indian people"** |
| *The Telangana movement was withdrawn after military defeat* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Withdrawn by a PARTY DECISION at the Third Party Congress, Calcutta, OCTOBER 1951 — after which the CPI contested the 1951-52 general election** |
| *The Chamber of Princes could legislate for the states* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Purely CONSULTATIVE AND ADVISORY, set up in 1921 on the Montford recommendations, with NO say in internal affairs and NO power to discuss existing rights** |
| *The Butler Committee defined paramountcy* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It left it UNDEFINED — Spectrum's "hydra-headed creature… left to feed on usage, the Crown's prerogative and the princes' implied consent." Its second finding was that states could not be handed over WITHOUT THEIR CONSENT** |
| *The 1935 federation gave the princes a minority of seats* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **125 of 375 in the Federal Assembly and 104 of 160 in the Council of States — a MAJORITY in the upper house. It never came into existence and was dropped after September 1939** |
| *Patel asked the princes to surrender internal sovereignty at accession* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Only DEFENCE, COMMUNICATION and EXTERNAL AFFAIRS — the three subjects the Crown's paramountcy had already covered and over which the states had never had control** |
| *All the princely states had joined by 15 August 1947* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **136 had joined; "others remained precariously outside"** |
| *Junagadh was settled by military action* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **By a PLEBISCITE. Hyderabad was the ARMY ("police action"); Kashmir was an INSTRUMENT OF ACCESSION** |
| *The Nizam sought an outlet to the sea through Bombay* | ⭐⭐⭐ **Through GOA** |
| *Kashmir's accession was endorsed only by the Maharaja* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It was also endorsed by SHEIKH ABDULLAH, the popular leader — which is what gave it political weight** |
| *The five unions included Mysore* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Mysore was NOT one of the five. The five were KATHIAWAR · MATSYA · PEPSU · RAJASTHAN · TRAVANCORE-COCHIN. Mysore accepted Union jurisdiction alongside them, which is a different thing** |
| *Part C states were the princely unions* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Part C = the 61 CENTRALLY ADMINISTERED states (Himachal, Vindhya Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Bhopal). The 216 smallest were absorbed into contiguous provinces as Part A** |
| *The Seventh Amendment created the linguistic states only* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It ALSO ABOLISHED PART B STATES AS A CLASS, merging Parts A and B into one category — which is what ended the princely states as a separate constitutional order** |

| Question shape | The line to take |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Examine the process by which the princely states were integrated into the Indian Union."** *(a named syllabus item)* | ⭐ **Structure it as ACCESSION then INTEGRATION — two separate steps.** Accession was made easy by asking only for the three subjects the states never controlled; integration followed through the three-part device (absorption, central administration, the five unions) and ended with the SEVENTH AMENDMENT. ⭐⭐⭐ **Then the three exceptions, each settled by a different instrument — plebiscite, army, signature** |

Discuss."** | ⭐ **562 states covering 712,508 sq mi; paramountcy left deliberately undefined by the Butler Committee so that it LAPSED rather than transferred; Cripps confirming it would not pass to any Indian party.** ⭐⭐ **Nothing in law delivered a united India — it was assembled between 1947 and 1956** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Assess the challenges before India in the first years of independence."** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Use Spectrum's THREE TIERS as the skeleton, then argue the causal link: the immediate tier RESHAPED the medium tier — the violence outside is part of why the Constituent Assembly chose a strong Centre ([Cl.12 Th.12](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/12_Theme12_Framing_the_Constitution.md))** |

A](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/13_Supplement_A_Understanding_Partition.md)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Why did Indian democracy survive its first decade when so many decolonised states did not?"** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The COMMUNIST case is the strongest evidence available and almost nobody uses it: an armed insurgency declared the state the main enemy in 1948 and stood for election in 1951-52.** ⭐ **Give BOTH causes — real repression (ban, detention without trial, the army in Hyderabad) AND the loss of the argument, since people "were not ready to reject the Congress so soon after independence"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Relief and rehabilitation after Partition reproduced existing inequalities."** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The differentiated entitlements — social and cultural capital got the better deal, "the depressed classes were given little or no consideration"** · **Bengal's migrants not recognised as refugees at all** · **"evacuee property" as empty rhetoric** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **"Trace the evolution of relations between the British and the princely states."** | ⭐ **The SIX STAGES verbatim, with the hinge at 1857: annexation abandoned because the states had proved to be "BREAKWATERS in political storms" — "punish or depose but not annex"** ⭐⭐ **and the sting: the ruler now held his gaddi as a GIFT, not as a right** |

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***PARAMOUNTCY*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The Crown's supremacy over the princely states — deliberately LEFT UNDEFINED by the Butler Committee (1927), and therefore held to LAPSE rather than transfer in 1947** |
| **RING FENCE** | ⭐ **Warren Hastings' policy (1765–1813) of creating BUFFER ZONES to defend the Company's frontiers** — the Subsidiary Alliance was its extension |
| **SUBORDINATE ISOLATION** | **1813–1857 — states keep INTERNAL administration, surrender ALL EXTERNAL sovereignty; Residents become controlling officers** |
| **SUBORDINATE UNION** | ⭐⭐ **1857–1935 — annexation abandoned; "punish or depose but not annex"; succession sanctioned by the Crown** |
| ***NARENDRA MANDAL*** | ⭐ **The CHAMBER OF PRINCES, 1921, on the Montford recommendations — consultative and advisory only** |
| **BUTLER COMMITTEE** | ⭐⭐ **1927 — examined the nature of Crown-state relations; held paramountcy supreme, and that states could not be handed to an Indian government WITHOUT THEIR CONSENT** |
| **INSTRUMENT OF ACCESSION** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The document by which a state acceded — initially on DEFENCE, COMMUNICATION and EXTERNAL AFFAIRS only** |
| **STANDSTILL AGREEMENT** | ⭐ **An interim arrangement continuing existing relations pending a decision — signed by HYDERABAD in November 1947** |
| ***RAZAKARS*** | ⭐⭐ **The Nizam of Hyderabad's police and stormtroopers, whose violence prompted the Indian "police action" of 1948** |
| **"POLICE ACTION"** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The official description of the 1948 Hyderabad operation — a phrase that ASSERTS the territory was already internal** |
| **PART A / PART B / PART C STATES** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The 1950 classification — A: former provinces (with 216 small states absorbed) · B: the former princely unions and Mysore · C: 61 centrally administered states. ⭐ PART B was ABOLISHED as a class by the SEVENTH AMENDMENT, 1956** |
| **PEPSU** | ⭐ **Patiala and East Punjab States Union — one of the FIVE unions** |
| **STERLING BALANCE** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **~₹550 million owed by BRITAIN JOINTLY TO BOTH DOMINIONS for Indian contributions to the Second World War; India withheld Pakistan's share, and GANDHI FASTED to force its transfer** |
| **EVACUEE PROPERTY** | ⭐⭐ **Property left behind by those who migrated, notionally protected by government for their return — in practice unenforceable, which made return impossible.** Later governed by the **Enemy Property Act, 1968** |
| **DELHI PACT ON MINORITIES** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The LIAQUAT-NEHRU PACT, 8 April 1950 — minority ministers, minority commissions, commissions of inquiry, and an agency to recover abducted women** |
| ***"YE AZADI JHOOTI HAI"*** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"This freedom is false" — the CPI's slogan of December 1947** |
| **B.T. RANADIVE LINE** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The CPI's insurrectionary line adopted at the Calcutta Congress, 1948 — the state to be replaced by a "people's democratic republic"** |
| **ZHDANOV THESIS** | ⭐ **The Soviet answer to the Marshall Plan, September 1947, urging greater activism by communist parties** |
| ***TEBHAGA*** | ⭐⭐ **"Three shares" — the Bengal sharecroppers' demand for two-thirds of the crop instead of one-half; revived during the insurgency** |

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NCERT explicitly hands this file the princely-state question and says the answer is not in its own book. This IS the answer**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The first cabinet contained the Scheduled Castes Federation and the Hindu Mahasabha; both men resigned within four years. PATEL held HOME AND STATES together**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE TIERS of challenge — and the immediate tier reshaped the medium one, which is why the Constitution has a strong Centre**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The princely states were the RESIDUE: the Company took the coasts, the river valleys and the fertile land, and left "the inaccessible and less fertile tracts"**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SIX STAGES, hinging on 1857: annexation abandoned because the states were "BREAKWATERS in political storms" — but the ruler now held his throne as A GIFT, not a right**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The BUTLER COMMITTEE left paramountcy undefined, so in 1947 it LAPSED rather than transferring — which is why 562 rulers briefly believed themselves sovereign**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PATEL AND MENON ASKED FOR NOTHING: defence, communication and external affairs were the three subjects the states had never controlled. 136 signed by 15 August**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE EXCEPTIONS, THREE INSTRUMENTS — JUNAGADH by plebiscite · HYDERABAD by army ("police action", Sept 1948; acceded Nov 1949) · KASHMIR by an Instrument of Accession under invasion, endorsed by Sheikh Abdullah, leaving 84,000 sq km occupied and Article 370 behind**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **INTEGRATION was a sorting problem — 216 absorbed (Part A) · 61 centrally administered (Part C) · FIVE UNIONS · then the SEVENTH AMENDMENT (1956) abolished Part B and made the set-up uniform**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE CPI CALLED INDEPENDENCE FAKE IN 1947 AND CONTESTED ELECTIONS IN 1951–52. That reversal is the best available evidence for why Indian democracy held**
