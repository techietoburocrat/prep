# 💥 PART A — THE FIRST PHASE OF REVOLUTIONARY ACTIVITY, 1907–1917

## 1️⃣ Why, and how ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

```
   ================================================================
   ** TWO PHASES, ONE ORIGIN **
   ================================================================

   ** PHASE 1 (1907-1917) ** -- a fallout of the SWADESHI AND
      BOYCOTT MOVEMENT.
   ** PHASE 2 ** -- a fallout of the NON-COOPERATION MOVEMENT
      ( see PART C ) .

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE MECHANISM IS THE SAME BOTH TIMES: ** a mass
      movement rises, then WITHDRAWS or STALLS -- and the
      YOUNGER PARTICIPANTS, unwilling to "disappear into the
      background", find every avenue of PEACEFUL protest closed
      by government repression, and conclude that the British
      ** "must be expelled physically by force." **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT MECHANISM IS THE SINGLE MOST REUSABLE IDEA IN THIS FILE.** *Revolutionary terrorism in India was not a rival strategy competing with mass politics from the start — **it was the RESIDUE mass politics left behind each time it paused.** ⭐⭐ **Two withdrawals, two waves: Swadeshi's decline produces Phase 1; Non-Cooperation's sudden withdrawal in 1922 produces Phase 2 (§10).*** ⚠️ **An answer that treats the revolutionaries as simply "impatient with Gandhi" misses that the SAME pattern predates Gandhi entirely.**

**⚔️ THE PROGRAMME — three methods, explicitly NOT a mass strategy:**

1. **Assassinations** of unpopular officials and of **traitors and informers within their own ranks**
2. ⭐ **"SWADESHI DACOITIES"** — armed robbery to fund revolutionary activity
3. *(during WWI)* **Military conspiracies**, hoping for help from Britain's wartime enemies

⭐⭐⭐ **The model was NOT mass revolution or subverting the Army — both considered and rejected as impractical.** *They followed **RUSSIAN NIHILISTS and IRISH NATIONALISTS**: individual heroic action, meant *"to strike terror in the hearts of the rulers, arouse people and remove the fear of authority from their minds."*

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SPECTRUM'S OWN CRITIQUE, worth reproducing in full because it is the chapter's thesis:** *the Extremist leaders *"failed to IDEOLOGICALLY COUNTER the revolutionaries"* because they never explained the difference **between a revolution based on MASS activity and one based on INDIVIDUAL violent activity** — so individualism took root by default, not by argument.*

```

   ** BENGAL, 1902-1915 -- THE FULL SEQUENCE **
   ================================================================

         Basu) and CALCUTTA -- ** THE ANUSHILAN SAMITI **
         ( Promotha Mitter; Jatindranath Banerjee, Barindra
         Kumar Ghosh ) -- limited to PHYSICAL AND MORAL

         ** Narendra Gosain ** was SHOT DEAD IN JAIL by
         two co-accused.

   1909  Public prosecutor shot dead in Calcutta.
   1910  A deputy SP shot dead leaving Calcutta High Court.
   1908  ** BARRAH DACOITY ** by Dacca Anushilan
         (Pulin Das) to raise funds.

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 1912  ** RASHBEHARI BOSE and SACHIN SANYAL **
```

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SPECTRUM'S OWN VERDICT ON BENGAL, AND IT IS A BALANCED ONE — REPRODUCE ALL THREE PARTS.** *It became *"the most substantial legacy of Swadeshi Bengal"*, inspiring educated youth for a generation. ⚠️ **BUT: an OVEREMPHASIS ON HINDU RELIGION KEPT MUSLIMS ALOOF; it encouraged "QUIXOTIC HEROISM" with NO INVOLVEMENT OF THE MASSES; and its NARROW UPPER-CASTE social base "SEVERELY LIMITED the scope" — so "it failed to withstand the weight of State repression."*** ⭐⭐⭐ **Note this critique in advance of Chittagong (§11), which will explicitly correct the religiosity and the caste-narrowness — you can then argue the second wave LEARNED from the first.**

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Ghadr Party — the total gap

**⚙️ WHO THEY WERE:** *mainly **EX-SOLDIERS AND PEASANTS who had migrated from PUNJAB to the USA and CANADA** for work, based along the **Pacific coast**.* **Pre-Ghadr activists:** Ramdas Puri, G.D. Kumar, Taraknath Das, Sohan Singh Bhakna, and ⭐ **LALA HARDAYAL (reached 1911)** — who set up **'Swadesh Sevak Home' at Vancouver** and **'United India House' at Seattle**. ⭐⭐ **The Ghadr Party itself was established in 1913, headquartered at SAN FRANCISCO.**

**📋 THE PROGRAMME:** *assassinate officials · publish revolutionary literature · **work among Indian troops stationed abroad** · procure arms · **bring about a SIMULTANEOUS revolt in all British colonies.***

⭐⭐ **Moving spirits:** **Lala Hardayal · Ramchandra · Bhagwan Singh · KARTAR SINGH SARABA · Barkatullah · Bhai Parmanand.**

**🚢 THE KOMAGATA MARU INCIDENT, 1914:**

```
   A ship carrying ** 370 PASSENGERS ** ( mainly SIKH and
   PUNJABI MUSLIM would-be immigrants ) sailed from SINGAPORE
   for VANCOUVER.

   -> ** TURNED BACK by CANADIAN AUTHORITIES ** after two months
      of privation -- believed to be at BRITISH INFLUENCE.
   -> The ship anchored at ** CALCUTTA, SEPTEMBER 1914 ** ;
      passengers refused to board the Punjab-bound train.
   -> ** CONFLICT WITH POLICE at BUDGE BUDGE near Calcutta --
      22 PEOPLE DIED. **
```

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Inflamed by this AND by the outbreak of WWI, the Ghadrites planned a coordinated armed revolt for ⭐ 21 FEBRUARY 1915, at the FEROZEPUR, LAHORE and RAWALPINDI garrisons.** ⚠️ **The plan was FOILED AT THE LAST MOMENT BY TREACHERY.** *The Defence of India Rules 1915 gave the authorities the tools: **rebellious regiments disbanded, leaders arrested and deported, and 45 HANGED.** Rashbehari Bose fled to Japan; Sachin Sanyal was transported for life.*

⭐ **Fund-raising note:** *the January–February 1915 Punjab political dacoities had **"a somewhat new social content"** — in at least 3 of 5 major cases the raiders targeted **MONEYLENDERS AND DEBT RECORDS**, not just cash — an early sign of class politics entering revolutionary method (compare **§13**, where the HSRA reaches the same point deliberately).*

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE DEFENCE OF INDIA ACT, MARCH 1915** — passed **primarily to smash the Ghadr movement**; NCERT does not carry this Act at all. It brought **large-scale detentions without trial, special courts, and army courts-martial**; besides Bengal and Punjab revolutionaries, **radical pan-Islamists — the ALI BROTHERS, MAULANA AZAD, HASRAT MOHANI — were interned for years.**

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SPECTRUM'S BALANCED EVALUATION OF GHADR:** *its achievement lay **"in the realm of ideology"** — it preached militant nationalism **with a completely secular approach**, a point worth keeping given Bengal's opposite failing (§2). ⚠️ **Politically and militarily it achieved little: no organised, sustained leadership; the extent of preparation required — organisational, ideological, financial, tactical — was underestimated; and Lala Hardayal himself was, in Spectrum's judgement, "perhaps… unsuited for the job of an organiser."***

**🌍 THE BERLIN COMMITTEE, 1915** — founded by **Virendranath Chattopadhyay, Bhupendranath Dutta, Lala Hardayal**, with **German foreign office backing under the ZIMMERMAN PLAN**, aiming to send volunteers and arms and incite Indian troops abroad. Missions went to **Baghdad, Persia, Turkey and Kabul**; one under **RAJA MAHENDRA PRATAP SINGH, BARKATULLAH and OBAIDULLAH SINDHI** reached Kabul to found **a "provisional Indian government"** with the crown prince **Amanullah**.

⭐ **And a scattered mutiny worth one line:** *the ⭐ **SINGAPORE MUTINY, 15 FEBRUARY 1915** — the Punjabi Muslim 5th Light Infantry and the 36th Sikh battalion, under Jamadar Chisti Khan, Jamadar Abdul Gani and Subedar Daud Khan — crushed; **37 executed, 41 transported for life.***

⭐⭐ **THE DECLINE OF PHASE 1** — three reasons: **prisoners released under the Defence of India Rules, cooling passions**; **Montagu's August 1917 statement and the talk of constitutional reform**; and **the arrival of Gandhi with non-violent non-cooperation, offering new hope.**

# ⚖️ PART B — THE SWARAJISTS AND THE NO-CHANGERS

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why the Congress split ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **This is, alongside the reform-frame in [file 05](05_Reform_Movements.md), the single most important analytical passage in the whole Spectrum series so far — and it is completely absent from NCERT.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** MARCH 1922 -- GANDHI ARRESTED. WHAT DO YOU DO NOW? **
   ================================================================

   ⭐⭐ ** SWARAJISTS ** -- C.R. DAS, MOTILAL NEHRU, AJMAL KHAN
       -- wanted to END the boycott of legislative councils, ENTER
       them, and use them ** "as an arena of political struggle" **
       -- to ** "END OR MEND" ** the councils: if government would
       not respond, OBSTRUCT them from within.

   ⭐⭐ ** NO-CHANGERS ** -- C. RAJAGOPALACHARI, VALLABHBHAI
       PATEL, RAJENDRA PRASAD, M.A. ANSARI -- opposed council
       entry, wanted CONSTRUCTIVE WORK, continued boycott and
       non-cooperation, and QUIET PREPARATION for resuming civil
       disobedience.
```

**⚖️ THE ARGUMENTS ON EACH SIDE:**

| ⭐ **SWARAJISTS SAID** | ⭐ **NO-CHANGERS SAID** |
|---|---|
| Council entry would **not negate** non-cooperation — it opens **a new front** | Parliamentary work would mean **neglect of constructive work, loss of revolutionary zeal, and political corruption** |
| In a political vacuum, council work **keeps up mass morale** and stops the government stuffing councils with **"undesirable elements"** who lend it legitimacy | **Constructive work would prepare everyone** for the next round of civil disobedience |
| The councils were an **arena of struggle**, NOT organs for gradual transformation | — |

**⚔️ THE SPLIT ITSELF:** the Swarajist proposal was **defeated at the GAYA session (December 1922)**; **C.R. DAS and MOTILAL NEHRU resigned** their presidentship and secretaryship, announcing the ⭐ **CONGRESS-KHILAFAT SWARAJYA PARTY** ("Swarajist Party"), **Das as president, Motilal Nehru as one of the secretaries.**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOTE THE RESTRAINT ON BOTH SIDES, BECAUSE IT IS THE POINT SPECTRUM MOST WANTS YOU TO SEE.** *Both sides **"wanted to avoid a 1907-type split"** and stayed in touch with the jailed Gandhi; both recognised the need for **a united front** and **accepted Gandhi's leadership**. ⭐⭐ **A disagreement about METHOD did not become a disagreement about LEADERSHIP or GOAL** — which is exactly why a compromise proved possible a year later.*

## 6️⃣ The 1923 compromise, the manifesto, and Gandhi's turn ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**🤝 THE DELHI COMPROMISE, SEPTEMBER 1923:** *Swarajists **allowed to contest as a group WITHIN Congress**, accepting the Congress programme **with one difference** — council entry. Elections to the new **Central Legislative Assembly and provincial assemblies** set for **November 1923**.*

**📜 THE SWARAJIST MANIFESTO, OCTOBER 1923 — a strong anti-imperialist line, and worth its own line-item:**

1. **British rule was guided by SELFISH interests**
2. **"Reforms" were a blind for continued exploitation, disguised as responsible government**
3. **They would demand SELF-GOVERNMENT in the councils**
4. ⭐⭐ **If refused, a policy of "UNIFORM, CONTINUOUS AND CONSISTENT OBSTRUCTION"**
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **The councils would be "WRECKED FROM WITHIN" by creating deadlocks on every measure**

**🔄 GANDHI'S ATTITUDE — INITIALLY OPPOSED, then reconciled, for THREE stated reasons:**

1. **Public opposition to council entry would be counter-productive**
2. ⭐⭐ **The Swarajists WON 42 of 141 elected seats** in November 1923 and a **clear majority in the Central Provinces**; in cooperation with **the Liberals, and independents like JINNAH and MALAVIYA**, they won majorities — the **"courageous and uncompromising manner"** of their conduct convinced Gandhi they would not become **"just another limb of colonial administration"**
3. ⭐⭐ **A late-1924 government crackdown on revolutionary terrorists AND the Swarajists angered Gandhi**, who then **"expressed his solidarity… by surrendering to their wishes"**

⭐⭐⭐ **The reconciliation was endorsed at the BELGAUM SESSION, December 1924 — the ONLY Congress session GANDHI HIMSELF PRESIDED OVER.**

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Achievements, drawbacks, and the collapse

**✅ FOUR ACHIEVEMENTS:**

| # | What |
|:--:|---|
| **1** | **With coalition partners, out-voted the government several times**, including on **budgetary grants**, and passed **adjournment motions** |
| **2** | **Powerful speeches** on self-government, civil liberties, industrialisation |
| **3** | ⭐⭐ **VITHALBHAI PATEL elected SPEAKER of the Central Legislative Assembly, 1925** |
| **4** | ⭐⭐⭐ **DEFEATED THE PUBLIC SAFETY BILL, 1928** — aimed at deporting **"undesirable and subversive foreigners"**, driven by government alarm at spreading **socialist and communist ideas** and suspected **Comintern** activity |

*(Plus: filled the **political vacuum** during recovery, exposed **the hollowness of the Montford scheme**, and demonstrated the councils **could be used creatively**.)*

**⚠️ FIVE DRAWBACKS:**

1. **No policy to coordinate militancy inside the legislatures with mass struggle outside** — relied **entirely on newspaper reporting**
2. **An obstructionist strategy had inherent limits**
3. **Coalition partners could not be relied on** for long, given conflicting ideas
4. ⚠️ **Failed to resist the PERKS AND PRIVILEGES of office**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FAILED TO SUPPORT THE PEASANTS' CAUSE AGAINST THE ZAMINDARS IN BENGAL — where most tenants were MUSLIM, costing the party MUSLIM SUPPORT**

**📉 THE DECLINE — four forces converging:**

```
   ** COMMUNAL RIOTS ** ( widespread, mid-1920s )
        +
   ** THE BENGAL TENANCY FAILURE ** ( §7, drawback 5 -- Muslim
      tenants abandoned by a party that would not confront
      Hindu zamindars )
        +
   ⭐⭐ ** RESPONSIVIST vs NON-RESPONSIVIST SPLIT ** -- the
      RESPONSIVISTS ( LALA LAJPAT RAI, MADAN MOHAN MALAVIYA,
      N.C. KELKAR ) wanted COOPERATION with government and
      office-holding, AND to protect "Hindu interests" --
      communalists then called MOTILAL NEHRU ( who opposed
      joining ) "ANTI-HINDU", while Muslim communalists called
      the Swarajists "ANTI-MUSLIM"
        +
   ⭐⭐ ** C.R. DAS'S DEATH, 1925 **

   = ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** MARCH 1926 -- the main leadership REITERATED
      faith in mass civil disobedience and WITHDREW from the
      legislatures. ** A remaining faction fought the 1926
      elections "as a party in disarray" -- won 40 seats at the
      Centre, some in Madras, ** ROUTED in UP, the Central
      Provinces and Punjab. **

   ⭐ ** 1930 -- the Swarajists FINALLY WALKED OUT ** , following
      the Lahore Congress's PURNA SWARAJ resolution and the
      start of Civil Disobedience.
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GOVERNMENT'S OWN STRATEGY, STATED PLAINLY, IS THE BEST ANALYTICAL LINE IN THE CHAPTER:** *Spectrum records that the government deliberately worked at ⭐⭐⭐ **"dividing the Swarajists — the more militant from the moderate, the Hindus from the Muslims — and it was successful."*** ⭐⭐ **The party did not merely decay from internal contradiction; it was actively split along the seam the Bengal tenancy failure had already opened.** *That is the same divide-and-govern instinct [Spectrum file 05](05_Reform_Movements.md) records in Syed Ahmed Khan's later career — an instrument used twice, on two different targets, thirty years apart.*

## 8️⃣ The No-changers' constructive work — and its own honest critique ⭐⭐⭐

**🏗️ WHAT THEY DID:**

| # | Work |
|:--:|---|
| **1** | **Ashrams among tribals and lower castes**, especially in **KHEDA and BARDOLI, Gujarat**; popularised **charkha and khadi** |
| **2** | **National schools and colleges**, teaching a **non-colonial ideological framework** |
| **3** | Work for **Hindu-Muslim unity, removing untouchability, boycotting foreign cloth and liquor**, and **flood relief** |
| **4** | Served as the **backbone of civil disobedience as active organisers** |

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND SPECTRUM'S OWN CRITIQUE, given as directly as any in this whole file:**

- **National education benefited ONLY the URBAN LOWER MIDDLE CLASSES AND RICH PEASANTS.** Enthusiasm surfaced **only in the excitement** of active movement; in quiet periods, **"the lure of degrees and jobs took the students to OFFICIAL schools and colleges"**
- **Khadi was an uphill task, since it was COSTLIER than imported cloth**
- ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Untouchability was campaigned against as a SOCIAL question, with NO EMPHASIS on the ECONOMIC grievances of landless and agricultural labourers, WHO WERE MOSTLY UNTOUCHABLES**

⭐ **And the closing note is worth keeping — it explains why this rivalry never became a rupture:** *despite working separately, Swarajists and No-changers **"kept on best of terms with one another and were able to unite whenever the time was ripe for a new political struggle."***

# 🚩 PART C — THE NEW FORCES OF THE 1920s

## 9️⃣ Six currents at once ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **Spectrum calls the 1920s a genuine watershed — the decade the movement stops being led exclusively by lawyers and becomes, in its own words, the entry of "Indian masses into the national movement." Six new currents crystallised together.**

### 🚩 9.1 Marxism and socialism, and the CPI

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The left wing WITHIN Congress** was led by **JAWAHARLAL NEHRU and SUBHASH CHANDRA BOSE** — younger nationalists, **inspired by the Soviet Revolution**, dissatisfied with Gandhian method, who were:

- **Critical of BOTH Swarajists and No-Changers**
- **Advocating PURNA SWARAJYA** — complete independence, not dominion status
- **Aware, though vaguely, of international currents**
- ⭐⭐ **Stressing that nationalism and anti-imperialism must combine with SOCIAL JUSTICE**, raising **internal class oppression by capitalists and landlords**

**🚩 THE CPI — a clean sequence:**

| Year | Event |
|:--:|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1920** | **FOUNDED AT TASHKENT** *(now capital of Uzbekistan)* by **M.N. ROY, ABANI MUKHERJI and others**, after the **Second Congress of the COMINTERN.** ⭐ **M.N. Roy was also THE FIRST INDIAN elected to Comintern leadership** |
| **1924** | **THE KANPUR BOLSHEVIK CONSPIRACY CASE** — **S.A. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmed, Shaukat Usmani, Nalini Gupta jailed** |
| ⭐ **1925** | **The Indian Communist Conference at KANPUR FORMALISED the foundation of the CPI** *(inside India)* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1929** | **THE MEERUT CONSPIRACY CASE** — **31 leading communists, trade unionists and left-wing leaders arrested and tried** |

⭐ **Workers' and peasants' parties** propagated Marxist ideas nationwide, **remaining part of the national movement** and working with Congress.

### 🎓 9.2 Youth activism

**Student leagues and conferences spread nationwide** — ⭐ **1928: NEHRU presided over the ALL BENGAL STUDENTS' CONFERENCE.**

### 🌾 9.3 Peasant agitations

**United Provinces** — tenancy revision, lower rents, protection from eviction, relief from indebtedness. Similar in **the Rampa region of Andhra, Rajasthan, and the ryotwari areas of Bombay and Madras.** ⭐⭐ **Gujarat — the BARDOLI SATYAGRAHA, led by VALLABHBHAI PATEL (1928)**, the campaign that earned him **"Sardar."**

### 🏭 9.4 Trade unionism

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **AITUC** | ⭐ **Founded 1920.** **LALA LAJPAT RAI first president; DEWAN CHAMAN LAL general secretary; TILAK a moving spirit** |
| ⭐⭐ **Major strikes of the 1920s** | **Kharagpur Railway Workshops · Tata Iron and Steel, Jamshedpur · BOMBAY TEXTILE MILLS (150,000 workers, FIVE MONTHS) · Buckingham Carnatic Mills** |
| **1928** | **A wave of strikes involving 5 LAKH workers** |

# 🔴 PART D — HRA TO HSRA: PUNJAB–UP–BIHAR

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ From Kakori to the gallows

**⚙️ WHY THE REVIVAL AFTER NON-COOPERATION:** *many revolutionaries had been released under a **1920 general amnesty**, meant to build goodwill for the Montford reforms — then **suspended their own activity** under Gandhi and C.R. Das's persuasion, to give Non-Cooperation a chance. ⚠️ **Its SUDDEN WITHDRAWAL (1922) left them disillusioned**, unattracted to either the Swarajists' parliamentary work or the No-changers' patient constructive work — so **revolutionary activity revived.***

⭐ **The names carried forward from Non-Cooperation into this phase:** Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee, Surya Sen, **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Chandrasekhar Azad**, Shiv Verma, Bhagwaticharan Vohra, Jaidev Kapur, Jatin Das.

**⚙️ THE FIVE INFLUENCES:** *the **post-war rise of trade unionism** (a class the revolutionaries wanted to harness) · the **Russian Revolution and Soviet consolidation** · the **new communist groups' Marxism** · journals celebrating revolutionary sacrifice — ***Atmasakti, Sarathi, Bijoli*** · and novels — **Sachin Sanyal's *Bandi Jiwan*** and **Sharatchandra Chatterjee's *Pather Dabi*** — the latter **more popular for being banned.**

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bhagat Singh's ideological rethinking — the strongest Mains asset in this file

⚠️ **This is Spectrum at its most substantial. Do not compress it to "he became a Marxist" — the ARGUMENT is the exam material.**

**🔄 THE SHIFT, TRACED IN STAGES:**

- **The HRA MANIFESTO, 1925**, already declared it stood for **"abolition of all systems which made exploitation of man by man possible"**; its organ ***Revolutionary*** proposed **NATIONALISING RAILWAYS and heavy industries** *(shipbuilding, steel)*, and starting **labour and peasant organisations** for **"organised and armed revolution."**
- **RAMPRASAD BISMIL**, in his final days, **appealed to the youth to give up pistols**, work in the **open movement**, strengthen **Hindu-Muslim unity**, and unite under Congress leadership — affirming **communism** and that **"every human being has equal rights over the products of nature."**
- ⭐⭐ ***"THE PHILOSOPHY OF THE BOMB"*, by BHAGWATICHARAN VOHRA** — the fullest statement of the revolutionary position.
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Before his own arrest, BHAGAT SINGH had already moved from individual heroic action toward MARXISM**, and toward the belief that only a **"popular, broad-based movement"** could succeed — revolution **"by the masses, for the masses."** ⭐ **He therefore helped found the PUNJAB NAUJAWAN BHARAT SABHA (1926)** as an **open, legal wing** for political work among youth, peasants and workers; and, with Sukhdev, the **LAHORE STUDENTS' UNION.**

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THEN WHY THE INDIVIDUAL ASSASSINATION AND THE BOMB, IF HE HAD ALREADY REJECTED INDIVIDUAL ACTION? SPECTRUM ANSWERS THIS DIRECTLY, AND IT IS THE MOST SOPHISTICATED PASSAGE IN THE CHAPTER.** *Two reasons: **acquiring a new ideology among the masses is a slow, historical process, but the moment required a QUICK change in thinking**; and the young revolutionaries faced **"the classic dilemma of how to mobilise people and recruit them."** ⭐⭐⭐ **They chose "PROPAGANDA BY DEED" — individual heroic action used strategically to draw attention, followed by using the COURT ITSELF as a forum for propaganda.*** ⭐⭐ **The Assembly bomb is the clearest possible instance: they made the device deliberately harmless and stayed to be arrested. The act was not the message — the TRIAL was.**

**📖 THE DEFINITION OF REVOLUTION, quoted in court — worth reproducing exactly:**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"Revolution does NOT necessarily involve sanguinary strife, nor is there a place in it for personal vendetta. It is NOT the cult of bomb and pistol. By revolution we mean the present order of things, which is based on manifest injustice, MUST CHANGE."***

**⚖️ THE CLASS ARGUMENT — and note its precision, which rules out simple anti-colonialism:**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ***"Peasants have to free themselves not only from the FOREIGN yoke, but also from the yoke of LANDLORDS AND CAPITALISTS."*** *And, more pointedly still:* ***"It matters little whether these exploiters are BRITISH capitalists, British and INDIAN capitalists in alliance, or even PURELY INDIANS."***

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THAT LAST SENTENCE IS THE ONE TO QUOTE, BECAUSE IT SEPARATES BHAGAT SINGH FROM EVERY OTHER STRAND IN THIS FILE.** *The Swarajists wanted an Indian assembly; the No-changers wanted Indian self-rule through non-violence; even the Ghadrites wanted the British replaced by Indians. ⭐⭐ **Bhagat Singh's target was not the NATIONALITY of the exploiter but the FACT of exploitation** — an independent India ruled by Indian capitalists would, on his own words, remain unfinished business.* ⚠️ **That is why his socialism is defined "scientifically, as abolition of capitalism and class domination", not merely as a policy preference.**

⭐⭐ **AND HE WAS DELIBERATELY SECULAR** — *two of the six rules Bhagat Singh drafted for the Punjab Naujawan Bharat Sabha were that members would **have nothing to do with communal bodies**, and would propagate **tolerance, treating religion as a matter of personal belief.** He also linked political and intellectual freedom directly — **"to be a revolutionary, one required immense moral strength, but one also required criticism and independent thinking."***

# 🚩 PART E — CHITTAGONG: THE SECOND WAVE IN BENGAL

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Bengal after 1922, and the two factions

⚠️ **After C.R. Das's death (1925), the Bengal Congress split into two factions — J.M. SENGUPTA'S (backed by the Anushilan group) and SUBHASH BOSE'S (backed by Yugantar).** In **1924, an assassination attempt on Calcutta Police Commissioner CHARLES TEGART by GOPINATH SAHA** killed a bystander named Day instead; the government's crackdown arrested **many, including Subhash Bose**; **Saha was hanged.**

## 1️⃣3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Surya Sen and the Chittagong Armoury Raid

**👤 THE MAN:** *had joined **Non-Cooperation**, became a **teacher in the national school at Chittagong**, was imprisoned **1926–28** for revolutionary activity, then worked as **secretary of the Chittagong District Congress Committee**. ⭐ **His own maxim: "Humanism is a special virtue of a revolutionary."** A lover of poetry, admirer of **Tagore and Qazi Nazrul Islam.***

```
   ================================================================
   ** APRIL 1930 -- THE CHITTAGONG ARMOURY RAID **
   ================================================================

   Sen, with ⭐ ** ANANT SINGH, GANESH GHOSH and LOKENATH
   BAUL ** , organised ** 65 ACTIVISTS ** under the banner of
   ** "INDIAN REPUBLICAN ARMY -- CHITTAGONG BRANCH". **

   ** THE PLAN: ** occupy the two main armouries, seize and
   supply arms, destroy telephone and telegraph lines, and cut
   the railway link with the rest of Bengal.

   ⭐⭐⭐ ** THE RAID SUCCEEDED. ** Sen ** HOISTED THE
   NATIONAL FLAG, TOOK SALUTE, and PROCLAIMED A
   PROVISIONAL REVOLUTIONARY GOVERNMENT. ** The group
   then dispersed into neighbouring villages and continued to
   raid government targets.

   ** Sen was arrested February 1933, HANGED JANUARY 1934 **
   -- but the raid ** "fired the imagination of the
   revolutionary-minded youth" ** and recruits kept coming.
```

**👩 THE NEW PHASE'S DISTINCTIVE FEATURE — LARGE-SCALE PARTICIPATION BY WOMEN:**

| Name | Role |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PRITILATA WADDEDAR** | **Died conducting a raid** |
| **KALPANA DUTT** | **Arrested and tried alongside Surya Sen; life sentence** |
| ⭐⭐ **SANTI GHOSH and SUNITI CHANDHERI** | **Schoolgirls of Comilla — shot dead the DISTRICT MAGISTRATE, December 1931** |
| ⭐⭐ **BINA DAS** | **Fired point-blank at the GOVERNOR while receiving her own degree, at convocation, February 1932** |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE FACT MOST LIKELY TO BE ASKED FROM THE WHOLE FILE, AND IT IS ABSENT FROM YOUR ENTIRE NCERT ARCHIVE.** *Women in this phase were not auxiliary — Spectrum records they **"provided shelter, carried messages, and fought with guns in hand."** ⭐⭐ **Two of the four named above carried out assassinations themselves. Bina Das fired at a governor at her OWN convocation** — the ceremony marking her formal entry into respectable colonial society became the moment of the attack.*

**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THREE WAYS THIS PHASE DELIBERATELY DIFFERED FROM THE FIRST (§2):**

1. **GROUP action against organs of the colonial STATE, replacing individual assassination** — meant to **set an example and demoralise the bureaucracy**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE HINDU RELIGIOSITY OF THE FIRST WAVE WAS SHED** — no more oath-taking rituals — *"this FACILITATED PARTICIPATION BY MUSLIMS."* **Surya Sen's own group included Satar, Mir Ahmed, Fakir Ahmed Mian and Tunu Mian**
3. *(unstated but implied throughout)* the turn toward **mass and class politics** already traced in §11

⚠️ **Yet drawbacks remained: the movement KEPT SOME CONSERVATIVE ELEMENTS, FAILED TO EVOLVE BROADER SOCIO-ECONOMIC GOALS, and — like the Swarajists — FAILED TO SUPPORT THE MUSLIM PEASANTRY AGAINST BENGAL'S ZAMINDARS.** ⭐⭐ *That third failing recurring across the Swarajists AND Chittagong is worth its own sentence in an answer — the SAME structural blind spot defeated two very different strategies.*

⭐ **THE OFFICIAL REACTION** — **20 repressive Acts**; villages in Chittagong burned, punitive fines imposed. **1933: NEHRU himself arrested for sedition and jailed two years**, for condemning imperialism and praising revolutionary heroism.

| *The HRA was founded by Bhagat Singh* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **By RAMPRASAD BISMIL, JOGESH CHANDRA CHATTERJEE and SACHIN SANYAL, October 1924. Bhagat Singh joined the REORGANISED HSRA in 1928** |
| *Bhagat Singh and Rajguru murdered Saunders because he killed Lajpat Rai* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Saunders was the officer responsible for the LATHI-CHARGE that caused Lajpat Rai's death — CHANDRASHEKHAR AZAD was the third man in the killing** |
| *The Assembly bomb of 1929 was meant to kill legislators* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The bombs were deliberately made HARMLESS — the goal was ARREST and a trial used as a propaganda forum** |
| *Jatin Das was hanged with Bhagat Singh* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Jatin Das DIED ON HUNGER STRIKE in jail (64th day) — he was never tried or hanged. Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were hanged 23 March 1931** |
| *Chandrashekhar Azad was hanged alongside Bhagat Singh* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Azad died in a POLICE ENCOUNTER in Allahabad, February 1931 — a month before the hangings** |
| *Surya Sen's Chittagong raid failed* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It SUCCEEDED — the flag was hoisted and a provisional government proclaimed. Sen was arrested only in 1933, hanged 1934** |
| *Bina Das was arrested before she could act* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **She fired point-blank at the Governor AT HER OWN CONVOCATION, February 1932** |

### 🟢 Mains angles

| Question shape | The line to take |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Revolutionary terrorism was the residue mass movements left behind." Examine.** | ⭐ **Use the two-phase structure explicitly: Phase 1 from Swadeshi's decline, Phase 2 from Non-Cooperation's sudden withdrawal.** ⭐⭐ **Both times, the same mechanism — younger activists unwilling to disappear, closed avenues, individual violence as the residual option** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Bhagat Singh's conception of revolution differed fundamentally from that of his predecessors." Discuss.** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Quote directly: "not the cult of bomb and pistol"; "it matters little whether these exploiters are British capitalists, British and Indian capitalists in alliance, or even purely Indians."** ⭐ **Then propaganda by deed as a CONSCIOUS bridging tactic, not a contradiction — acquiring mass ideology takes time the moment did not allow** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Assess the Swarajist experiment within the Congress, 1922–30."** | ⭐ **Structure by the four achievements against the five drawbacks, then the four-part collapse — communal riots, the Bengal tenancy failure, the Responsivist split, Das's death.** ⭐⭐ **Close with the government's own strategy of dividing "the more militant from the moderate, the Hindus from the Muslims" — the party was not merely defeated, it was targeted** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The 1920s saw the crystallisation of new political forces alongside Gandhian mass politics."** | ⭐ **The six currents as a set: Marxism/CPI, youth activism, peasant agitation, trade unionism, caste movements, and revolutionary socialism.** ⭐⭐ **Note that ALL SIX regarded themselves as working alongside, not against, the Congress mainstream** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"Compare the first and second waves of Bengal revolutionary terrorism."** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1908–15 was individual, Hindu-coded, upper-caste and doomed by its own narrowness; 1930s Chittagong was GROUP action, deliberately DE-RELIGIONISED to include Muslims, and included women as combatants, not auxiliaries.** ⭐ **Both shared the same blind spot — failure to support Muslim peasants against Bengal's zamindars** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **"The Ghadr Party's significance lay in ideology rather than in achievement."** | ⭐ **Its secular, cross-religious militant nationalism as the achievement; its lack of organisation, underestimated preparation, and Hardayal's unsuitability as the failure — Spectrum's own balanced verdict, quotable almost verbatim** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **"Trace the role of women in the revolutionary movement of the 1930s."** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Pritilata Waddedar, Kalpana Dutt, Santi Ghosh, Suniti Chandheri, Bina Das — combatants and organisers, not messengers alone. Contrast with the near-total absence of women from the 1907–17 phase** |

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **"SWADESHI DACOITY"** | ⭐⭐ **Armed robbery undertaken specifically to fund revolutionary activity** |
| **ANUSHILAN SAMITI** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The Calcutta secret society (1902) at the centre of the Alipore case; later split into eastern and western wings** |
| ***DAR-UL-ISLAM* / GERMAN PLOT** | ⭐⭐ **The WWI-era scheme to import German arms and organise an all-India insurrection — also called the ZIMMERMAN PLAN** |
| **PROPAGANDA BY DEED** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A dramatic individual action carried out to attract public attention and then used, via the ARREST and TRIAL, as a forum for spreading ideology — the HSRA's Assembly bomb is the clearest example** |
| **"END OR MEND"** | ⭐⭐⭐ **The Swarajists' formula for council entry: use the councils constructively if possible, obstruct them if not** |
| **RESPONSIVISTS** | ⭐⭐ **The Swarajist faction (Lajpat Rai, Malaviya, Kelkar) favouring cooperation and office-holding with government, and protection of "Hindu interests"** |
| **COMINTERN** | ⭐⭐ **The Communist International — the CPI was founded following its Second Congress, at Tashkent, 1920** |
| **PURNA SWARAJYA** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Complete independence — the demand the Congress left (Nehru, Bose) pushed against both Swarajists and No-Changers** |

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Revolutionary terrorism is the RESIDUE mass movements left behind on withdrawal — Phase 1 from Swadeshi's decline, Phase 2 from Non-Cooperation's**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BENGAL'S FIRST WAVE (1908–15): Muzaffarpur, Alipore, the Delhi bomb, the German Plot — undone by Hindu religiosity, upper-caste narrowness, and no mass base**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE GHADR PARTY: secular, cross-Punjab, cross-religious militancy — undone by organisation, not by ideology. The Komagata Maru and 21 February 1915 are its two hinge dates**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SWARAJISTS: formed after the 1922 Gaya defeat, entered the 1923 councils on a manifesto of obstruction, won real victories (the Public Safety Bill defeat, Vithalbhai Patel as Speaker) — and were broken by Bengal's Muslim tenants, the Responsivist split, and a government strategy of deliberate division**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE NO-CHANGERS built the constructive programme that fed civil disobedience — and Spectrum itself notes its narrow class reach and the untouchability campaign's blindness to economic grievance**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SIX NEW FORCES OF THE 1920s: Marxism/CPI, youth, peasants, trade unions, caste movements, revolutionary socialism — all operating alongside, not against, Congress**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE CPI: TASHKENT 1920, formalised at KANPUR 1925, tested at MEERUT 1929**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **HRA (1924, Kakori 1925) → HSRA (1928, Saunders, the Assembly bomb) → the hangings of 1931**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BHAGAT SINGH'S SHIFT: from individual heroism to Marxism, and a definition of revolution that targets exploitation regardless of the exploiter's nationality — "purely Indians" included**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CHITTAGONG, 1930: group action, deliberately de-religionised, women as combatants — Pritilata Waddedar, Kalpana Dutt, Santi Ghosh, Suniti Chandheri, Bina Das**
