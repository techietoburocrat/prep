# 🌾 PART A — THE PEASANT MOVEMENTS BEFORE GANDHI

## 1️⃣ Why the peasantry was impoverished ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**⚙️ FOUR STRUCTURAL CAUSES, and note they are STRUCTURAL, not simply "bad harvests":**

1. **Colonial economic policies**
2. **The ruin of handicrafts, which OVERCROWDED THE LAND** *(the same mechanism [file 03 §2](03_Resistance_Before_1857.md) traces for the 1770s–1850s, now running into the twentieth century)*
3. **The new land revenue system**
4. **The colonial administrative and judicial system**

⭐⭐ **In zamindari areas**: high rents, illegal levies, arbitrary evictions, unpaid labour. **In ryotwari areas**: the government itself levied heavy revenue directly. ⚠️ **Both funnelled the peasant to the moneylender**, who extracted **high interest**, and who — on default — could **seize mortgaged land and cattle**. ⭐⭐⭐ **Over large areas, actual cultivators were reduced to tenants-at-will, sharecroppers and landless labourers.** ⚠️ **Some, driven to desperation, turned to robbery, dacoity and what Spectrum calls "social banditry."**

## 2️⃣ Three nineteenth-century revolts, in full ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 🌱 2.1 The Indigo Revolt, 1859-60

⚠️ **This is the single most-asked topic in this file and it does not appear anywhere in your ~62,000 lines of NCERT History notes.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE INDIGO REVOLT, BENGAL, 1859-60 **
   ================================================================

   ** THE COERCION: ** European planters forced peasants to grow
   ** INDIGO instead of more paying crops like RICE ** , through
   ** ADVANCE SUMS and FRAUDULENT CONTRACTS ** used AGAINST
   the peasants later. ⭐⭐ ** Intimidation: kidnappings, illegal
   confinement, FLOGGING, attacks on women and children,
   seizure of cattle, burning and demolition of houses,
   destruction of crops. **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE RISING, 1859: ** led by ** DIGAMBAR BISWAS
   and BISHNU BISWAS of NADIA district ** -- peasants
   ** REFUSED to grow indigo ** , resisted the planters'
   ** LATHIYALS ** ( backed by police and the courts ) , and
   organised a COUNTER-FORCE.
      -> planters retaliated with EVICTIONS and RAISED RENTS.
      -> the RYOTS ANSWERED WITH A RENT STRIKE and physical
         resistance to eviction, and gradually learned to use
         ** LEGAL MACHINERY ** -- fund collection, court action.

   ⭐⭐ ** THE INTELLIGENTSIA'S ROLE: ** the Bengali intelligentsia
   supported the peasants through NEWSPAPER CAMPAIGNS, mass
   meetings, MEMORANDA on grievances, and LEGAL SUPPORT.

   ** THE OUTCOME: ** an ** INDIGO COMMISSION ** was appointed;
   in ** NOVEMBER 1860 ** the government notified that ryots
   COULD NOT BE COMPELLED to grow indigo, and disputes would
   be settled legally. ⭐ ** But the planters simply CLOSED THEIR
   FACTORIES, and indigo cultivation was VIRTUALLY WIPED OUT
   from Bengal by the end of 1860. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE ENDING IS THE POINT MOST ANSWERS MISS.** *The peasants won the legal argument — the government sided with them — **and the planters simply left, taking the industry with them.** ⭐⭐ **A victory that removes the exploiter also removes the crop, the market and the livelihood built around it.** That is a genuinely different kind of outcome from Champaran, where indigo cultivation itself was already declining and Gandhi's intervention came fifty-eight years later, into an industry already in retreat — worth noting if a question asks you to compare the two indigo episodes.*

### 📜 2.2 The Pabna Agrarian Leagues

⭐ **Eastern Bengal, 1870s-80s.** Zamindars **raised rents beyond legal limits** and **prevented tenants acquiring occupancy rights under Act X of 1859**, through **forcible eviction, seizure of cattle and crops, and prolonged litigation** where the poor peasant was structurally disadvantaged.

⭐⭐ **The peasants of YUSUFSHAHI PARGANA, Patna district, formed an AGRARIAN LEAGUE** — a **rent strike**, funded court challenges, spreading through Patna and other East Bengal districts. ⚠️ **The method was overwhelmingly LEGAL — "very little violence."** *(Continued to 1885; most cases resolved, partly by official persuasion, partly by zamindars' own fear.)*

⭐⭐⭐ **The outcome: THE BENGAL TENANCY ACT, 1885** — protecting tenants against the worst zamindari oppression. ⭐ **Supporters included BANKIM CHANDRA CHATTERJEE, R.C. DUTT, and the Indian Association under SURENDRANATH BANERJEA.**

### 💰 2.3 The Deccan Riots

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Western India, ryotwari, and — unusually — the moneylender rather than the state or the zamindar as the direct target.**

```
   ** THE SETUP: ** heavy ryotwari taxation; moneylenders, mostly
   ** OUTSIDERS -- MARWARIS OR GUJARATIS ** , as the chief
   beneficiaries of peasant distress.

   ** THE TRIGGER: ** a crash in COTTON PRICES after the American
   Civil War ended ( 1864 ) + a ** 50% land revenue rise in
   1867 ** + a run of bad harvests.

   ⭐⭐ ** 1874 -- A SOCIAL BOYCOTT: ** ryots refused to buy from
   the moneylenders' shops; NO ONE would cultivate their fields,
   or serve them as BARBERS, WASHERMEN or SHOEMAKERS.
   Spread through Poona, Ahmednagar, Sholapur, Satara.

   ⭐⭐⭐ ** ESCALATION: ** the social boycott became AGRARIAN
   RIOTS -- systematic attacks on moneylenders' houses and
   shops, with ** DEBT BONDS AND DEEDS SEIZED AND PUBLICLY
   BURNT. **

   ** REPRESSED. ** As a conciliatory measure: ⭐⭐ ** THE
   DECCAN AGRICULTURISTS RELIEF ACT, 1879. **
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE DEED-BURNING IS WORTH NOTICING AS A REPEATED METHOD, NOT A ONE-OFF.** *[File 03 §14](03_Resistance_Before_1857.md) records the SAME act — burning debt bonds and account books — in the Deccan riots of 1875, sourced from Cl.12 Th.9. ⭐⭐ **The document, not the grain or the cash, was the target both times: peasants who could not read the bond could still destroy it, and its destruction erased the debt as effectively as payment would have.***

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ How peasant movements changed after 1857 — Spectrum's own frame

⚠️ **This is the analytical passage worth memorising in full, because it is exactly the kind of "compare pre- and post-1857 resistance" question UPSC asks — and it gives you both halves ready-made.**

**📋 EIGHT FEATURES OF THE NEW PATTERN:**

1. **Peasants themselves became the main force**, fighting directly for their own demands
2. **Demands centred almost wholly on ECONOMIC issues**
3. **Directed against IMMEDIATE enemies** — foreign planters, indigenous zamindars, moneylenders
4. **Aimed at SPECIFIC, LIMITED objectives** — redress of particular grievances
5. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **COLONIALISM ITSELF WAS NOT THE TARGET**
6. **Not aimed at ending the SYSTEM of subordination or exploitation**
7. **Territorial reach limited**
8. **No continuity of struggle or long-term organisation** — but **a strong new awareness of LEGAL RIGHTS**, asserted in and outside the courts

**⚠️ THREE WEAKNESSES, following directly from the frame:**

1. **No adequate understanding of colonialism AS A SYSTEM**
2. **No new ideology, no new social/economic/political programme**
3. ⭐⭐ **However militant, the struggles occurred WITHIN THE FRAMEWORK OF THE OLD SOCIETAL ORDER, lacking a positive conception of an alternative society**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PUT POINTS 5 AND 8 TOGETHER AND YOU HAVE THE ANSWER TO "WERE THESE NATIONALIST MOVEMENTS?"** *They fought the LOCAL agent of exploitation — the planter, the zamindar, the moneylender — **not the colonial state that stood behind all three.** ⭐⭐ **That is structurally the same finding [file 03's §21](03_Resistance_Before_1857.md) reaches about the pre-1857 civil uprisings: resemblance across separate risings was produced by common CONDITIONS, not by a common national impulse.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The nineteenth-century peasant movements and the pre-1857 civil rebellions are, on this reading, the SAME PHENOMENON continued into a new century — legal method replacing the earlier armed one, but the horizon just as local.**

## 4️⃣ Twentieth-century movements before the mass phase ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 🏛️ 4.1 The UP Kisan Sabha, 1918 — NOT the Awadh Kisan Sabha of 1920

⚠️⚠️⚠️ **THE TRAP: your NCERT notes already give you the AWADH KISAN SABHA of October 1920 (Baba Ramchandra, Nehru's tour). Spectrum records an EARLIER, SEPARATE body, and confusing the two is a standard error.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** UP KISAN SABHA, FEBRUARY 1918 **
   ================================================================

   ** BACKGROUND: ** after 1857 the Awadh taluqdars had recovered
   their lands, strengthening their hold over the province's
   agrarian society. Cultivators faced ** HIGH RENTS, SUMMARY
   EVICTIONS ( "BEDAKHALI" ), ILLEGAL LEVIES, RENEWAL FEES
   ( "NAZRANA" ) ** -- worsened by WWI price inflation.

   ⭐ ** FOUNDED BY: ** ** GAURI SHANKAR MISHRA and INDRA
   NARAYAN DWIVEDI ** , with ** MADAN MOHAN MALAVIYA's **
   support -- driven mainly by ** HOME RULE activists. **

   ⭐⭐ ** GROWTH: ** by JUNE 1919, ** 450 BRANCHES. **
   Other leaders: ** Jhinguri Singh, Durgapal Singh, BABA
   RAMCHANDRA. **

   ⭐⭐⭐ ** JUNE 1920: ** Baba Ramchandra URGED NEHRU to visit
   these villages -- and Nehru developed close contacts.
      -> ** THIS IS WHERE NCERT'S STORY BEGINS. ** The
         separate ** AWADH KISAN SABHA ( OCTOBER 1920 ) **
         arose FROM differences within nationalist ranks --
         a distinct, later, more radical body.
```

⭐⭐⭐ **The Awadh Kisan Sabha's programme (already in your NCERT notes, but useful to have alongside the UP body for contrast):** *refuse to till *bedakhali* land, refuse *hari* and *begar*, boycott non-compliers, use panchayats for disputes.* ⚠️ **By January 1921 the pattern shifted rapidly** to *"looting of bazaars, houses, granaries and clashes with police"*, centred in **RAI BARELLY, FAIZABAD and SULTANPUR.** ⭐ **Declined from repression and the AWADH RENT (AMENDMENT) ACT.**

### 🤝 4.2 The Eka Movement, late 1921

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Northern UP — HARDOI, BAHRAICH, SITAPUR.** Three issues: **rents 50% above recorded rates**; **oppression by *thikadars*** (revenue-collection contractors); **the practice of share-rents.**

⭐⭐⭐ **The method was a symbolic RELIGIOUS RITUAL of collective vowing** — the assembled peasants swore to *(i)* **pay only the recorded rent, but ON TIME** · *(ii)* **not leave when evicted** · *(iii)* **refuse forced labour** · *(iv)* **give no help to criminals** · *(v)* **abide by panchayat decisions.**

⭐⭐ **Leadership: MADARI PASI and other LOW-CASTE leaders**, alongside **many small zamindars** — an unusual cross-class alliance. ⚠️ **Crushed by severe repression by March 1922.**

### ☪️ 4.3 The Mappila Revolt, 1921 — the economic story behind a "communal" label

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **You already have Mappila material in [file 03 §14](03_Resistance_Before_1857.md), on the ECONOMIC mechanism of land tenure. Spectrum here narrates the 1921 EVENT — how it began as agrarian, merged with Khilafat, and slid into communalism, step by step.**

```
   ** GRIEVANCES: ** lack of security of tenure, high rents,
   renewal fees, oppressive exactions -- landlords ( jenmies )
   were ** MOSTLY HINDU ** , tenants ** MOSTLY MUSLIM. **

   ⭐⭐ ** THE MERGER: ** the Congress's local demand for
   tenant-landlord legislation encouraged the Mappilas; the
   movement then ** MERGED WITH THE ONGOING KHILAFAT
   AGITATION. ** ** Gandhi, Shaukat Ali and Maulana Azad **
   addressed Mappila meetings.

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AUGUST 1921 -- THE TURN: ** the arrest of the
   respected priest-leader ** ALI MUSALIAR ** sparked large-
   scale riots. INITIAL TARGETS: symbols of British authority
   ( courts, police stations, treasuries ) AND unpopular Hindu
   ** JENMIES. **

   ⚠️⚠️⚠️ ** THE PIVOT: ** once the British declared MARTIAL
   LAW and repression intensified, ** MANY HINDUS were seen
   by the Mappilas as HELPING THE AUTHORITIES. ** An
   ANTI-GOVERNMENT, ANTI-LANDLORD affair ** "ACQUIRED
   COMMUNAL OVERTONES." ** ⭐⭐ ** The communalisation
   COMPLETED THE ISOLATION of the Mappilas from the wider
   Khilafat-Non-Cooperation Movement. **

   By December 1921, all resistance had ended.
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE MECHANISM IS THE POINT, AND IT IS THE ONE ANSWER THAT ACTUALLY EXPLAINS "WHY DID AN AGRARIAN REVOLT TURN COMMUNAL?"** *The shift did not come from an underlying religious hatred surfacing. **It came from a WARTIME PERCEPTION** — that Hindus were siding with the authorities under martial law — **which then RECODED an existing economic conflict (Muslim tenant vs Hindu landlord) in religious terms.** ⭐⭐ **State repression, not religious doctrine, is Spectrum's own explanation for the pivot — and it is a far more defensible Mains answer than treating the revolt as inherently communal from the start.***

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bardoli — the full course NCERT never gives

⚠️ **NCERT's own Prelims trap tells you Gandhi blessed rather than led Bardoli. Spectrum supplies everything else — and it is a genuinely detailed campaign, worth knowing beyond the headline fact.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** BARDOLI SATYAGRAHA, 1926-28 **
   ================================================================

   ⭐ ** THE TRIGGER, JANUARY 1926: ** authorities raised land
   revenue in Bardoli taluqa ( Surat district ) by ** 30 PER
   CENT. ** A ** BARDOLI INQUIRY COMMITTEE ** found the hike
   UNJUSTIFIED.

   ⭐⭐⭐ ** FEBRUARY 1926 -- VALLABHBHAI PATEL called to lead.
   ** The WOMEN OF BARDOLI gave him the title ** "SARDAR". **

   ⭐⭐ ** THE ORGANISATION: **
        - Peasants resolved to REFUSE payment of the revised
          assessment until an INDEPENDENT TRIBUNAL was
          appointed, or the OLD amount accepted as full
          payment.
        - ⭐ ** 13 "CHHAVANIS" ** ( workers' camps ) set up
          across the taluqa.
        - ** "BARDOLI SATYAGRAHA PATRIKA" ** published, to
          mobilise opinion.
        - ⭐ ** AN INTELLIGENCE WING ** to ensure compliance
          with the movement's resolutions.
        - Dissenters faced a ** SOCIAL BOYCOTT. **
        - ** SPECIAL EMPHASIS on mobilising WOMEN. **
        - ⭐ ** K.M. MUNSHI and LALJI NARANJI RESIGNED
          from the Bombay Legislative Council ** in support.

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** AUGUST 1928 -- CLIMAX: ** massive tension; the
   prospect of a Bombay RAILWAY STRIKE; ** GANDHI ARRIVED
   at Bardoli to stand by for any emergency. **

   ** THE SETTLEMENT: ** the government sought a graceful
   exit -- it set a condition ( first pay the enhanced rent -
   NOT actually done ) , then appointed a committee, which
   found the hike unjustified and recommended a rise of
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** ONLY 6.03 PER CENT ** ( against the original
   30% ) .
```

## 6️⃣ The 1930s: the All India Kisan Sabha and organised peasant politics ⭐⭐⭐⭐

⭐ **Context:** *the 1930s peasant awakening drew on the **Great Depression**'s effect on rural incomes, the **Civil Disobedience Movement**'s no-rent/no-revenue form in many areas, and — after the 1932 decline of active civil disobedience — **new political entrants looking for an outlet, who turned to organising peasants.***

**🏛️ THE ALL INDIA KISAN CONGRESS/SABHA (AIKS):**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Founded** | **Lucknow, APRIL 1936** |
| **President** | ⭐ **SWAMI SAHJANAND SARASWATI** |
| **General Secretary** | ⭐ **N.G. RANGA** |
| **Publication** | A **kisan manifesto**; a periodical under **INDULAL YAGNIK** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Political impact** | **AIKS and Congress held sessions together at FAIZPUR, 1936**; the **Congress's 1937 agrarian manifesto was strongly influenced by the AIKS agenda** |

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Tebhaga Movement, 1946-47

⚠️ **The direct sequel to [Cl.12 Th.9's](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md) *jotedars* and *bargadars* — the same figures, forty years further on, now organised.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** SEPTEMBER 1946 -- THE BENGAL PROVINCIAL KISAN SABHA'S
      CALL **
   ================================================================

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** THE DEMAND: ** implement, through mass struggle,
   the FLOOD COMMISSION's recommendation of ** TEBHAGA --
   TWO-THIRDS' SHARE ** -- to the ** BARGARDARS ** ( sharecroppers,
   also called BAGCHASI or ADHYAR ) , INSTEAD OF the customary
   ONE-HALF. Bargardars worked land rented from the
   ** JOTEDARS. **

   ⭐⭐ ** THE METHOD: ** communist cadres, including URBAN
   STUDENT MILITIAS, went to the countryside to organise the
   bargardars. Central slogan: ⭐⭐⭐ ** "NIJ KHAMARE DHAN
   TOLO" ** -- sharecroppers taking the paddy to their OWN
   THRESHING FLOOR, not to the jotedar's, to ENFORCE tebhaga
   by simple physical possession.

   ** THE CENTRE: ** north Bengal, principally among
   ** RAJBANSHIS ** -- a low caste of tribal origin. Muslims
   also participated in large numbers.

   ⚠️⚠️ ** WHY IT DISSIPATED -- FOUR CAUSES: **
        (i)  the LEAGUE MINISTRY's sop of the BARGARDARI BILL
        (ii) intensified repression
        (iii) the popularisation of the HINDU MAHASABHA's
              agitation for a SEPARATE BENGAL
        (iv) ⭐⭐ RENEWED RIOTS IN CALCUTTA, which ended any
              prospect of sympathetic support from the urban
              sections
```

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SLOGAN IS THE SINGLE BEST EXAM-READY FACT IN THIS FILE, BECAUSE IT ENCODES THE WHOLE STRATEGY IN FOUR WORDS.** *"Take the paddy to your OWN threshing floor" is not a demand addressed to a court or a government — **it is an instruction for immediate, physical enforcement**, bypassing the entire legal apparatus that the nineteenth-century movements (§2) had relied on. ⭐⭐ **Compare that directly with the Indigo Revolt's turn to "legal machinery": the movement had come full circle from litigation back to direct action, sixty years and one World War later.***

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Telangana Movement — from the peasants' side

⚠️ **[Spectrum file 04 §14](04_Integration_and_the_New_Nation.md) already covers Telangana from the STATE's side — the CPI, "ye azadi jhooti hai", the Ranadive line, the ban and the 1951 reversal. What follows is the SAME movement seen from inside the villages, and the two accounts complete each other.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** SCALE: ** the biggest peasant guerrilla war in modern
   Indian history -- ** 3,000 VILLAGES, 3 MILLION PEOPLE. **
   ================================================================

   ⭐⭐ ** THE STRUCTURE OF DOMINATION: ** Hyderabad state under
   the Asaf Jahi Nizams combined a ** SMALL URDU-SPEAKING
   MUSLIM ELITE ** ruling over ** predominantly Hindu, Telugu/
   Marathi/Kannada-speaking populations ** ; TOTAL lack of
   political and civil liberties; and grossest exploitation by
   ⭐ ** DESHMUKHS, JAGIRDARS and DORAS (landlords) ** through
   ** FORCED LABOUR ("VETHI") and ILLEGAL EXACTIONS. **

   ⭐ ** WARTIME ROOTS: ** communist-led guerrillas had already
   built a base through the ** ANDHRA MAHASABHA ** , organising
   around wartime exactions, ration abuse, excessive rent, and
   vethi.

   ⭐⭐⭐ ** JULY 1946: ** a deshmukh's thug MURDERED a village
   militant in JANGAON taluq, NALGONDA. Spread to WARANGAL
   and KHAMMAM.

   ⭐⭐ ** METHOD: ** peasants organised into ** VILLAGE
   SANGHAMS ** , fighting with LATHIS, STONE SLINGS and CHILLI
   POWDER against brutal repression. Peak intensity:
   ** AUGUST 1947 - SEPTEMBER 1948. ** The peasants routed the
   ** RAZAKARS ** ( Nizam's stormtroopers ) ; the movement
   fizzled out once Indian security forces took over Hyderabad.
```

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SEVEN POSITIVE ACHIEVEMENTS — a clean, high-value list:**

1. **In guerrilla-controlled villages, *vethi* and forced labour DISAPPEARED**
2. **Agricultural wages were RAISED**
3. **Illegally seized lands were RESTORED**
4. **Steps taken toward LAND CEILINGS and redistribution**
5. **Irrigation improved; cholera combated**
6. **An improvement in the condition of WOMEN**
7. ⭐⭐⭐ **The autocratic-feudal regime of India's BIGGEST princely state was shaken, clearing the way for ANDHRA PRADESH ON LINGUISTIC LINES — realising another aim of the national movement**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **CONNECT ACHIEVEMENT 7 STRAIGHT TO [FILE 04](04_Integration_and_the_New_Nation.md), AND YOU HAVE A COMPLETE ARGUMENT ABOUT POST-1947 INDIA THAT MOST CANDIDATES NEVER MAKE.** *A COMMUNIST INSURGENCY that the state suppressed by force **nonetheless produced the political conditions for a LINGUISTIC STATE the Congress government later created.** ⭐⭐ **Repression and reform are not opposites here — the crushing of Telangana's armed wing and the eventual creation of Andhra Pradesh are two acts in the SAME process:** the old feudal order was broken either way, and only the METHOD of who did the breaking, and on whose terms, was contested.*

# 🏭 PART B — THE WORKING-CLASS MOVEMENT

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why the labour movement was always political

⚠️ **This entire Part is a total gap in your notes. Nothing anywhere in your NCERT History archive covers the Indian labour movement.**

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE FRAMING SENTENCE:** *the Indian working class faced **"two basic antagonistic forces — an imperialist political rule and economic exploitation at the hands of BOTH foreign and native capitalist classes."*** ⭐⭐ **Under those circumstances the labour movement became, inevitably, intertwined with the political struggle for national emancipation** — it could never simply be a wages-and-hours movement the way it could be in an already-independent country.

**⏳ THE EARLY EFFORTS, 1870-1908 — isolated, philanthropic, pre-organisational:**

| Year | Who | What |
|:--:|---|---|
| **1870** | ⭐ **SASIPADA BANERJEA** | Started a **workingmen's club** and the newspaper ***Bharat Shramjeevi*** |
| **1878** | **SORABJEE SHAPOORJI BENGALEE** | Tried, unsuccessfully, to pass a **labour-conditions bill** in the Bombay Legislative Council |
| **1880** | ⭐ **NARAIN MEGHAJEE LOKHANDAY** | Newspaper ***Deenbandhu***; set up the **BOMBAY MILL AND MILLHANDS ASSOCIATION** |
| ⭐⭐ **1899** | *(collective)* | **First strike by the GREAT INDIAN PENINSULAR RAILWAYS** — got wide support; **TILAK's *Kesari* and *Maharatta*** had campaigned for it for months |

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WHY THE MODERATE NATIONALISTS DID NOT SUPPORT LABOUR — Spectrum gives four honest reasons:**

1. **Indifference to labour's cause**
2. **A distinction between labour in INDIAN-owned factories and BRITISH-owned ones**
3. **A belief that labour legislation would erode the COMPETITIVE EDGE of Indian-owned industry**
4. **No wish to divide the movement along CLASS lines**

⭐ **Consequently, the Moderates OPPOSED the Factory Acts of 1881 and 1891.** *(⭐ Notable individual exceptions: Bipin Chandra Pal and G. Subramanya Aiyar pressed for better conditions.)*

**🚩 DURING SWADESHI (1905-08):** *workers took part in wider political strikes, organised by **Ashwini Coomar Banerjea, Prabhat Kumar Roy Chaudhuri, Premtosh Bose, Apurba Kumar Ghosh**, in **government press, railways and jute**. ⭐ **SUBRAMANIYA SIVA and CHIDAMBARAM PILLAI led strikes in TUTICORIN and TIRUNELVELI and were arrested.** The biggest strike of the period followed **Tilak's own arrest and trial.***

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The AITUC, and Gandhi at Ahmedabad

**⚙️ WHY THE MOMENT ARRIVED — three converging factors:**

1. **WWI and after: soaring exports and prices, massive profiteering for industrialists, LOW WAGES for workers** — worker discontent
2. **Gandhi's arrival broadened the national movement, emphasising mobilisation of workers AND peasants**
3. ⭐ **International events: the Soviet republic, the Comintern, and the founding of the ILO** — all lent the Indian movement a new frame of reference

**🏛️ THE AITUC — a clean founding, and the single most important date in this Part:**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **31 OCTOBER 1920** |
| **First president** | **LALA LAJPAT RAI** *(that year's Congress president)* |
| **First general secretary** | **DEWAN CHAMAN LAL** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Lajpat Rai's formula** | *"**Imperialism and militarism are the TWIN CHILDREN OF CAPITALISM.**"* — **the first to link capitalism with imperialism explicitly** |
| **Presided later by** | ⭐ **C.R. DAS chaired the THIRD and FOURTH sessions** |
| **Congress endorsement** | The **GAYA session, 1922, welcomed the AITUC** and formed a committee to assist it; **C.R. Das** argued Congress must take up workers' and peasants' causes **or risk their isolation from the movement** |
| **Close contacts** | **Nehru, Subhas Bose, C.F. Andrews, J.M. Sengupta, Satyamurthy, V.V. Giri, Sarojini Naidu** |
| **Early ideology** | Influenced initially by **British Labour Party social-democratic ideas**, and by **Gandhian trusteeship and class-collaboration philosophy** |

**👔 GANDHI AT AHMEDABAD, 1918:** *he helped organise the **AHMEDABAD TEXTILE LABOUR ASSOCIATION**, and through a protest **secured a 27.5% WAGE HIKE — later raised to 35% by arbitrator's award.***

## 1️⃣2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The two Acts, and Meerut

**⚖️ THE TRADE UNION ACT, 1926 — three provisions, and a clean Prelims item:**

1. **Recognised trade unions as LEGAL associations**
2. **Laid down conditions for REGISTRATION and regulation**
3. ⭐ **Secured CIVIL AND CRIMINAL IMMUNITY for legitimate union activity — but placed RESTRICTIONS on POLITICAL activity**

**🔥 LATE 1920s — militancy under communist influence:** *a **six-month Bombay Textile Mills strike, 1928**, led by the **GIRNI KAMGAR UNION**; the whole of 1928 saw **"unprecedented industrial unrest"**, and communist groups crystallised — **S.A. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmed, P.C. Joshi, Sohan Singh Josh.***

**⚖️ THE TRADE DISPUTES ACT (TDA), 1929 — the government's answer, and three restrictions:**

1. **Compulsory Courts of Inquiry and Consultation Boards** for settling disputes
2. ⭐⭐ **Strikes in public utilities (posts, railways, water, electricity) made ILLEGAL unless every individual striking worker gave ONE MONTH'S ADVANCE NOTICE**
3. **Forbade COERCIVE or purely POLITICAL trade union activity — and even SYMPATHETIC strikes**

*(Alongside it, the **Public Safety Ordinance, 1929** — the same measure the HSRA's Assembly bomb in [file 06 §10](06_Revolutionaries_and_Swarajists.md) protested.)*

**⚖️ THE MEERUT CONSPIRACY CASE, MARCH 1929:**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **31 labour leaders arrested; a trial lasting THREE AND A HALF YEARS convicted MUZAFFAR AHMED, S.A. DANGE, JOGLEKAR, PHILIP SPRATT, BEN BRADLEY, SHAUKAT USMANI and others.** ⭐ **The trial got worldwide publicity — but WEAKENED the working-class movement**, which is the opposite of the effect the Lahore Conspiracy Case trial had for the HSRA in [file 06](06_Revolutionaries_and_Swarajists.md).

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOTE THE CONTRAST WITH BHAGAT SINGH'S TRIAL, BECAUSE IT IS A GENUINE PUZZLE WORTH RAISING IN AN ANSWER.** *Both were show trials the government used against radicals; both got wide publicity. ⭐⭐ **The Lahore case made Bhagat Singh "a household name" and strengthened the HSRA's cause; the Meerut case WEAKENED the labour movement.*** ⚠️ **The difference is plausibly one of AUDIENCE: Bhagat Singh's trial was staged for an Indian public already primed by mass nationalism to receive martyrdom; the Meerut defendants were communists whose politics had less of that pre-built sympathy, and the three-and-a-half-year length itself drained momentum rather than building it.**

**📉 THE 1931 SPLIT:** *a **corporatist trend under N.M. JOSHI** broke away to form the **All India Trade Union Federation**. **The communists rejoined the AITUC in 1935**, forming a left front with Congress socialists and leftist nationalists (Nehru, Bose).*

---

## 1️⃣3️⃣ Under Congress rule, the war, and after ⭐⭐⭐

**🏛️ UNDER CONGRESS MINISTRIES (1937-39):** *the AITUC **supported Congress candidates** in 1937; provincial Congress governments were **generally sympathetic to workers' demands**, and passed favourable legislation.*

**⚔️ DURING WWII:** *workers **initially opposed** the war; after 1941, when the **USSR joined the Allies**, the communists called it a **"PEOPLE'S WAR"** and supported it — **dissociating from Quit India** and advocating **industrial peace**.*

**🕊️ 1945-47 — the post-war upsurge:** *1945: **Bombay and Calcutta dockworkers refused to load ships supplying troops in Indonesia**. 1946: **workers struck in support of the Naval Ratings**. **In the last year of foreign rule**, strikes hit **posts, railways and many other establishments.***

**🔴 AFTER INDEPENDENCE:** *the working-class movement **"got polarised on the basis of political ideologies"*** — a single sentence, but it is the seed of every subsequent trade-union split (INTUC, AITUC, and later CITU, BMS) that structures Indian labour politics down to the present.

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE INDIGO REVOLT (1859-60) — Digambar and Bishnu Biswas, a government win, and the planters simply LEAVING, taking the industry with them**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PABNA (legal, rent strikes → the Bengal Tenancy Act 1885) and the DECCAN RIOTS (moneylenders, deed-burning → the Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act 1879) — same era, opposite methods, both legislated**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **SPECTRUM'S EIGHT-POINT FRAME: post-1857 peasant movements fought local exploiters for limited ends — colonialism itself was never the target**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE UP KISAN SABHA (1918) IS NOT THE AWADH KISAN SABHA (1920) — the first predates and feeds into the second**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE MAPPILA REVOLT TURNED COMMUNAL THROUGH MARTIAL LAW, NOT THROUGH DOCTRINE — an economic conflict recoded once Hindus were perceived aiding the state**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BARDOLI'S FULL COURSE: 30% hike → 13 chhavanis, an intelligence wing, a social boycott → 6.03% final settlement**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TEBHAGA'S SLOGAN — "take the paddy to your own threshing floor" — replaced litigation with direct physical enforcement**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TELANGANA'S SEVEN ACHIEVEMENTS cleared the ground for Andhra Pradesh on linguistic lines — repression and reform as one process**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LABOUR MOVEMENT WAS POLITICAL BY NECESSITY — facing BOTH imperial rule AND native capital, it could never be wages-only**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AITUC (31 Oct 1920, Lajpat Rai/Chaman Lal) → the Trade Union Act 1926 → the Trade Disputes Act 1929 → the Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929, WEAKENED the movement, unlike Lahore)**
