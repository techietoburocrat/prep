# 🏛️ PART A — SIMON COMMISSION AND THE NEHRU REPORT

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Simon Commission — appointment, response, report

```
   ================================================================
   ** WHY THE COMMISSION, AND WHY THE ANGER **
   ================================================================

   The GoI Act 1919 provided for a review commission ** TEN
   YEARS ** from the Act. ⚠️ ** THE INDIAN STATUTORY COMMISSION
   ("SIMON COMMISSION"), ALL-WHITE, SEVEN MEMBERS ** , chaired
   by ** SIR JOHN SIMON ** , was appointed by ⭐ ** STANLEY
   BALDWIN's Conservative government on 8 NOVEMBER 1927 ** --
   ** TWO YEARS EARLY. **

   ⭐⭐ ** WHY EARLY: ** the Conservatives FEARED DEFEAT by
   Labour and did not want India's future left in ** "irresponsible
   Labour hands." ** ⭐ Also: the LEE COMMISSION (recruitment
   failure), the MUDIMAN COMMISSION (diarchy deadlock) and the
   LINLITHGOW COMMISSION (agrarian crisis) had all flagged
   problems with the 1919 Act. ⭐⭐ ** LORD BIRKENHEAD **, the
   Secretary of State, had repeatedly TAUNTED Indians for their
   inability to agree on a constitutional scheme -- and it was he
   who appointed the Commission.

   ⚠️⚠️⚠️ ** WHAT ANGERED INDIANS: NOT THE COMMISSION'S
   PURPOSE BUT ITS COMPOSITION ** -- the EXCLUSION of Indians,
   and the underlying idea that FOREIGNERS would judge India's
   fitness for self-government. Seen as a violation of
   self-determination and ⭐ ** "a deliberate insult to the
   self-respect of Indians." **
```

**⚔️ INDIAN RESPONSE, BY GROUP:**

| Group | Position |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **CONGRESS** *(Madras, Dec 1927, under M.A. ANSARI)* | **Boycott "at every stage and every form."** ⭐⭐ **NEHRU also got a snap resolution passed declaring COMPLETE INDEPENDENCE as the Congress goal** |
| **HINDU MAHASABHA liberals** | Supported the boycott |
| ⭐⭐ **MUSLIM LEAGUE — SPLIT** | **JINNAH's Calcutta session opposed the Commission**; a **rival session at LAHORE under MUHAMMAD SHAFI supported the government** |
| **UNIONISTS (Punjab) and JUSTICE PARTY (south)** | **Did NOT boycott** |
| **Public** | **Landed BOMBAY, 3 FEBRUARY 1928** — countrywide hartal; **black flags and "SIMON GO BACK" wherever it went** |

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Ambedkar and the Simon Commission — a total gap, worth its own space

> ⚠️ **Appointed by the BOMBAY LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL to work with the Commission; testified OCTOBER 1928.**

- Argued for ⭐⭐ **UNIVERSAL ADULT FRANCHISE for BOTH sexes** — provincial autonomy, dyarchy at the Centre. *(⭐ Spectrum notes: **most European countries did not yet guarantee this either.**)*
- On behalf of the **BAHISHKRITA HITAKARINI SABHA**, submitted a memorandum on the **rights and safeguards required for the depressed classes**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Argued there was NO LINK between the depressed classes and the Hindu community** — they should be treated as a **distinct, independent minority**
- Said the depressed classes needed **GREATER political protection than any other minority**, given **educational backwardness, economic poverty, social enslavement**, and grave political disabilities **unique to them**
- **Demanded representation on the SAME BASIS as the Muslim minority** — **reserved seats** if universal franchise came; **a SEPARATE ELECTORATE** if it did not
- Wanted safeguards either constitutionally, or via **"advice in the instrument to the governor"** regarding depressed-class education and public service entry

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **THE OUTCOME:** *the Simon Report **DID grant reserved seats**, but conditioned candidacy on the **governor first endorsing the candidate's "competence"** — Ambedkar was **displeased**, and in any case **the report remained a dead letter.***

**📋 THE SIMON REPORT'S OWN RECOMMENDATIONS (published May 1930, two-volume):**

*Abolish dyarchy → representative provincial government with autonomy · governor discretionary power over internal security · reject responsible central government — governor-general appoints the cabinet fully · retain and EXTEND communal electorates until Hindu-Muslim tension eased · federalism accepted "not in the near future" · a Consultative Council of Greater India · NWFP and Baluchistan to get local legislatures · Sindh separated from Bombay, Burma separated from India · Indianise the army but retain British forces.* ⚠️ **By the time it appeared, events had already overtaken it.**

**⚡ POLICE REPRESSION** — *lathicharges spared no one: **Nehru and G.B. Pant beaten at Lucknow**; ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Lala Lajpat Rai took severe chest blows in October 1928 and died 17 November 1928***, saying beforehand: ⭐ ***"The blows which fell on me today are the last nails driven into the coffin of British Imperialism."***

## 2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Nehru Report and Jinnah's Fourteen Points

⚠️ **The first major Indian attempt to draft its own constitution — and its collapse is the single clearest illustration of the communal deadlock's mechanics.**

**🏛️ THE COMMITTEE:** an **All Parties Conference (Feb 1928)** appointed a sub-committee under **MOTILAL NEHRU** — members **Tej Bahadur Sapru, Subhas Bose, M.S. Aney, Mangal Singh, Ali Imam, Shuab Qureshi, G.R. Pradhan**. **Finalised by August 1928.** ⚠️ **Unanimous except on ONE point** — the majority wanted **"dominion status"** as basis; a section (including the younger militants) wanted **"complete independence"**, with the majority granting them liberty of action.

**📜 MAIN RECOMMENDATIONS:**

1. **Dominion status** *(to the disgust of Nehru and the younger section)*
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **REJECTION of separate electorates** — **joint electorates with SEATS RESERVED for Muslims** where they were a **minority**, in **proportion to population**, with right to contest additional seats — **NOT** in Punjab/Bengal, where Muslims were already a majority
3. **Linguistic provinces**
4. **Nineteen fundamental rights** — including **equal rights for women, right to form unions, universal adult suffrage**
5. **Responsible government** at Centre and provinces — a **500-member House of Representatives** (adult suffrage) and a **200-member Senate** (elected by provincial councils)
6. **Full protection of Muslim cultural/religious interests**
7. ⭐⭐ **Complete dissociation of State from religion**

**⚔️ THE COMMUNAL RESPONSES:**

| Party | Position |
|---|---|
| **Delhi Proposals (Dec 1927, accepted by Congress at Madras)** | **Joint electorates with reserved Muslim seats · ONE-THIRD Muslim representation at the Centre · proportional representation in Punjab/Bengal · THREE new Muslim-majority provinces (Sindh, Baluchistan, NWFP)** |
| ⭐ **Hindu Mahasabha** | **Vehemently opposed** new Muslim-majority provinces and Punjab/Bengal seat reservation; demanded a **strictly unitary structure** |
| **Nehru Report's concessions to Hindu opinion** | **Joint electorates everywhere but reservation only where Muslims were a minority · Sindh's separation from Bombay conditional on dominion status AND weightage for the Hindu minority there · a broadly UNITARY structure, residual powers at Centre** |

**⚔️ JINNAH'S THREE AMENDMENTS at Calcutta (Dec 1928)** — *rejected*:
1. **One-third Muslim representation at the Centre**
2. **Proportional reservation in Bengal/Punjab till adult suffrage**
3. **Residual powers to the PROVINCES**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Jinnah's Fourteen Points, March 1929 — the basis of ALL future League propaganda

1. **Federal constitution, residual powers to provinces**
2. **Provincial autonomy**
3. **No constitutional amendment by the Centre without state concurrence**
4. **Adequate Muslim representation everywhere, without reducing existing Muslim majorities**
5. **Adequate Muslim representation in services**
6. **One-third Muslim representation at the Centre**
7. **One-third Muslims in any cabinet, Centre or province**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Separate electorates**
9. **No bill passable if three-fourths of an affected minority object**
10. **No territorial change to affect Muslim majorities in Punjab, Bengal, NWFP**
11. **Separation of Sindh from Bombay**
12. **Constitutional reforms in NWFP and Baluchistan**
13. **Full religious freedom for all**
14. **Protection of Muslim rights — religion, culture, education, language**

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **READ POINTS 8 AND 3-4 TOGETHER, BECAUSE THEY MARK THE MOMENT THE LEAGUE ABANDONED COMPROMISE.** *The Nehru Report offered joint electorates with weighted representation; **Jinnah's Point 8 flatly demands separate electorates back.** ⭐⭐ **The gap between the two documents — one drafted with Muslim participation, the other reacting against it within months — is the clearest textual evidence of how quickly cooperative constitution-making broke down after 1928.** ⚠️ **The younger Congress also rejected the Report — for the OPPOSITE reason (dominion status, not communalism) — so it satisfied literally no significant faction: Nehru/Bose formed the INDEPENDENCE FOR INDIA LEAGUE in protest.***

# ⚖️ PART B — STRATEGY, THE 1935 ACT, AND CONGRESS RULE

## 3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The S-T-S vs S-V debate

⚠️ **After Civil Disobedience was suspended (1934), the Congress split three ways on what to do next — and the argument is a genuine political-philosophy debate worth reproducing.**

| Position | Who | Argument |
|---|---|---|
| **Council entry** | **M.A. Ansari, Asaf Ali, Bhulabhai Desai, Satyamurthy, B.C. Roy** | Council work would **keep up morale in a period of apathy**, **build a second front**, and give Congress **prestige** — without implying faith in constitutional politics |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Nehru's S-V line** | **Jawaharlal Nehru** | Called withdrawal + council entry **"a spiritual defeat," "a surrender of ideals," "a retreat from revolutionary to reformist mentality."** ⭐ **"The basic goal before the Indian people… is abolition of capitalism and establishment of socialism."** Wanted **class organisations (kisan sabhas, trade unions) AFFILIATED to Congress** — *"no genuine anti-imperialist struggle without incorporating the class struggle of the masses."* ⭐⭐ **Argued for STRUGGLE-VICTORY (S-V) — continuous confrontation, no truce phase — because "real power cannot be won by two annas and four annas"** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Gandhi's S-T-S line** | Gandhi and most Congressmen | **Struggle-Truce-Struggle** — mass struggle, then a **reprieve phase to let both masses AND government recoup/respond**, then struggle again if government did not respond |

**🤝 THE RESOLUTION:** *Gandhi **conciliated** the council-entry faction by permitting it — *"parliamentary politics cannot lead to freedom, but those… who could not offer satyagraha… should not remain unoccupied."*** **May 1934, AICC at Patna — a Parliamentary Board set up.** ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **October 1934 — GANDHI RESIGNED FROM THE CONGRESS**, to serve it *"in thought, word and deed"* rather than as a member — squeezed between parliamentarians he disagreed with and socialists led by Nehru. *(⭐ November 1934 elections: Congress won **45 of 75 reserved Indian seats** in the Central Legislative Assembly.)*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Government of India Act, 1935 — full architecture

⚠️ **The single most important Act in this file, and the direct constitutional ancestor of 1950. Passed August 1935, following the (Congress-boycotted) Third Round Table Conference.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** 1. THE ALL-INDIA FEDERATION -- AND WHY IT NEVER STARTED **
   ================================================================
   To include ALL British Indian provinces + chief commissioners'
   provinces + THE PRINCELY STATES.

   ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** CONDITIONAL on: (i) states allotted 52 Council
   of States seats agreeing to join; (ii) their AGGREGATE
   POPULATION being AT LEAST 50% of all princely-state
   population. **

   -> ** NEITHER CONDITION WAS MET. ** The federation NEVER
      CAME UP. The central government ran on the ⭐ 1919 ACT
      UNTIL 1946.

   ================================================================
   ** 2. FEDERAL EXECUTIVE **
   ================================================================
   Governor-general the PIVOT. Reserved subjects (foreign
   affairs, defence, tribal areas, ecclesiastical) administered
   ON HIS OWN, via executive councillors NOT responsible to
   the legislature. Transferred subjects via MINISTERS
   responsible to the legislature.
   ⭐ Governor-general retained INDIVIDUAL JUDGEMENT over
      "special responsibilities" for security.

   ================================================================
   ** 3. FEDERAL LEGISLATURE **
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐ ** Bicameral. ** Council of States: 260 members, PARTLY
   directly elected, 40% NOMINATED by princes -- PERMANENT
   body, one-third retiring every 3 years. Federal Assembly:
   375 members, PARTLY INDIRECTLY elected, ONE-THIRD
   nominated by princes -- 5-year term.
   ⚠️ ** ODDLY: election to the UPPER house was DIRECT; to
      the LOWER house, INDIRECT. **
   Federal Assembly could no-confidence ministers; Council of
   States COULD NOT.
   ⭐ 80% of the budget was NON-VOTABLE.
   Governor-general retained residuary powers: restore cuts,
   certify rejected bills, ordinances, veto.

   ================================================================
   ** 4. PROVINCIAL AUTONOMY -- REPLACING DYARCHY **
   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Provinces given AUTONOMY and a SEPARATE LEGAL
   IDENTITY, freed from Secretary of State/Governor-General
   superintendence -- deriving authority DIRECTLY from the
   Crown. Independent financial powers; could borrow on own
   security.
   Governor = Crown's nominee, with special powers over
   minorities, civil servants' rights, law and order, British
   business, excluded areas, princely states -- and could TAKE
   OVER administration INDEFINITELY.
   ⭐ Separate electorates per the COMMUNAL AWARD made
      operational. Women got the franchise on the SAME BASIS
      as men. Ministers answerable to, removable by, the
      legislature. ⚠️ 40% of the provincial budget non-votable.
```

**⚖️ EVALUATION — five points:**

1. **"Safeguards" and "special responsibilities" of the governor-general were BRAKES on proper functioning**
2. **Governors retained extensive provincial powers**
3. ⭐⭐ **Enfranchised only 14% of the British Indian population**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Extended communal electorates PROMOTED SEPARATIST TENDENCIES which culminated in Partition**
5. **A rigid constitution — amendment reserved to the BRITISH PARLIAMENT**

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE LONG-TERM BRITISH STRATEGY, stated with unusual candour by Spectrum — six moves worth knowing as a set:**

*Revive constitutionalist moderates who had lost ground during CDM · convince Congressmen of the futility of extra-legal struggle by combining earlier repression with present reform · make officeholders reluctant to return to sacrifice · use reform to create Congress dissension — placate the right, crush the radical left by police measures · let provincial autonomy breed autonomous PROVINCIAL power centres, PROVINCIALISING Congress and weakening its central leadership.*

⭐ **Quoted verdicts:** **Nehru** — *"we are provided with a car, all brakes and no engine."* **B.R. Tomlinson** — *"the process of constitutional advance in India is determined by the need to attract Indian collaborators to the Raj."*

⚠️⭐⭐ **NATIONALIST RESPONSE:** *the Congress **rejected it root and branch**, demanding instead a **Constituent Assembly on adult franchise**. ⭐ **Hindu Mahasabha and the National Liberal Federation supported working the Act.***

## 5️⃣ The second stage debate and the 1937 elections ⭐⭐⭐⭐

⚠️ **Everyone agreed to oppose the Act; the question was whether Congress should ACCEPT OFFICE if it won provincial majorities.**

| Position | Argument |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **Left (Nehru, Bose, socialists, communists)** | **Office acceptance would negate the Act's rejection** — *"assuming responsibility without power"* — and sidetrack revolutionary goals. **Counter-strategy: enter councils to CREATE DEADLOCKS** *(the Swarajist model again)*, with a long-term reliance on integrating peasant/worker class organisations |
| **Office-acceptance faction** | Combating the Act was compatible with **short-term tactical office-holding**, since mass struggle was not currently possible; **leaving administration to pro-government forces would be worse** |

**🗳️ THE OUTCOME:** *Gandhi, initially opposed, agreed by early 1936. **Lucknow (1936) and Faizpur (1937)** — contest elections, postpone the office decision. Elections held **February 1937 in ELEVEN PROVINCES**. ⭐ **An estimated 30.1 million enfranchised (14% of the population, including 4.25 million women); 15.5 million voted.*** **Congress won 716 of 1,161 contested seats** — a majority everywhere **except Bengal, Assam, Punjab, Sindh, NWFP** *(largest party in the first three of those).*

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Congress rule in the provinces, 1937-39 — the twenty-eight months, in full

⚠️ **You already know Gandhi "blessed but did not lead" this period; Spectrum gives the actual RECORD of the ministries.**

⭐⭐ **Gandhi's advice:** hold office **"lightly, not tightly"** — as **"crowns of thorns"**, to be used in ways *"not expected or intended by the British"*, and to prove Congress could govern with **minimum reliance on police and army.**

**✅ CIVIL LIBERTIES:** *emergency-powers laws repealed · bans lifted on the Hindustan Seva Dal, Youth Leagues, banned books/journals · press restrictions and blacklists lifted · confiscated arms/licences restored · CID stopped shadowing politicians · political prisoners released, deportation/internment revoked · confiscated Bombay lands and CDM-era pensions restored.* ⚠️ **Blemishes: socialists Yusuf Maherally and S.S. Batliwala arrested in Madras; K.M. Munshi, Bombay's home minister, used the CID against communists.**

**🌾 AGRARIAN REFORM — SIX CONSTRAINTS explaining why zamindari was NOT abolished then:**

1. **Inadequate powers**
2. **Inadequate finances — the Government of India took the lion's share**
3. ⭐⭐ **A strategy of CLASS ADJUSTMENT — zamindars had to be conciliated, not confronted**
4. **Time — Congress politics was built for confrontation, not administration**
5. ⭐⭐ **War clouds from 1938**
6. **The REACTIONARY Legislative Councils (dominated by landlords/moneylenders in UP, Bihar, Bombay, Madras, Assam) had to be conciliated for legislation to pass**

⚠️ **Result: land-reform laws WERE passed (debt relief, forest grazing fees, rent arrears, tenures), but benefits went mostly to STATUTORY/OCCUPANCY tenants — sub-tenants and agricultural labourers, unmobilised, gained little.**

**🏭 LABOUR:** *the approach — **advance workers' interests while promoting "industrial peace"** — reduce strikes, push compulsory arbitration, mediate between capital and labour. ⚠️ **Militant protests were treated as LAW-AND-ORDER problems, and Section 144 arrests followed** — largely successful, **except in Bombay**. **Nehru was privately unhappy but publicly defended the ministries.***

**🎓 SOCIAL WELFARE:** *prohibition in some areas · Harijan welfare (temple entry, public facilities, scholarships, government/police recruitment) · attention to education, public health, sanitation · khadi subsidies · prison reform · indigenous enterprise support · ⭐⭐ **the NATIONAL PLANNING COMMITTEE, set up under Congress president SUBHAS BOSE, 1938.***

**📋 EXTRA-PARLIAMENTARY WORK:** *mass literacy campaigns · Congress police stations and panchayats · Congress Grievance Committees · support for the STATES' PEOPLES' MOVEMENTS.*

**⚖️ EVALUATION — seven points:**

1. **Confirmed that self-government was necessary for radical social transformation**
2. **Proved state power could be used without co-option**
3. **Ministries CONTROLLED communal riots**
4. **The bureaucracy's morale FELL**
5. **Neutralised many previously hostile elements (landlords, etc.)**
6. **Made a future independent India tangible**
7. **Weakened the myth that Indians could not administer**

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **AND TWO COMPLICATIONS SPECTRUM ADMITS HONESTLY:** *increased industrial unrest amid a Congress-capitalist rapprochement produced **the anti-labour BOMBAY TRADERS DISPUTES ACT, 1938**; and the **MUSLIM LEAGUE's PIRPUR COMMITTEE (1938)** compiled a report alleging Congress ministries interfered in religious rites, suppressed Urdu for Hindi, denied representation, and economically oppressed Muslims.* ⭐ **The ministries resigned in OCTOBER 1939, over the war declaration.**

# ⚔️ PART C — THE BOSE CRISIS, THE AUGUST OFFER, INDIVIDUAL SATYAGRAHA

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Haripura, Tripuri, and the Forward Bloc — a total gap

⚠️ **This entire sequence is absent from your NCERT notes and is a standard source of Prelims and Mains questions on the Congress's internal politics before the war.**

```
   ================================================================
   ** FEBRUARY 1938 -- HARIPURA (Gujarat) **
   ================================================================
   ⭐ ** SUBHAS BOSE elected president UNANIMOUSLY. ** Argued
   the provincial ministries had ⭐ ** "IMMENSE REVOLUTIONARY
   POTENTIAL." ** Instrumental in founding the ** NATIONAL
   PLANNING COMMITTEE. ** The session pledged MORAL SUPPORT
   to agitators in the princely states.

   ================================================================
   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** JANUARY 1939 -- BOSE STANDS AGAIN, GANDHI
      OBJECTS **
   ================================================================
   Bose said he represented ** "new ideas… from the
   progressive sharpening of the anti-imperialist struggle." **
   ⭐ ** Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Kripalani and others backed
   PATTABHI SITARAMAYYA instead. **

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** BOSE WON, 1580 v. 1377 ** -- with FULL SUPPORT
   of the Congress Socialist Party and the communists. Gandhi:
   ⚠️ ** "Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat." ** A Gandhi-versus-
   Bose contest was now open.

   ================================================================
   ** MARCH 1939 -- TRIPURI (Central Provinces) **
   ================================================================
   The Working Committee is NOMINATED by the president, not
   elected -- so the presidential poll had just handed BOSE
   the right to remake it. ⭐ ** WC members RESIGNED en masse
   ** rather than serve under him.

   Bose, ILL but attending, PROPHESIED an imperialist war and
   proposed ⭐⭐ ** a SIX-MONTH ULTIMATUM to Britain for
   independence -- and if rejected, MASS CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE. **

   ⭐⭐ ** GOVIND BALLABH PANT's resolution ** -- reaffirming
   Gandhian policy and asking Bose to nominate the WC ** "in
   accordance with the wishes of Gandhiji" ** -- PASSED
   without Left opposition. Gandhi then said he would NOT
   impose a working committee, and left the choice to Bose --
   who REFUSED to nominate one.

   ⚠️⚠️ ** THE STANDOFF: ** Bose wanted an immediate Gandhi-
   led struggle; Gandhi held the time was not ripe.
   ⭐ ** GANDHI: "The views you express seem to be so
   diametrically opposed to those of the others and my own
   that I do not see any possibility of bridging them." **

   -> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** APRIL 1939 -- BOSE RESIGNED. ** Even the
   socialists/communists preferred a united, Gandhi-led
   Congress over a split that would leave the Left a splinter
   group. RAJENDRA PRASAD elected president.

   ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ** MAY 1939 -- THE FORWARD BLOC formed
   ** (at Makur, Unnao) ** as a new party WITHIN Congress. **
   After Bose called an all-India protest ( 9 July ) against an
   AICC resolution, the CWC took DISCIPLINARY ACTION:
   ** August 1939 -- Bose REMOVED as Bengal PCC president
   and BARRED FROM ELECTIVE OFFICE for THREE YEARS. **
```

⭐ **A curiosity worth one line — at Tripuri, Congress resolved to send a MEDICAL MISSION to China, in solidarity against Japanese aggression.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Gandhi and Bose — the ideological differences, condensed

⚠️ **Despite the break, both held deep mutual respect** — Gandhi called Bose *"Prince among the Patriots"*; Bose called Gandhi *"The Father of Our Nation"* in a 1944 Rangoon broadcast, even while advocating force.

| Axis | Gandhi | Bose |
|---|---|---|
| **Method** | ***Ahimsa* and satyagraha** — an unarmed mass had little chance in armed rebellion; violence bred *"hatred and bitterness"* | ⚠️ **Believed violent resistance ALONE could expel British rule**; civil disobedience alone was insufficient without readiness for total revolution |
| **Means and ends** | ⭐⭐ **Means and ends must be EQUALLY good** — would not ally with Fascism or Nazism, which he saw as **dangerous powers, not just aggressors** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A pragmatist about wartime alliance** — took Axis help as **strategy, not ideological kinship**, while admiring Nazi/Fascist DISCIPLINE and rejecting their racial theory |
| **Government** | ⭐ **Advocated a stateless, DECENTRALISED *Ramrajya* — self-sufficient village republics, minimal state, distrust of representative democracy and party politics** | Initially favoured democracy; later held that **an AUTHORITARIAN phase** was necessary first for socialist economic reform *(his Provisional Government of Azad Hind, Oct 1943, made him head of state, PM, and war/foreign minister at once)* |
| **Ideology** | Dismissed both capitalism AND Western socialism (tied to industrialisation) | Called his own synthesis ***"samyavada"*** — Socialism's justice/equality combined with Fascist discipline; explicitly **NOT a communist**, valued nationalism before internationalism |

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE POLITICAL FACTS (HARIPURA, TRIPURI, THE FORWARD BLOC) ARE THE HIGH-YIELD PRELIMS MATERIAL; THE IDEOLOGY TABLE IS THE MAINS ASSET.** *A question on "leadership and ideology in the freedom struggle" can use Gandhi-Bose as a structured comparison — non-violence vs strategic force, decentralised *Ramrajya* vs a planned economy, means-as-ends vs pragmatic alliance.*

## 8️⃣ The August Offer and Individual Satyagraha ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**📜 THE AUGUST OFFER, AUGUST 1940 — Viceroy LINLITHGOW, following the fall of Belgium, Holland, France:**

1. **Dominion status as the objective**
2. **Expanded Viceroy's Executive Council, with an Indian MAJORITY** drawn from major parties
3. **A post-war Constituent Assembly**, mainly Indian-decided, subject to defence/minority/treaty/services obligations
4. **No future constitution without MINORITY consent**

⚠️ **RESPONSES:** *Congress REJECTED it — Nehru: **"Dominion status concept is dead as a doornail."** Gandhi said it **widened the gulf**. ⭐ **The Muslim League welcomed the veto assurance and reiterated Partition as the only solution.*** ⭐⭐ **EVALUATION: for the first time, the inherent Indian right to frame its own constitution was recognised. July 1941: the Executive Council was enlarged to an 8-of-12 Indian majority — but the British kept DEFENCE, FINANCE, HOME.**

**✊ INDIVIDUAL SATYAGRAHA, LATE 1940:**

⭐⭐⭐ **Three aims:** *(i)* show nationalist patience was **not weakness** · *(ii)* express that Indians made **no distinction between Nazism and the "double autocracy" ruling India** · *(iii)* give the government **one more peaceful opportunity** to accept Congress demands.

⭐⭐ **Method:** *a **SELECTED individual per locality** would make an **anti-war declaration**; if unarrested, would repeat it and then **march toward villages and DELHI — the "DELHI CHALO MOVEMENT."*** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **VINOBA BHAVE was the FIRST satyagrahi; NEHRU the SECOND.** By **May 1941, 25,000 convicted.**

⭐ **December 1941, on release:** *the CWC, overriding Gandhi and Nehru, offered war cooperation IF full independence followed and power was substantially transferred immediately. ⭐⭐ It was at this juncture that **GANDHI DESIGNATED NEHRU AS HIS SUCCESSOR** — despite deep temperamental differences (religion, industrialisation, the State) — because, per Rajmohan Gandhi's *The Good Boatman*, Nehru alone among the alternatives (Patel, Rajaji, Azad, Kripalani, Prasad) most reliably reflected Gandhi's **pluralist, all-India, non-sectional** vision.*

# 🚩 PART D — THE POST-WAR UPSURGE, 1945-46

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why the British changed course

⚠️ **This entire event sequence — the INA trials and the RIN Mutiny — is what Spectrum treats as the ACTUAL end of British rule, and it is absent from your NCERT History archive.**

⭐⭐ **TWO STRANDS of the last two years:** *(i)* **negotiations** — government, Congress, League — ending in freedom-with-partition; *(ii)* **sporadic, militant, UNITED mass action** by workers, peasants, states' peoples — the **INA Release Movement, RIN Revolt, Tebhaga, the Worli revolt, Punjab kisan morchas, the Travancore struggle (Punnapra-Vayalar), and Telangana.**

**⚙️ SEVEN REASONS FOR THE BRITISH SHIFT:**

1. **Global power shift** — UK no longer a superpower; **both the US and USSR favoured Indian freedom**
2. **The new LABOUR government** (Attlee PM, Pethick Lawrence Secretary of State, from July 1945) was **more sympathetic**
3. **A wave of socialist-radical governments across Europe**
4. ⭐⭐ **British soldiers exhausted, the economy shattered** — by 1945 Britain owed India **£1.2 billion**, drained further by US Lend-Lease debt *(not settled until 2006)*
5. **Anti-imperialist ferment in South-East Asia** — Vietnam, Indonesia
6. ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **Officials feared a REPEAT OF 1942 — but WORSE**, combining communications attacks, agrarian revolt, labour trouble, and **ARMY DISAFFECTION joined by INA-experienced ex-servicemen**
7. **Elections were overdue** — last held 1934 (Centre), 1937 (provinces)

**🕊️ THE WAVELL PLAN — mentioned, but noted as FAILING to break the deadlock, backed by the outgoing Conservative government.**

## 🔟 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The INA trials — a landmark agitation

⚠️ **June 1945: ban on Congress lifted, leaders released.** *Elections announced August 1945; a post-election Constituent Assembly announced September 1945.*

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE TRIALS:** *the British planned public trials of **hundreds** of INA prisoners, dismissed from service, with **~7,000 detained without trial.** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The FIRST TRIAL, RED FORT, NOVEMBER 1945, put on the same dock a HINDU (PREM KUMAR SEHGAL), a MUSLIM (SHAH NAWAZ KHAN) and a SIKH (GURBAKSH SINGH DHILLON)** — a symbolic misstep that unified Indian opinion instantly.*

**🤝 CONGRESS SUPPORT:** *a **strong resolution at its first post-war session, Bombay, September 1945.*** Defence organised by **Bhulabhai Desai, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Kailash Nath Katju, Nehru, Asaf Ali.** ⭐ **An INA Relief and Enquiry Committee** distributed aid and arranged employment.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SCALE — "landmark on many counts":** *press coverage, pamphlets, graffiti, meetings, ⭐⭐ **INA Day (12 Nov 1945) and INA Week (5-11 Nov)**. Spread from Delhi/Bombay/Calcutta/Madras/UP/Punjab to **Coorg, Baluchistan, Assam**. **Fund contributions came from film stars, municipal committees, overseas Indians, gurudwaras, and TONGAWALLAS.*** ⭐⭐ **Supporters spanned Congress, League, CPI, Unionists, Akalis, Justice Party, the Ahrars, the RSS, the Hindu Mahasabha, the Sikh League** — an almost unprecedented breadth. **Even loyalists and armed-forces men attended meetings and contributed funds.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The central theme became: does Britain have the RIGHT to decide this at all?**

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The three winter upsurges, and the RIN Mutiny

⭐⭐⭐ **THREE EVENTS, ONE PATTERN:**

1. **21 November 1945, Calcutta** — protest over the INA trials; **police firing killed two**
2. **11 February 1946, Calcutta** — protest over a **seven-year sentence given to INA officer Rashid Ali**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **18 February 1946, Bombay — THE RIN MUTINY**

```
   ================================================================
   ** THE RIN MUTINY, 18 FEBRUARY 1946 **
   ================================================================
   ~1,100 ratings of HMIS TALWAR struck over: RACIAL
   DISCRIMINATION (equal pay demand) · bad food · officer abuse
   · the ARREST of a rating for writing "QUIT INDIA" on the
   ship · the INA TRIALS · Indian troops deployed in Indonesia.

   ⭐⭐ Ratings hoisted the TRICOLOUR, the CRESCENT, and the
   HAMMER-AND-SICKLE together on the mast -- other ratings
   joined, drove through Bombay with Congress flags; crowds
   fed them and shopkeepers offered supplies free.
```

**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE THREE-STAGE PATTERN, common to all three upsurges:**

| Stage | What happened |
|:--:|---|
| **I — Defiance and repression** | A group (students, or the ratings) defies authority; met with lathi-charge or firing |
| **II — The city joins in** | Meetings, hartals, attacks on Europeans, police, trams, banks; roads/rail blocked — **Calcutta and Bombay virtually paralysed** |
| **III — Solidarity spreads** | ⭐⭐ **Sympathetic strikes in military establishments — Karachi, Madras, Vizag, Calcutta, Delhi, Cochin, Jamnagar, the Andamans, even BAHRAIN and ADEN.** RAF strikes at Bombay, Poona, Calcutta, Jessore, Ambala |

⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **RESOLUTION: PATEL AND JINNAH TOGETHER persuaded the ratings to surrender, 23 February, with a no-victimisation assurance.**

**⚖️ IMPACT — Spectrum's own strong claims, worth quoting:**

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ *the RIN revolt was **"seen as an event marking the END OF BRITISH RULE."*** *Fearless mass action, and — crucially — **revolt inside the ARMED FORCES**, had a **"great liberating effect on the minds of people."***

⭐⭐ **CONCESSIONS THAT FOLLOWED:** *1 December 1946 — only INA members accused of **murder or brutal treatment of fellow prisoners** would still be tried. **January 1947 — the first batch's sentences remitted.** **By February 1947 — Indian troops withdrawn from Indo-China and Indonesia.***

> 🎯 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE ARGUMENT WORTH CARRYING INTO ANY ANSWER ON "WHY DID BRITAIN LEAVE IN 1947?"** *Textbook answers default to the Cabinet Mission and Partition negotiations — the ELITE story. ⭐⭐ **Spectrum's own claim is that the RIN Mutiny mattered MORE, because it revealed the one thing the Raj could never survive losing: the loyalty of its own armed forces.** ⭐⭐⭐ **A negotiated transfer of power to political leaders is one kind of retreat; an army that mutinies alongside civilians is a different, much more urgent kind of warning — and reason 6 above (§9) shows the British own officials feared EXACTLY that combination.***

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE SIMON COMMISSION WAS ALL-WHITE — the exclusion, not the review itself, was the grievance. AMBEDKAR TESTIFIED, arguing the depressed classes needed independent political protection**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE NEHRU REPORT (dominion status, joint electorates with reservation) UNRAVELLED WITHIN MONTHS into JINNAH'S FOURTEEN POINTS, which explicitly reversed the joint-electorate compromise**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NEHRU'S S-V VS GANDHI'S S-T-S — a genuine strategic disagreement, resolved by Gandhi conceding council entry and then RESIGNING FROM CONGRESS in October 1934**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE 1935 ACT'S ALL-INDIA FEDERATION NEVER STARTED — its conditions were never met, and the Centre ran on the 1919 Act until 1946**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **PROVINCIAL AUTONOMY (1935) REPLACED DYARCHY, ENFRANCHISED ONLY 14%, AND SPECTRUM ITSELF SETS OUT THE SIX-POINT BRITISH STRATEGY BEHIND IT**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **TWENTY-EIGHT MONTHS OF CONGRESS RULE — real civil-liberties gains, agrarian reform blocked by six structural constraints, an "industrial peace" labour policy that failed in Bombay**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **BOSE WON THE 1939 PRESIDENCY AGAINST GANDHI'S CANDIDATE, THEN RESIGNED WHEN THE WORKING COMMITTEE WOULDN'T SERVE HIM — the FORWARD BLOC followed in May 1939**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE AUGUST OFFER WAS REJECTED BY BOTH CONGRESS AND (implicitly) THE LEAGUE — for opposite reasons**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **VINOBA BHAVE, NOT NEHRU, WAS THE FIRST INDIVIDUAL SATYAGRAHI — Nehru was second, and it was on his release that Gandhi named him successor**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE INA TRIALS AND THE RIN MUTINY — NOT THE CABINET MISSION — ARE SPECTRUM'S OWN CANDIDATE FOR "THE EVENT THAT MARKED THE END OF BRITISH RULE"**
