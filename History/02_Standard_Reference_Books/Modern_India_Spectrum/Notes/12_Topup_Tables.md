# 🏰 PART A — INDIA ON THE EVE OF BRITISH CONQUEST *(Ch.4)*

## 1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why the Mughal Empire fell — five historians, five verdicts

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A genuinely citable Mains asset: Spectrum quotes five named historians giving five DIFFERENT primary explanations for the same collapse — an ideal "historiographical debate" answer.**

| Historian | Primary explanation |
|---|---|
| **Satish Chandra** | The **jagirdari crisis** — a growing financial crisis of the jagir system, factional struggle among nobles for shrinking assignments, and the Mughals' failure to accommodate the Marathas within the ruling class |
| **Irfan Habib** | Contemporary 17th-18th c. sources themselves stressed **economic and administrative causes**, not religious reaction or national consciousness |
| **William Irvine** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Military inefficiency was the principal, near-sole cause** — the empire had "lost all military energy at the centre" long before any external conqueror arrived |
| **Sidney Owen** | A **"rottenness at the core of Indian society"** — depraved royalty, selfish nobles, corrupt public service |
| **J.N. Sarkar** | **Aurangzeb's own religious policy** — reversing Akbar's tolerance and reimposing jizya turned the Rajputs from staunchest supporters into enemies, while the Marathas delivered a second, fatal blow |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The Jagirdari Crisis, explained

The nobility held power through **jagirs** (revenue assignments) and **mansabs** (ranks). Akbar had organised them well, but under the later Mughals, **divisiveness by religion, homeland and tribe** fractured the nobility, and **mutual rivalry over a shrinking pool of assignable land** (as conquered territory stopped expanding but claimants kept growing) crippled the state's ability to reward service — the underlying **fiscal-military mechanism** behind "decline," not just "weak rulers."

### The eight causes "in a nutshell"

⭐⭐⭐ Personal despotism dependent on the ruler's character · no fixed law of succession → wars of succession · nobility's degeneration alongside the rulers' · deterioration of the army · an empire too vast for the era's transport/communication · **Aurangzeb's religious policy** (Rajput/Sikh/Jat/Maratha revolts) · **Aurangzeb's Deccan policy**, a complete failure · Iranian and Afghan invasions delivering the final blow.

# 🇮🇳 PART D — INDEPENDENCE WITH PARTITION *(Ch.25)*

## 4️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Mountbatten Plan and the Indian Independence Act, as clause tables

⚠️ **[Cl.12 Suppl. A](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/13_Supplement_A_Understanding_Partition.md) already covers Partition's human and political story in full — this adds only the PLAN'S OWN CLAUSES.**

| Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947) — main points |
|---|
| Punjab and Bengal legislatures to **vote by community (Hindu/Muslim groups)** on partition — a simple majority in either group would trigger it |
| A **partitioned province → two dominions, two constituent assemblies** |
| **Sindh** to decide for itself |
| **Referendums** in NWFP and Sylhet district (Bengal) |
| Princely-state **independence ruled out** — accede to India or Pakistan |
| **Independence for Bengal** as a whole ruled out |
| **Hyderabad's accession to Pakistan** ruled out *(Mountbatten backed Congress here)* |
| Freedom fixed for **15 August 1947**; a **Boundary Commission** to demarcate if partition proceeded |

⭐ **V.P. Menon's innovation, folded into the Plan:** immediate transfer of power on **dominion status** *(with a right to secede)*, avoiding the wait for a full Constituent Assembly agreement.

> ⚠️ **A discarded precursor worth knowing — "Plan Balkan" (March-May 1947):** Mountbatten's own earlier idea, envisaging power transferred to **separate provinces or a loose confederation**, with princely states free to join either dominion or **stay independent**. **Abandoned after Nehru reacted violently against it.**

### Why Congress accepted Dominion Status, despite Lahore 1929's "Purna Swaraj" spirit

Peaceful, quick transfer of power · urgency to assume authority amid worsening communal violence · continuity in the bureaucracy and army.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Indian Independence Act, 1947

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Passed** | British Parliament, **5 July 1947**; royal assent **18 July**; in force **15 August 1947** |
| **Creates** | **Two independent dominions** — India and Pakistan |
| **Interim governance** | Each dominion run under the **GoI Act, 1935** until its own Constitution was adopted |
| **Legislature** | Each dominion's Constituent Assembly to also act as its legislature; the old Central Legislative Assembly and Council of States **automatically dissolved** |
| ⚠️ **Independence dates** | **Pakistan: 14 August. India: 15 August 1947** |

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A named historiographical counter-view, worth citing:** **R.C. Majumdar** argued there is *"no basis for the claim that the Civil Disobedience Movement directly led to independence"* — Gandhi's campaigns, the WWI-era revolutionary bid for German help, and Bose's WWII-era INA all failed individually, but their **cumulative effect**, especially the **INA trials'** revelation that the British could no longer trust sepoy loyalty, is what actually forced the exit. **A genuine "what really caused independence" debate to cite against the standard Congress-led narrative.**

# 🏛️ PART E — ADMINISTRATIVE AND JUDICIAL MACHINERY *(Ch.26 — CROSS-LINK ONLY, per Rule 3)*

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **This section deliberately does NOT rewrite the Regulating Act, Pitt's India Act, the Charter Acts, or the Councils/GoI Acts — [Laxmikanth Ch.1](../../../../Polity/02_Laxmikanth/01_Historical_Background.md) already has all of that, with memory hooks. What follows is the machinery those Acts never describe: who actually staffed the administration, policed it, defended it and judged it.**

## 5️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Evolution of the Civil Services

| Stage | Development |
|---|---|
| **Cornwallis (1786-93)** | First organised the civil services — raised salaries, banned private trade/gifts, enforced seniority-based promotion |
| **Wellesley (1800)** | Fort William College for recruit training; disapproved 1806, replaced by **Haileybury College, England** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Charter Act, 1853** | Ended Company patronage — recruitment via **open competition** *(but examinations stayed in England, in English, on Greek/Latin classics)* |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Indian Civil Service Act, 1861** | Reserved posts for covenanted servants; **max entry age cut from 23 (1859) → 22 (1860) → 21 (1866) → 19 (1878, under Lytton)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1863** | **Satyendranath Tagore** — first Indian to qualify for the ICS |
| **Statutory Civil Service, 1878-79** *(Lytton)* | One-sixth of covenanted posts nominated to "Indians of high family" — **failed, abolished** |
| **Aitchison Committee, 1886** | Renamed the service tiers (Imperial/Provincial/Subordinate); raised age to 23; simultaneous India-England exams **resolved by Commons in 1893 but never implemented** — Kimberley: *"It is indispensable that an adequate number of members of civil service shall always be Europeans"* |
| **Montford Reforms, 1919** | *"The more Indians we can employ... the better"* — recommended simultaneous exams and **one-third recruitment in India** |
| **Lee Commission, 1924** | 50:50 European-Indian parity in ICS within 15 years; a **Public Service Commission** *(as the 1919 Act had already laid down)* |
| **GoI Act, 1935** | Federal and Provincial Public Service Commissions — **but real power stayed British; Indianisation never meant transferred power** |

## 6️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐ Evolution of the Police System

⭐ **Pre-British:** faujdars (law & order), amils (revenue + rebels), kotwals (city policing) — no formal unified system.

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| **1791, Cornwallis** | Modernised the old **thana** system under an Indian **daroga** + a European **SP** per district; relieved zamindars of police duty |
| **1808, Mayo** | An SP per division, aided by **goyendas (spies)** — who committed abuses |
| **1861, Indian Police Act** *(after the 1860 Police Commission)* | A **civil constabulary** with village watchmen linked to the formal force; IG (province) → DIG (range) → SP (district). ⚠️ **No All-India Police was ever created — the Act only set provincial guidelines** |
| **1902** | A **CID** in every province + a **Central Intelligence Bureau** |

## 7️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Army — "martial races" as deliberate policy

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A sharper, more citable fact than most textbooks give: the post-1857 army reorganisation was explicit social engineering.**

- **One-third European ratio** enforced after 1857 *(vs 14% before)* — Bengal Army at 1:2 Europeans:Indians, Madras/Bombay at 2:5
- **No Indian officers** until 1918; highest pre-1914 rank for an Indian was **subedar**; even as late as **1926, the Indian Sandhurst Committee only envisaged 50% Indianised officers by 1952**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The "martial races" doctrine**, under C-in-C **Lord Roberts (1887-92)**: Sikhs, Gurkhas and Pathans — who had **helped suppress 1857** — recruited preferentially; soldiers from Awadh, Bihar, Central India, South India *(who had rebelled)* declared "non-martial." **Charles Wood's own words:** *"I wish to have a different and rival spirit in different regiments, so that Sikh might fire into Hindu, Gorkha into either, without any scruple."* — **divide-and-rule applied directly to army composition**

## 8️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Development of the Judiciary

| Stage | Development |
|---|---|
| **1726** | **Mayor's Courts** at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras — beginning of common-law precedent in India |
| **Warren Hastings (1772-85)** | District **Diwani Adalats** (civil, Hindu/Muslim law) → appeal to **Sadar Diwani Adalat**; District **Fauzdari Adalats** (criminal) → **Sadar Nizamat Adalat**; **Supreme Court at Calcutta (Regulating Act, 1773)** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Cornwallis (1786-93)** | **Separation of revenue and justice** administration; a full civil-court gradation (Munsiff → Registrar → District → 4 Circuit Courts → Sadar Diwani Adalat → King-in-Council); the **Cornwallis Code** — sovereignty of law, officials answerable to civil courts |
| **Bentinck (1828-33)** | Circuit Courts abolished, folded into collectors; **Persian dropped as court language** — vernacular or English |
| **1833** | **Macaulay's Law Commission** → Civil Procedure Code (1859), **Indian Penal Code (1860)**, Criminal Procedure Code (1861) |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **1865** | Supreme Court + Sadar Adalats **merged into three High Courts** — Calcutta, Bombay, Madras |
| **1935** | GoI Act provides for a **Federal Court** *(set up 1937)* |

> ⚠️ **Balanced evaluation:** rule of law and codification replaced personal/religious justice, and even government officials became answerable to civil courts — but the system grew **complicated, expensive, and manipulable by the rich**, and European judges often misread Indian custom.

## 9️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Local Self-Government — from Mayo to Dyarchy

| Milestone | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **1864-68** | First local bodies — nominated, headed by district magistrates; pure tax-collection instruments |
| **Mayo's Resolution, 1870** | Financial decentralisation — provinces could raise **local taxes** for education/sanitation/medical/roads |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Ripon's Resolution, 1882** | **Ripon = "father of local self-government in India."** Non-officials to be a **majority**, and to **chair** local bodies where possible; official interference minimised to a check-and-revise role |
| ⚠️ **Real limitations** | Elected members stayed a **minority** in district boards; **franchise very limited**; government could **suspend or supersede at will** — Curzon actually **tightened** central control |
| **Royal Commission on Decentralisation, 1908** | More powers/funds for **village panchayats**; sub-district boards per taluka/tehsil |
| **Resolution of May 1918** | Local self-government named the **first step** toward the "responsible government" promised in Montagu's August 1917 Declaration |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Under Dyarchy (GoI Act 1919)** | Local self-government made a **"transferred" subject** under popular ministerial control *(mechanism detail: [Spectrum file 08](08_From_Simon_to_Cabinet_Mission.md))* |

# 💰 PART G — ECONOMIC IMPACT OF BRITISH RULE *(Ch.28)*

## 1️⃣1️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Deindustrialisation and the drain theory, as one linked argument

⚠️ **Land-revenue systems (Permanent Settlement etc.) are thoroughly covered at [Cl.12 Th.9](../../../01_NCERT/Class_12_Themes_in_Indian_History_III/Notes/09_Theme9_Colonialism_and_the_Countryside.md). This adds the industrial-destruction and drain ARGUMENTS specifically.**

### Deindustrialisation — three sectors, deliberately destroyed

| Sector | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Textiles** | Britain **stopped paying for Indian textiles in pounds**, paying instead from Bengal's own revenue at depressed rates — the classic textbook case |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Shipbuilding** | Surat, Malabar, Bengal and Masulipatnam had thriving yards. **1813 law**: ships under 350 tonnes barred from the India-Britain route, sidelining most Bengal-built ships. **1814 law**: Indian-built ships denied "British-registered" status, locking them out of American/European trade |
| **Steel** | Firms like **Tata**, having fought for permission to produce at all, were **forced into higher-grade steel for British use** and barred from the larger low-grade market — growth deliberately capped |

> ⚠️⭐⭐⭐ **A modern citation worth using directly:** **Shashi Tharoor**, *An Era of Darkness*: *"deindustrialisation was a deliberate British policy, not an accident... systematic destruction abetted by tariffs and regulatory measures that stacked the decks in favour of British industry."*

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Drain Theory — components, not just the label

| Component of the "drain" |
|---|
| Salaries and pensions of British civil/military officials |
| Interest on India's foreign loans |
| Profits on British investment in India |
| "Home charges" — stores purchased in Britain for Indian departments |
| Payments for British shipping, banking and insurance services |

**Author: Dadabhai Naoroji**, *Poverty and UnBritish Rule in India*. **Mechanism, not just moralising:** the drained wealth **retarded Indian capital formation** while **accelerating British capital formation**, whose surplus then **re-entered India as finance capital — draining India a second time.** Other analysts: **M.G. Ranade, R.C. Dutt** *(The Economic History of India)*, Gokhale, G. Subramaniya Iyer, Prithwishchandra Ray.

### The famine record

⚠️⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Between 1850 and 1900, roughly 2.8 crore (28 million) people died in famines** — Spectrum's own framing: *"not just foodgrain scarcity, but a direct result of poverty unleashed by colonial forces."*

# 📰 PART H — DEVELOPMENT OF THE INDIAN PRESS *(Ch.29)*

## 1️⃣2️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The Press Acts, as one timeline

⭐ *(The newspaper-editor pairs and the four expatriate revolutionary papers are already at [Spectrum file 11](11_Sources_and_Historiography.md) — this adds only the repressive legislation.)*

| Year | Act | What it did |
|:--:|---|---|
| **1878** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Vernacular Press Act** *("the Gagging Act")* | Magistrates could bond/seize the property of vernacular (NOT English) papers for "disaffection"; **no right of appeal**. Repealed by **Ripon, 1882** |
| **1898** | **IPC Section 124A amended + new Section 153A** | Criminalised bringing the government "into contempt" or creating inter-class hatred |
| **1908** | **Newspaper (Incitement to Offences) Act** | Let magistrates confiscate press property for material inciting violence — aimed squarely at Extremist papers |
| **1910** | **Indian Press Act** | Revived VPA's worst features — security deposits at registration, forfeiture powers |
| **1921** | *(Repeal)* | **Sapru Committee** recommended repealing both 1908 and 1910 Acts |
| **1931 (amplified 1932)** | **Indian Press (Emergency Powers) Act** | Sweeping provincial powers to suppress Civil Disobedience propaganda, later widened to "all activities undermining government authority" |

⭐⭐⭐ **Tilak's 1897 sedition trial** *(over "Shivaji's Utterances" in Kesari, justifying Afzal Khan's killing by Shivaji)* earned him **18 months' rigorous imprisonment** and the title **"Lokmanya."** His own words on it: *"The government has converted the entire nation into a prison and we are all prisoners."* His **1908 trial and Mandalay transportation** triggered a Bombay **textile and railway workshop strike** that **Lenin himself hailed as "the entrance of the Indian working class on the political stage."**

# 🎓 PART I — DEVELOPMENT OF EDUCATION *(Ch.30)*

## 1️⃣3️⃣ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The education-policy timeline, dated

| Year | Milestone |
|:--:|---|
| **1781, 1791** | Calcutta Madrasah *(Warren Hastings)*, Sanskrit College, Benaras *(Jonathan Duncan)* — for Muslim/Hindu law respectively, not general education |
| **1813** | **Charter Act** — ₹1 lakh/year pledged for education *(not actually released till 1823, amid the Orientalist-Anglicist row)* |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1835** | **Macaulay's Minute** — settled the row for **Anglicists**: English-medium, Western science/literature only. **"Indian in blood and colour, but English in tastes, in opinions, in morals and in intellect"** — the **"downward filtration theory"**: educate a thin elite, let knowledge trickle down |
| **1854** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Wood's Despatch** — the **"Magna Carta of English Education in India."** First comprehensive plan: government to take **direct responsibility** for mass education *(on paper, reversing filtration)*; a full hierarchy (vernacular primary → Anglo-vernacular high school → district college → presidency university); **secular** instruction; **grants-in-aid** to encourage private effort; stress on **female and vocational** education |
| **1857** | Universities at **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras** established |
| **1882-83** | **Hunter Commission** *(W.W. Hunter)* — focused on primary/secondary education; control shifted to **district/municipal boards**; secondary split into **literary vs vocational** streams |
| **1904** | **Indian Universities Act** *(after the Raleigh Commission, 1902)* — pushed research emphasis, but **cut fellow numbers, gave government veto power over senate regulations, tightened private-college affiliation** — officially justified by the claim that private colleges had become **"factories producing political revolutionaries"** |

### 🔴 Prelims traps

| ⚠️ The claim that looks right | ✅ The truth |
|---|---|
| *All historians agree the Mughal decline was primarily religious* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Five different named historians (Chandra, Habib, Irvine, Owen, Sarkar) give FIVE different primary causes — only Sarkar centres religion** |
| *Rani Lakshmibai died at Jhansi* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **She died on the battlefield at GWALIOR, June 1858, having marched there with Tantia Tope after losing Jhansi** |
| *The Congress attended and boycotted the Round Table Conferences consistently* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Boycotted the First, attended (Gandhi alone) the Second, boycotted the Third** |
| *The Indian Independence Act created a single date of independence for both nations* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Pakistan: 14 August. India: 15 August 1947 — different days** |
| *Ripon is remembered chiefly for the Ilbert Bill* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **He is formally titled "the father of local self-government in India" for his 1882 Resolution — the Ilbert Bill controversy came in the SAME viceroyalty but is a separate legacy** |
| *The British created a unified All-India Police force under the 1861 Act* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **They did not — the Act only laid down provincial guidelines; there was never an All-India Police** |
| *Indians were allowed into the army's officer ranks from the start of the 20th century* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Not until 1918 — the highest pre-1914 rank open to an Indian was subedar** |
| *Wood's Despatch (1854) introduced the downward filtration theory* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The opposite — filtration was MACAULAY's 1835 approach; Wood's Despatch explicitly (on paper) reversed it by asking government to take direct responsibility for mass education** |
| *The Vernacular Press Act applied equally to English and vernacular papers* | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **It targeted vernacular papers only — English papers were exempt, which was its "worst feature"** |

## 🔑 Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **JAGIRDARI CRISIS** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Satish Chandra's term for the fiscal-military breakdown as assignable land failed to keep pace with claimants under the later Mughals** |
| **DOWNWARD FILTRATION THEORY** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Macaulay's 1835 approach — educate a thin English-medium elite and let knowledge "filter" to the masses** |
| **MARTIAL RACES DOCTRINE** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The post-1857 army recruitment ideology privileging Sikhs, Gurkhas and Pathans as "martial," and 1857-rebel regions as "non-martial"** |
| **DRAIN OF WEALTH** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **Naoroji's theory — a measurable, mechanistic transfer of Indian wealth to Britain via salaries, interest, home charges and shipping/insurance/banking payments** |
| **PLAN BALKAN** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Mountbatten's discarded March-May 1947 alternative — province-by-province transfer, abandoned after Nehru's objection** |

## 🔁 Recap — the ten things to carry away

1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **FIVE NAMED HISTORIANS GIVE FIVE DIFFERENT PRIMARY CAUSES FOR THE MUGHAL DECLINE — CHANDRA (JAGIRDARI), HABIB (ECONOMIC), IRVINE (MILITARY), OWEN (SOCIETAL ROT), J.N. SARKAR (RELIGIOUS POLICY)**
2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **1857'S STORM CENTRES EACH HAD A NAMED LEADER AND A NAMED BRITISH OFFICER WHO DEFEATED THEM — JHANSI/HUGH ROSE, DELHI/JOHN NICHOLSON, LUCKNOW/COLIN CAMPBELL**
3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THREE ROUND TABLE CONFERENCES, THREE DIFFERENT CONGRESS POSTURES — BOYCOTT, GANDHI ALONE, BOYCOTT AGAIN**
4. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **INDIA AND PAKISTAN GOT DIFFERENT INDEPENDENCE DATES — 15 AND 14 AUGUST 1947 RESPECTIVELY**
5. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **RIPON IS THE "FATHER OF LOCAL SELF-GOVERNMENT," A SEPARATE LEGACY FROM THE ILBERT BILL CONTROVERSY IN THE SAME VICEROYALTY**
6. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE BRITISH NEVER CREATED AN ALL-INDIA POLICE — THE 1861 ACT ONLY SET PROVINCIAL GUIDELINES**
7. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE "MARTIAL RACES" ARMY DOCTRINE WAS EXPLICIT DIVIDE-AND-RULE, IN CHARLES WOOD'S OWN WORDS**
8. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **DEINDUSTRIALISATION WAS ENFORCED THROUGH SPECIFIC LAWS — THE 1813/1814 SHIPPING TONNAGE ACTS AND TATA'S STEEL-GRADE RESTRICTIONS — NOT JUST MARKET FORCES**
9. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ **WOOD'S DESPATCH (1854) REVERSED MACAULAY'S 1835 "DOWNWARD FILTRATION" APPROACH, ON PAPER**
10. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **THE VERNACULAR PRESS ACT (1878) TARGETED ONLY NON-ENGLISH PAPERS — ITS SINGLE MOST CRITICISED FEATURE**
